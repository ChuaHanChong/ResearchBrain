# Data-efficient Domain Randomization with Bayesian Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Bayesian Domain Randomization (BayRn) is an algorithm that adaptively adjusts simulation parameter distributions based on sparse real-world returns, leveraging Bayesian Optimization to close the sim-to-real gap. This method enables robust policy transfer for robotic tasks, outperforming static domain randomization and mitigating issues observed in explicit system identification approaches.

## Problem

- The 'reality gap' causes policies trained in simulation to fail on physical robots due to inherent environment mismatches.
- Static domain randomization methods are suboptimal as they do not explicitly optimize for real-world performance and require significant prior knowledge.
- Existing adaptive domain randomization techniques often integrate system identification with policy optimization, leading to circular dependencies and potential instability.

## Method

- **BayRn** proposes a ==bilevel optimization== framework that iteratively refines the domain parameter distribution based on real-world returns.
- ==Bayesian Optimization (BO)== is used for sample-efficient selection of the next optimal domain distribution parameters, leveraging a ==Gaussian Process== model of real-world performance.
- Policies are trained in simulation using standard Reinforcement Learning algorithms (e.g., **PPO**, **PoWER**), with simulator parameters randomized according to the BO-optimized distribution.

## Results

- Policies trained with BayRn achieved higher median performance and lower variance on the physical Furuta pendulum compared to Uniform Domain Randomization (UDR) and avoided issues faced by SimOpt.
- BayRn demonstrated improved sim-to-real transferability for the Barrett WAM ball-in-a-cup task, an open-loop control problem where UDR experienced unexpected failures.
- Policies trained without any domain randomization consistently failed to transfer to physical systems for both robotic tasks, underscoring the necessity of domain randomization techniques.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - BayRn implicitly identifies robust domain parameter distributions by directly optimizing for real-world policy performance, bypassing the complexities and potential instabilities of explicit system identification.
> - Bayesian Optimization's sample efficiency significantly reduces the number of costly real-world interactions required for adaptive sim-to-real transfer.
> - Adapting the domain parameter distribution through real-world feedback leads to more robust and consistently performing policies on physical systems compared to fixed, handcrafted randomization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a fundamental challenge in robot control: transferring policies learned in simulation to physical systems. This challenge, known as the "reality gap" or "sim-to-real problem," arises from inherent mismatches between simulated environments and their real-world counterparts. Overcoming this gap is critical for leveraging the cost-effectiveness and data generation capabilities of simulations for robotics research and development.

Historically, two primary strategies have been employed to tackle the reality gap. The first involves enhancing the fidelity of the generative model (simulator) to closely mirror reality, often through system identification techniques. While this approach can lead to high-performance controllers for a specific, well-matched domain, it often results in reduced transferability due to an optimistic bias and the difficulty of modeling all physical phenomena accurately. The second strategy involves introducing variability into the generative model by randomizing its physical parameters, a method known as domain randomization. This approach, while presenting a harder learning problem for the agent due to increased data variability, has demonstrated success in enabling sim-to-real transfer for various robotic tasks.

Within domain randomization, existing approaches largely fall into two categories: those using static (fixed) domain parameter distributions and those employing adaptive distributions. Many state-of-the-art methods rely on static, often handcrafted, distributions for randomizing simulator parameters. While effective in certain scenarios, this fixed-distribution approach is suboptimal because it does not explicitly optimize for real-world performance and assumes substantial prior knowledge regarding the uncertainty of domain parameters.

More advanced research has explored adaptive domain randomization, where the distribution of simulator parameters is dynamically adjusted during training. These adaptive methods often integrate system identification with policy optimization. However, such intertwined processes can introduce circular dependencies, where a poorly performing policy might yield ineffective system identification updates, leading to a downward spiral.

This paper positions Bayesian Domain Randomization (BayRn) as an adaptive approach that distinguishes itself from prior work. Unlike methods that necessitate explicit system identification, BayRn focuses on optimizing the domain parameter distribution based solely on real-world returns. This design choice aims to mitigate the complexities of coupled system identification and policy optimization, while providing a data-efficient mechanism for adapting the simulation environment. Compared to other adaptive methods like "learning to simulate" (which uses REINFORCE), self-paced contextual reinforcement learning (which requires a known target distribution), or active domain randomization (which relies on discriminator-based rewards), BayRn leverages Bayesian Optimization for its sample efficiency and robustness in exploring the domain parameter space, avoiding the fragility of certain non-stationary processes. It also contrasts with approaches like Fingerprint Policy Optimization by directly using real-world returns to guide the Bayesian Optimization process without requiring complex policy abstractions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a data-efficient sim-to-real algorithm capable of adapting the domain parameter distribution used for training robotic policies in simulation, thereby maximizing performance on the real-world target system. This objective is driven by several motivations:

1. **Addressing the Reality Gap Efficiently:** Learning control policies for robots in the real world is resource-intensive due to the high cost and time associated with acquiring sufficient data. Simulations offer a cost-effective alternative for generating vast amounts of data. However, the performance gap between policies trained in simulation and their application in reality necessitates strategies like domain randomization. The paper aims to improve the efficiency of this transfer process.

2. **Overcoming Limitations of Static Domain Randomization:** Current domain randomization techniques often employ static, predetermined distributions for simulator parameters. This approach is considered suboptimal because it does not directly optimize for the policy's performance on the physical system. Furthermore, it assumes a level of prior knowledge about the real system's uncertainties that may not always be available or accurate, leading to either overly conservative or insufficient randomization. The motivation is to move beyond fixed distributions to an adaptive paradigm.

3. **Automating the Randomization Process:** The manual selection and tuning of domain randomization parameters typically involve significant trial and error by human experts. Automating the adjustment of these parameters can reduce development time and dependency on expert knowledge, making sim-to-real transfer more accessible and scalable.

4. **Improving Real-World Performance through Feedback:** Closing the feedback loop with sparse real-world data allows the learning process to be guided by actual performance on the target domain. This iterative refinement of the simulator's parameter distribution is motivated by the expectation that it will lead to policies that are more robust and achieve higher performance on the real system compared to policies trained without such feedback.

5. **Avoiding Complex System Identification:** Many adaptive sim-to-real methods integrate system identification routines to match simulator parameters to reality. However, these can suffer from circular dependencies, where the quality of identified parameters depends on the exploratory behavior of the current policy, and vice-versa. The motivation for BayRn is to achieve adaptive randomization without explicit system identification, simplifying the overall algorithm and potentially enhancing its robustness against poor initial policies.

Based on these motivations, the specific contributions outlined by the authors are:
- Introducing **Bayesian Domain Randomization (BayRn)**, an algorithm that closes the reality gap by training policies in randomized simulations and adapting the simulator parameter distribution based solely on real-world returns.
- Utilizing **Bayesian Optimization (BO)** to enable sample-efficient selection of the next training environment, effectively automating the process of identifying an optimal source domain distribution.
- Conducting **experimental validation** in both sim-to-sim and sim-to-real settings on two distinct robotic tasks, comparing BayRn against multiple baseline methods.
- Demonstrating BayRn's **compatibility** with both step-based and episodic Reinforcement Learning algorithms as policy optimization subroutines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Bayesian Domain Randomization (BayRn), addresses the problem of source domain adaptation based on real-world (target domain) returns through a bilevel optimization formulation. This structure separates the high-level goal of finding optimal domain randomization parameters from the low-level task of policy training within a randomized simulator.

**Bilevel Optimization Formulation:**
The core problem is formulated as:
1. **Upper-Level Problem:** Find the domain distribution parameters `φ*` that maximize the expected real-world return `J_real(θ*(φ))` of a policy `θ*` trained using `φ`. This involves optimizing over the space `Φ` of domain parameter distributions.
2. **Lower-Level Problem:** For a given set of domain distribution parameters `φ`, find the policy parameters `θ*` that maximize the expected return `E_ξ~ν(ξ;φ)[J(θ, ξ)]` in the randomized simulation environment. Here, `ξ` represents specific domain parameters sampled from the distribution `ν(ξ;φ)`.

**BayRn Algorithm (Algorithm 1):**
BayRn iteratively refines the domain parameter distribution `φ` by leveraging Bayesian Optimization (BO) and real-world feedback.

1. **Initialization Phase:**
 - An empty dataset `D` is initialized to store `(φ, J_real(θ*))` pairs.
 - `n_init` initial domain distribution parameter sets `φ` are randomly sampled from the specified parameter space `Φ`.
 - For each `φ`, a policy `θ*` is trained in simulation using a chosen policy optimizer (`PolOpt`), where simulator parameters `ξ` are drawn according to `ν(ξ;φ)`.
 - Each trained policy `θ*` is then evaluated on the real robot for `n_τ` rollouts to estimate its real-world return `J_real(θ*)`.
 - These initial `(φ, J_real(θ*))` pairs are added to `D`, and a Gaussian Process (GP) model is updated with this data. The GP models the unknown function mapping `φ` to `J_real(θ*)`.

2. **Sim-to-Real Loop:**
 - **Acquisition Function Maximization:** In each iteration, BO uses the current GP model to propose the next optimal `φ*` by maximizing an acquisition function (e.g., Expected Improvement). This function balances exploration (sampling in uncertain regions of `Φ`) and exploitation (sampling in regions predicted to have high returns).
 - **Policy Training:** A new policy `θ*` is trained in simulation using `PolOpt`, with `ξ` randomized according to the newly proposed `ν(ξ;φ*)`.
 - **Real-World Evaluation:** The new `θ*` is evaluated on the physical system for `n_τ` rollouts, and its real-world return `J_real(θ*)` is estimated.
 - **GP Update:** The `(φ*, J_real(θ*))` pair is added to `D`, and the GP's posterior distribution is updated, refining its model of the `φ` to `J_real` relationship.
 - **Termination:** The loop continues until a task-specific success threshold `J_succ` is met or a maximum number of iterations (`n_iter,max`) is reached.

**Key Algorithmic Components:**
- **Policy Optimizer (PolOpt):** BayRn is designed to be compatible with various Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithms. For the experiments, Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) was used for step-based tasks and Policy learning by Weighting Exploration with the Returns (PoWER) for episodic tasks.
- **Bayesian Optimization (BO):** This sequential, derivative-free global optimization strategy is central to solving the upper-level problem. BO builds a probabilistic model (GP) of the objective function (real-world return as a function of `φ`) and uses it to guide subsequent evaluations. Its sample efficiency is crucial given the high cost of real-world interactions. The GP was implemented using BoTorch, employing a zero-mean prior and a Matérn 5/2 Kernel.
- **Indirect System Identification:** BayRn explicitly avoids a direct system identification objective. Instead, the BO process implicitly identifies effective `φ` settings that lead to robust real-world policies by optimizing for `J_real(θ*)`.

**Experimental Setup:**
The methodology was validated on two robotic platforms:
- **Quanser Furuta Pendulum:** An underactuated system for a swing-up and balance task. Rollouts were 6 seconds at 100 Hz. Policies were Feedforward Neural Networks (FNN) combined with PPO.
- **Barrett WAM Robotic Arm:** A 4-DOF tendon-driven arm for a ball-in-a-cup task. Episodes were 3.5 seconds at 500 Hz. Policies were RBF-based with PoWER.

**Domain Parameter Randomization:**
The domain parameters `ξ` (e.g., masses, lengths, damping, stiction) were randomized by defining distributions `ν(ξ;φ)`. `φ` represents the parameters of these distributions (e.g., mean and variance). Normal distributions were used for masses and lengths, while uniform distributions were used for friction and damping-related parameters. The range of `φ` values was defined for each parameter (Table I).

**Baselines for Comparison:**
- **PPO/PoWER (No Domain Randomization):** Policies were trained only in a simulation with nominal (CAD/data sheet) parameters, representing standard RL without transfer techniques.
- **Uniform Domain Randomization (UDR):** This baseline uses a fixed, handcrafted distribution for domain parameters during simulation training. It serves as a static DR approach.
- **SimOpt:** An adaptive sim-to-real algorithm that iteratively adapts domain parameters by minimizing the discrepancy between real-world and simulated trajectories. It uses Relative Entropy Policy Search (REPS) to update simulator parameters. *Notably, SimOpt was not applicable to the ball-in-a-cup task due to its requirement for real-world trajectory observations, which were not recorded for the open-loop policy.*

For sim-to-real experiments, 20 policies were trained for each algorithm, with each policy evaluated through 5 real-world rollouts, totaling 700 real-world interactions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation of BayRn was conducted in both sim-to-sim and sim-to-real settings on two distinct robotic tasks.

**Sim-to-Sim Results (Furuta Pendulum):**
In a simplified sim-to-sim experiment, BayRn was tasked with adapting the means of the pendulum and rotary pole masses (two domain distribution parameters). The results indicated that the maximizer of the Gaussian Process (GP) mean function, representing BayRn’s final estimate of the optimal domain distribution parameters (`φ*`), closely aligned with the specified ground truth parameters (`φ_GT`). Specifically, `φ* = [0.0266, 0.1084]^T` was found to be in close proximity to `φ_GT = [0.0264, 0.1045]^T`. This finding qualitatively demonstrated that BayRn's Bayesian Optimization component could effectively converge its belief about the domain parameters towards a known optimal configuration. Furthermore, the posterior standard deviation of the GP was observed to reduce in the vicinity of sampled parameter configurations, indicating a decrease in uncertainty regarding the target domain return as more data was acquired. This outcome suggests BayRn’s ability to implicitly learn an effective domain parameter distribution without explicit system identification.

**Sim-to-Real Results (Furuta Pendulum - Swing-Up and Balance):**
For the swing-up and balance task on the physical Furuta pendulum, BayRn was compared against SimOpt, Uniform Domain Randomization (UDR), and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) without domain randomization.
- **PPO (No DR):** Policies trained solely on nominal simulation parameters completely failed to transfer to the real system, despite performing excellently in simulation. This underscored the presence of a significant reality gap and the necessity of domain randomization for this task.
- **BayRn, SimOpt, and UDR:** All three methods that employed some form of domain randomization achieved median performance above the task's success threshold, indicating successful sim-to-real transfer.
- **UDR:** While capable of transfer, UDR exhibited a significantly higher variance in performance across different policy training runs. This suggests that relying on a fixed, handcrafted distribution for randomization can lead to less consistent policy performance on the real system.
- **SimOpt:** This method generally solved the task, but its system identification subroutine was observed to occasionally converge to extreme or unfavorable domain distribution parameters. This could lead to subsequent policies being poorly trained and providing low returns, which then further degraded the system identification process in a "downward spiral."
- **BayRn:** Demonstrated robust transferability with higher median performance and comparatively lower variance than UDR. This indicates a more reliable and consistent performance on the real system. The final estimated mean domain parameters from BayRn showed less than a 10% deviation from the nominal values, yet these subtle differences were crucial for successful transfer. The variances indicated higher uncertainty on parameters like link lengths, suggesting where the model was most uncertain.

**Sim-to-Real Results (Barrett WAM - Ball-in-a-Cup):**
The ball-in-a-cup task, an open-loop control problem, involved comparisons between BayRn, UDR, and PoWER (without domain randomization). SimOpt was not applicable here due to its reliance on real-world trajectory observations for its system identification component, which were not available for the open-loop policy.
- **PoWER (No DR):** As with the Furuta pendulum, policies trained without domain randomization failed to transfer.
- **UDR:** Faced unexpected failures during experiments, which the authors attributed to a potential high dependency on the initial state in the randomized environments.
- **BayRn:** Showed improved sim-to-real transferability, particularly significant for this open-loop task where domain mismatch tends to be amplified. The ability of BayRn to adapt the domain distribution based on sparse real-world success signals proved beneficial.

In summary, the results indicate that BayRn effectively bridges the reality gap for complex robotic tasks. It performs comparably or superiorly to existing adaptive methods while being more robust and consistent than static domain randomization, and requires less explicit input or reliance on detailed real-world trajectories.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Bayesian Domain Randomization (BayRn) presents a significant advancement in the field of sim-to-real transfer for robotic control, offering several key contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Data Efficiency for Real-World Interaction:** A primary challenge in robot learning is the prohibitive cost and time required for real-world data collection. BayRn addresses this by leveraging Bayesian Optimization, a methodology well-suited for optimizing expensive black-box functions. By using sparse real-world return values to guide the adaptation of simulation parameters, BayRn minimizes the number of costly physical robot experiments, making the sim-to-real transfer process more practical and accessible.

2. **Reduction of Prior Knowledge and Manual Tuning:** Traditional domain randomization often necessitates significant expert knowledge to define appropriate static distributions for simulator parameters. This process is typically iterative and reliant on trial-and-error. BayRn automates the search for effective domain parameter distributions, thereby reducing the reliance on extensive prior knowledge and minimizing manual tuning efforts. This lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners in applying domain randomization techniques.

3. **Improved Robustness and Performance of Transferred Policies:** The experimental results demonstrate that policies trained with BayRn exhibit higher median performance and lower variance on physical systems compared to policies trained with static domain randomization (UDR). This indicates that the adaptive strategy of BayRn leads to more consistently robust policies that can better cope with the inherent discrepancies between simulation and reality.

4. **Alternative to Explicit System Identification:** Many adaptive sim-to-real approaches integrate explicit system identification, which can be prone to circular dependencies and require well-exciting policies for effective updates. BayRn bypasses this by implicitly inferring suitable domain parameter distributions through the objective of maximizing real-world returns. This design choice simplifies the algorithm and makes it more resilient to initial policy performance issues.

5. **Flexibility and Broad Applicability:** The method's compatibility with both step-based (e.g., PPO) and episodic (e.g., PoWER) reinforcement learning algorithms enhances its versatility. This allows BayRn to be applied across a wider range of robotic tasks and control paradigms, from continuous control to tasks with sparse, episodic rewards.

6. **Enabling Complex Robotic Behaviors:** By providing a more reliable and automated path for sim-to-real transfer, BayRn contributes to the broader goal of deploying advanced, autonomously learned behaviors on physical robots. Its success on challenging tasks like the Furuta pendulum swing-up and balance, and the ball-in-a-cup manipulation, demonstrates its potential for enabling more complex and dexterous robotic skills.

7. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies avenues for future work, such as incorporating quantile Gaussian processes to improve scalability to higher-dimensional domain parameter spaces. This suggests that BayRn could serve as a foundational method for exploring even more sophisticated and data-rich real-world robotic applications, where a multitude of physical parameters require randomization and adaptation.

In essence, BayRn represents a step towards more autonomous, robust, and data-efficient robotic learning by providing a systematic approach to adapt simulation environments based on real-world performance feedback, thus accelerating the deployment of learned policies in physical systems.
