# TRANS: Terrain-aware Reinforcement Learning for Agile Navigation of Quadruped Robots under Social Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> The TRANS framework by Zhu et al. introduces a deep reinforcement learning approach that allows quadrupedal robots to navigate complex, uneven terrains while robustly interacting with dynamic human environments. It integrates terrain-aware locomotion with socially intelligent navigation, achieving a 97.6% success rate in challenging simulations and demonstrating effective transfer to physical hardware.

## Problem

- Quadrupedal navigation systems often decouple high-level planning from low-level control, leading to kinematically or dynamically infeasible trajectories on uneven terrain.
- Many robot navigation methods assume static environments or simplified human behavior, which limits their applicability in dynamic, human-populated settings with complex social interactions.
- End-to-end learning methods for robot navigation frequently require computationally intensive, high-frequency exteroceptive sensing or suffer from significant sim-to-real transfer gaps.

## Method

- A two-stage deep ==reinforcement learning== framework (**TRANS**) is proposed, comprising decoupled training for a terrain-aware locomotion controller (TRANS-Loco) and a socially interactive navigation policy (TRANS-Nav).
- TRANS-Loco uses an asymmetric actor-critic DRL architecture with a temporal encoder to learn robust locomotion on uneven terrains from proprioceptive data, avoiding explicit terrain maps or direct contact sensors.
- TRANS-Nav employs transformed historical LiDAR scans to implicitly capture social interactive features for navigation, and the two pre-trained policies are subsequently unified and fine-tuned to enable integrated whole-body navigation.

## Results

- The unified TRANS policy achieved a **97.6%** success rate and **2.4%** collision rate across 500 randomized trials in simulation environments featuring uneven terrain and dynamic pedestrians, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines.
- TRANS-Loco demonstrated high linear velocity tracking performance (mean errors: ∆v_x=**0.10** m/s, ∆v_y=**0.11** m/s) on uneven terrains, showing comparable learning efficiency to privileged methods without explicit terrain data.
- The framework was successfully deployed on a Unitree Go2 robot, demonstrating robust navigation on real-world uneven surfaces and slopes, and exhibited robust zero-shot generalization to unseen environments with **1.5** times higher pedestrian density.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An asymmetric actor-critic architecture combined with a CNN-based temporal encoder can effectively infer implicit terrain features from proprioceptive history, enabling robust locomotion on uneven terrains without explicit terrain maps.
> - Transforming historical LiDAR scans to filter out ego-motion provides a robust and compact representation for identifying dynamic pedestrians and static obstacles, enhancing socially interactive navigation performance.
> - A hierarchical, warm-started DRL approach, integrating pre-trained locomotion and navigation policies, leads to more efficient learning and robust performance in complex, dynamic, and uneven environments compared to end-to-end or fully decoupled methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of autonomous robots in real-world, human-populated environments requires capabilities that address both physical complexity and social dynamics. This paper contributes to the fields of quadrupedal locomotion and robot navigation, specifically at their intersection.

Traditionally, quadrupedal robot navigation systems often decouple high-level motion planning from low-level locomotion control. This separation can lead to inconsistencies where planned trajectories neglect the robot's physical constraints or terrain-specific challenges, potentially resulting in control failures. Conversely, end-to-end learning methods aim to unify these layers but often require high-frequency exteroceptive sensing, which can be computationally intensive, prone to noise, and challenging to deploy on physical hardware. Furthermore, many existing navigation approaches for robots, including quadrupedal platforms, primarily assume static environments. This assumption limits their applicability in dynamic, human-populated settings where implicit social interactions and unpredictable pedestrian behaviors are prevalent.

For wheeled mobile robots, navigation in static environments is well-established. However, navigating dynamic environments with complex human-robot interactions remains challenging. Model-based approaches (e.g., Dynamic Window Approach, Social Force Model, Optimal Reciprocal Collision Avoidance, Model Predictive Control) frequently rely on assumptions of constant pedestrian velocities, perfect state information, or reciprocity, which do not always hold in real-world scenarios. These methods can also exhibit reactive, oscillating, or overly conservative behaviors. Model-free learning pipelines (e.g., Deep Reinforcement Learning, Imitation Learning, Inverse Reinforcement Learning) offer a more integrated approach but often depend on high-fidelity expert demonstrations, assume omnidirectional kinematics, or face significant sim-to-real transfer gaps due to reliance on raw sensor observations.

Research in quadrupedal locomotion has extensively explored traversal of irregular surfaces. Techniques often involve generating explicit elevation maps, which demand considerable onboard computational resources and can be affected by sensor noise and motion blur. To circumvent these issues, approaches like teacher-student paradigms and asymmetric actor-critic architectures have emerged, allowing robots to infer terrain features from proprioceptive data. However, integrating these advanced locomotion capabilities with robust navigation strategies for dynamic, socially interactive environments presents an ongoing challenge. Most work on quadrupedal navigation is confined to static terrains, neglecting the complexities introduced by human presence and interaction.

This work addresses these limitations by proposing a deep reinforcement learning framework that unifies terrain-aware quadrupedal locomotion with socially intelligent navigation. It aims to bridge the gap between high-level planning and low-level control, account for dynamic social interactions, and enable robust real-world deployment on complex terrains without requiring explicit terrain maps.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and agile navigation framework for quadrupedal robots operating in real-world environments characterized by both physically challenging (uneven) terrains and socially dynamic (human-populated) interactions. This goal is motivated by several limitations identified in current robotic navigation paradigms:

1. **Decoupling of Motion Planning and Locomotion Control**: Existing quadrupedal navigation frameworks often separate high-level path planning from low-level gait control. This decoupling frequently results in planning decisions that disregard the robot's whole-body physical constraints and its specific interactions with uneven terrain. Such a lack of integration can lead to suboptimal performance or complete failure when the robot attempts to execute trajectories that are kinematically or dynamically infeasible for its locomotion system.
2. **Reliance on High-Frequency, High-Fidelity Sensing**: End-to-end navigation methods, which attempt to integrate planning and control, commonly demand high-frequency exteroceptive sensing (e.g., precise terrain maps or full-body contact information). In real-world applications, such sensing is often noisy, computationally expensive, and difficult to maintain in real-time on resource-constrained hardware. This dependency limits the practicality and scalability of these approaches.
3. **Assumption of Static Environments and Human Behavior**: A significant portion of existing robot navigation research assumes static environments or simplified models of human behavior (e.g., constant pedestrian velocities). This assumption renders many algorithms unsuitable for deployment in dynamic, human-populated settings where interactions are complex, reciprocal, and unpredictable. The ability to navigate safely and efficiently among humans in various social contexts is crucial for real-world applications such as search and rescue, last-mile delivery, and facility inspection.
4. **Sim-to-Real Transfer Challenges**: Policies trained in simulation often face a substantial "sim-to-real gap" when deployed on physical hardware. Discrepancies between simulated and real-world physics, sensor noise, and environmental details can lead to performance degradation. Overcoming this gap without relying on extensive real-world data collection is a significant motivation.

To address these limitations, the paper sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:

- **Develop a Terrain-Aware Locomotion Controller (TRANS-Loco)**: Design an agile quadrupedal locomotion controller using an asymmetric actor-critic deep reinforcement learning (DRL) architecture. This controller should enable robust traversal of uneven terrains without requiring explicit terrain maps or direct contact state observations, thereby simplifying hardware deployment.
- **Create a Socially Interactive Navigation Policy (TRANS-Nav)**: Develop a navigation policy capable of handling interactions with pedestrians in dynamic environments. This policy should implicitly capture social interactive features from transformed LiDAR scans, mapping them directly to ego-agent actions under differential-drive kinematics, facilitating sim-to-real transfer.
- **Propose a Unified Navigation and Locomotion Architecture (TRANS)**: Integrate the independently trained locomotion and navigation policies into a single, hierarchical framework. This unified policy aims to optimize navigation paths while adhering to the robot’s whole-body constraints and maintaining terrain awareness.
- **Validate Through Comprehensive Benchmarks and Hardware Evaluations**: Conduct extensive comparative analyses against state-of-the-art baselines in both quadrupedal locomotion and robot navigation in simulation. Subsequently, demonstrate the practical efficacy and sim-to-real transfer potential of the unified framework through experiments on a physical quadruped robot.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to provide a scalable and robust solution for deploying quadrupedal robots in complex, human-centric real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named TRANS (Terrain-aware Reinforcement learning for Agile Navigation under Social interactions), employs a two-stage deep reinforcement learning (DRL) architecture comprising three distinct pipelines: TRANS-Loco, TRANS-Nav, and a unified TRANS. All tasks are formulated as Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs) and solved using model-free reinforcement learning.

#### 3.1. Two-Stage Training Architecture

1. **Stage 1: Decoupled Training**:
 - **TRANS-Loco (Locomotion Controller)**: A specialized policy for robust quadrupedal locomotion on uneven terrains is trained. This training occurs in IsaacSim using high-fidelity, full-body quadruped models.
 - **TRANS-Nav (Navigation Policy)**: A separate policy for navigation in socially interactive environments is developed. This policy uses a simplified Center-of-Mass (CoM) model governed by differential kinematics and associated constraints.
2. **Stage 2: Unified Retraining**:
 - The pre-trained TRANS-Loco and TRANS-Nav policies are integrated into a unified architecture. This combined policy is then fine-tuned to enable the quadruped robot to navigate challenging terrains while performing reactive collision avoidance with dynamic pedestrians and static obstacles.

#### 3.2. TRANS-Loco: Quadrupedal Locomotion Controller

TRANS-Loco builds upon the DreamWaQ architecture, employing an asymmetric actor-critic DRL framework.

- **Observations and States**:
 - **Actor (s_t^l,a)**: Receives proprioceptive observations (angular velocity, gravity vector, target velocity command, joint positions, joint velocities, previous action). A temporal encoder (CNN + MLP) processes a history of these observations to extract a latent representation (z_t^l) that implicitly encodes terrain geometry and contact dynamics, along with an estimated body linear velocity (ˆv_t). This ensures hardware deployment without explicit terrain maps or contact sensors.
 - **Critic (s_t^l,c)**: During training, the critic has access to both proprioceptive observations and privileged exteroceptive information from the simulator, including a local terrain height map, full-body contact forces, and ground-truth body linear velocity. This privileged information aids in more accurate value estimation.
- **Actions (a_t^l)**: Consist of 12 angular offsets relative to the robot's default standing configuration. These offsets define target joint positions, which are then tracked by a low-level Proportional-Derivative (PD) controller that computes the required joint torques.
- **Networks**:
 - **Actor and Critic Networks**: Both use Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) with Exponential Linear Unit (ELU) activation functions in hidden layers and linear output layers.
 - **Temporal Auto-Encoder**: A variational temporal encoder (CNN + MLP) processes historical observations to extract latent features (z_t^l) and estimated body linear velocity (ˆv_t). A decoder (MLP) reconstructs the proprioceptive state. The network parameters are optimized via a multi-objective loss combining the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) loss for control, Mean Squared Error (MSE) for velocity estimation, and a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) loss for representation learning.
- **Rewards**: A composite reward structure, based on DreamWaQ, includes task rewards (penalizing deviations from commanded linear and angular velocities) and stability rewards (penalizing vertical body velocity, angular velocity in XY plane, high torques, angular accelerations, and encouraging appropriate feet air time).
- **Curriculum Learning and Domain Randomization**: Training environments include diverse terrains (flat, slopes, stairs, rough, stepped grids) with adaptively modulated difficulty. Domain Randomization (DR) is applied to system dynamics (friction, mass, CoM), observations (noise), and reset conditions to enhance sim-to-real transferability.

#### 3.3. TRANS-Nav: Navigation under Social Interactions

TRANS-Nav is designed for navigation in dynamic, human-populated environments.

- **Actions and Kinematics**: The quadruped robot is abstracted as a circular ego-agent with differential-drive kinematics, generating linear (v_t^n) and angular (w_t^n) velocity commands. Lateral velocity commands are excluded to prevent self-collisions on uneven terrain.
- **Observations and States (s_t^n,*)**:
 - **Transformed LiDAR Scans**: Historical LiDAR scans are transformed into the current robot frame. This process filters out ego-motion, allowing the network to isolate and represent the independent movements of pedestrians and identify static obstacles. The observation (O_t^n) consists of the current and several past transformed LiDAR scans.
 - **Goal Position (g_t)**: The distance and relative orientation to the goal in the robot's frame.
 - **Previous Action (a_t-1^n)**: Included to account for reciprocal interactions and robot-pedestrian dynamics.
 - The state (s_t^n,*) is symmetric for both the actor and critic, as stable LiDAR data is considered readily available in real-world scenarios.
- **Networks**:
 - **Actor and Critic Networks**: Both are MLPs with Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) activation functions in hidden layers and linear output layers. The actor outputs parameters for a normal distribution for continuous actions.
 - **Encoder Network**: Uses a combination of CNNs and MLPs to extract latent features (z_t^n,a and z_t^n,c) from the transformed LiDAR scans for the actor and critic, respectively.
 - **Optimization**: The network parameters are updated using the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm. Twin critics are used to mitigate value overestimation. For stability, CNN updates are primarily performed for the critic encoder and then copied to the actor encoder.
- **Rewards**: The reward function is structured to prioritize collision avoidance and goal reaching. It provides positive reward for goal arrival, negative penalties for collision, and discomfort penalties when approaching obstacles or pedestrians too closely. It also rewards progress towards the goal.
- **Pedestrian Motions and Domain Randomization**: Pedestrian motions are simulated using ORCA, modified to make pedestrians less reactive to the robot (if robot speed is too low), forcing the robot to proactively avoid. Uniform noise is applied to pedestrian velocities and static obstacle positions for domain randomization.

#### 3.4. Unified Quadrupedal Navigation (TRANS)

This stage integrates the pre-trained TRANS-Loco and TRANS-Nav policies.

- **Expanded Framework**:
 - The simplified CoM model is replaced with a high-fidelity quadruped robot model in IsaacSim, allowing full quadrupedal dynamics to be considered.
 - The pre-trained TRANS-Loco policy is frozen and interfaced directly with the navigation policy.
 - The unified state space (s_t^u,*) combines the latent features and observations from both TRANS-Nav (transformed LiDAR, goal, previous navigation action) and TRANS-Loco (estimated body linear velocity, latent locomotion features, current proprioceptive observations). Locomotion-related states are updated at a lower frequency (every 10 navigation control cycles).
 - **Additional Rewards**: A penalty is added for robot falls, and a regularization term is included to penalize abrupt changes in angular velocity.
 - **Action Clipping**: To ensure stable motion and prevent the robot from freezing or entering kinematically unstable states on uneven terrain, linear and angular velocities are set to zero if the commanded linear velocity falls below a specified threshold (0.3 m/s).
- **Network Parameters**: The unified networks use the same architecture as TRANS-Nav but with expanded input shapes to accommodate the combined state space. Parameters are initialized using a "warm-start" procedure, transferring weights from the pre-trained TRANS-Nav networks and padding new dimensions with zeros. The SAC algorithm is used for optimization.

This hierarchical and integrated approach aims to leverage the strengths of both specialized controllers while addressing the complex interactions between high-level navigation goals and low-level quadrupedal dynamics in challenging environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive simulation evaluations for each component and the unified framework, followed by hardware implementation results.

#### 4.1. Simulation Evaluations for Locomotion (TRANS-Loco)

- **Learning Efficiency**: TRANS-Loco demonstrated learning efficiency comparable to the "Privilege" method, which has full access to ground-truth terrain data. This performance exceeded that of other methods like SLR and teacher-student paradigms (L2T, TAR), which showed slower or no progression in terrain level. The DreamWaQ-A1 variant failed to progress beyond flat terrain.
- **Ablation Studies**:
 - Replacing TRANS-Loco's CNN-based encoder with an MLP architecture (TRANS-Loco-MLP) on both Unitree Go2 and A1 platforms resulted in degraded learning efficiency and lower achieved terrain levels. This suggests the CNN-based encoder is effective in extracting implicit terrain features.
 - The fine-tuned reward structure was observed to be important for stabilizing training, particularly for the Unitree A1 platform, where the original DreamWaQ struggled.
- **Command Tracking**: TRANS-Loco (on Unitree Go2) achieved high linear velocity tracking performance (mean errors: ∆v_x=0.10±0.12 m/s, ∆v_y=0.11±0.13 m/s), outperforming most non-privileged baselines. Qualitative analysis indicated superior adaptability and faster recovery from obstacles compared to DreamWaQ, which often exhibited lower body clearance and slower recovery.

#### 4.2. Simulation Evaluations for Navigation (TRANS-Nav)

- **Learning Efficiency**: TRANS-Nav exhibited robust learning efficiency and stable convergence, outperforming DRL-based baselines like RGL, LNDNL, and HEIGHT. DRL-VO achieved comparable efficiency only after its network structure was adapted to that proposed in TRANS-Nav, highlighting the architectural contribution. RGL was computationally intensive and converged to suboptimal success rates under ego-agent velocity constraints.
- **Quantitative Performance (500 trials)**: TRANS-Nav achieved the highest Success Rate (SR=0.972), the lowest Collision Rate (CR=0.028), and a zero Timeout Rate (TR=0.000) among all evaluated methods (classical geometric planners, MPC-based, and DRL-based).
 - Model-based MPC approaches (N-MPC, NeuPAN) often suffered from the "frozen robot" problem due to conservative planning or incompatibility with ORCA-based pedestrian models. T-MPC was aggressive, leading to high collision rates.
 - Geometric methods (DWA, ORCA, AVOCADO) also showed degraded performance under stricter velocity constraints.
 - Compared to other DRL methods, TRANS-Nav's approach, relying solely on transformed LiDAR scans, yielded superior performance without requiring explicit object maps or pedestrian states, unlike DRL-VO.
- **Trajectory Analysis**: In representative scenarios, TRANS-Nav produced efficient and safe trajectories, balancing goal-reaching speed with collision avoidance. Other methods exhibited behaviors ranging from aggressive (RGL, T-MPC), overly conservative (N-MPC, LNDNL), or prone to "discomfort zones" (DWA).
- **Ablation Studies**:
 - Replacing SAC with PPO for TRANS-Nav resulted in zero success rate.
 - Enabling CNN updates in both actor and critic networks (TRANS-Nav-CNN) significantly degraded learning performance, confirming the benefit of updating only the critic encoder.
 - Using raw LiDAR scans without transformation (TRANS-Nav-Direct) led to a decrease in success rate and an increase in navigation time, validating the effectiveness of the proposed LiDAR transformation.
 - TRANS-Nav maintained high performance even with domain randomization applied to pedestrian velocities and obstacle positions, demonstrating robustness.

#### 4.3. Simulation Evaluations for Unified Policy (TRANS)

- **Overall Performance**: The unified TRANS policy achieved the highest Success Rate (SR=0.976) and lowest Collision Rate (CR=0.024) across 500 randomized trials in uneven, socially interactive environments.
- **Impact of Design Choices**:
 - Traditional decoupled pipelines (Nav-Loco, TRANS-Loco-DWA) showed reduced success rates (SR=0.924, 0.894 respectively) and increased timeouts/falls, indicating the limitations of separating planning and control.
 - Retraining the unified policy from scratch (TRANS-Direct) yielded performance comparable to the decoupled baseline (SR=0.924), suggesting the necessity of guided learning.
 - Warm-start initialization (TRANS-Warm-Start, SR=0.954) improved the success rate, demonstrating the benefit of pre-training.
 - Action clipping (TRANS-Clip, SR=0.916) alone slightly degraded performance in simulation but was identified as crucial for sim-to-real transfer.
 - The synergy between warm-start initialization and action clipping was critical, boosting the success rate to 0.976, highlighting adaptability to constrained action spaces.
- **Failure Analysis**: Out of 500 trials, 12 collisions occurred (3 head-on, 3 robot rear-end, 6 pedestrian rear-end). Failures were attributed to reactive pedestrian dynamics, mutual avoidance errors, and robot drift or getting stuck on challenging terrains, particularly when attempting in-place rotations.
- **Zero-Shot Generalization**: When tested in unseen environments with 1.5 times the pedestrian density (up to 10 pedestrians), the unified TRANS policy exhibited only marginal degradation in performance (SR decreased from 0.976 to 0.926, average NT increased from 19.26 s to 20.53 s), demonstrating robust generalization.

#### 4.4. Hardware Implementations

- **System Deployment**: The system was deployed on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot with Livox MID-360 LiDAR and ZED2 stereo camera, processed on an external laptop. A multi-rate ROS Noetic setup and CPU affinity were used for real-time operation (critical modules operating well within timing budgets, e.g., Vel-Cmd at 0.8 ms inference).
- **Real-world Performance**: Evaluated on diverse terrains including indoor flat, uneven surfaces, outdoor slopes, and stairs.
 - Failures were predominantly observed during stair traversal (4 out of 10 trials) and were frequently linked to localization drift caused by high-frequency structural vibrations and dynamic environmental features. The camera's limited Field of View (FoV) was also noted as a factor.
 - **Virtual Pedestrians/Obstacles**: In structured indoor rough terrain with virtual dynamic obstacles, TRANS (SR=0.92, NT=19.15 s) outperformed DWA (SR=0.82, NT=16.40 s). DWA's longer inference time (30 ms vs. 0.8 ms for TRANS) and inability to account for quadrupedal constraints contributed to its lower success rate and increased timeouts/falls.
 - **Stairs with Virtual Pedestrians**: Ascending stairs proved more challenging (8/10 success, 1 fall, 22.65 s NT) than descending (9/10 success, 0 fall, 17.38 s NT). This indicated that simultaneous collision avoidance and stair climbing remains a practical challenge.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that advances the capabilities of quadrupedal robots in complex, unstructured, and socially dynamic environments. The TRANS framework offers several contributions with potential impacts on various applications.

#### 5.1. Significance of Contributions

- **Unified and Hierarchical Control**: The development of a unified architecture that integrates high-level motion planning with low-level locomotion control is a notable contribution. This integration directly addresses the limitations of decoupled systems, ensuring that navigation decisions are dynamically feasible and terrain-aware, respecting the robot's whole-body constraints.
- **Terrain-Aware Locomotion without Explicit Maps**: TRANS-Loco's ability to achieve robust locomotion on uneven terrains without relying on explicit terrain maps or contact state estimation simplifies hardware deployment and reduces computational overhead. By inferring implicit environmental features from proprioceptive history using an asymmetric actor-critic architecture, the framework enhances adaptability to unforeseen or dynamic terrains.
- **Socially Interactive Navigation via Transformed LiDAR Scans**: The proposed method of using transformed LiDAR scans to implicitly capture socially interactive features is significant. This approach reduces the reliance on precise state estimation of individual agents, which can be noisy and difficult to obtain in real-world settings. By filtering ego-motion from historical scans, the framework more effectively discerns pedestrian movements and static obstacles, thereby improving navigation performance and facilitating sim-to-real transfer.
- **Robustness and Generalization**: The comprehensive simulation benchmarks and ablation studies demonstrate the framework's robustness across various challenging scenarios, including high pedestrian densities and diverse terrains. The observed zero-shot generalization to unseen, more crowded environments suggests the policy's capacity to adapt beyond its training distribution.
- **Demonstrated Sim-to-Real Transfer**: Successful deployment on a physical Unitree Go2 quadruped robot across different real-world terrains, including slopes and stairs, confirms the practical feasibility of the framework. This validation is critical for transitioning research findings to tangible applications.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

The capabilities demonstrated by TRANS have the potential to impact several real-world applications:

- **Search and Rescue Operations**: Quadrupedal robots equipped with such navigation systems could traverse challenging debris fields and unstable terrains in disaster zones while cautiously navigating around human survivors or rescue personnel.
- **Last-Mile Delivery**: Autonomous delivery robots could operate in urban and suburban environments, handling varied surfaces (e.g., sidewalks, grass, stairs) and dynamically interacting with pedestrians, cyclists, and other vehicles to ensure safe and efficient deliveries.
- **Facility Inspection and Surveillance**: Robots could perform autonomous patrols in industrial complexes, construction sites, or public spaces, navigating complex layouts, uneven floors, and dynamic human traffic for monitoring and inspection tasks.
- **Human-Robot Collaboration**: Enhanced social navigation capabilities could facilitate more natural and safer human-robot interaction in shared spaces, fostering acceptance and integration of robots into daily life.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

The paper also acknowledges several limitations that suggest avenues for future research:

- **Sim-to-Real Discrepancies**: The most notable challenges in hardware implementation occurred during stair traversal, indicating persistent discrepancies between simulated and real-world physics for highly dynamic tasks.
- **Localization Drift**: High-frequency vibrations from quadrupedal locomotion were observed to induce localization drift in the LiDAR SLAM algorithm, impacting real-world navigation accuracy. Future work will focus on enhancing localization stability through more robust state estimation techniques.
- **Limited Sensor Field of View**: The onboard camera's limited Field of View (FoV) in hardware experiments, compared to the 360-degree FoV in simulation, is a practical constraint. Assumptions about pedestrian avoidance outside the camera's range might not always hold.
- **Human Behavior Modeling**: The use of ORCA for simulating multi-agent interactions, while standard, does not fully capture the complexity and heterogeneity of real-world human behaviors. This can impede the transferability of navigation policies. Future research will explore integrating more sophisticated and diverse human interaction models into the simulation pipeline.
- **Recovery Behaviors and Rotational Stability**: The analysis of failure cases indicates a need for improved stability during high-torque rotational maneuvers and the development of safe backward locomotion capabilities to recover from stalled or "frozen" states without compromising balance.

Overall, the TRANS framework represents an advancement towards agile, terrain-aware, and socially intelligent navigation for quadrupedal robots, demonstrating strong foundations for practical deployment while also identifying critical areas for future development.
