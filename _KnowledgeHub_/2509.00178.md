# Poke and Strike: Learning Task-Informed Exploration Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> A reinforcement learning framework is introduced that enables robots to autonomously learn exploratory motions and infer task-relevant physical properties of objects. This approach integrates an online property estimator and an uncertainty-based switching mechanism, leading to successful one-shot manipulation tasks in both simulation, achieving a 90.1% success rate for striking, and real-world robot experiments.

## Problem

- Robots performing dynamic, one-shot manipulation tasks often face unknown object physical properties that must be accurately identified prior to execution, as immediate adaptation or retries are not possible.
- Current exploratory behaviors frequently rely on human-designed, task-specific motions which are suboptimal, difficult to generalize, inefficient due to fixed durations, and cumbersome to create for every new task.
- Existing system identification techniques often assume equal importance for all identified parameters, failing to prioritize properties most relevant to the task, and typically require re-optimization or retraining at test time.

## Method

- A reinforcement learning framework is proposed to learn a ==task-informed exploration policy== and an ==online physical property estimator== simultaneously, leveraging a ==privileged task policy==.
- Exploration rewards are automatically generated by analyzing the sensitivity of the privileged task policy's performance to estimation errors, thereby guiding the robot to prioritize the identification of task-relevant properties.
- An ==uncertainty-based mechanism==, utilizing an ==ensemble of neural networks== to estimate predictive uncertainty, intelligently determines when to cease exploration and transition to immediate task execution, balancing accuracy and efficiency.

## Results

- Achieved a **90.1%** success rate on the one-shot Striking task in simulation, significantly outperforming all tested baselines (maximum **40%**), and demonstrated consistent performance improvements for the Edge Pushing task.
- Task-informed exploration rewards effectively captured task-relevant physical properties, yielding tighter estimation thresholds for critical parameters like Center of Mass and dynamic friction, ensuring higher accuracy where needed.
- Validated on a KUKA iiwa robot arm for Striking (**8/9** successful runs) and Edge Pushing (**8/10** successful runs), confirming the practical applicability of the learned exploration policy and uncertainty-based switching in physical environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Task performance is highly sensitive to errors in estimating specific physical properties, and this sensitivity can be automatically leveraged to design effective, prioritized exploration rewards without manual specification.
> - The simultaneous training of an exploration policy with an online property estimator allows for real-time inference and immediate task execution after exploration, circumventing the need for simulator queries or re-optimization at test time.
> - Predictive uncertainty of property estimates, derived from an ensemble of neural networks, serves as a reliable proxy for actual estimation error, enabling adaptive and robust policy switching from exploration to task execution in real-world scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge within interactive perception and robot manipulation, specifically concerning tasks that require identifying unknown physical properties of objects through active interaction. This area is distinct from identifying visual properties, as it necessitates physical engagement with the environment.

Current approaches to exploratory behaviors in robotics often rely on human-designed, task-specific exploratory motions. These pre-defined strategies can be suboptimal, difficult to generalize, and cumbersome to create for every new task. Some existing methods, such as those proposed by Memmel et al. and Liang et al., have explored learning informative exploratory motions. However, these methods typically involve inefficiencies at test time, requiring extensive simulator queries and re-optimization or retraining of task motions for each object after properties are identified. Furthermore, these approaches often employ fixed exploration durations, which can be either too short (insufficient information) or excessively long (inefficient). A common limitation across several system identification techniques is the assumption of equal importance for all identified parameters, failing to prioritize properties that are most relevant to the task at hand.

Domain randomization (DR) is another technique used to enhance policy robustness to varying environmental factors, including physical properties. While DR improves generalization, policies trained solely with this method may exhibit average performance across diverse conditions. More adaptive DR methods necessitate obtaining posterior distributions of object properties and retraining policies for specific properties, adding complexity and computational overhead.

The concept of privileged learning, where a robot has access to information unavailable during real-world deployment (e.g., ground truth physical properties), has been utilized to accelerate training or inform policy design. However, most privileged learning applications in this context focus on inferring hidden states or parameters from passive observation during task execution, rather than active exploration motions specifically designed to uncover these parameters.

This research distinguishes itself by proposing a framework that directly addresses these limitations. It introduces a method for automatically discovering task-informed exploration strategies through reinforcement learning, eliminating the need for manual design. By leveraging rewards derived from the sensitivity of a privileged task policy to estimation errors, the approach implicitly prioritizes the identification of task-relevant physical properties. Crucially, it integrates an online property estimator that allows for immediate task execution after exploration, circumventing the need for simulator queries or re-optimization at test time. Additionally, the inclusion of an uncertainty-based mechanism enables adaptive exploration durations, ensuring sufficient accuracy while minimizing exploration time. The framework extends privileged learning by focusing on active exploration to identify physical parameters that are critical for one-shot, irreversible robotic tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from the inherent challenges in dynamic robotic tasks where the robot must interact with objects possessing unknown physical properties. Examples include striking a puck into a goal, pushing a box to a table edge, or balancing a pole with unknown mass distribution. In such "one-shot" tasks, initiating the task with incorrect assumptions about object properties can lead to irreversible failure, making online adaptation or correction impractical without human intervention. The robot must accurately infer these properties *prior* to task execution.

Unlike visual characteristics that can often be identified passively, physical properties like friction, mass, weight distribution, and restitution necessitate active physical interaction—e.g., pushing, poking, shaking, or stirring—to reveal themselves. While humans naturally develop and adapt exploratory behaviors, enabling robots to autonomously generate informative physical explorations remains a significant research hurdle. Relying on pre-defined, human-engineered exploratory motions for every new task is often suboptimal and resource-intensive.

To address these fundamental challenges, the authors outline three primary objectives for their work:

1. **Automatic Discovery of Informative Exploratory Motions:** The first objective is to develop a method that can automatically discover and learn exploratory motions. These motions should be specifically designed to identify task-relevant physical properties, even if they differ from the optimal motions required for the task itself. The learned exploratory behaviors should also be adaptable to different tasks.
2. **Adaptive Transition from Exploration to Task Execution:** The second objective is to create a mechanism that intelligently determines when to cease exploration and transition to task execution. This mechanism must ensure that the property estimates obtained are sufficiently accurate for successful task completion, while simultaneously minimizing the exploration time, thereby enhancing efficiency.
3. **Immediate Task Execution Post-Exploration:** The third objective is to enable the robot to execute the primary task immediately after the exploration phase. This avoids the inefficiencies and computational overhead associated with traditional methods that require additional simulator queries or re-optimization of task policies at the time of deployment with newly identified properties.

These objectives collectively aim to improve the autonomy, efficiency, and success rate of robots performing interactive, one-shot manipulation tasks with unknown object dynamics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed approach is a task-informed exploration framework based on reinforcement learning (RL). It leverages a privileged task policy to automatically generate rewards for training an exploration policy, enabling the identification of task-relevant properties. An uncertainty-based mechanism governs the transition from exploration to task execution.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is formulated as a family of Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) where the environment's dynamics are conditioned on unknown physical properties ϕ of the manipulated object. The physical properties ϕ are assumed constant throughout an episode but vary between episodes. The goal is to learn a policy π that maximizes the expected discounted return across different values of ϕ, acknowledging that ϕ is hidden and must be inferred. Given the one-shot nature of the tasks, immediate adaptation or retries are not possible, necessitating pre-task exploration and property inference.

#### 3.2. Privileged Task Policy Learning

The initial step involves training a privileged task policy (π_task) using RL in a simulated environment. This policy has access to the ground truth physical property values (ϕ\*), which are unavailable in real-world deployment. This privileged access is hypothesized to reduce exploration needs and prevent convergence to suboptimal policies. During training, π_task receives ϕ\*, the current state s_t, and the goal g, and outputs actions a_t. Task rewards (r_task) are defined based on task objectives, with positive rewards for success and negative rewards for constraint violations. Initial states are randomized to prepare the policy for environments perturbed by exploration.

#### 3.3. Learning of Exploration and Property Estimation

To address the unavailability of ground truth properties in real-world settings, two components are trained simultaneously:
1. **Exploration Policy (π_exp):** This policy learns to perform motions that are informative for estimating ϕ. It takes the current robot and object states (s_t) as input and outputs actions (a_t). The exploration policy reward (r_exp) is structured to incentivize accurate property estimation while respecting operational constraints. A positive reward (r_estimation) is given if the estimation errors (ε_estimation,j) for all physical properties fall below pre-defined thresholds (ε_threshold,j). The estimation error is calculated as the absolute difference between the ground truth (ϕ\*_j) and the estimated property value (ˆϕ_j). A negative reward (r_failure) is applied for violating workspace boundaries, preventing infeasible task execution. On-policy RL is used for stable training, especially given the non-stationary nature of rewards, which depend on the simultaneously trained estimator.
2. **Online Physical Property Estimator (f_ϕ):** This module infers physical properties from state observations gathered during exploration. It employs a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network to capture essential temporal information for object dynamics. The estimator is trained concurrently with the exploration policy. After each training update of π_exp, a new dataset (D) of rollouts (states s_t and corresponding ground truth ϕ\*) is collected. This dataset is then used to update f_ϕ by minimizing an estimation loss (L_est), defined as the negative log-likelihood of a multivariate Gaussian distribution (Appendix A.2, Eq. 4). This simultaneous training on fresh data from the most recent exploration policy helps mitigate out-of-distribution issues commonly encountered with learned estimators.

#### 3.4. Task-Informed Exploration Reward Design

A core contribution is the automatic generation of estimation error thresholds (ε_threshold,j) for the exploration rewards, eliminating the need for manual specification.
- **Modeling Task Performance Sensitivity:** The method assumes a uni-modal relationship between task success rate (y) and estimation error (ε) for each physical property. To model this, a parametric uni-modal function g_j(ε) is fitted to empirical data (D_ε,j). This data is collected by systematically perturbing the ground truth value of one property (j) at a time (while keeping others unperturbed) and rolling out the privileged task policy, recording the resulting task success rates. The rate at which task success deteriorates with increasing error indicates the task's sensitivity to that property.
- **Computing Task-Informed Exploration Rewards:** From the fitted function g_j, an estimation error threshold (ε_threshold,j) is computed for each property j. This threshold ensures that the task success rate remains above a specified proportion (p) of the maximum possible success rate achieved by the privileged task policy (g_j(ε_threshold,j) ≥ p ⋅ max_ε g_j(ε)). Properties more relevant to the task, exhibiting higher sensitivity, will yield tighter thresholds, thereby guiding the exploration policy to estimate these properties with greater accuracy.

#### 3.5. Uncertainty-Based Policy Switching

To enable autonomous transition from exploration to task execution in real-world settings where ground truth estimation error is unavailable, the method incorporates an uncertainty-based switching mechanism.
- **Uncertainty Estimator:** Predictive uncertainty of the physical property estimator (f_ϕ) is estimated using an ensemble of M neural networks. Each network outputs both a predicted mean (ˆϕ_i,t) and a predicted covariance (ˆΣ_i,t), capturing both aleatoric (data noise) and epistemic (model limitation) uncertainty. The ensemble is approximated by a single Gaussian distribution with a mean (ˆϕ_t) and covariance (ˆΣ_t) derived from the mixture (Eq. 4). The mean serves as the predicted physical property value, and the covariance serves as the measure of predictive uncertainty.
- **Computing Uncertainty Thresholds:** Uncertainty thresholds required for successful task execution are determined by rolling out the exploration and task policies in simulation to collect uncertainty data correlated with task outcomes (success/failure). For each property, the p-th percentile of uncertainty values from successful task trials is calculated. These thresholds indicate a level of uncertainty below which the task policy is likely to succeed, providing a robust criterion for switching from exploration to task execution without direct access to estimation error during deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the proposed task-informed exploration approach in both simulation and on a physical robot across various manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Task Performance

On the one-shot Striking task, the proposed method achieved a 90.1% success rate, substantially outperforming all tested baselines, which reached a maximum of 40%. The exploration policy alone achieved a 92.3% success rate in estimating properties, leading to a 98.7% success rate for the subsequent task execution. This demonstrates that the learned exploration policy provides sufficiently accurate property estimates for successful task performance. Consistent performance improvements were observed for the Edge Pushing task.

- **DR Baselines:** Domain Randomization (DR) and its variants (DR+Stack, DR+LSTM, DR+Stack+Est) yielded low success rates (23.3% to 35.4%). DR alone often learned an average motion, while methods with temporal information struggled to associate exploratory motions with delayed task rewards or balance exploration and task execution within a single policy.
- **Privileged Baselines:** Methods leveraging a privileged policy (Student, UP-OSI, RMA) also showed limited success (23.0% to 33.7%). This is attributed to the inherent lack of exploratory behavior in the privileged task policy, making direct imitation or execution with online estimation insufficient for tasks requiring pre-interaction property identification.

#### 4.2. Task-Informed Exploration Rewards Capture Task-Relevant Physical Properties

The analysis of task sensitivity, depicted through uni-modal functions relating task success rate to property estimation error (Figure 4), indicated that the method successfully identifies task-relevant properties. For the Striking task, the Center of Mass (CoM) in the y-direction and dynamic friction showed a sharper decline in success rate with increasing error, implying higher relevance to task performance compared to properties like static friction. This resulted in tighter estimation thresholds for these critical parameters in the exploration reward design, aligning with physical intuition. Similar distinct relationships reflecting varying levels of relevance were observed for the Edge Pushing and CartPole tasks.

#### 4.3. Contribution of Task-Informed Exploration Rewards and Uncertainty Estimates

- **Task-Informed Exploration Rewards:** An ablation study comparing task-informed rewards with task-agnostic rewards on the Striking task demonstrated the efficacy of the proposed reward design.
 - Using uniformly high estimation thresholds (task-agnostic Max.) resulted in 91.7% exploration success but only 47.3% task success, as it failed to incentivize accurate estimation of truly task-relevant properties.
 - Using uniformly low estimation thresholds (task-agnostic Min.) led to only 8.0% exploration success and 2.9% task success, due to the high difficulty of achieving uniformly high accuracy for all properties, resulting in sparse rewards.
 - In contrast, task-informed thresholds achieved 92.3% exploration success and 90.1% task success, highlighting the importance of adaptively setting thresholds based on property relevance.
- **Uncertainty Estimates:** The study showed that both estimation errors (RMSE) and predictive uncertainties decreased as exploration progressed, particularly for task-relevant properties like CoM in the y-direction and dynamic friction (Figure 5a, Appendix C.6). Furthermore, low uncertainty in task-relevant property estimates correlated with successful task completion during sim2sim transfers, while high uncertainty was associated with failures (Figure 5b, Appendix C.6). These findings support the use of uncertainty as a reliable proxy for estimation error, allowing for effective policy switching in physical setups.

#### 4.4. Physical Robot Performance

The approach was validated on a KUKA iiwa robot arm for the Striking and Edge Pushing tasks.
- **Striking Task:** For pucks with varying surface friction (ball bearings, nylon, aluminum), the online property estimator successfully identified distinct dynamic friction values (converging to 0.09, 0.12, and 0.15, respectively) as uncertainty decreased below the computed thresholds (Figure 6b, Figure 19). This led to 8 out of 9 successful runs across three trials per puck. However, for pucks with significantly shifted centers of mass, the exploration phase often failed due to inaccurate estimates and high uncertainty (Figure 20). This discrepancy was attributed to a sim-to-real mismatch in the dynamic model, where simulation treated puck-table contact as a point contact while real-world interaction involved full surface contact, altering friction distribution.
- **Edge Pushing Task:** When pushing a box with shifted CoM (eggs placed on left or right side), the robot's CoM estimates converged to the correct off-center locations (e.g., 5.8cm), with uncertainty dropping below the computed threshold (Figure 21). This resulted in 5 out of 5 successful runs for left-shifted CoM and 3 out of 5 for right-shifted CoM.

These results from physical robot experiments confirm the practical applicability of the learned exploration policy, online property estimator, and uncertainty-based policy switching for achieving one-shot task success in real-world scenarios, despite encountering some sim-to-real challenges related to fundamental model mismatches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in robot autonomy for interactive tasks requiring the identification of unknown physical properties. The proposed task-informed exploration approach offers several key contributions:

1. **Enhanced Autonomy in One-Shot Tasks:** By enabling robots to autonomously learn informative exploratory motions and infer task-relevant physical properties, the method significantly reduces the need for human intervention in complex, one-shot tasks where failure is irreversible. This capability is crucial for deploying robots in unstructured or dynamic environments where object properties cannot be known a priori.
2. **Efficient and Adaptive Exploration:** The introduction of automatically generated, task-informed exploration rewards guides the robot to prioritize the estimation of properties most critical for task success. Coupled with an uncertainty-based policy switching mechanism, the robot can adaptively determine the optimal exploration duration, ensuring sufficient accuracy while minimizing wasted time, thus improving operational efficiency.
3. **Real-Time Adaptability:** The simultaneous training of an online property estimator with the exploration policy allows for immediate task execution after exploration, eliminating the inefficiencies of traditional methods that require offline simulator queries or retraining at test time. This enables robots to adapt their task execution plans in real-time based on newly inferred object properties.
4. **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** By explicitly learning to explore and estimate object parameters, and utilizing uncertainty for decision-making, the framework offers a pathway for more robust sim-to-real transfer, specifically addressing parameter mismatches between simulated and physical environments.
5. **Generalizability:** The demonstration of the task-informed reward design on diverse tasks like Striking, Edge Pushing, and CartPole suggests the generalizability of the core principles to various manipulation and control problems involving unknown system dynamics.

The potential impact of this work is broad. It could lead to the development of more versatile and intelligent robotic systems capable of handling a wider array of objects and tasks in domains such as manufacturing, logistics, service robotics, and hazardous environment exploration. By reducing the reliance on meticulously modeled environments and pre-programmed behaviors, this research contributes to the vision of truly adaptive and autonomous robots that can learn and operate effectively in the complexities of the real world.
