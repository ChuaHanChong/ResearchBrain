# Beyond Viewpoint Generalization: What Multi-View Demonstrations Offer and How to Synthesize Them for Robot Manipulation?

> [!summary] Summary
> A controlled study of multi-view robot manipulation demonstrations, plus RoboNVS, a geometry-aware self-supervised pipeline that turns monocular demos into novel-view videos by aligning DepthCrafter and DA3 depth into a watertight mesh and completing occlusions with a LoRA-tuned Wan2.1 video diffusion model. Multi-view training lifts fixed-view success rates, and RoboNVS-augmented data raises real-robot success from 5% to 65% on the hardest task.

## Problem

- Prior work credits multi-view demonstrations only with cross-view robustness and camera invariance, leaving unmeasured whether viewpoint diversity improves manipulation capability under strictly fixed-viewpoint evaluation.
- Large-scale robot datasets are overwhelmingly monocular, so multi-view supervision stays scarce and adding synchronized real-world cameras is costly, labor-intensive, and hard to retrofit.
- Existing novel-view synthesis generates frames independently or without explicit 3D constraints, producing geometric distortion, temporal inconsistency, and hallucinated robot structures unusable as demonstrations.

## Method

- Controlled ==RoboTwin 2.0== protocol holds policy and training fixed while varying only viewpoint coverage along a 1.70 m, 45-degree elevation arc, testing ==Diffusion Policy==, ==ACT==, ==DP3==, and ==Pi0==.
- **RoboNVS** depth alignment fuses ==DepthCrafter== relative depth with ==DA3== metric depth by a global ==scale-shift== fit in ==inverse-depth space==, then builds a ==DW-Mesh== rendered along target camera trajectories.
- A ==Wan2.1-14B DiT== video model with ==LoRA== inpaints rendered occlusion masks under a ==flow-matching== objective; ==Bi-directional Masking== trains on complementary masks to diversify occlusion patterns.

## Results

- Multi-view supervision raised the Pi0 VLA average by **+7.2** on seen and **+7.3** on unseen tasks, with the largest gains under randomized **11k**-texture backgrounds.
- Success is non-monotonic in view spread: mean success peaked at **49.7%** for plus-minus **40**-degree offsets versus **14.3%** single-view, falling to **25.7%** at **60** degrees.
- RoboNVS beat EX-4D on PSNR **14.07** vs **12.72** and FVD **31.40** vs **42.66**, lifting real Franka success to **70%**, **60%**, and **65%** across three tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Viewpoint diversity is not just robustness insurance; it reshapes the encoder, shifting gradient attribution from background onto the end-effector and manipulated objects.
> - Adding synchronized cameras extends scaling past single-view saturation at near-zero collection cost, making viewpoint coverage a cheaper data axis than new scenes.
> - More views is not better: moderate offsets add geometric cues cheaply, while extreme viewpoints inject distribution shift that degrades fixed-view performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robot manipulation policies are commonly trained using demonstrations collected under controlled camera configurations. However, a significant challenge in real-world deployment is the distribution shift that occurs when camera viewpoints during inference differ from those used during training. This shift can substantially degrade policy performance and generalization ability.

Existing research often addresses this issue by incorporating multi-view demonstrations during training. The prevalent understanding is that multi-view data primarily improves cross-view robustness and invariance, allowing policies to handle varying camera perspectives by reducing their reliance on view-specific appearance cues. This perspective suggests that the benefit of multi-view supervision would diminish or disappear when policies are evaluated under the same viewpoint used for training.

This paper positions itself by investigating a less explored aspect of multi-view learning: whether viewpoint diversity can enhance the fundamental manipulation capabilities of learned policies, even when evaluated strictly from the original training viewpoint. This inquiry extends beyond the established understanding of multi-view data solely as a means to improve viewpoint robustness.

Furthermore, the work addresses the practical limitation of scarcity in large-scale multi-view robotic datasets and the logistical difficulties of collecting synchronized multi-view data in real-world settings. This gap in data availability limits the widespread application of multi-view learning. The paper contributes to the broader landscape of visual augmentation for imitation learning by proposing a geometry-aware novel-view synthesis framework, RoboNVS, which aims to generate physically consistent multi-view data from monocular inputs. This approach offers a method to scale viewpoint diversity without the significant costs associated with extensive real-world multi-camera data collection, thereby impacting data collection strategies for robot learning. The framework leverages advancements in video diffusion models and monocular depth estimation, adapting them for robotic manipulation scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to conduct a systematic empirical study to determine if multi-view demonstrations fundamentally enhance the manipulation capability of learned policies, even when evaluation is restricted to the original training viewpoint, beyond merely improving robustness to camera shifts.

This primary objective is motivated by several considerations:

- **Re-evaluating the role of multi-view data:** Most existing explanations attribute the value of multi-view demonstrations primarily to improved cross-view robustness. The authors question whether this is the only, or even the primary, benefit. Understanding if multi-view supervision provides intrinsic performance gains for a fixed evaluation viewpoint could alter how multi-view data is perceived and utilized in robot learning.
- **Guiding data collection strategies:** Improving policy performance typically involves collecting demonstrations across diverse backgrounds, object configurations, and scene variations, which is resource-intensive. In contrast, capturing synchronized additional viewpoints of an existing demonstration may require less incremental effort if a multi-camera setup is already present. If multi-view supervision can improve fixed-view manipulation capability, it could present a more efficient and scalable strategy for expanding robot learning datasets compared to collecting entirely new scenes.
- **Addressing data scarcity and collection challenges:** Despite the potential benefits, large-scale multi-view demonstrations are scarce in existing robotic datasets. Collecting additional viewpoints in real-world settings is often difficult. These practical limitations motivate the development of methods to generate multi-view data synthetically.

The secondary objective is to propose and develop a framework, RoboNVS, for synthesizing novel-view videos from monocular inputs. This objective is motivated by:

- **Overcoming data limitations:** The scarcity of large-scale multi-view datasets hinders the broader application of multi-view learning benefits.
- **Practical feasibility:** The difficulty and cost associated with collecting additional synchronized viewpoints in real-world environments necessitate a synthetic generation approach. RoboNVS aims to provide a geometry-aware, self-supervised method to synthesize physically consistent and diverse viewpoints, thereby augmenting imitation learning datasets and scaling viewpoint diversity for robot manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a two-part approach: an empirical study to understand multi-view gains in robot learning and the development of a novel-view synthesis framework, RoboNVS.

**Part 1: Understanding Multi-View Gains in Robot Learning**

- **Experimental Setup:**
 - **Camera Configuration:** Cameras were positioned on the upper hemisphere, maintaining a fixed distance of 1.70 m and an elevation of 45°. Azimuth angles were sampled every 10° within ±60°.
 - **Demonstrations:** Experiments typically used 50 demonstrations per viewpoint. Single-view training utilized demonstrations from a canonical 0° camera, while multi-view training augmented this with synchronized viewpoints from additional azimuth angles.
 - **Environment Modes:** Two settings were used: "Clean" (uniform white background) and "Random" (tabletop textures sampled from an 11k-texture pool per rollout, introducing appearance variation).
 - **Policy Architectures:** Two paradigms were evaluated: Diffusion Policy (using ResNet-18 visual backbone, three-frame observation window, Franka robot) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) (fine-tuned Pi0 model with LoRA, Aloha robot).
 - **Platform and Tasks:** Experiments were conducted in Robotwin2.0, involving 12 manipulation tasks. Success rates were reported over 50 evaluation rollouts.
 - **Evaluation Protocol:** All policies were evaluated exclusively using a single canonical 0° camera to isolate the effect of multi-view supervision during training, rather than additional viewpoints at test time.

- **Specific Investigations:**
 - **When Multi-View Helps:** Compared single-view (0° only) and multi-view (0° ±10°) training under "Clean" and "Random" environment modes.
 - **Transferability:** Assessed multi-view benefits on a multi-task VLA model for both seen and unseen tasks.
 - **Scaling Behavior:**
 - *View Expansion:* Progressively expanded the view set symmetrically (e.g., 0°, 0° ±10°..., 0° ±60°) for both Diffusion Policy and VLA.
 - *Viewpoint Magnitude:* Isolated the effect of viewpoint offset by training with {0°, +k°, -k°} for k ∈ {10, 20..., 60}.
 - *Demonstrations per View:* Increased demonstrations per added view from 10 to 50, with the optimal viewpoint configuration fixed for each task.
 - *Overall Data Budget:* Compared scaling laws by increasing single-view demonstrations (10 to 640) against multi-view training (0° + best-performing view, same number of demonstrations per view).
- **Dissecting Mechanisms:**
 - **Representation Quality:** Used Grad-CAM and input gradients to analyze spatial sensitivity, and linear probing (R² for end-effector/object pose prediction) to evaluate linear decodability from frozen visual features.
 - **Policy Head Robustness:** Froze a pretrained DINOv2 encoder and trained only the policy head, comparing test MSE under single-view vs. multi-view supervision.
 - **Optimization Dynamics:** Analyzed training trajectories (success rates over training steps) for multi-task VLA models to observe stability and convergence.

**Part 2: Multi-View Augmentation via Novel View Synthesis (RoboNVS)**

- **Problem Formulation:** The goal is to synthesize novel-view video sequences {ˆI_t} corresponding to a target camera trajectory {G_t} from a monocular input video {I_t}, maintaining scene geometry and temporal consistency.
- **Method Overview:** RoboNVS is a geometry-aware self-supervised framework. It first reconstructs 3D scene geometry from monocular depth estimation, creates a watertight mesh, and renders partial RGB frames and occlusion masks under target camera trajectories. These geometric priors then condition a diffusion-based video model to complete missing regions.
- **Depth Alignment:**
 - Combines DepthCrafter (temporally consistent relative depth) and DA3 (metric depth, camera pose).
 - Aligns DepthCrafter's relative depth with DA3's metric depth using an optimal global scale-shift transformation in inverse depth space.
 - Aligned depths are converted into a simplified DW-Mesh for geometric priors.
- **Diffusion-based Video Completion:**
 - A video diffusion model (Wan2.1-14B, DiT-based) is fine-tuned with LoRA.
 - Input: Masked video (from mesh rendering), occlusion mask sequence, and the first frame of the input video as a reference.
 - Optimization: Uses a flow-matching objective to predict the velocity field, enabling reconstruction of missing regions while preserving geometric structure.
- **Bi-directional Masking Strategy:**
 - To address the mismatch between typical inpainting masks (foreground) and novel-view occlusion masks (peripheral regions), the model is trained with both the original mask (M_t) and its complementary mask (¯M_t = 1 - M_t). This strategy aims to increase mask diversity during training and improve robustness to unseen occlusion patterns.
- **Inference:** The geometric pipeline is applied to a monocular video to obtain rendered frames and occlusion masks for the target viewpoint. These, along with the source reference frame, are fed into the diffusion model for completion.
- **Implementation Details:** RoboNVS was trained on an aggregated dataset of approximately 10,000 video sequences from Open X-Embodiment (OXE) subsets (Droid, RT-1, BridgeData V2) and other datasets (RH20T, Robotwin, RoboCoin). Fine-tuning used AdamW optimizer, batch size of 1 per GPU (total 8), for approximately one week.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID, FVD, and VBench were used for synthesized video quality.
- **Baselines:** Compared against one-stage end-to-end models (ReCamMaster, ZeroNVS-ft) and two-stage inpainting methods (EX-4D, TrajectoryCrafter, CogNVS), along with an ablation of RoboNVS without bi-directional masking.

**Part 3: Real-World Evaluation**

- **Experimental Setup:** A 7-DoF Franka Panda robot with a top-down RealSense D435 camera was used for three manipulation tasks (Bell Pushing, Fruit Pick-and-Place, Lego Pick-and-Place).
- **Data:** 30 expert trajectories from the base 0° monocular viewpoint.
- **Evaluation:** Policies were trained on augmented datasets and evaluated under the base 0° view, with object positions randomly perturbed, over 20 trials.
- **Comparison:** Baseline (monocular only), EX-4D, EX-4D with Depth Alignment (EX-4D w/ DA), and RoboNVS. All models generated four synthetic views (±10°, ±20°).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the impact of multi-view demonstrations and the performance of the proposed RoboNVS framework.

**Findings from Multi-View Gains Study:**

- **Fixed-View Performance Improvement:** Multi-view demonstrations consistently improved manipulation success rates even when policies were evaluated from the exact same viewpoint (0° camera) used during training. This effect was more pronounced in environments with randomized backgrounds, indicating benefits beyond mere viewpoint mismatch.
- **Transferability to VLA Models and Multi-Task Settings:** The benefits of multi-view supervision extended to large pretrained VLA models and multi-task learning pipelines, showing improved average success rates on both seen (+7.2%) and unseen tasks (+7.3%).
- **Non-Monotonic Scaling with View Coverage:** Performance did not improve monotonically with increasing viewpoint diversity. Moderate viewpoint diversity, typically achieved with 4–8 additional views or azimuth offsets between ±10° and ±40°, yielded the most significant improvements. Excessively large viewpoint variations (e.g., ±50°–±60°) sometimes led to performance degradation.
- **Scaling with Demonstrations:** With an optimal viewpoint configuration, increasing the number of demonstrations per added view from 10 to 50 consistently improved performance, suggesting that multi-view supervision benefits from additional data without early saturation.
- **Breaking Single-View Saturation:** Multi-view training continued to provide measurable gains as the overall dataset size increased, even in regimes where single-view training performance plateaued. This indicates that multi-view data can raise performance ceilings after single-view data saturation.

**Mechanistic Insights:**

- **Manipulation-Relevant Visual Representations:** Grad-CAM and input gradient analysis showed that multi-view supervision encouraged visual encoders to concentrate attention on manipulation-relevant regions (end-effector, manipulated objects) and reduced dependence on background appearance. Linear probing revealed lower R² values for pose decoding from multi-view features, suggesting that gains might stem from richer non-linear representations rather than linearly decodable pose information.
- **Improved Policy Head Robustness:** When the visual encoder was frozen, multi-view supervision consistently reduced the test MSE of the policy head, especially under random backgrounds. This indicates that multi-view training improved the robustness of the mapping from visual features to actions.
- **Enhanced Optimization Dynamics:** Multi-view supervision led to smoother training trajectories, more stable improvements, and higher final performance compared to single-view training, suggesting improved optimization stability and sample efficiency.

**Findings from RoboNVS Evaluation:**

- **Quantitative Visual Quality (Simulation):** RoboNVS achieved higher performance across standard visual quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID, FVD) and perceptual quality (VBench) compared to existing generative baselines. This indicated generation of high-fidelity, realistic, and temporally coherent novel-view videos.
- **Qualitative Visual Quality (Simulation):** RoboNVS produced more geometrically consistent and temporally stable novel-view videos, mitigating artifacts such as object duplication, structural distortion, and blurred textures observed in other generative methods. The bi-directional masking strategy contributed to reducing ghosting and structural artifacts.
- **Real-World Policy Performance:** Policies trained on data augmented by RoboNVS consistently outperformed those trained with monocular data alone or data augmented by other generative baselines (EX-4D, EX-4D w/ DA) on real-world Franka Panda tasks. For example, on the Lego Pick-and-Place task, RoboNVS-augmented training resulted in a 65% success rate, compared to 30% for EX-4D and 5% for the monocular baseline. This suggests that the synthetic multi-view data provided by RoboNVS supplied effective geometric and spatial priors for downstream policy learning in real environments.
- **Qualitative Real-World Synthesis:** RoboNVS demonstrated improved geometric plausibility in target-view mask generation and more complete inpainting of masked regions, including robotic arm structures and end-effectors, enhancing real-world visual fidelity and temporal stability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers several points of significance and potential impact in the field of robot learning:

- **Refined Understanding of Multi-View Data:** The study systematically challenges the prevailing assumption that multi-view demonstrations primarily enhance cross-view robustness. By demonstrating that multi-view data can directly improve manipulation capabilities even under fixed-view evaluation, the work provides a more comprehensive understanding of its value. This finding suggests that multi-view supervision is not merely a domain randomization technique but a method for enriching learned representations and improving intrinsic policy performance.
- **Enhanced Data Collection Strategies:** The observed benefits of multi-view data, particularly its ability to scale performance beyond single-view saturation and the non-monotonic nature of view coverage, provide empirical guidance for efficient data collection. Capturing synchronized additional viewpoints, when a multi-camera setup is available, can be a more cost-effective and scalable strategy for expanding robot learning datasets than collecting entirely new scenes with varying object configurations and backgrounds.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity through Synthesis:** The development of RoboNVS directly addresses a practical limitation in robot learning: the scarcity of large-scale multi-view demonstrations and the difficulty of collecting them in real-world settings. By providing a geometry-aware framework to synthesize high-fidelity, physically consistent novel-view videos from monocular inputs, RoboNVS enables researchers to augment existing datasets, making the benefits of multi-view learning more accessible.
- **Improved Policy Generalization and Robustness:** The study's findings collectively indicate that multi-view supervision, whether real or synthetically generated by RoboNVS, leads to policies with enhanced manipulation performance, improved generalization to diverse visual conditions (including varying backgrounds), and greater robustness. This has implications for deploying robots in less controlled and more dynamic real-world environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** RoboNVS provides a tool that can be integrated into existing robot learning pipelines. Future work can build upon this by further improving generation fidelity, exploring its combination with other data augmentation strategies, and contributing to the development of richer and more scalable robotic manipulation datasets. The mechanistic analysis also opens avenues for deeper investigation into how multi-view learning influences representation learning and policy optimization.
