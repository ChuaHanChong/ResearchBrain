# Visual-Policy Learning through Multi-Camera View to Single-Camera View Knowledge Distillation for Robot Manipulation Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> A knowledge distillation framework enables a single-camera robot policy to acquire robust generalization capabilities from a multi-camera teacher, further enhanced by aggressive viewpoint randomization during student training. This approach allows a single-camera system to perform complex manipulation tasks with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and improved robustness to camera perturbations compared to multi-camera systems.

## Problem

- Multi-camera systems provide robust generalization for robot manipulation but introduce high hardware costs and deployment complexity.
- Single-camera policies, despite their practicality, inherently lack generalization to varied viewpoints and unseen visual configurations.
- Data augmentation and randomization alone are insufficient to achieve broad viewpoint robustness for single-view policies, often failing on complex tasks.

## Method

- The approach employs a knowledge distillation framework to transfer a multi-camera teacher policy's generalized capabilities to a single-camera student policy.
- The student policy is trained with extensive camera viewpoint randomization, including extreme changes, and data augmentation, alongside a curriculum learning strategy.
- Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) is utilized as the underlying ==reinforcement learning== algorithm, with a focus on minimizing MSE between student and teacher actions and features.

## Results

- Student policies, distilled from multi-camera teachers, successfully performed complex tasks like asymmetrical mug grasping in simulation, which single-camera teachers completely failed to learn.
- Student policies trained with randomization achieved **96.0%** success for cube and **81.7%** for mug under random camera views in simulation, outperforming their multi-camera teachers (**68.7%** and **41.3%** respectively).
- Policies demonstrated successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a real robot, achieving up to **80%** success for cube and **70%** for mug across diverse real-world camera views, with further improvements when incorporating robot joint states.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Knowledge distillation from a multi-camera teacher is critical for a single-camera policy to learn under aggressive camera randomization; without it, training under such conditions fails completely.
> - The combination of multi-view knowledge distillation and aggressive student-side randomization results in single-view policies that exhibit superior robustness to camera perturbations compared to the original multi-view teacher policies.
> - Direct Euclidean distance minimization for feature alignment between teacher and student encoders effectively transfers high-level visual features for robust representation learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper is situated at the intersection of deep reinforcement learning (DRL), computer vision, and robotic manipulation. In recent years, vision-based DRL has made significant strides in enabling robots to learn complex control policies directly from high-dimensional visual inputs. However, a persistent challenge in this field is the **generalization capability** of learned policies to new and diverse real-world scenarios, particularly concerning variations in viewpoints and camera configurations.

Previous research has explored several avenues to tackle this generalization problem:
1. **Data Randomization and Augmentation:** Methods like changing background colors, textures, or applying pixel-level augmentations (random shifts, crops, color jittering, Gaussian noise) have been used to create diverse training data and learn robust representations-. However, these techniques often fall short in addressing more fundamental changes, such as significant variations in camera viewpoint or parameters, as they are typically applied to data from a fixed perspective.
2. **Multi-View Training:** To explicitly enhance viewpoint robustness, researchers have trained policies using multiple camera views simultaneously. This often involves combining third-person (broad, fixed perspective) and first-person or egocentric (closer, end-effector mounted) cameras-. While successful in improving generalization and performance, deploying multiple cameras in real-world scenarios introduces significant practical challenges, including increased hardware costs, design constraints, and the complexity of coordinating and synchronizing multiple camera streams. This trade-off between performance/generalization and real-world deployment complexity forms a core motivation for the presented work.
3. **Knowledge Distillation (KD):** Originating from supervised learning, KD has been widely adopted in RL to transfer knowledge from a larger, more accurate "teacher" model to a smaller, more efficient "student" model. A particularly relevant application of KD in robotics is **privileged information transfer**, where a teacher policy trained with privileged (e.g., full state, simplified simulation) information guides a student policy that only has access to real-world sensory data-. This paradigm has been successful in sim-to-real transfer and learning robust locomotion or manipulation skills. Another related approach uses strong image augmentations in conjunction with KD for zero-shot generalization of visual policies, where an expert policy trained with weak augmentations distills knowledge to a student trained with strong augmentations.

This paper advances the state-of-the-art by specifically addressing the multi-view vs. single-view dilemma within the context of KD. Instead of using privileged *state* information or simply stronger *augmentations*, it distills knowledge from a *multi-camera view teacher* (which enjoys superior generalization due to rich visual input) to a *single-camera view student*. Crucially, the student is trained with aggressive camera viewpoint randomization and data augmentation, a combination that has been shown to be ineffective without the guidance of a multi-view teacher. By doing so, the authors aim to combine the generalization benefits of multi-view systems with the practical simplicity of single-camera deployment, thereby bridging a significant gap in robust, vision-based robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are driven by a critical challenge in vision-based robot manipulation: achieving robust generalization across varying visual configurations while maintaining practical deployability in real-world environments.

**Motivation:**
1. **Trade-off between Performance and Practicality:** While using multiple camera views (e.g., egocentric and multiple third-person cameras) significantly enhances the generalization capabilities and performance of visual policies, particularly for complex manipulation tasks, it introduces substantial practical complexities in real-world deployments. These include the need for additional hardware components, intricate design constraints, and the technical challenges of coordinating and synchronizing multiple camera streams.
2. **Limitations of Single-Camera Policies:** Conversely, single-camera policies, despite their hardware simplicity, inherently struggle with generalization to unseen visual configurations, especially when the camera's viewpoint or parameters deviate from the training setup. Existing data augmentation and randomization methods, while beneficial, are often insufficient to endow single-view policies with the broad viewpoint robustness required for diverse real-world scenarios. The paper explicitly notes that significant camera randomization alone, without the guidance of knowledge distillation, fails to teach a single-view policy the necessary skills.
3. **Need for Robustness to Camera Perturbations:** In practical applications, the exact camera placement might vary, or the robot might operate in environments where dynamic changes to viewpoints are unavoidable. A robust policy must be able to consistently locate and interact with objects irrespective of these camera location perturbations.

**Key Objectives:**
1. **Develop a novel approach** to enable a single-camera visual policy to achieve the robust generalization performance typically associated with multi-camera systems.
2. **Leverage knowledge distillation** to transfer rich, multi-view information from a "teacher" policy (trained with multiple cameras in simulation) to a "student" policy that operates solely on a single camera viewpoint.
3. **Enhance student policy robustness** against camera location perturbations and unseen visual configurations by training it with aggressive domain randomization, including extreme viewpoint changes, and data augmentation techniques.
4. **Validate the efficacy and efficiency** of the proposed method in both simulation and real-world environments, demonstrating its ability to perform challenging manipulation tasks (e.g., grasping an asymmetrical object like a mug) that a single-camera policy alone cannot accomplish.
5. **Showcase zero-shot transfer capability** to real-world scenarios, implying that policies learned in simulation can be directly deployed without further fine-tuning, thus increasing practicality and reducing deployment effort.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on a novel knowledge distillation framework that transfers the generalized capabilities of a multi-camera view teacher policy to a robust single-camera view student policy. The approach leverages the advantages of multi-view observations during training while ensuring practical single-view deployment.

**A. Problem Formulation (POMDP and SAC)**
The environment is treated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) where the agent perceives the state through camera observations. The action space is continuous, encompassing 3-DoF end-effector position (X, Y, Z), 1-DoF yaw rotation, and 1-DoF gripper control. The authors utilize Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), a state-of-the-art off-policy maximum entropy DRL algorithm, known for its good performance in sparse reward setups and sample efficiency. SAC maximizes an objective combining expected return with an entropy term to encourage exploration.

**B. Overall Framework: Multi-View to Single-View Knowledge Distillation**
The core idea is to train a teacher policy (π_t) with multiple camera views and then distill its "knowledge" to a student policy (π_s) that only uses a single camera.

1. **Teacher Policy (π_t) Training:**
 - **Observation Space:** The teacher policy receives observations (o_t) from a rich set of cameras: a single egocentric camera (I_e) mounted on the robot's end-effector, and N stationary third-person view cameras ([I_θ1_t..., I_θN_t]). The specific setups explored are a two-camera teacher (front + egocentric) and a three-camera teacher (front + egocentric + side, minimizing redundancy).
 - **Network Architecture:** Both teacher and student share the same network architecture, comprising a 12-layer convolutional encoder (f_ϕt for teacher, f_ϕs for student) with a single-layer self-attention module, followed by a SAC policy.
 - **Reward Scheme:** A delayed reward scheme is used, where the agent receives a +100 reward at each time step after successfully completing the task (grasping and lifting an object), accumulating until the episode ends, thus incentivizing fast task completion.
 - **Initial Training Aid:** To accelerate teacher training, 50 human demonstrations (5,000 transitions) collected via a 3D mouse are added to the replay buffer. These demonstrations are *only* used for teacher training.
 - **Training Process:** The teacher is initially trained from fixed viewpoints. Only the critic's loss is used to update the weights of the encoder network for learning representations.

2. **Student Policy (π_s) Training (Knowledge Distillation):**
 - **Observation Space:** The student policy (π_s) operates with observations (o_s) from a *single* third-person camera (I_θk_t), specifically the front-facing camera in the experiments.
 - **Knowledge Distillation Loss (L_KD):** The student policy learns by imitating the teacher's actions. The primary distillation objective is to minimize the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the student's actions and the teacher's actions: L_KD = ||π_s(o_s) - π_t(o_t)||.
 - **Feature Distillation (Encoder Alignment):** To ensure the student learns robust visual features, its encoder (f_ϕs) is guided by the teacher's features. Two methods were investigated:
 - **Euclidean Distance (L_MSE):** Minimizing the Euclidean distance between the teacher's encoded features (o_t) and the student's encoded features (o_s): L_MSE = ||o_t - o_s||^2. This method was found to be superior.
 - **Pairwise Similarity Matrix (L_sim):** Aligning the pairwise similarity matrix of teacher observations with that of student observations, using cosine distance.
 - **Robustness Enhancement through Randomization:** This is a crucial element differentiating the student's training. To make the student robust to unseen visual configurations and camera perturbations:
 - **Camera Randomization:** The camera angle (θ_k) and parameters (field of view, aspect ratio) for the student's single view are constantly randomized, sampled from a uniform distribution. PyBullet's synthetic camera capabilities allow for arbitrary camera positions and parameters.
 - **Data Augmentation:** Standard data augmentation techniques are applied: pixel shifting (randomly in two dimensions) and color augmentation (changing brightness, contrast, saturation).
 - **Curriculum Learning:** A simple curriculum training approach is employed. Initially, the randomized camera parameter range is small, similar to the teacher's fixed viewpoint. As training progresses (every 50 episodes), this range is incrementally widened up to a maximum to gradually increase task difficulty and foster greater robustness.

**C. Experimental Setup:**
- **Simulation Environment:** PyBullet with a 7-DoF Franka Panda Emika manipulator.
- **Real-World Environment:** Panda robot with Panda Hand gripper and an Intel RealSense D435i RGBD camera, controlled via Polymetis.
- **Observations:** Both teacher and student policies use 84x84 RGB camera images.
- **Tasks:** Grasping and lifting a cube (symmetrical, easier) and a mug (asymmetrical, more challenging) to a predetermined height. Objects are spawned at random positions on the table for each episode.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides compelling evidence for the effectiveness of the proposed multi-camera view to single-camera view knowledge distillation approach, both in simulation and real-world scenarios.

**A. Simulation Experiments:**

1. **Teacher Performance:**
 - For the **cube lifting task**, all teacher policies (single-camera π_t1cam, two-camera π_t2cam, three-camera π_t3cam) successfully learned the task. However, the multi-camera teachers (π_t2cam, π_t3cam) consistently achieved higher average returns and success rates than the single-camera teacher (π_t1cam).
 - For the more challenging **mug lifting task** (due to its asymmetry), the single-camera teacher (π_t1cam) *failed entirely*, demonstrating its inability to learn effective grasping strategies from a limited viewpoint. In stark contrast, both multi-camera teachers (π_t2cam, π_t3cam) successfully learned to lift the mug. This highlights the critical advantage of multi-view information for complex manipulation.

2. **Student Performance via Knowledge Distillation:**
 - Students distilled from multi-camera teachers (π_s_t2cam, π_s_t3cam) significantly *outperformed* the student distilled from the single-camera teacher (π_s_t1cam) in both cube and mug lifting tasks. Crucially, for the mug task, π_s_t3cam and π_s_t2cam learned to lift the mug effectively, whereas π_s_t1cam (like its teacher) failed. This confirms that knowledge distillation can imbue a single-view policy with capabilities that its single-view counterpart cannot acquire on its own.
 - **Robustness to Random Camera Views:** In a key finding, the student policies trained with extensive camera randomization (π_s_t2cam, π_s_t3cam) demonstrated *superior performance* compared to their respective teacher policies (π_t2cam, π_t3cam) and a cross-view attention baseline when evaluated under *random camera view settings*. For instance, π_s_t3cam achieved 96.0% success for cube and 81.7% for mug in random angle settings, while its teacher π_t3cam only reached 68.7% and 41.3% respectively. This indicates that the combination of multi-view knowledge distillation and aggressive student-side randomization leads to exceptionally robust single-view policies.
 - **Importance of KD:** A critical ablation showed that a single-view agent trained with *only* camera randomization (without knowledge distillation) resulted in a **0% success rate** for both tasks, unequivocally proving that the teacher's guidance is indispensable for learning under such extensive visual perturbations.

3. **Ablation Study on Feature Alignment:**
 - Comparing two feature distillation methods, minimizing the Euclidean distance between teacher and student features (L_MSE) yielded superior student performance compared to aligning pairwise similarity matrices (L_sim). This suggests that direct feature alignment is more effective for transferring high-level visual features in this framework.

**B. Real Robot Experiments (Zero-Shot Transfer):**

1. **Direct Transfer Success:** The policies trained in simulation were directly transferred to a real Panda robot without any further training or fine-tuning, demonstrating successful **zero-shot sim-to-real transfer**.
2. **Comparative Performance:**
 - For the **cube lifting task**, the student policy π_s_t3cam consistently achieved higher success rates (ranging from 50% to 80% across different random views) compared to π_s_t1cam (20% to 60%).
 - For the **mug lifting task**, π_s_t3cam demonstrated success rates between 50% and 70% across various random camera views. Crucially, as the single-camera teacher failed in simulation, there was no comparable π_s_t1cam policy for the mug task in the real world, reinforcing the notion that this method enables tasks previously intractable for single-view systems.
 - **Effect of Robot State:** Augmenting visual features with robot joint states (π_s_t3cam+state) further improved real-world success rates for the mug task (ranging from 65% to 80%), indicating that combining visual and proprioceptive information can enhance zero-shot transfer performance.
3. **Sim-to-Real Gap:** The average success rates in the real world were generally lower than in simulation, a common observation attributed to the inherent domain gap between synthetic and physical environments. However, the consistent success across diverse camera viewpoints in the real world (as demonstrated in the accompanying video) still validates the policy's robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a highly significant contribution to the field of vision-based robotic manipulation, particularly by addressing the practical challenges of deploying advanced learning algorithms in real-world settings.

1. **Bridging the Generalization-Practicality Gap:** The most prominent impact is the successful reconciliation of the trade-off between the high generalization capabilities offered by multi-camera systems and the hardware simplicity and efficiency of single-camera setups. This approach makes robust vision-based policies more accessible and deployable in scenarios where multiple cameras are impractical due to cost, complexity, or physical constraints.
2. **Enhanced Robustness to Viewpoint Variation:** The work convincingly demonstrates that a single-camera policy, when guided by a multi-view teacher and trained with aggressive camera randomization, can achieve *superior* robustness to viewpoint changes compared to even its multi-camera teacher. This is a crucial advancement, as it implies that the distillation process combined with targeted student training can unlock a level of environmental adaptability not easily attainable otherwise.
3. **Enabling Complex Tasks for Simpler Systems:** The ability of the single-camera student policy to successfully perform challenging tasks like grasping an asymmetrical mug – a task that a single-camera teacher completely failed to learn – highlights the method's potential to significantly expand the operational capabilities of robots equipped with minimal visual sensing hardware.
4. **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful direct transfer of policies from simulation to a real robot without further training is a powerful testament to the learned policies' robustness and generalization. This feature dramatically reduces the effort and time required for real-world deployment, making robot learning more practical for various applications.
5. **Reduced Hardware and Calibration Overhead:** By achieving high performance with a single camera, the proposed method alleviates the need for complex multi-camera calibration and synchronization, simplifying robot system design and reducing overall operational costs.
6. **Advancement in Knowledge Distillation for RL:** The research provides valuable insights into the effective application of knowledge distillation, particularly the finding that direct Euclidean distance minimization between teacher and student features is more effective for high-level visual feature transfer in this context. It reinforces KD as a powerful tool for transferring complex, generalized skills in RL.
7. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper opens several avenues for future work, such as investigating the integration of point-cloud and depth information to further enhance zero-shot sim-to-real transfer performance, or exploring more sophisticated automatic curriculum learning methods for dynamic adjustment of camera parameter ranges.

In essence, this research significantly contributes to making advanced robot manipulation skills learned via reinforcement learning more practical, robust, and deployable outside controlled laboratory environments, paving the way for wider adoption of intelligent robotic systems in diverse real-world applications.
