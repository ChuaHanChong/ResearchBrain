# Visual-Policy Learning through Multi-Camera View to Single-Camera View Knowledge Distillation for Robot Manipulation Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> A knowledge distillation framework enables a single-camera robot policy to acquire robust generalization capabilities from a multi-camera teacher, further enhanced by aggressive viewpoint randomization during student training. This approach allows a single-camera system to perform complex manipulation tasks with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and improved robustness to camera perturbations compared to multi-camera systems.

## Problem

- Multi-camera systems provide robust generalization for robot manipulation but introduce high hardware costs and deployment complexity.
- Single-camera policies, despite their practicality, inherently lack generalization to varied viewpoints and unseen visual configurations.
- Data augmentation and randomization alone are insufficient to achieve broad viewpoint robustness for single-view policies, often failing on complex tasks.

## Method

- The approach employs a knowledge distillation framework to transfer a multi-camera teacher policy's generalized capabilities to a single-camera student policy.
- The student policy is trained with extensive camera viewpoint randomization, including extreme changes, and data augmentation, alongside a curriculum learning strategy.
- Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) is utilized as the underlying ==reinforcement learning== algorithm, with a focus on minimizing MSE between student and teacher actions and features.

## Results

- Student policies, distilled from multi-camera teachers, successfully performed complex tasks like asymmetrical mug grasping in simulation, which single-camera teachers completely failed to learn.
- Student policies trained with randomization achieved **96.0%** success for cube and **81.7%** for mug under random camera views in simulation, outperforming their multi-camera teachers (**68.7%** and **41.3%** respectively).
- Policies demonstrated successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a real robot, achieving up to **80%** success for cube and **70%** for mug across diverse real-world camera views, with further improvements when incorporating robot joint states.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Knowledge distillation from a multi-camera teacher is critical for a single-camera policy to learn under aggressive camera randomization; without it, training under such conditions fails completely.
> - The combination of multi-view knowledge distillation and aggressive student-side randomization results in single-view policies that exhibit superior robustness to camera perturbations compared to the original multi-view teacher policies.
> - Direct Euclidean distance minimization for feature alignment between teacher and student encoders effectively transfers high-level visual features for robust representation learning.
