# LEAP Hand: Low-Cost, Efficient, and Anthropomorphic Hand for Robot Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A new anthropomorphic robot hand, LEAP Hand, offers superior dexterity, robustness, and performance for robot learning tasks at a cost of 2000 USD, significantly lower than commercial alternatives. This open-source platform, developed at Carnegie Mellon University, aims to democratize access to hardware for dexterous manipulation research by addressing limitations in cost, repairability, and anthropomorphic design of existing systems.

## Problem

- Existing dexterous robot hands are either prohibitively expensive (e.g., Shadow Hand at >$100,000 USD), have limited dexterity, or are unreliable and difficult to repair (e.g., Allegro Hand).
- A lack of suitable, accessible, and robust anthropomorphic hardware limits real-world robot learning for complex dexterous manipulation tasks, keeping much of the research confined to simulation.
- The absence of a standardized, low-cost platform hinders reproducibility, community collaboration, and the practical application of advanced robot learning algorithms in dexterous manipulation.

## Method

- Developed **LEAP Hand**, a low-cost, ==direct-driven== robot hand featuring a novel ==Universal Abduction-Adduction Mechanism== that allows each finger to maintain four degrees of freedom across its full range of motion.
- Utilized a ==modular design== leveraging off-the-shelf components and commodity 3D-printed parts, enabling rapid assembly, low manufacturing cost, and easy in-house repair.
- Provided comprehensive open-source documentation, software APIs (ROS/Python), and high-fidelity simulation models (Pybullet, ==Isaac Gym==) to facilitate ==sim-to-real transfer== and broader community adoption.

## Results

- The LEAP Hand can be assembled in under **4 hours** at a cost of **2000 USD**, which is **1/8th** the cost of the Allegro Hand and **1/50th** that of the ShadowHand.
- It demonstrated superior dexterity with the largest thumb-to-finger opposability volume and consistently larger manipulability ellipsoid volumes across various finger configurations compared to other hands.
- The hand exhibited high robustness, holding a **2kg** weight on a single fingertip for an hour and maintaining low joint angle error (**0.025 radians**) over an hour of continuous grasping, outperforming the Allegro Hand's durability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A novel kinematic design, the Universal Abduction-Adduction Mechanism, enables full anthropomorphic dexterity in direct-driven robot hands, maintaining all degrees of freedom regardless of finger flexion or extension.
> - High-performance, anthropomorphic dexterous robot hardware can be developed and manufactured at a significantly lower cost (2000 USD) and with easier repairability than current commercial solutions.
> - Providing open-source hardware designs, robust simulation models, and APIs is essential for democratizing access and accelerating progress in real-world dexterous manipulation research.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous manipulation, the ability of a robot hand to precisely manipulate objects, has long been considered one of the grand challenges in robotics. While significant strides have been made in other areas, such as bipedal and quadrupedal locomotion or simple two-finger grasping, advanced in-hand manipulation has largely remained confined to simulation environments. This disparity stems primarily from the lack of suitable, accessible, and robust robotic hardware.

The existing landscape of dexterous robot hands presents a trade-off between capability, cost, and maintainability:
- **High-End Tendon-Driven Hands:** Robots like the Shadow Hand and ADROIT are highly capable and anthropomorphic, featuring complex joint designs that can approximate human-like ball joints. However, their exorbitant cost (often exceeding $100,000 USD) and intricate tendon-driven mechanisms lead to significant maintenance requirements, making them accessible only to a select few, well-funded research laboratories.
- **Low-Cost, Limited Dexterity Hands:** The Inmoov hand offers an inexpensive, open-source alternative, but its tendon-driven design is often weak, difficult to calibrate, and provides only one degree of freedom (DoF) per finger, severely limiting its dexterity for complex tasks. Soft, deformable hands, while flexible, pose significant challenges for simulation and control.
- **Direct-Driven Hands:** The Allegro Hand has gained popularity in research due to its relatively lower cost (~$16,000 USD) and direct-drive actuation (motors in the finger joints). However, it is frequently criticized for being unreliable, difficult to repair (due to closed-source components), having weak motors for many tasks, and lacking a truly anthropomorphic kinematic structure, particularly in its metacarpophalangeal (MCP) joints.
- **Specialized Hands:** Other designs like D'Manus (part of the ROBEL suite) offer robustness and open-source availability but deviate significantly from human hand kinematics (e.g., two fingers and a thumb), limiting their applicability for tasks requiring human-like dexterity or for learning from human demonstrations. Hands featuring novel actuation methods, such as rollers or fluid/linear actuators, are still in early development, often complex, and not readily available.

The critical gap identified by the authors is the absence of a "good hand hardware for machine learning" – one that is durable, repeatable, low-cost, versatile, and anthropomorphic. This void hinders the ability of the broader research community to reproduce results, build upon existing work, and accelerate progress in real-world dexterous manipulation, a situation contrasting sharply with the widespread availability of hardware for locomotion or two-finger grasping. The LEAP Hand directly aims to fill this critical gap, positioning itself as an accessible, high-performance platform to democratize and accelerate dexterous manipulation research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the development of LEAP Hand is to **democratize access to hardware for anthropomorphic dexterous manipulation research**, thereby accelerating progress in robot learning for complex manipulation tasks. The authors identify the lack of suitable and accessible hardware as the major bottleneck in transitioning dexterous manipulation from simulation to real-world applications.

To address this overarching goal, the LEAP Hand was designed with several key objectives:
- **Low Cost:** To make dexterous manipulation hardware affordable for a wider range of research labs, the target cost was set significantly lower than existing commercial options. The paper highlights achieving a cost of 2000 USD, which is 1/8th the cost of the Allegro Hand and 1/50th that of the ShadowHand.
- **Ease of Assembly and Repair:** To reduce barriers to entry and maintenance, the hand was designed for rapid assembly (under 4 hours) using readily available, off-the-shelf components and parts fabricated with a standard commodity 3D printer. Its modular design ensures easy in-house repair, eliminating the need to send the hand back to manufacturers.
- **Robustness and Durability:** Robot learning experiments can be harsh on hardware due to repeated collisions and prolonged operation. The LEAP Hand aims to be robust enough to withstand such treatment and durable for continuous use, capable of exerting large torques consistently.
- **High Dexterity:** The hand must be capable of a wide range of manipulation tasks, including both power and precision grasps, and complex in-hand object re-orientation.
- **Anthropomorphism:** Crucially, the hand's kinematic structure should closely mimic that of a human hand. This anthropomorphic design facilitates transfer learning from human demonstrations, whether through direct teleoperation (e.g., using VR gloves or human hand tracking) or by learning from passive video data (e.g., internet videos of humans manipulating objects), aligning with the research group's existing work on learning from human demonstrations.
- **Open-Source Availability:** To foster community engagement and progress, the authors commit to open-sourcing the design files (URDF model, 3D CAD), assembly instructions, software APIs (ROS/Python), and a robust sim-to-real pipeline.

By simultaneously addressing cost, reliability, dexterity, and anthropomorphism, the LEAP Hand seeks to provide a versatile and practical platform that can unlock new possibilities for real-world robot learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The development of LEAP Hand followed a holistic approach encompassing kinematic design, material selection, fabrication strategies, and software integration, all while prioritizing the core objectives of low cost, robustness, and anthropomorphism.

#### 3.1. Kinematic Design and Analysis

The central innovation lies in its novel kinematic structure, particularly the **Universal Abduction-Adduction Mechanism** for the fingers.
- **Anthropomorphic Foundation:** The design aims to replicate the human hand's four degrees of freedom per finger: a two-DoF ball joint at the metacarpophalangeal (MCP) joint (for abduction/adduction and flexion/extension), and single-DoF hinge joints at the proximal interphalangeal (PIP) and distal interphalangeal (DIP) joints. The opposable thumb is also integrated.
- **Addressing Direct-Driven Limitations:** Unlike tendon-driven hands that can place large motors remotely, direct-driven hands must house motors within the fingers, limiting kinematic flexibility. Previous direct-driven designs (like Allegro Hand or a baseline LEAP-C Hand) struggle to perfectly approximate the MCP ball joint, losing one DoF in either the extended or flexed finger position. This typically occurs because the motor responsible for abduction-adduction (MCP-2) has its axis fixed to the palm.
- **The LEAP Hand Innovation:** The key idea is to bring the MCP-2 motor's axis of rotation to the *frame of reference of the first finger joint* and ensure it is *always perpendicular* to it. This allows the finger to maintain abduction-adduction capability across its entire range of flexion-extension. This results in superior dexterity, enabling both abduction/adduction in the extended position (like LEAP-C) and pronation/supination in the flexed position (like Allegro), without sacrificing a DoF.
- **Evaluation of Dexterity:** The kinematic design was quantitatively evaluated using:
 - **Thumb Opposability Volume:** This metric measures the shared workspace between the thumb and individual fingers, crucial for versatile grasping. LEAP Hand demonstrated a significantly larger opposability volume compared to Allegro and LEAP-C hands (Table II).
 - **Manipulability Ellipsoid Volume:** Based on Yoshikawa's work, this metric quantifies the ease of fingertip movement in various directions at specific joint poses. LEAP Hand consistently exhibited larger ellipsoid volumes (for both linear and angular components) across "Down," "Up," and "Curled" finger positions (Table I), indicating superior dexterity.

#### 3.2. Hand Design Principles & Fabrication

- **Low-Cost & Repairability:** The hand leverages a mix of off-the-shelf and 3D-printable parts. Critical structural components, such as reinforced plastic brackets, are inexpensive ($10) and sourced from Robotis. The remaining parts (palm, wire guides, soft rubber fingertips) are 3D-printed using common FDM printers (e.g., Ender 3, costing ~200 USD) and materials like PLA and Ninjaflex. This approach enables assembly in under 4 hours and easy in-house repair.
- **Robustness:**
 - **Motor Selection:** Dynamixel motors are chosen for their high torque output relative to their size, while still enabling human-like joint movement velocities (around 8 rad/sec).
 - **Structural Integrity:** The design maximizes motor mass within the hand while minimizing other components to achieve high strength.
 - **Software Current Limiting:** To protect the hand and manipulate fragile objects, the control software supports current or torque limiting.
- **Modularity:** The design allows for modifications like changing the number or length of fingers and the spacing between them, facilitating specialized tasks or research analyses.

#### 3.3. Software and Simulation

- **Control Modes:** LEAP Hand supports various control modes: position, current, velocity, and a crucial current-based position control (which caps maximum current/torque to prevent damage).
- **APIs:** It provides standard interfaces through ROS, Python, and C++.
- **Simulation Environment:** A detailed 3D model of the hand is provided for Pybullet, and a high-fidelity simulator compatible with Isaac Gym is released. This simulator is crucial for training policies in virtual environments and then transferring them to the real world (sim-to-real).

#### 3.4. Experimental Evaluation

The capabilities of LEAP Hand were rigorously evaluated through a series of quantitative and qualitative experiments, comparing it against competitors, primarily the Allegro Hand, and a baseline LEAP-C Hand (a version of the Kitech-Hand built with LEAP Hand's motors for fair comparison).
- **Hardware Tests:**
 - **Endurance Test:** Holding a 2kg weight on a single fingertip for an hour, monitoring angle error, current usage, and motor temperature.
 - **Repeatability Test:** Continuous grasping and ungrasping of a 25g object for an hour, measuring joint angle error over time.
 - **Pull-out Force Test:** Measuring the maximum force a flexed finger can resist before failure (slipping or excessive deviation).
- **Application Tests (Robot Learning Tasks):**
 - **Grasping Test:** Teleoperating various robot hands (LEAP, LEAP-C, Allegro, D'Manus, Inmoov) to grasp a variety of everyday objects, assessing the type of grasp achieved and the perturbation force resisted.
 - **Teleoperation from Uncalibrated Human Video:** Comparing LEAP Hand and Allegro Hand performance on 10 diverse teleoperated tasks (e.g., picking up objects, rotating boxes, opening drawers) using the Robotic Telekinesis method, measuring success rate and completion time.
 - **Behavior Cloning from Demonstrations:** Utilizing pre-trained policies from internet videos (VideoDex) and fine-tuning with minimal teleoperated demos, comparing LEAP Hand and Allegro Hand on several manipulation tasks.
 - **Sim2Real In-Hand Manipulation:** Training a reinforcement learning policy (PPO with BPPT) in Isaac Gym for blind in-hand rotation of a cube, then transferring it to the real LEAP Hand and comparing angular velocity performance with Allegro in simulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments presented in the paper convincingly demonstrate that the LEAP Hand significantly outperforms its closest competitors in terms of cost-efficiency, mechanical robustness, dexterity, and practical utility for robot learning applications.

- **Cost-Effectiveness and Assembly:**
 - The LEAP Hand can be assembled in **under 4 hours** at a cost of **2000 USD**. This represents a dramatic cost reduction, being 1/8th the price of the Allegro Hand and 1/50th that of the ShadowHand.
 - Its reliance on off-the-shelf and commodity 3D-printed parts makes it **easily repairable** in-house, eliminating the need for expensive manufacturer servicing.

- **Enhanced Dexterity and Anthropomorphism:**
 - **Universal Abduction-Adduction:** The novel kinematic mechanism allows LEAP Hand's fingers to maintain all degrees of freedom regardless of their flexed or extended position, a significant advantage over Allegro Hand and LEAP-C Hand, which lose dexterity in certain poses (Table I).
 - **Thumb Opposability:** Quantitatively, LEAP Hand shows the **largest thumb-to-finger opposability volume** compared to Allegro and LEAP-C Hand (Table II), indicating superior versatility for various grasps.
 - **Manipulability:** The hand exhibits **consistently larger manipulability ellipsoid volumes** across various finger configurations (Table I), confirming its superior ability to move the fingertip in various directions.

- **Robustness, Strength, and Durability:**
 - **Endurance Test:** LEAP Hand successfully held a **2kg weight on a single fingertip for one hour** with minimal angle error and stable motor current and temperature. The Allegro Hand was unable to perform this test.
 - **Repeatability Test:** Over an hour of continuous grasping, LEAP Hand maintained a consistent and low joint angle error (0.025 radians in the up position, 0.005 radians in the down position). In contrast, the Allegro Hand experienced significantly higher errors and began to fail after only 15 minutes due to motor overheating (Figure 7).
 - **Pull-out Force Test:** LEAP Hand demonstrated a substantial pull-out force of **19.5 Newtons** (N) per finger. This makes it the strongest among anthropomorphic robot hands tested, notably outperforming the Allegro Hand (8.5 N), and close to the adult human hand's average grip strength (26.5 N, though this comparison is between a single-finger pullout and total grip strength).

- **Performance in Robot Learning Applications:**
 - **Grasping Test:** LEAP Hand successfully grasped **all tested everyday objects**, performing a wide variety of both power and precision grasps. It consistently resisted significant perturbation forces (up to 20 N), outperforming Allegro, especially in complex grasps like the tripod, cigarette, or flat-hand cupping (Table V).
 - **Teleoperation from Human Video:** An operator demonstrated that LEAP Hand either **outperformed or matched Allegro Hand in 9 out of 10 teleoperated tasks**, achieving faster completion times in 8 of 10 tasks (Table IV). This superior performance is attributed to LEAP Hand's better morphology, accuracy, and responsiveness.
 - **Behavior Cloning:** When pre-trained on internet videos and fine-tuned with minimal demonstrations, LEAP Hand **outperformed Allegro Hand on 12 out of 14 behavior cloning tasks** (Table VI), highlighting its consistency and strength in learned policies.
 - **Sim2Real In-Hand Manipulation:** In a challenging blind in-hand cube rotation task, the LEAP Hand achieved a **faster angular velocity (0.2288 rad/s in simulation)** compared to Allegro Hand (0.0828 rad/s) (Table VII). This demonstrates the fidelity of its simulation model and the practical benefit of its superior kinematics, enabling successful and robust transfer of learned policies from simulation to the real world.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LEAP Hand represents a significant advancement in hardware for robot learning, with profound implications for the future of dexterous manipulation research.

- **Democratization of Dexterous Manipulation:** The most immediate and impactful contribution of LEAP Hand is its role in democratizing access to high-performance, anthropomorphic dexterous manipulation hardware. By drastically reducing the cost (1/8th of Allegro Hand, 1/50th of ShadowHand) and simplifying maintenance, it opens the door for a much wider array of research institutions and individual labs to engage in real-world dexterous manipulation research. This broader participation is critical for accelerating innovation, fostering reproducibility, and building a stronger, more collaborative research community, much like what has been observed in locomotion and two-finger grasping robotics.

- **Accelerating Robot Learning Research:**
 - **Real-World Learning:** The robustness, durability, and reliable performance of LEAP Hand enable longer, more intensive real-world robot learning experiments, reducing downtime and hardware-related failures. This is crucial for data-hungry learning algorithms like reinforcement learning and behavior cloning.
 - **Leveraging Human Data:** Its anthropomorphic design is a game-changer for learning from human demonstrations. The ability to directly map human hand poses and joint angles to the robot, as demonstrated in teleoperation and behavior cloning from internet videos, allows researchers to tap into the vast, freely available human motion data. This can significantly reduce the data collection burden and enable more intuitive human-robot interaction for learning.
 - **Sim-to-Real Bridge:** The release of a high-fidelity Isaac Gym simulator alongside the physical hardware is invaluable. It allows for rapid iteration of policies in simulation, which can then be effectively transferred to the real LEAP Hand, thereby accelerating research into complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks and bridging the persistent sim-to-real gap.

- **Setting a New Standard:** The LEAP Hand demonstrates that it is possible to achieve superior dexterity, strength, and robustness in a robot hand while maintaining a low cost and ease of repair. It sets a new benchmark for what is achievable in practical, accessible robot manipulation hardware, forcing a re-evaluation of existing trade-offs. Its performance consistently surpasses that of the widely used Allegro Hand across multiple metrics and tasks, solidifying its position as a compelling alternative.

- **Foundation for Future Innovations:** By open-sourcing the entire platform (hardware designs, assembly instructions, software APIs, simulation environment), the authors invite the global robotics community to build upon their work. This collaborative approach can lead to further improvements, integration with novel sensing modalities (e.g., planned low-cost touch sensors), and the development of even more advanced manipulation capabilities, ultimately pushing the boundaries of what autonomous robots can achieve in complex, human-centric environments. The LEAP Hand is not just a research outcome; it is a catalyst for future research.
