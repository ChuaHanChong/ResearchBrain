# Learn to Teach: Sample-Efficient Privileged Learning for Humanoid Locomotion over Diverse Terrains

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Georgia Institute of Technology developed L2T-RL, a one-stage teacher-student framework that concurrently trains policies for humanoid locomotion, addressing sample inefficiency and the sim-to-real gap. The approach enabled the Digit robot to perform robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer across diverse, challenging real-world terrains, achieving high success rates in simulation and reducing training sample requirements by 50%.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning policies for robotics face a 'sim-to-real' gap, where policies trained in simulation often fail on physical hardware due to environmental and dynamic discrepancies.
- Existing domain randomization techniques, used to bridge the sim-to-real gap, typically require high sample complexity and extensive simulation time.
- Traditional two-stage teacher-student learning paradigms suffer from sample inefficiency (discarding teacher samples) and an 'imitation gap' where student policies struggle to mimic teacher actions based on privileged information.

## Method

- The '**Learn to Teach**' (L2T) framework unifies teacher and student policy learning into a single, interactive training stage, leveraging a shared replay buffer to reuse collected simulator samples.
- A sample mixing mechanism probabilistically blends actions from the teacher (with privileged information) and the student (with restricted observations) during environment interaction, fostering a bidirectional feedback loop.
- The teacher policy uses an MLP with full state observations, while the student policy uses an LSTM with noisy proprioceptive inputs, enabling it to infer latent states from sequential, partial observations.

## Results

- L2T-RL achieved a **94%** student success rate across 4096 rough terrain environments in simulation, outperforming asymmetric learning baselines like CTS (**74%**) and ROA (**73%**).
- The framework demonstrated a **50%** reduction in sample requirements compared to conventional two-stage teacher-student learning, saving approximately 12 hours of GPU training time.
- Policies trained with L2T-RL enabled zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on the physical Digit robot, achieving robust locomotion over diverse real-world uneven terrains (e.g., gravel, sand, wet grass, slopes) and demonstrating resilience to external perturbations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating teacher and student learning into a unified, interactive stage with a shared replay buffer significantly improves sample efficiency by recycling data and synchronizing learning trajectories.
> - The sample mixing mechanism effectively mitigates the imitation gap by exposing the teacher to student-generated 'out-of-distribution' data and allowing the student to continuously track the evolving teacher's optimal behavior.
> - A student policy relying solely on hardware-realistic proprioceptive inputs can achieve robust and generalizable locomotion performance comparable to a privileged teacher, without needing complex depth estimation modules.
