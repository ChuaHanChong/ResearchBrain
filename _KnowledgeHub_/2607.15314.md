# Cura 1T: Specialized Model for Agentic Healthcare

> [!summary] Summary
> Cura 1T is a specialized one-trillion-parameter large language model designed to integrate comprehensive patient care, clinical reasoning, and agentic tasks within a single framework. It achieves top-tier performance on various healthcare benchmarks while effectively retaining general AI capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing large language models often excel at specific clinical tasks but lack a unified model capable of comprehensively handling patient care, clinical reasoning, and interactive agentic tasks simultaneously.
- Specializing LLMs for healthcare is challenging due to data scarcity, fragmentation across diverse sources, high sensitivity, and inter-task interference where improving one capability can inadvertently degrade others.
- The manual, iterative process of identifying model failures, curating targeted data, and refining models for healthcare is time-consuming and inefficient for human experts.

## Method

- Developed **Cura 1T**, a one-trillion-parameter large language model post-trained from the ==Kimi-K2.6== base, featuring a 256K context window and native text/vision input, using a specialized ==adapter-training stack==.
- Implemented a ==human-gated self-evolution loop== orchestrated by an LLM agent, systematically curating and refining the post-training data mixture, prioritizing data recipes over hyperparameter tuning.
- The training protocol within the loop involves a sequence of ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)==, ==Reinforcement Learning (RL)==, and ==Self-Distillation Fine-Tuning (SDFT)== for efficient, targeted model improvement.

## Results

- Cura 1T achieved substantial improvements over its Kimi-K2.6 base model, with task success scores rising by **+0.093** on MedAgentBench, **+0.159** on HealthBench Professional, and **+0.086** on MedXpertQA.
- The model demonstrated top-tier performance, ranking at or near the top among frontier baselines like Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5 on a comprehensive suite of healthcare benchmarks.
- Evaluations on out-of-domain benchmarks (AIME, GPQA-Diamond, τ²-Bench) confirmed that the intensive healthcare specialization did not overtly degrade Cura 1T's general reasoning or agentic capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An iterative, data-first self-evolution loop, managed by an LLM agent with human oversight, is highly effective for specializing large models in complex, data-constrained domains like healthcare.
> - Systematic data curation strategies, including 'retention anchors' and targeted data synthesis (e.g., for reasoning correction, knowledge injection, behavior calibration), are crucial for preventing catastrophic forgetting and mitigating inter-task interference.
> - Specific failure modes in high-stakes healthcare tasks (e.g., brittle EHR tool execution, rubric omissions in patient communication, premature diagnoses) can be precisely identified and addressed through tailored data generation, leading to robust and reliable improvements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has shown considerable progress, with frontier models demonstrating the ability to answer complex clinical questions, as exemplified by prior work like Med-PaLM (Singhal et al., 2023). However, the deployment of such models in real-world healthcare settings necessitates a broader and more robust capability profile that extends beyond mere question answering. The existing research landscape, while strong on individual components, has limitations in integrating these capabilities into a single, specialized model.

Current advancements in healthcare AI for LLMs generally fall into three categories:
1. **Patient Care:** Focused on generating clinician- and patient-facing responses that adhere to medical guidelines, as seen in benchmarks like HealthBench (Arora et al., 2025; OpenAI, 2026).
2. **Clinical Reasoning:** Addressing expert medical questions, often incorporating multimodal data like text and images, evaluated by datasets such as MedQA, MedMCQA, and MedXpertQA (Jin et al., 2020; Pal et al., 2022; Zuo et al., 2025).
3. **Healthcare Agentic Tasks:** Involving multi-turn diagnostic workflows, electronic health record (EHR) tool use, and complex, long-horizon tasks under specific protocols, benchmarked by systems like AgentClinic, MedAgentBench, CHI-Bench, PhysicianBench, and HealthAgentBench (Schmidgall et al., 2024; Jiang et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026b,a). Models like Baichuan-M4 have also explored continuous care with tool use and patient memory (Baichuan AI and THUBPM Group, Tsinghua University, 2026).

Despite these individual advances, the paper identifies a gap: the comparative lack of a single, specialized LLM designed to serve all three use cases comprehensively. The authors frame this as primarily a data-construction problem rather than solely a model-training or hyperparameter-tuning challenge. Healthcare data is recognized as less abundant and more sensitive, requiring high-quality supervision and fragmented across various sources (guidelines, exams, patient interactions, images, EHR workflows). A key challenge is that improving one capability through data additions can inadvertently degrade others, due to uneven failure modes (e.g., rubric omissions, missing facts, brittle reasoning, malformed tool calls).

This work positions itself within the broader trend of recursive self-improvement and automated research workflows. These methodologies, exemplified by efforts like Self-Refine, Reflexion, OPRO, DSPy, TextGrad, and autoresearch repositories (Madaan et al., 2023; Shinn et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2023; Khattab et al., 2023; Yuksekgonul et al., 2024; autoresearch contributors, 2026), aim to automate the research process. Cura 1T applies this closed-loop measurement discipline not to prompts, code, or hyperparameters, but specifically to the curation and refinement of the post-training data mixture.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop Cura 1T, a healthcare-specialized Large Language Model (LLM) that can proficiently handle a broad spectrum of healthcare use cases within a single, unified model. This includes patient care, clinical reasoning over text and images, and interactive healthcare agentic tasks.

The motivation for this work is multi-faceted and addresses several limitations observed in the current landscape of LLM applications in healthcare:

1. **Bridging Capability Gaps in Healthcare Deployment:** While frontier LLMs have demonstrated strong performance on specific clinical questions, real-world healthcare deployment demands a more expansive and less forgiving capability profile. This includes generating appropriate patient- and clinician-facing responses, performing expert medical reasoning (potentially across text and images), and executing complex multi-turn workflows involving tool use and electronic health records. Existing models often excel in isolated tasks but do not consistently integrate all these functionalities.

2. **Addressing Diverse and Interconnected Healthcare Use Cases:** The paper highlights three distinct yet interconnected healthcare use cases that are central to comprehensive medical applications:
 - **Patient Care:** Requires nuanced, rubric-compliant, clear, and safe responses in clinical conversations.
 - **Clinical Reasoning:** Involves complex problem-solving, factual recall, and differential diagnosis based on expert medical questions and multimodal data.
 - **Healthcare Agentic Tasks:** Entails multi-step processes like gathering evidence, interactive diagnosis, utilizing tools (e.g., EHR systems), and adhering to strict task protocols.
 The motivation stems from the observation that a single specialized LLM covering all these use cases together remains an underexplored area.

3. **Overcoming the Data-Centric Challenges of Healthcare LLMs:** The authors identify the core challenge as a "data-construction problem." Healthcare data is characterized by:
 - **Scarcity and Unevenness:** Compared to domains like coding or mathematics, high-quality, task-specific medical training signal is less abundant.
 - **Fragmentation:** Relevant examples are distributed across diverse sources such as guidelines, exams, patient interactions, images, and EHR workflows.
 - **Sensitivity:** The high-stakes nature of healthcare makes obtaining and curating high-quality supervision data difficult and error-intolerant.
 - **Inter-task Interference:** A critical motivation is that specific failure modes are unevenly distributed across task categories, and attempts to improve one behavior can inadvertently degrade others (e.g., adding examples for one task might erode previously correct behavior in another). The motivation is to avoid generic updates and instead identify and target specific data needs.

4. **Enhancing Training Efficiency and Iteration through Automation:** The manual process of iterating through the full post-training cycle (planning, training, evaluation, failure diagnosis, data mixture rebuilding) is time-consuming for human experts. The motivation is to leverage recent advancements in automated research workflows and agentic models to create a "human-gated self-evolution loop." This loop aims to accelerate the identification of missing data, the synthesis of targeted examples, and the curation of data mixtures that promote positive transfer across tasks without forgetting.

In summary, the motivation for Cura 1T is to develop a robust, specialized LLM for comprehensive healthcare applications by systematically addressing the challenges of data sparsity, fragmentation, and inter-task interference through an automated, data-centric refinement process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Cura 1T is developed by post-training the Kimi-K2.6 base model, which features a one-trillion-parameter architecture, a 256K context window, and native text plus vision input capabilities. The methodology centers on a specialized adapter-training stack and a human-gated self-evolution loop designed to address the data-mixture challenge inherent in healthcare specialization.

#### 3.1. Training Protocol and Infrastructure

The training process for Cura 1T employs a thin adapter-training stack, leveraging Low-Rank Adapters (LoRA) (Hu et al., 2021) to efficiently update a small number of parameters while keeping the base model weights intact. The training progresses through a sequence of stages:

1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** This serves as the initial, low-cost step within each evolution round. It is used by the training agent to verify that proposed data mixtures and hyperparameters are well-formed, facilitate stable training, and move target metrics in the desired direction. This acts as a screening phase.
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Following a successful SFT screen, RL is applied to improve the model's policy against task-level reward signals.
3. **Self-Distillation Fine-Tuning (SDFT):** As the final step in a round, SDFT (Shenfeld et al., 2026) is utilized. In this process, the model trains from its own on-policy samples (student distribution, πθ(·|x)) towards a teacher distribution (π(·|x, c)) conditioned on additional, "privileged" context (c). This context represents extra information used to generate a clean teacher trajectory, such as an intervened or augmented trajectory, reference behavior, or verified knowledge. The student model only sees the original prompt after the context is removed. This approach anchors updates to trajectories the model can produce, which is noted as important for long medical reasoning traces where copying off-policy chains of thought could damage native reasoning.

The consolidated Cura 1T model is ultimately trained from the cumulative data mixture refined and validated across all successful capability loops.

#### 3.2. Human-Gated Self-Evolution Loop

The core of the methodology is a self-evolution loop orchestrated by an LLM agent, with human oversight, as depicted in Figure 2. This design prioritizes the data recipe as the primary search object, given the scarcity and unevenness of healthcare-specific training data. The loop consists of the following stages:

1. **Plan:** The training agent defines target behaviors, selects relevant benchmarks and metrics, designs the data recipe, and proposes candidate hyperparameters. This stage requires human approval before proceeding.
2. **Train:** The agent launches LoRA adapter training using the SFT-to-RL-to-SDFT stack. SFT is used for mixture and hyperparameter screening, RL for reward-driven policy improvement, and SDFT for consolidating the final adapter.
3. **Evaluate:** The agent runs the candidate model against relevant benchmarks, collects graded trajectories, and performs failure analysis.
4. **Refine:** This is a crucial data-centric stage where the agent uses observed failures to:
 - Perform root-cause analysis to identify specific failure modes.
 - Synthesize targeted training data addressing these failures.
 - Curate the next data mixture by integrating new and existing examples.
 - Validate candidate data rows (checking format, safety, PII risk, duplicates, and coverage of the intended repair) before proposing subsequent steps.
 The data synthesis process relies on several agent skills:
 - **Retention Anchor:** Introduces examples of capabilities the model already handles to prevent catastrophic forgetting during targeted repairs.
 - **Reasoning Correction:** Edits failed reasoning traces using a frontier model and then seeds new questions requiring similar reasoning.
 - **Knowledge Injection:** Identifies missing clinical knowledge, grounds it with retrieved documents, and synthesizes closed-book QA examples with explicit reasoning.
 - **Behavior Calibration:** Compares desired with observed answer behaviors, synthesizes rubrics to articulate the gap, and generates responses conditioned on these rubrics.
 - **Other:** Addresses task-specific failures outside the general reasoning, knowledge, or behavior categories, such as agentic workflow errors.
 - **Data Mixture Curation:** Merges synthesized correction rows with retention anchors, then deduplicates, caps, and reweights the candidate training set.
5. **Review:** A human expert acts as a gate, inspecting the validated data refinement and the candidate model's performance. Based on this review, a decision is made to "keep," "revert," "continue to refine," or "deploy" the model, thus closing the loop and initiating the next round of iteration or finalizing the model.

This iterative, data-first approach allows for targeted improvement of specific capabilities while maintaining performance on existing ones, through systematic identification of missing data and its strategic integration into the training mixture.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Cura 1T involved a comprehensive suite of healthcare benchmarks, alongside a set of out-of-domain benchmarks to assess general capabilities retention. The results indicate that Cura 1T generally ranks at or near the top among frontier baselines in healthcare tasks and maintains competitive performance on out-of-domain evaluations.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Against Base Model (Kimi-K2.6)

Table 1 summarizes the performance improvements of Cura 1T over its Kimi-K2.6 base across five key healthcare benchmarks:

- **MedAgentBench:** Cura 1T achieved a task success score of 0.940, representing an improvement of +0.093 from the base model's 0.847.
- **HealthBench Professional:** Cura 1T scored 0.662, an increase of +0.159 from the base model's 0.503.
- **HealthBench Hard:** Cura 1T scored 0.368, an increase of +0.146 from the base model's 0.222.
- **MedXpertQA:** Cura 1T achieved an overall pass@1 score of 0.655, improving by +0.086 from the base model's 0.569.
- **AgentClinic:** Cura 1T demonstrated an overall pass@1 score of 0.796, an improvement of +0.042 from the base model's 0.754.

#### 4.2. Benchmark-Specific Findings and Evolution Path (Figure 3)

- **MedAgentBench (Healthcare Agentic Tasks):** This benchmark evaluates FHIR tool-use tasks against a live server. The primary failure mode of the base model was brittle execution of EHR writes (e.g., missing fields or incorrect coded values).
 - **Evolution:** The "Tool-use" intervention, which involved synthetic trajectories for FHIR write patterns, initially improved performance. This was further enhanced by "Tool-use + retention," adding successful trajectories to prevent degradation of other workflow aspects. A "Harness bug fix" corrected evaluation configuration.
 - **Result:** The self-evolution rounds raised task success from 0.883 to 0.973 during development. The consolidated Cura 1T achieved 0.940, surpassing Claude Opus 4.8 (0.937) and GPT-5.5 (0.894).

- **HealthBench (Patient Care):** This benchmark assesses open-ended clinical responses against physician-authored rubrics. Failures were dominated by omitted required points.
 - **Evolution:** An initial "Behavior correction" mixture was reverted due to substantial degradation on a subset of tasks despite aggregate gains. A subsequent "Clean behavior mix" maintained the target while refining data by removing boilerplate, adding clearer principle-rubric examples, and shortening training.
 - **Result:** Cura 1T achieved the strongest score on HealthBench Professional (0.662) and a competitive score on HealthBench Hard (0.368).

- **MedXpertQA (Clinical Reasoning - Text and Multimodal):** This benchmark tests expert medical reasoning. The base model exhibited both reasoning gaps and missing clinical knowledge.
 - **Evolution:** Initial "Reasoning correction" interventions were reverted as they proved unstable or insufficiently factually comprehensive. "Knowledge injection + retention" subsequently improved performance by adding closed-book clinical knowledge and mixing in examples the model already handled. Further "Mixture refinement" balanced retained examples. A "Data mixture tuning" round was reverted due to non-termination.
 - **Result:** The consolidated Cura 1T achieved an overall pass@1 of 0.655. It improved over the base and Claude Opus 4.8 (0.628), ranking second overall to GPT-5.5 (0.675).

- **AgentClinic (Interactive Diagnosis):** This benchmark evaluates multi-turn diagnostic encounters in a simulated clinic, adapted to a tool-native format. The main failure mode was premature diagnosis.
 - **Evolution:** "Single-turn reasoning" was reverted because it did not preserve clinical conduct. "Interactive trajectory + retention" successfully taught the model to elicit evidence before committing to a diagnosis, while mixing in correctly answered cases.
 - **Result:** Cura 1T achieved an overall pass@1 of 0.796, improving from the base's 0.754 and matching the best reported NEJM score in the tool-native harness.

#### 4.3. Out-of-Domain Evaluation (Figure 4)

Cura 1T was also evaluated on benchmarks outside the healthcare domain to assess whether specialization negatively impacted general capabilities.

- **Reasoning Benchmarks:** Across AIME 2025, AIME 2026, and GPQA-Diamond, Cura 1T performed on par with frontier comparators.
- **Agentic Benchmarks:** On τ²-Bench (covering airline, retail, and telecom domains), Cura 1T performed comparably on τ²-airline and surpassed models with publicly reported scores on τ²-retail and τ²-telecom.

These out-of-domain results suggest that the healthcare-focused training strategy employed in Cura 1T did not overtly degrade the model's general reasoning or agentic capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Cura 1T and its associated self-evolution loop methodology carries several significant implications for the field of large language model research and its application in specialized domains, particularly healthcare.

**Methodological Innovation in Specialized LLM Training:** The most significant contribution is the demonstration of a repeatable, data-first self-evolution loop. This approach reframes specialized LLM training from a primary focus on hyperparameter search or generic data dumps to a systematic, agent-managed curation of the post-training data mixture. This is particularly impactful for domains like healthcare where training data is sparse, sensitive, and fragmented, and where targeted interventions are more effective than broad updates. The loop's ability to diagnose failures, synthesize specific data (e.g., reasoning corrections, knowledge injections, behavior calibrations, retention anchors), and iteratively refine the data recipe offers a robust framework for continuous model improvement.

**Advancement in Comprehensive Healthcare AI:** Cura 1T represents a step toward a single, specialized LLM capable of integrating patient care, clinical reasoning (text and multimodal), and complex agentic tasks within a unified architecture. This addresses a recognized gap in the research landscape, where progress has often been strong on individual components but limited in their holistic integration. A model capable of navigating diverse healthcare scenarios—from empathetic patient communication to detailed diagnostic reasoning and EHR tool interaction—has the potential to simplify AI deployment and enhance consistency across healthcare workflows.

**Evidence Against Catastrophic Forgetting:** The reported out-of-domain evaluation results are significant because they indicate that the intensive healthcare specialization did not overtly degrade Cura 1T's general reasoning or agentic capabilities in other domains. This suggests that the "retention anchor" strategy and careful data mixture curation within the self-evolution loop were effective in preventing catastrophic forgetting, a common challenge in continual learning and specialized fine-tuning of large models. Maintaining general capabilities while achieving deep specialization is crucial for the versatility and long-term utility of such AI systems.

**Enhanced Reliability in High-Stakes Environments:** The methodology's focus on granular failure analysis and targeted data synthesis directly addresses critical failure modes observed in healthcare tasks, such as brittle EHR tool execution, rubric omissions in patient communication, and premature diagnoses. By systematically improving these specific weak points through data, the approach aims to enhance the reliability and clinical appropriateness of AI outputs, which is paramount in a high-stakes domain like healthcare.

**Implications for Future Research and Development:** The self-evolution loop provides a blueprint for developing and continuously refining specialized AI agents in other complex, data-constrained domains. It highlights the importance of detailed error analysis and data engineering as central to achieving robust performance. Furthermore, the explicit identification of limitations (compute, data, full-parameter updates) points to clear directions for future research, including broadening clinical behavior coverage and tackling longer-horizon agentic tasks.

**Responsible AI Development:** The paper's explicit disclaimer that Cura 1T is a research model and not a medical service, emphasizing that benchmark scores do not equate to safety for unsupervised clinical use, reinforces the importance of responsible AI development. This transparency is crucial for managing expectations and guiding the safe integration of such advanced AI systems into healthcare.
