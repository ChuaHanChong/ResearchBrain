# PVI: Plug-in Visual Injection for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Lionrock AI Lab developed Plug-in Visual Injection (PVI), a method to enhance Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by providing fine-grained geometric and temporal visual cues directly to the action expert, bypassing VLM limitations. PVI improved the average success rate on bimanual simulation tasks from 35.7% to 59.7% and enabled successful real-world bimanual cloth folding.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in VLA architectures often attenuate fine-grained geometric and temporal cues crucial for precise robotic manipulation.
- Current methods for injecting auxiliary visual features into VLA models frequently neglect temporal dynamics or require substantial architectural modifications.
- A lightweight and compatible method was needed to integrate rich visual information, including temporal context, into pretrained VLA models without extensive changes.

## Method

- **PVI** introduces a lightweight, encoder-agnostic module that directly injects auxiliary visual representations into the action expert of a pretrained VLA model.
- It uses a ==zero-initialized projection== from an auxiliary visual encoder (e.g., ==V-JEPA2== for temporal features) into the action expert's embedding space.
- A trainable "copy" branch of the action expert is conditioned on these auxiliary features, with its outputs ==residually injected== layer-wise into the frozen main action expert.

## Results

- PVI achieved a **24.0 percentage point** increase in average success rate (from **35.7%** to **59.7%**) on 20 bimanual simulation tasks compared to the fine-tuned GR00T N1.5 baseline.
- Temporal video features from V-JEPA2, when injected via PVI, yielded an average success rate of **69.4%**, significantly surpassing static DINOv2 features (**56.8%**) on 10 simulation tasks.
- The method enabled successful, closed-loop, long-horizon bimanual cloth folding on a real dual-arm robot, showcasing its practical applicability for complex deformable object manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs' focus on semantic abstraction often leads to the loss of fine-grained geometric and temporal details essential for precise robot manipulation.
> - Temporal video features consistently offer greater performance improvements than static image features for closed-loop bimanual manipulation tasks, emphasizing the value of dynamics.
> - A dual-pathway architecture with layer-wise residual injection and zero-initialization ensures stable and effective integration of auxiliary visual cues without perturbing pretrained VLA behavior.
