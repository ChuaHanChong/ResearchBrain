# PVI: Plug-in Visual Injection for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Lionrock AI Lab developed Plug-in Visual Injection (PVI), a method to enhance Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by providing fine-grained geometric and temporal visual cues directly to the action expert, bypassing VLM limitations. PVI improved the average success rate on bimanual simulation tasks from 35.7% to 59.7% and enabled successful real-world bimanual cloth folding.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in VLA architectures often attenuate fine-grained geometric and temporal cues crucial for precise robotic manipulation.
- Current methods for injecting auxiliary visual features into VLA models frequently neglect temporal dynamics or require substantial architectural modifications.
- A lightweight and compatible method was needed to integrate rich visual information, including temporal context, into pretrained VLA models without extensive changes.

## Method

- **PVI** introduces a lightweight, encoder-agnostic module that directly injects auxiliary visual representations into the action expert of a pretrained VLA model.
- It uses a ==zero-initialized projection== from an auxiliary visual encoder (e.g., ==V-JEPA2== for temporal features) into the action expert's embedding space.
- A trainable "copy" branch of the action expert is conditioned on these auxiliary features, with its outputs ==residually injected== layer-wise into the frozen main action expert.

## Results

- PVI achieved a **24.0 percentage point** increase in average success rate (from **35.7%** to **59.7%**) on 20 bimanual simulation tasks compared to the fine-tuned GR00T N1.5 baseline.
- Temporal video features from V-JEPA2, when injected via PVI, yielded an average success rate of **69.4%**, significantly surpassing static DINOv2 features (**56.8%**) on 10 simulation tasks.
- The method enabled successful, closed-loop, long-horizon bimanual cloth folding on a real dual-arm robot, showcasing its practical applicability for complex deformable object manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs' focus on semantic abstraction often leads to the loss of fine-grained geometric and temporal details essential for precise robot manipulation.
> - Temporal video features consistently offer greater performance improvements than static image features for closed-loop bimanual manipulation tasks, emphasizing the value of dynamics.
> - A dual-pathway architecture with layer-wise residual injection and zero-initialization ensures stable and effective integration of auxiliary visual cues without perturbing pretrained VLA behavior.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of robot manipulation, specifically focusing on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. VLA models aim to enable generalist robot manipulation by integrating language comprehension with visuomotor control. A common architectural paradigm in this area involves pairing a Vision-Language Model (VLM) for semantic understanding with a diffusion or flow-matching Transformer, referred to as an action expert, for generating continuous action sequences. This setup leverages the semantic generalization capabilities of large VLMs.

However, existing VLA architectures face a structural limitation. VLMs, often optimized for semantic abstraction and trained on static visual observations, tend to attenuate fine-grained geometric cues (e.g., object geometry, edge alignments, grasp affordances) and typically lack explicit temporal evidence. These specific types of visual information are critical for dexterous manipulation tasks, especially those requiring precision, state tracking, and coordination over time.

Prior research has attempted to mitigate this bottleneck by injecting auxiliary visual features. These approaches generally fall into two categories:
1. **Enriching the VLM pathway:** Fusing geometry-grounded or spatially enriched features into the VLM's visual tokens before semantic processing.
2. **Bypassing the VLM:** Injecting auxiliary visual representations directly into the action expert via side branches, often drawing inspiration from conditional generation paradigms.

While these strategies have demonstrated improvements in manipulation precision by recovering geometric detail, they commonly rely on static spatial representations, neglecting the temporal dynamics inherent in closed-loop manipulation. Recent efforts have begun to explore temporal and video-based representations within VLA models, demonstrating their complementary value. However, these often necessitate substantial architectural modifications to the pretrained backbone or the VLM-to-action interface to accommodate temporal inputs, raising challenges regarding lightweight integration and compatibility with existing pretrained VLA weights. This paper addresses this gap by proposing a minimally invasive method for injecting diverse visual information, including temporal dynamics, into existing VLA models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a lightweight, minimally invasive, and encoder-agnostic method for injecting auxiliary visual representations into pretrained VLA models, specifically targeting the limitations of existing VLM-based approaches. The motivation stems from several identified bottlenecks and unmet needs:

1. **VLM Bottleneck in Fine-Grained Manipulation:** Existing VLA models that couple VLMs with action experts often suffer from the VLM's inherent design. VLMs are optimized for high-level semantic abstraction and language grounding, which can lead to the compression or attenuation of fine-grained geometric cues (such as precise object boundaries, local depth, and contact surfaces) and the absence of explicit temporal evidence (like motion continuity, progressive contact, and sub-step transitions). These specific visual details are crucial for successful and precise dexterous manipulation, particularly in tasks requiring sub-centimeter accuracy or bimanual coordination.

2. **Underexplored Temporal Information:** While previous auxiliary visual injection methods have addressed the lack of geometric detail by introducing static spatial representations (e.g., object-centric or 3D geometric features), they largely overlook temporal dynamics. Closed-loop manipulation inherently demands understanding how a scene evolves over time, including motion progression, alignment convergence, contact transitions, and the completion of subtasks. These temporal signals are often ambiguous or entirely absent from single-frame static observations. Integrating temporal context is therefore critical for enhancing the robustness and capability of manipulation policies.

3. **Need for Lightweight and Compatible Injection:** Existing approaches that incorporate temporal or video-based representations typically require significant architectural modifications to the pretrained VLM backbone or the VLM-to-action interface. Such extensive changes can be resource-intensive, requiring retraining from scratch or complex adaptation procedures, which contradicts the goal of leveraging existing powerful pretrained VLA weights efficiently. There is a need for a mechanism that can inject rich visual information, including temporal cues, without altering the core architecture of the pretrained VLA model and with minimal retraining effort.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes Plug-in Visual Injection (PVI), designed to:
- Bypass the VLM bottleneck by directly supplying fine-grained geometric and temporal cues to the action expert.
- Enable the systematic injection and comparison of diverse auxiliary visual representations, including temporal video features, using an encoder-agnostic interface.
- Achieve this integration in a lightweight and stable manner, preserving the pretrained behavior of the VLA model and requiring only single-stage fine-tuning of the newly introduced injection pathway.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Plug-in Visual Injection (PVI), is a lightweight, encoder-agnostic module designed to inject auxiliary visual representations directly into the flow-matching action expert of a pretrained VLA model. PVI operates without modifying the original VLA's architecture or requiring retraining from scratch. The core VLA architecture under consideration consists of a Vision-Language Model (VLM) for semantic grounding and a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) as the action expert for continuous action generation.

PVI comprises three main components:

#### 3.1. Encoder-Agnostic Auxiliary Visual Feature Extraction

PVI's design allows for flexible integration of various auxiliary visual encoders. The process involves:
- **Auxiliary Encoder (E):** A frozen auxiliary visual encoder `E` extracts features `z_aux` from raw observations. This encoder is agnostic to the type of visual information (static images, temporal video clips, or a combination). Examples include temporal dynamic representations from video clips (e.g., V-JEPA2) or spatial geometric representations from images (e.g., DINOv2).
- **Zero-initialized Projection:** A trainable linear projection matrix `W_proj` maps the extracted `z_aux` features into the `D`-dimensional embedding space of the DiT action expert, resulting in `˜z_aux = z_aux W_proj`. `W_proj` is initialized to zero, ensuring that the auxiliary pathway initially provides no signal to the action expert, thereby preserving pretrained behavior at the start of training.

#### 3.2. Dual-Pathway DiT with Layer-Wise Residual Injection

PVI augments the VLA with a side-branch visual injection pathway while keeping the original VLM and main DiT frozen:
- **Shared Initial Features:** Both the frozen main DiT and the trainable copy branch receive the same initial token sequence `h_0`, which encodes proprioceptive state and noisy action tokens.
- **Trainable Copy Branch:** A trainable copy branch is created, mirroring the architecture of the main DiT. This copy branch is initialized with the pretrained weights of the main DiT, providing a strong prior for feature processing.
- **Dual Conditioning:** While the main DiT blocks are conditioned on the VLM embeddings `z_vl` (e.g., via cross-attention), the copy branch is conditioned on the projected auxiliary features `˜z_aux`. This allows the copy branch to specialize in processing auxiliary visual information while maintaining structural alignment with the main DiT.
- **Layer-Wise Residual Injection:** For each Transformer block `i` (from 1 to `N`), the hidden states `h_copy_i` from the copy branch are mapped by a zero-initialized linear injection layer `Z_i`. The output of `Z_i(h_copy_i)` is then added residually to the corresponding hidden states `h_main_i` of the frozen main DiT. This mechanism allows auxiliary visual information to influence the action generation process at every layer of the DiT, enhancing it without directly modifying the pretrained main pathway. The zero initialization of `Z_i` ensures that, at the start of training, the auxiliary pathway produces zero control signals, preventing perturbation of the frozen main pathway.

#### 3.3. Training Strategy

The training process for PVI is designed for stability and minimal intervention:
- **Frozen Components:** The VLM backbone, the main DiT of the VLA, and the auxiliary visual encoder `E` are all kept frozen. This preserves their pretrained knowledge and reduces the computational cost of training.
- **Trainable Parameters:** Only the newly introduced components are trained: the projection layer `W_proj`, the blocks of the copy branch `{f_copy_i}`, the injection layers `{Z_i}`, and any embodiment-specific state/action adapters.
- **Initialization:** As previously mentioned, the copy branch is initialized from the pretrained weights of the main DiT. `W_proj` and `{Z_i}` are initialized to zero. This ensures that training begins from a state functionally equivalent to the original pretrained VLA, allowing for a gradual and stable integration of auxiliary information without abrupt changes to learned behavior.
- **Loss Function:** PVI uses the standard flow-matching loss, identical to the baseline VLA. No auxiliary supervision is introduced; all newly added parameters are trained end-to-end solely through the action prediction objective.

This methodology provides a stable, efficient, and flexible framework for enhancing VLA models with rich auxiliary visual signals, particularly temporal information, while minimizing modifications to and preserving the capabilities of the original pretrained architecture.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PVI was conducted through a series of experiments, including comparisons with alternative injection strategies, a representation study, ablations, multi-task scalability tests, and real-robot demonstrations. The base VLA model used for instantiation was GR00T N1.5.

#### 4.1. Comparison of Injection Strategies

PVI was compared against four other plausible strategies for injecting auxiliary visual features into the DiT action expert: Concat-style, ControlNet-style, ReferenceNet-style, and ControlVLA-style. All methods used V-JEPA2 as the auxiliary encoder (8 frames at 4 fps) and were fine-tuned with the same budget on 20 bimanual simulation tasks in RoboTwin 2.0.

- **PVI's Superior Performance:** PVI demonstrated a substantial improvement, increasing the average success rate from 35.7% (fine-tuned GR00T N1.5 baseline) to 59.7% (+24.0 percentage points).
- **Performance of Alternatives:** Concat-style showed a moderate improvement to 43.6% (+7.9 pp). ControlVLA-style, ControlNet-style, and ReferenceNet-style achieved average success rates of 37.0% (+1.3 pp), 34.4% (-1.35 pp), and 37.4% (+1.7 pp), respectively, remaining close to or slightly below the baseline.
- **Task-Specific Gains:** PVI yielded particularly large gains on tasks with low baseline success rates, such as `beat_block_hammer` (39% to 84%), `click_alarmclock` (48% to 87%), `move_playingcard_away` (23% to 84%), and `scan_object` (4% to 42%).
- **Conclusion:** The results indicate that the injection mechanism is a significant factor, with PVI's dual-conditioning and behavior-preserving layer-wise injection strategy outperforming competing designs under identical data and training conditions.

#### 4.2. Encoder-Agnostic Injection and Representation Study

Leveraging PVI's encoder-agnostic interface, a controlled study compared different auxiliary visual representations on 10 simulation tasks.

- **Consistent Improvements:** All three PVI instantiations consistently improved over the fine-tuned GR00T N1.5 baseline (39.8% average success).
- **Static vs. Temporal Features:**
 - Injecting static DINOv2 image features (GR00T+PVI@DINOv2) achieved 56.8% average success (+17.0 pp relative to baseline).
 - Injecting temporal V-JEPA2 video features (GR00T+PVI@V-JEPA2) yielded the largest improvement, reaching 69.4% average success (+29.6 pp relative to baseline).
- **Combined Features:** Combining both V-JEPA2 and DINOv2 features (GR00T+PVI@V-JEPA2+DINOv2) resulted in 68.9% average success (+29.1 pp), which was on par with V-JEPA2 alone. This suggests that static appearance features provided minimal additional signal beyond what temporal features already captured.
- **Conclusion:** Temporal features were more critical for closed-loop bimanual manipulation, especially tasks requiring multi-phase coordination and state tracking, providing a consistent advantage over static representations.

#### 4.3. Ablations and Sensitivity Analysis

Ablation studies examined PVI's sensitivity to temporal context length and stabilization choices on 10 simulation tasks.

- **Temporal Context Length:**
 - Varying V-JEPA2 frames (at 4 fps): 2 frames (71.6% avg. success) and 4 frames (71.8%) achieved the best performance.
 - Longer contexts showed diminishing returns: 8 frames yielded 69.4%, and 16 frames resulted in 66.6%.
 - The default 8-frame setting, while effective, was not optimal, suggesting potential for further gains with task-specific tuning.
- **Stabilization and Adaptation:**
 - Freezing the auxiliary-to-DiT projector (PVI@V-JEPA2, 8f) significantly reduced performance to 55.3%, indicating the importance of learning feature alignment.
 - Removing zero initialization (PVI@V-JEPA2, 8f) yielded comparable or slightly higher final success (71.8%), but zero initialization was retained for its guarantee of behavior-preserving initialization and stable integration.

#### 4.4. Multi-Task Scalability

PVI was evaluated in multi-task settings on 20-task and 50-task RoboTwin 2.0 mixtures, with 50 demonstrations per task.

- **20-Task Mixture:** PVI improved average success from 61.15% to 69.15% (+8.00 pp).
- **50-Task Mixture:** PVI showed consistent gains from 61.32% to 63.56% (+2.24 pp), winning on the majority of individual tasks.
- **Conclusion:** Auxiliary visual injection through PVI remained beneficial even when training a shared policy across diverse manipulation primitives.

#### 4.5. Real-World Bimanual Deformable-Object Demonstration

To assess real-world practicality, PVI was deployed on an Airbot dual-arm platform for long-horizon bimanual cloth folding.

- **Task Complexity:** The task involved an eight-stage folding sequence, demanding precise two-arm coordination, contact-rich interaction, and sustained shape reasoning over a deformable garment.
- **Deployment:** The GR00T N1.5 checkpoint, augmented with PVI, was fine-tuned using a single-stage procedure without task-specific engineering.
- **Performance:** The resulting policy successfully executed the entire folding sequence in closed loop, driven by language instructions, without manual resets between subtasks. It demonstrated capabilities in precise pinching, global shape adjustment, symmetric bimanual coordination, contact-rich sliding with tension control, and multi-stage final folding.
- **Conclusion:** This qualitative demonstration provided evidence for PVI's effectiveness and practicality for challenging long-horizon deformable-object manipulation on real bimanual hardware.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Plug-in Visual Injection (PVI) as a method for enhancing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, demonstrating several significant contributions to the field of robotic manipulation:

1. **Lightweight and Non-Invasive VLA Enhancement:** PVI offers a framework for injecting auxiliary visual representations into pretrained VLA models without modifying their core architecture. This design preserves the extensive pretrained knowledge of existing VLM and action expert components, facilitating stable, single-stage fine-tuning. This approach reduces the computational cost and complexity typically associated with adapting VLA models to integrate new visual modalities, making it more accessible for deployment and further research.

2. **Effective Utilization of Fine-Grained Visual Cues:** The work systematically demonstrates that VLMs, while strong in semantic abstraction, often attenuate geometric and temporal cues critical for dexterous manipulation. PVI's mechanism effectively bypasses this bottleneck by directly providing these fine-grained signals to the action expert, resulting in substantial performance improvements on a range of bimanual manipulation tasks.

3. **Validation of Temporal Representations:** Through a controlled study, PVI provides empirical evidence that temporal video representations (e.g., V-JEPA2) consistently yield larger gains in manipulation success compared to strong static image features (e.g., DINOv2). This highlights the importance of temporal dynamics for tasks requiring state tracking, motion progression, contact transitions, and multi-phase coordination, thereby guiding future research towards incorporating time-aware visual inputs. The finding that combining static and temporal features offers little additional benefit beyond temporal features alone further streamlines the choice of auxiliary visual encoders.

4. **Robust Injection Mechanism:** The comparative study against various alternative injection strategies (Concat, ControlNet-style, ReferenceNet-style, ControlVLA-style) indicates that the specific design of PVI—featuring a dual-conditioning trainable copy branch with zero-initialized layer-wise residual injection—is particularly effective. This suggests that the "how" of injecting auxiliary information is as crucial as the "what," providing a proven architectural pattern for stable integration.

5. **Scalability and Real-World Applicability:** PVI's effectiveness extends to multi-task regimes, where it consistently improves shared policies across diverse manipulation primitives. Furthermore, the successful real-robot demonstration of long-horizon bimanual cloth folding validates PVI's practicality and generalization capabilities beyond simulated environments, positioning it as a viable method for real-world robot deployment, especially for complex tasks involving deformable objects and precise coordination.

The potential impact of PVI includes accelerating the development of more capable and generalist robot manipulation systems. By providing a stable and efficient way to integrate rich visual signals, PVI can enable VLA models to tackle more complex and precise tasks, move towards more human-like dexterity, and facilitate easier adaptation to new environments or tasks. This foundational contribution supports the evolution of VLA systems towards higher fidelity visuomotor control by effectively bridging the gap between high-level semantic understanding and low-level physical interaction dynamics.
