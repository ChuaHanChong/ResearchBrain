# LeVERB: Humanoid Whole-Body Control with Latent Vision-Language Instruction

> [!summary] Summary
> LeVERB is a framework designed for humanoid robots to execute agile whole-body actions through latent vision-language instructions, enabling zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. The framework achieved an average 58.5% success rate across diverse tasks in simulation, which is a 7.8 times improvement over a naive hierarchical VLA, and successfully generalized to unseen commands on a Unitree G1 robot.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotics are confined to quasi-static manipulation, limited by simplistic, hand-crafted action vocabularies.
- Humanoid whole-body control (WBC) demands high-dimensional, agile behaviors that traditional VLA interfaces struggle to capture, hindering the deployment of advanced VLA capabilities.
- A significant lack of large-scale, robot-specific visual-language demonstration data impedes the training of effective VLA systems for humanoids.

## Method

![[2506.13751-method.png]]
*The LeVERB training pipeline combines synthetic data generation with hierarchical learning, using a residual CVAE architecture for vision-language alignment and DAgger for action distillation.*

- Developed **LeVERB-Bench**, a scalable pipeline generating photorealistic and dynamically accurate synthetic visual-language humanoid WBC data from human ==MoCap== for training and benchmarking.
- Introduced a ==dual-process hierarchical control== architecture with **LeVERB-VL** (high-level VLA reasoning at 10 Hz) and **LeVERB-A** (low-level reactive WBC at 50 Hz) interconnected by a learned ==latent "verb" vector==.
- Trained LeVERB-VL using a ==residual Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE)== for semantic alignment of vision and language inputs, and LeVERB-A via ==DAgger== to distill actions from teacher policies using samples from the latent distribution.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **58.5%** across **10 diverse task categories** on LeVERB-Bench in simulation, representing a **7.8 times** improvement over a naive hierarchical VLA (**7.5%** success rate).
- Successfully demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a **Unitree G1** humanoid robot, enabling robust responses to varied human language commands and spatial reasoning based on visual cues.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of the adversarial discriminator for cross-modal latent distribution alignment and the residual CVAE architecture for enhanced semantic reasoning and robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning an expressive, structured latent action vocabulary is crucial for VLA models to capture complex humanoid whole-body motions and their semantics, effectively transcending explicit low-dimensional action interfaces.
> - A hierarchical, dual-process architecture effectively decouples high-level semantic reasoning from high-frequency low-level dynamics control, addressing computational challenges and improving sim-to-real robustness.
> - Sampling from the predicted multimodal latent distribution during low-level policy training is essential for the policy to fully capture diverse semantic intentions, preventing a mismatch with the VLA interface distribution.
