# LeVERB: Humanoid Whole-Body Control with Latent Vision-Language Instruction

> [!summary] Summary
> LeVERB is a framework designed for humanoid robots to execute agile whole-body actions through latent vision-language instructions, enabling zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. The framework achieved an average 58.5% success rate across diverse tasks in simulation, which is a 7.8 times improvement over a naive hierarchical VLA, and successfully generalized to unseen commands on a Unitree G1 robot.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotics are confined to quasi-static manipulation, limited by simplistic, hand-crafted action vocabularies.
- Humanoid whole-body control (WBC) demands high-dimensional, agile behaviors that traditional VLA interfaces struggle to capture, hindering the deployment of advanced VLA capabilities.
- A significant lack of large-scale, robot-specific visual-language demonstration data impedes the training of effective VLA systems for humanoids.

## Method

![[2506.13751-method.png]]
*The LeVERB training pipeline combines synthetic data generation with hierarchical learning, using a residual CVAE architecture for vision-language alignment and DAgger for action distillation.*

- Developed **LeVERB-Bench**, a scalable pipeline generating photorealistic and dynamically accurate synthetic visual-language humanoid WBC data from human ==MoCap== for training and benchmarking.
- Introduced a ==dual-process hierarchical control== architecture with **LeVERB-VL** (high-level VLA reasoning at 10 Hz) and **LeVERB-A** (low-level reactive WBC at 50 Hz) interconnected by a learned ==latent "verb" vector==.
- Trained LeVERB-VL using a ==residual Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE)== for semantic alignment of vision and language inputs, and LeVERB-A via ==DAgger== to distill actions from teacher policies using samples from the latent distribution.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **58.5%** across **10 diverse task categories** on LeVERB-Bench in simulation, representing a **7.8 times** improvement over a naive hierarchical VLA (**7.5%** success rate).
- Successfully demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a **Unitree G1** humanoid robot, enabling robust responses to varied human language commands and spatial reasoning based on visual cues.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of the adversarial discriminator for cross-modal latent distribution alignment and the residual CVAE architecture for enhanced semantic reasoning and robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning an expressive, structured latent action vocabulary is crucial for VLA models to capture complex humanoid whole-body motions and their semantics, effectively transcending explicit low-dimensional action interfaces.
> - A hierarchical, dual-process architecture effectively decouples high-level semantic reasoning from high-frequency low-level dynamics control, addressing computational challenges and improving sim-to-real robustness.
> - Sampling from the predicted multimodal latent distribution during low-level policy training is essential for the policy to fully capture diverse semantic intentions, preventing a mismatch with the VLA interface distribution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "LeVERB" operates at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, humanoid robotics, and whole-body control (WBC). Recent advancements in VLA models have enabled impressive semantic understanding and zero-shot generalization in robotics, particularly in tasks like tabletop and mobile manipulation. These models leverage deep learning to bridge high-level perception (vision and language) with low-level control.

However, the authors identify a critical gap: most existing VLA systems for robotics assume an accurate low-level controller with a pre-defined, often hand-crafted, action "vocabulary." This vocabulary typically consists of low-dimensional commands such as end-effector poses or root velocities, confining prior work to quasi-static tasks and preventing the execution of agile, whole-body behaviors essential for humanoid robots. Humanoid robots are inherently high-dimensional, nonlinear dynamic systems, requiring continuous, high-frequency control for stability and agility.

**LeVERB positions itself as a pioneering effort to bridge this gap.** It directly addresses the challenge of applying VLA models to complex humanoid whole-body control by:
- **Moving beyond explicit, low-dimensional action vocabularies:** Prior works often rely on explicit interfaces (e.g., base velocities, end-effector poses), which simplify modularity but limit expressiveness and generalization, leading to unnatural or jittery motions. LeVERB proposes a *learned "latent vocabulary"* that is expressive enough to capture complex whole-body motions and their semantics.
- **Introducing a hierarchical control architecture:** Inspired by human "fast and slow" thinking (cortical high-level reasoning vs. spinal/subcortical reactive control), LeVERB adopts a System-1/System-2 architecture. This allows for high-level (slow) semantic reasoning from vision and language inputs to guide low-level (fast) reactive control based on proprioception. This addresses the high inference latency issue common in large end-to-end VLA models, which is particularly problematic for dynamic humanoid systems.
- **Addressing data scarcity:** A major impediment to VLA training for humanoids is the lack of large-scale, robot-specific visual-language demonstration data. LeVERB tackles this by developing a scalable synthetic data generation pipeline (LeVERB-Bench) that produces photorealistic and dynamically accurate data.
- **Achieving zero-shot sim-to-real transfer:** While much research in humanoid control happens in simulation, transferring these policies to real hardware without fine-tuning (zero-shot) remains a significant challenge. LeVERB explicitly aims for and demonstrates this capability for its vision-language latent interface.

In comparison to existing work:
- **Humanoid WBC:** While physics-based animation and reinforcement learning have achieved impressive motion tracking (e.g., DeepMimic, PULSE), they often rely on privileged simulation states or explicit commands, lacking visual grounding and robust high-level reasoning. LangWBC introduces language-conditioned latent control but lacks visual input.
- **Hierarchical VLA:** While hierarchical VLA (e.g., AGIbot, HAMSTER) has shown promise in manipulation by decoupling high-level and low-level control, prior real-world approaches for dynamic systems (e.g., legged robots) primarily use *explicit* interfaces (e.g., end-effector poses, base velocities). LeVERB is the first to demonstrate a *hierarchical latent architecture* for vision-language-driven whole-body control on real humanoid robots.
- **Humanoid Benchmarks:** Existing benchmarks either focus solely on locomotion, operate in state-space without visuals, or use non-photorealistic renderings, thus failing to provide suitable visual-language data for sim-to-real WBC. LeVERB-Bench fills this void.

Thus, LeVERB marks a significant step forward by bringing advanced VLA capabilities to the challenging domain of agile, visually-grounded humanoid whole-body control with real-world applicability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of the LeVERB project is to empower humanoid robots with the ability to perceive complex environments, understand intuitive language commands, and execute agile, expressive whole-body actions. To achieve this, the authors identify and aim to address several key challenges:

1. **Bridging the Gap in VLA for Humanoids:** The primary motivation stems from the observation that existing VLA models, despite their strengths in semantic understanding and zero-shot generalization, have largely been confined to quasi-static manipulation tasks. They often rely on simplistic, hand-crafted action vocabularies that fail to capture the high-dimensional, nonlinear dynamics and agile whole-body behaviors required by humanoid robots.
2. **Developing an Expressive Latent Action Vocabulary:** A core objective is to move beyond explicit, low-dimensional action interfaces. The authors propose to learn a "latent vocabulary" that is sufficiently expressive to cover a wide range of whole-body motions and effectively capture the semantics encoded in both visual and linguistic inputs. This latent space should serve as a rich, abstract representation of desired robot behaviors.
3. **Integrating a Versatile Whole-Body Control Layer:** Alongside the latent vocabulary, the project aims to develop a robust low-level whole-body control (WBC) layer capable of dynamically translating these latent "verbs" into feasible, dynamics-level motor commands that can be transferred zero-shot to the real world.
4. **Addressing Data Scarcity for Humanoid VLA:** Recognizing the prohibitive cost and complexity of collecting large-scale, robot-specific visual-language data for humanoids, a key objective is to create an efficient and scalable synthetic data generation pipeline. This pipeline must produce photorealistic, physics-based demonstrations suitable for training VLA models and for benchmarking.
5. **Establishing a Sim-to-Real-Ready Benchmark:** To facilitate progress in this underexplored area, the authors aimed to introduce the first sim-to-real-ready, vision-language, closed-loop benchmark for humanoid WBC, comprising a diverse set of tasks and environments.
6. **Developing a Hierarchical, Decoupled Control Architecture:** Motivated by the computational demands of large VLA models and the high-frequency control needs of humanoids, an objective was to design a dual-process architecture. This architecture would decouple high-level (vision-language) reasoning from low-level (dynamics-level) action generation, allowing for efficient training and inference, and reducing the need for computationally expensive photorealistic rendering during frequent control loops.
7. **Enabling Zero-Shot Generalization:** A crucial practical objective is to achieve zero-shot deployment, allowing the trained LeVERB model to generalize to unseen scenarios and real-world humanoid hardware without requiring additional fine-tuning.

In essence, LeVERB seeks to unlock the full potential of VLA models for complex, agile humanoid behaviors by addressing fundamental challenges in data generation, architectural design, and control interface learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LeVERB introduces a novel hierarchical latent instruction-following framework for humanoid vision-language whole-body control, built upon a meticulously designed benchmark and a dual-process control architecture.

#### 3.1. LeVERB-Bench: Synthetic Data Generation and Benchmark

The authors first address the data scarcity problem by introducing **LeVERB-Bench**, a scalable pipeline for synthetic visual-language humanoid WBC data generation and closed-loop benchmarking.
- **Data Generation:** The core innovation is replaying retargeted human MoCap (motion capture) data in IsaacSim. This allows for:
 - Bypassing the need for robust dynamic control during data collection.
 - Leveraging kinematic poses to provide rich task-level semantics for VLA understanding.
 - Supporting future integration of broader human motion data.
- **Photorealistic Rendering:** IsaacSim's ray-tracing capabilities are utilized for accurate scene lighting and shadows, minimizing the sim-to-real gap.
- **Procedural Randomization:** To maximize data diversity from a limited set of MoCap trajectories, a procedural generation pipeline randomizes:
 - Scene backgrounds, object properties (colors, textures).
 - Task setups, including objective and distractor objects.
 - Multiple camera views (egocentric and randomized third-person).
 - Mirroring of rollouts.
- **Language Annotation:** Egocentric text commands are either manually annotated or generated using a Vision-Language Model (VLM).
- **Data Scale:** The pipeline generated 17.1 hours of photorealistic motion rollouts from 154 trajectories (each randomized 100 times). An additional 2.7 hours of "language-only" data (460 trajectories) are augmented by injecting spatial cues into text to compensate for the lack of visual input. This comprehensive dataset covers over 150 tasks across 10 categories.

#### 3.2. Dual-Process Humanoid Control Architecture

LeVERB adopts a dual-process, hierarchical architecture inspired by human cognition (System 1 for fast, reactive control; System 2 for slow, high-level reasoning).

- **Decoupling Vision-Language and Actions:**
 - **LeVERB-VL (System 2):** A high-level vision-language policy that interprets visual and linguistic instructions and operates at a lower frequency (10 Hz).
 - **LeVERB-A (System 1):** A low-level reactive whole-body control (WBC) policy that consumes latent "verbs" from LeVERB-VL and generates dynamics-level actions at a high frequency (50 Hz).
 - A **latent vector `z`** serves as a one-way interface, encoding complex whole-body motion objectives. This decoupling avoids heavy graphical computations at high frequencies, enabling separate training.

#### 3.3. LeVERB-VL Training: Vision-Language-Action Semantic Alignment

The goal of LeVERB-VL is to map vision and language inputs into a structured, semantically meaningful latent space. It uses a **residual Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE)** architecture:
- **VLA Prior ($\rho_{\theta_{VL}}$):** Consists of a frozen SigLiP Vision Transformer (ViT-B/16) for vision encoding (egocentric and third-person views) and a SigLiP text encoder. These embeddings are fed into a Transformer backbone and MLP head to predict the mean ($\mu_\rho$) and variance ($\sigma_\rho$) of the latent distribution based on current vision and language inputs.
- **Kinematics Encoder ($E_\psi$):** An MLP that receives flattened ground-truth future states ($s_{t+1:t+M}$) and predicts its own mean ($\mu_E$) and variance ($\sigma_E$) for the latent distribution. This "privileged" encoder helps the VLA prior focus on semantics by providing motion-specific details.
- **Residual Latent Space:** The final latent distribution for training is constructed with a mean $\mu = \mu_\rho + \mu_E$ and variance $\sigma = \sigma_E$. A KL divergence loss is applied to this posterior to encourage the VLA prior to learn semantic information not inferable from the future states alone.
- **Kinematics Decoder ($D_\psi$):** An MLP that takes the sampled latent $z_t$ and current state $s_t$ to reconstruct the future states ($\hat{s}_{t+1:t+M}$).
- **Discriminator ($f_\psi$):** To align the latent spaces learned from vision-language data and language-only data (where white noise images are used for "blind" trajectories), a discriminator is employed. It takes $z_t$ and predicts if an actual image was present. A Gradient Reversal Layer (GRL) encourages the VLA to produce modality-invariant latents.
- **Training Objective:** Combines trajectory reconstruction loss, KL divergence for distribution alignment, and adversarial classification loss from the discriminator.

#### 3.4. LeVERB-A Training: Distilling Actions with Learned Latent Distribution

After LeVERB-VL is trained and its latent space frozen, LeVERB-A is trained:
- **Teacher Policies ($T_\xi$):** Multiple vision-language-agnostic teacher policies are first trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). These teachers track diverse retargeted kinematic trajectories, receiving *privileged proprioceptive observations* and *reference motions* as commands. Domain randomization is extensively used to enable zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
- **Student Policy ($\tau_{\theta_A}$):** A Transformer-based student policy is trained via DAgger (Dataset Aggregation) to distill actions from the teacher policies.
 - **Input:** Proprioceptive observations (base linear/angular velocity, joint positions/velocities, gravity vector) and a sampled latent code $z_t$ from LeVERB-VL's output distribution (sampled every 5 steps to match the 10 Hz rate of LeVERB-VL).
 - **Latent Sampling:** Critically, LeVERB-A samples $z_t$ from the *predicted latent distribution* ($\mu_{\rho,t}, \sigma_{\rho,t}$), rather than just using the mean. This captures the multimodal mapping between semantics and motions, which is vital for performance.
 - **Loss Function:** Huber loss against the teacher's expert actions.
 - **Deployment:** At inference, LeVERB-A is conditioned on the predicted mean $\mu_\rho$ from LeVERB-VL.

This multi-stage, hierarchical approach, coupled with robust data generation and specific architectural choices like the residual CVAE and latent sampling, forms the backbone of LeVERB's ability to tackle complex humanoid control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates the effectiveness of LeVERB through comprehensive evaluations in simulation using LeVERB-Bench and through real-world zero-shot deployment on a humanoid robot.

#### 4.1. Closed-Loop Evaluation on LeVERB-Bench (Simulation)

LeVERB-Bench offers a rigorous evaluation environment with unseen material and object combinations, closely mimicking real-world deployment scenarios.
- **Overall Performance:** LeVERB achieved an impressive average success rate of **58.5%** across 10 diverse task categories (e.g., visual navigation, sitting, locomotion). This significantly outperforms a naive hierarchical VLA implementation (NS variant), which achieved a mere 7.5% success rate, representing a **7.8 times improvement**.
- **Category-Specific Success:** LeVERB demonstrated high success rates on simpler tasks like "Visual Navigation – Front Objective" (80%) and "Language-Only Locomotion" (100%). Even on challenging tasks like "Visual Navigation – Rear Cluttered" and "Visual Navigation – Sit," LeVERB achieved 25% and 5% success rates, respectively, which were notably higher than most ablated variants.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study Insights

The ablation studies provided critical insights into the contribution of each proposed component:
- **No Discriminator (ND):** Removing the adversarial discriminator led to a significant performance drop, particularly in visual navigation tasks (e.g., 80% to 15% for VNF Objective). This highlights the discriminator's crucial role in aligning the latent vocabulary distributions between vision-language and language-only demonstrations, enabling cross-modal generalization.
- **No Kinematics Encoder (NE):** This variant showed a slight performance decrease (53% average success rate). This suggests that the residual CVAE architecture, where the kinematics encoder helps capture fine-grained motion details, allows the VLA prior to focus more effectively on semantic reasoning, leading to better robustness in unseen scenes.
- **No LeVERB-VL (NVL):** Directly conditioning the low-level controller on visual and language embeddings (bypassing the high-level policy) resulted in minimal success rates on vision-language tasks (e.g., 0% for VNF Objective). This strongly validates the necessity of the VLA planning capacity for tasks involving visual feedback. However, NVL showed excellent performance on language-only locomotion (100%), confirming that simple atomic commands can be learned without complex high-level reasoning.
- **No Low-level Sampling (NLS):** Training LeVERB-A using only the mean of the latent distribution (instead of sampling) led to reduced performance. This confirms that sampling from the learned multimodal latent distribution during LeVERB-A training is essential for the low-level policy to fully capture the diverse semantic intentions encoded by LeVERB-VL, preventing a mismatch in the interface distribution.
- **No Sampling (NS):** This variant, representing a naive hierarchical VLA with deterministic conditioning, largely failed across almost all tasks (7.5% average success rate). This emphasizes the importance of a structured latent space and the ability to sample from it to achieve effective, generalizable control, demonstrating the superiority of the CVAE-based approach.

#### 4.3. Real-World Deployment (Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real)

LeVERB successfully demonstrated dynamics-level zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a **Unitree G1 humanoid robot**.
- **Vocabulary Generalization:** The robot responded robustly to variations in human language commands (e.g., "get up" / "stand up from box," "rest on the box" / "take a seat"), demonstrating the VLA's ability to generalize to unseen verb-object combinations.
- **Spatial Reasoning from Vision:** The robot successfully executed different sit-down maneuvers based on the visual location and orientation of a target chair (e.g., facing the chair vs. chair on the side). This confirmed LeVERB's capacity for spatial reasoning grounded in visual feedback.
- **Open-Loop Latent Replay:** The latent verbs generated by LeVERB-VL in simulation were successfully open-loop replayed to LeVERB-A in the real world, accomplishing the tasks. This is a critical validation of the dynamics-level sim-to-real readiness of LeVERB, paving the way for future full vision-in-the-loop real-world deployments. The system runs with LeVERB-VL on an external PC (10 Hz) and LeVERB-A on the robot's onboard CPU (50 Hz), demonstrating practical real-time operation.

These results collectively establish LeVERB as a robust and pioneering solution for vision-language-driven whole-body control in humanoids, with validated sim-to-real capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LeVERB represents a significant advancement in the field of robotics, particularly for humanoid control and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its contributions and potential impact are multi-faceted:

1. **Pioneering Humanoid VLA-WBC:** LeVERB is presented as the first vision-language latent action model specifically designed for humanoid whole-body control, and critically, the first to demonstrate zero-shot sim-to-real results using a latent vision-language interface on real humanoid hardware. This pushes the boundaries of VLA applications beyond simpler manipulation tasks to the complex, high-dimensional domain of bipedal locomotion and interaction.
2. **Addressing a Critical Research Gap:** By tackling the limitations of existing VLA systems that rely on explicit, low-dimensional action vocabularies, LeVERB enables robots to execute agile, expressive whole-body behaviors. This is a crucial step towards unleashing the full capabilities of humanoid robots in dynamic, unstructured environments.
3. **Scalable and Realistic Data Generation:** The introduction of LeVERB-Bench as a sim-to-real-ready, photorealistic benchmark is a major contribution. It provides a much-needed resource for generating diverse, high-quality, robot-specific visual-language data, which is currently a bottleneck for training sophisticated humanoid VLA models. This benchmark will accelerate research and allow for standardized evaluation.
4. **Novel Hierarchical Control Architecture:** The dual-process (System 1/System 2) architecture with its CVAE-based VLA and latent action vocabulary is an elegant solution to manage the computational demands and disparate control frequencies inherent in complex humanoid tasks. Decoupling high-level semantic reasoning from low-level dynamics control enhances efficiency, robustness, and interpretability.
5. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The use of a structured latent space, a residual CVAE for semantic alignment, and an adversarial discriminator for modality-invariant latent representations significantly improves the model's ability to generalize to unseen visual scenarios and varied language instructions. The validation of latent sampling for LeVERB-A training further ensures that the low-level policy accurately captures the full multimodal semantics.
6. **Practical Real-World Deployability:** The successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a Unitree G1 humanoid robot is a testament to the robustness and practicality of the LeVERB framework. This demonstrates that complex, instruction-driven humanoid behaviors can be achieved in physical robots without extensive real-world data collection or fine-tuning, a significant hurdle in robotics research. This paves the way for humanoids to perform unscripted tasks in human-centric environments.
7. **Foundation for Future Research:** LeVERB opens several exciting avenues for future work, including the integration of long-term planning capabilities, faster vision feedback loops for even more agile and dexterous tasks, and post-training fine-tuning through techniques like reinforcement learning to further refine policy performance and latent vocabulary alignment.

In conclusion, LeVERB represents a landmark achievement in making intelligent humanoid robots more capable and versatile. By seamlessly integrating high-level semantic understanding from vision and language with robust, agile whole-body control, it brings us closer to a future where humanoids can interact intelligently and naturally within complex human environments.
