# VideoBooth: Diffusion-based Video Generation with Image Prompts

> [!summary] Summary
> VideoBooth generates customized videos based on both an image prompt for subject appearance and a text prompt for actions, achieving high fidelity and temporal consistency without requiring per-subject model fine-tuning. The framework demonstrates superior preservation of visual characteristics, scoring highest on CLIP-Image (74.7971) and DINO (65.0979) metrics compared to baseline methods.

## Problem

- Text prompts alone are often insufficient to precisely control the specific visual appearance and intricate details of subjects in generative video models.
- Integrating image prompts into video generation presented challenges in accurately capturing fine visual attributes, maintaining temporal consistency across frames, and generating realistic dynamic movement.
- Existing image-prompt customization methods for generative models often required computationally intensive, per-subject fine-tuning, limiting scalability and on-demand applicability.

## Method

- **VideoBooth** introduces a feed-forward framework that integrates visual information from an image prompt into a pre-trained ==text-to-video diffusion== model.
- A ==coarse-to-fine visual embedding== strategy is employed, using an image encoder for high-level semantics and an ==attention injection== module for fine, multi-scale spatial details.
- A ==sequential training== strategy ensures the coarse embeddings are learned first, followed by the fine attention injection, preventing the fine module from dominating and causing distortions.

## Results

- VideoBooth achieved the highest quantitative scores for image alignment, with a CLIP-Image score of **74.7971** and a DINO score of **65.0979**, outperforming Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, and ELITE baselines.
- A user study confirmed superior subjective quality, with VideoBooth receiving the **highest preference ratios** across image alignment, text alignment, and overall quality.
- Ablation studies validated the efficacy of the coarse-to-fine strategy and sequential training, demonstrating that the full VideoBooth model consistently preserves visual details and maintains temporal consistency in generated videos.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A coarse-to-fine visual embedding approach, combining an image encoder with direct attention injection, is effective for accurately capturing and preserving subject appearance from an image prompt in generated videos.
> - Sequential training of the coarse and fine visual embedding modules is critical; unified training leads to ineffective representations and visual distortions due to the fine module's dominance.
> - Injecting noisy latent representations of the image prompt directly into cross-frame attention modules, especially by refining the first frame's values and propagating them, significantly enhances temporal consistency of the subject's appearance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative AI has seen rapid advancements, particularly in text-to-image synthesis with models like Stable Diffusion. Building upon this, research has increasingly focused on extending generative capabilities to video, leading to the development of text-to-video models. However, a common limitation in both image and video generation has been the precision of content control when relying solely on text prompts. Text descriptions often prove insufficient for accurately depicting specific subject appearances or capturing intricate visual details desired by users, especially for customized content.

To address this, researchers have explored incorporating image prompts into generative models. Existing approaches for text-to-image customization with image prompts generally fall into two categories:
1. **Fine-tuning based methods:** These approaches, exemplified by Textual Inversion and DreamBooth, involve optimizing parts of the model's parameters using a few reference images of the target subject. While capable of producing plausible results, they typically require multiple reference images and involve computationally intensive fine-tuning for each new subject, limiting their scalability and real-world applicability for diverse, on-the-fly customization.
2. **Encoder-based methods:** More recent works, such as ELITE, propose training an encoder to embed image prompts directly into the model, allowing for tuning-free inference. These methods aim to overcome the computational and data demands of fine-tuning.

VideoBooth situates itself within this landscape by extending the encoder-based, tuning-free paradigm to the more complex domain of text-to-video generation with image prompts. This task presents additional challenges, primarily maintaining temporal consistency of the subject's appearance across frames while generating dynamic movements. Direct adaptation of image-based methods to video often results in inconsistent appearances or unnatural motions. VideoBooth aims to address these specific challenges by introducing a novel framework that integrates image prompt information effectively in a feed-forward manner, thereby advancing the capabilities of customized video content creation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the VideoBooth research is to enable the generation of high-quality, customized videos where the appearance of subjects is precisely controlled by an image prompt, in conjunction with a text prompt that describes the desired action or scene. A core aim is to achieve this without requiring model fine-tuning at inference time, distinguishing it from parameter-optimization based methods.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several identified limitations and challenges in current generative video systems:

- **Inadequacy of Text Prompts for Appearance Specification:** Text-driven video generation, despite its progress, often struggles to accurately capture the specific visual appearance of a desired subject. Users frequently find it difficult to articulate every nuanced attribute of an object or character through text alone. Even with extensive descriptive text, models may fail to interpret these attributes precisely. For instance, describing a unique pet or a particular style of car with text can be cumbersome and still yield inaccurate results (as illustrated in Figure 2 of the paper).
- **Need for Direct Visual Control:** Image prompts offer a more intuitive and direct mechanism for users to convey specific visual characteristics. They can complement text prompts by providing rich, detailed information that is challenging to describe textually, thereby enhancing the accuracy of subject appearance in generated content.
- **Challenges in Integrating Image Prompts into Video Generation:** While image prompts have been successfully incorporated into static image generation, extending this to video introduces significant complexities. The primary challenges include:
 1. **Accurate Attribute Capture:** The model must effectively extract and embed the visual attributes from the image prompt into the generated video.
 2. **Temporal Consistency:** Unlike static images, video requires that the subject's appearance remains consistent across multiple frames, even as it moves or its pose changes. Direct adaptation of image-based customization methods often fails to maintain this temporal consistency, leading to flickering or distorted appearances.
 3. **Dynamic Movement:** The generated video must depict realistic and natural dynamic movement of the subject while faithfully preserving its appearance.
- **Demand for a Tuning-Free Framework:** Existing methods that allow for subject customization often require fine-tuning the model for each new subject, which is computationally expensive and time-consuming. This limits their practical utility for widespread, on-demand content creation. The authors are motivated to develop a generalizable, feed-forward framework that can accept a wide range of image prompts without requiring re-training or fine-tuning at inference time.

In summary, VideoBooth is motivated by the need for more precise and intuitive control over subject appearance in video generation, aiming to overcome the limitations of text-only prompts and the computational overhead of per-subject fine-tuning through a novel, feed-forward architectural design.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VideoBooth is a feed-forward framework designed for video generation conditioned on both an image prompt (I) and a text prompt (T). The methodology integrates visual information from the image prompt into a pre-trained text-to-video diffusion model through a coarse-to-fine visual embedding strategy.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Pretrained Text-to-Video Model

The proposed VideoBooth framework is built upon a pre-trained text-to-video diffusion model. This base model typically incorporates several key features for video processing:
- **Inflated 2D Convolution:** Standard 2D convolutions from text-to-image models are extended to 3D convolutions to encode temporal dimensions in video features.
- **Cross-Frame Attention Module:** This module enhances temporal consistency by allowing each frame's features to attend to and reference the first frame and the previous frame. It operates across both spatial and temporal domains.
- **Temporal Attention Module:** An additional module that operates specifically on the temporal domain, enabling attention across all frames to further improve temporal coherence.

#### 3.2. Coarse Visual Embeddings via Image Encoder

This component aims to inject high-level semantic information from the image prompt into the model:
- **Feature Extraction:** A pre-trained CLIP image encoder is used to extract visual features (f_V) from the image prompt (I).
- **Embedding to Text Space:** These visual features (f_V) are then mapped into the text embedding space via a series of Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) layers, producing the image embedding (f_I). This step addresses the discrepancy between CLIP image and text embeddings.
- **Text Embedding Integration:** The image embedding (f_I) is integrated with the text embedding (f_T), obtained from the CLIP text encoder for the text prompt (T). Specifically, f_I replaces the word embeddings corresponding to the target subject within the text prompt. For example, if the text prompt is "Papillon dog celebrates birthday" and the image prompt is a papillon dog, the "papillon dog" word embeddings would be substituted by f_I.
- **Training Strategy:** During this coarse stage of training, the CLIP image encoder's parameters are kept frozen. Only the MLP layers and the Key (K) and Value (V) projection layers within the cross-attention module of the diffusion model (which handle the fused text condition) are fine-tuned. This ensures the model learns to accommodate the combined image-text embeddings.

#### 3.3. Fine Visual Embeddings via Attention Injection

While coarse embeddings provide high-level information, they may lose fine visual details and lack spatial specificity. This component is designed to address this:
- **Latent Representation of Image Prompt:** The image prompt (I) is passed through the VAE encoder of Stable Diffusion to obtain its latent representation (x_I).
- **Noise Application:** To ensure consistency with the diffusion process, which operates on noisy latents, appropriate noise (ϵ) corresponding to the current timestep (t) is added to the clean latent (x_I) to create x_I_t.
- **Cross-Frame Attention Injection:** The noisy latent representation (x_I_t) is injected directly into the cross-frame attention modules of the text-to-video model. This is done by generating additional keys (K_I) and values (V_I) from x_I_t using separate K and V projection layers (initialized from the original K and V projections).
 - **First Frame Refinement:** The values of the first generated frame (V_0) are initially refined using a combination of its own keys (K_0) and values (V_0), along with the keys (K_I) and values (V_I) from the image prompt. The concatenated keys K = [K_I, K_0] and values V = [V_I, V_0] are used in the attention calculation to produce V_new_0.
 - **Temporal Propagation:** For subsequent frames (i > 0), the updated first frame's value (V_new_0) is used along with the previous frame's value (V_i-1) and keys (K_0, K_i-1) to update their respective values (V_new_i). This mechanism ensures that the detailed visual cues from the image prompt, now embedded in the refined first frame, are consistently propagated throughout the entire video sequence.
- **Multi-scale Injection:** The diffusion U-Net has multiple cross-frame attention layers operating at different scales. VideoBooth injects multi-scale latent representations of the image prompt (obtained from different stages of the U-Net) into these corresponding layers, allowing for refinement of details at various granularities.

#### 3.4. Coarse-to-Fine Training Strategy

The two visual embedding modules are trained sequentially:
1. **Stage 1 (Coarse Training):** The model is trained to learn the coarse visual embeddings through the image encoder and fine-tune the cross-attention projections.
2. **Stage 2 (Fine Training):** After Stage 1, the attention injection module is trained to embed the fine visual details.
This sequential approach is critical, as ablation studies indicated that unified training leads to the attention injection module dominating and the image encoder learning ineffective representations, resulting in visual distortions and overfitting.

#### 3.5. VideoBooth Dataset

To support this new task, the authors created the VideoBooth dataset:
- **Source:** A subset of the WebVid dataset, a large text-to-video dataset.
- **Image Prompt Generation:** For each video, the subject from its first frame is segmented using Grounded-SAM (Grounded Segment Anything). Noun chunks from the original text prompt are used as word prompts for Grounded-SAM.
- **Data Filtering:** Small and excessively large segmented objects are filtered out. Videos featuring moving objects (e.g., dogs, cats, cars, elephants) are prioritized using keyword filtering.
- **Size:** The current version comprises 48,724 training video-image prompt pairs and a test benchmark of 650 pairs, all derived from a 2.5 million subset of WebVid.

#### 3.6. Watermark Removal Module (Supplementary)

An additional module, a small U-Net, was developed and fine-tuned on the Vimeo dataset to remove watermarks present in the WebVid dataset. This module is added before the final convolution layer of the base video model and its output is added as a residual to the original features. This module is trained to remove watermarks without affecting the core generative capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides quantitative and qualitative evidence to demonstrate the effectiveness of VideoBooth.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparisons

VideoBooth's performance was quantitatively evaluated against three baseline methods adapted for video generation: Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, and ELITE. The evaluation used three metrics:
- **CLIP-Text:** Measures the alignment between generated videos and text prompts (cosine similarity of CLIP text embeddings of prompts and CLIP image embeddings of frames).
- **CLIP-Image:** Measures the alignment between generated videos and image prompts (cosine similarity of CLIP image embeddings of prompts and generated frames).
- **DINO:** Measures fine-grained image similarity, trained to differentiate objects of the same classes (ViT-S/16 model for feature extraction).

The results, as presented in Table 1, indicate:
- **Image Alignment:** VideoBooth achieved the highest scores for both CLIP-Image (74.7971) and DINO (65.0979), surpassing ELITE (73.7518 CLIP-Image, 58.9522 DINO), DreamBooth, and Textual Inversion. This suggests superior fidelity in preserving the visual characteristics of the image prompts.
- **Text Alignment:** VideoBooth demonstrated comparable performance in CLIP-Text (30.0967) with ELITE (30.0881). DreamBooth exhibited a higher CLIP-Text score, which the authors attribute to its token insertion strategy potentially leading to a stronger text-bias rather than image prompt correlation.

#### 4.2. User Study

A user study involving 25 participants was conducted to assess subjective quality. Users were presented with groups of videos generated by the four methods and asked to rate them based on image alignment, text alignment, and overall quality. The study results (Figure 6) show that:
- VideoBooth achieved the highest user preference ratios across all three dimensions, indicating that users perceived its generated videos as having better alignment with image and text prompts, and higher overall quality compared to the baselines.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Comparisons

Qualitative comparisons, exemplified in Figure 5, illustrate VideoBooth's ability to generate videos that closely match the appearance specified in the image prompt:
- In examples such as a dog, a cat playing with a yellow ball, and a car in a bush, VideoBooth consistently rendered subjects that retained specific details (e.g., fur patterns, facial features, car model, and color) from the image prompt.
- Baseline methods often struggled, with Textual Inversion sometimes failing to embed the subject entirely, and DreamBooth and ELITE showing some coarse appearance transfer but often missing fine details or producing distorted results.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

An ablation study was performed to assess the contribution of VideoBooth's individual components and the coarse-to-fine training strategy (Table 2, Figure 7, Figure A9):
- **Only Coarse Embeddings:** The model could embed the general appearance but missed fine details (e.g., specific dog body patterns differed from the image prompt).
- **Only Fine Embeddings:** Without coarse guidance, the model struggled to maintain temporal consistency. While the first frame might capture the image prompt's appearance, subsequent frames often showed distorted subjects due to overfitting to the initial frame and insufficient propagation of appearance. In some cases, even the first frame failed to embed the image prompt accurately.
- **Unified Training (Coarse and Fine together):** Training both modules simultaneously resulted in the fine attention injection module dominating, causing the coarse image encoder to learn ineffective representations. This led to behavior similar to "Only Fine Embeddings," where the model overfitted or generated distorted subjects, particularly with larger motions.
- **Full Model (Coarse-to-Fine Training):** The complete VideoBooth framework, utilizing the sequential coarse-to-fine training, achieved the best performance in both quantitative metrics and visual quality. It consistently preserved all visual details from the image prompt and maintained temporal consistency across frames.

These findings collectively support the conclusion that VideoBooth effectively leverages both coarse and fine visual embeddings, combined with a dedicated training strategy, to generate customized, high-quality videos that accurately reflect image prompts while maintaining temporal coherence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VideoBooth framework presents several significant contributions to the field of generative artificial intelligence and holds potential for various applications.

#### 5.1. Technical Advancements

- **Tuning-Free Image-Prompted Video Generation:** The core technical achievement is enabling high-fidelity video generation from an image prompt without requiring subject-specific fine-tuning at inference time. This is a notable departure from previous optimization-based methods, offering a more efficient and scalable solution.
- **Coarse-to-Fine Visual Embedding Strategy:** The proposed two-stage, coarse-to-fine approach for embedding image prompt information is a novel architectural design. The combination of an image encoder for high-level semantic capture and an attention injection module for multi-scale, spatial detail refinement addresses the dual challenges of appearance fidelity and temporal consistency in video generation.
- **Dedicated Dataset Development:** The creation of the VideoBooth dataset specifically tailored for image-prompted video generation fills a data gap and provides a valuable resource for future research and benchmarking in this emerging task.
- **Improved Temporal Consistency:** The attention injection mechanism, particularly the method of updating the first frame's values with image prompt information and then propagating this to subsequent frames, directly contributes to maintaining consistent subject appearance throughout the video.

#### 5.2. Practical Utility and Applications

- **Customized Content Creation:** VideoBooth provides a powerful tool for users to generate videos featuring specific subjects (e.g., personal pets, unique objects, custom characters) with high visual fidelity. This capability has wide-ranging applications in marketing, entertainment, personalized storytelling, and social media content creation, where precise control over subject appearance is critical.
- **Enhanced User Control:** By offering image prompts as a direct control mechanism, VideoBooth makes video generation more intuitive and accessible for users who may struggle to articulate complex visual details through text alone.
- **Efficiency and Scalability:** The tuning-free, feed-forward nature of the model means that a single VideoBooth model can generate videos for an arbitrary number of diverse image prompts without re-training. This significantly reduces computational costs and turnaround times, making personalized video generation more practical for large-scale use.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework and insights gained from VideoBooth regarding multi-modal integration and coarse-to-fine strategies could inform the development of more advanced generative models capable of handling increasingly complex and precise user controls.

#### 5.3. Potential Negative Societal Impacts

The authors acknowledge a potential negative societal impact:
- **Generation of Fake Videos:** Like many powerful generative AI models, VideoBooth could be misused to create deepfakes or other deceptive video content. The authors suggest that advancements in fake video detection methods might offer a partial mitigation to this risk. This highlights the ongoing need for responsible development and deployment of generative technologies, alongside parallel efforts in detection and ethical guidelines.

Overall, VideoBooth represents a significant step forward in making personalized and controlled video generation more accessible and capable, with implications for a wide array of creative and commercial applications, while also necessitating consideration of its ethical dimensions.
