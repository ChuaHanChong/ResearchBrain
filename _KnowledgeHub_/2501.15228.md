# Improving Retrieval-Augmented Generation through Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Renmin University of China, Baidu Inc., and Carnegie Mellon University developed MMOA-RAG, a framework that models Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipelines as cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning tasks to jointly optimize multiple interacting components. This approach significantly enhances the accuracy and factuality of generated answers on question-answering benchmarks, surpassing existing RAG optimization methods.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often generate outdated or fabricated information due to their static internal knowledge post-pre-training.
- Traditional RAG systems optimize individual components independently, leading to 'misalignment' where local objectives do not perfectly serve the global goal of generating accurate answers.
- Existing end-to-end RAG optimization methods using reinforcement learning are often limited to simplified pipelines, failing to capture complex interdependencies among multiple interacting components.

## Method

- The **MMOA-RAG** framework re-conceptualizes the RAG pipeline as a ==Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning== (Co-MARL) problem.
- Trainable RAG components (Query Rewriter, Selector, Generator) are designated as independent yet collaborative RL agents.
- These agents are jointly optimized using Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (MAPPO) with a shared global reward based on the F1 score of the final answer.

## Results

- MMOA-RAG consistently achieved superior performance on HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, and AmbigQA datasets, showing improvements of up to +**1.80** F1 score over the best baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the joint optimization of all three agents (Query Rewriter, Selector, Generator) is necessary for achieving the highest RAG system performance.
- The framework demonstrated strong generalizability across different RAG configurations and robustness in out-of-domain scenarios and when integrated with various retriever models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Viewing the RAG pipeline as a multi-agent cooperative task enables holistic optimization, effectively aligning the objectives of individual modules towards a unified goal.
> - Jointly optimizing all key RAG modules (Query Rewriter, Selector, Generator) leads to greater system efficacy than optimizing components in isolation or in simplified two-stage settings.
> - A warm-start with supervised fine-tuning before Multi-Agent RL is crucial for stabilizing training and reducing the exploration space for agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have revolutionized natural language processing, excelling in tasks like question answering, information retrieval, and various forms of reasoning. However, a significant limitation of LLMs is their inability to update internal knowledge post-pre-training, leading to the generation of outdated or fabricated information, commonly known as "hallucinations."

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) emerged as a powerful paradigm to address this by enabling LLMs to retrieve relevant, up-to-date information from external knowledge sources before generating responses. A typical RAG system is not monolithic but rather a complex pipeline comprising multiple interconnected modules, such as query rewriting, first-stage retrieval, re-ranking, document preprocessing (selection/filtering), and answer generation.

The existing research landscape for optimizing RAG systems primarily faces two challenges:
1. **Module-wise Optimization:** Traditional approaches optimize each RAG component independently through supervised fine-tuning (SFT) using human-annotated data. While effective for individual modules, this often leads to a "misalignment" problem where the local objective of a component (e.g., retrieving relevant documents) does not perfectly align with the global objective of the entire system (generating an accurate final answer). A document highly relevant by a retriever's metric might not ultimately contribute to a better answer.
2. **Limited Scope of End-to-End Optimization:** Recent efforts have explored reinforcement learning (RL) for end-to-end optimization of RAG systems, aiming to propagate rewards from the final output back to intermediate modules. However, many of these approaches focus on simplified pipelines, often with only two components (e.g., retriever and generator). More sophisticated methods (like DPO or PPO) often concentrate on optimizing individual RAG modules in isolation or don't adequately model the complex interdependencies and collaborative dynamics among multiple interacting components. Effectively capturing these intricate interdependencies and achieving truly joint optimization for complex RAG architectures remains an open research challenge.

This paper addresses this critical gap by proposing a novel framework that views the entire RAG pipeline as a *multi-agent cooperative task*. By treating each trainable component as an independent yet collaborative RL agent, the authors aim to achieve comprehensive joint optimization, thereby overcoming the limitations of independent or limited end-to-end optimization strategies. This moves beyond optimizing single components or simple two-stage pipelines towards a holistic, cooperative learning approach for complex RAG systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research is to overcome the inherent misalignment issues and suboptimal performance that arise from independently optimizing individual components within complex Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipelines. Traditional methods, relying on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for each module, often fail to ensure that the local objectives of components (e.g., retrieval relevance) truly contribute to the overarching goal of generating high-quality, factual answers.

The key objectives of this work are:
1. **Model Complex RAG as a Multi-Agent Cooperative Task:** To conceptualize the entire RAG pipeline, which comprises multiple interconnected modules, as a cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning (Co-MARL) problem. This involves treating each trainable module (such as query rewriter, document selector, and answer generator) as an individual reinforcement learning agent.
2. **Develop a Joint Optimization Algorithm:** To design and implement a multi-agent reinforcement learning algorithm, specifically leveraging Multi-Agent PPO (MAPPO), that can jointly optimize all these interconnected agents. The goal is to harmonize their individual objectives towards a unified, shared reward signal (e.g., the F1 score of the final answer).
3. **Enhance RAG Performance and Factuality:** Through this joint optimization, to significantly boost the overall performance of the RAG system in question-answering tasks, leading to more accurate and factual answers compared to existing baseline optimization methods.
4. **Demonstrate Generality and Robustness:** To empirically validate the effectiveness, necessity, and generalizability of the proposed multi-agent joint optimization framework across various RAG pipeline configurations and different QA benchmarks, as well as its robustness in out-of-domain scenarios.

In essence, the authors aim to build a more intelligent and collaborative RAG system where components "learn to work together" seamlessly, driven by the ultimate goal of producing the best possible answer, rather than optimizing for fragmented, local metrics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, named Multi-Module joint Optimization Algorithm for RAG (MMOA-RAG), reconceptualizes the RAG pipeline as a Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (Co-MARL) problem.

#### 3.1. Modeling RAG as Co-MARL

- **Agents (G):** The framework designates three key modules as RL agents: the Query Rewriter (QR), the Selector (S), and the Generator (G). These are typically implemented using Large Language Models (LLMs) and thus are trainable.
- **Environment Component:** The Retriever module is treated as a fixed part of the environment, not an RL agent, to optimize computational efficiency, though its output significantly influences the downstream agents.
- **Shared Reward (R):** All agents in the fully cooperative setting work together to maximize a single, shared global reward, which is defined as the F1 score of the final answer generated by the Generator against the ground truth.
- **Observation (O) and Action (A):** Each agent has its specific observation space (input prompt, question, documents) and action space (LLM vocabulary for generation, or constrained vocabulary for selection).

#### 3.2. MMOA-RAG Architecture

The RAG pipeline consists of four main modules:
1. **Query Rewriter (QR):** Takes the initial question `q` and reformulates it into a set of sub-questions `subq` (e.g., for multi-hop reasoning). Its observation `O_QR` includes its prompt and `q`. Its action space `A_QR` is the LLM vocabulary.
2. **Retriever:** (Fixed component) Retrieves a set of `K` candidate documents `D` from a corpus for each sub-question.
3. **Selector (S):** Filters `D` to produce a more refined subset `D_selected` useful for answering `q`. Its observation `O_S` includes its prompt, `q`, and `D`. Its action space `A_S` is constrained to output document IDs (e.g., "Document0, Document3").
4. **Generator (G):** Uses `q` and `D_selected` to generate the final predicted answer `Ans_predict`. Its observation `O_G` includes its prompt, `q`, and `D_selected`. Its action space `A_G` is the LLM vocabulary.

#### 3.3. Reward Function Design

The reward for each agent `i` (`R_i`) is a combination of the `R_shared` (F1 score of the final answer) and an individual penalty term `P_i`: `R_i = R_shared + P_i`.
- **Query Rewriter (QR):** `P_QR` penalizes generating an excessive number of sub-questions (e.g., >4).
- **Selector (S):** `P_S` penalizes duplicate document IDs or malformed output.
- **Generator (G):** `P_G` penalizes excessively long generated answers.
These penalties are mostly zero unless specific undesirable behaviors occur, ensuring stability and guiding the agents while primarily relying on the shared F1 score.

#### 3.4. Training Process

The training process involves two main stages:
1. **Warm Start with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - Before Multi-Agent RL, each trainable module (QR, S, G) undergoes SFT to ensure the underlying LLM can follow instructions for its specific task and to reduce the exploration space for MARL.
 - **QR SFT Data:** Uses publicly available query rewriting data (e.g., from Rewrite-Retrieve-Read).
 - **Selector SFT Data:** A novel heuristic approach is used: words from the question and golden answer are extracted. If any of these words appear in a candidate document, its ID is labeled as useful. This provides a cost-effective way to generate SFT labels for the Selector.
 - **Generator SFT Data:** Golden answers are used as ground truth for training.
 - All modules share the same Llama-3-8B-Instruct LLM, applying parameter sharing to reduce computational overhead.
2. **Multi-Agent Optimization with MAPPO:**
 - After SFT, the RAG system is further optimized using the Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (MAPPO) algorithm.
 - **Actor-Critic Architecture:** PPO's Actor-Critic setup is used, with an Actor model (`θ`) generating actions and a Critic model (`ϕ`) estimating state-value functions. An SFT model (`θ_SFT`) serves as a baseline.
 - **Loss Function:** The overall loss `L(θ, ϕ)` combines Actor loss `L_Actor(θ)` and Critic loss `L_Critic(ϕ)`.
 - `L_Actor(θ)` is a clipped objective function, similar to PPO, using an importance sampling ratio and Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) for advantage function estimation.
 - The final reward `R(s_i, a_i)` for the Actor loss incorporates the `R_i` (shared F1 + penalty) and a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence penalty term (similar to InstructGPT) to prevent policies from drifting too far from the SFT baseline.
 - `L_Critic(ϕ)` uses a clipped squared error loss for value function approximation.
 - **Parallel Minibatch:** Training is accelerated by processing minibatches in parallel.

This comprehensive approach allows for end-to-end optimization of multiple interacting RAG components, ensuring their objectives are collaboratively aligned towards maximizing the quality of the final generated answer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on three public QA datasets (HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, and AmbigQA) using Llama-3-8B-Instruct as the backbone LLM yielded several significant findings:

**RQ1: How does MMOA-RAG perform compared to existing RAG optimization methods?**
- **Superior Performance:** MMOA-RAG consistently achieved the best performance across all evaluated metrics (Accuracy, Exact Match, F1 score) and datasets. For instance, on HotpotQA, MMOA-RAG showed improvements of +1.12 Acc, +1.55 EM, and +1.80 F1 over the best baseline (RetRobust or Rewrite-Retrieve-Read). Similar gains were observed on 2WikiMultihopQA and AmbigQA, demonstrating its overall effectiveness.
- **SFT vs. No-Train RAG:** Vanilla RAG with SFT significantly outperformed Vanilla RAG without training, indicating that fine-tuning is crucial for LLMs to effectively utilize external knowledge in RAG.
- **Beyond Individual RL Components:** Methods like Rewrite-Retrieve-Read (optimizing Query Rewriter with PPO) and BGM (optimizing a bridge/selector with PPO) showed improvements over SFT-only RAG. However, MMOA-RAG, which jointly optimizes Query Rewriter, Selector, and Generator, substantially surpassed these baselines, highlighting the benefits of multi-module joint optimization.
- **Robustness to Different Retrievers and Out-of-Domain Scenarios:** MMOA-RAG also outperformed most baselines when integrated with different retrievers (BGE and E5) and demonstrated superior generalization capabilities in out-of-domain experiments (trained on HotpotQA, tested on AmbigQA), underscoring its robustness and adaptability.

**RQ2: How does the joint optimization of individual modules in the RAG pipeline contribute to the effectiveness of MMOA-RAG framework?**
- **Necessity of Joint Optimization:** Ablation studies confirmed that the complete MMOA-RAG, where all three agents (Query Rewriter, Selector, Generator) are jointly optimized, achieved the highest performance. This directly validates the core hypothesis that multi-agent joint optimization is critical for RAG system effectiveness.
- **Agent Contribution Analysis:**
 - Excluding any single agent from joint optimization (e.g., "MMOA-RAG w/o G") led to a performance decrease, indicating that each module contributes to the overall system efficacy.
 - "MMOA-RAG w/o S" (Selector excluded) performed best among the ablation variants. The authors suggest this might be because the Generator, through joint optimization, develops some "denoising capabilities," making it less reliant on an optimized Selector.
 - For the single-hop AmbigQA dataset, the Generator module was identified as the most critical component, as its exclusion led to the poorest performance and slowest training convergence.
- **Faster Convergence:** The training reward curve showed that MMOA-RAG (with all agents jointly optimized) achieved the fastest convergence rate and the highest final reward value, indicating efficient and effective learning.

**RQ3: Can MMOA-RAG exhibit generalizability across different RAG systems?**
- **Adaptability to Varying Configurations:** Experiments on RAG systems with different numbers of agents (QR+S+G, S+G, QR+G) demonstrated MMOA-RAG's strong generalizability. In all configurations, joint optimization using MAPPO consistently outperformed systems trained only with SFT, with performance gains (`∆`) ranging from 1.37 to 3.98 F1 percentage points.
- **Greater Gains on Multi-Hop Datasets:** The performance improvements from MAPPO over SFT were more pronounced on multi-hop datasets (HotpotQA and 2WikiMultihopQA, around 3 percentage points) compared to the single-hop AmbigQA dataset (around 2 percentage points). This suggests that multi-module joint optimization is particularly beneficial for complex, multi-hop question-answering tasks where inter-module misalignment might be more severe.

A qualitative case study further illustrated these findings, showing that the MAPPO-trained system effectively decomposed a complex query, retrieved the correct document, and generated an accurate answer, whereas the SFT-only system failed to do so due to poor document selection and an incorrect final answer.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper, "Improving Retrieval-Augmented Generation through Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning," represents a significant step forward in the optimization of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems. Its primary significance lies in its innovative conceptualization and robust empirical validation of a multi-agent cooperative learning framework for complex RAG pipelines.

**Key Contributions and Novelty:**
- **Pioneering Multi-Agent RAG Optimization:** The most notable contribution is the novel modeling of a complex RAG pipeline, comprising multiple distinct components (Query Rewriter, Selector, Generator), as a multi-agent collaborative task. This moves beyond the prevalent practice of optimizing components in isolation or in simplified two-stage settings.
- **Holistic Optimization:** By employing Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (specifically MAPPO) with a shared global reward, the paper provides a powerful mechanism to align the objectives of all trainable modules towards the ultimate goal of generating accurate answers. This addresses the long-standing challenge of "misalignment" in modular RAG systems.
- **Empirical Validation:** The extensive experiments across various datasets, ablation studies, and generality tests rigorously demonstrate the effectiveness, necessity, and adaptability of the MMOA-RAG framework. The consistent outperformance of strong baselines underscores its practical utility.
- **Enhanced Performance and Robustness:** The results show tangible improvements in answer accuracy (F1, EM, Acc) and demonstrate the framework's robustness across different retriever models and in out-of-domain scenarios, suggesting a more reliable and generalizable RAG system.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advancing RAG Research:** This work opens new avenues for RAG system design and optimization, pushing the boundaries beyond simple pipeline stages. It establishes a strong case for viewing RAG as an intricate ecosystem of collaborating intelligent agents, inspiring future research into more complex interactions and decision-making within such systems.
- **More Factual and Reliable LLM Applications:** By improving the factuality and reducing hallucinations in LLM-powered question answering, MMOA-RAG has direct implications for building more trustworthy AI applications in critical domains such as scientific research, education, customer service, and knowledge management, where accuracy is paramount.
- **Complex Information Seeking:** The framework's particular effectiveness in multi-hop question answering suggests its high value for tasks requiring intricate information synthesis from multiple sources, which is common in professional and academic contexts.
- **Blueprint for Agentic AI Systems:** The multi-agent approach in MMOA-RAG serves as a compelling example for developing other agentic AI systems where multiple specialized models need to coordinate towards a common goal. This could extend to areas beyond RAG, such as complex reasoning, planning, and task execution in diverse AI domains.

**Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several exciting avenues for future work, including applying MMOA-RAG to even more complex RAG workflows structured as directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) or dynamic workflows. Critically, they highlight the need to incorporate cost and latency considerations into the reward function, moving beyond purely effectiveness-based metrics. This would enable the development of "partially cooperative" RAG scenarios that balance performance with practical constraints, further enhancing the real-world applicability of this innovative framework.

In conclusion, "Improving Retrieval-Augmented Generation through Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning" offers a robust and conceptually elegant solution to a fundamental challenge in RAG optimization. Its multi-agent approach provides a powerful paradigm for building more intelligent, collaborative, and ultimately, more effective and reliable Retrieval-Augmented Generation systems.
