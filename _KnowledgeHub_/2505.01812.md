# $\textit{New News}$: System-2 Fine-tuning for Robust Integration of New Knowledge

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Harvard University and NTT Research developed the New News dataset and System-2 Fine-tuning (Sys2-FT) protocols to enable Large Language Models to robustly internalize new, plausible information into their model weights. This approach significantly bridges the gap between naive fine-tuning and in-context learning, with the Self-QA protocol achieving near in-context learning performance in quantitative domains while preserving general model capabilities.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle to internalize new information into their permanent weights, exhibiting a substantial "fine-tuning to in-context learning gap" (FT-ICL gap).
- Existing knowledge editing methods often focus on isolated factual corrections and can introduce unintended "ripple effects," lacking a holistic approach for complex, plausible knowledge updates.
- The absence of robust in-weight knowledge integration limits LLMs' adaptability and continuous learning capabilities in dynamic environments.

## Method

- Introduced the =="New News" dataset==, comprising **75** manually curated, hypothetical yet plausible news items across **5** diverse domains, each with **5** downstream evaluation questions.
- Developed ==System-2 Fine-tuning (Sys2-FT) protocols== (==Paraphrase==, ==Implication==, ==Self-QA==) which leverage the LLM's own ==in-context learning== ability to generate diverse "replay elements" (synthetic data) from the news.
- Fine-tuned models using ==LoRA== on these generated conversation-formatted replay elements, where the ==Self-QA protocol== involves the model generating questions and then answering them based on the provided news.

## Results

- **Sys2-FT** protocols, especially the Self-QA method, significantly improved models' ability to internalize new knowledge, often achieving **near-ICL performance** in quantitative domains like mathematics and coding.
- Sys2-FT largely preserved the models' general capabilities (MMLU-Pro), mathematical reasoning (GSM8K), and instruction-following abilities (IFEval), with only minor degradation in specific cases.
- The study quantitatively confirmed a substantial "FT-ICL gap," showing that naive fine-tuning performed poorly compared to in-context learning for integrating new information.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A "Contextual Shadowing Effect" was discovered, where explicitly including the original news during Sys2-FT data generation catastrophically degrades the learning signal for in-weight integration.
> - The "Curse of Overexposure" suggests that excessive fine-tuning on new information can sometimes degrade the model's in-context learning ability for that same knowledge.
> - Preliminary evidence indicates a compute-dependent scaling law for Sys2-FT, where larger models are more sample-efficient learners, requiring less effective data to achieve comparable performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a critical challenge in the development of robust and continuously learning Large Language Models (LLMs): the effective integration of new information into their model weights, rather than merely processing it temporarily through in-context learning (ICL). The paper positions itself within several established research areas while highlighting a unique focus:

- **Knowledge Editing:** The authors acknowledge a shared motivation with knowledge editing (e.g., ROME, MEND) in updating models' factual understanding. However, they draw a key distinction: traditional knowledge editing often focuses on precisely altering specific subject-relation-object facts or counterfactual information. In contrast, this work aims to integrate *new, non-counterfactual, and plausible* information (dubbed "news") and its complex downstream implications into the model's broader "world model." This makes the task more akin to human belief updating and less about isolated factual correction, thereby avoiding common pitfalls of knowledge editing like "ripple effects" and incoherent reasoning.
- **Belief Update:** The paper connects to the growing body of work suggesting LLMs possess internal "world model representations" and "beliefs." While some prior work has attempted to revise beliefs using simpler counterfactuals or architecture-dependent edits, this paper offers an *architecture-agnostic* method (fine-tuning) for integrating plausible, complex new scenarios, moving beyond simple factual changes to a more holistic update of the model's understanding.
- **Knowledge Distillation:** The concept of "System-2 Fine-tuning" (Sys2-FT) is presented as a form of knowledge distillation, particularly "context distillation." This involves distilling knowledge from a model that has processed information in-context into its weights. The Sys2-FT protocols, especially Implication and Self-QA, are described as more aggressive data augmentation schemes compared to previous context distillation works, aiming for a deeper and more robust internalization of knowledge.
- **In-context Learning (ICL) vs. Fine-tuning (FT):** A core tension explored in the paper is the "FT-ICL gap." While LLMs excel at ICL, efficiently processing new information when explicitly given in the prompt, they struggle to consolidate this knowledge into their permanent weights through naive fine-tuning. This paper systematically compares these two paradigms and proposes Sys2-FT as a method to significantly improve the performance of fine-tuning, aiming to bridge this gap. This contributes to the ongoing debate about the strengths and weaknesses of ICL versus FT for various tasks and knowledge acquisition.

Overall, this work carves out a niche by focusing on the robust, continuous, and generalizable integration of *plausible new knowledge* into LLM weights, a capability crucial for models to adapt to a continuously changing world, much like intelligent biological agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to systematically study and improve the ability of Large Language Models (LLMs) to internalize new information ("news") into their model weights, making it a permanent part of their knowledge base. This contrasts with the temporary understanding achieved through in-context learning (ICL), where knowledge is only available as long as it's present in the input prompt.

The key motivations driving this research are:

- **Mimicking Human Intelligence:** Humans and intelligent animals possess a fundamental cognitive ability to internalize new information, accurately understand its implications, and update their world models accordingly to perform downstream tasks. The authors argue that while LLMs demonstrate impressive ICL abilities, the challenge of consolidating this new knowledge into their weights is a "hallmark of general intelligence" that current models struggle with.
- **Addressing the FT-ICL Gap:** The paper identifies a significant "fine-tuning to in-context learning gap" (FT-ICL gap). This gap highlights that simply fine-tuning a model on new information often yields poor performance compared to providing the same information directly in the context. The motivation is to reduce or eliminate this disparity, enabling LLMs to learn and retain new knowledge more effectively through fine-tuning.
- **Developing Robust Knowledge Integration Methods:** Previous work on knowledge injection via fine-tuning has often been unreliable. The authors aim to "set up the groundwork" for systematically studying this problem and developing robust, architecture-agnostic methods that enable models to appropriately internalize new, complex, and plausible information, rather than just isolated facts.
- **Enhancing LLM Adaptability and Continuity:** In a dynamic world, LLMs need to continuously learn and adapt their beliefs. Without robust in-weight learning, models would inevitably lose all understanding of new information once it is removed from the context. This research seeks to pave the way for LLMs that can efficiently learn and adapt to changing environments, fostering continuous learning.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Approaches:** Current knowledge editing techniques often suffer from ripple effects or ill-defined target effects for complex updates. This work seeks a more natural and comprehensive approach to knowledge integration, inspired by human memory consolidation processes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a novel dataset and a suite of fine-tuning protocols to address the challenge of robustly integrating new knowledge into LLM weights.

#### 3.1. The New News Dataset

To systematically measure knowledge integration, the authors created **New News**, a dataset designed to evaluate a model's ability to internalize hypothetical yet plausible and non-counterfactual information.
- **Composition:** The dataset comprises 75 unique "news" items, each accompanied by 5 downstream evaluation questions (totaling 375 questions).
- **Domains:** The news spans five diverse domains:
 - **Mathematics:** Introducing novel mathematical concepts (e.g., a new operation) and testing their logical application.
 - **Coding:** Describing API updates for common programming packages and probing their usage.
 - **Discoveries:** Presenting belief-changing scientific or historical findings.
 - **Leaderboards:** Announcing record-breaking achievements in sports, music, or geography.
 - **Events:** Reporting plausible political or world events.
- **Design Philosophy:** The news items are manually curated to be simple yet possess non-trivial downstream implications, whose correct answers critically depend on the model having properly internalized the news. This design distinguishes it from simple factual edits, focusing on complex reasoning. The manual curation process was chosen after finding LLMs unreliable for generating suitable news and questions.

#### 3.2. System-2 Fine-tuning (Sys2-FT) Protocols

Inspired by human memory consolidation processes (rehearsal, rephrasing, self-explanation, schema integration), the authors propose **System-2 Fine-tuning (Sys2-FT)**. This general approach leverages the LLM's own in-context learning abilities to generate "replay elements" or synthetic data, which are then used for fine-tuning.

- **General Approach:** Given original "news" data, the model is prompted to generate diverse, relevant information (replay elements) such as paraphrases, implications, or question-answer pairs. These generated data points are then formatted into conversations for fine-tuning.
- **Specific Protocols Explored:**
 - **Paraphrase Protocol:** The model is prompted to generate multiple rephrased versions of the news in a sequential, multi-turn conversation. This aims to enhance the diversity of representation for the new knowledge.
 - **Implication Protocol:** The model is prompted to reason about and generate various implications or consequences of the given news. This encourages deeper processing and understanding of the news's broader impact.
 - **Self-QA Protocol:** This two-stage process first prompts the model (with the news in context) to generate multiple questions related to the news. In the second stage, the model (again with the news in context) generates answers to these self-generated questions. The resulting QA pairs serve as fine-tuning data, aiming to capture both the understanding of the news and its relevant consequences.
- **Data Generation Details:** For each news item, 1024 conversations of replay elements are generated, resulting in 15,360 conversations per data split. Diverse system, user, and assistant prompts are used during generation to enhance variability.
- **Fine-tuning Procedure:**
 - **Models:** The Qwen 2.5 family of models (from 0.5B to 32B parameters) was primarily used, with additional experiments on Llama 3.1 8B.
 - **Format:** The generated replay elements are arranged in a conversation format for fine-tuning. The loss is computed only on the assistant's tokens, following standard supervised fine-tuning.
 - **Hyperparameters:** Standard LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) was used with specific settings (e.g., r=16, alpha=32). Training typically ran for 4 epochs, saving 80 checkpoints.
 - **Naive FT Baseline:** A baseline of "naive fine-tuning" was established by prompting the model to output the news itself, using a simpler conversation format.

#### 3.3. Evaluation

- **Downstream QA Accuracy:** Models are evaluated on the 375 downstream questions from the New News dataset.
- **Robustness:** Each question is evaluated 5 times with randomized answer options to mitigate positional biases.
- **Best Checkpoint Selection:** For each fine-tuning run, the best performing checkpoint (not necessarily the final one) is selected for reporting, ensuring an accurate representation of peak performance.
- **General Capability Preservation:** Models' general knowledge (MMLU-Pro), mathematical reasoning (GSM8K), and instruction-following abilities (IFEval) are assessed after Sys2-FT to ensure that new knowledge integration does not catastrophically degrade pre-existing capabilities.
- **Continual Learning/Forgetting:** Experiments were conducted to evaluate how much previously learned knowledge is retained when the model is continually fine-tuned on new news categories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several significant findings regarding knowledge integration in LLMs:

#### 4.1. The Fine-tuning to In-Context Learning (FT-ICL) Gap

The research consistently demonstrated a substantial gap between the performance of naive fine-tuning (FT) and in-context learning (ICL) across all model sizes and data domains in the New News dataset. While larger models (3B-32B) showed high ICL accuracy, their performance plummeted with naive fine-tuning, confirming the challenge of consolidating new knowledge into model weights. Smaller models (0.5B, 1.5B) struggled with ICL, indicating limitations in their reasoning capabilities over hypothetical scenarios.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness of System-2 Fine-tuning (Sys2-FT)

- **Bridging the Gap:** Sys2-FT protocols significantly improved models' ability to internalize new knowledge compared to naive fine-tuning.
- **Self-QA's Superiority:** Among the explored protocols (Paraphrase, Implication, Self-QA), the Self-QA protocol emerged as consistently the strongest and most robust method. For larger models, Self-QA sometimes achieved near-ICL performance, particularly in quantitative domains like mathematics and coding, while also preserving general capabilities.
- **Domain Specificity:** Quantitative domains (Math and Coding) benefited most significantly from Sys2-FT, suggesting that structured, logic-heavy information is more amenable to this form of in-weight learning.

#### 4.3. Contextual Shadowing Effect and the Curse of Overexposure

The study identified two surprising and detrimental interactions between FT and ICL:

- **Contextual Shadowing Effect:** When the original news was explicitly prefixed to the Sys2-FT generated data during training (i.e., the news was given in-context *before* the replay elements), it catastrophically degraded the learning signal. This phenomenon was robust across all Sys2-FT protocols and model scales. The authors hypothesize that if models can effectively use the news via ICL in the prompt, the learning signal from the subsequent answer tokens is "shadowed away" as it's no longer surprising or novel to the model. This has significant implications for how training data, especially documents where concepts are introduced and then applied, should be structured.
- **Curse of Overexposure:** In some training runs, as models improved at answering news-related questions through fine-tuning, their *in-context learning ability* on the same news degraded. This suggests a potential trade-off where overexposure to news during FT might negatively impact the model's ICL circuits, although prolonged training sometimes mitigated this effect.

#### 4.4. General Capability Preservation and Continual Learning

- **Preservation of General Capabilities:** Self-QA Sys2-FT largely preserved the models' pre-existing general knowledge (MMLU-Pro), mathematical reasoning (GSM8K), and instruction-following capabilities (IFEval). Only a slight degradation was observed in GSM8K for the discoveries domain in some cases.
- **Small Forgetting in Continual Learning:** Experiments on continual fine-tuning (training on one news category, then another) showed relatively small forgetting of previously learned knowledge, suggesting Sys2-FT can contribute to more stable continuous learning.

#### 4.5. Analysis of Sys2-FT Across Data Generation Models

- **Synergy of Model and Data Quality:** Cross-model experiments revealed that successful knowledge integration requires *both* a sufficiently capable base model *and* high-quality generated data. There wasn't a smooth trade-off; models in a "rectangular region" of capability (strong model, good data) performed best.
- **Weak-to-Strong Generalization:** Stronger models fine-tuned with lower-quality data generated by weaker models could still surpass the performance of the weak models themselves, aligning with "weak-to-strong generalization" observations.

#### 4.6. Emerging Scaling Law of Sys2-FT

Preliminary evidence suggested an empirical, compute-dependent scaling relation for Sys2-FT. For models larger than 3B, similar evaluation accuracy could be achieved given the same fine-tuning compute, regardless of model size. This implies that larger models are more *sample-efficient learners* during Sys2-FT, requiring less effective data to reach a given performance level.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes substantial contributions to the field of Large Language Models, particularly in their capacity for continuous and robust knowledge acquisition, with several significant implications:

- **Advancing LLM Adaptability:** The introduction of the New News dataset and the effectiveness of System-2 Fine-tuning (Sys2-FT), especially the Self-QA protocol, mark a crucial step towards building LLMs that can truly internalize and robustly integrate new, plausible information into their permanent knowledge base. This is vital for developing models that can adapt to a constantly evolving world without needing to be retrained from scratch or relying solely on the fleeting nature of in-context learning.
- **A New Benchmark for Knowledge Integration:** The New News dataset fills a gap in existing benchmarks by focusing on the complex task of integrating non-counterfactual, hypothetical information and its downstream implications. This provides a valuable tool for researchers to systematically evaluate and compare future knowledge integration methods beyond simple factual editing.
- **Insights into FT-ICL Interaction:** The discovery of the "Contextual Shadowing Effect" and the "Curse of Overexposure" offers critical insights into the complex and sometimes counter-intuitive interactions between fine-tuning and in-context learning. These findings highlight that learning in weights and learning in context are not always complementary and can even be at odds. This has profound implications for how training data is prepared, particularly for documents where new information is introduced and then elaborated upon. For practical fine-tuning or even pre-training, it suggests that simply concatenating new information into the context might hinder its long-term internalization, necessitating more sophisticated data organization strategies.
- **Inspiration from Cognitive Science:** Drawing parallels to human memory consolidation, Sys2-FT provides a biologically inspired framework for enhancing machine learning. This interdisciplinary approach can foster further research into how cognitive processes inform and improve artificial intelligence.
- **Practical Implications for LLM Deployment:** For real-world applications, LLMs need to be updated with fresh information (e.g., current events, new scientific discoveries, updated regulations). Sys2-FT offers a more robust and reliable method for these updates compared to naive fine-tuning, potentially reducing the need for costly full model retraining and improving the accuracy and coherence of model responses to novel situations. The preservation of general capabilities and limited catastrophic forgetting further bolsters its practicality for continuous learning scenarios.
- **Theoretical Contributions through Scaling Laws:** The preliminary evidence of an emerging scaling law for Sys2-FT suggests that larger models are more data-efficient learners in this specific task. This insight could guide future model architecture design and training resource allocation, indicating that investing in larger models might yield disproportionately better results for knowledge integration tasks.

In conclusion, this paper not only provides effective methods for addressing a fundamental limitation of current LLMs but also uncovers critical nuances in the interplay between different learning paradigms. Its findings lay important groundwork for building more intelligent, adaptive, and continuously learning AI systems.
