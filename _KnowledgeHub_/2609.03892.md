# GraFT: A Training-Free Framework for Spatial Reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models via 3D Scene Graphs

> [!summary] Summary
> GraFT is a training-free framework giving a frozen multimodal LLM 3D spatial reasoning by routing each question to one of three capabilities derived from a single hierarchical 3D scene graph: deterministic symbolic geometry tools, a task-conditioned bird's-eye-view rendering, and geometry-ranked egocentric frames. It lifts open backbones up to 65% on VSI-Bench, reaching 51.4 average.

## Problem

- Fine-tuning MLLMs on curated spatial data or attaching 3D encoders binds spatial competence to a training distribution, a fixed backbone, and one input format.
- Prior structured-prompting methods apply one fixed rendering to every question, cluttering the top-down view with whole-scene content irrelevant to the asked relation.
- Tool-augmented spatial agents re-run depth, pose, or detection perception per question, and learned priors give unreliable metric estimates compared with direct geometric computation.

## Method

- A ==hierarchical 3D scene graph== stores building, floor, room, and object nodes, each object carrying oriented box center, extents, quaternion, normal, and the posed frames where it is visible.
- A ==symbolic module== answers metric questions in one deterministic tool call (count, size, distance, proximity, room), so no arithmetic passes through the backbone.
- A ==BEV module== renders only query-relevant boxes to scale, rotated so the facing direction points up; an ==egocentric module== ranks frames by a visibility-gated product of centrality, proximity, and corner coverage.

## Results

- On VSI-Bench metric subtasks with ground-truth perception, symbolic tools beat serialized-graph in-context arithmetic by **36.9 points** on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, narrowing its gap to GPT-o3 from **33.9** to **2.9**.
- On ScanQA with a frozen Qwen2.5-VL-7B, top-2 selected frames raise CIDEr from **58.0** to **73.6** and BLEU-1 from **22.2** to **34.2**, beating fine-tuned Chat-3D.
- On full VSI-Bench under realistic perception, GraFT reaches **51.4** average with InternVL2-8B, ranking first overall; a 3B backbone hits **47.5**, above the strongest 72B general-purpose baseline at **40.9**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Delegating exact geometry to deterministic functions rather than in-context arithmetic decouples accuracy from backbone strength, letting small models nearly match frontier ones.
> - Evidence should be matched per task rather than fixed in advance; one compact symbolic structure can serve numeric, allocentric, and pixel-level queries simultaneously.
> - Selective, uncluttered renderings beat full-scene visualizations, but the whole approach is capped by upstream scene-graph quality, which drops sharply under realistic perception.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses the persistent challenge of enabling Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to perform reliable 3D spatial reasoning. Current MLLMs reportedly exhibit deficiencies in precise geometric measurement, transforming between egocentric (first-person) and allocentric (global) viewpoints, and grounding fine-grained visual attributes of objects within a 3D scene. This limitation hinders their application in critical domains such as augmented reality, autonomous driving, and embodied artificial intelligence.

Existing approaches to improve spatial reasoning in MLLMs primarily fall into two categories:
1. **Training-based methods:** These involve supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning of MLLMs on large-scale datasets specifically curated for spatial reasoning (e.g., SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT, MM-Spatial, SpaceR).
2. **Explicit 3D representation methods:** These integrate explicit 3D data, such as point clouds, into MLLMs through specialized encoders (e.g., 3D-LLM, PointLLM, Chat-Scene).

The paper identifies several limitations with these prevailing strategies. Both approaches necessitate extensive data curation and significant computational resources for training. Furthermore, they often commit to a fixed spatial prior or an encoder tied to a specific input format *before* the task is encountered. This rigid coupling can be inefficient and suboptimal, as the evidence required for spatial reasoning varies widely depending on the task type. For instance, tasks requiring global layout benefit from map-like views, attribute-centric tasks require suitable 2D visual inputs, and precise metric grounding is often more reliably obtained through deterministic geometric tools rather than learned priors.

GraFT positions itself as an alternative by proposing a **training-free framework** that leverages a 3D scene graph (3DSG) to provide task-appropriate 3D structural evidence to a *frozen* MLLM. This approach diverges from training-dependent solutions by externalizing the 3D structure and dynamically tailoring the input to the MLLM based on the specific spatial reasoning demand. It also differs from other structured visual prompting methods (e.g., GPT4Scene, Struct2D) by providing selective and task-conditioned renderings, rather than a fixed visual representation. Compared to tool-augmented MLLMs that re-run perception for each query (e.g., SpatialAgent, TIGeR, SpaceTools), GraFT amortizes the perception cost by constructing the 3DSG once and performing deterministic reasoning over it. The work also builds upon the growing adoption of 3DSGs in robotics and embodied AI, broadening their interface beyond simple textual serialization.

By adopting a training-free paradigm, GraFT aims to make advanced spatial reasoning more accessible, flexible, and efficient, circumventing the need for continuous retraining or backbone-specific adaptations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the 3D spatial reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in a manner that is robust, adaptable, and does not require task-specific training or fine-tuning of the MLLM backbone.

The motivation for developing GraFT stems from observed limitations in current MLLMs' ability to process and reason about the 3D physical world:

1. **Unreliable Geometric Measurement:** MLLMs frequently struggle with precise geometric tasks, such as accurately determining object sizes, distances between objects, or room dimensions. Relying on learned priors from visual inputs alone often introduces inaccuracies. The goal is to provide a mechanism for deterministic, high-precision metric answers.

2. **Egocentric-to-Allocentric Viewpoint Transformation:** MLLMs, typically trained on egocentric (first-person) views, often falter when required to reason about global scene layouts or relationships from an allocentric (bird's-eye) perspective. This gap limits their effectiveness in tasks like route planning or understanding relative directions of distant objects. The objective is to facilitate this transformation efficiently and clearly.

3. **Inadequate Fine-Grained Visual Attribute Grounding:** While MLLMs generally possess strong visual comprehension, grounding fine-grained visual attributes (e.g., color, texture, specific details) of a queried object can be challenging, particularly if the object is partially obscured, far away, or presented in a suboptimal frame within a video sequence. The motivation is to ensure the MLLM receives the most informative visual evidence for such tasks.

The researchers argue that existing solutions, which often involve extensive supervised fine-tuning or the integration of specialized 3D encoders, are hindered by:
- **High Cost and Data Requirements:** These methods demand large-scale, meticulously curated datasets and significant computational resources for training.
- **Lack of Flexibility:** Solutions tend to be coupled to specific MLLM backbones or input formats, limiting their adaptability to new models or diverse task requirements.
- **Fixed Spatial Priors:** Learned spatial priors can be constrained by their training distribution, making them less robust to novel scene configurations or unexpected queries.
- **Heterogeneous Task Demands:** A single, monolithic approach struggles to optimally address the varied evidence needs of different spatial reasoning tasks.

GraFT's motivation is to overcome these limitations by introducing a training-free framework that:
- Leverages the compact and incrementally maintainable structure of a 3D Scene Graph (3DSG).
- Dynamically provides *task-appropriate* evidence to a *frozen* MLLM.
- Enables the MLLM to utilize its inherent capabilities (e.g., tool invocation, structured visual interpretation) without requiring modifications to its parameters or re-training.
This approach is intended to provide a more modular, efficient, and generalizable solution for spatial reasoning in MLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GraFT operates as a training-free reasoning layer designed to augment a frozen multimodal large language model (MLLM) backbone with 3D spatial reasoning capabilities. Its core approach involves leveraging a hierarchical 3D scene graph (3DSG) to supply task-appropriate evidence to the MLLM, without altering the MLLM's parameters.

#### 3.1. Scene Representation

GraFT assumes the availability of a hierarchical 3DSG, denoted as G = (N, E), generated by an upstream perception pipeline. This graph comprises nodes (N) at four levels: building, floor, room, and object, with edges (E) defining containment relationships between adjacent layers. Object nodes (o_i) store crucial details: a class label (ℓ_i), a 3D box center (c_i) in world coordinates, full extents (s_i) (width, depth, height), an orientation (R_i) as a unit quaternion, a unit surface normal (n_i), and a set of calibrated video frames (V_i) in which the object is visible. Each frame t within V_i provides its camera orientation (R_t), center (p_t), and intrinsics (K_t).

#### 3.2. Spatial Reasoning Capabilities (Modules)

GraFT provides three distinct spatial reasoning modules, each tailored to a specific family of tasks. The MLLM employs a canonical tool-selection step to determine which module is most appropriate for a given query.

- **Symbolic Module: Geometric Tools (Section 3.1)**
 - **Purpose:** To provide deterministic and precise metric answers that depend on object geometry.
 - **Mechanism:** Instead of having the MLLM estimate quantities from images, GraFT directly computes them from the precise 3D attributes (c_i, s_i, R_i) stored in the 3DSG. This delegates numerical computation to external, deterministic functions.
 - **Implemented Tools:**
 - `count`: Lists object classes and their instance counts.
 - `size`: Returns an object's width, depth, and height.
 - `distance`: Measures surface-to-surface distance between two object classes' bounding boxes.
 - `proximity`: Ranks candidate classes by their closest distance to an anchor object.
 - `room`: Returns a room's width, depth, height, and floor area.
 - **Principle:** Each tool call is a single, deterministic step, ensuring accuracy is inherited from the 3DSG perception rather than potentially adding error through MLLM inference.

- **BEV Module: Selective Allocentric Rendering (Section 3.2)**
 - **Purpose:** To enable reasoning about global scene arrangement and egocentric-to-allocentric conversions.
 - **Mechanism:** Generates a task-conditioned bird's-eye-view (BEV) rendering directly from the 3DSG. Unlike prior methods that render the full scene, GraFT selectively draws *only* the task-relevant objects as clean boxes with their exact proportions, reducing visual clutter.
 - **Dynamic Viewpoint:** The BEV's viewpoint (c_a) and heading (c_f) are dynamically set by the task. The scene is rotated such that the facing direction (c_f - c_a) aligns with the top of the image.
 - **Optional Enhancements:** An anchor-to-target arrow can be overlaid to explicitly visualize spatial relationships.
 - **Output:** The rendered BEV image is provided to the MLLM, often accompanied by a textual key listing object coordinates in the observer's frame. (Algorithms 1 and 2 detail this for relative direction and route planning tasks).

- **Egocentric Module: Frame Retrieval (Section 3.3)**
 - **Purpose:** To ground visual attribute questions in pixels by providing the most informative first-person views of a queried object.
 - **Mechanism:** For a queried object o_i, GraFT scores all associated posed frames in V_i and forwards only the top-k clearest views to the MLLM.
 - **Visibility Gate:** A frame t is determined visible (v_i,t = 1) if three geometric conditions are met:
 - `Corner Projection`: At least two of the object's eight bounding box corners project within the camera's frustum and image plane.
 - `Depth Range`: The camera-object distance (d_i,t) falls within a predefined informative range (0.5m to 4m).
 - `Viewing Angle`: The camera's optical axis faces the object within a specific angle (cosine of angle ≥ 0.5).
 - **Frame Scoring (s(o_i, t)):** For visible frames, a score is computed as the product of three terms (each in):
 - `Centrality (s_ctr)`: Rewards objects positioned near the image center.
 - `Proximity (s_prox)`: Rewards closer objects, floored at one meter to prevent extreme values.
 - `Coverage (s_cov)`: Measures the fraction of the object's box corners that are visible within the image, favoring more complete views.
 - **Selection:** Frames are ranked by their score s(o_i, t), near-duplicate viewpoints are removed, and the top-k are forwarded.
 - **Application:** For tasks like VSI-Bench's "appearance-order," objects are ranked by the chronological time their first visible frame surpasses a score threshold (Algorithm 3 and Table A1 detail parameters).

#### 3.3. Perception Settings

Experiments are conducted under three perception settings for 3DSG construction:
- **(i) Video input:** Raw video provided to the MLLM, without a 3DSG.
- **(ii) Ground-truth perception:** 3DSG constructed from ground-truth object annotations of datasets like ScanNet.
- **(iii) Realistic perception:** 3DSG built by an offline perception pipeline, involving scene reconstruction and object detection (e.g., Mask3D and UniDet3D).

This modular, training-free architecture allows GraFT to adapt to diverse spatial reasoning tasks by providing tailored, structured evidence to a frozen MLLM.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of GraFT was evaluated on two spatial reasoning benchmarks: VSI-Bench and ScanQA, using various frozen MLLM backbones including GPT-o3, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-3B, and InternVL2-8B.

#### 4.1. Symbolic Tools for Metric Estimation (Table 1)

On the five metric subtasks of VSI-Bench, GraFT's symbolic module demonstrated consistent improvements over both raw video input and directly serializing the 3DSG as text for MLLM reasoning.
- **Performance:** GraFT, with its deterministic geometric tools, improved average accuracy by +5.9 points (GPT-o3, ground-truth perception) and +1.5 points (GPT-o3, realistic perception) compared to the 'Serialized Graph' approach. For the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, these gains were more substantial, at +36.9 and +16.7 points, respectively.
- **Backbone Robustness:** GraFT notably reduced the performance gap between a weaker backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and a stronger one (GPT-o3). Under GraFT, Qwen2.5-VL-7B's performance was within 2.0-2.9 points of GPT-o3 under realistic and ground-truth perception, whereas with 'Serialized Graph', the difference was 17.2-33.9 points. This indicates that deterministic tools can enable less capable MLLMs to achieve precise metric reasoning.

#### 4.2. BEV Rendering for Egocentric-to-Allocentric Reasoning (Table 2)

For the two directional subtasks of VSI-Bench, the BEV module (using GPT-o3) showed enhanced performance.
- **Performance:** GraFT improved average accuracy by +35.2 points (ground-truth perception) and +15.8 points (realistic perception) over the 'Video input' baseline.
- **Comparison to Prior BEV Methods:** GraFT surpassed existing BEV methods like Struct2D and GPT4Scene. Under ground-truth perception, GraFT outperformed Struct2D by +3.6 average points. Under realistic perception, it achieved +18.1 points over GPT4Scene and +3.3 points over Struct2D. This suggests that GraFT’s selective rendering of only task-relevant objects provides a clearer, more effective input for MLLMs.

#### 4.3. Egocentric Frame Retrieval on ScanQA (Table 3)

The egocentric module was evaluated on ScanQA for visual attribute questions, using a frozen Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone.
- **Performance:** GraFT, by selecting the top-k informative frames, improved every metric over uniform frame sampling. CIDEr scores increased from 58.0 to 73.6 (+15.6 points), and BLEU-1 from 22.2 to 34.2 (+12.0 points).
- **Ranking:** GraFT achieved the highest scores across all five caption metrics (BLEU-1, BLEU-4, METEOR, ROUGE, CIDEr), outperforming both zero-shot general-purpose MLLMs (InternVL2-8B, MiniCPM-V-2.6) and the fine-tuned Chat-3D.
- **Frame Budget Ablation (Table B2):** Even a single selected frame (k=1) outperformed uniform 8-frame sampling, indicating that the geometric selection criterion is more critical than the sheer number of frames.

#### 4.4. GraFT versus Prior Work (Tables 4 & 5)

GraFT was compared against Struct2D on VSI-Bench subsets (Table 4) and against a wider range of proprietary, open-source, and fine-tuned spatial models on the complete VSI-Bench (Table 5).
- **Against Struct2D (Table 4):** GraFT consistently outperformed Struct2D (which is fine-tuned) across various backbones and perception settings. For GPT-o3, GraFT achieved +9.3 and +2.7 average points under ground-truth and realistic perception, respectively. For Qwen2.5-VL-7B under realistic perception, GraFT gained +6.8 average points.
- **On Complete VSI-Bench (Table 5):**
 - **Overall Performance:** GraFT achieved an average score of up to 51.4 (with InternVL2-8B backbone) under realistic perception. This result surpassed all evaluated proprietary models (best was Gemini-1.5 Pro at 45.4) and all general-purpose open-source baselines (best was LLaVA-NeXT-Video-72B at 40.9).
 - **Comparison to Fine-tuned Models:** GraFT also outperformed several prominent models explicitly fine-tuned for spatial reasoning (e.g., VG LLM-8B, Spatial-MLLM, OCR) by 0.7 to 6.0 points on average.
 - **Backbone Efficiency:** GraFT consistently improved the performance of smaller backbones. For example, a Qwen2.5-VL-3B model paired with GraFT scored 47.5, surpassing the strongest 72B general-purpose baseline (LLaVA-NeXT-Video-72B at 40.9).
- **Single-Module Ablation (Table B1):** Confirmed that the full GraFT system, which dynamically routes tasks to the most appropriate module, performs better (50.0 average) than any single module applied across all tasks (45.9 for Tools, 29.4 for BEV, 35.4 for Ego). This highlights the benefit of task-specific evidence provision.

These findings suggest that GraFT, by leveraging a 3DSG and dynamic evidence provision, enables frozen MLLMs to perform robust 3D spatial reasoning, often surpassing models that undergo extensive task-specific training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of GraFT introduces a paradigm for spatial reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) that presents several implications and potential impacts on the field:

1. **Reduced Training Dependency:** A primary significance of GraFT is its training-free nature. It demonstrates that MLLMs can achieve proficient 3D spatial reasoning without requiring large-scale, costly supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning. This reduces the computational and data annotation overhead traditionally associated with developing spatial intelligence in AI systems. The ability to use a frozen MLLM backbone means that new spatial tasks or environments do not necessarily necessitate retraining the core model.

2. **Enhanced Flexibility and Generalizability:** By decoupling the spatial reasoning mechanism from the MLLM backbone, GraFT offers increased flexibility. It is not tied to a specific MLLM architecture or a particular 3D input format (beyond the 3DSG interface). This modularity allows the framework to be applied across different MLLMs and potentially adapt to diverse 3D datasets and tasks more readily than tightly integrated, fine-tuned solutions.

3. **Leveraging MLLM Capabilities Effectively:** GraFT optimizes the use of existing MLLM capabilities. Instead of forcing MLLMs to learn complex 3D geometry from scratch, it provides them with pre-processed, structured evidence (symbolic facts, clean BEV renderings, high-quality egocentric views). This allows MLLMs to focus on their strengths in natural language understanding, tool invocation, and visual interpretation, leading to improved performance.

4. **Improved Performance and Accessibility:** The reported results indicate that GraFT-augmented MLLMs can match or surpass the performance of both proprietary and fine-tuned spatial reasoning models on benchmarks like VSI-Bench and ScanQA. Notably, even smaller MLLM backbones (e.g., a 3B parameter model) achieve competitive results, outperforming much larger general-purpose baselines. This suggests that advanced spatial reasoning could become more accessible for deployment on resource-constrained platforms.

5. **Standardization of 3D Scene Graphs:** The work reinforces the utility of 3D Scene Graphs (3DSGs) as a unified, compact, and interpretable representation for 3D environments. By showcasing how a single 3DSG can support multiple, complementary reasoning capabilities (metric, allocentric, egocentric), GraFT promotes 3DSGs as a robust interface for embodied AI and other 3D understanding applications.

6. **Foundation for Future Work:** The framework's modular design suggests avenues for expansion. New reasoning modules could be developed atop the 3DSG to address additional spatial tasks or incorporate new types of evidence, further extending the capabilities of frozen MLLMs without requiring architectural changes. The identified limitation of accuracy being bounded by scene-graph quality also points to future research in developing more robust and precise upstream 3DSG construction pipelines.

In summary, GraFT contributes to the field by offering a practical and efficient solution to enhance MLLMs' 3D spatial reasoning, potentially accelerating the development of more intelligent and adaptable AI agents for understanding and interacting with the physical world.
