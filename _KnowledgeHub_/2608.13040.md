# Latent On-Policy Self-Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> LOPD makes the privileged context of an on-policy self-distillation teacher learnable instead of hand-designed. A LoRA encoder plus QFormer compressor turns retrieved past trajectories into continuous latent tokens conditioning a frozen self-teacher, which gives dense token-level reverse-KL supervision on the student's own prefixes, held informative by a privileged-margin constraint. It wins all ten backbone-benchmark comparisons and needs under 30% of GRPO's rollouts.

## Problem

- Existing on-policy self-distillation conditions the self-teacher on designer-specified artifacts: verified answers, reasoning traces, environment feedback, or successful rollouts, fixing a priori what experience the teacher sees.
- Hand-crafted contexts are not universally useful: the same artifact helps one benchmark and hurts another, so prescribed formats can become sparse, mismatched, or overly prescriptive.
- Rule-based experience transformation blocks end-to-end learnability, so agents cannot discover which aspects of prior experience actually supervise their current trajectories, limiting scalable continual self-improvement.

## Method

- ==Latent-context composer==: a dense retriever pulls top-3 successful trajectories; a frozen encoder with ==LoRA== plus a ==QFormer-style cross-attention compressor== emits 32 continuous latent tokens per experience as teacher-only context.
- ==On-policy latent self-distillation==: a frozen-backbone teacher re-scores the student's visited prefixes with latent context; ==reverse KL== over top-20 logits plus a tail bucket trains student and composer jointly, gradients flowing to the latent input.
- ==Privileged-margin constraint==: a dual-variable penalty forces the teacher's outcome-weighted token log-probability advantage above m, plus an anchor term to the cold-start context, structurally excluding teacher-student collapse.

## Results

- Best aggregate in all ten backbone-benchmark comparisons: Qwen3-8B EnvScaler **66.4** versus **60.2** best baseline, ACEBench **62.7** versus **58.0**, BFCL-v3 **29.88** versus **29.00**.
- Coding transfers: Olmo3-7B reaches LiveCodeBench average **50.98** and EvalPlus **78.41**, beating the strongest alternative by **2.69** and **0.55** points; Qwen3-4B reaches **48.78** and **81.36**.
- Ablations: margin m=0.05 gives **0.637** EnvScaler reward versus **0.551** unconstrained and **0.573** frozen composer; LOPD passes GRPO's final **0.611** within **320** of **1,600** generations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimize the representation of experience end-to-end rather than hand-designing artifacts; learned continuous context adapts across tasks where a fixed format helps one regime and hurts another.
> - Any learnable teacher context needs an explicit informativeness constraint: without a verified privilege margin, distillation collapses the teacher onto the student, the lower-resistance solution.
> - Privileged supervision can be internalized: the deployed student keeps the teacher's procedural behavior with no retrieval or latent context, but the latents stay largely uninterpretable.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a central problem in self-evolving artificial intelligence: how agents can effectively learn from experience and integrate this learning into their policies. On-policy distillation (OPD) has emerged as a significant paradigm for experience learning in large language models (LLMs). OPD involves a student model sampling its own trajectories, and a teacher then providing dense, token-level supervision on the states the student visits. This approach mitigates the training-inference mismatch issues prevalent in off-policy imitation learning and converts sparse outcome-based reinforcement learning signals into fine-grained distributional guidance.

A specialized form of OPD, known as On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD), is particularly relevant. OPSD eliminates the need for a separate, more powerful teacher by instantiating both the teacher and student from the same base model. The student operates under the standard task and interaction history, while the teacher is augmented with "privileged information." This privileged context, such as verified reasoning traces, final answers, environmental feedback, or successful past rollouts, makes the teacher's distribution more informative, enabling dense feedback without external models.

The existing OPSD methods, however, predominantly rely on "designer-specified privileged artifacts." These artifacts, whether oracle answers, textual reflections, successful rollouts, or retrieved documents, are typically hand-designed or extracted using predefined rules. This reliance constrains the self-distillation process to a specific information format chosen by a human designer, potentially limiting the teacher's ability to learn which aspects of prior experience are most effective for supervising the student's current trajectory. This limitation hinders the end-to-end learnability and scalability necessary for continual self-improvement in complex, evolving AI systems. Latent On-Policy Self-Distillation (LOPD) aims to address this fundamental limitation by proposing a mechanism for the privileged context itself to be learned end-to-end from experience.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether the privileged context used by an OPSD self-teacher can be learned end-to-end from experience, rather than being a designer-specified artifact. This objective stems from a core research question:

- **Research Question:** Can privileged context itself be learned end-to-end from experience, so that the self-teacher, rather than a designer-specified rule, determines what experiential knowledge to retain and how to encode it for dense on-policy supervision?

The motivation for this objective is rooted in the perceived limitations of current OPSD approaches:
1. **Fixed Information Format:** Existing methods force the teacher to utilize privileged information in a predetermined, often textual or discrete, format. This pre-definition can restrict the teacher's capacity to extract and leverage the most relevant and effective signals from prior experience.
2. **Scalability Challenges:** For AI agents to continually self-improve and evolve in diverse and dynamic environments, their learning mechanisms must be scalable. Relying on human-designed or rule-extracted privileged contexts introduces a bottleneck, as these artifacts may not universally apply or maintain their utility across varying tasks and learning states.
3. **Lack of Adaptability:** A fixed context struggles to adapt to the specific needs of a student's current trajectory or the nuances of a new task. The goal is to allow the teacher to learn *what* evidence is useful and *how* to represent it in a way that optimizes the student's learning process.

LOPD is motivated by the principle that for self-evolving AI to scale, neither the content of useful experience nor its representation should be fixed by heuristics. Instead, a scalable system should learn end-to-end what evidence to preserve and how to transform it into effective supervision. The paper positions OPSD as a concrete mechanism through which this broader principle can be realized.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LOPD introduces a framework that conceptualizes privileged context as a learnable latent substrate. The methodology is structured around several key components:

#### 3.1. Problem Setup: Privileged Context in OPSD

The work begins by formalizing the OPSD setup. A student policy $\pi_S(· | s_t)$ generates a multi-turn trajectory $\tau$ from task $x$ and interaction history $s_t$. Traditional OPSD conditions a teacher $\pi_T(· | s_t, c_{fix})$ on a fixed, designer-specified privileged context $c_{fix} = \Phi_{fix}(x, E)$, derived from an experience source $E$. The distillation objective $L_{OPSD}(\theta)$ minimizes a token-level divergence between the student's distribution $p_S$ and the teacher's distribution $p_T$ on the student's visited prefixes. LOPD replaces $\Phi_{fix}$ with a learnable composer $\Phi_\phi$, which generates a continuous latent context $c_\phi$.

#### 3.2. Learnable Latent Privileged Context

LOPD instantiates $\Phi_\phi$ as a latent-context composer, comprising an encoder and a compressor:
- **Experience Bank and Retrieval:** Offline, successful rollouts (task descriptions and action-result traces) are stored in an experience bank. For a given task $x$, a dense retriever (QWEN3-EMBEDDING-8B) identifies the top-J most relevant experiences $E = \{m_j\}$ based on cosine similarity between task queries and stored task-trajectory embeddings. The retriever uses asymmetric encoding, where document embeddings combine the task and trajectory, and query embeddings use an instruction-aware prompt.
- **Encoder ($\text{Enc}_\psi$):** This component encodes each retrieved experience item $m_j$ (concatenated with the current task description) into hidden states $H_j \in \mathbb{R}^{T_j \times d}$. The encoder is implemented using a frozen backbone LLM augmented with a trainable LoRA adapter ($\psi$).
- **Compressor ($\text{Comp}_\chi$):** The variable-length hidden states $H_j$ are compressed into a fixed number of continuous latent tokens $E_j = [\langle e_{j,1} \rangle, \dots, \langle e_{j,K} \rangle] \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times d}$. This is achieved using a QFormer-style cross-attention mechanism with learned queries ($Q_\chi \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times d}$). The parameters of the LoRA adapter $\psi$ and the compressor $\chi$ constitute the trainable composer parameters $\phi = (\psi, \chi)$. The composer is cold-started on successful trajectories and then jointly optimized.
- **Latent Context Formation:** The latent tokens from all retrieved experiences are concatenated to form the complete learnable privileged context $c_\phi(x, E) = \bigoplus_{j=1}^J (\langle e_{j,1} \rangle \oplus \dots \oplus \langle e_{j,K} \rangle)$, which is then supplied to the teacher.

#### 3.3. Latent On-Policy Self-Distillation Process

The core self-distillation loop involves:
- **Student Rollout:** The student policy $\pi_S(\cdot | s_t)$ generates a multi-turn trajectory $\tau$.
- **Teacher Evaluation:** A fixed reference copy of the base model ($\bar{\theta}$) acts as the teacher $\pi_T(\cdot | s_t, c_\phi(x, E), a_{t,<n})$, conditioned on the same student prefixes $a_{t,<n}$ and the learnable latent context $c_\phi$.
- **Distillation Objective ($L_{distill}$):** The student is optimized by minimizing the reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence $\text{D}_{\text{KL}}(\tilde{p}_{S,t,n} \| \tilde{p}_{T,t,n})$ between its (top-M plus tail) next-token distribution $\tilde{p}_S$ and the teacher's (top-M plus tail) distribution $\tilde{p}_T$. The reverse KL encourages the student to focus its probability mass on tokens supported by the teacher. Critically, while $\bar{\theta}$ is fixed, the gradients flow from $p_T$ back to $c_\phi$ and thus to the composer parameters $\phi$.

#### 3.4. Privileged-Margin Constraint

To prevent the composer from trivializing the supervision by making $p_T$ collapse to $p_S$, LOPD introduces a privileged-margin constraint:
- **Per-token Privilege ($\delta_{t,n}$):** This measures the teacher's log-probability advantage over the student for the sampled token $a_{t,n}$: $\delta_{t,n}(\phi) = \log \pi_T(a_{t,n} | \dots) - \text{sg}[\log \pi_S(a_{t,n} | \dots)]$, where $\text{sg}$ denotes a stop-gradient.
- **Outcome-Weighted Privilege ($\Delta(\phi)$):** The per-token privileges are aggregated and weighted by a trajectory-level verification signal $A(\tau) = 2r(\tau) - 1 \in [-1, 1]$ (where $r(\tau)$ is the outcome reward). This ensures the teacher maintains a verifiable log-probability advantage, especially for successful trajectories.
- **Full Objective:** The overall objective function is a minimax optimization:
 $\min_{\theta,\phi} \max_{\beta \ge 0} L_{distill}(\theta, \phi) + \beta(m - \Delta(\phi)) + \lambda \|c_\phi - \text{sg}[c_{\phi_0}]\|^2_2$
 Here, $\beta$ is a dual variable updated via $\beta \leftarrow [\beta + \eta_\beta(m - \Delta(\phi))]^+$, ensuring the teacher maintains a minimum privilege level $m$. An anchor term $\lambda \|c_\phi - \text{sg}[c_{\phi_0}]\|^2_2$ penalizes significant drift of the latent context from its cold-start initialization $c_{\phi_0}$.

#### 3.5. Algorithm Summary

The LOPD algorithm iteratively samples tasks, performs on-policy rollouts with the student, retrieves experiences, constructs learnable latent context for the teacher, evaluates the teacher on the student's trajectory prefixes, and jointly updates the student policy $\theta$ and composer parameters $\phi$ while adjusting the dual variable $\beta$. During inference, only the trained student policy $\pi_S(\theta)$ is used, without the composer or latent context.

#### 3.6. Latent Injection Mechanism

The latent tokens are injected into the backbone's input embedding sequence. A text-level sentinel string `<|LATENT PH|>` is used in the prompt template. This string is tokenized, and the resulting dummy token IDs are replaced with the continuous latent token embeddings from the composer before the first transformer layer. This method avoids tokenizer modification and allows gradients to flow back to the composer parameters. The encoder's LoRA adapter is active only during experience compression, not during the main forward pass, to prevent interference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of LOPD was conducted across two post-training settings: agentic tool-use and coding, using three model backbones (QWEN3-4B, QWEN3-8B, OLMO3-7B).

#### 4.1. Performance on Tool-Use and Code Generation

LOPD demonstrated robust performance, achieving the best aggregate results in all ten backbone-benchmark comparisons:
- **Tool Use:**
 - On QWEN3-4B, LOPD improved EnvScaler scores from 61.8 (GRPO) to 63.7, BFCL-v3 from 25.25 (GRPO) to 27.38, and ACEBench from 56.0 (GRPO/Skill-SD) to 60.6.
 - With QWEN3-8B, LOPD achieved 66.4 on EnvScaler, 29.88 on BFCL-v3, and 62.7 on ACEBench, surpassing the strongest baselines (e.g., EnvScaler: 60.2 from Skill-SD; ACEBench: 58.0 from GRPO).
- **Code Generation:**
 - For QWEN3-4B, LOPD improved LiveCodeBench aggregate to 48.78 and EvalPlus aggregate to 81.36.
 - With OLMO3-7B, it reached 50.98 on LiveCodeBench and 78.41 on EvalPlus, outperforming alternatives by 2.69 and 0.55 points, respectively.
These consistent improvements across diverse tasks and model architectures suggest the transferability of LOPD's benefits.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Baselines and Role of Learnable Context

- **Inconsistency of Hand-Crafted Contexts:** The study observed that existing OPSD methods using fixed, designer-specified privileged contexts did not always improve upon the vanilla model, and their utility could even reverse across different settings. For instance, SDPO sometimes performed worse than the vanilla model on BFCL-v3 and LiveCodeBench, and OPSD also showed performance drops in some cases. This highlights that simply providing privileged information is not sufficient; its representation and alignment with the task and student's learning state are critical.
- **Advantage of Learnable Context:** In contrast, LOPD consistently outperformed the vanilla model across all aggregate backbone-benchmark settings. It also regularly exceeded prescribed-context baselines (OPSD, SDFT, Skill-SD) by notable margins, such as 14.4, 10.2, and 6.2 points on EnvScaler with QWEN3-8B, respectively. This suggests that allowing the teacher to learn a compact, continuous representation of experience from retrieved trajectories, adapting the privileged signal to the task and student's visited prefixes, is more effective than relying on hand-crafted artifacts.

#### 4.3. Framework Analysis

- **Effect of Joint Optimization:** Ablation studies showed that joint optimization of the privileged context is crucial. Training with a frozen composer (ϕ_0) yielded an EnvScaler reward of 0.573. Without the privileged-margin constraint (m=0), performance degraded to 0.551, indicating that unconstrained distillation can lead to uninformative latent context. However, with an appropriate margin (m $\ge$ 0.02), LOPD surpassed the frozen-composer baseline, reaching 0.637 at m=0.05. This demonstrates that the distillation process, when regulated by the margin constraint, enables the composer to learn effective supervision signals.
- **Training Dynamics and Sample Efficiency:** LOPD achieved strong performance with fewer on-policy generations. It surpassed 0.61 mean reward after 320 generations and reached 0.637 by generation 576, sustaining this level through 1,600 generations. This indicates higher learning efficiency compared to GRPO (0.611 at 1,600 generations) and Skill-SD (0.588 at 1,600 generations), suggesting a denser learning signal is extracted per trajectory.
- **Sensitivity Analysis:**
 - **Latent Capacity:** Performance showed a threshold for latent token capacity, with 32 tokens per experience proving sufficient for significant gains (0.637 EnvScaler reward) before plateauing.
 - **Retrieval Count:** Retrieving three experiences (n_ret=3) was found to be optimal for EnvScaler reward (0.637), with diminishing returns or no monotonic benefit from additional retrievals.
- **Behavioral Internalization:** Analysis of agent behavior on the EnvScaler test set revealed that the distilled student internalized the procedural guidance from the latent context. The LOPD student used more environment steps but made significantly fewer tool calls per step (1.11 vs. 3.50 for vanilla), indicating a shift towards more sequential planning. Its first-step response was 37.5% shorter, repeated tool calls decreased, and reward per tool call increased. This behavioral shift, evident even without privileged context at inference, supports the internalization of the teacher's guidance.
- **Case Study:** Qualitative inspection of LM-head projections of the 32 latent tokens revealed fragmented mixtures of multilingual and code-like tokens, not a verbatim reproduction of retrieved solutions. This is consistent with a distributed latent representation, where the teacher functionally uses encoded information without it being directly decodable into a clear procedure.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research makes a contribution to the field of self-evolving AI by reformulating the role of privileged context in on-policy self-distillation.
- **End-to-End Learnability:** LOPD demonstrates that privileged context itself can be learned end-to-end from raw experience. This moves beyond the reliance on human-engineered or rule-based artifacts, allowing the self-teacher to automatically identify and encode task-relevant supervision signals. This shift enhances the scalability and adaptability of self-improvement mechanisms for AI agents.
- **Scalable Agent Evolution:** By enabling the agent to discover *what* evidence to preserve and *how* to encode it directly from trajectories, LOPD offers a more self-directed paradigm for agent evolution. This reduces the dependency on designer-specified experience formats, which can be brittle or non-transferable across diverse tasks and learning environments.
- **Improved Learning Efficiency and Performance:** The consistent empirical improvements across various benchmarks and model backbones, coupled with higher sample efficiency, indicate that learnable latent context provides a denser and more effective learning signal. This suggests that the approach can accelerate the learning process and lead to more capable agents.
- **Broader Applicability:** The method's effectiveness across both agentic tool use and code generation domains, despite different interaction patterns and reward structures, suggests broad applicability for tasks requiring complex, multi-step reasoning and interaction.
- **Future Research Directions:** LOPD opens avenues for further research into the nature of latent representations for experience, exploring richer experience repositories, and developing more sophisticated learnable retrievers. The finding that the latent tokens are not directly human-interpretable but are functionally effective also encourages deeper investigation into the mechanisms of latent computation within LLMs for reasoning and memory.

In essence, LOPD represents a step towards making experience learning a fully end-to-end process, where the representation of experiential knowledge is optimized for the policies it aims to improve, rather than being a static input. This is a foundational aspect for developing more autonomous and continually learning AI systems.
