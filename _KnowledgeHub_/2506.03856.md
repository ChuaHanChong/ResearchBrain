# Phase-based Nonlinear Model Predictive Control for Humanoid Walking Stabilization with Single and Double Support Time Adjustments

> [!summary] Summary
> A phase-based Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) framework unifies the optimization of Zero Moment Point (ZMP) modulation, step location, and both single and double support phase durations for humanoid walking. This approach demonstrated enhanced robustness, tolerating up to 108 N·s impulses and enabling stable walking on 0.03m uneven terrain, through coordinated balance strategies.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots face challenges in maintaining robust balance due to their complex, nonlinear dynamics and environmental uncertainties, requiring effective disturbance rejection.
- Many existing control methods for humanoid locomotion simplify the gait cycle by neglecting explicit optimization of the Double Support Phase (DSP) duration, limiting control authority during critical weight-shifting.
- Prior DSP optimization efforts often relied on heuristic methods or linearized models, which could lead to sensitivity to disturbances or model mismatches.

## Method

- Developed a phase-based Nonlinear ==Model Predictive Control== (NMPC) framework that operates within a larger control architecture comprising a walking pattern generator and a whole-body controller.
- Formulated the NMPC to simultaneously optimize ZMP modulation, step location, and both Single Support Phase (SSP) and Double Support Phase (DSP) durations for a preview horizon.
- Derived nonlinear Divergent Component of Motion (DCM) error dynamics to explicitly incorporate phase duration adjustments, while enforcing ZMP input continuity and managing footstep adjustments.

## Results

- Demonstrated improved robustness in simulations, tolerating impulses up to 108 N·s in various directions and consistently higher disturbance tolerance across different impact timings.
- Achieved stable walking on challenging terrains, with 4/5 success on 0.03m elevation differences and full success across a range of compliant ground settings, outperforming baseline methods.
- Validated on the TOCABI humanoid robot, showing successful balance recovery from external pushes (e.g., **26.98** N·s backward) and adaptation to unexpected 3 cm terrain height changes without exteroceptive sensing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit optimization of Double Support Phase (DSP) duration allows for faster disturbance rejection by enabling quicker transitions to the next Single Support Phase (SSP) and more agile stepping.
> - A unified Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) framework that fully integrates ZMP modulation, step location, and both SSP/DSP duration adjustments provides superior robustness compared to methods with sequential or heuristic strategies.
> - Enforcing ZMP input continuity across phase transitions and disabling footstep updates during DSP contributes to dynamically consistent balance control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robot locomotion and balance control are fundamental research areas aiming to enable robots to operate stably in complex and uncertain real-world environments. The field faces challenges due to the inherent nonlinearity, high degrees of freedom, and underactuated nature of humanoid robot dynamics, alongside potential external disturbances from uneven terrain or collisions.

Current research extensively utilizes optimization-based control techniques, such such as Quadratic Programming (QP) and Model Predictive Control (MPC), often relying on simplified reduced-order models like the Linear Inverted Pendulum Model (LIPM), Single Rigid Body Model (SRBM), or Divergent Component of Motion (DCM). These approaches typically optimize variables such as contact wrenches (or the Zero Moment Point, ZMP), step location, and Single Support Phase (SSP) duration (step timing) to maintain balance.

Despite advancements, a notable limitation in many existing methods is the inadequate incorporation of the Double Support Phase (DSP) into the optimization problem. Often, the DSP is either omitted from the gait cycle or assumed to have a fixed duration. However, the DSP is functionally important for proper weight shifting and ensuring smooth, continuous transitions in robot Center of Mass (CoM) acceleration, thereby avoiding discontinuous joint torque commands which can be challenging for robots lacking high-bandwidth actuators.

Previous attempts to optimize DSP duration include heuristic methods, such as one that scales DSP duration based on planned footstep updates, which can be sensitive to disturbance timing. Other approaches, like an LMPC framework by Egle et al., optimize ZMP modulation, step location, and both SSP and DSP durations. However, these formulations often introduce simplifying assumptions, such as linearizing DCM-ZMP dynamics by setting ZMP modulation in the current phase and future phase duration adjustments to zero, which can lead to model mismatches and require additional compensatory mechanisms.

This paper addresses these limitations by proposing a framework that explicitly integrates DSP duration optimization and maintains a unified, nonlinear representation of the robot's dynamics across both SSP and DSP. The work positions itself to overcome the challenges of dynamic inconsistency and reliance on simplifying assumptions observed in prior methods, contributing to more robust and adaptable humanoid balance control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust balance control framework for humanoid robots that can effectively counter various disturbances. This is to be achieved by simultaneously optimizing several critical control parameters within a single, unified formulation: ZMP modulation, step location, SSP duration (step timing), and DSP duration.

The specific motivations and objectives driving this work are as follows:

- **Addressing the complexity of humanoid dynamics and environmental uncertainties:** Humanoid robots operate in environments characterized by uncertainties and disturbances. Their complex, nonlinear, high-degree-of-freedom, and underactuated dynamics make robust balance control a significant challenge. The research aims to provide a control solution that can effectively manage these complexities.
- **Overcoming limitations of existing balance control strategies:** Many current optimization-based control methods for humanoid locomotion simplify the gait cycle by neglecting the explicit optimization of the DSP duration. This can lead to a reduction in control authority, particularly during critical weight-shifting phases, and can result in discontinuous robot CoM accelerations and joint torques, posing implementation difficulties for robots without high-performance actuators.
- **Improving upon prior DSP optimization attempts:** Earlier methods that did incorporate DSP duration adjustments often relied on heuristics or simplified linear models. Heuristic approaches may lack general applicability or responsiveness to unpredictable disturbances, while linearized models can suffer from model mismatch and require complex coordination mechanisms, potentially leading to reduced fidelity compared to approaches that retain full nonlinear dynamics.
- **Developing a unified and phase-consistent control framework:** A key motivation is to integrate the optimization of ZMP modulation, step location, and both SSP and DSP durations into a single Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) formulation. This unification is intended to ensure dynamically reliable balance control irrespective of whether the robot is in an SSP or DSP. Prior DCM-based control frameworks often support balance control primarily during the SSP, necessitating an extension of DCM tracking control to both phases.
- **Ensuring dynamic consistency across phase transitions:** The framework aims to enforce ZMP input continuity during contact-phase transitions and to disable footstep updates during the DSP. These design choices are intended to promote dynamically consistent balance control and enhance performance compared to methods that lack such integrated constraints.
- **Validation through comprehensive evaluation:** The research seeks to validate the effectiveness of the proposed framework through extensive simulation and real-world experiments. This validation focuses on demonstrating improved robustness under large external disturbances and on challenging terrains, with comparisons against established baseline controllers to quantify performance gains.

In summary, the motivation stems from the need for a more comprehensive and dynamically consistent control framework that leverages the full control authority available through simultaneous optimization of all major balance strategies, explicitly including the DSP, to enhance humanoid robot stability in realistic scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology integrates a phase-based Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) framework into an overall control architecture for humanoid balance. This architecture comprises a walking pattern generator, the NMPC, and a QP-based whole-body controller.

**Overall Control Framework:**
The control loop operates as follows:
1. **Reference Generation:** A footstep planner generates reference footstep locations (F_ref) and nominal phase durations (T). A ZMP planner then uses these to generate a reference ZMP trajectory (Z_ref), which is fixed at the center of the support foot during SSP and linearly interpolated between the feet during DSP. A walking pattern generator produces reference CoM position and velocity trajectories (C_ref, C_dot_ref) to track Z_ref while minimizing CoM jerk. The Divergent Component of Motion (DCM) trajectory (Xi_ref) is derived from the CoM trajectories.
2. **Phase-based NMPC (Core Contribution):** The proposed NMPC receives the current DCM measurement (ξ_t) and the reference ZMP and DCM trajectories. It optimizes a set of decision variables (v_i) for a preview horizon of n_c phases. These variables include ZMP modulation terms (z_ctrl_0,i, z_ctrl_T,i), step location adjustments (f_ctrl_T_new,i), and phase duration adjustments (δT_i). The primary objective of this optimization is to minimize the DCM offset error (b_err_T_new,i).
3. **Control Output Processing:**
 - The NMPC outputs (optimized ZMP modulation, step location adjustment, phase duration adjustment) are used to compute desired control inputs.
 - The ZMP control input (z_ctrl_t,1) is linearly interpolated over the optimized phase duration (T_new,1). The desired ZMP (z_des_t,1) is formed by adding this control input to the reference ZMP.
 - A ZMP distributor converts z_des_t,1 into desired contact wrenches (w_des_c) for the support feet.
 - The target swing foot location (f_des_T_new,1) is determined by adding the optimized step location adjustment to the reference landing position.
 - The optimized phase duration (T_new,1) is calculated by adding the phase duration adjustment to the reference duration. A fifth-order spline generates the swing foot trajectory (f_des_t,1).
4. **Phase Transition:** When the current time within a phase reaches T_new,1, a phase transition is triggered, and the system progresses to the next phase with time reset.
5. **Whole-Body Control:**
 - Desired angles for lower body joints (q_des_lb) are computed via analytical inverse kinematics, based on the optimized foot and CoM trajectories. Upper body joint angles (q_des_ub) remain constant.
 - A kinematic-level PD controller computes desired joint accelerations (q_ddot_des).
 - A QP-based whole-body controller (WBC) computes feedforward torque (τ_ff) by solving a QP that incorporates full-body dynamics and soft contact constraints, aiming to track q_ddot_des and w_des_c.
 - The final torque command (τ_cmd) applied to robot actuators combines τ_ff with motor-level PD feedback torque (τ_pd).

**Phase-based NMPC Formulation:**
The NMPC is formulated as a constrained nonlinear optimization problem:

1. **Nonlinear DCM Error Dynamics:**
 - The core of the NMPC is the derivation of nonlinear DCM error dynamics. Building upon the LIPM and DCM definitions, the paper extends the DCM-ZMP relationship to explicitly incorporate phase duration adjustment (T_new = T + δT).
 - A key assumption is that the reference DCM end point (ξ_ref_T) remains constant despite phase duration adjustments, which helps in aligning the updated DCM trajectory with the current measured DCM.
 - This leads to the formulation of a nonlinear equality constraint (F_i(v_i, ξ_err_t,i, t) = 0). This constraint expresses the DCM end-point error (ξ_err_T_new), decomposed into step location adjustment (f_ctrl_T_new) and DCM offset error (b_err_T_new), as a function of the optimization variables. This constraint is crucial for minimizing the DCM offset error by optimizing ZMP modulation, step location adjustment, and phase duration adjustment.
2. **Optimization Variables:** For each previewed phase i, the optimization variable v_i is a 9-dimensional vector comprising:
 - ZMP start control modulation (z_ctrl_0,i)
 - ZMP end control modulation (z_ctrl_T,i)
 - Step location adjustment (f_ctrl_T_new,i)
 - DCM offset error (b_err_T_new,i)
 - Phase duration adjustment (δT_i)
3. **Cost Function:** The objective function minimizes a weighted sum of the squared optimization variables across all n_c previewed phases. The weights (W_v_i) are tuned to prioritize the minimization of the DCM offset error, while also penalizing large ZMP modulations, step location deviations, and phase duration changes.
4. **Constraints:**
 - **Equality Constraints:**
 - Nonlinear DCM error dynamics (F_i = 0): Applied for the current phase (i=1) and ensures continuity of error dynamics for future phases (i=2 to n_c). The DCM start point error of phase i is equal to the DCM end point error of the previous phase.
 - ZMP input continuity (z_ctrl_T,i = z_ctrl_0,i+1): Guarantees smooth ZMP transitions between consecutive phases.
 - **Inequality Constraints:**
 - ZMP feasibility bounds: The ZMP control inputs (z_ctrl_0,i, z_ctrl_T,i) must remain within the physical limits of the support foot polygon.
 - Step location adjustment limits: During SSP, step adjustments are confined to the robot's reachable region. During DSP, step location adjustments are disabled (f_ctrl_T_new,i = 0). This is managed using a standard big-M method with binary indicators for alternating SSP/DSP.
 - Phase duration adjustment limits: These prevent excessive swing foot speeds during SSP and abrupt ZMP shifts during DSP.

**Solver Implementation:**
The NMPC problem, being a constrained nonlinear optimization, is solved using Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP). The active-set QP solver qpOASES is used within each SQP iteration. For real-time performance, the number of SQP iterations is limited, and early termination criteria are applied.

**System Specifications:**
The proposed method was validated using simulations in MuJoCo and hardware experiments on the TOCABI humanoid robot (1.8m tall, 100kg, 33 degrees of freedom). The NMPC and walking pattern generator operated at 100 Hz, while the main control loop ran at 2 kHz. The preview horizon (n_c) for the NMPC was set to three phases. RBDL was used for kinematics and dynamics computations. Reference SSP and DSP durations were set to 0.6 s and 0.3 s, respectively.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of the proposed NMPC framework was evaluated through extensive simulations and real-robot experiments.

#### 4.1. Simulations for Comparative Ablation Study on Balance Strategy Combinations

An ablation study was conducted to analyze the impact of different combinations of balance strategies on robustness against external disturbances. Four methods were compared under an 88 N·s external impulse applied to the robot's pelvis in the forward direction:
- **Method 4 (ZMP only):** Resulted in the fastest loss of balance, indicating ZMP control alone was insufficient for large disturbances.
- **Method 3 (ZMP + Step location, fixed SSP/DSP durations):** Also led to a fall. Although step location was optimized, the fixed phase timings meant the robot had to wait for the predetermined step timing, causing balance loss before the adjusted foot placement could be executed.
- **Method 2 (ZMP + Step location + SSP duration adjustment, fixed DSP duration):** Successfully maintained balance.
- **Method 1 (Proposed: ZMP + Step location + SSP/DSP duration adjustments):** Demonstrated faster disturbance rejection compared to Method 2. By actively shortening the DSP duration, the robot transitioned to the next SSP more quickly, enabling more agile stepping and faster re-establishment of a stable support polygon. This suggests that DSP duration adjustment contributes to recovery performance.

#### 4.2. Simulations for Comparative Evaluation of Three Baseline Controllers

The proposed method was compared against three baseline controllers: the LMPC method, the Heuristic method, and the NMPC-w/o-DSP method. All other local controllers were kept identical for fair comparison.

- **Robustness against External Forces:**
 - **Maximum Recoverable Impulses from Various Directions:** The proposed method consistently exhibited higher recoverable impulses across all tested push directions (Fig. 4). For example, in directions between 60° and 120°, it tolerated impulses up to 108 N·s, showing average improvements of 17.5% over the heuristic method and 15.8% over the NMPC-w/o-DSP method. Under backward disturbances (240°–300°), it maintained balance with 48 to 58 N·s impulses, whereas all baselines failed beyond 40 N·s.
 - **Maximum Recoverable Impulses at Various Timings:** The proposed method consistently achieved higher disturbance tolerance across all impact timings within a single step cycle (Fig. 5). This included variations in impulse timing during both SSP and DSP.
 - **Discussion:** The observed performance differences were attributed to the architectural design of the controllers. The heuristic method's stepping mechanism deactivated step adjustment in late SSP and only used ZMP modulation during DSP, limiting its adaptability. The LMPC method activated stepping strategies only when ZMP commands exceeded the support polygon, introducing delays. The NMPC-w/o-DSP method relied on simple DCM feedback during DSP as its formulation was primarily for SSP, thus lacking model-predictive control authority and DSP duration adjustment capability. The proposed method's seamless integration and coordinated use of all strategies across both SSP and DSP, along with its full nonlinear dynamics formulation, addressed these limitations.

- **Robust Walking on Challenging Terrain:**
 - **Uneven Terrain (Table 2, Fig. 6 top):** The proposed method consistently outperformed all baselines. It maintained reliable walking even as maximum elevation differences increased, demonstrating 4/5 success at 0.03m and 2/5 at 0.04m maximum elevation. Baselines showed noticeable performance degradation beyond approximately 0.02m.
 - **Compliant Terrain (Table 3, Fig. 6 bottom):** The proposed method, along with the NMPC-w/o-DSP method, succeeded in all trials across the tested range of ground compliance settings. The LMPC and heuristic methods became less reliable with increasing ground softness, with frequent failures at higher compliance values.
 - **Discussion:** The improved robustness of the proposed method on challenging terrains was attributed to its unified NMPC framework, which allows for coordinated utilization of ZMP modulation, step location, and phase duration adjustments. This integrated approach, which can deploy stepping strategies earlier and more consistently than sequential or heuristic methods, proved advantageous in scenarios with persistent disturbances. The proposed method's ability to incorporate ZMP modulation and duration adjustment during DSP contributed to additional performance gains compared to the NMPC-w/o-DSP method, particularly on uneven terrain.

#### 4.3. Hardware Experiments

Real-robot experiments on the TOCABI humanoid validated the robustness of the proposed method under external pushes and unexpected terrain perturbations.

- **Robust Walking under External Push:**
 - **Backward Push (26.98 N·s):** The robot successfully maintained balance. In response to a negative x-direction DCM error, the NMPC saturated the ZMP modulation (at -7 cm) and generated step location adjustments. Crucially, the SSP duration was shortened from 0.60 s to 0.56 s, and the DSP duration was reduced to its lower bound of 0.10 s. The robot executed three backward steps, recovering nominal walking in approximately 1.5 seconds and moving backward by 16.7 cm (Fig. 7).
 - **Lateral Push (21.66 N·s):** The robot maintained balance against a lateral disturbance. A negative y-direction DCM error caused the NMPC to utilize ZMP modulation (to -7 cm) and step location adjustment (to -9 cm). The DSP duration was reduced to 0.18 s. During the subsequent SSP, the NMPC optimized ZMP, step location, and step timing. The robot performed two lateral steps, returning to nominal walking in about 1.3 seconds and moving laterally by 5.9 cm (Fig. 8).
 - **Discussion:** These experiments showed that the proposed method enables coordinated balance control by actively exploiting step adjustments even before ZMP saturation, unlike the LMPC method. Furthermore, its dynamically consistent ZMP modulation and duration adjustment during DSP, facilitated by the nonlinear DCM-ZMP dynamics and constraints, contrasted with the NMPC-w/o-DSP method.

- **Robust Walking under Unexpected Terrain Perturbations:**
 - The robot successfully navigated an unforeseen 3 cm high mat placed at the swing-foot landing location while walking in place (Fig. 9). This was achieved without exteroceptive sensing or ankle force/torque feedback, demonstrating the controller's ability to adapt to sudden changes in ground height through a coordinated combination of ankle and stepping strategies provided by the NMPC and WBC.

Overall, the results indicate that the proposed framework's integrated optimization of multiple balance strategies, including both SSP and DSP duration adjustments, within a phase-consistent nonlinear MPC formulation, provided improved robustness and adaptability compared to established baseline methods across various challenging scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents a phase-based Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) framework that unifies the optimization of Zero Moment Point (ZMP) modulation, step location, Single Support Phase (SSP) duration, and Double Support Phase (DSP) duration for humanoid walking stabilization. This work contributes to the field of humanoid robotics by addressing several limitations present in existing balance control strategies.

The primary significance of this work lies in its comprehensive integration of multiple balance recovery strategies within a single, phase-consistent optimization problem. Unlike previous approaches that often treat DSP as a fixed duration or activate stepping strategies sequentially, this framework provides dynamically consistent control across both SSP and DSP. This architectural choice is demonstrated to contribute to enhanced robustness, particularly under large external disturbances and on challenging, uncertain terrains. The paper provides evidence that this coordinated approach generally performs better than methods relying on heuristic adjustments or linearized dynamics, especially when disturbances are substantial or persistent.

The potential impacts of this research include:

- **Enhanced Humanoid Locomotion Robustness:** The demonstrated improvements in disturbance rejection and stable walking on uneven and compliant terrains suggest that the proposed framework can enable humanoid robots to operate more reliably in unpredictable real-world environments. This capability is critical for deploying humanoids in a wider array of applications, such as disaster response, manufacturing, or personal assistance, where unforeseen interactions and challenging ground conditions are common.
- **Advancement in Model Predictive Control for Humanoids:** The explicit incorporation of nonlinear DCM error dynamics and the joint optimization of all relevant balance parameters, including both phase durations, represent a refinement in the application of NMPC to complex, underactuated systems like humanoids. The method of ensuring ZMP input continuity and dynamically managing footstep adjustments during DSP contributes to the theoretical and practical development of MPC for bipedal locomotion.
- **Foundation for Future Hybrid Control Systems:** The paper concludes by suggesting future work that involves integrating reinforcement learning with the existing model predictive control structure. The robust model-based foundation established by this work could serve as a reliable baseline for learning-based components, enabling adaptive corrections for model mismatches, contact uncertainties, and environmental properties (e.g., friction). Such hybrid approaches have the potential to further extend the versatility and resilience of humanoid locomotion in highly unstructured environments.
- **Practical Applicability:** Validation through hardware experiments on the TOCABI humanoid robot confirms the real-world feasibility of the proposed approach. This moves the research beyond simulation, demonstrating that the computational demands and algorithmic complexities can be managed on actual robotic platforms.

In summary, this research provides a robust, integrated control solution that moves toward more autonomous and adaptable humanoid walking, with implications for both academic advancements in robotic control theory and practical applications of humanoid robots.
