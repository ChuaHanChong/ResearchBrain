# Hide-and-Seek in Trajectories: Discovering Failure Signals for VLA Runtime Monitoring

> [!summary] Summary
> Hide-and-Seek introduces a lightweight, VLA-agnostic framework that localizes failure-indicative actions using only trajectory-level supervision, addressing the limitations of prior failure detection methods. This approach delivers state-of-the-art performance across diverse VLA policies and real-world scenarios, while operating over 2,000 times faster than VLM-based monitors.

## Problem

- High supervision costs for obtaining granular, step-level failure annotations in long-horizon VLA trajectories.
- Label noise introduced by uniformly propagating trajectory-level failure labels across all timesteps in prior methods like SAFE.
- Computational impracticality and high inference overhead of existing methods, such as action resampling or VLM judges, for real-time robotic deployment.

## Method

- **Hide-and-Seek** extracts internal action embeddings from VLA models and processes them with a lightweight LSTM-based sequential detector.
- It employs two novel contrastive losses: an inter-trajectory loss to discriminate salient failure signals between success and failure trajectories, and an intra-trajectory loss to induce temporally structured failure signals within failed trajectories.
- Functional conformal prediction is used for runtime monitoring to convert continuous failure scores into binary alarms while controlling the false alarm rate.

## Results

- Hide-and-Seek consistently outperformed 12 baseline methods across simulation benchmarks (LIBERO-10, VLABench) and a real-world robot (UFactory xArm 6), achieving state-of-the-art balanced accuracy (e.g., 0.852 for OpenVLA on LIBERO-10 seen tasks).
- It demonstrated strong generalization to unseen tasks and various VLA policies (OpenVLA, π0, π0.5), maintaining stable performance across splits and outperforming baselines by up to +**11.7%** bACC and +**15.0%** TWA on unseen real-world tasks.
- The framework achieved a favorable accuracy–timeliness trade-off, operating over 2,000 times faster than VLM-based monitors (0.001s vs. 2.343s per step) while providing prompt failure detection before annotated onsets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Internal action embeddings from VLA models provide richer and more practical failure signals for real-time detection than relying solely on external VLM analysis.
> - Coarsely supervised learning can effectively localize fine-grained failure signals in trajectories by contrasting between and within successful and failed executions.
> - Temporal context captured by a lightweight sequence model (LSTM) is crucial for accurate and timely failure detection, outperforming stateless classifiers in VLA monitoring.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a significant advancement in robotics, enabling agents to interpret natural language instructions and perform complex tasks by learning from diverse, multimodal demonstrations. These models demonstrate generalization capabilities to novel environments and embodiments, suggesting a pathway towards more versatile and intelligent robotic systems. However, despite these advancements, VLAs exhibit susceptibility to execution failures, which impedes their robust deployment in real-world scenarios. Detecting such failures during execution is therefore a critical requirement for enhancing the reliability and safety of embodied systems.

Existing approaches to robotic failure detection present several limitations. One challenge is the high supervision cost associated with obtaining granular, step-level failure annotations, particularly for long-horizon, stochastic trajectories. Methods that rely on propagating trajectory-level failure labels uniformly across all timesteps (e.g., SAFE) introduce substantial label noise, as many actions preceding the actual failure onset are mislabeled. Another limitation pertains to computational practicality, with techniques based on action resampling or external Vision-Language Model (VLM) judges incurring significant inference overhead, thereby precluding real-time deployment. Furthermore, many prior methods are tailored for specialist policies on fixed tasks, which restricts their applicability to the diverse, open-world settings encountered by modern generalist VLAs. This work addresses these challenges by proposing a lightweight failure detector that localizes failure signals without requiring expensive step-level annotations or incurring high computational costs, thereby aiming to improve the reliability and practicality of VLA deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a lightweight and effective runtime failure detection framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The proposed framework, named Hide-and-Seek, aims to discover failure-indicative actions from trajectory-level supervision alone, without requiring costly and difficult-to-scale step-level annotations.

The motivation behind this work stems from several limitations observed in current VLA deployment and existing failure detection methods:

- **Vulnerability of VLA Models:** While VLA models exhibit promising generalization capabilities, they remain susceptible to execution failures that can undermine reliability and increase deployment costs in real-world settings. For example, a subtle grasp failure in a household robot, if undetected, could lead to a dropped item or require costly recovery.
- **High Supervision Cost for Step-Level Annotations:** Obtaining precise, step-level failure annotations across long-horizon, stochastic trajectories is an expensive and labor-intensive process. This cost barrier limits the scalability of failure detection methods that rely on such fine-grained labels.
- **Limitations of Trajectory-Level Label Propagation:** Prior methods, such as SAFE, simplify annotation by uniformly assigning trajectory-level failure labels to all timesteps within a failed trajectory. However, this approach mislabels a significant number of normal actions occurring before the actual failure onset, introducing substantial label noise that can compromise the efficacy of the learned detector. The paper highlights this as a "coarsely supervised learning problem," where fine-grained signals need to be extracted from coarse labels.
- **Computational Impracticality of Existing Approaches:** Certain failure detection methods, particularly those based on action resampling or external VLM judges, incur high inference latency. This computational overhead makes them impractical for real-time deployment in embodied systems, where timely detection is critical for intervention or recovery. For instance, VLM-based monitors might detect failures only after they have fully manifested.
- **Limited Generalizability of Prior Detectors:** Many existing failure detection methods are designed for specialist policies operating on fixed tasks. This specialization restricts their applicability to the diverse and open-world contexts that generalist VLA models are designed to handle.

To address these motivations, Hide-and-Seek aims to:
1. Formulate VLA failure detection as a coarsely supervised learning problem.
2. Localize failure-indicative actions within a trajectory using only trajectory-level success/failure outcomes.
3. Induce temporally structured failure signals that differentiate normal execution from the failure phase.
4. Achieve state-of-the-art failure detection performance with a practical accuracy-timeliness trade-off.
5. Generalize effectively to both seen and unseen tasks and different VLA policies, including autoregressive and flow-matching-based paradigms.
6. Be lightweight and computationally efficient for real-time runtime monitoring.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Hide-and-Seek formulates VLA failure detection as a coarsely supervised learning problem, aiming to localize failure-indicative actions from trajectory-level labels. The approach centers on a lightweight sequential detector and novel contrastive objectives.

**Action Embedding Extraction and Temporal Aggregation:**
At each timestep *t*, a VLA model receives an observation *o<sub>t</sub>* (RGB images, natural language instruction, robot state) and outputs an action *a<sub>t</sub>*. The method extracts an internal action embedding *h<sub>t</sub> ∈ R<sup>d</sup>* from the VLA model's action token or action head. For autoregressive policies (e.g., OpenVLA), embeddings are averaged across layers and degrees of freedom. For flow-matching policies (e.g., π<sub>0</sub>, π<sub>0.5</sub>), hidden states before the velocity prediction head are extracted at the final denoising step. To mitigate temporal redundancy and reduce noise, non-overlapping sliding window aggregation with a window size *w* is applied, using average pooling within each window to produce *N<sub>τ</sub> = ⌈T/w⌉* window-level embeddings, forming a trajectory *τ = (h<sub>1</sub>, h<sub>2</sub>..., h<sub>T</sub>)*.

**Detector Architecture:**
The failure detector, *f<sub>ϕ</sub>*, is instantiated as a lightweight sequence model, typically a single-layer Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network with a hidden size of 256. It processes a trajectory prefix *τ<sub>≤t</sub> = (h<sub>1</sub>, h<sub>2</sub>..., h<sub>t</sub>)* and produces a scalar failure score *s<sub>t</sub> = f<sub>ϕ</sub>(τ<sub>≤t</sub>) ∈* via a sigmoid output layer.

**Training Objectives:**
The core of Hide-and-Seek involves two complementary contrastive losses:

1. **Inter-trajectory Contrastive Loss (L<sub>inter</sub>):** This loss aims to discriminate the most salient failure signals between failure and successful trajectories. For a failure trajectory *τ<sub>f</sub>* from the set of failure trajectories *D<sub>f</sub>*, at least one step is expected to have an elevated failure score. This score should be higher than the most "failure-resembling" action (hardest false-positive) in a successful trajectory *τ<sub>s</sub>* from *D<sub>s</sub>*. The margin-based loss is defined as:
 *L<sub>inter</sub> = (1/|D<sub>f</sub>||D<sub>s</sub>|) Σ<sub>τf∈Df</sub> Σ<sub>τs∈Ds</sub> max(0, m<sub>r</sub> - max<sub>1≤t≤Nτf</sub> s<sub>t</sub><sup>τf</sup> + max<sub>1≤t≤Nτs</sub> s<sub>t</sub><sup>τs</sup>)*
 where *m<sub>r</sub> > 0* is a margin hyperparameter. This objective adaptively discovers salient failure signals without assuming their temporal location and imposes structural regularity.

2. **Intra-trajectory Contrastive Loss (L<sub>intra</sub>):** This loss shapes the score dynamics *within* each failure trajectory to induce a temporally structured failure signal. It identifies a proxy failure onset *t<sub>onset</sub>* as the point of the sharpest increase in failure score: *t<sub>onset</sub> = argmax<sub>t</sub>(s<sub>t</sub> - s<sub>t-1</sub>)*. For failure trajectories, the loss encourages the average post-onset score to exceed the average pre-onset score:
 *L<sub>intra</sub> = (1/|D<sub>f</sub>|) Σ<sub>τf∈Df</sub> max(0, m<sub>o</sub> - (1/(N<sub>τf</sub> - t<sub>onset</sub> + 1)) Σ<sub>t≥tonset</sub> s<sub>t</sub><sup>τf</sup> + (1/(t<sub>onset</sub> - 1)) Σ<sub>t<tonset</sub> s<sub>t</sub><sup>τf</sup>)*
 where *m<sub>o</sub> > 0* is a margin hyperparameter. Gradients for *L<sub>intra</sub>* flow through the score values *s<sub>t</sub>* but not through the *t<sub>onset</sub>* index selection (using `stop-gradient`). This converts trajectory-level supervision into a signal that sharpens the boundary between normal execution and the failure phase.

**Overall Objective:**
The complete training objective is a weighted combination of the two losses: *L = L<sub>inter</sub> + λ L<sub>intra</sub>*, where *λ* controls the loss weight. A warmup schedule is used where *L<sub>inter</sub>* is optimized alone initially until *t<sub>onset</sub>* stabilizes, after which *L<sub>intra</sub>* is introduced.

**Runtime Monitoring via Conformal Prediction:**
After training, the detector is deployed to monitor VLA execution. At each timestep *t*, it produces a failure score *s<sub>t</sub>*. To convert this into a binary alarm, functional conformal prediction (CP) is employed. This method constructs a time-varying prediction band, *ζ<sub>t</sub> = μ<sub>t</sub> + h · σ(t)*, from a held-out calibration set of successful trajectories. *μ<sub>t</sub>* is the per-step mean failure score for successful trajectories, *σ(t)* is a modulation function capturing typical score spread, and *h* is a quantile calibrated on maximum modulated deviations. An alarm is raised at the earliest timestep *t* where *s<sub>t</sub> ≥ ζ<sub>t</sub>*. This provides a statistically principled way to control the false alarm rate on successful trajectories, under a user-specified significance level *α*.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Hide-and-Seek was conducted on two simulation benchmarks (LIBERO-10, VLABench) and a real-world robotic platform (UFactory xArm 6), using three VLA policies: OpenVLA, π<sub>0</sub>, and π<sub>0.5</sub>. Performance was assessed using balanced accuracy (bACC), weighted accuracy (wACC), and time-weighted accuracy (TWA), averaged over multiple random seeds and false alarm rates (α).

**Simulation Environment Performance:**
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Hide-and-Seek consistently outperformed all 12 baseline methods across all VLA policies and benchmarks. For example, on LIBERO-10 with OpenVLA, it achieved 0.852 bACC on seen tasks and 0.834 bACC on unseen tasks, significantly surpassing the next best baseline, SAFE-MLP, which scored 0.823 bACC (seen) and 0.775 bACC (unseen).
- **Generalization to Unseen Tasks:** The method demonstrated strong generalization capabilities to unseen tasks. Unlike many OOD detection-based methods, which often degrade substantially on unseen tasks (e.g., Mahalanobis dropping from 67.0% to 51.3% bACC on OpenVLA), Hide-and-Seek maintained stable and high performance.
- **Superiority over Classifier-Based Baselines:** Compared to classifier-based methods like SAFE-LSTM and SAFE-MLP, which uniformly propagate trajectory labels, Hide-and-Seek showed notable improvements. It outperformed SAFE-MLP by +6.8% bACC and +8.1% TWA on seen tasks, and +7.2% bACC and +10.8% TWA on unseen tasks on VLABench with π<sub>0.5</sub>. This indicates the effectiveness of adaptively discovering failure-indicative moments.

**Real-World Robot Performance:**
- **Robust Real-World Deployment:** On a UFactory xArm 6 platform, Hide-and-Seek consistently outperformed all baselines for both CUBE and KITCHEN task suites, on both seen and unseen tasks. It achieved the best TWA across all four settings, with gains such as +11.7% bACC and +15.0% TWA over SAFE-MLP on unseen CUBE tasks. The method maintained stable performance across splits, suggesting effective transferability of discovered failure signals to real-world execution.

**Accuracy–Timeliness Trade-off:**
- **Prompt and Accurate Detection:** Hide-and-Seek achieved a favorable accuracy–timeliness trade-off, consistently located in the top-left region of accuracy-detection time curves (Figure 3), indicating earlier and more accurate failure detection compared to baselines.
- **Alignment with Failure Onset:** The method raised alarms slightly before the GPT-5.2 annotated failure onset (positive detection lag, e.g., +0.042 for OpenVLA seen tasks), enabling timely intervention. In contrast, SAFE-MLP often exhibited delayed detection.

**Ablation Studies and Additional Analysis:**
- **Detector Architecture:** Context-utilizing backbones (LSTM, GRU, Transformer) significantly outperformed a simple MLP, underscoring the importance of temporal context. The LSTM generally performed best among the recurrent architectures, showing strong and consistent performance.
- **Loss Component Analysis:** The inter-trajectory contrastive loss (L<sub>inter</sub>) alone already achieved strong performance, surpassing the best baseline. Combining it with the intra-trajectory contrastive loss (L<sub>intra</sub>) yielded further improvements (+1.8% on OpenVLA, +3.2% on π<sub>0</sub>), demonstrating that L<sub>intra</sub> effectively sharpens the implicitly discovered onset signal.
- **Efficiency and Effectiveness vs. VLM Monitors:** Hide-and-Seek significantly outperformed a VLM-based runtime monitor (Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct) by +13.1% bACC while operating over 2,000x faster (0.001s vs. 2.343s per step). This indicates that VLA latent action embeddings provide richer and more practical failure signals for real-time deployment than visual observations alone.
- **Comparison with Uniform Labeling:** The approach substantially outperformed a baseline that uniformly assigned trajectory-level labels to every timestep (+19.4% bACC on OpenVLA, +18.6% on π<sub>0</sub>), confirming that uniform supervision introduces significant label noise.
- **Robustness to Hyperparameters:** Performance remained stable across a range of loss margin hyperparameters, indicating robustness to margin choices.
- **Effect of Window Size:** Moderate window sizes (e.g., w=4 for OpenVLA, w=8 for π<sub>0</sub>) improved performance, suggesting that temporal aggregation effectively reduces noise while preserving informative dynamics, with optimal window size potentially aligning with the policy’s action chunking structure.
- **Failure Onset Proxy Validation:** The proxy failure onset *t<sub>onset</sub>* (point of sharpest score increase) closely approximated human-annotated failure onsets, performing on par with an oracle onset and significantly better than using the peak score timestep.
- **Layer-wise Embeddings:** Mid-to-late transformer layers provided slightly stronger and more stable performance than early layers for OpenVLA, but layer-averaged representations performed comparably or better, removing the need for specific layer selection.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Hide-and-Seek framework introduces a novel connection between coarsely supervised learning and embodied failure detection, representing a significant advancement for the robust deployment of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in real-world settings.

The primary significance of this work lies in its ability to address critical limitations of existing failure detection methodologies:
- **Reduced Annotation Burden:** By effectively discovering failure-indicative actions from trajectory-level supervision alone, Hide-and-Seek eliminates the need for expensive and labor-intensive step-level annotations. This makes failure detection more scalable and practical for complex, long-horizon robotic tasks.
- **Enhanced Real-time Practicality:** The method's lightweight nature and computational efficiency, demonstrated by operating over 2,000 times faster than VLM-based monitors, enable real-time runtime monitoring. This is crucial for practical deployment scenarios where timely detection is necessary for intervention.
- **Improved Accuracy and Timeliness:** The proposed inter-trajectory and intra-trajectory contrastive objectives enable the detector to localize critical failure signals and induce temporally structured scores. This results in state-of-the-art multi-task failure detection performance, with alarms raised promptly near the actual failure onset, providing a practical accuracy–timeliness trade-off.
- **Generalization Across Policies and Tasks:** Hide-and-Seek consistently outperforms baselines on both seen and unseen tasks across diverse VLA policies (OpenVLA, π<sub>0</sub>, π<sub>0.5</sub>) and benchmarks (LIBERO, VLABench, real-world platform). This broad applicability enhances its utility for generalist robotic systems.
- **Robustness to Label Noise:** The method's core mechanism of contrasting specific failure-indicative actions against non-failure actions directly addresses the label noise introduced by uniform propagation of trajectory-level labels, leading to more reliable failure signals.

The potential impact of this research extends to improving the safety, reliability, and autonomy of robotic systems:
- **Enabling Proactive Intervention and Recovery:** The ability to detect failures early, before they fully manifest or become irreversible, opens avenues for closed-loop control systems. Robots can pause execution, request human assistance, or initiate recovery procedures, minimizing damage and improving task success rates.
- **Foundation for Adaptive and Interactive Systems:** Early failure signals can be integrated into systems that require clarification for ambiguous instructions, generate additional demonstrations, or leverage human-in-the-loop assistance when high failure risk is detected.
- **Broad Applicability in Embodied AI:** The framework's architecture-agnostic design and demonstrated performance across different VLA paradigms suggest its potential for widespread adoption in various embodied AI applications, from industrial automation to household robotics.

While the work shows promise, potential impacts also include considerations for false negatives (missed failures) and false positives (unnecessary interventions). The authors position Hide-and-Seek as a practical monitoring component within a broader system, acknowledging the need for complementary safeguards. Overall, this research significantly contributes to making VLA-powered robots more dependable and safer for real-world interaction.
