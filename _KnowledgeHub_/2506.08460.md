# MOBODY: Model Based Off-Dynamics Offline Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> An offline reinforcement learning framework called MOBODY addresses the challenge of learning policies for target environments with mismatched dynamics by explicitly learning target dynamics and leveraging them for exploration. The framework improves performance by an average of 44% over baselines in tasks with gravity and friction shifts, demonstrating robustness in scenarios with significant dynamics discrepancies.

## Problem

- Learning policies for a target environment using a primary offline dataset from a source (e.g., simulator) and a limited offline dataset from the target (e.g., real-world), where dynamics are mismatched.
- Existing methods for 'off-dynamics offline RL' are conservative, optimizing policies primarily in 'low-shift regions' of the state-action space.
- This conservatism restricts exploration, leading to suboptimal performance when optimal target trajectories are in 'high-shift regions' or require actions outside low-shift data.

## Method

- **MOBODY** (Model-Based Off-Dynamics Offline Reinforcement Learning) explicitly learns the ==target dynamics== by decomposing the dynamics model into separate action encoders (for source and target) and shared state encoder and transition function.
- It generates 'fake' target domain transitions by rolling out the current policy within the learned target dynamics model, augmenting the training data.
- Policy optimization uses an enhanced dataset (including real target data, augmented source data, and fake rollouts) combined with a target Q-weighted behavior cloning loss to prioritize actions with high estimated target-domain Q-values.

## Results

- MOBODY achieved an average improvement of **44%** over the best-performing baseline, REAG, across 32 MuJoCo tasks involving gravity and friction shifts.
- It demonstrated superior performance, particularly in challenging scenarios with significant dynamics shifts (e.g., HalfCheetah-Friction-**0.1**, Ant-Friction-5.0), where other methods largely failed.
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of MOBODY's custom dynamics learning, the cycle transition loss, and the target Q-weighted behavior cloning in improving policy performance and robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing the dynamics model with shared latent state representations and domain-specific action encoders effectively leverages limited target data and abundant source data under dynamics mismatch.
> - Generating synthetic rollouts using learned target dynamics enables effective policy exploration beyond the static offline datasets, addressing the limitations of conservative existing methods.
> - A target Q-weighted behavior cloning loss acts as a robust regularization mechanism, guiding policy optimization towards high-value actions in the target domain while mitigating out-of-distribution issues.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) typically involves an agent learning a policy through continuous interaction with an environment to maximize cumulative reward. While successful in various domains, RL often faces practical limitations such as safety concerns or high interaction costs. Offline Reinforcement Learning (Offline RL) addresses these limitations by learning policies solely from pre-collected datasets, eliminating the need for real-time environmental interaction.

This paper specifically focuses on "off-dynamics offline RL," a sub-area within offline RL and domain adaptation. In this setting, the agent must learn a policy for a target environment using a primary offline dataset from a source environment (e.g., a simulator) and a smaller, limited offline dataset from the target environment (e.g., a real-world system). The core challenge lies in the "mismatched dynamics" between the source and target environments, meaning the transition probabilities from a given state and action to the next state differ between the two domains. The reward function, however, is assumed to be consistent across domains.

Existing approaches to off-dynamics offline RL generally fall into two categories:
1. **Reward Regularization Methods**: These methods, such as DARA (Liu et al., 2022) or REAG (Wang et al., 2026), modify the reward signal in the source data based on factors like state visitation frequency or estimated dynamics gaps. The goal is to make the source data more relevant to the target domain.
2. **Data Filtering Methods**: Approaches like BOSA (Liu et al., 2024a) or VGDF (Xu et al., 2023) identify and remove source transitions that exhibit a high dynamics shift relative to the target domain.

A common limitation of these existing methods is their tendency to optimize the policy primarily using data from "low-shift regions"—parts of the state-action space where the dynamics mismatch is minimal. This conservative approach restricts the agent's ability to explore potentially high-reward states in the target domain that may reside in "high-shift regions" or require trajectories outside the scope of low-shift data. This can lead to suboptimal performance, especially when dynamics shifts are significant, or the optimal target trajectories are not well-represented in the low-shift data.

MOBODY seeks to address this limitation by introducing a model-based approach to off-dynamics offline RL, which aims to explicitly learn the target dynamics and leverage them for exploration, thereby allowing the policy to venture beyond conservative low-shift regions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an off-dynamics offline reinforcement learning algorithm that can effectively learn policies for a target environment with mismatched dynamics, particularly in scenarios where the dynamics shift is substantial or optimal trajectories extend into high-dynamics-shift regions. The authors aim to overcome the limitations of existing reward regularization and data filtering methods, which tend to confine policy optimization to regions of low dynamics shift.

The core motivation is rooted in the observation that current methods often fail when:
1. **Significant Dynamics Shift**: The discrepancy between source and target dynamics is large.
2. **Optimal Trajectories Outside Low-Shift Regions**: The most rewarding actions and state transitions in the target domain do not align with the regions of minimal dynamics mismatch.

To address these issues, the authors pose the question: Can a policy be directly optimized using learned target dynamics to enable broader exploration of the target domain, rather than being restricted to only low-dynamics-shift regions?

This motivation leads to two specific, detailed objectives:
1. **Develop a Robust Target Dynamics Learning Mechanism**: Given the scarcity of target-domain offline data and the inherent dynamics mismatch, a critical objective is to design a method capable of accurately learning target-domain dynamics by effectively utilizing both the limited target data and the abundant, but mismatched, source data. This involves identifying and leveraging shared structural knowledge between domains while explicitly accounting for domain-specific differences.
2. **Integrate Dynamics Learning into Policy Optimization for Enhanced Exploration**: The learned target dynamics should be used to generate synthetic (rollout) data, allowing the policy to explore the target domain beyond the confines of the original offline datasets. This requires a policy optimization strategy that can effectively incorporate this augmented data while mitigating risks associated with out-of-distribution actions and model inaccuracies. Specifically, the authors aim to introduce a regularization mechanism that guides the policy towards actions with high estimated target-domain Q-values.

By achieving these objectives, MOBODY aims to enable more effective policy learning in complex off-dynamics offline RL settings, leading to policies that can better adapt and perform in the true target environment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MOBODY (Model-Based Off-Dynamics Offline Reinforcement Learning) proposes a model-based approach to address the off-dynamics offline RL problem. The methodology is divided into two main components: learning the target dynamics and optimizing the policy using these learned dynamics.

#### 3.1. Learning the Target Dynamics

The central challenge in dynamics learning for off-dynamics RL is the limited target data and the fundamental difference in transition dynamics between source and target domains. MOBODY addresses this by decomposing the dynamics model and employing representation learning techniques.

**Decomposition of the Dynamics**:
The authors observe that while dynamics differ (i.e., $p_{src}(s'|s,a) \neq p_{trg}(s'|s,a)$), high-level structural knowledge (e.g., robot motion) can be shared. To achieve the same next state from the same current state, different actions are often required in the two domains. This insight motivates the use of:
- **Separate Action Encoders**: $\psi_{src}$ and $\psi_{trg}$ are learned for source and target actions, respectively, to encode them into a shared latent action representation.
- **Shared State Encoder and Transition Function**: $\phi_E$ maps raw states $s$ to a latent state representation $z_s = \phi_E(s)$. A unified transition function $\phi_T$ then maps the combined latent state and action representation ($z_s + \psi(z_s, a)$) to the predicted next state $\hat{s}' = \phi_T(z_s + \psi(z_s, a))$. This additive structure for state-action representation is motivated by common model-based RL implementations.

**Representation Learning Techniques and Loss Functions**:
The dynamics model (comprising $\phi_E$, $\phi_T$, $\psi_{src}$, $\psi_{trg}$) is trained using a combination of loss functions:

1. **Transition Loss ($L_{dyn}$)**: This is a standard Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss that minimizes the difference between the predicted next state ($\hat{s}'$) and the true next state ($s'$). Separate transition losses are computed for source ($L_{src}^{dyn}$) and target ($L_{trg}^{dyn}$) data, leveraging their respective action encoders ($\psi_{src}$ and $\psi_{trg}$). This loss primarily drives the learning of the shared transition function $\phi_T$.

 $L_{src}^{dyn} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \|s' - \phi_T(z_s + \psi_{src}(z_s, a))\|^2$
 $L_{trg}^{dyn} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \|s' - \phi_T(z_s + \psi_{trg}(z_s, a))\|^2$

2. **Encoder Loss ($L_{rep}$)**: This loss encourages the latent state-action representation ($z_s + \psi(z_s, a)$) to be close to the representation of the next state ($\phi_E(s')$). It aims to refine the action encoders by pushing the state-action embedding towards the corresponding next state embedding. A stopping gradient is applied to $\phi_E(s')$ to ensure the encoder loss primarily influences $\psi$. Separate encoder losses are used for source ($L_{src}^{rep}$) and target ($L_{trg}^{rep}$).

 $L_{src}^{rep} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \|\text{stop_grad}(\phi_E(s')) - (z_s + \psi_{src}(z_s, a))\|^2$
 $L_{trg}^{rep} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \|\text{stop_grad}(\phi_E(s')) - (z_s + \psi_{trg}(z_s, a))\|^2$

3. **Cycle Transition Loss ($L_{cycle}$)**: To enhance the quality of state representation $\phi_E$ and prevent mode collapse, a VAE-style objective is introduced. This loss reconstructs the input state $s$ from its latent representation $z_s$ via the transition function $\phi_T$. It treats $\phi_E$ as an encoder and $\phi_T$ as a decoder, effectively ensuring $\phi_E$ learns a meaningful state representation that $\phi_T$ can decode. The loss includes a reconstruction term and a KL divergence term (for VAE's latent space regularization).

 $L_{cycle} = \frac{1}{2N}\sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{j=1}^{d_z} (\mu_{i,j}^2 + \sigma_{i,j}^2 - \log \sigma_{i,j}^2 - 1) + \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \|s_i - \hat{s}_i\|^2$
 where $\hat{s} = \phi_T(\phi_E(s) + 0)$ (i.e., with zero action input).

**Reward Learning and Uncertainty Quantification (UQ)**:
- **Reward Model**: Since the reward function $r(s, a, s')$ is assumed to be domain-agnostic, a shared reward model $\hat{r}(s, a, s')$ is learned from the combined source and target datasets. The loss minimizes MSE for both true next states and predicted next states to prepare for inference where only predicted next states are available.
- **Uncertainty Quantification**: An ensemble of $N=7$ dynamics models is trained. The uncertainty $u(s, a)$ is defined as the maximum standard deviation of the predicted next states across the ensemble's state dimensions. The estimated reward for policy learning is penalized by this uncertainty: $\tilde{r}(s, a, s') = \hat{r}(s, a, s') - \beta u(s, a)$.

The total dynamics learning loss combines these components:
$\min E_{D_{src}} [L_{src}^{dyn}] + E_{D_{trg}} [L_{trg}^{dyn}] + E_{D_{src} \cup D_{trg}} [L_{reward} + \lambda_{rep}(L_{cycle} + L_{rep})]$
where $L_{rep} = L_{src}^{rep} + L_{trg}^{rep}$ and $\lambda_{rep}$ is a scalar weight.

#### 3.2. Policy Learning with Target Q-Weighted Behavior Cloning Loss

Once the target dynamics are learned, MOBODY proceeds with model-based offline RL training.

**Enhanced Target Data ($D_{enhanced}$)**:
The Q-function and policy are learned using an "enhanced" dataset:
$D_{enhanced} = D_{src\_aug} \cup D_{trg} \cup D_{fake}$
- $D_{trg}$: Original limited target dataset.
- $D_{fake}$: New target domain transitions generated by rolling out the current policy within the learned target dynamics model. This allows for exploration beyond the static offline data.
- $D_{src\_aug}$: Source data ($D_{src}$) augmented with DARA (Dynamics-Aware Reward Augmentation) regularization. DARA uses domain classifiers to penalize rewards of source transitions that exhibit a large dynamics shift, making them more aligned with the target domain.

**Learning the Q-function**:
Standard Temporal Difference (TD) learning is used to learn the Q-functions ($Q_\theta$) based on the $D_{enhanced}$ dataset, following common off-policy RL practices.

$\min L_Q = \min E_{D_{enhanced}} [\frac{1}{2} (r + \gamma \max_{a'} Q_{\theta'}(s', a') - Q_\theta(s, a))^2]$

**Policy Optimization with Target Q-weighted Behavior Cloning**:
To address the challenge of out-of-distribution (OOD) actions in offline RL and the specific dynamics shift in off-dynamics settings, MOBODY introduces a target Q-weighted behavior cloning (BC) loss. Unlike vanilla BC, which may lead to suboptimal performance by imitating source actions that perform poorly in the target domain, this loss prioritizes actions with high target-domain Q-values. It is inspired by advantage-weighted regression but uses the Q-value directly, avoiding the need for an additional value network and simplifying optimization.

The policy loss is formulated as:
$\pi = \arg \min_{\pi} \left[ - E_{(s,a) \in D_{enhanced}} [\lambda Q(s, \pi(s))] + E_{(s,a) \in D_{src\_aug} \cup D_{trg}} \left[ \text{exp}\left( \frac{Q(s, \pi(s))}{\frac{1}{N}\sum_i |Q(s_i, \pi(s_i))|} \right) (\pi(s) - a)^2 \right] \right]$
where $\lambda$ is a scaling factor to balance the Q-value term and the weighted BC term, and the exponential weighting up-weights the BC loss for actions with higher Q-values.

This comprehensive approach combines model-based learning for dynamics extrapolation with careful policy regularization to navigate the challenges of off-dynamics offline reinforcement learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MOBODY was conducted on MuJoCo (HalfCheetah, Ant, Walker2d, Hopper) and Adroit (Pen, Door) environments, encompassing various types and levels of dynamics shifts, including gravity, friction, kinematics, and morphology. The results are presented as normalized scores relative to random and expert policies.

**Performance on MuJoCo Gravity/Friction Shift**:
- MOBODY demonstrated an average improvement of **44%** over the best-performing baseline, REAG, across 32 tasks involving gravity and friction shifts.
- It achieved the best or second-best performance in 28 out of 32 tasks.
- The improvements were particularly pronounced in **challenging scenarios with significant dynamics shifts** (e.g., HalfCheetah-Friction-0.1, Ant-Friction-5.0, Walker2d-Friction-5.0), where existing reward regularization or data filtering methods struggled significantly or failed. This indicates MOBODY's superior ability to explore high-reward states outside low-shift regions.

**Performance on MuJoCo Kinematics/Morphology and Adroit Manipulation Shift**:
- MOBODY achieved a higher overall score, showing an average **25%** improvement across these tasks compared to baselines.
- It performed best in 29 out of 40 tasks in these categories.
- Baseline methods like DARA and BOSA did not show significant improvements over a basic IQL, suggesting that their assumptions (e.g., KL divergence between dynamics, reliance on limited target dynamics models) can degrade performance when dynamics shifts are complex or target data is scarce.
- In a few instances, MOBODY slightly underperformed SRPO or other baselines (less than 1.7% difference), primarily in cases where all methods achieved very low rewards (indicating extreme difficulty) or where baselines already performed very well under small shifts, reducing the observable benefit of MOBODY's exploration.

**Ablation Studies**:
The ablation studies systematically investigated the contribution of MOBODY's key components:

1. **Effectiveness of Dynamics Learning (A1)**: Replacing MOBODY's custom dynamics learning with a model trained solely on limited target data resulted in significant performance degradation. This validates the necessity of MOBODY's specialized dynamics model, which leverages both source and target data with separate action encoders and shared components.
2. **Effectiveness of Policy Learning (A2)**: Substituting MOBODY's policy learning (including Q-weighted BC) with Conservative Q-Learning (CQL) led to reduced performance. This indicated that MOBODY's policy optimization effectively utilizes the learned dynamics for exploration and regularization.
3. **Role of Cycle Transition Loss (A3)**: Removing the cycle transition loss from the dynamics model resulted in performance degradation in most settings, suggesting its importance in learning robust state representations and preventing mode collapse.
4. **Role of Target Q-weighted Behavior Cloning (A4)**: Replacing the target Q-weighted BC loss with a vanilla BC loss led to underperformance in most scenarios. This demonstrates the regularization benefits of prioritizing actions with high target-domain Q-values.
5. **Comparison of Dynamics Learning Approaches**: MOBODY consistently outperformed alternative dynamics learning strategies (target-only, combined source+target, pretrain-finetune) in both rollout MSE (lower error) and policy reward (higher score). This reinforces the benefit of MOBODY's approach in explicitly learning shared structure while accounting for domain-specific dynamics differences.
6. **Model-Based Rollout as Main Driver**: A comparison of variants with and without model-based rollouts revealed that incorporating MOBODY's rollouts provided substantial performance gains. This indicates that the novel dynamics learning and subsequent exploration are the primary sources of improvement. While the Q-weighted BC itself showed less standalone improvement, it served as an essential auxiliary regularizer that further boosted performance when combined with rollouts.
7. **Empirical Validation of Target Q-weighted BC**: Direct comparison showed that the target Q-weighted BC loss performed better than an IQL-style advantage-weighted regression for BC in the MOBODY framework. The authors suggest this is due to the simpler form, avoiding an extra value network, and its direct focus on high Q-values rather than potentially diminishing advantages.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MOBODY presents a novel approach to off-dynamics offline reinforcement learning, addressing a critical limitation in the field: the inability of existing methods to effectively explore and learn policies in high-dynamics-shift regions of the target environment. Its significance and potential impact stem from several aspects:

1. **Enhanced Exploration in Mismatched Dynamics**: By explicitly learning target-domain dynamics and using them to generate synthetic rollouts, MOBODY enables policy exploration beyond the low-shift regions favored by previous reward regularization or data filtering methods. This is particularly impactful in scenarios with large dynamics shifts where optimal target policies may diverge significantly from source behavior.
2. **Robustness to Significant Dynamics Mismatches**: The empirical results demonstrate that MOBODY outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, especially in challenging environments with pronounced dynamics shifts (e.g., extreme gravity or friction changes). This indicates a higher degree of robustness and generalization capability compared to prior work.
3. **Novel Dynamics Learning Framework**: The proposed dynamics learning architecture, which combines shared state representations and a unified transition function with separate, domain-specific action encoders, represents a novel strategy for leveraging limited target data and abundant source data under dynamics mismatch. This framework effectively captures both commonalities and differences across domains.
4. **Effective Policy Regularization**: The introduction of a target Q-weighted behavior cloning loss provides an effective mechanism for guiding policy optimization. By favoring actions with high target-domain Q-values, it mitigates the risks of out-of-distribution actions inherent in offline RL while specifically accounting for dynamics shifts.
5. **Potential for Data Augmentation in Offline RL**: MOBODY demonstrates the potential of carefully learned dynamics models for data augmentation in offline RL, extending the utility of limited target datasets and mismatched source data. This could be particularly valuable in real-world applications where target-domain data collection is costly or impractical.

**Limitations and Future Work**:
The authors acknowledge certain limitations, which also point to future research directions:
- **Shared Structure Assumption**: MOBODY relies on the assumption of a common state representation and a shared transition function in the latent space. While empirically validated across various shifts, extreme dynamics mismatches might violate this assumption, necessitating more advanced domain adaptation or zero-shot transfer techniques.
- **Sparse Reward Settings**: Similar to many existing methods, MOBODY currently struggles in environments with sparse rewards (e.g., Antmaze). Addressing sparse-reward off-dynamics RL represents a significant future challenge that may require fundamentally different methodological approaches.

Overall, MOBODY contributes to the growing field of offline reinforcement learning by providing a model-based solution specifically tailored for off-dynamics scenarios. Its ability to improve performance and robustness under significant domain shifts offers a step towards more practical and reliable deployment of RL systems in real-world applications.
