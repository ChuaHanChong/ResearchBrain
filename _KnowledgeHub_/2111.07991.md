# LiT: Zero-Shot Transfer with Locked-image text Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> Google Research introduces Locked-image Text Tuning (LiT), a method that aligns a randomly initialized text encoder with a frozen, powerful pre-trained image encoder using contrastive learning on image-text data. This approach achieves 85.2% zero-shot ImageNet accuracy and 82.5% on ObjectNet, significantly improving efficiency by requiring 40 times less data compared to training both encoders from scratch.

## Problem

- Existing zero-shot transfer methods like CLIP and ALIGN require simultaneous training of large image and text encoders from scratch, leading to high computational cost and vast data requirements.
- Coupling image representation learning with vision-language alignment can lead to sub-optimal visual representations when trained on noisy web-scale image-text data.
- The need for more data- and compute-efficient ways to build high-performing zero-shot vision-language models.

## Method

- The ==Locked-image Text Tuning (LiT)== method, specifically the `Lu` strategy, ==freezes a powerful, pre-trained image encoder==, leveraging its already strong visual representations.
- A randomly initialized text encoder is trained using a ==contrastive loss== to align its embeddings with the fixed embeddings of the frozen image encoder, using image-text pairs.
- This approach ==decouples== the learning of general visual features from the task of vision-language alignment, significantly reducing computational load and allowing reuse of existing powerful image models.

## Results

- Achieved a new state-of-the-art zero-shot ImageNet accuracy of **85.2%**, effectively halving the gap to supervised fine-tuning.
- Set a new record of **82.5%** accuracy on ObjectNet, outperforming CLIP by **10.2%** and a fine-tuned ViT-g/14 by over **10%**.
- Demonstrated significantly improved data efficiency, reaching **81.7%** ImageNet accuracy with **300 million** image-text pairs, in contrast to **12.8 billion** pairs needed by from-scratch methods like CLIP for a lower score.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Locking the pre-trained image encoder preserves its general visual representations, preventing specialization to potentially noisy image-text data and leading to superior zero-shot transfer performance.
> - The quality and generality of the image features, often learned from large, clean image-only datasets, are more critical for zero-shot performance than training the image model directly on image-text pairs.
> - Decoupling image feature learning and text alignment significantly enhances both training efficiency and zero-shot transfer accuracy across diverse datasets and architectures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper is positioned at the intersection of transfer learning, zero-shot learning, and contrastive vision-language modeling.

**Transfer Learning** has long been a dominant paradigm in computer vision, where models pre-trained on large, often supervised datasets (like ImageNet) are then fine-tuned for downstream tasks. This approach typically requires task-specific labeled data for fine-tuning.

**Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL)** takes this a step further, aiming to classify unseen categories at test time without any labeled examples for those categories during training. Traditional ZSL often relies on semantic side information (e.g., attributes) to transfer knowledge. More recently, the field has evolved towards **Zero-Shot Transfer**, where models are pre-trained on diverse data (often web-scale image-text pairs) and then directly applied to new tasks without any task-specific fine-tuning or data. This is akin to the prompting paradigm seen in large language models like GPT-3, where natural language instructions guide the model.

Key to this recent surge in zero-shot transfer capabilities are models like **CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training)** by OpenAI and **ALIGN (A Large-scale Image-text Dataset for Learning General-purpose Visual Representations)** by Google. These methods employ contrastive learning to align image and text embeddings by training both an image encoder and a text encoder from scratch on vast amounts of noisy image-text pairs scraped from the web. While highly effective, these approaches are computationally intensive and require training both large image and text models simultaneously from scratch, demanding immense data and compute resources.

LiT builds directly upon the contrastive learning framework of CLIP and ALIGN but proposes a more **data- and compute-efficient strategy**. It addresses a perceived inefficiency: while image-text data is excellent for learning *correspondences* between modalities, it might not be the *optimal* source for learning high-quality, general-purpose *image descriptors* themselves. Instead, excellent image descriptors can be learned from large, well-curated, purely image-based datasets (e.g., JFT-300M, ImageNet-21k) using supervised or self-supervised methods. LiT aims to combine the strengths of these two worlds: leveraging powerful pre-trained image models for their robust visual features, and then using image-text data solely to align a text model with these frozen visual representations. This "decoupling" is a significant departure from the from-scratch approach of CLIP/ALIGN and represents an important advancement in making such powerful zero-shot capabilities more accessible and efficient.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the LiT research are:

1. **Improve Zero-Shot Transfer Performance:** The core goal is to develop models that can perform exceptionally well on new vision tasks (like image classification or retrieval) without requiring any task-specific data or fine-tuning, pushing the boundaries of zero-shot accuracy beyond existing state-of-the-art methods like CLIP and ALIGN.
2. **Enhance Data and Compute Efficiency:** Training large image and text models from scratch on web-scale image-text data (as in CLIP/ALIGN) is extremely resource-intensive. LiT aims to reduce this burden by leveraging already-trained, powerful image models. This allows for faster training iterations and potentially lower hardware requirements for achieving competitive results.
3. **Decouple Image Representation Learning from Vision-Language Alignment:** The authors hypothesize that learning optimal image features and learning the alignment between image and text are distinct tasks. They aim to empirically demonstrate that pre-training image models on clean, large-scale image-only datasets, and then using image-text data *only* for alignment, yields superior performance and efficiency.
4. **Increase Accessibility to High-Performance Zero-Shot Models:** By reusing publicly available pre-trained vision models and potentially public image-text datasets, LiT seeks to lower the barrier for researchers and practitioners to develop powerful zero-shot models, thereby facilitating wider participation and contributions to the field.
5. **Verify Robustness and Generalizability:** The method aims to work reliably across various pre-training methods (supervised, self-supervised), diverse architectures (ResNet, Vision Transformers, MLP-Mixer), and different image-text datasets, demonstrating its broad applicability.
6. **Explore Multilingual Capabilities:** A secondary but important motivation is to investigate how LiT can be extended to multilingual scenarios, potentially enabling zero-shot transfer for non-English languages without requiring extensive translated datasets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of LiT is a novel strategy called **contrastive-tuning**, which refines the established contrastive learning framework for image-text alignment.

**Contrastive Learning Framework:**
The foundation is the widely used contrastive loss, which encourages embeddings of paired images and texts to be similar, while pushing non-paired (negative) embeddings apart. This paper adopts the global contrastive loss, computing it jointly across all devices for better performance, as found in previous works and validated by their ablations.

**Contrastive-Tuning Design Choices:**
The key innovation lies in how the image and text "towers" (encoders) are initialized and trained. The authors introduce a two-character notation to denote these choices:
- **L (Locked):** Weights are frozen (not updated during training), initialized from a pre-trained model.
- **U (Unlocked):** Weights are trainable, initialized from a pre-trained model.
- **u (unlocked/random):** Weights are trainable, randomly initialized.

Thus, a setup like `uu` would mean both image and text towers are randomly initialized and trained (equivalent to training CLIP/ALIGN from scratch). `UU` means both are initialized from pre-trained models and then fine-tuned together.

**Locked-image Tuning (LiT) - The Proposed Method:**
The paper's flagship method is **Lu**, which they explicitly name "Locked-image Tuning" (LiT). In LiT:
1. **Image Tower (L):** An existing, powerful image model (e.g., a Vision Transformer pre-trained on JFT-3B or ImageNet-21k using supervised or self-supervised methods) is used. Crucially, its weights are **locked** (frozen) throughout the contrastive-tuning process. This means the image encoder's learned representations remain fixed.
2. **Text Tower (u):** A text model (e.g., a Transformer-based model like BERT or a ViT-like text encoder) is randomly initialized, and its weights are **unlocked** (trainable).
3. **Alignment:** During training, only the text tower's weights are updated to learn how to produce embeddings that align with the *fixed* representations of the pre-trained image tower. An optional linear projection head is added to each tower to map representations to a common dimensionality for the contrastive loss.

**Underlying Hypothesis:**
The core hypothesis is that a pre-trained image model, particularly one trained on clean, large-scale image datasets, already possesses highly general and robust visual representations. The contrastive-tuning with image-text data then *only needs to teach the text model to "read out" suitable representations from this strong image model* for vision-language alignment, rather than simultaneously re-learning image features. Fine-tuning the image model (as in `Uu` or `UU`) might specialize its representations too much to the potentially noisy image-text data, harming its overall generalizability.

**Data and Computational Aspects:**
- LiT is evaluated on public datasets like YFCC100m and CC12M, as well as a large-scale private dataset of 4 billion image-text pairs (similar to ALIGN).
- The computational efficiency stems from not needing to backpropagate gradients through the large image tower, significantly reducing training time and memory usage. Image embeddings can even be pre-computed if no augmentations are used, further accelerating the process.
- The method is tested with various image architectures (ResNet, ViT, MLP-Mixer) and pre-training strategies (supervised, DINO, MoCo-v3) for the image tower, and different text architectures (ViT-transformer, T5, mT5, BERT).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents several compelling findings:

1. **State-of-the-Art Zero-Shot Transfer Accuracy:**
 - With a powerful pre-trained ViT-g/14 image model (from JFT-3B), LiT achieves an unprecedented **85.2% zero-shot transfer accuracy on ImageNet**. This halves the gap between previous best zero-shot methods (CLIP, ALIGN, around 76%) and supervised fine-tuning results on ImageNet (around 90%).
 - On challenging out-of-distribution (OOD) datasets like **ObjectNet**, LiT sets a new state-of-the-art with **82.5% accuracy**, outperforming CLIP by 10.2% and a fine-tuned ViT-g/14 by over 10%. Consistent improvements are observed across ImageNet-v2, -R, -A, and -ReaL.

2. **Superior Data and Compute Efficiency:**
 - LiT achieves 81.7% ImageNet accuracy with only 300 million seen image-text pairs, whereas previous from-scratch methods (like CLIP) required 12.8 billion pairs (40 times more data) to reach 76.2%.
 - The ability to reuse pre-trained image models and freeze their weights significantly reduces the computational resources needed for contrastive-tuning, making high-performance zero-shot models more accessible.

3. **Empirical Validation of "Locked" Image Tower (`Lu` > `Uu` > `uu`):**
 - Extensive ablation studies consistently show that **locking the pre-trained image tower (`Lu`) works best** across various datasets and training durations.
 - While initializing both towers from pre-trained models and fine-tuning them (`UU`) or just the image tower (`Uu`) performs better than training both from scratch (`uu`), `Lu` still yields the highest zero-shot transfer accuracy.
 - The reason for `Lu`'s effectiveness is that fine-tuning the image tower (`Uu` or `UU`) leads to a better contrastive loss on the *image-text dataset itself*, but it **worsens the generality of the visual representation** and performs poorer on *out-of-distribution (zero-shot) tasks*. Locking the image tower preserves its strong, general features.

4. **Generality Across Architectures and Pre-training Methods:**
 - LiT is not specific to Vision Transformers; it works reliably with ResNet and MLP-Mixer architectures.
 - It performs well with both supervised (e.g., AugReg on ImageNet-21k) and self-supervised (e.g., DINO, MoCo-v3) pre-trained image models, indicating that the *generality* and *quality* of the image features are more important than the specific pre-training method. Narrowly pre-trained models (e.g., on Places dataset) perform worse on general image-text tasks.

5. **Impact of Text Model Choices:**
 - Increasing text tower capacity consistently improves performance.
 - While initializing the text model with pre-trained weights offers small, consistent improvements, the choice of text model architecture and tokenizer can have a more significant impact, especially on smaller datasets.

6. **De-duplication's Minor Role:**
 - Experiments show that the presence of duplicate examples between upstream (pre-training) and downstream (evaluation) datasets has a negligible impact on LiT's zero-shot transfer results, supporting previous findings that large models may not simply memorize duplicates.

7. **Preliminary Multilingual Potential:**
 - Training LiT on unfiltered, multilingual YFCC100m data (instead of English-only) significantly improves performance on non-English languages (e.g., Thai, Chinese, Arabic) without hurting English performance.
 - Using a multilingually pre-trained text model (like mT5) and a multilingual tokenizer further enhances performance for low-resource languages, suggesting LiT can unlock multimodal models for a wider global audience.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Locked-image Tuning" (LiT) method presented in this paper carries significant implications for the future of computer vision and multimodal AI:

1. **Democratization of Zero-Shot Models:** By drastically improving data and compute efficiency, LiT makes it feasible for researchers and developers with more modest computational resources (relative to those required for training CLIP or ALIGN from scratch) to create high-performing zero-shot vision-language models. This could broaden participation in this advanced field and accelerate innovation.
2. **New Paradigm for Vision-Language Pre-training:** LiT introduces a powerful paradigm shift by decoupling image representation learning from vision-language alignment. This suggests that the "best" way to learn visual features might indeed be different from the "best" way to align them with language. This insight could guide future research towards specialized and optimized pre-training strategies for each modality.
3. **Leveraging Existing Vision Backbones:** LiT provides a practical method to "upgrade" any strong, pre-trained vision model (even those not originally designed for vision-language tasks) into a zero-shot learner by simply attaching and tuning a text tower. This enhances the utility of a vast ecosystem of existing image models.
4. **Unprecedented Zero-Shot Performance:** Halving the gap between from-scratch contrastive learning and task-specific supervised fine-tuning on ImageNet, and setting new state-of-the-art on demanding OOD benchmarks like ObjectNet, positions LiT as a benchmark-setting method that pushes the capabilities of zero-shot transfer.
5. **Foundation for Multilingual Multimodal AI:** The preliminary multilingual experiments are highly promising, demonstrating that LiT can enable zero-shot transfer for diverse languages without the need for extensive, often scarce, translated paired data. This has a profound potential for making powerful AI models more inclusive and globally accessible.
6. **Guidance for Future Research:** The detailed ablations regarding locking mechanisms, pre-training methods, and text model choices provide valuable insights for the community. The open question about further optimizing learning rate schedules for locked/unlocked towers and extending to a broader range of tasks (detection, segmentation, VQA) highlights fertile ground for future work.

**Limitations and Societal Impact:**
The authors acknowledge limitations, such as restricting evaluation to classification and retrieval tasks and noting that retrieval performance doesn't always show as clear a benefit from `Lu` as classification. They also wisely highlight the dual-use nature of such powerful models, emphasizing that while they offer many useful applications, they can also be repurposed for malicious content generation, necessitating further research into ethical AI development and model behavior control.
