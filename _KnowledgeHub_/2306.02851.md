# Scene as Occupancy

> [!summary] Summary
> OccNet reconstructs 3D occupancy from multi-view cameras via a cascade voxel decoder that progressively lifts BEV features into voxel space using temporal self-attention and 3D deformable spatial cross-attention. The occupancy descriptor feeds detection, segmentation, and planning, cutting collision rate 15%-58% over box/BEV-seg baselines.

## Problem

- 3D bounding boxes oversimplify irregular object shapes (e.g. construction-vehicle crane arms), losing fine-grained geometric detail needed for safe planning.
- Prior vision-centric occupancy methods (MonoScene, VoxFormer, OccDepth, TPVFormer) ignore temporal context despite its proven value in BEV perception.
- No coarse-to-fine voxel reconstruction scheme exists; one-stage direct voxel or BEV-only methods trade off performance against computational cost.

## Method

- ==Cascade Voxel Decoder== progressively lifts BEV feature into voxel feature across N steps, increasing height resolution while decreasing channel dimension at each stage.
- ==3D Deformable Attention== extends 2D deformable attention to voxel space so each query samples sparse local 3D points, keeping temporal self-attention tractable.
- **OpenOcc** benchmark: dense nuScenes-based 3D occupancy plus per-voxel flow annotations, built via separate background/foreground point accumulation and hole-filling.

## Results

- OccNet beats TPVFormer by **3.31** mIoU points (**26.98** vs **23.67**) on semantic scene completion, with ResNet50 variant beating BEVDet by **6.99** mIoU points.
- Camera-only OccNet reaches **60.46** mIoU on LiDAR segmentation, close to LiDAR-based RangeNet++ (**65.50**) and beating it on bicycle IoU (**32.58** vs **21.30**).
- Occupancy-based motion planning reduces collision rate by **15%-58%** versus BEV-segmentation or 3D-box-based planning policies across 1s-3s horizons.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified 3D occupancy descriptor generalizes across detection, segmentation, and planning better than task-specific BEV or box representations.
> - Dense annotation (vs sparse LiDAR-derived occupancy) meaningfully improves downstream vision-only 3D perception, favoring investment in annotation density over sparsity.
> - Occupancy-based pretraining transfers better to low-data 3D detection fine-tuning than standard detection pretraining, suggesting occupancy as a general foundation representation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving (AD) systems rely heavily on robust perception to understand the surrounding environment. Traditionally, perception systems have employed various representations:
- **3D Bounding Boxes:** These provide a coarse, simplified representation of objects, typically by fitting an axis-aligned box around them. While useful for high-level detection and tracking, they fail to capture fine-grained geometry, especially for irregularly shaped vehicles or complex scenes. This oversimplification can lead to safety issues in planning, as protruding parts of objects (e.g., a crane arm, an extended ladder) are not explicitly modeled, as highlighted in the paper (Figure 1).
- **Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) Segmentation:** Projects scene elements onto a 2D grid from an overhead perspective, offering a unified representation for various objects and road structures. However, BEV segmentation implicitly collapses height information, making it challenging to differentiate objects at different elevations or to understand 3D occlusions.
- **LiDAR Point Clouds:** Provide dense 3D geometric information, often used for semantic segmentation at the point level. While highly accurate, LiDAR sensors are expensive, can be affected by weather conditions, and their data can be sparse, especially at longer ranges, making holistic scene understanding difficult.

In recent years, the concept of **3D Occupancy** has gained traction, notably advocated by industry leaders like Mobileye and Tesla. 3D Occupancy quantizes the physical 3D scene into a structured grid map, where each cell is assigned a semantic label (e.g., occupied by a car, pedestrian, or empty space). This representation is "geometry-aware," capable of capturing fine details and irregular shapes, bridging the gap between coarse bounding boxes and raw point clouds.

Prior and concurrent research has primarily focused on "3D Semantic Scene Completion (SSC)," a task closely related to 3D occupancy prediction, where the goal is to infer the complete 3D geometry and semantics of a scene from limited input (e.g., a single image or depth map). Works like MonoScene, VoxFormer, OccDepth, and TPVFormer have explored various approaches to SSC.

This paper positions itself within this evolving landscape by addressing several key limitations of prior work:
- **General Descriptor:** Unlike previous works that focus solely on scene completion, this paper proposes 3D occupancy as a *general scene descriptor* that can enhance a *wide span of driving tasks*, including detection, segmentation, and planning. This aims to establish occupancy as a foundational representation for AD.
- **Temporal Information:** It explicitly incorporates temporal context, a crucial aspect for dynamic driving scenes often overlooked in earlier SSC models. Temporal clues are vital for consistent 3D reconstruction and predicting object motion.
- **Coarse-to-Fine Approach:** It adopts a cascade voxel decoder, a progressive refinement strategy that is more efficient and effective for recovering detailed 3D geometric representation compared to one-stage methods.
- **Vision-Centric Solution:** It focuses on vision-only inputs, which are more cost-effective and prevalent in current AD systems, unlike LiDAR-dependent approaches.
- **Benchmark Development:** Recognizing the lack of a high-quality, dense, and comprehensive 3D occupancy benchmark, the authors introduce **OpenOcc**, built on nuScenes, complete with flow annotations, to facilitate standardized evaluation and comparison for the community.

In essence, "Scene as Occupancy" represents a significant step towards a more unified and fine-grained perception paradigm for autonomous driving, moving beyond simplified representations to capture the richness and complexity of the real world for safer and more robust planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **establish and validate 3D occupancy as a superior, general-purpose scene representation for autonomous driving**. This overarching goal is driven by several motivations:

1. **Bridging the Human-Machine Perception Gap:** Human drivers can effortlessly describe complex traffic scenes with fine-grained detail and context (e.g., "There is a truck carrying a huge protruding gas pipe"). Current vision-centric AD systems struggle with this level of detailed, geometry-aware perception using conventional representations like 3D bounding boxes. The paper argues that a 3D occupancy map, which quantizes the physical 3D scene into structured semantic cells, provides a more intuitive and rich description akin to human perception.

2. **Addressing Limitations of Conventional Representations:**
 - **3D Bounding Boxes:** These oversimplify object shapes, failing to capture critical details (like a crane arm extending from a construction vehicle) that are crucial for safe collision avoidance and planning.
 - **BEV Segmentation:** While providing a unified overhead view, it inherently loses height information, which is vital for distinguishing objects at different elevations or understanding complex 3D scenarios.
 - **LiDAR-based methods:** Rely on expensive sensors and can suffer from sparsity and limited range, making holistic 3D scene understanding challenging for vision-only systems.

3. **Facilitating Downstream Driving Tasks:** The authors hypothesize that a dense, geometry-aware 3D occupancy representation can serve as a robust "Scene Descriptor" that inherently facilitates a wide range of downstream tasks beyond just object detection. This includes more accurate motion planning, semantic scene completion, and BEV segmentation, leading to improved overall AD system performance.

4. **Leveraging Temporal Information and Efficient Decoding:** Previous attempts at 3D occupancy or scene completion often neglected temporal context or struggled with computational efficiency when reconstructing dense 3D features from multi-view images. A key motivation is to design an efficient vision-centric pipeline that effectively utilizes temporal clues and a coarse-to-fine decoding strategy to reconstruct high-quality 3D occupancy.

5. **Establishing a Standardized Benchmark:** The lack of a high-quality, dense 3D occupancy benchmark hinders fair comparison and progress in the field. A crucial objective is to provide OpenOcc, a new benchmark with dense semantic and flow annotations, to enable rigorous evaluation of vision-centric 3D occupancy models.

In summary, the paper aims to move autonomous driving perception beyond simplistic representations by championing 3D occupancy as a rich, unified scene descriptor, developing an efficient architecture (OccNet) to achieve this, and providing a public benchmark (OpenOcc) to accelerate research in this promising direction, ultimately contributing to safer and more robust autonomous driving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper introduces **OccNet**, a multi-view vision-centric pipeline designed to reconstruct 3D occupancy and use it as a general scene descriptor for various autonomous driving tasks. The methodology can be broken down into two main stages: **Reconstruction of Occupancy** and **Exploitation of Occupancy**, alongside the creation of the **OpenOcc benchmark**.

#### 3.1. Reconstruction of Occupancy

This stage focuses on generating a comprehensive and representative 3D occupancy embedding from multi-view camera images.
- **Multi-view Feature Extraction:** The pipeline starts by extracting multi-view features ($F_t$) from surrounding camera images using a backbone network (e.g., ResNet with FPN).
- **BEV Encoder (Temporal Integration):** The extracted image features ($F_t$) are fed into a BEV encoder, which, similar to BEVFormer, transforms 2D image features into a Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) representation. Crucially, this encoder incorporates temporal information by also taking history BEV features ($B_{t-1}$) and current BEV queries ($Q_t$) through a spatial-temporal-transformer block. This temporal fusion helps build a more consistent and robust understanding of the dynamic scene over time.
- **Cascade Voxel Decoder:** This is a core innovation of OccNet, designed to efficiently and effectively lift the 2D BEV features into a 3D voxel representation. Instead of directly creating a dense 3D voxel feature map (which would be computationally expensive), the decoder operates in a cascade fashion. It progressively refines intermediate voxel features ($V'_{t,i}$) over *N* steps, gradually increasing the height resolution ($Z_i$) and decreasing feature dimension ($C_i$).
 - **Voxel-based Temporal Self-Attention:** Within each decoder step, current voxel features interact with history voxel features ($V'_{t-1,i}$) using a newly designed **3D Deformable Attention (3D-DA)** module. This module extends 2D deformable attention to 3D, allowing each voxel query to attend only to a small set of important local voxels, thus significantly reducing computational cost compared to full self-attention in 3D.
 - **Voxel-based Spatial Cross-Attention:** The voxel features also interact with the multi-scale image features ($F_t$) via 2D deformable attention. This allows the model to learn voxel-wise features by sampling 3D points from the voxels and projecting them to corresponding image views, maintaining height information.

#### 3.2. Exploitation of Occupancy

Once the 3D occupancy descriptor (the final refined voxel feature map $V_t$) is obtained, it can be used for a wide range of autonomous driving tasks:
- **Semantic Scene Completion (SSC):** A simple MLP head is attached to the occupancy descriptor to predict the semantic label for each voxel, along with a flow head to estimate flow velocity for occupied voxels.
- **3D Object Detection:** The 3D occupancy descriptor is first compacted into a BEV representation, then a query-based detection head (inspired by Deformable DETR) predicts 3D bounding boxes. This demonstrates how fine-grained occupancy can aid traditional detection.
- **BEV Segmentation:** Similar to 3D detection, the 3D occupancy map can be squeezed along the height dimension to generate a 2D BEV segmentation map, which includes drivable areas, lanes, and semantic segmentation of vehicles and pedestrians.
- **Motion Planning:** The generated BEV segmentation map (derived from 3D occupancy or 3D boxes) is fed into a cost-based planning module. Candidate trajectories are sampled, and their safety, comfort, and progress costs are computed. The richer, geometry-aware information from occupancy directly influences the safety cost function, leading to more robust planning, further refined by a GRU module using front-view vision features.

#### 3.3. OpenOcc: 3D Occupancy Benchmark

To enable rigorous evaluation and foster community research, the authors developed **OpenOcc**, the first dense and high-quality 3D occupancy benchmark built upon the popular nuScenes dataset.
- **Annotation Process:** OpenOcc addresses the sparsity of existing LiDAR-based datasets by generating dense annotations. This is achieved by:
 - **Independent Accumulation:** Separating LiDAR points into static background and foreground (moving objects). Background points are accumulated in a global coordinate system, while object points are accumulated in their respective object coordinate systems (using interpolated 3D boxes for intermediate frames where explicit annotations are missing).
 - **Voxelization and Labeling:** Dense accumulated points are voxelized, and labels are assigned based on majority vote.
 - **Flow Annotation:** Additional flow velocity annotations are provided for foreground object voxels, extending utility for motion prediction and planning tasks.
 - **Data Refinement:** Post-processing steps include filling holes, refining z-axis translations (a known nuScenes issue), and marking invisible voxels from camera views for more realistic camera-based training.
- **Benchmark Scope:** OpenOcc comprises 34,149 annotated frames with over 1.4 billion 3D occupancy cells across 16 classes (10 foreground objects, 6 background stuffs), offering a comprehensive and rich dataset for 3D geometry learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical experiments conducted on the proposed OpenOcc benchmark strongly support the superiority of 3D occupancy as a scene representation and the effectiveness of the OccNet architecture across various autonomous driving tasks.

#### 4.1. Semantic Scene Completion (SSC)

- OccNet significantly outperforms previous state-of-the-art methods in SSC. Using a ResNet50 backbone, OccNet achieves an mIoU of 19.48 and IoU_geo of 37.69, surpassing BEVDet4D (mIoU 9.85), BEVDepth (mIoU 11.88), and BEVDet (mIoU 12.49) by large margins.
- When compared to TPVFormer (ResNet101), a dedicated surrounding 3D semantic occupancy prediction model, OccNet (ResNet101) achieves a mIoU of 26.98, outperforming TPVFormer's 23.67. While TPVFormer performed slightly better on large objects like cars and trucks due to its sampling strategy, OccNet showed substantial gains (e.g., 10 points mIoU improvement) on small objects like pedestrians and traffic cones, demonstrating its ability to capture fine-grained details.

#### 4.2. Occupancy for LiDAR Segmentation

- By converting semantic occupancy predictions to point labels, OccNet (camera input only) shows performance comparable to LiDAR-based segmentation models like RangeNet++ (60.46 mIoU vs. 65.50 mIoU). This is a remarkable finding, suggesting that vision-only models trained on dense occupancy can approach the quality of LiDAR-based methods for 3D scene understanding.
- OccNet also outperforms TPVFormer (camera-based) by 2 points in mIoU for LiDAR segmentation, further validating its superior 3D geometric representation.
- The results also show that finer voxel resolutions (e.g., 0.25m vs. 0.5m) lead to better LiDAR segmentation performance, as expected, confirming that higher granularity better captures scene details.

#### 4.3. Occupancy for 3D Detection

- **Joint Training:** Jointly training 3D occupancy prediction with 3D object detection improves detector performance (mAP and NDS) across different OccNet variants (BEVNet, VoxNet, OccNet). This suggests that the shared learning of scene geometry through occupancy aids 3D bounding box regression.
- **Pre-training:** OccNet, pre-trained on the semantic scene completion task, acts as an excellent general representation learner for 3D space. When used for pre-training, it significantly boosts 3D detection performance (mAP and NDS by about 10 points) when fine-tuned on smaller-scale detection datasets, outperforming models pre-trained on traditional FCOS3D. This highlights the transferability of occupancy features.

#### 4.4. Occupancy for BEV Segmentation

- Pre-training on the occupancy task also leads to higher IoU scores for BEV segmentation (both semantic and map segmentation) in the fine-tuning stage, compared to pre-training on 3D detection tasks. This further supports the versatility of occupancy as a pre-training objective.

#### 4.5. Occupancy for Motion Planning

- The most significant practical impact is observed in motion planning. When the planning policy uses BEV segmentation maps derived from OccNet's 3D occupancy predictions (rather than 3D bounding boxes or direct BEV segmentation), the **collision rate is reduced by 15% to 58%**.
- Ground truth occupancy data further demonstrates superior collision avoidance compared to ground truth bounding boxes.
- The use of full 16 classes of occupancy (including background objects) in planning leads to better L2 distance metrics and enables safer decisions by considering more comprehensive scene elements that 3D boxes might ignore (e.g., avoiding collisions from background objects as visualized).

#### 4.6. Model Efficiency and Irregular Objects

- **Efficiency:** OccNet achieves the best balance between performance (mIoU and IoU_geo) and efficiency (lower parameters and reasonable memory/FPS) compared to simpler BEVNet and VoxelNet architectures, validating the effectiveness of the cascade voxel decoder.
- **Irregular Objects:** The paper specifically demonstrates that 3D occupancy better represents irregular objects (trucks, trailers, construction vehicles) compared to 3D bounding boxes, especially at finer voxel resolutions (0.25m vs. 0.5m). This fine-grained detail is critical for safety.
- **Dense vs. Sparse Occupancy:** Models pre-trained on dense occupancy (OpenOcc) significantly outperform those pre-trained on sparse occupancy, underscoring the importance of high-quality, comprehensive annotations for effective 3D perception learning.

In summary, the findings consistently validate that 3D occupancy, particularly as generated by OccNet and evaluated on OpenOcc, offers a richer, more accurate, and more useful scene representation for autonomous driving, leading to tangible improvements across various perception and, crucially, planning tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Scene as Occupancy" paper presents a highly significant contribution to the field of autonomous driving perception, with several potential impacts:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Scene Representation:** The most profound impact lies in advocating for 3D occupancy as a fundamental and unified scene representation for autonomous driving. Moving beyond simplistic 3D bounding boxes or 2D BEV maps, 3D occupancy provides a geometry-aware, fine-grained, and dense description of the entire physical world. This is a crucial step towards more human-like scene understanding for AD systems.

2. **Enhanced Safety and Robustness in Planning:** The demonstrated significant reduction in collision rates (15-58%) when planning is guided by occupancy predictions is a direct and highly practical impact. By providing rich, detailed information about both foreground objects and background elements, occupancy enables autonomous vehicles to make more informed and safer decisions, especially concerning irregular obstacles or complex environments where bounding boxes fall short. This addresses a critical challenge in real-world AD deployment.

3. **Unifying Perception Tasks:** OccNet's design of a general "occupancy embedding" that supports multiple downstream tasks (semantic scene completion, 3D detection, BEV segmentation, and motion planning) streamlines the perception pipeline. This unification reduces the need for task-specific models and potentially fosters a more holistic understanding of the environment.

4. **Advancing Vision-Centric Autonomous Driving:** The paper makes a strong case for the capabilities of vision-only systems. By demonstrating that camera-based OccNet can achieve LiDAR-comparable performance for semantic segmentation and significantly improve planning, it reduces the reliance on expensive LiDAR sensors, potentially lowering the cost and increasing the scalability of autonomous driving solutions. This aligns with trends seen in leading AD companies like Tesla.

5. **Setting a New Benchmark for Research:** The introduction of OpenOcc, the first dense, high-quality 3D occupancy benchmark with semantic and flow annotations on nuScenes, is a major contribution to the research community. It provides a standardized, challenging dataset for fair comparison and accelerates research in 3D occupancy prediction and its applications. This will likely spur further innovation and attract more researchers to this promising area.

6. **Towards Foundation Models for Autonomous Driving:** The authors' aspiration for the occupancy framework to become a "foundation model" for autonomous driving highlights a long-term vision. Just as large language models serve as foundations for various NLP tasks, a comprehensive 3D scene descriptor could serve as the core perception module for a wide array of AD functions, from planning to simulation and human-machine interaction. This work lays critical groundwork for such a future.

In conclusion, "Scene as Occupancy" pushes the boundaries of autonomous driving perception by introducing a more comprehensive scene representation and a powerful, efficient vision-centric model to achieve it. Its tangible improvements in safety-critical tasks like motion planning, coupled with the release of a crucial benchmark, position this work as a foundational contribution that will influence the future development of robust and intelligent autonomous systems.
