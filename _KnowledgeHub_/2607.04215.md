# Anytime Plug-and-Play Control with Contract-Based Distributed MPC

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ETH Zurich and EPFL developed a distributed Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework that guarantees recursive feasibility and collision avoidance for multi-agent systems under "anytime" plug-and-play operations. The contract-based approach, utilizing time-varying cells and safety envelopes, enables dynamic, aggressive maneuvers like overtaking while maintaining safety in high-speed, uncoordinated multi-agent scenarios, as validated in simulations and hardware experiments with miniature race cars.

## Problem

- Existing Distributed Model Predictive Control (DMPC) methods struggle to provide explicit safety guarantees under "anytime" plug-and-play (PnP) conditions, where agents can uncoordinatedly join or leave the network.
- Centralized MPC approaches suffer from cubic computational growth, limiting scalability for large multi-agent systems and requiring global information and extensive communication.
- Traditional DMPC methods often rely on fixed or request-based PnP network topologies, failing to address the challenges of mobile robots where communication is position-dependent and highly transient.

## Method

- A distributed ==receding-horizon control== strategy is introduced where each agent solves a local optimization problem using neighbor-only communication and ==contract-based constraints==.
- Global collision-avoidance constraints are replaced with local contracts comprising time-varying convex "==cells==" for current neighbors and "==safety envelopes==" for non-neighbors, guaranteeing collision avoidance and recursive feasibility during topology changes.
- The framework is augmented into an "Anytime Plug-and-Play **Multi-Trajectory Model Predictive Contouring Control (mt-MPCC)**" to enable aggressive maneuvers like overtaking while preserving safety guarantees.

## Results

- The proposed DMPC framework provides theoretical guarantees for recursive feasibility, collision avoidance, and constraint satisfaction for multi-agent systems under "anytime" PnP conditions.
- Simulations with up to **eight agents** demonstrated effective management of complex interactions, including high-speed overtaking, intersection navigation, and head-on interactions with oncoming traffic.
- Hardware experiments with miniature race cars confirmed real-time performance and robustness, maintaining minimum pairwise distances above the safety threshold (Figure 10a) and showcasing collision-free operation despite dynamic, frequently changing neighbor relationships (Figure 10b).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling collision avoidance via local, contract-based constraints (cells and safety envelopes) allows DMPC to handle uncoordinated network topology changes ("anytime" PnP) with formal safety guarantees.
> - Safety envelopes, by constraining incremental movements, ensure recursive feasibility even as an agent's awareness set shifts due to its motion, maintaining the ability to form new safe contracts.
> - A multi-trajectory MPCC approach effectively balances the need for formal safety guarantees (via a "safe" trajectory) with aggressive performance (via an "exploitation" trajectory), sharing the same applied control input.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of multi-agent robotic systems, such as autonomous vehicles and drones, necessitates efficient and safe coordination mechanisms. Model Predictive Control (MPC) is a framework frequently employed for multi-agent coordination. However, centralized MPC faces challenges related to scalability, experiencing cubic computational growth, and requiring global information and communication.

Distributed MPC (DMPC) approaches aim to address these limitations by enabling individual agents to solve local optimization problems through limited information exchange with neighboring agents. This paradigm enhances scalability and preserves privacy. The effectiveness of DMPC is significantly influenced by the nature of the communication network, which can be categorized into fixed, time-varying (constant agents, changing neighbors), and plug-and-play (PnP). PnP networks, where agents can dynamically enter or leave, are further differentiated into request-based (requiring approval) and "anytime" (allowing uncoordinated joining/leaving).

While many DMPC methods accommodate fixed or request-based PnP topologies, a challenge remains in scenarios demanding "anytime" PnP capabilities, particularly for mobile robots where dynamics are decoupled but constraints and communication are position-dependent. Existing approaches generally do not provide explicit guarantees for safety under these uncoordinated network changes.

This research addresses this gap by proposing a DMPC framework specifically designed for "anytime" PnP operations. It aims to ensure safety and recursive feasibility in environments where agents can join or leave the network arbitrarily and without prior coordination. The paper distinguishes its approach from other related works, including those with global coordination (e.g., priority-based, leader-follower, centralized SQP), constraint-based methods (e.g., safety tubes, consistency constraints, request-based contract constraints, Control Barrier Functions), and traffic-specific applications (e.g., assisting take-over, highway merging). The presented method distinguishes itself by targeting fully distributed, anytime plug-and-play operations with neighbor-only communication and formal recursive feasibility and safety guarantees, a combination not comprehensively covered by the other categories.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this work arises from the challenges posed by time-varying communication topologies in mobile multi-robot applications, particularly the need to manage scenarios where agents can join or leave a network at any time without centralized coordination or approval—termed "anytime" plug-and-play (PnP). Such dynamic environments are characteristic of real-world deployments of robotic fleets, where communication links are intrinsically transient due to agent movement, entry, or exit.

The primary objective is to develop a distributed multi-agent control algorithm that can robustly handle these "anytime" PnP conditions. Specifically, the authors aim to:

1. **Propose a Distributed Model Predictive Control (DMPC) framework** that explicitly guarantees collision avoidance and constraint satisfaction for multi-agent systems operating under "anytime" PnP operations. This involves designing mechanisms to handle uncoordinated agent departures and arrivals while maintaining system safety.
2. **Develop a contract-based mechanism** for decoupling collision avoidance constraints, relying on local communication. The key tools for this are time-varying cells and safety envelopes, which are intended to enable fully decoupled constraints and ensure recursive feasibility and robustness despite topology changes.
3. **Augment the safety-guaranteed framework for practical autonomous driving scenarios.** This involves extending the core DMPC approach to support aggressive maneuvers, such as overtaking, while rigorously preserving the established safety guarantees.
4. **Demonstrate the real-world applicability and effectiveness** of the proposed method through validation in high-fidelity simulations and physical hardware experiments. This validation is conducted in a challenging autonomous multi-agent driving scenario involving high-speed, dynamic interactions, and agents moving in opposite directions, with an emphasis on frequent topology changes.

The overall goal is to provide a scalable, efficient, and formally guaranteed control strategy for multi-agent systems operating in highly dynamic and unpredictable environments, where traditional centralized or request-based DMPC approaches fall short.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a distributed receding-horizon control strategy designed for multi-agent systems with "anytime" plug-and-play (PnP) capabilities. Each agent solves a local optimization problem using information exchanged exclusively with its direct neighbors. The core of the approach involves replacing the global, coupled collision-avoidance constraints with local contracts.

**Problem Formulation:**
The work begins by defining a centralized finite-horizon optimal control problem (FHOCP) for a set of agents, where each agent has nonlinear dynamics and is subject to local state and input constraints, as well as coupled collision-avoidance constraints. The objective is to minimize a sum of local cost functions. The distributed strategy aims to solve this problem by allowing each agent to determine its control action based on its local objective while satisfying safety constraints through contracts.

**Contract Ingredients:**
At each time step, an agent `i` computes a contract `C_i,t` based on its current position and communication with current neighbors. This contract consists of two main components:

1. **Cells (`P^{N+1}_{i,t}`):** These are convex, time-varying regions defined in the position space for each agent across the prediction horizon. Their primary function is to ensure collision-free trajectories between *current* neighbors. The framework ensures that if neighboring agents remain within their assigned cells, they avoid collisions (Proposition 1). An "admissible" collection of cells requires that the physically inflated cells (accounting for agent volume `B_{\epsilon/2}`) for any two neighbors are disjoint. The existence of such admissible cells for any collision-free trajectories is also demonstrated (Lemma 1).
 - **Practical Implementation for Autonomous Driving:** For the autonomous driving application, cells are implemented as separating hyperplanes, similar to Voronoi partitioning. A safety margin `\epsilon^{(i,j)}` is included to maintain a minimum separation distance. To facilitate aggressive maneuvers like overtaking, the orientation of these hyperplanes is dynamically adjusted. For instance, they can be tilted (e.g., at a 45-degree angle to the direction of motion) or adapted based on local track curvature to encourage overtaking on the inner side of a curve.

2. **Awareness Sets and Safety Envelopes (`E^N_i`):** These components address topology changes and recursive feasibility.
 - **Awareness Set (`A(p_i)`):** This is a tightened version of the agent's communication set, accounting for the agent's physical size (`A(p_i) := \bar{R}(p_i) \ominus B_{\epsilon/2}`). It represents the region around an agent within which another agent can be detected early enough to establish communication and become a neighbor before a collision occurs. For non-neighboring agents, if their predicted trajectories remain within their respective awareness sets, collision avoidance is guaranteed (Proposition 2).
 - **Safety Envelopes (`E^N_i`):** A challenge with the awareness set is that simply constraining predicted trajectories to stay within the initial awareness set does not guarantee recursive feasibility after shifting the control horizon (as the awareness set itself shifts with the agent's current position). To overcome this, safety envelopes are introduced. These are sequences of scaled-down convex sets (`E_{i,k} = \alpha_{i,k} A(0_3)`) applied to constrain the *incremental movements* of an agent's predicted position (`p_{i,k+1|t} - p_{i,k|t} \in E_{i,k}`). The scaling coefficients `\alpha_{i,k}` are non-increasing along the prediction horizon and sum to one, ensuring that the cumulative effect keeps the entire predicted trajectory (and any shifted tail of it) within the corresponding awareness set (Proposition 3). This guarantees that recursive feasibility is maintained even when the awareness set shifts.
 - **Practical Implementation for Autonomous Driving:** Safety envelopes are designed as squares. Different scaling strategies for `\alpha_{i,k}` were evaluated, including uniform, continuously decreasing, and a "1/3 vs. 2/3" scheme where the first third of envelopes sum to half the awareness set size, and the remaining two-thirds sum to the other half. The latter was reported to yield improved speed.

**Local FHOCP and Theoretical Analysis:**
Each agent `i` solves a local FHOCP (Problem 30) that incorporates its local state and input constraints, the cell-membership constraints, and the safety-envelope constraints. A terminal equality constraint is used to simplify the recursive-feasibility argument, allowing the optimizer to select an admissible equilibrium compatible with local and terminal contract constraints.

**Theorem 1** formally proves recursive feasibility, collision avoidance, and constraint satisfaction for the resulting closed-loop system under anytime PnP conditions. The proof demonstrates that from any feasible optimal solution at time `t`, a feasible candidate solution for time `t+1` can be constructed. This construction explicitly handles existing neighbors (whose shifted trajectories remain collision-free due to cell compatibility), disappearing neighbors (whose constraints are removed), and newly appearing neighbors (whose shifted trajectories are shown to be collision-free through the properties of awareness sets and safety envelopes).

**Algorithm 1** outlines the online procedure: at each time step, agents exchange previously planned position trajectories with neighbors, compute new cells, solve their local FHOCP, apply the first control input, and store the new planned trajectory.

**Application to Autonomous Driving (mt-MPCC):**
The framework is augmented into an "Anytime Plug-and-Play Multi-Trajectory Model Predictive Contouring Control (mt-MPCC)" (Problem 31). This involves optimizing two coupled trajectories per agent: a "safe" trajectory (implementing the Anytime PnP MPC) and an "exploitation" trajectory (an MPCC focused on high-speed reference tracking without considering other agents). Both trajectories share the same first control input, ensuring that the applied control action maintains the safety and recursive-feasibility guarantees of the safe trajectory, while the exploitation trajectory can improve performance. The MPCC cost function is modified with a yaw-alignment term to enable more aggressive and natural overtaking maneuvers.

**Implementation Details:**
The method is implemented using the CRS software framework, utilizing `acados` with SQP-RTI as the MPC solver for efficient handling of time-varying constraints. Experiments run with a horizon length of 15 at a 20Hz sampling rate. State estimation is performed using an Extended Kalman Filter, and global pose information is provided by an external motion capture system.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings and demonstrates the capabilities of the proposed "Anytime Plug-and-Play Control with Contract-Based Distributed MPC" through both theoretical analysis and experimental validation.

**Theoretical Guarantees:**
The primary theoretical finding is that the proposed DMPC framework provides formal guarantees for recursive feasibility, collision avoidance, and constraint satisfaction in multi-agent systems. This is achieved through the use of contract-based constraints, specifically time-varying cells and safety envelopes, which decouple collision avoidance and ensure robustness to dynamic changes in network topology. The recursive feasibility proof (Theorem 1) confirms that even under uncoordinated "anytime" agent joining or leaving, the system can maintain safe operation.

**Scalability and Communication Efficiency:**
The approach inherently scales efficiently with the number of agents due to its reliance on local, distance-based neighbor definitions and neighbor-to-neighbor communication. No centralized coordination or global knowledge is required for managing topology changes, which is a significant advantage for large-scale multi-robot deployments.

**Performance and Dynamic Maneuvers:**
The multi-trajectory MPCC augmentation successfully enables agents to perform dynamic and aggressive maneuvers, such as overtaking, while preserving the established safety guarantees. The adaptive design of cells (e.g., angled hyperplanes, curvature-dependent modifications) was found to reduce conservatism and promote more natural and efficient movement patterns in complex driving scenarios. The "1/3 vs. 2/3" safety envelope design reportedly resulted in improved speed performance compared to alternative designs.

**Simulation Validation:**
Simulations with up to eight agents on a figure-eight track demonstrated the method's effectiveness in managing complex interactions:
- **Overtaking:** Faster agents successfully overtook slower agents, utilizing the inner side of curves facilitated by curvature-dependent hyperplane angling (Figure 8).
- **Intersection Management:** Agents navigated busy intersections by appropriately slowing down and diverting from the centerline to avoid collisions with crossing traffic (Figure 7, blue bubble).
- **Oncoming Traffic:** In high-density scenarios with agents moving in opposite directions, the system effectively managed head-on interactions, requiring multiple agents to simultaneously adjust trajectories to avoid collisions (Figure 7, purple bubble).

**Hardware Validation:**
Real-world experiments using custom-built 1/28th scale miniature race cars provided empirical validation of the algorithm's real-time performance and robustness.
- **Collision Avoidance:** Experiments with two to six agents, including scenarios with oncoming traffic, consistently demonstrated collision-free operation. Quantitative results (Figure 10a) showed that the minimum pairwise distance between any two agents remained above the prescribed safety threshold throughout the experiments, confirming the effectiveness of the collision avoidance mechanism in a physical setting.
- **Dynamic Topology Handling:** The hardware experiments confirmed the "anytime" plug-and-play capability by showing that the number of neighboring agents for any given car varied dynamically over time (Figure 10b). The system maintained safety despite these frequent changes, highlighting its robustness to uncoordinated entries and exits from the communication network.
- **Complex Interaction Resolution:** An example from the hardware experiments (Figure 9) illustrates five agents successfully navigating a congested intersection simultaneously without collisions, showcasing the method's ability to resolve complex multi-agent interactions in real-time.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of multi-agent control by addressing a notable challenge in distributed systems: guaranteeing safety and feasibility under "anytime" plug-and-play (PnP) operations. The significance and potential impact of this work can be understood through several points:

1. **Addressing a Critical Gap in DMPC:** The work directly tackles the limitations of existing distributed MPC approaches that often rely on fixed network topologies, centralized coordination, or request-based PnP operations. By providing formal guarantees for collision avoidance and recursive feasibility when agents can arbitrarily join or leave the network, this method expands the applicability of DMPC to highly dynamic and unpredictable real-world scenarios.

2. **Enhancing Autonomy and Safety in Multi-Robot Systems:** The proposed framework enables groups of mobile robots, such as autonomous vehicles or drones, to operate more autonomously and safely in environments with fluid communication and agent populations. This is particularly relevant for applications where pre-negotiated handshakes or global coordination are impractical or impossible.

3. **Scalability and Decentralization:** The core design, which relies on local, neighbor-to-neighbor communication and contract-based decoupling of constraints, inherently promotes scalability. This makes the approach suitable for managing larger fleets of robots where centralized control becomes computationally intractable or communication-intensive. The complete decentralization reduces single points of failure and enhances system robustness.

4. **Foundation for Robust Robotic Applications:** The theoretical guarantees and experimental validation in a challenging autonomous driving context provide a strong foundation for developing and deploying robust multi-robot systems. The ability to handle high-speed, dynamic interactions, including overtaking and oncoming traffic scenarios, without collisions is a critical requirement for advanced robotic applications.

5. **Path for Future Research:** The paper identifies areas for further investigation, such as adaptive tuning of safety envelopes (potentially through learning-based methods) and exploring communication-free distributed architectures. This suggests that the current work serves as a significant stepping stone, opening new avenues for improving the balance between safety, performance, and communication efficiency in multi-agent control.

In summary, this work represents an advancement in distributed control for multi-agent systems by providing a formally guaranteed and experimentally validated solution for collision-free operation under "anytime" plug-and-play conditions. This capability is crucial for the safe and efficient deployment of autonomous mobile robots in dynamic, real-world environments.
