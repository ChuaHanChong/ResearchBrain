# Barkour: Benchmarking Animal-level Agility with Quadruped Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Google DeepMind researchers introduce Barkour, a standardized benchmark inspired by dog agility competitions, designed to evaluate the agile locomotion of quadruped robots. The work establishes baseline performance using specialized and generalist (Locomotion-Transformer) learning-based policies, demonstrating zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a custom robot and achieving a top agility score of 0.91.

## Problem

- The robotics field lacked standardized, intuitive methods for evaluating legged robot agility, making cross-research comparisons difficult.
- Existing locomotion benchmarks often focused on subsets of skills or lacked a unified environment, failing to assess holistic, diverse agile behaviors.
- There was a need for control strategies enabling robots to achieve animal-level agility and generalize across varied terrains and obstacles, beyond robust, moderate-speed locomotion.

## Method

- A comprehensive, ==obstacle-course-based benchmark==, **Barkour**, was designed, inspired by dog agility competitions, featuring varied tasks like weave poles, an A-frame, and broad jumps, with a time-based scoring system and penalties.
- Two learning-based baseline control policies were developed: a ==modular system of specialized policies== and a unified ==generalist policy== (**Locomotion-Transformer**) for diverse agile behaviors.
- Policies were trained using ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)== in the GPU-accelerated NVidia IsaacGym simulator with extensive ==domain randomization==, enabling zero-shot transfer to a custom-built physical quadruped robot.

## Results

- The specialist policy approach achieved a mean Barkour score of **0.77 ± 0.064** with an average completion time of **24.6 ± 1.1 seconds**, and a best single run of **0.91** in **20.4 seconds**.
- The Locomotion-Transformer, a generalist policy, yielded a mean Barkour score of **0.73 ± 0.062** with an average completion time of **25.8 ± 1.6 seconds**, showcasing smooth transitions and adaptability to varied course layouts.
- Specialist policies demonstrated **100%** success for weave poles and A-frame traversal, while the jumping policy cleared the **0.5m** broad jump in **38%** of trials, with the robot accelerating to **2.3 m/s** before the jump.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Barkour benchmark effectively challenges robot agility, revealing a significant performance gap between current robots (top score 0.91, ~20s completion) and biological animals (dogs with perfect scores in ~9s).
> - While specialized policies achieved higher individual task success rates, the generalist Transformer-based policy provided smoother transitions between behaviors and demonstrated better generalization to modified course configurations without explicit policy switching.
> - Attempts to train a single multi-task reinforcement learning policy to solve the full Barkour course in simulation were unsuccessful on real hardware, underscoring the benchmark's complexity and the value of structured or specialized learning approaches.
