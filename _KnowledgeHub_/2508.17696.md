# Fair Cooperation in Mixed-Motive Games via Conflict-Aware Gradient Adjustment

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University developed FCGrad, a method for multi-agent reinforcement learning that dynamically adjusts policy gradients to promote fair cooperation in mixed-motive games. The approach achieved superior fairness and competitive collective performance in sequential social dilemma environments, ensuring more equitable reward distribution among agents.

## Problem

- Multi-agent systems in mixed-motive settings often suffer from a deterioration of collective welfare when agents prioritize self-interest.
- Even when cooperation is achieved in mixed-motive scenarios, it frequently leads to unfair outcomes where some agents consistently receive lower task-specific rewards.
- Existing methods, like reward restructuring or gradient alignment, fail to simultaneously address both cooperative performance and fairness in task-specific reward distribution.

## Method

- The Fair and ==Conflict-aware Gradient== Adjustment (**FCGrad**) algorithm dynamically adjusts policy gradients for each agent based on individual and collective objectives.
- It detects conflicts between individual and collective gradients (negative inner product) and, in such cases, projects the gradient of the 'disadvantaged' objective onto the normal plane of the other, prioritizing the objective with lower current return.
- When no conflict is detected, FCGrad computes a weighted sum of individual and collective gradients to balance objectives, then updates policy parameters using this adjusted gradient.

## Results

- FCGrad demonstrated superior fairness across all evaluated environments (Unfair Coins, Cleanup, Harvest) as measured by geometric mean, minimum individual return (α-fairness), Gini coefficient, and Jain's index.
- In Unfair Coins, FCGrad achieved nearly identical returns for agents despite highly asymmetric reward probabilities, outperforming baselines which resulted in unfair distributions.
- The method maintained competitive collective performance while ensuring more equitable task-specific reward distribution in Cleanup and Harvest environments, even with spatial asymmetries, where baselines often led to specialized, unfair agent behaviors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly addressing conflicts in gradient space through projection allows for targeted improvement of the less-performing objective without detriment to the other.
> - The method provides theoretical guarantees for monotonic non-decreasing improvement in both individual and collective objectives.
> - FCGrad ensures fairness by proving the convergence of the value gap between individual and collective objectives to zero over time, leading to equitable reward distribution.
