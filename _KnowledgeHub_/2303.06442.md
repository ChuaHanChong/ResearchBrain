# Fine-grained Visual Classification with High-temperature Refinement and Background Suppression

> [!summary] Summary
> The HERBS network improves fine-grained visual classification by integrating Background Suppression and High-temperature Refinement modules, enabling learning across diverse feature scales and mitigating background noise. It achieved 93.1% Top-1 accuracy on CUB-200-2011 and 93.0% on NABirds, setting new benchmarks for these datasets.

## Problem

- Fine-grained visual classification (FGVC) struggles with high inter-class similarity and large intra-class variation, requiring models to distinguish subtle, localized features.
- Prior FGVC methods often over-rely on enhancing discriminative regions, potentially losing global context or failing to address distracting background noise.
- Models frequently lack mechanisms for adaptively learning diverse feature scales, leading to either over-localization or an inability to capture fine details.

## Method

- A novel High-temperaturE Refinement and Background Suppression (**HERBS**) network is introduced, designed to be modular and compatible with various CNN and Transformer backbones.
- A ==Background Suppression (BS)== module identifies and suppresses non-discriminative background regions while enhancing foreground features through a unique loss formulation using `tanh`.
- A ==High-temperature Refinement== module employs ==temperature-controlled knowledge distillation== with a dynamic temperature schedule to encourage learning diverse features, balancing global context and fine-grained details.

## Results

- HERBS achieved state-of-the-art Top-1 accuracy of **93.1%** on the CUB-200-2011 dataset and **93.0%** on the NABirds dataset.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the Background Suppression and High-temperature Refinement modules independently contribute to accuracy improvements, with their combined use (HERBS) yielding the highest gains (**+1.0-1.6%** over baselines).
- Qualitative analyses via heatmaps showed HERBS generates attention maps that maintain focus on discriminative regions while appropriately incorporating broader contextual areas and effectively filtering out background noise.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly suppressing background noise can significantly improve FGVC accuracy by enhancing the signal-to-noise ratio for discriminative features, even when strong foreground signals are weak.
> - A dynamic, temperature-controlled refinement process allows models to explore diverse feature scales (from broad context at high temperatures to fine details at low temperatures) during training, leading to more robust feature representations.
> - The proposed modules can be effectively integrated into both CNN and Transformer architectures, demonstrating broad applicability and providing consistent performance gains across different backbone types.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Fine-grained visual classification (FGVC) is a specialized and challenging sub-field within computer vision. Unlike coarse-grained classification (e.g., distinguishing "animals" from "vehicles"), FGVC aims to differentiate between highly similar subordinate categories, such as different bird species (sparrows vs. finches), dog breeds, or vehicle models. The inherent difficulty stems from two primary issues:
1. **High inter-class similarity:** Categories often share numerous visual characteristics, with discriminative features being extremely subtle and localized (e.g., a specific feather pattern, a slight difference in beak shape).
2. **Large intra-class variation:** Images within the same fine-grained category can appear significantly different due to variations in pose, lighting, background, and occlusion.

Historically, FGVC methods have evolved through several stages:
- **Object-part-based methods:** Early approaches often relied on manually annotating object parts (eyes, feet, etc.) to guide models in learning local discriminative features. While effective, this requires costly expert annotations.
- **Weakly supervised methods:** To overcome annotation burdens, these methods use class activation maps (CAM) or similar techniques to automatically identify discriminative regions from image-level labels.
- **Attention-based methods:** Leveraging attention mechanisms, these approaches enhance feature learning by directing the model's focus to salient regions, effectively locating object details.
- **Transformer-based methods:** With the advent of Vision Transformers (ViT), self-attention maps have been utilized to capture foreground information, pushing the boundaries of accuracy in various vision tasks, including FGVC.

A common thread across many of these prior works has been the singular focus on *enhancing discriminative regions*. While crucial, this intense focus can inadvertently lead to two problems that this paper aims to address:
1. **Neglect of background information:** The background, while not the primary object, can provide valuable contextual cues or, more importantly, *noise* that can distract the model. Ignoring it as a source of *suppressible information* is a missed opportunity.
2. **Loss of global/contextual information:** Over-reliance on extremely subtle, localized features can cause models to lose broader contextual understanding, which might be critical for distinguishing objects that appear similar at a fine scale but differ in overall gestalt or environmental interaction.

The proposed HERBS network directly confronts these limitations by explicitly incorporating modules for **background suppression** and **high-temperature refinement**. This approach positions the work as an advancement that complements existing discriminative feature enhancement techniques by refining feature learning across scales and actively mitigating noise. It integrates well with both traditional Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) backbones and modern Transformer architectures, demonstrating its versatility and broad applicability within the FGVC domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:
1. **Improve the accuracy of fine-grained visual classification tasks** by developing a novel network architecture that effectively handles the challenges of high inter-class similarity and intra-class variation.
2. **Enable the learning of diverse and appropriate feature scales:** The model should be able to capture both broad contextual information and fine-grained details, adapting its focus as needed during the learning process.
3. **Suppress background noise and irrelevant features:** Actively identifying and diminishing the influence of non-discriminative background regions to allow the model to focus more effectively on the target object.

The motivation behind these objectives stems from the shortcomings observed in prior FGVC methodologies:
- **Over-reliance on subtle features:** Existing methods often concentrate too heavily on tiny discriminative regions, risking the loss of broader global features and contextual information, which can be essential for robust classification.
- **Ignoring the utility of background:** While enhancing foreground is critical, the "unselected" or background regions are not merely passive; they can contain noise or information that, if properly processed (e.g., suppressed), can improve classification. The paper argues that excluding unimportant regions is beneficial, especially when strong discriminative foreground regions are hard to identify.
- **Lack of flexible feature scale learning:** Models often struggle to dynamically adjust their receptive fields or attention scales. A mechanism that refines features from coarse (global) to fine (local) without losing either type of information is needed. The "high-temperature refinement" is explicitly motivated by the need to learn diverse features (texture, shape, appearance) across various object categories, ensuring that the model doesn't overfit to a single scale of features.

By addressing these motivations, the authors aim to develop a more robust and adaptable FGVC framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a novel network named **High-temperaturE Refinement and Background Suppression (HERBS)**. HERBS is designed to be highly modular and can be integrated with various backbone architectures (both CNN-based like ResNet and Transformer-based like Swin Transformer). The core of HERBS consists of two main modules: the Background Suppression (BS) module and the High-temperature Refinement module, integrated into a pipeline that often includes a feature fusion module (like Path Aggregation Network, PA, or Feature Pyramid Network, FPN).

**Network Architecture Overview:**
The HERBS network typically comprises:
1. **Backbone:** Extracts initial features (e.g., Swin Transformer, ResNet-50).
2. **Feature Fusion Module:** Enhances multi-scale feature representation, similar to PA or FPN, with top-down and bottom-up paths.
3. **HERBS Module:** Integrates the Background Suppression and High-temperature Refinement components with multiple classifiers.

**A. Background Suppression (BS) Module:**
This module aims to differentiate foreground (discriminative) and background (noisy/unselected) regions and then suppress the latter while enhancing the former.
1. **Classification Map Generation:** For each feature map `h_s_i` from the `i`-th backbone block, a classification map `Y_i` is generated by a linear classifier.
2. **Foreground Selection:** From `Y_i`, a maximum score map `P_max,i` is derived. Features corresponding to the top-K scores are selected as foreground (discriminative areas). `K_i` is a dynamically decreasing value across layers (e.g., K1=256, K2=128, K3=64, K4=32) to allow earlier layers to have a broader focus.
3. **Graph Convolution and Merged Prediction:** The selected foreground features are merged using a graph convolution module to make a classification prediction `Y_m` (merged classification prediction). This output contributes to `loss_m`, a standard cross-entropy loss against the ground truth.
4. **Background Suppression:** The unselected classification maps are denoted as `Y_d` (dropped maps). To suppress these background features, the hyperbolic tangent function `tanh` is applied to `Y_d`, yielding `P_d`. A `loss_d` is then calculated as the mean squared error between `P_d` and a pseudo-target of -1. The use of `tanh` and a target of -1 (rather than softmax and `1/C_gt`) is crucial, as it allows for feature value separation, acknowledging that "background" might contain elements from other classes, not just pure noise.
5. **Layer-wise Loss:** To prevent all blocks from overly focusing on the same locations, a layer-wise classification loss `loss_l` is introduced. It computes cross-entropy for predictions from each backbone block after global average pooling.

The total BS loss is `loss_bs = λ_m * loss_m + λ_d * loss_d + λ_l * loss_l`, where `λ` are weighting hyperparameters (`λ_m=1, λ_d=5, λ_l=0.3`).

**B. High-temperature Refinement Module:**
This module addresses the challenge of learning diverse features across different scales, preventing over-localization and ensuring global context is maintained. It conceptually draws from knowledge distillation.
1. **Multi-Classifier Setup:** The module uses two classifiers (`k1` and `k2`) applied to feature maps from different layers or paths (e.g., `k1` from top-down path, `k2` from bottom-up path).
2. **Temperature-Controlled Distillation:** The objective is to make classifier `k1` learn the output distribution of classifier `k2`. This is achieved using a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence loss (`loss_r`) between their softmax outputs, but with a crucial "temperature" parameter `T_e`.
3. **Dynamic Temperature Schedule:** The initial temperature `T` is set high (e.g., 64 or 128), and it gradually decreases as training epochs `e` increase, following a decay function.
 - **High Temperature (early training):** Encourages `k1` to explore diverse features and tolerate initial inaccuracies, leading to broader attention and capturing global/contextual information.
 - **Low Temperature (later training):** As `T` decreases, the model focuses more on the target class and learns finer, more discriminative features, refining the attention.

The total HERBS loss is `loss_herbs = loss_bs + λ_r * loss_r`, with `λ_r=1`. The final classification decision integrates predictions from multiple classifiers across the network.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on two standard fine-grained bird classification datasets: **CUB-200-2011** (200 categories, ~11.8k images) and **NABirds** (555 species, ~48.5k images). The input image sizes were 448x448 for ResNet-50 and 384x384 for Swin Transformer.

**A. State-of-the-Art Performance:**
- HERBS achieved **93.1% Top-1 accuracy on CUB-200-2011**, surpassing previous state-of-the-art methods (e.g., MetaFormer at 92.4%, DCAL at 92.0%) by a significant margin of 0.7%.
- On the larger **NABirds dataset, HERBS reached 93.0% Top-1 accuracy**, also outperforming prior SOTA methods (e.g., MetaFormer at 92.7%, SR-GNN at 91.2%).
These results firmly establish HERBS as a leading solution for FGVC tasks on these benchmarks.

**B. Ablation Studies:**
The studies systematically evaluated the contribution of each proposed module:
- **Individual Module Contributions (Swin Transformer backbone):**
 - Swin-Base baseline: 91.3%
 - Swin-Base + PA: 91.9% (+0.6%)
 - Swin-Base + Refinement: 91.5% (+0.2%)
 - Swin-Base + BS: 91.8% (+0.5%)
 - **Swin-Base + HERBS (PA + Refinement + BS): 92.3% (+1.0%)**
 - Similar trends were observed with Swin-Large, with HERBS achieving 93.1% (+1.1%) over the 92.0% baseline.
- **CNN Backbone (ResNet-50):**
 - ResNet-50 baseline: 88.2%
 - **ResNet-50 + HERBS: 89.8% (+1.6%)**
 Notably, HERBS provided an even larger accuracy improvement for ResNet-50 than for Swin Transformer, suggesting its effectiveness across different architectural paradigms. This larger gain might be partly attributed to the CNN's potentially lesser inherent capability to handle multi-scale information compared to Transformers.

**C. Qualitative Analysis (Heatmaps):**
Visualizations of heatmaps demonstrated HERBS's superior ability to manage attention:
- The original backbone showed attention spread over large background areas.
- Adding only PA or single-classifier approaches showed narrowly focused attention.
- Multi-classifier PA increased attention area but could also reduce accuracy if not properly constrained.
- **HERBS generated heatmaps that maintained detailed focus on discriminative regions while simultaneously capturing a broader, appropriate contextual area**, effectively filtering out background noise. This illustrated that HERBS achieved a balance between fine-grained detail and global context.

**D. Performance on Fine-Classes:**
HERBS improved performance on generic classes with multiple subcategories in CUB-200-2011. It showed higher precision and, crucially, a **lower number of false positives (FP)**. A lower FP count indicates that when the model makes a mistake, it tends to misclassify within similar generic categories rather than making completely unrelated errors, which is highly beneficial for user trust and subsequent analysis (e.g., in medical diagnosis).

**E. Hyperparameter Sensitivity:**
- **Background Suppression Intensity (λ_d):** Increasing `λ_d` (e.g., from 0 to 5) led to better background suppression, as confirmed by heatmaps. The use of `tanh` for mapping classification results in `loss_d` was shown to be more stable and effective than `softmax`, supporting the rationale of separating foreground/background by feature values rather than strict probabilities.
- **High-Temperature (T):** The optimal Top-1 and Top-3 accuracy was observed at `T=64`. High temperatures were linked to a higher "false-true rate" (wrong in top-down but correct in bottom-up paths), which correlates with the model's exploration of diverse features and a broader attention area in early training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HERBS framework represents a significant step forward in fine-grained visual classification, offering several key contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Robustness to Background Noise:** By explicitly suppressing background noise, HERBS enhances the signal-to-noise ratio for discriminative features. This is crucial in real-world scenarios where images often contain cluttered or irrelevant backgrounds that can confuse models. This approach can be particularly impactful in applications like medical imaging, where contextual noise might be prevalent, or in industrial inspection, where anomalies need to be detected against varying backgrounds.
2. **Balanced Feature Learning (Global and Local):** The high-temperature refinement module effectively addresses the trade-off between capturing global context and focusing on fine details. This ability to learn diverse features across scales makes the model more adaptable and less prone to over-specialization on narrow regions, leading to more robust and accurate classifications. This has implications for domains requiring both macro and micro-level analysis, such as identifying plant diseases (overall leaf health vs. specific lesion patterns).
3. **Backbone Agnostic Design:** The modular nature of HERBS allows its integration with various CNN and Transformer backbones. This flexibility means that researchers and practitioners can leverage the benefits of HERBS with their preferred or existing base models without extensive re-engineering, accelerating its adoption and impact across different applications.
4. **Improved Interpretability and Trustworthiness:** By consciously separating foreground and background, and showing a lower false positive rate within fine-grained classes, HERBS offers a more interpretable and reliable classification process. In critical applications like clinical diagnosis or quality control, knowing that misclassifications are likely within very similar categories (rather than wildly off-target) can increase user confidence and streamline human review processes.
5. **New Research Directions:** The paper opens avenues for future work, such as exploring adaptive strategies for selecting temperature and suppression intensity hyperparameters. Investigating low-computation-cost versions of HERBS could further enhance its practical utility in resource-constrained environments.

Overall, HERBS provides a promising and practical solution for improving the performance of fine-grained visual classification tasks, pushing the accuracy benchmarks and addressing fundamental challenges in feature learning and noise suppression. Its comprehensive approach and demonstrated effectiveness make it a valuable addition to the computer vision community's toolkit.
