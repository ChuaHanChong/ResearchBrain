# TactiDex: A Real-World Tactile-Guided Benchmark for Human-Like Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> TactiDex is a real-world hand-object interaction benchmark pairing whole-hand tactile pressure with synchronized hand kinematics, wrist and object 6D poses across 757 sequences and 49 objects. Built on it, TactiSkill trains a residual reinforcement-learning policy with a tri-component tactile reward combining contact guidance, human force alignment, and a safety cap, lifting tactile-aware success from 39.35% to 64.64% over kinematic-only transfer.

## Problem

- Human-to-robot dexterous transfer pipelines align kinematic trajectories only, reproducing surface motion while contact formation, force modulation, and interaction stability diverge from human behavior.
- Existing hand-object datasets infer contact from geometric proximity or visual observation, leaving real force modulation physically unmeasured, so objectives implicitly favor motion matching.
- Prior tactile reinforcement learning treats pressure as an auxiliary observation, and naive force-matching rewards cause unstable optimization, reward exploitation, or physically unrealistic simulated behavior.

## Method

- **TactiDex** capture: inner mocap glove plus outer 162-element tactile glove, hardware-synced with ==OptiTrack==, then two-stage ==tactile-constrained post-optimization== removing floating contacts and mesh penetration.
- **TactiSkill** couples a frozen ==kinematic base policy== with a ==residual policy== repurposed as a force modulator, under ==asymmetric actor-critic== where only the critic sees simulated contact forces.
- ==Tri-component tactile reward== sums a binary contact-guidance bonus, a tanh-based force-distribution alignment term, and an exponential safety penalty above a per-finger dynamic force limit.

## Results

- TactiSkill reaches **81.95%** kinematic and **64.64%** tactile-aware success versus **72.91%** and **39.35%** for the kinematic residual baseline, with contact F1 rising from **0.5569** to **0.7384**.
- Safety and tracking both improve: PeakSafe@3N **72.80%** versus **63.66%**, SafeTac@3N **53.57%** versus **35.84%**, object translation error **0.9577 cm**, rotation error **6.1974 degrees**.
- Ablations confirm each term: dropping the bonus lowers contact F1 to **0.5425**, dropping alignment lowers tactile success to **42.20%**, dropping safety cuts SafeTac@3N to **38.72%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mean force error is a gameable metric; without an explicit peak penalty a policy attains the lowest average error while emitting destructive force spikes.
> - Measured contact supervision acts as a physical regularizer that also improves geometric tracking, so physical realism and kinematic accuracy are complementary rather than competing.
> - Structuring tactile reward into contact triggering, distribution matching, and force capping stabilizes learning where plain force regression fails; simulation-hardware compliance gaps remain unresolved.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work addresses a recognized gap in the field of dexterous robotic manipulation, particularly in the transfer of human skills to robots. Current approaches predominantly rely on kinematic imitation, where robotic systems replicate human hand trajectories and visual observations. While these methods have shown progress, they often overlook the critical role of tactile feedback in establishing and maintaining stable hand-object interactions (HOI). Human dexterity fundamentally relies on continuous regulation of contact forces, a characteristic that kinematic mimicry alone does not capture.

Existing HOI datasets primarily focus on motion capture data or egocentric visual observations, with some recent efforts incorporating tactile sensing. However, these tactile-rich datasets often focus on perception tasks rather than dynamic manipulation control, or they suffer from limitations such as reliance on simulated geometry rather than real-world tactile fidelity, or lack of precise synchronization between tactile and kinematic data. This absence of comprehensive, synchronized tactile-kinematic benchmarks has constrained learning objectives to prioritize motion matching over physically grounded interaction, leading to robots that can mimic movements but lack human-like contact behavior and force modulation.

This paper positions itself as a response to this challenge by introducing a real-world, tactile-rich HOI dataset and a corresponding transfer framework. It aims to bridge the gap between superficial motion imitation and physically plausible, human-like dexterous manipulation by providing a benchmark that explicitly integrates whole-hand tactile signals with multi-granularity kinematic and object states, along with a learning framework that leverages these signals as structured supervision. This approach diverges from previous work by moving beyond auxiliary use of tactile signals towards making them a central component for learning contact-rich manipulation policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance dexterous manipulation from purely kinematic imitation to physically grounded, human-like interaction. This overarching goal is motivated by the observation that human dexterity is intrinsically tied to continuous regulation of contact through tactile feedback, which governs contact formation, force modulation, and manipulation stability. Current human-to-robot transfer pipelines, relying mainly on kinematic trajectories, result in robots that can imitate motion but often fail to achieve physically plausible and stable interactions at the contact level.

Specifically, the authors identify several key motivations and objectives:

1. **Address the limitations of kinematic-centric transfer:** The paper highlights that existing methods, while advanced in trajectory reproduction, frequently result in contact-level discrepancies such as unstable grasps, excessive force, or physically implausible interactions. The motivation is to move beyond geometric alignment to achieve consistency in force modulation and pressure distribution, which are crucial for delicate, contact-rich tasks.
2. **Establish a tactile-rich benchmark:** A significant motivation is the absence of benchmarks that provide strictly synchronized tactile-kinematic demonstrations and standardized contact-aware evaluation protocols. The paper aims to create TactiDex, a comprehensive real-world dataset that aligns whole-hand tactile signals with multi-granularity kinematic and object states. This dataset is intended to serve as a foundation for studying tactile-aware human-to-robot transfer and enabling physically grounded modeling of human dexterity.
3. **Develop a tactile-driven transfer framework:** Direct incorporation of raw pressure measurements into reinforcement learning (RL) objectives can be unstable. Therefore, a core objective is to propose TactiSkill, a tactile-driven transfer framework that effectively translates human demonstrations into physically plausible robotic execution. This framework is designed to leverage tactile signals as structured supervision, moving beyond their use as passive observations or auxiliary data.
4. **Formulate a robust tactile reward mechanism:** To stabilize contact-rich interaction and achieve human-like force distributions, the paper aims to design a tri-component tactile reward. This reward is motivated by the need to unify guidance for meaningful contact formation, alignment with human force distribution patterns, and constraints against excessive forces into a single, stable optimization objective.
5. **Demonstrate superior performance and physical realism:** The ultimate objective is to demonstrate, through experiments on single and bimanual tasks, that tactile-guided transfer frameworks like TactiSkill can achieve superior performance in manipulation success and physical realism compared to purely kinematic approaches. This includes validating the framework's effectiveness through real-world deployment on dexterous robotic hands.

In essence, the work is motivated by the understanding that true human-like dexterous manipulation requires physically grounded interaction, not just motion mimicry, and aims to provide both the data and the methodological framework to achieve this.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces TactiDex, a real-world tactile-guided benchmark, and TactiSkill, a tactile-driven human-to-robot transfer framework. The methodology can be divided into data collection and processing, and the policy learning framework.

#### 3.1. TactiDex Dataset (Section 3)

**Hardware Setup:**
A multimodal data collection system was built to synchronously record hand contact information, hand kinematic states, and accurate object 6D poses.
- **Tactile Measurement:** Custom tactile gloves with 162 piezoresistive sensing elements were used, densely distributed across fingertips and palms. These gloves provide force resolution up to 0.01 N at 17 Hz. A redesigned dual-glove structure allows the tactile glove to be worn externally over a motion capture glove, ensuring stable tactile signal acquisition without degrading kinematic tracking.
- **Motion Capture:** An OptiTrack motion capture system, equipped with 8 PX13W cameras operating at 120 fps, provided global 6-DoF poses for the wrist and objects within a 1.2 m × 1.8 m workspace. Hand kinematics were captured by a motion-tracking glove, which fused IMU measurements with optical tracking data. This setup ensures inherent alignment of hand and object poses within a unified spatial and temporal coordinate system.

**Data Collection and Processing:**
- **Synchronized Multimodal Capture:** A strict protocol ensured frame-level temporal alignment between the 120 Hz kinematic/pose streams and the tactile pressure data, facilitating precise temporal modeling of contact.
- **Object Modeling and Calibration:** 49 diverse objects were selected. Their 3D meshes were reconstructed using a hybrid pipeline of mobile 3D scanning and SAM3D-based methods. Specialized non-reflective textured tapes and temporary fiducial markers were used to overcome challenges with reflective or symmetric surfaces, ensuring high-fidelity meshes. Each object was dynamically calibrated within the OptiTrack system.
- **Task Coverage and Data Scope:** The dataset comprises 757 interaction sequences, covering single-hand and bimanual manipulation tasks, from basic pick-and-place to complex tool use and functional operations. Each sequence includes synchronized multimodal sensory data, interaction-phase timestamps, task identifiers, and natural-language descriptions.
- **Optimization and Processing:** The MANO model was used for kinematic representation, with parameters fitted to raw 3D motion-capture keypoints via L-BFGS optimization. To ensure anatomical and temporal consistency, shape parameters were shared across frames for each subject, and regularization was applied for pose and temporal smoothness.
- **Tactile-Constrained Post-Optimization:** A two-stage post-optimization process addressed issues like "floating" or penetration.
 1. Genuine contact intervals were identified by gating geometric proximity with measured tactile pressure. Within these intervals, a stable reference grasp frame was optimized for spatial alignment using tactile-modulated per-finger weights (active fingers attracted, inactive fingers down-weighted).
 2. The refined grasp was propagated across the contact interval via temporal inverse kinematics, and a collision-aware adjustment optimized finger articulation against the object’s Signed Distance Field (SDF) to eliminate residual penetrations. This pipeline ensured kinematically precise and physically grounded interactions.

#### 3.2. TactiSkill Framework (Section 4)

TactiSkill is built on a "Tactile-Guided Tri-Component Transfer Framework" that leverages synchronized contact signals to guide robotic policy learning within a constrained Markov Decision Process (MDP). The goal is to replicate human kinematics and dynamic contact patterns.

- **Problem Formulation:** The state space `S` includes standard proprioception (joint positions/velocities) and object states (pose `p_t`). Crucially, a "Target Tactile Reference" `F_human_t` from TactiDex guides the robot on desired normal forces. The action space `A` consists of residual joint position commands (`Δq_t`). The objective is to minimize kinematic errors while maximizing force consistency with human demonstrations.

- **Policy Architecture:** A residual reinforcement learning architecture is used, combining a frozen kinematic imitation policy `π_base` and a learnable residual policy `π_res`. `π_res` acts as a "Force Modulator," inducing and regulating simulated contact forces.

- **Three-Step Pipeline:**
 1. **Finger-wise Sensor-to-Sim Data Alignment:** A non-linear calibration mapping function `M` converts raw human tactile sensor signals (ADC readings) into aligned normal forces (in Newtons) consistent with the simulation environment, establishing a unified physical baseline for supervision.
 2. **Asymmetric Observation with Privileged Tactile Information:** An asymmetric Actor-Critic architecture is employed.
 - The **Actor** receives only the target tactile reference `F_human_t` (mimicking real-world constraints where the robot has a desired force reference but cannot foresee exact contact dynamics).
 - The **Critic** receives privileged information: real-time simulated fingertip contact forces `F_sim_t`. This allows the Critic to accurately evaluate value gradients, guiding the Actor to adjust joint poses to approximate the target force distribution.
 3. **Physics-Aware Reward: The Tri-Component Supervision:** The composite tactile reward `R_tactile` (Eq. 1) regulates normal contact forces `F_i` for each finger, comprising three components:
 - **Tactile Guidance (r_guide, Eq. 2):** A binary contact bonus that rewards the agent for replicating an active contact event initiated by the human demonstrator (`F_sim_i > τ` AND `F_human_i > τ`). It addresses "air grasping."
 - **Human-Like Alignment (r_align, Eq. 3):** Uses a Tanh-based distance metric to minimize the discrepancy between simulated and human forces (`|F_sim_i - F_human_i| / σ`). This provides normalized gradients, preserves force distribution, and resists outliers.
 - **Contact Constraints (r_safe, Eq. 4):** An exponential penalty applied when simulated force exceeds a dynamic safety limit (`F_limit = F_human_i + δ`). This prevents unrealistically high forces and promotes stable interaction.

- **Total Reward Formulation:** The final reward function `R_total` (Eq. 5) combines the tactile supervision (`R_tactile`) with standard kinematic constraints (`r_im` for pose tracking, `r_task` for object trajectory, `r_reg` for energy efficiency). The `R_tactile` term acts as a dynamic regularizer, ensuring human-like and physically stable interaction forces.

#### 3.3. Real-World Deployment (Section 5.4)

The trained policies are deployed on a bimanual platform with two 7-DoF Franka arms and two Inspire dexterous hands. A retargeting objective (Eq. 7) is used to map the simulated 12-DoF trajectory `q_sim_1:T` to the 6-actuator Inspire hand `~q_1:T`. Arm motions are executed by solving inverse kinematics to track the retargeted wrist pose. Tactile sensing on the Inspire hands is available but is not used as feedback in the current open-loop deployment, demonstrating the robustness of the learned policies from simulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of TactiSkill involved comparing its performance against a kinematic baseline (ManipTrans) and conducting an ablation study where core tactile components were removed ("w/o Bonus," "w/o Align," "w/o Safety"). The evaluation was performed on 73 representative interaction sequences, covering single-hand and bimanual tasks.

#### 4.1. Data Split and Evaluation Metrics

The policies were assessed across four dimensions:
1. **Kinematic and Object Tracking:** Object Tracking Error (OTE-t for translation, OTE-r for rotation), Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE), and Mean Per-Fingertip Position Error (TipErr).
2. **Tactile and Physical Fidelity:** Mean Tactile Force Error (MTFE), and Contact F1-Score (binarized contact states against ground truth).
3. **Safety and Reliability:** Peak transient force discrepancy (MaxErr), PeakSafe@3N (fraction of episodes where MaxErr < 3N), and SafeTac@3N (tactile-aware success with MaxErr < 3N).
4. **Task Success Rate (SR):** Kinematic Success Rate (SR_kin, based on criteria for OTE, MPJPE, TipErr), and a stricter Tactile-Aware Success Rate (SR_tac, requiring kinematic success plus MTFE ≤ 3.0 N and Contact F1 ≥ 0.3).

#### 4.2. Main Results and Ablative Analysis

- **Insufficiency of Pure Kinematic Imitation:** The kinematic baseline ("Base") achieved a SR_kin of 72.91% and low OTE-t (1.1947 cm), but its tactile-aware success rate (SR_tac) dropped significantly to 39.35%. This baseline also exhibited a low Contact F1-score (0.5569) and safety metrics (SafeTac@3N = 35.84%). This indicates that geometric alignment alone often results in "air grasping" or mesh penetration, lacking physical realism.

- **Synergistic Effect of Tactile Guidance (TactiSkill Full):** The full TactiSkill framework demonstrated superior overall performance. It achieved the highest SR_kin (81.95%) and lowest tracking errors (OTE-t = 0.9577 cm, OTE-r = 6.1974°). Critically, it significantly outperformed all baselines in physical fidelity, securing the highest Contact F1-score (0.7384) and boosting SR_tac to 64.64%. The episode-level safety metrics also showed improvement, with PeakSafe@3N at 72.80% and SafeTac@3N at 53.57%. These results suggest that tactile supervision acts as a powerful physical regularizer, preventing invalid geometric optima and shifting from passive trajectory following to active force regulation.

- **Ablation on Contact Initiation and Alignment:**
 - **"w/o Bonus"**: Removing the contact bonus led to a plummet in Contact F1-score (0.5425) and a drop in PeakSafe@3N (57.00%), indicating a failure to initiate stable grasps.
 - **"w/o Align"**: Omitting force alignment resulted in spatially awkward grasps and degraded dynamic stability, with a low Contact F1-score (0.5134) and subpar SR_tac (42.20%). This highlights the importance of matching human force distribution patterns.

- **The Paradox of the Safety Penalty ("w/o Safety"):** Surprisingly, removing the safety penalty yielded the lowest mean errors (MTFE = 0.0921 N). However, this was deceptive, as the unconstrained policy allowed occasional, highly destructive force impulses to optimize the average metric. Peak safety metrics degraded, with PeakSafe@3N at 68.70% and SafeTac@3N at 38.72%. This finding emphasizes that the safety term is crucial for suppressing momentary destructive behaviors, leading to genuinely reliable, safety-aware manipulation at the task level, rather than just minimizing average errors.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Analysis

Qualitative results (Figure 3) demonstrated that TactiSkill enabled dexterous hands to establish flush and physically stable contact, avoiding unnatural penetrations or hovering. The policy adapted multi-fingered postures to diverse object geometries in single-hand tasks and exhibited coordination in bimanual scenarios, maintaining secure grasps and regulating interactive forces.

#### 4.4. Real-World Deployment

The policies were successfully deployed on a bimanual Franka-Inspire platform (Figure 4). Despite the Inspire hand's underactuation and the open-loop nature of the deployment (tactile sensing not used as online feedback), the framework successfully reproduced stable, contact-rich manipulation on physical hardware. This suggests that the policies learned robust, physics-aware force distributions during training, allowing for effective zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces TactiDex, a tactile-rich benchmark, and TactiSkill, a tactile-guided transfer framework, which collectively address a critical limitation in dexterous robotic manipulation: the gap between kinematic imitation and physically grounded human-like interaction.

**Significance:**

- **Pioneering Tactile-Rich Benchmark:** TactiDex is presented as the first high-precision, long-horizon dataset for dexterous bimanual manipulation that synchronously integrates whole-hand tactile signals, fine-grained hand kinematics, and object 6D trajectories. This dataset provides a unique resource for the research community, enabling contact-aware evaluation and facilitating the development of policies that learn from the physical intent embedded in human demonstrations.
- **Advancing Human-to-Robot Transfer:** The TactiSkill framework shifts the paradigm of human-to-robot skill transfer from purely geometric alignment to physical interaction alignment. By leveraging tactile sensing as a core supervisory signal and introducing a tri-component tactile reward, the framework enables robotic policies to actively regulate contact formation, force magnitude, and temporal alignment. This moves beyond motion mimicry to achieve physically plausible and stable interactions.
- **Demonstrated Robustness and Fidelity:** The quantitative and qualitative experimental results demonstrate that tactile-guided transfer significantly improves geometric robustness, physical fidelity, and manipulation success across diverse manipulation tasks. The ability to enhance contact F1-score, reduce tactile force errors, and improve safety metrics (PeakSafe@3N, SafeTac@3N) indicates a substantial improvement in the realism and reliability of robotic manipulation.
- **Successful Real-World Deployment:** The successful deployment of TactiSkill-derived policies on a physical bimanual robotic platform, even without real-time tactile feedback, validates the framework's effectiveness in bridging the sim-to-real gap. This suggests that policies can learn robust, physics-aware force distributions in simulation that generalize to real hardware.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Foundation for Next-Generation Dexterous Robots:** This work lays a foundation for developing more capable dexterous robots that can interact with objects and environments with human-like sensitivity and control. This could lead to robots performing delicate tasks requiring fine force control, such as surgery, assembly of fragile components, or handling irregular objects.
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** By enabling robots to learn physically grounded interactions, the research contributes to increasing robot autonomy in unstructured environments where contact dynamics are crucial. Robots could better adapt to uncertainties in object properties or environmental conditions.
- **Safer Human-Robot Interaction:** The emphasis on contact constraints and safety metrics suggests potential for developing robots that interact more safely with humans and objects by avoiding excessive forces or unstable behaviors.
- **New Research Directions:** TactiDex and TactiSkill open new avenues for research in multimodal learning, sim-to-real transfer for contact-rich tasks, and the integration of diverse sensory modalities (vision, proprioception, touch) for comprehensive understanding of HOI. Future work could explore closed-loop adaptation using real tactile feedback and more complex, long-horizon tasks.

In summary, this research provides both a novel dataset and a methodological framework that explicitly address the critical role of tactile feedback in dexterous manipulation, moving the field closer to achieving truly human-like robotic interaction.
