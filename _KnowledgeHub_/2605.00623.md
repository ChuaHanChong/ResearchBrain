# Recovering Hidden Reward in Diffusion-Based Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> ENERGYFLOW unifies generative action modeling with inverse reinforcement learning by parameterizing a scalar energy function whose negative gradient directly defines the diffusion policy's denoising field. This framework achieves competitive imitation performance on benchmarks and real robots, while also providing an effective reward signal for downstream reinforcement learning.

## Problem

- Diffusion-based policies excel at imitation but lack explicit reward signals, limiting their robustness and generalization to novel situations.
- Designing effective reward functions for complex robotic tasks is challenging, and traditional Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) methods are often computationally intensive and prone to training instabilities.
- The absence of an explicit underlying intent or task preference in pure behavior cloning methods restricts generalization capabilities and adaptability.

## Method

- ENERGYFLOW parameterizes a scalar energy function whose action gradient corresponds to the denoising field used in diffusion models, effectively unifying generative action modeling and reward extraction.
- It enforces a conservative vector field by explicitly defining the score function as the negative gradient of the learned energy, which serves as a beneficial inductive bias.
- A 'centered shaping' mechanism extracts a stable, state-dependent reward signal from the learned energy function, suitable for training downstream ==reinforcement learning== agents without adversarial methods.

## Results

- Achieved **93.8%** average success on RoboMimic and **92.5%** on Meta-World, outperforming Diffusion Policy (**91.2%** on RoboMimic), and demonstrated **100%** success on real AGIBOT G1 robot tasks.
- The 'Centered Energy' reward signal effectively trained Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) agents to near-oracle performance on RoboMimic tasks, outperforming sparse and raw energy rewards.
- Showed superior out-of-distribution generalization and robustness under increasing initial state perturbations compared to baselines, validating the theoretical benefits of the conservative field constraint.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The score function of a maximum-entropy optimal policy is theoretically equivalent to the gradient of its soft Q-function, connecting diffusion models to reward functions.
> - Enforcing a conservative vector field by deriving the score from a scalar energy function significantly reduces hypothesis complexity, leading to improved out-of-distribution generalization.
> - The framework allows for the identifiability of relative action preferences within a given state, providing a robust and stable reward signal for policy optimization.
