# Recovering Hidden Reward in Diffusion-Based Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> ENERGYFLOW unifies generative action modeling with inverse reinforcement learning by parameterizing a scalar energy function whose negative gradient directly defines the diffusion policy's denoising field. This framework achieves competitive imitation performance on benchmarks and real robots, while also providing an effective reward signal for downstream reinforcement learning.

## Problem

- Diffusion-based policies excel at imitation but lack explicit reward signals, limiting their robustness and generalization to novel situations.
- Designing effective reward functions for complex robotic tasks is challenging, and traditional Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) methods are often computationally intensive and prone to training instabilities.
- The absence of an explicit underlying intent or task preference in pure behavior cloning methods restricts generalization capabilities and adaptability.

## Method

- ENERGYFLOW parameterizes a scalar energy function whose action gradient corresponds to the denoising field used in diffusion models, effectively unifying generative action modeling and reward extraction.
- It enforces a conservative vector field by explicitly defining the score function as the negative gradient of the learned energy, which serves as a beneficial inductive bias.
- A 'centered shaping' mechanism extracts a stable, state-dependent reward signal from the learned energy function, suitable for training downstream ==reinforcement learning== agents without adversarial methods.

## Results

- Achieved **93.8%** average success on RoboMimic and **92.5%** on Meta-World, outperforming Diffusion Policy (**91.2%** on RoboMimic), and demonstrated **100%** success on real AGIBOT G1 robot tasks.
- The 'Centered Energy' reward signal effectively trained Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) agents to near-oracle performance on RoboMimic tasks, outperforming sparse and raw energy rewards.
- Showed superior out-of-distribution generalization and robustness under increasing initial state perturbations compared to baselines, validating the theoretical benefits of the conservative field constraint.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The score function of a maximum-entropy optimal policy is theoretically equivalent to the gradient of its soft Q-function, connecting diffusion models to reward functions.
> - Enforcing a conservative vector field by deriving the score from a scalar energy function significantly reduces hypothesis complexity, leading to improved out-of-distribution generalization.
> - The framework allows for the identifiability of relative action preferences within a given state, providing a robust and stable reward signal for policy optimization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research integrates two distinct areas within artificial intelligence for robotic control: diffusion-based policies for behavior cloning and inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) for reward recovery.

**Diffusion-Based Policies:** Recent advancements in generative models, particularly diffusion models, have been applied to robot learning, demonstrating capabilities in acquiring complex manipulation skills from expert demonstrations. These "diffusion policies" learn to generate actions by iteratively denoising corrupted samples conditioned on the current state. They excel at modeling complex, multi-modal action distributions, which is beneficial for capturing diverse expert behaviors. However, a common limitation of these policies is their reliance on a pure behavior cloning (BC) objective. BC imitates observed trajectories without explicitly modeling the underlying intent or task preferences that make certain actions desirable. This can lead to decreased robustness and limited generalization when faced with situations that deviate from the training demonstration distribution.

**Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL):** IRL methods aim to infer an underlying reward function from expert demonstrations, thereby capturing the expert's intent. This inferred reward can then be used to train reinforcement learning (RL) agents, potentially leading to more robust and adaptable policies. Traditional IRL approaches, especially those based on maximum-entropy principles or adversarial training (e.g., GAIL, AIRL), often involve substantial computational overhead and can suffer from training instabilities, such as mode collapse or convergence issues. Energy-Based Models (EBMs) offer an alternative to adversarial methods by directly parameterizing a scalar energy function representing rewards, but they typically require computationally expensive Monte Carlo Markov Chain (MCMC) sampling for inference or partition function approximation, which scales poorly to high-dimensional action spaces.

**Bridging the Gap:** This paper addresses the limitations of both fields by proposing a framework that simultaneously achieves the generative capabilities of diffusion policies and the reward-extraction benefits of IRL. Existing works have explored combining diffusion models with RL for adaptation, or using diffusion models as discriminators in IRL, but they often treat these components as separate. This work, in contrast, postulates a fundamental connection between the denoising mechanism of diffusion models and the gradient of an underlying energy-based reward function. By establishing theoretical equivalence and enforcing structural constraints, the paper aims to provide a unified approach that benefits from both paradigms without incurring the typical drawbacks of traditional IRL or the generalization limitations of pure BC.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce ENERGYFLOW, a framework designed to unify generative action modeling with inverse reinforcement learning. This unification is pursued by parameterizing a scalar energy function whose gradient directly corresponds to the denoising field used in diffusion models.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations in current robot learning paradigms:

1. **Modeling Intent Beyond Behavior Cloning:** While diffusion-based policies are effective at imitating expert actions, they typically operate under a behavior cloning (BC) objective. This means they learn *what* actions to take but not *why* those actions are optimal, neglecting the underlying intent or task preferences. This absence of an explicit reward signal limits the policy's robustness and its ability to generalize to novel or out-of-distribution states, as matching action likelihood alone may not be sufficient for reliable action selection in varied scenarios.
2. **Challenges of Reward Design and Inverse Reinforcement Learning:** Reward-driven reinforcement learning (RL) is crucial for developing complex cognitive abilities in embodied agents. However, designing effective reward functions for real-world robotic tasks is often a meticulous and challenging process. Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) offers a solution by learning rewards from demonstrations, but it frequently introduces significant computational overhead and can be prone to training instabilities characteristic of adversarial optimization schemes.
3. **Exploiting Implicit Reward Signals in Diffusion Models:** The authors hypothesize that a reward signal is already implicitly present within diffusion-based imitation processes. By drawing connections between diffusion models and energy-based modeling, they aim to explicitly recover this signal. The objective is to construct an energy landscape that not only induces a generative vector field for action sampling but also directly provides a reward signal consistent with maximum-entropy IRL principles, without requiring adversarial training.
4. **Improving Generalization and Robustness:** The paper seeks to demonstrate that imposing structural constraints, specifically enforcing a conservative vector field (i.e., one that is the gradient of a scalar potential), can serve as a beneficial inductive bias. This constraint is hypothesized to reduce the complexity of the hypothesis space, thereby tightening generalization bounds and improving the policy's performance in out-of-distribution scenarios, which is a known challenge for imitation learning methods.
5. **Efficiency and Practicality:** The work also aims to provide a reward extraction method that is efficient and yields a stable signal for downstream RL, contrasting with the computational intensity and instability often associated with traditional IRL or MCMC-based EBMs.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
- Propose ENERGYFLOW, parameterizing a scalar energy function to unify action generation and reward extraction.
- Theoretically establish the equivalence between the score function of a maximum-entropy optimal policy and the gradient of its soft Q-function.
- Prove that enforcing a conservative field, by parameterizing energy and deriving the score from its gradient, reduces hypothesis complexity and improves out-of-distribution generalization.
- Characterize the identifiability of recovered rewards and bound the propagation of score estimation errors to action preferences.
- Empirically demonstrate that ENERGYFLOW achieves competitive imitation performance, provides an effective reward signal for downstream RL, and exhibits improved out-of-distribution generalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of ENERGYFLOW is built upon theoretically unifying denoising score matching with inverse reinforcement learning by leveraging an energy-based parameterization.

**Theoretical Analysis:**
1. **Equivalence Between Scores and Reward Gradients (Theorem 3.3):** The paper establishes that under the assumption of Maximum Entropy Optimality (Assumption 3.1), where an expert policy follows a Boltzmann distribution based on a soft Q-function, the true score function (gradient of the log expert policy) is directly proportional to the gradient of the expert's soft Q-function. This implies that if a parameterized energy function $E_\phi(a, s)$ is trained such that its negative action-gradient approximates the expert's score, then $E_\phi$ recovers the soft Q-function up to a state-dependent constant. This theoretical link suggests that score matching, typically used for generative modeling, can implicitly recover reward information.
2. **Enforcing Conservative Fields (Theorem 3.6):** A crucial aspect is that for the recovered "reward" to be well-defined and consistent, the learned score field must be conservative, meaning it must be the gradient of a scalar potential. A non-conservative field would imply cyclic preferences, violating rational decision-making axioms. To guarantee this, ENERGYFLOW parameterizes a scalar energy network $E_\phi(a, s, t)$ and defines the score function as $S_\phi(a, s, t) = - \nabla_a E_\phi(a, s, t)$. This construction inherently ensures the field is conservative. The paper theoretically demonstrates that this integrability constraint acts as a powerful inductive bias, reducing hypothesis complexity and tightening generalization bounds compared to unconstrained vector fields, especially in high-dimensional action spaces.
3. **OOD Generalization (Lemma 3.8):** Building on the conservative field constraint, the paper proves that this structural constraint helps improve out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization. By forcing the model to extrapolate a coherent energy landscape, it prevents the learning of arbitrary vector directions, thus leading to more robust performance when initial states are perturbed.
4. **Identifiability and Robustness (Proposition 3.9, Theorem 3.11):** The analysis confirms that while the absolute Q-function cannot be uniquely identified (due to an unknown state-dependent integration constant $c(s)$), the relative ranking of actions *within a given state* is precisely preserved. The paper also provides a bound on how score estimation errors propagate to action preferences, showing a Lipschitz-continuous relationship, which implies graceful degradation under approximate learning.

**Methodology Implementation (ENERGYFLOW Framework):**
1. **Architecture:**
 - **Energy Function Parameterization:** Instead of directly predicting a vector-valued noise or score, ENERGYFLOW parameterizes a scalar energy function $E_\phi(a, s, t)$.
 - **Backbone:** A 1D Conditional U-Net, similar to Diffusion Policy, is used.
 - **State and Time Conditioning:** State observations are flattened and projected via an MLP, and diffusion timesteps are encoded with sinusoidal embeddings. These are injected into the U-Net blocks via Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM).
 - **Scalar Output Head:** The critical modification is replacing the standard vector output head of a U-Net with a 3-layer MLP that produces a single scalar energy value.
 - **C² Differentiability:** All ReLU activations are replaced with Mish activations to ensure the network is twice-differentiable (C²) for computing the gradient of the score (which is the second derivative of the energy).
2. **Training Paradigm:**
 - **Denoising Score Matching:** The energy landscape is estimated using a denoising score matching objective. The model is trained to minimize the difference between the negative gradient of its energy function ($-\nabla_a E_\phi(a_t, s, t)$) and the true noise scaled by $\sigma(t)$ (representing the true score for a noisy sample $a_t$).
 - **Automatic Differentiation:** PyTorch's `autograd.grad` with `create_graph=True` is used to compute the gradient of the scalar energy with respect to the action $a_t$, forming the score function used in the loss.
 - **Noise Schedule:** A geometric noise schedule is employed (variance-exploding formulation).
3. **Reward Extraction for Downstream RL:**
 - **Centered Shaping:** To address the state-dependent offset $c(s)$ (Proposition 3.9) that would introduce high variance in a raw energy reward, a "centered shaping" mechanism is introduced. The reward signal is defined as $\tilde{r}_\phi(a, s) = - (E_\phi(a, s, \epsilon) - E_{a' \sim N(0,I)}[E_\phi(a', s, \gamma)])$, where a state-dependent baseline (expected energy under a reference action distribution) is subtracted. This normalization ensures the reward reflects only relative action preferences within a state, providing a stable signal for policy gradient updates in downstream RL.
 - **Inference:** Actions are generated by integrating the probability-flow ODE from a noisy action $a_T$ down to $a_\gamma$ (where $\gamma$ is a small endpoint time) using the learned score $S_\phi = -\nabla_a E_\phi$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of ENERGYFLOW addressed six research questions, yielding the following main findings:

1. **Imitation Learning Performance (RQ1):**
 - ENERGYFLOW achieved high imitation performance on both RoboMimic and Meta-World benchmarks.
 - On RoboMimic, it attained an average success rate of 93.8%, surpassing Diffusion Policy (91.2%) and Flow Policy (89.6%). Notable improvements were observed on challenging tasks like ToolHang (84.2% for ENERGYFLOW vs. 77.2% for Diffusion Policy).
 - On Meta-World, ENERGYFLOW also led with a 92.5% average success rate.
 - These results suggest that the conservative parameterization and flow-matching training objective can enhance energy-based policy representation beyond existing energy-based approaches.

2. **Real Robot Deployment (RQ2):**
 - The learned energy-parameterized policy was successfully deployed on an AGIBOT G1 physical robot.
 - ENERGYFLOW achieved a 100% success rate on two contact-rich manipulation tasks, "Bottle" and "Drawer," under various initial position changes, based on 20 expert demonstration trajectories.
 - Qualitative observations indicated that ENERGYFLOW produced smoother trajectories with reduced hesitation near contact points, suggesting effective transfer to real-world scenarios.

3. **Reward Quality (RQ3):**
 - The learned energy function, when processed with centered shaping, proved to be an effective reward signal for downstream reinforcement learning.
 - Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) agents trained with "Centered Energy" rewards on RoboMimic Square and Transport tasks learned more effectively than with sparse rewards or raw energy rewards.
 - "Centered Energy" rewards enabled SAC agents to achieve near-oracle success rates, similar to agents trained with dense oracle rewards.
 - The combination of "Centered Energy+Sparse" rewards performed optimally, indicating that the energy reward provides useful step-by-step guidance, while the sparse reward ensures optimization for task completion.

4. **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization (RQ4):**
 - Under increasing initial position perturbations, ENERGYFLOW demonstrated superior stability and robustness compared to baselines like Diffusion Policy and Flow Policy.
 - While all methods performed well in-distribution, ENERGYFLOW maintained robust success rates at medium and large perturbation levels, where unconstrained models showed degradation.
 - This finding supports the theoretical prediction (Lemma 3.8) that the curl-free (conservative) constraint acts as a geometric regularizer, improving extrapolation capabilities in tasks with spatial variability.

5. **Reward Extraction Sensitivity (RQ5):**
 - The performance remained robust across three orders of magnitude for the time parameter $\gamma$ used in energy evaluation (from $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-2}$).
 - Degradation was observed only at larger values of $\gamma$ (e.g., $\gamma \ge 0.1$), where the noised distribution significantly diverged from the data distribution, weakening the score function approximation.

6. **Inference Efficiency (RQ6):**
 - ENERGYFLOW demonstrated a balance of speed and utility, achieving high success rates with inference latency comparable to non-energy-based Flow Policy (e.g., 9.8ms for K=10 steps).
 - This contrasts with Implicit BC, which required significantly more computationally expensive Langevin sampling for high performance, suggesting that ENERGYFLOW provides the benefits of an explicit energy function without the typical runtime prohibitive costs of other EBMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ENERGYFLOW framework represents a significant development in robot learning by integrating two previously distinct paradigms: diffusion-based policy learning and inverse reinforcement learning (IRL).

**Theoretical Advancements:**
- **Unified Framework:** The work formally establishes a connection between the score function in diffusion models and the gradient of an expert's soft Q-function under maximum-entropy optimality. This theoretical unification provides a foundation for simultaneously performing generative action modeling and reward extraction.
- **Structural Constraints for Generalization:** The proof that enforcing a conservative (curl-free) vector field, by parameterizing a scalar energy function and deriving the score from its gradient, significantly reduces hypothesis complexity and tightens generalization bounds is a notable theoretical contribution. This provides a principled explanation for improved robustness, particularly in out-of-distribution scenarios.
- **Reward Identifiability and Robustness:** The characterization of within-state action ranking identifiability and the bounded error propagation for action preferences provide crucial insights into the reliability and limitations of the extracted reward signal.

**Practical Implications for Robot Learning:**
- **Effective Reward Signal without Adversarial Training:** ENERGYFLOW offers a method to automatically extract a dense, meaningful reward signal from expert demonstrations without the computational overhead or instability associated with adversarial IRL methods. This substantially simplifies the process of applying RL to complex robotic tasks, where manual reward design is often challenging. The "centered shaping" technique further refines this signal, making it highly effective for downstream policy optimization.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The empirical validation of improved out-of-distribution generalization under varying initial conditions highlights the practical benefit of the conservative field constraint. This is critical for deploying robots in real-world, less-controlled environments where deviations from training data are common.
- **Simultaneous Policy and Reward Learning:** The framework allows for the learning of a high-performing generative policy and an interpretable reward function within a single, unified training process. This offers a more holistic approach to learning from demonstrations, where the "what" (actions) and the "why" (rewards) are intrinsically linked.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Demonstrating competitive inference speed while providing an explicit energy function offers a practical advantage over other energy-based methods that often incur high computational costs due to MCMC sampling.

**Broader Impact on Embodied AI:**
This research has the potential to advance embodied AI by enabling robots to learn more robustly and adaptably from human demonstrations. By providing a principled way to recover the intent behind expert actions, ENERGYFLOW could facilitate the development of agents capable of not just mimicking behaviors but also understanding the underlying task objectives. This could lead to more intelligent and resilient robotic systems in various applications, including manufacturing, healthcare, and logistics, where complex manipulation tasks are prevalent. The framework also opens avenues for further research into integrating generative models with value-based reinforcement learning in a theoretically grounded and practically efficient manner.
