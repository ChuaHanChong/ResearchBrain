# HRM-Agent: Training a recurrent reasoning model in dynamic environments using reinforcement learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Melbourne, Australia, and Cerenaut AI researchers adapted the Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM) for reinforcement learning in dynamic maze environments, demonstrating that its recurrent internal state can maintain and adapt learned plans across environmental changes. The HRM-Agent achieved approximately 99% success rates in navigation tasks while efficiently reusing prior computations through its carried-forward latent state.

## Problem

- Existing reasoning models like Large Language Models (LLMs) are often inefficient and struggle with adaptive, sustained reasoning in dynamic and partially observable environments.
- The Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM) was previously limited to supervised, static, and fully-observable problems, restricting its real-world applicability.
- Reinforcement learning agents require mechanisms to effectively handle hierarchical, long-horizon planning and maintain consistent internal states across dynamic environmental changes.

## Method

- Adapted the ==Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM)== by replacing its output head with a ==Deep Q-Network (DQN) head== for action value prediction.
- Trained the HRM-Agent entirely from scratch using reinforcement learning, without any supervised losses or human-defined problem decompositions.
- Introduced a =="Carry Z" mechanism== where the agent's recurrent latent state from the previous environment step is carried forward to the next, promoting computational reuse.

## Results

- The HRM-Agent achieved a high success rate of approximately **99%** in goal navigation within both dynamic Four-Rooms and random maze environments.
- Demonstrated efficient path planning, with mean episode lengths approaching theoretical optimums, indicating learned deliberate reasoning.
- The "Carry Z" variant consistently led to faster convergence of the recurrent latent state and exhibited greater consistency in internal plans compared to the "Reset Z" variant.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The intrinsic recurrent reasoning capabilities of HRM can be successfully leveraged within an RL framework to develop adaptive agents for dynamic and uncertain environments.
> - Carrying forward the recurrent latent state (z) allows the agent to efficiently reuse computations, accelerate internal plan convergence, and maintain consistency in its reasoning across environmental timesteps.
> - The agent's recurrent state appears to function as an internal "plan" or "belief state" that dynamically adapts to environmental changes without being reset.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper positions itself at the intersection of advanced reasoning models, particularly the Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM), and reinforcement learning (RL) in dynamic and uncertain environments. It addresses significant limitations of current state-of-the-art AI, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs), and extends a promising new architecture to a more challenging domain.

**The Landscape of Reasoning Models:**
The paper begins by critiquing current LLMs, highlighting their inefficiencies, non-adaptive computational complexity, and unreliability in tasks requiring joint review of outputs, such as maze navigation. While techniques like "Chain of Thought" (CoT), "Tree-of-Thought" (ToT), "Thinking tokens," and "agentic" architectures have emerged, the authors argue these often rely on human insight for problem decomposition, which contrasts with the deep learning ethos of learned models outperforming human-defined strategies.

The Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM) by Wang et al. is presented as a significant counterpoint. HRM offers data-efficient reasoning in a compact model with a *recurrent inference process* that adapts computational effort using Adaptive Computation Time (ACT). Its dual recurrent modules (high-level H, low-level L) enable it to solve complex tasks like Sudoku, ARC, and maze path-planning with significantly fewer parameters and training examples than traditional deep reasoning models. Crucially, recent derivatives like the Tiny Recursive Model (TRM) [Jolicoeur-Martineau, 2025] and analyses by the ARC-prize foundation [ARC-Prize-team, 2025] underscore the recurrent process as the core driver of HRM's reasoning abilities. However, HRM has so far been limited to supervised, static, and fully-observable problems.

**The Landscape of Reinforcement Learning:**
RL has proven effective in diverse domains, but "reasoning" within RL contexts can manifest in many ways, from long-horizon planning (e.g., AlphaGo [Silver et al., 2016]) to hierarchical skill discovery. Many real-world RL problems are dynamic, uncertain, or partially observable (POMDPs), requiring agents to continuously integrate new information and adapt plans. Traditional Q-learning agents struggle with hierarchical, long-horizon reasoning in such settings. The Deep Recurrent Q Network (DRQN) [Hausknecht and Stone, 2015] introduced LSTMs to maintain an internal belief state, demonstrating the value of explicit recurrence for consistency in dynamic environments.

LLMs are also fine-tuned with RL (RLHF, RLAIF) to improve reasoning and goal-directed interaction. However, LLM-based approaches face challenges in dynamic/PO environments due to high inference latency from autoregressive rollouts and the assumption of accessible information at reasoning time. This highlights a gap for architectures that can interleave memory, reasoning, and action selection across multiple timesteps more efficiently.

**Bridging the Gap:**
This paper directly addresses the gap between HRM's powerful reasoning in static settings and the demands of dynamic, uncertain RL environments. It explores whether HRM's inherent recurrent reasoning can be leveraged in an agent trained *purely* by RL, without supervised signals. By doing so, it aims to create an agent that can incrementally develop and adapt solutions, continually integrating new information, without human-designed problem decompositions. The work specifically builds on DRQN's insight regarding recurrent state maintenance for consistent planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether the impressive reasoning capabilities of the Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM), previously demonstrated in static and fully-observable tasks, can be successfully extended to dynamic, uncertain, and partially observable environments within a reinforcement learning (RL) framework.

The core motivations driving this objective are:

- **Addressing HRM's Limitations:** The original HRM, despite its efficiency and reasoning power, was designed for and applied only to supervised, static, and fully-observable problems. Many real-world challenges, however, are characterized by dynamism, uncertainty, and partial observability. The authors are motivated to unlock HRM's potential for these more complex scenarios.
- **Enabling Incremental Solution Development:** In dynamic environments, the "correct" action is often undefined, and solutions must be constructed incrementally over a series of actions, continually adapting to environmental changes. Training HRM with RL allows it to learn from qualitative feedback (rewards) and develop solutions progressively, a capability absent in its original supervised form.
- **Leveraging Recurrent Inference for Temporal Integration:** A key strength of HRM is its recurrent inference process, which allows it to adapt computational effort. The authors hypothesize that this recurrence could be leveraged to integrate and reuse computation from previous time-steps, crucial for maintaining consistent plans and belief states in dynamic and uncertain settings. This directly contrasts with current LLM-based reasoning, which often re-computes from scratch at each step.
- **Moving Beyond Human-Defined Problem Decompositions:** The paper critiques how many existing "agentic" systems or LLM-based approaches rely on human-designed processes to decompose problems. The authors are motivated by the deep learning principle that learned models tend to outperform human-defined decompositions, aiming for a fully learned reasoning process within the agent.
- **Evaluating Efficiency and Generalization:** The research seeks to validate if a compact model like HRM can learn generalizable planning policies in dynamic, novel maze environments, rather than memorizing optimal paths for specific static configurations.

In essence, the research aims to transform HRM from a powerful static reasoner into a robust, adaptive agent capable of learning complex, goal-directed behavior in the fluid and unpredictable real-world challenges that RL is designed to tackle.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adapt the Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM) into an RL agent, termed HRM-Agent, and train it exclusively using reinforcement learning in dynamic maze environments.

**Model Architecture:**
- **HRM-Agent Core:** A variant of the original HRM model.
- **Output Head Replacement:** The HRM output head is replaced with a **Deep Q-Network (DQN)** head [Mnih et al., 2015], which outputs Q-values (expected rewards) for each possible action.
- **Action Selection:** An Epsilon-Greedy exploration strategy is used during training, with Epsilon set to zero for validation.
- **Off-Policy Training:** The model is off-policy, meaning it can learn from past trajectories.
- **Model Size:** The hidden size of the model was reduced to 64, resulting in a compact model with approximately 500,000 trainable parameters.
- **Adaptive Computation Time (ACT):** The ACT component, a feature of the original HRM, was *disabled* to simplify the analysis of the recurrent state convergence dynamics.
- **Two "Time" Dimensions:** The model operates with two temporal dimensions: "environment time" (steps within an episode where the environment changes) and "recurrent time" (internal "thinking" steps within a single environment time-step, where the environment does not change).

**Recurrent State Initialization (Key Variants):**
The research introduces and evaluates two variants concerning the initialization of the recurrent latent state `z` (comprising `z_L` for low-level and `z_H` for high-level hierarchy) across environment time-steps:
1. **"Carry Z" Variant:** At the start of an episode, `z` is initialized randomly. For all subsequent environment steps within the episode, the initial `z` is the final converged `z` from the *previous environment time-step*. Crucially, gradients are *not* propagated across environment time-steps, and `z` is detached.
2. **"Reset Z" Variant (Ablation):** The initial `z` is always a random vector at the beginning of *every* environment time-step, completely discarding information from previous steps. This serves as a baseline to assess the benefit of carrying forward `z`.

**Training Process:**
- **Reinforcement Learning Only:** The model is trained entirely from random parameter initialization using RL, without any supervised losses, labels, or target values.
- **Loss Function:** Minimizes the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the current model's predicted Q-values and bootstrapped target values `y`.
- **Target Network:** A separate target network (`θ−`) is used to generate stable target values, with its weights slowly updated from the current model (`θ`).
- **No Gradient Accumulation:** Consistent with the original HRM paper, gradients are not accumulated during the recurrent convergence process.

**Environments:**
Two dynamic and uncertain maze navigation tasks, adapted from the NetHack Learning Environment (NLE) via MiniHack and Gymnasium libraries, are used:
1. **Four-Rooms Environment (Figure 1a):**
 - A simple maze with four rooms connected by doorways.
 - **Dynamic Element:** Doors are added to doorways, with one door *always* closed. This closed door randomly changes with probability p=0.05 at each environment step, forcing re-planning.
 - **Task:** Navigate from a random start to a random goal.
 - **Rewards:** +1 for reaching the goal, -0.01 for attempting to walk into an obstacle.
 - **Purpose:** Ideal for analyzing recurrent state `z` dynamics due to its controlled changes.
2. **Dynamic, Random Maze Environment (Figure 1b, 1c):**
 - A grid with permanent walls, *N=10 random fixed walls* (constant per episode), and *M=5 random doors* (open/close independently with p=0.05 at each step).
 - **Path Guarantee:** A* path-planning ensures at least one path exists when all doors are open, but paths can be blocked when doors close.
 - **Purpose:** Forces the agent to learn a *generalized* planning policy for novel mazes, rather than memorizing a single environment. Tests greater generalization.

**Evaluation:**
The experiments evaluate:
- **Goal-Navigation Performance:** Fraction of episodes where the agent reaches the goal.
- **Path Efficiency:** Mean episode length (shorter implies more efficient paths).
- **Recurrent Convergence Dynamics:** Measured by the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the recurrent state `z_i` at step `i` and its final converged value `z_NT`. This is analyzed for both `z_L` and `z_H` under the four conditions (Carry Z/Reset Z, environment changed/not changed).
- **Recurrent State Divergence:** Measured by the MSE between `z_1` (after the first forward pass) and `z_NT` to assess consistency of the initial "plan" with the final converged plan.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research successfully demonstrates the adaptation of the Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM) to a reinforcement learning (RL) framework for dynamic environments, with key findings supporting its efficacy and the importance of recurrent state management.

#### 4.1. Validation of Concept: Successful Goal Navigation and Efficient Path Planning

- **High Goal Achievement:** The HRM-Agent model achieved approximately **99% success rate** in reaching goals in both the Four-rooms and Dynamic Random Maze environments after sufficient training (Figure 3). This high rate, especially in the Four-rooms environment where navigation through multiple doorways is required, validates that the model can learn to reason and plan in a dynamic RL setting.
- **Efficient Path Lengths:** The mean episode length, a measure of path efficiency, approached the theoretical optimum (approximately 10 steps) in both environments (Figure 4). This indicates that the agent learned to plan deliberate, efficient paths rather than relying on simple heuristics, demonstrating a form of reasoning.
- **Generalization in Random Mazes:** Success in the dynamic random maze environment, with its large number of possible configurations, suggests that the HRM-Agent developed a generalized policy for path planning in unseen mazes, rather than simply memorizing solutions for specific layouts.

#### 4.2. Analysis of Recurrent Convergence Dynamics and Computational Reuse

The analysis of the recurrent state `z` dynamics during validation episodes provided compelling evidence for computational reuse and the benefits of carrying forward the latent state.

- **Faster Convergence with "Carry Z":** In both environments (Figure 5 and 6), the "Carry Z" variant consistently showed that the recurrent state `z` (both `z_L` and `z_H`) converged faster to its final values compared to the "Reset Z" variant. This implies that the model, when allowed to carry forward its previous internal "plan," requires fewer internal recurrent steps to finalize its reasoning for the current environment step.
- **Adaptation to Environmental Changes:**
 - For the "Carry Z" variant, the initial distance between `z_1` and `z_NT` (the first recurrent step's state vs. the final converged state) was greater when the environment had changed compared to when it had not. This behavior is expected if `z` represents a plan; a change in the environment necessitates a greater adjustment to the plan, leading to a larger initial divergence from the final plan. Despite this, the initial `z` was still closer to the final `z` in "Carry Z" compared to "Reset Z."
 - The "Reset Z" variant, by contrast, showed a large initial change in `z` from its random initialization, regardless of whether the environment had changed, indicating it effectively started reasoning from scratch at each step.
- **Consistency of Plans with "Carry Z":** The divergence plot (Figure 7), specifically for the dynamic random maze environment, showed that the final latent state `z_NT` was significantly closer to the initial latent state `z_1` for the "Carry Z" variant compared to "Reset Z." This suggests that "Carry Z" promotes greater consistency in the agent's internal plans across environment time-steps, even when minor environmental changes occur. This consistency can be vital for the stable execution of long-term plans in dynamic settings.
- **Training Speed Advantage:** Although not the primary focus of the recurrent dynamics analysis, the results for the random maze environment indicated that "Carry Z" enabled faster achievement of high goal-achievement rates and efficient path lengths in 4 out of 5 training runs.

Overall, the findings strongly support the hypothesis that the HRM-Agent is constructing and maintaining a representation of its plan (a form of reasoning) within its recurrent latent state. The ability to "carry forward" this state allows the model to efficiently reuse computation, adapt existing plans, and maintain a consistent intent across dynamic environmental changes, rather than re-evaluating the problem from scratch at every moment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant proof-of-concept for extending the capabilities of the Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM) into complex, dynamic environments using reinforcement learning. Its findings have several important implications and potential impacts on the field of AI:

- **Broadening HRM's Applicability:** The most immediate impact is demonstrating that HRM, initially successful in static, supervised tasks, can be effectively trained purely with RL and applied to dynamic and uncertain environments. This unlocks a vast new range of potential applications for HRM-like architectures, from robotics and autonomous navigation to complex strategic games and interactive problem-solving where sequential decision-making and adaptation are paramount.
- **Advancing Adaptive Reasoning in RL Agents:** The HRM-Agent introduces a novel way for RL agents to handle temporal dependencies and dynamic changes. By showing that "carrying forward" the recurrent latent state (z) improves both convergence speed and plan consistency, the work highlights a critical mechanism for building more intelligent and robust agents. This approach allows agents to "think" more efficiently by building upon previous computations rather than re-evaluating from scratch, a major advantage in real-time, dynamic scenarios.
- **Addressing Limitations of Current LLMs in Dynamic Environments:** The paper implicitly offers an alternative to Large Language Models (LLMs) which, despite their impressive capabilities, struggle with efficiency and sustained reasoning in dynamic, partially observable environments due to high inference latency and the need for full information access at each reasoning step. HRM-Agent's recurrent architecture, with its ability to adapt and reuse internal states, could provide a more suitable foundation for agents operating in such contexts.
- **Foundation for More Complex RL Tasks:** By validating the HRM-Agent in dynamic maze navigation, the research lays the groundwork for tackling significantly more complex RL problems. The stated future work, including partially observable settings (agent-centered observation in NLE), restoring Adaptive Computation Time (ACT), and exploring continual/few-shot learning, points towards building agents capable of sophisticated, lifelong learning and adaptation.
- **Contribution to the Theory of "Thinking" in AI:** The analysis of recurrent state convergence dynamics provides empirical evidence for how an AI model can maintain and evolve an internal "plan" or "belief state." This contributes to our understanding of how learned reasoning processes can operate and adapt over time, without explicit human programming or decomposition of the planning process. The observation that carrying forward `z` maintains a more consistent internal state supports the idea of an agent having "continuity of intent."
- **Efficiency in Model Design:** The HRM-Agent achieves its performance with a relatively small parameter count (~500k), reinforcing the HRM's original promise of data-efficient and compact reasoning. This is significant in an era dominated by ever-larger models, suggesting that architectural innovations like recurrent inference can provide powerful reasoning capabilities without massive computational overhead.

In conclusion, this work significantly pushes the boundaries of the Hierarchical Reasoning Model, validating its potential as a powerful component in adaptive, efficient, and context-aware reinforcement learning agents for dynamic and uncertain environments. It opens exciting avenues for developing more autonomous and intelligent AI systems that can reason and adapt in ways that more closely mimic biological intelligence.
