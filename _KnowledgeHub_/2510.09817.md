# Cross-Sensor Touch Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> University of Michigan researchers developed two generative methods, Touch2Touch (T2T) and Touch-to-Depth-to-Touch (T2D2), for translating raw tactile signals between different vision-based sensors like Soft Bubble and GelSlim. These methods enable zero-shot reuse of sensor-specific algorithms in robotic manipulation tasks such as peg-in-hole insertion and marble rolling, enhancing interoperability in tactile robotics.

## Problem

- Tactile sensors exhibit significant diversity and a lack of standardization, requiring specialized algorithms and models for each unique sensor.
- Machine learning models trained on data from one tactile sensor typically fail to generalize to others due to data distribution shifts, making algorithm reuse and adaptation time-consuming and expensive.
- This fragmentation hinders the broader adoption and scalability of tactile robotics, as porting solutions across different hardware platforms is often impractical.

## Method

- **Touch2Touch (T2T):** An end-to-end conditional diffusion model that directly translates tactile signals from a source sensor to a target sensor, leveraging precisely paired training data.
- **Touch-to-Depth-to-Touch (T2D2):** A two-stage method that uses an intermediate, sensor-agnostic depth representation to facilitate signal translation, thereby removing the requirement for paired tactile data between source and target sensors.
- Both approaches aim to enable direct, 'zero-shot' transfer and reuse of existing sensor-specific perception and control pipelines across heterogeneous tactile sensors without retraining.

## Results

- T2T consistently achieved higher fidelity (e.g., PSNR 30.92, SSIM 0.93 for Soft Bubble to GelSlim transfer) and lower errors in object pose estimation compared to T2D2, especially with paired data.
- T2D2 demonstrated efficient scalability, successfully incorporating new sensors like DIGIT into the pipeline by fine-tuning the depth estimator with significantly less paired data.
- The framework enabled successful zero-shot transfer in robotic tasks: T2T improved peg-in-hole insertion success (e.g., 21/30 for pencil) and T2D2 allowed a GelSlim-trained marble rolling policy to operate on a DIGIT sensor with 15/20 success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Despite varied hardware designs, vision-based tactile sensors often capture fundamentally similar geometric and contact information, which enables learning generative mappings for signal translation.
> - T2T achieves higher fidelity when precise paired data is available, while T2D2 offers flexibility and scalability by integrating new sensors with minimal paired data (e.g., requiring approximately one-tenth the data for DIGIT integration).
> - Generative translation of raw touch signals provides a viable pathway to sensor interoperability, allowing direct reuse of established, sensor-specific perception and manipulation algorithms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Tactile sensing is recognized as a fundamental technology for dexterous manipulation in robotics. However, unlike visual sensing, tactile sensors exhibit significant diversity and a lack of standardization in their design and output modalities. Vision-based tactile sensors, such as GelSight, Soft Bubble, GelSlim, DIGIT, and DenseTact, convert physical touch into visual-like signals (2D images or 3D representations), but each sensor often necessitates the development of specialized algorithms and models. This sensor diversity presents a considerable challenge: algorithms and machine learning models trained for one sensor typically fail to generalize to others due to significant data distribution shifts, making reuse and adaptation time-consuming and expensive.

Previous research in cross-sensor tactile representation has aimed to address this challenge through various approaches. Some works, like UniTouch and AnyTouch, focus on aligning tactile embeddings with pre-trained image and text embeddings, supporting multi-sensor training with sensor-specific tokens. Others, such as Sparsh and T3, encode images from different sensors into a shared representation space using self-supervised learning. CTTP utilizes paired tactile data and contrastive learning to learn a common feature space. These methods typically operate on abstract feature representations, requiring downstream tasks to adapt to this specific representation.

In contrast, this work proposes a different approach: the explicit transfer of *raw touch signals* between sensors. This strategy aims to overcome the limitations of sensor-specific algorithms by enabling direct translation of sensory input, thereby allowing existing downstream touch processing models to be applied "zero-shot" without retraining or modification. The paper leverages recent advancements in cross-modal generation, particularly diffusion models, which have been successfully applied in domains like text-to-image and audio-to-image synthesis. While some existing works explore estimating touch from visual signals, this research distinguishes itself by focusing on generating tactile signals from other tactile signals. Furthermore, it is presented as the first work to address cross-sensor generation across a set of diverse visuo-tactile sensors, including the Soft Bubble (non-gel-based) and the GelSlim and DIGIT (gel-based), which exhibit different deformation characteristics, compliance, contact areas, and image qualities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the existing fragmentation in the field of tactile sensing for robotics. The proliferation of diverse visuo-tactile sensors, each with unique designs, sensing principles, and output characteristics, has led to a siloed development environment. Algorithms, perception models, and control strategies are typically tailored to a specific sensor, rendering them incompatible with others. This lack of interoperability hinders the broader adoption and scalability of tactile robotics, as adapting or porting solutions across different hardware platforms is often resource-intensive and impractical.

The central hypothesis guiding this work is that despite their varied hardware designs, vision-based tactile sensors often capture fundamentally similar information, such as an object’s surface geometry and the precise shape of contact. This shared geometric information suggests a potential for translating tactile signals between different sensor modalities.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of this research are:

1. **Enable Cross-Sensor Model Transfer:** To develop methodologies that allow machine learning models trained on data from one tactile sensor to be effectively used with input from a different tactile sensor, thereby reducing the need for sensor-specific algorithm development and retraining.
2. **Propose Two Generative Approaches:**
 - To introduce an end-to-end method, termed "Touch2Touch (T2T)," for cross-sensor tactile signal generation that utilizes paired data and a conditional diffusion model to produce fine-grained target signals.
 - To propose a two-stage method, termed "Touch-to-Depth-to-Touch (T2D2)," which leverages an intermediate depth representation. This approach is designed to facilitate signal translation without the need for paired tactile data between the source and target sensors, addressing data collection challenges.
3. **Evaluate Generation Quality and Utility:** To quantitatively and qualitatively assess the performance of both proposed methods using standard image generation metrics (PSNR, SSIM, FID) and a tactile-specific metric (in-hand object pose estimation accuracy).
4. **Demonstrate Practical Robotic Applications:** To validate the utility of the cross-sensor generation framework in realistic robotic manipulation scenarios, specifically through successful transfer of capabilities for peg-in-hole insertion and behavior cloning tasks across heterogeneous tactile sensors. This aims to showcase the practical benefits of enabling precise task transfer and policy reuse.

Ultimately, the research seeks to offer flexible solutions for sensor translation, adapting to varying data availability and application demands, and thereby fostering greater interoperability and reusability within tactile robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes two distinct approaches for cross-sensor tactile image generation: a one-stage end-to-end method utilizing paired data (Touch2Touch, T2T) and a two-stage method employing an intermediate depth representation that does not require paired tactile data (Touch-to-Depth-to-Touch, T2D2). Both methods are evaluated on their ability to facilitate downstream robotic tasks.

#### 3.1. Dataset Collection

Two types of datasets were collected using robotic setups:

- **Paired Multimodal Tactile Signals:** A KUKA LBR Med 14 R820 robotic arm with a WSG-50 gripper was used to collect paired tactile signals from Soft Bubble and GelSlim sensors. The setup ensured that both sensors contacted the same physical location on target objects, with compliance differences compensated by gripper adjustments. Objects with distinctive geometric features (e.g., hex key elbow) were chosen to ensure both sensors captured salient information despite their differing resolutions and contact areas (Soft Bubble covers ~16x more surface area than GelSlim but at lower resolution). Post-processing ensured semantic alignment of the collected pairs.
- **Multimodal Touch-Depth Pairs:** A separate dataset was constructed for supervised learning of tactile-to-depth and depth-to-tactile translation. A robot equipped with various vision-based tactile sensors (Soft Bubbles, GelSlims, DIGITs, dotless GelSlims) interacted with 12 indenters of known geometry (flat, curved, angled profiles). For each indenter, 60 tactile samples were collected at distinct poses within a controlled 10 mm × 10 mm translation and 45° orientation sampling grid. Each tactile observation was paired with a ground-truth depth map, generated from the robot's proprioceptive data, calibrated camera models, and geometric rendering of the known object and contact poses.

#### 3.2. One-Stage Generation (Touch2Touch - T2T)

The T2T method directly translates a tactile signal from a source sensor to a target sensor using paired training data.

- **Model Architecture:** A latent diffusion model, adapted from Stable Diffusion, is employed. It generates the target sensor's tactile signal by conditioning on the source sensor's measurement.
- **Conditioning:** A ResNet-18 (or ResNet-50 for GelSlim encoding as per Appendix A.1) encoder processes the source touch image into a 2D feature map. This feature map is then concatenated with the noise signal and fed into the denoising UNet of the diffusion model.
- **Sensor Focus:** The model was specifically applied to translate between GelSlim and Soft Bubble sensors.
- **Post-processing for Soft Bubble Generation:** When generating Soft Bubble tactile images from GelSlim input, three post-processing steps were applied to ensure geometric accuracy:
 1. The 3-channel generated signal was averaged to a single channel.
 2. The prediction was normalized to the range of depth map values ([-1, 1]) using global min/max values from the training dataset.
 3. Pixel values were shifted by renormalizing using the mean and standard deviation of generated and ground truth Soft Bubble images from the training dataset to correct for scaling and bias.
- **Baseline:** The T2T model was compared against VQ-VAE, a generative model for cross-modal translation.

#### 3.3. Two-Stage Generation (Touch-to-Depth-to-Touch - T2D2)

The T2D2 method addresses the challenge of requiring paired tactile data by using depth as an intermediate, sensor-agnostic representation. This framework consists of three sequential modules:

- **Depth Estimation Model:**
 - **Goal:** To predict a depth map ($D'_S$) and a contact mask ($M'_S$) from a source tactile image ($I_S$). This captures both the sensor's contact geometry and the object's surface geometry.
 - **Architecture:** An adapted version of Depth Anything V2, a monocular depth estimation model, is used. A dedicated decoder head is added to predict a binary contact map from intermediate high-resolution features of the depth decoder.
 - **Training:** The model is trained on real touch signals paired with ground-truth depth maps (derived from neural tactile de-rendering). The loss function combines a scale-invariant logarithmic loss for depth prediction with a binary cross-entropy loss for contact mask prediction.
- **Depth Adaptation Stage:**
 - **Goal:** To transform the estimated depth map from the source sensor's frame to an equivalent depth map ($D''_T$) and mask ($M''_T$) as if the target sensor were in contact. This accounts for differences in sensor field of view, coordinate systems, and camera poses.
 - **Process:**
 1. Contact pixels ($\Omega$) are identified using the predicted contact mask ($M'_S$).
 2. For each contact pixel, its depth value is back-projected into 3D space using the source sensor's inverse intrinsic matrix ($K_S^{-1}$).
 3. The resulting 3D point is then transformed from the source sensor frame to the target sensor frame using a rigid transformation ($T_{S \to T}$). This transformation is composed of transformations to and from a common alignment frame ($T_{S \to A}$ and $T_{A \to T}$).
 4. The transformed 3D point cloud is then used to generate the target sensor's depth map and contact mask by projecting points into the target sensor's image plane.
- **Generating Touch from Depth:**
 - **Goal:** To synthesize the target sensor's tactile signal from the adapted, sensor-specific depth map.
 - **Model:** A latent diffusion model, similar to the one used in T2T, is employed. However, this model is conditioned on the depth map rather than another tactile image.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of the proposed T2T and T2D2 methods was evaluated using a combination of visual metrics, a tactile-specific metric, and performance on downstream robotic manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Visual and Tactile Metrics

Performance was quantified using Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM), and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) for visual quality, and translation and angular errors for in-hand object pose estimation as a tactile-specific metric. The evaluation was conducted on scenarios involving "Unseen Grasps" and "Unseen Tools."

- **T2T vs. T2D2 Performance:** T2T, which relies on paired data and direct image-to-image translation, generally achieved higher fidelity. It consistently demonstrated superior PSNR, SSIM, and lower FID scores compared to T2D2, indicating generated images that were sharper, more perceptually accurate, and better aligned distributionally with ground truth. For instance, in the "Bubbles to GelSlims" transfer under "Unseen Grasps," T2T achieved a PSNR of 30.92 and SSIM of 0.93, whereas T2D2 yielded 21.69 and 0.83, respectively. This structural fidelity in T2T translated to lower translation and angular errors in the pose estimation task.
- **Impact of Translation Direction:** Translating tactile signals from GelSlims to Bubbles proved to be a more challenging task for both models than the reverse. This was evidenced by consistently higher angular and translation errors in the GelSlims to Bubbles direction (e.g., T2T: 0.92 mm / 1.87 deg for G->B vs. 0.40 mm / 1.27 deg for B->G on unseen grasps). This disparity is primarily attributed to the Soft Bubble sensor's significantly larger contact area compared to the GelSlim. Translating from a smaller to a larger sensor requires the models to effectively infer or "outpaint" tactile information beyond the spatial extent of the input, increasing ambiguity and error.
- **Integration of DIGIT with T2D2:** The T2D2 model demonstrated an increased error when translating from DIGIT to either GelSlims or Bubbles, which is consistent with the greater structural differences of the DIGIT sensor. However, the qualitative results for DIGIT translations retained coherent tactile features. A key finding was the efficiency of incorporating new sensors like DIGIT into the T2D2 pipeline: fine-tuning the depth estimator required approximately one-tenth of the paired data typically needed for an end-to-end model, highlighting T2D2's scalability for new sensors with minimal supervision.

#### 4.2. Downstream Robotic Task Results

The practical utility of cross-sensor generation was demonstrated through two robotic manipulation tasks:

- **Peg-in-Hole Insertion:** This task involved a robot equipped with GelSlim sensors performing insertion using algorithms originally designed for the Soft Bubble sensor. The T2T model predicted Soft Bubble signals from GelSlim readings, which were then used for object pose estimation via Iterative Closest Point (ICP) alignment. T2T consistently outperformed the VQ-VAE baseline across various insertion tasks with unseen tools and real objects. For example, in pencil insertion, T2T achieved a success rate of 21/30 trials compared to VQ-VAE's 7/30. This illustrates T2T's efficacy in generating accurate cross-modal tactile signals for precise manipulation.
- **Marble Rolling Policy Transfer:** A behavior cloning policy, trained exclusively on GelSlim tactile data to roll a marble to the sensor's center, was transferred to a robot equipped with a DIGIT sensor. The T2D2 model translated DIGIT inputs into GelSlim-equivalent tactile signals, enabling the original GelSlim-trained policy to operate without modification. The policy achieved a success rate of 15/20 trials using the translated DIGIT inputs, compared to 20/20 with real GelSlim signals. This experiment demonstrated that the cross-sensor generation framework supports both perception and sensor-conditioned control, facilitating zero-shot policy reuse across different tactile hardware.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic tactile sensing by addressing the challenge of sensor diversity and lack of standardization. The work demonstrates that despite significant differences in hardware design and signal modalities, vision-based tactile sensors often encode fundamentally similar geometric and contact information. This underlying consistency enables the translation of tactile signals between different sensor outputs through learned generative mappings.

The proposed T2T and T2D2 approaches offer distinct advantages, highlighting a trade-off between data requirements and signal fidelity. T2T, by leveraging precisely paired data, generates high-fidelity and structurally accurate tactile signals, leading to strong performance in both visual and tactile-specific metrics. This approach is beneficial when comprehensive paired datasets can be acquired. Conversely, T2D2 provides greater flexibility by operating with unpaired data and an intermediate depth representation. While this flexibility may introduce some degradation in fine-grained accuracy for precision-sensitive tasks (e.g., in-hand pose estimation), its ability to integrate new sensors with minimal paired data (e.g., 1/10th the data for DIGIT integration) presents a scalable solution for diversifying tactile sensing capabilities.

The primary impact of this work lies in demonstrating that generative translation between sensors can serve as a viable pathway to **sensor interoperability**. Instead of pursuing universal tactile representations or jointly training models across all sensor types, the research shows that explicit translation of raw touch signals allows for the direct reuse of existing, often highly optimized, sensor-specific perception and manipulation pipelines without any retraining. This capability has several implications:

- **Reduced Development Costs:** It can significantly lower the barrier to entry and cost associated with developing new tactile-enabled robotic systems, as algorithms and policies can be shared across heterogeneous hardware.
- **Accelerated Research:** Researchers can more easily build upon existing work, applying solutions developed for one sensor to a wider range of tactile platforms.
- **Modular Tactile Intelligence:** The framework promotes a more modular approach to tactile intelligence, where specialized sensor capabilities can be "borrowed" or shared on demand via generative translation, fostering innovation and resource efficiency.

While the study establishes the feasibility of cross-sensor tactile generation for vision-based sensors and specific manipulation contexts, it also identifies limitations. The need for precisely paired data for T2T, and the sensitivity of T2D2 to depth estimation and calibration errors, are practical considerations. Furthermore, the assumption of sufficient semantic overlap between source and target signals might not hold for drastically different contact modalities or highly complex object geometries, especially when translating from smaller to larger contact areas. Expanding this framework to other tactile modalities (e.g., resistive, magnetic) or more dynamic interactions remains an open area for future research. Nevertheless, this work presents a foundational step towards a more unified and interoperable ecosystem for tactile robotics.
