# Cross-Sensor Touch Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> University of Michigan researchers developed two generative methods, Touch2Touch (T2T) and Touch-to-Depth-to-Touch (T2D2), for translating raw tactile signals between different vision-based sensors like Soft Bubble and GelSlim. These methods enable zero-shot reuse of sensor-specific algorithms in robotic manipulation tasks such as peg-in-hole insertion and marble rolling, enhancing interoperability in tactile robotics.

## Problem

- Tactile sensors exhibit significant diversity and a lack of standardization, requiring specialized algorithms and models for each unique sensor.
- Machine learning models trained on data from one tactile sensor typically fail to generalize to others due to data distribution shifts, making algorithm reuse and adaptation time-consuming and expensive.
- This fragmentation hinders the broader adoption and scalability of tactile robotics, as porting solutions across different hardware platforms is often impractical.

## Method

- **Touch2Touch (T2T):** An end-to-end conditional diffusion model that directly translates tactile signals from a source sensor to a target sensor, leveraging precisely paired training data.
- **Touch-to-Depth-to-Touch (T2D2):** A two-stage method that uses an intermediate, sensor-agnostic depth representation to facilitate signal translation, thereby removing the requirement for paired tactile data between source and target sensors.
- Both approaches aim to enable direct, 'zero-shot' transfer and reuse of existing sensor-specific perception and control pipelines across heterogeneous tactile sensors without retraining.

## Results

- T2T consistently achieved higher fidelity (e.g., PSNR 30.92, SSIM 0.93 for Soft Bubble to GelSlim transfer) and lower errors in object pose estimation compared to T2D2, especially with paired data.
- T2D2 demonstrated efficient scalability, successfully incorporating new sensors like DIGIT into the pipeline by fine-tuning the depth estimator with significantly less paired data.
- The framework enabled successful zero-shot transfer in robotic tasks: T2T improved peg-in-hole insertion success (e.g., 21/30 for pencil) and T2D2 allowed a GelSlim-trained marble rolling policy to operate on a DIGIT sensor with 15/20 success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Despite varied hardware designs, vision-based tactile sensors often capture fundamentally similar geometric and contact information, which enables learning generative mappings for signal translation.
> - T2T achieves higher fidelity when precise paired data is available, while T2D2 offers flexibility and scalability by integrating new sensors with minimal paired data (e.g., requiring approximately one-tenth the data for DIGIT integration).
> - Generative translation of raw touch signals provides a viable pathway to sensor interoperability, allowing direct reuse of established, sensor-specific perception and manipulation algorithms.
