# Selective Expert Guidance for Effective and Diverse Exploration in Reinforcement Learning of LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> The MENTOR framework introduces selective expert guidance into Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Language Models, improving exploration by focusing expert input at critical decision points to balance effectiveness and diversity. It achieves average performance increases of 3.2% to 4.3% across various LLM backbones on reasoning tasks, while mitigating entropy collapse and enhancing reasoning diversity by 9.2% in pass@k metrics.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Language Models (LLMs) often struggles with insufficient high-quality exploration, particularly for models with fewer parameters.
- This limitation involves a lack of effectiveness in discovering correct reasoning trajectories for challenging tasks, leading to stalled policy improvements due to near-zero advantages.
- Existing expert imitation methods, while improving effectiveness, restrict exploration diversity by forcing adherence to full expert trajectories, potentially leading to entropy collapse and suboptimal convergence.

## Method

- The **MENTOR** (==Mixed-policy Expert== Navigation for Token-level Optimization of Reasoning) framework provides selective expert guidance, applying expert knowledge only at high-entropy, critical decision points within a reasoning trajectory.
- Mixed-policy Rollout generates trajectories by dynamically interpolating between the model's own policy and an expert policy, with expert influence weighted by the token-level entropy of the model's distribution.
- Mixed-policy GRPO integrates these selectively guided trajectories into the policy update via a modified GRPO objective, where mixed-policy advantages are positive-only and gradually annealed to encourage self-driven exploration.

## Results

- MENTOR consistently improved average performance over on-policy RL by **3.2%** for LLaMA3.1-**8B**, **4.3%** for Qwen2.5-**3B**, and **3.9%** for Qwen2.5-**7B** across mathematical and general reasoning benchmarks.
- The framework mitigated entropy collapse during training, maintaining greater exploration diversity and allowing the policy to converge to a broader, more robust set of optimal trajectories.
- A significant average gain of **9.2%** in pass@32 performance on AIME and AMC benchmarks demonstrated enhanced reasoning diversity, while models selectively absorbed valuable expert reasoning patterns (e.g., 'verify') rather than superficial imitation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality exploration in RLVR necessitates a balance between finding correct solutions (effectiveness) and maintaining diverse reasoning paths (diversity) to prevent premature convergence and entropy collapse.
> - Expert guidance is most impactful when strategically applied to critical decision points (high-entropy tokens) rather than enforcing full trajectory imitation, which can overconstrain the model's exploration space.
> - Dynamically adjusting expert influence based on the model's uncertainty at each token step allows for effective leveraging of expert knowledge while preserving the model's capacity for autonomous and diverse exploration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has emerged as a significant method for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs). This approach has demonstrated success in improving performance on complex tasks such as mathematics and programming, as evidenced by models like OpenAI-o1, DeepSeek-R1, and Kimi-1.5. These improvements are often attributed to the models' ability to generate detailed Chains of Thought (CoT) during problem-solving, a phenomenon sometimes referred to as test-time scaling.

However, a known limitation within the RLVR paradigm is its dependence on the inherent capabilities of the base LLM. Studies have indicated that the performance gains observed on powerful base models are not consistently reproducible when RLVR is applied to models with fewer parameters. This issue arises because effective RLVR training necessitates high-quality exploration, encompassing both the discovery of correct solutions (effectiveness) and the generation of varied reasoning paths (diversity).

Existing strategies to address this challenge typically involve imitating expert trajectories. While such imitation can improve the effectiveness of exploration by guiding the model toward successful reasoning paths and reducing unproductive exploration, it often overlooks the crucial aspect of diversity. Forcing models to adhere to fixed expert trajectories can restrict their exploration space, potentially leading to entropy collapse—a condition where the model rapidly converges to a narrow set of solutions—and ultimately trapping it in suboptimal outcomes. The problem of diversity reduction can be exacerbated by gradient imbalance, which may cause models to overfit expert patterns, particularly when these patterns differ significantly from the model's own reasoning style. Although some previous work has attempted to mitigate this through token reweighting within expert trajectories, this is considered a superficial solution as the fundamental exploration space remains constrained by the fixed expert demonstrations.

This research introduces MENTOR, a framework positioned to address the dual requirements of effectiveness and diversity in RLVR exploration. MENTOR deviates from full expert imitation by proposing that expert guidance is only necessary at critical decision points rather than throughout the entire reasoning trajectory. This selective guidance aims to allow models to leverage expert knowledge to improve solution discovery while simultaneously preserving a broader exploration space, thereby mitigating the diversity issues observed in prior approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the reasoning abilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) through Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) by improving the quality of exploration during training. The authors identify specific challenges that motivate their proposed framework.

**Detailed Problem Identification:**
The effectiveness of RLVR, particularly for LLMs with limited parameters, is hindered by insufficient high-quality exploration. This limitation manifests in two critical aspects:

1. **Effectiveness Issue:** When LLMs encounter tasks that are overly challenging relative to their base capabilities, they frequently struggle to generate any correct reasoning trajectories. This results in ineffective exploration, where few or no rewarding solutions are discovered. The consequence is that the normalized advantages used in RL update mechanisms (e.g., GRPO) tend towards zero, rendering policy improvements difficult or impossible to achieve. This can occur either because low-probability correct trajectories are missed due to a restricted sampling budget, or, more severely, because the model's inherent capabilities limit its ability to find *any* correct solutions within its exploration support set.

2. **Diversity Issue:** Even when LLMs manage to find correct solutions, these solutions often originate from a limited, high-probability region of the overall optimal trajectory set. This phenomenon leads to a rapid shrinkage of the policy's support set, resulting in what is termed "entropy collapse." While this premature concentration might offer short-term performance gains, it ultimately constrains the model's long-term performance by trapping it in suboptimal solutions and limiting its ability to generalize. Furthermore, a reduction in exploration diversity on solved problems can negatively propagate to unsolved problems, further restricting overall learning potential.

**Critique of Existing Solutions:**
Current methods designed to improve RLVR effectiveness, such as incorporating expert solutions, primarily focus on enhancing effectiveness by guiding models towards known correct paths. However, these methods typically involve imitating entire expert trajectories. The authors contend that while this reduces ineffective exploration, it imposes a fundamental limitation on exploration diversity. By forcing models to adhere to fixed expert trajectories, these approaches restrict the exploration space, accelerating entropy collapse. This issue is compounded by gradient imbalance, which can cause the model to rapidly overfit to superficial aspects of expert trajectories, particularly when the expert's reasoning patterns diverge from the model's natural tendencies. Attempts to mitigate this through token-level reweighting are described as providing only superficial relief, as the core constraint of fixed expert trajectories remains.

**Core Insight and Motivation for MENTOR:**
The authors propose that the inefficiency and lack of diversity in existing expert-guided RLVR approaches stem from a misapplication of expert knowledge. Their core insight is that expert guidance is critically valuable for steering the model at *key decision points* within a reasoning trajectory, but not necessarily for dictating every token. Tokens contribute unequally to reasoning; some high-entropy tokens represent critical forks in the reasoning path, while others are lower impact and vary stylistically. Blindly imitating entire trajectories, including low-impact tokens, can distract the model from learning essential reasoning decisions and unnecessarily restrict its exploration.

### 3. Key Objectives of MENTOR

Based on this motivation, the objectives for the MENTOR framework are:
1. To formalize the understanding that high-quality exploration in RLVR requires both effectiveness (discovering correct solutions) and diversity (avoiding entropy collapse and suboptimal convergence).
2. To develop a novel method that strategically leverages expert knowledge by providing guidance only at critical decision points, thereby enabling models to achieve both effective and diverse exploration simultaneously.
3. To demonstrate experimentally that this selective expert guidance leads to consistent performance improvements across various LLM backbones and tasks, mitigates entropy collapse, and allows models to internalize the essence of expert strategies rather than just superficial imitation.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The MENTOR (Mixed-policy Expert Navigation for Token-level Optimization of Reasoning) framework is designed to integrate selective expert guidance into Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) training. It achieves a balance between enhancing exploration effectiveness and maintaining diversity through two primary components: Mixed-policy Rollout and Mixed-policy GRPO.

#### 4.1. Mixed-policy Rollout

This component focuses on how reasoning trajectories are generated during the exploration phase. The central idea is to provide expert guidance only at points in the reasoning process where it is most impactful, specifically at critical decision points represented by high-entropy tokens.

- **Rationale:** The authors identify that tokens contribute disproportionately to reasoning trajectories. High-entropy tokens often correspond to critical decision forks, while low-entropy tokens might represent stylistic variations with minimal impact on the core reasoning. By focusing guidance on high-entropy tokens, the model can leverage expert knowledge where it is most needed without being overly constrained by less critical elements of an expert trajectory.

- **Mixed-policy Distribution (Equation 6):** At each decoding step `t`, a token-level mixed-policy distribution, `π_mix(· | q, y_<t)`, is defined. This distribution is an interpolation between the current on-policy distribution `π_θ(· | q, y_<t)` (generated by the model being trained) and an expert distribution `π_*(· | q, y_<t)` (derived from a stronger reference model). The interpolation is formulated as:
 `π_mix(· | q, y_<t) = (1 − w_t) π_θ(· | q, y_<t) + w_t π_*(· | q, y_<t)`
 The expert model `π_*` is typically a larger or domain-adapted model.

- **Interpolation Weight (`w_t`):** The degree of expert influence at each step `t` is controlled by the weight `w_t`. This weight is dynamically determined by the token-level entropy `H_t` of the on-policy distribution at that step:
 `w_t = min(1, H_t / γ_p)`
 Where `H_t = − ∑_y π_θ(y | q, y_<t) log π_θ(y | q, y_<t)`, and `γ_p` represents the p-quantile of entropies observed across tokens in the current batch. This mechanism ensures that high-entropy tokens, which indicate greater uncertainty or critical decision points for the policy model, receive stronger expert guidance. Conversely, low-entropy tokens, where the model is more confident or the decision is less critical, remain closer to the on-policy distribution.

- **Benefits of Selective Guidance:** This approach aims to achieve effectiveness by increasing the probability of discovering correct trajectories at uncertain decision points. Simultaneously, it preserves diversity by preventing the policy from collapsing to fixed expert solutions, as expert guidance is limited to a few specific positions.

- **Accelerating Mixed-policy Rollout:** To mitigate the computational cost of performing forward passes for both `π_θ` and `π_*` at every step, MENTOR employs a modified Speculative Sampling approach.
 - The policy model `π_θ` first auto-regressively generates `K` candidate tokens.
 - Concurrently, the expert model `π_*` computes its distributions for these candidates in parallel.
 - The mixed-policy distribution `π_mix` is constructed for each candidate.
 - Each candidate token `˜y_t` is then validated with an acceptance probability: `min(1, π_mix(˜y_t | q, ˜y_<t) / π_θ(˜y_t | q, ˜y_<t))`.
 - If a candidate is accepted, the process continues. If rejected, a new token is resampled from the residual distribution `(π_mix(· | q, ˜y_<t) − π_θ(· | q, ˜y_<t))_+`.
 - This method is proven to be unbiased (Appendix A.2) while substantially accelerating sampling from the mixed policy, leveraging the fact that `π_mix` deviates from `π_θ` only for a few tokens.

#### 4.2. Mixed-policy GRPO

This component describes how the trajectories generated by the mixed-policy rollout are integrated into the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm for policy updates.

- **Objective (Equation 9):** The policy model is optimized by maximizing a modified GRPO objective. This objective incorporates both on-policy and mixed-policy rollouts.

- **Trajectory Collection:** For each query, two sets of trajectories are collected:
 - `G_on = {τ}^N_1`: Trajectories sampled from the on-policy model `π_θ`.
 - `G_mix = {τ}^N_2`: Trajectories sampled from the mixed-policy `π_mix`.

- **Advantage Estimation:**
 - **On-policy Advantages (Equation 10):** For trajectories in `G_on`, the standard GRPO group-wise standardization is used to calculate advantages `ˆA_i,t(τ)`. This promotes self-improvement based on the policy's own exploration.
 - **Mixed-policy Advantages (Equation 11):** For trajectories in `G_mix`, the advantage function is designed to reward only successful exploration and ignore failures. It is defined as the positive excess of a trajectory's reward `R_i` over the mean reward of the on-policy rollouts:
 `ˆA_i,t(τ) = α · [R_i − mean({R_j}_τ_j∈G_on)]_+ / R_range`
 The `[x]_+ = max(x, 0)` ensures that only trajectories yielding rewards above the on-policy average contribute positively. `R_range` is a fixed span for normalization.
 - **Weighting Coefficient (`α`):** A coefficient `α` balances the contribution of mixed-policy samples. `α` is initialized to 1 and gradually decayed to 0 using a cosine schedule over 120 training steps. This schedule facilitates a smooth transition from expert-guided exploration in early training to more self-driven exploration as the model matures.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate MENTOR involved various LLM backbones and a range of mathematical and general reasoning benchmarks.

#### 5.1. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets and Models:**
 - **Qwen2.5 (Team, 2024):** Qwen2.5-7B-Base and Qwen2.5-3B-Base were used. The MATH dataset (Hendrycks et al.) was used for training, specifically problems with difficulty levels 3–5, with test set overlaps removed. This resulted in 8,889 training examples.
 - **LLaMA3.1 (Dubey et al., 2024):** LLaMA3.1-8B-Base was used. Due to the difficulty of the MATH dataset for this model, a simplified dataset was constructed from OpenR1-MATH-220K (Hugging Face, 2025), focusing on problems with response lengths under 4K tokens, to enable successful GRPO training.
- **Expert Models:** For Qwen2.5, OpenR1-Qwen-7B (trained on a distilled DeepSeek-R1 dataset) served as the expert. For LLaMA3.1, the expert was a further fine-tuned LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct model, using the same dataset and settings as OpenR1-Qwen-7B.
- **Evaluations:**
 - **In-domain performance:** Assessed on MATH, AIME24, AIME25, and AMC (Li et al., 2024). Avg@32 at temperature 0.6 was reported for AIME and AMC due to smaller test sets; pass@1 at temperature 0 was reported for MATH.
 - **Out-of-domain performance:** Evaluated on MMLU-Pro (Wang et al., 2024) and GPQA-diamond (Rein et al.) using pass@1 at temperature 0, to gauge generalization of reasoning ability.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Base:** The pre-trained model without fine-tuning.
 - **On-policy RL:** Standard GRPO, enhanced with token-level loss and Clip-Higher from DAPO (Yu et al., 2025).
 - **LUFFY (Yan et al., 2025):** Integrates full expert trajectories within GRPO rollouts.
 - **QuestA (Li et al., 2025):** Provides the first half of expert trajectories as hints.
- **Training Details:** Conducted using EasyR1 (Zheng et al., 2025) framework with Verl and vLLM. Hyperparameters included a rollout batch of 128, learning rate 1e-6, generation temperature 1.0, higher-clip 0.28, up to 8k tokens per sequence, 8 rollouts per prompt, no KL divergence or entropy regularization. Mini-batch size 64. MENTOR-specific parameters: `α` initialized to 1 and annealed to 0 (cosine schedule over 120 steps), 4 mixed-policy rollouts, `p` for `γ_p` set to 0.95 (95th percentile of entropies), `γ_p` initialized to 999 at the first step. Reward setting involved a 9:1 (outcome:format) ratio for Qwen2.5 models and 8:2 for LLaMA3.1-8B.

#### 5.2. Main Results

- **Consistent Performance Gains:** MENTOR consistently outperformed the on-policy RL baseline across all three tested LLM backbones (LLaMA3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen2.5-7B) on both in-domain mathematics benchmarks and out-of-domain general reasoning tasks.
 - Average performance improvements over on-policy RL were reported as 3.2% for LLaMA3.1-8B, 4.3% for Qwen2.5-3B, and 3.9% for Qwen2.5-7B.
 - For Qwen2.5-7B, MENTOR increased the average MATH score from 76.8% to 81.4%, and showed relative gains of +4.1, +7.4, and +7.1 percentage points on AIME24, AIME25, and AMC, respectively. This indicates that learned reasoning abilities generalize beyond the training domain.

- **Improved Balance in Expert Guidance:** MENTOR demonstrated a more effective trade-off between expert guidance and autonomous exploration compared to other baselines.
 - LUFFY, which uses full expert trajectories, showed limited improvements, suggesting that extensive imitation can constrain exploration and lead to overfitting of superficial patterns.
 - QuestA, providing partial expert hints, showed effectiveness dependent on model capacity. It yielded gains on stronger models (Qwen2.5-7B: +1.3 points) but showed diminished (Qwen2.5-3B: +0.8 points) or even negative effects (LLaMA3.1-8B: -1.6 points) on weaker models, as these models struggled to complete solutions without subsequent guidance.
 - MENTOR’s selective guidance enabled it to consistently outperform these methods, demonstrating a more robust approach to integrating expert knowledge.

#### 5.3. Training Dynamics

- **Entropy Dynamics:** MENTOR mitigated the rapid entropy collapse observed in on-policy RL. This indicates that MENTOR maintained greater exploration diversity throughout training. The final entropy converged to a slightly higher level than with on-policy RL, suggesting an expanded and more diverse final set of reachable optimal trajectories, which correlated with stronger ultimate performance.

- **Response Length Dynamics:** In early training, MENTOR-trained models produced longer responses, attributed to adopting expert-style reasoning forks (e.g., "verify," "wait"). As training progressed and the `α` coefficient for expert advantage decayed, response lengths gradually decreased. This suggests a shift from initial expert imitation to a more efficient, self-driven reasoning pattern, where the model selectively retains useful expert tokens while discarding redundant ones.

#### 5.4. Analysis of Reasoning Pattern

- **Selective Absorption of Expert Knowledge:** An analysis of high-frequency reasoning tokens (Figure 3, Appendix C) revealed that MENTOR models selectively absorbed valuable aspects of expert reasoning. Tokens such as "verify" and "check," which are indicative of robust reasoning, were adopted, while redundant tokens like "okay" or "wait," frequently seen in LUFFY’s output, were avoided. This suggests that MENTOR facilitates the internalization of the essence of expert strategies rather than mere surface-level imitation, leading to more efficient reasoning.
- In contrast, LUFFY showed indiscriminate imitation of expert patterns, including redundant tokens. QuestA, due to its partial hint mechanism, did not effectively adopt valuable tokens like "verify" which typically appear later in reasoning trajectories.

#### 5.5. Analysis of Reasoning Diversity

- **Pass@k Performance:** To quantify reasoning diversity, pass@k (specifically pass@32) was used. MENTOR achieved a significant average gain of 9.2% in pass@32 performance on AIME24, AIME25, and AMC benchmarks (Figure 4) compared to other baselines. On-policy RL sometimes showed stagnant or declining pass@k, indicating reduced diversity. LUFFY and QuestA improved pass@k by expanding the capability boundary through expert input but were limited by their inherent tendency towards excessive imitation. MENTOR's approach allowed for a clear enhancement of reasoning diversity.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents MENTOR, a framework that provides selective expert guidance, addressing a notable limitation in the application of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly concerning exploration quality.

The primary significance lies in the demonstration that explicitly addressing both the effectiveness and diversity of exploration during RLVR training can lead to consistent and substantial performance improvements. By proposing and validating the concept that expert guidance is most impactful at critical decision points rather than across entire reasoning trajectories, MENTOR offers a novel approach to integrate expert knowledge without stifling a model's autonomous exploration capabilities.

The potential impacts of this work include:

- **Enhanced LLM Reasoning Capabilities:** MENTOR consistently improves performance on challenging mathematical reasoning tasks and generalizes these gains to out-of-domain benchmarks. This suggests a more robust method for developing LLMs with advanced reasoning abilities.
- **Improved Training Efficiency and Stability:** By mitigating entropy collapse, a common issue in RL, MENTOR contributes to more stable and effective training processes. This could reduce the resources and iterations required to achieve high-performing LLMs.
- **Broader Applicability to Diverse Model Scales:** The framework's consistent performance across models of varying parameter counts, including those with limited capacities, indicates its potential to democratize access to advanced RLVR techniques. This could enable smaller models to achieve reasoning capabilities that were previously more accessible only to larger, more resource-intensive models.
- **Deeper Learning of Expert Strategies:** The analysis of reasoning patterns suggests that MENTOR promotes the internalization of the *essence* of expert strategies, rather than superficial imitation. This could lead to more robust, adaptable, and generalizable reasoning skills in LLMs.
- **New Directions for Expert Guidance:** The research opens avenues for further investigation into how expert guidance can be provided and leveraged more effectively, potentially extending to multimodal reasoning contexts or more dynamic, adaptive guidance mechanisms.

In summary, MENTOR contributes to the field by providing a principled method for high-quality exploration in RLVR, leading to more capable, diverse, and robust LLMs for complex problem-solving.
