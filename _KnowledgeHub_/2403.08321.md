# ManiGaussian: Dynamic Gaussian Splatting for Multi-task Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel framework, ManiGaussian, extends Gaussian Splatting to predict future scene states conditioned on robot actions, improving language-conditioned robotic manipulation. This approach enables agents to learn and leverage scene-level spatiotemporal dynamics for more robust task completion.

## Problem

- Perceptive robotic manipulation methods often struggle with comprehensive 3D scene understanding, especially in unstructured environments with occlusions or limited camera views.
- Generative methods, like NeRFs, reconstruct static 3D scenes but largely ignore spatiotemporal dynamics, failing to model how objects interact or move during manipulation.
- Robots lack an explicit understanding of scene-level dynamics, which is crucial for accurately predicting action outcomes and successfully completing complex human-specified manipulation tasks.

## Method

- Introduces a ==Dynamic Gaussian Splatting== (==DGS==) framework that models the propagation of geometric (position, rotation) and semantic features of objects in an embedding space over time.
- Develops a ==Gaussian World Model== with a ==Deformation Predictor== to infer changes in Gaussian parameters (positions, rotations) for each primitive based on current scene state and robot actions, effectively predicting future scene states. This is the core of **ManiGaussian**.
- Utilizes a comprehensive set of learning objectives including ==future scene consistency loss==, which forces the model to learn and embed scene-level spatiotemporal dynamics by comparing predicted and actual future scenes.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **44.8%** across **10** challenging RLBench tasks, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art generative method (GNFactor) by **13.1%** absolute (**41.3%** relative).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the dynamic modeling component (deformation predictor and future scene consistency loss) significantly boosted performance by **4.4%**.
- Demonstrated superior training efficiency, being **2.29x** faster than GNFactor, while also showcasing improved novel view synthesis and accurate future state prediction qualitatively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly learning scene-level spatiotemporal dynamics through action-conditioned future scene reconstruction in a Gaussian embedding space is crucial for robust robotic manipulation.
> - Gaussian Splatting, known for its efficiency in 3D representation, can be effectively extended to model dynamic, interactive scenes for predictive control in robotics.
> - Distilling high-level semantic features from pre-trained foundation models into the Gaussian representation enhances contextual understanding for complex manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "ManiGaussian" addresses a critical challenge in general intelligent robotics: enabling language-conditioned manipulation tasks in unstructured, unseen environments. This area demands robots that can not only perceive their surroundings but also understand the physical implications of their actions and the dynamic interactions between objects.

The existing research landscape for robotic manipulation policies can be broadly categorized into:

- **Perceptive Methods:** These methods extract semantic features from visual inputs (e.g., 2D images, 3D point clouds, voxels) and directly use them to predict robot actions. Examples include InstructRL and Hiveformer (2D), PolarNet and Act3D (3D point clouds), and PerAct (voxels). While making progress, they often struggle with comprehensive 3D understanding, especially in unstructured environments with occlusions, as they heavily rely on extensive multi-view camera setups or gripper-mounted cameras. They primarily focus on static scene semantics.

- **Generative Methods:** These approaches aim to capture rich 3D scene structure by reconstructing the environment, often leveraging self-supervised learning for novel view synthesis. Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and their variants (e.g., GNFactor) fall into this category. While proficient at reconstructing static scenes and generating novel views, a significant limitation is their tendency to ignore *spatiotemporal dynamics*—the physical interactions between objects during manipulation. This oversight can lead to an inability to complete human goals that require a nuanced understanding of how objects move and interact.

- **World Models:** These models aim to predict future states of an environment given current observations and actions, thereby encoding environmental dynamics. Historically, world models have been used in various domains like autonomous driving, game agents, and simpler robotic control tasks. Early world models often operated in latent spaces, which, while effective, sometimes lacked the explicit representative ability for complex tasks. More recent advancements have explored explicit representations in image or language domains (e.g., UniPi, Dynalang). The integration of world models with expressive visual representations is a crucial step towards more capable embodied agents.

- **Gaussian Splatting (GS):** A relatively new and highly efficient 3D scene representation technique, Gaussian Splatting has rapidly gained prominence for its ability to achieve high-fidelity, real-time novel view rendering, often outperforming NeRF-based methods in both quality and speed. Recent works have explored extending GS for generalization across diverse scenes (e.g., Pixelsplat), enriching semantic information (e.g., LangSplat, Feature 3DGS), and reconstructing deformable or dynamic scenes from videos (e.g., Dynamic 3D Gaussians, EndoGaussian).

"ManiGaussian" strategically positions itself at the intersection of these fields. It explicitly addresses the fundamental limitation of *generative methods* in robotic manipulation—the neglect of spatiotemporal dynamics—by integrating the efficiency of *Gaussian Splatting* with the predictive power of a *world model*. While dynamic Gaussian Splatting has been explored for reconstructing scenes from existing videos (interpolation), ManiGaussian pioneers its application to *extrapolate future states conditioned on robot actions* for interactive agents, a crucial, previously "unexplored" aspect according to the authors. By doing so, ManiGaussian offers a novel approach to learn scene-level dynamics, providing a more robust and comprehensive visual representation for complex manipulation tasks than prior art.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the ManiGaussian research is to develop autonomous robotic agents capable of performing language-conditioned manipulation tasks in highly unstructured and often unseen environments. This ambitious goal is driven by the desire for "general intelligent robots" that can seamlessly interact with the human world.

The key objectives and motivations can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging the Gap in Dynamic Scene Understanding:** The primary motivation stems from the limitations of conventional robotic manipulation methods. Perceptive methods, despite their advancements, often struggle with robust 3D scene understanding, especially when faced with occlusions or needing a comprehensive view of the workbench from limited camera inputs. Generative methods, while capable of reconstructing 3D scene structures, critically fail to model the *spatiotemporal dynamics*—the intricate physical interactions and changes that occur between objects during manipulation. This lack of dynamic understanding is a severe impediment, as successful manipulation often hinges on anticipating how objects will move or react when acted upon. The paper highlights this failure with a compelling example (Figure 1), where a state-of-the-art generative method (GNFactor) misinterprets scene dynamics, leading to task failure.

- **Enabling Accurate Action Prediction through Dynamics:** The core objective is to explicitly learn and embed scene-level spatiotemporal dynamics into the robot's visual representation. By understanding how objects propagate and interact over time, the robot can make more accurate and effective action predictions, ensuring that its actions lead to the desired human-specified goals. This goes beyond simply mimicking expert demonstrations; it requires a deeper, physical reasoning about the scene.

- **Leveraging Efficient 3D Representations:** The motivation also includes capitalizing on the recent advancements in 3D scene representations. While Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) provided a breakthrough, Gaussian Splatting (GS) offers superior efficiency, fidelity, and editability. The authors aim to adapt and extend this powerful representation to handle dynamic, interactive scenes, making it suitable for real-time robotic control. The challenge is to evolve GS from a static or video-reconstruction (interpolation) tool into a dynamic, action-conditioned prediction model.

- **Providing Informative Supervision via Future Scene Reconstruction:** A crucial objective is to establish a mechanism for the robot to learn these dynamics. This is achieved by building a "Gaussian world model" that can reconstruct future scenes based on current observations and predicted actions. By comparing these reconstructed future scenes with actual future scenes, the model gains a powerful self-supervised signal for learning the underlying environmental dynamics.

In essence, ManiGaussian seeks to overcome the "blind spot" of physical interaction understanding in existing generative robotic manipulation methods by pioneering a dynamic 3D visual representation (Dynamic Gaussian Splatting) and a predictive learning framework (Gaussian World Model) to enable more intelligent and robust robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ManiGaussian introduces a novel framework for multi-task robotic manipulation, built upon a **Dynamic Gaussian Splatting (DGS) framework** and a **Gaussian world model**. The core idea is to explicitly learn scene-level spatiotemporal dynamics via future scene reconstruction in a Gaussian embedding space.

#### 3.1. Overall Pipeline

The ManiGaussian pipeline takes single-view RGB-D images and human language instructions as input.
1. **Preprocessing:** Visual input from RGB-D cameras is first transformed into a volumetric representation through lifting and voxelization.
2. **Dynamic Gaussian Splatting Framework:** This component models the propagation of diverse semantic features within the Gaussian embedding space over time.
 - A **Gaussian Regressor (g_ϕ)** infers the current Gaussian mixture distribution ($\theta^{(t)}$) of geometric and semantic features in the scene from the volumetric representation. Each Gaussian primitive $i$ is parameterized by $\theta^{(t)}_i = (\mu^{(t)}_i, c^{(t)}_i, r^{(t)}_i, s^{(t)}_i, \sigma^{(t)}_i, f^{(t)}_i)$, representing position, color, rotation, scale, opacity, and high-level semantic features, respectively.
 - Crucially, for rigid objects in manipulation, color ($c$), scale ($s$), opacity ($\sigma$), and semantic features ($f$) are considered time-independent. However, **positions ($\mu$) and rotations ($r$) are dynamic**, changing over time due to physical interactions. These changes are formulated as $\mu^{(t+1)}_i = \mu^{(t)}_i + \Delta\mu^{(t)}_i$ and $r^{(t+1)}_i = r^{(t)}_i + \Delta r^{(t)}_i$.
 - Semantic features ($f^{(t)}_i$) are distilled from a pre-trained visual encoder like Stable Diffusion, enriching the Gaussian representation with high-level contextual information.
3. **Gaussian World Model:** This module parameterizes the distributions within the DGS framework and provides crucial supervision by reconstructing future scenes.
 - A **Representation Network (q_ϕ)** learns high-level visual features ($v^{(t)}$) from the current visual observation ($o^{(t)}$).
 - The **Gaussian Regressor (g_ϕ)** then predicts the current Gaussian parameters ($\theta^{(t)}$) from $v^{(t)}$.
 - A **Deformation Predictor (p_ϕ)** is central to learning dynamics. It takes the current Gaussian parameters ($\theta^{(t)}$) and the robot action ($a^{(t)}$) as input to infer the changes in Gaussian positions ($\Delta\mu^{(t)}_i$) and rotations ($\Delta r^{(t)}_i$) for each Gaussian primitive. This prediction results in the future Gaussian parameters ($\theta^{(t+1)}$).
 - Finally, a **Gaussian Renderer (R)** projects these predicted future Gaussian parameters ($\theta^{(t+1)}$) into a specific view to reconstruct the future scene ($o^{(t+1)}$).
4. **Action Prediction:** The learned Gaussian parameters, which now encode geometric, semantic, and dynamic information, are fed into a multi-modal transformer, PerceiverIO, along with human language instructions. PerceiverIO then predicts the optimal robot actions, including target translation, rotation, gripper openness, and collision avoidance.

#### 3.2. Learning Objectives

ManiGaussian is trained with a combination of loss functions designed to ensure accurate scene representation, semantic understanding, action prediction, and—most importantly—dynamic modeling:

1. **Current Scene Consistency Loss (L_Geo):** An L2 loss ensures that the scene rendered from the current Gaussian parameters ($\hat{C}^{(t)}$) is consistent with the groundtruth current observation ($C^{(t)}$). This loss primarily trains the Gaussian regressor.
2. **Semantic Feature Consistency Loss (L_Sem):** A cosine distance loss encourages the semantic features projected from the Gaussian parameters ($\hat{F}^{(t)}$) to align with feature maps extracted by powerful pre-trained foundation models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) ($F^{(t)}$). This distills rich high-level semantic knowledge into the Gaussian representation.
3. **Action Prediction Loss (L_Act):** A cross-entropy loss is used for the action decoder (PerceiverIO) to predict the correct robot actions (translation, rotation, openness, collision avoidance) based on expert demonstrations.
4. **Future Scene Consistency Loss (L_Dyna):** This is a crucial L2 loss that enforces consistency between the predicted future scene ($\hat{C}^{(t+1)}(a^{(t)}, o^{(t)})$ from the Gaussian world model and the actual realistic future scene ($C^{(t+1)}$). By requiring the model to accurately predict how the scene will change given an action, this loss forces the DGS framework to learn and embed scene-level spatiotemporal dynamics.

The overall training objective is a weighted sum of these losses: $L = L_{Act} + \lambda_{Geo} L_{Geo} + \lambda_{Sem} L_{Sem} + \lambda_{Dyna} L_{Dyna}$. A warm-up phase is used where the deformation predictor is frozen initially to allow for stable learning of the representation and Gaussian regressor before jointly training the entire Gaussian world model and action decoder.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of ManiGaussian was rigorously evaluated on 10 challenging language-conditioned manipulation tasks from the RLBench dataset, comprising 166 variations in object properties and scene arrangements. The experiments used a single front camera for RGB-D input and compared ManiGaussian against state-of-the-art perceptive (PerAct, PerAct with 4 cameras) and generative (GNFactor) methods.

#### 4.1. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods

- **Superior Performance:** ManiGaussian achieved an average success rate of **44.8%** across all 10 tasks, significantly outperforming all baseline methods.
- **Significant Improvement:** It outperformed the previous state-of-the-art generative method, GNFactor (which achieved 31.7%), by a substantial **13.1% absolute margin**, representing a **41.3% relative improvement**.
- **Task-Specific Dominance:** ManiGaussian consistently ranked as the best or second-best method across individual tasks. Notably, it showed dramatic improvements in tasks requiring complex object interaction and understanding of dynamics, such as "sweep to dustpan" (64% vs. GNFactor's 28%) and "drag stick" (92% vs. GNFactor's 37.3%). This highlights its ability to comprehend and leverage scene dynamics.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

A detailed ablation study confirmed the importance of each proposed component in ManiGaussian:
- **Geometric Representation (Gaussian Regressor):** Introducing the Gaussian regressor to predict Gaussian parameters (encoding geometric features) alone boosted performance by 15.6% over a vanilla baseline. This demonstrates the effectiveness of Gaussian Splatting for spatial information modeling, particularly in tasks requiring geometric reasoning (e.g., Occlusion, Tools, Screw).
- **Semantic Features:** Adding high-level semantic features (distilled from pre-trained foundation models) and their corresponding consistency loss further improved the average success rate by 2.4%. This indicates the value of rich semantic information for robotic manipulation.
- **Dynamic Modeling (Deformation Predictor & Future Scene Consistency Loss):** The inclusion of the deformation predictor and the future scene consistency loss, which are responsible for learning spatiotemporal dynamics, resulted in a significant 4.4% performance boost. This component proved crucial for improving task completion in 4 out of 6 task categories, especially "long-horizon" tasks, validating the hypothesis that scene-level dynamics are vital.
- **Cumulative Effect:** Combining all proposed techniques (geometric, semantic, and dynamic understanding) led to the highest success rate of 44.8%, unequivocally demonstrating the necessity and synergistic effect of each component.

#### 4.3. Efficiency

- **Faster Training:** Beyond superior performance, ManiGaussian exhibited better training efficiency. It achieved 1.18 times better performance and trained 2.29 times faster than GNFactor, showcasing the efficiency benefits of an explicit Gaussian scene representation compared to implicit methods like NeRF.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis

- **Improved Trajectory Generation:** Case studies (Figure 4) illustrated that ManiGaussian successfully generates action sequences that correctly complete human instructions, even when previous methods like GNFactor fail due to a misunderstanding of object interaction or semantics (e.g., "Slide the block to yellow target," "Turn left tap").
- **Accurate Novel View Synthesis and Future Prediction:** ManiGaussian demonstrated superior detail in current scene reconstruction and, crucially, accurately predicted future states (e.g., gripper and cube positions) based on its understanding of physical interactions (Figure 5). This visually confirms that ManiGaussian effectively learns intricate scene-level dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ManiGaussian makes a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by effectively addressing a long-standing challenge: integrating scene-level spatiotemporal dynamics into robust 3D visual representations for language-conditioned tasks. Its impact and significance can be understood through several key aspects:

- **Novelty in Dynamic 3D Representation for Robotics:** The paper introduces the first formulation of a **dynamic Gaussian Splatting framework** specifically designed for robotic manipulation. While Gaussian Splatting has revolutionized novel view synthesis, and dynamic variants exist for video reconstruction, ManiGaussian is groundbreaking in extending it to *predict future scene states conditioned on robot actions*. This shift from interpolation (reconstructing observed dynamics) to action-conditioned extrapolation (predicting unseen dynamics) is fundamental for interactive agents.
- **Enhanced Physical Reasoning:** By explicitly modeling the propagation of object positions and rotations in the Gaussian embedding space and using a **Gaussian world model** for future scene reconstruction, ManiGaussian endows robotic agents with a sophisticated understanding of physical interactions. This capability for "physical reasoning" is crucial for completing complex manipulation tasks that require more than just static scene understanding or trajectory imitation.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance and Generalizability:** The substantial performance gains (13.1% absolute improvement over previous SOTA) on a diverse and challenging multi-task benchmark like RLBench highlight the practical effectiveness and generalizability of ManiGaussian. This demonstrates its potential to enable robots to operate more reliably in varied and unstructured environments.
- **Efficiency Advantage:** The explicit nature of Gaussian Splatting, as opposed to implicit representations like NeRFs, translates into significant computational benefits. ManiGaussian's faster training time (2.29x faster than GNFactor) and superior performance underscore its efficiency, which is vital for real-world robotic deployment where computational resources and training data might be limited.
- **Advancing Embodied AI:** ManiGaussian's success in learning how scenes change in response to actions pushes the boundaries of embodied AI. It moves robots closer to becoming truly intelligent agents that can not only perceive and understand human instructions but also reason about the physical consequences of their actions to achieve goals. This capability is foundational for future developments in areas like autonomous assembly, household robotics, and human-robot collaboration.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work provides a robust framework that can serve as a springboard for several future research directions. This includes exploring more complex interactions (e.g., deformable objects, liquids), integrating richer forms of semantic understanding, scaling to even larger and more varied datasets, and transitioning from simulation to real-world deployment. The explicit representation of dynamics could also facilitate model-predictive control or other planning algorithms that benefit from accurate forward models.

**Limitations:** The authors acknowledge a limitation: the current Gaussian Splatting framework necessitates **multiple view supervision with camera calibration**. While feasible in controlled environments or simulations (like RLBench, which uses 20 cameras for supervision), this requirement can pose a practical challenge for deployment in highly unstructured real-world settings where precise multi-camera setups or calibration might be difficult or costly. Future work might explore reducing this dependency to single-view inputs or developing more robust uncalibrated multi-view learning.

In conclusion, ManiGaussian represents a significant step forward in robotic manipulation, offering a powerful, efficient, and dynamic 3D scene understanding framework that enables robots to comprehend physical interactions and successfully execute language-conditioned tasks with unprecedented accuracy. Its contributions are poised to accelerate the development of more capable and general-purpose intelligent robots.
