# EvoScene-VLA: Evolving Scene Beliefs Inside the Action Decoder for Chunked Robot Control

> [!summary] Summary
> EvoScene-VLA enhances chunked robot control by integrating a mechanism within the action decoder to evolve scene beliefs based on the robot's own actions and correct them with new observations. This approach yielded improved success rates of 88.5% in randomized simulations and 42.0% in real-robot cleaning tasks, showing gains of +2.4% and +4.7% respectively over baselines.

## Problem

- Current chunked Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies often base multi-step action predictions solely on initial visual observations, leading to "stale targets" when robot actions change the scene mid-chunk.
- Robot policies typically lack a persistent, action-informed record of how their actions transform the environment, forcing continuous re-inference from potentially partial or noisy visual evidence.
- This limitation reduces robustness and can cause execution failures in dynamic manipulation environments where the robot's interactions modify the scene.

## Method

- **EvoScene-VLA** extends a VLA framework by introducing a recurrent latent scene interface, using "observation slots" for current image information and "prior slots" to carry an action-updated scene state from previous chunks.
- The action decoder performs joint denoising, simultaneously predicting the next action chunk and a corresponding "scene chunk," which then becomes the prior for the subsequent control call.
- During training, a two-level Geometric Anchor (local depth reconstruction and global 3D Foundation Model distillation) grounds scene slots in 3D structure, and a Scene Predictor generates future scene-token targets, both of which are discarded at inference.

## Results

- In RoboTwin simulations, EvoScene-VLA achieved an average success rate of **89.1%** (+**1.9%** over baseline) in "Clean" settings and **88.5%** (+**2.4%** over baseline) in "Rand" (randomized initial conditions) settings.
- On real-robot cleaning tasks using the Galaxea R1-Lite platform, EvoScene-VLA improved the average success rate to **42.0%**, representing a +**4.7%** gain over the LingBot-VLA* baseline.
- Ablation studies confirmed that all proposed components, including the local depth anchor, global 3D foundation model anchor, Scene Predictor, and the recurrent prior, cumulatively contributed to the observed performance improvements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Maintaining an action-updated and persistent scene representation across control chunks is crucial for robust robot control in dynamic environments where actions induce scene changes.
> - The action expert itself can be trained through co-denoising to produce the action-updated scene prior at inference time, integrating scene evolution directly into the control loop without needing separate online world models.
> - Grounding abstract scene latent tokens in concrete 3D structure through a two-level geometric anchoring mechanism ensures the recurrent scene state encodes meaningful geometric information relevant to manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot control has increasingly leveraged Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, particularly in "chunked" control paradigms where multi-step robot actions are predicted based on visual observations and language instructions. Current chunked VLA policies typically condition their decisions primarily on the immediate visual observation. However, robot actions inherently alter the scene through contact, occlusion, and object manipulation. These changes can occur within an action chunk, meaning that subsequent steps in the chunk might be based on a visual context that no longer accurately reflects the scene. While fresh observations arrive between chunks, policies often lack a compact, action-informed record of how their previous actions transformed the environment, necessitating re-inference of scene changes from potentially partial, noisy, or occluded visual evidence.

Existing approaches in this landscape address certain aspects but not the specific challenge of maintaining an *action-updated* scene prior across control calls:
- **Spatial VLAs** (e.g., SpatialVLA, QDepth-VLA, 3DS-VLA) focus on enhancing geometric reasoning *within* a single image using depth supervision, 3D encoding, or multi-view features. These methods improve current-frame geometry but do not update the scene representation based on actions once they have been executed.
- **Temporal VLAs** (e.g., MemoryVLA, HAMLET, TraceVLA) attempt to retain historical observations through memory banks or recurrent states. While these approaches leverage past visual data, they do not inherently specify how recent or planned *actions* should explicitly update the scene state.
- **Action-conditioned prediction methods** (e.g., FLARE, UP-VLA) anticipate future representations or states conditioned on actions. However, these predictions are typically consumed internally for the current decision and then discarded, failing to establish a persistent, self-correcting scene state that carries over to subsequent control calls.

EvoScene-VLA addresses a perceived gap by introducing a mechanism for maintaining an action-updated scene representation that persists across control chunks, updates based on the policy's own actions, and corrects against new visual observations. This design is posited as a missing component for robust chunked robot control, particularly in scenarios where the robot's actions significantly alter the environment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for EvoScene-VLA stems from the limitations of current chunked Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, which are observed to frequently fail in dynamic environments where robot actions change the scene.
The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing Action-Observation Mismatch:** Current chunked VLA policies predominantly base their multi-step action predictions solely on the initial visual observation for that chunk. This creates a mismatch when robot actions, such as wiping, closing a drawer, or lifting an object, modify the scene's geometry, object states, or occlusions *before* the next visual observation is available. The policy might commit to a "stale target" based on outdated visual information, leading to execution failures, as illustrated by examples like a gripper retracting before reaching an object it has moved.

- **Necessity of a Persistent Scene State:** The paper argues for a persistent scene representation that is not re-inferred from scratch with each new observation. Without such persistence, the policy lacks cross-chunk context, forcing it to continuously re-deduce the state of the world, including changes it has itself caused.

- **Incorporating Action-Driven Updates:** A crucial aspect identified is the need for the scene representation to be explicitly updated by the actions the robot generates. Since robot actions are the direct cause of scene changes, the policy itself should be equipped to predict how these actions transform the environment. This ensures that the scene prior passed to subsequent control calls accurately reflects the post-action state.

- **Enabling Observation-Based Correction:** While action-driven updates are important, prediction errors can accumulate. Therefore, the scene representation must be able to correct itself against fresh visual evidence from new observations. This self-correction mechanism prevents compounding errors and maintains the accuracy of the persistent scene prior.

- **Improving Robustness in Dynamic Environments:** The overall objective is to enhance the robustness and success rates of VLA policies, especially in settings with randomized initial conditions or real-world tasks where scene states evolve significantly due to robot interaction. Tasks such as cleaning surfaces, stacking objects, or interacting with movable parts like microwave doors are highlighted as scenarios where an action-updated scene prior would offer a substantial advantage.

In summary, the authors aim to integrate a mechanism within the VLA architecture—specifically, within the action decoder—that allows the robot to maintain, update, and correct an internal, geometry-aware "scene belief" across control chunks, thereby bridging the gap between planned actions and the resulting scene dynamics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EvoScene-VLA is designed as an extension of the LingBot-VLA framework, introducing a recurrent latent scene interface to address the identified limitations of chunked VLA policies. Its methodology can be broken down into several interconnected components:

#### 3.1. Overview

EvoScene-VLA augments the Vision-Language Model (VLM) prefix with two groups of scene slots: "observation slots" that extract information from the current image, and "prior slots" that carry the action-updated scene state from the previous control chunk. During each VLM call, the model integrates information from the current observation with the inherited prior. The action decoder then performs a joint denoising process, outputting both the next action chunk and a corresponding "scene chunk." This denoised scene chunk then becomes the prior for the subsequent control call, closing the recurrent loop. Two training-only modules, Geometric Anchor and Scene Predictor, are utilized during training to supervise this process, but are discarded at inference time.

#### 3.2. Recurrent Scene Prefix

The VLM prefix is augmented with two types of scene slots:
- `s_obs^(v)`: Per-view observation slots (N slots, D dimension) designed to gather geometric evidence from each of V camera views.
- `s_bar_t`: Prior slots (N slots, D dimension) that inherit the action-updated scene state from the preceding chunk.
The full prefix consumed by the VLM is `[x_t, s_obs^(1:V), s_bar_t, l]`, where `x_t` is the multi-view image and `l` is the language instruction. For the first chunk of an episode, `s_bar_t` is initialized from learnable scene embeddings; for subsequent chunks, it receives the denoised scene token from the previous step.

An asymmetric attention mask governs information flow within this prefix:
- Image and language tokens do not attend to scene slots, preserving their pretrained pathways.
- Observation slots attend to their respective view's image tokens and to other slots within their own observation group.
- Prior slots attend to all observation slots and themselves, but not to image or language tokens.
This design ensures that current visual evidence reaches the prior slots exclusively via the observation slots, creating a bottleneck that isolates the scene state from directly influencing image or language tokens while allowing it to be corrected by fresh visual data. The VLM output at the prior-slot positions, `s_p`, represents the corrected scene state for the current chunk.

#### 3.3. Two-Level Geometric Anchor (Training-Only)

This module ensures that the scene slots encode meaningful 3D structure rather than generic image features. It comprises two complementary branches:

- **Local Anchor: Cross-View Masked Depth Reconstruction:**
 - This branch grounds each observation slot `s_obs^(v)` in per-view geometry while encouraging cross-view integration.
 - For a target view, its VLM image tokens are masked and replaced with a learned embedding `m`.
 - A lightweight cross-attention head `g_depth` attempts to recover the masked view's depth representation from the remaining unmasked views and the aggregated scene representation `s_p`.
 - The prediction `f_hat_d_t,i` is supervised using an L1 loss (`L_geo`) against features from a frozen Monocular Depth Teacher (MDT) applied to the unmasked target image `f_d_t,i`. This forces `g_depth` to rely on aggregated cross-view evidence.

- **Global Anchor: 3D Foundation Model Decoding:**
 - This branch grounds the cross-view representation `s_p` in metric 3D by distilling knowledge from a frozen multi-view 3D Foundation Model (3DFM).
 - A view-conditioned decoder `g_3D` takes learnable queries `q_dec` and uses `s_p` as keys and values. A linear projector `W_proj` maps the decoder output to the 3DFM feature space.
 - The projected features `P_t` are supervised with an L1 loss (`L_rep`) to match the frozen 3DFM's features `Z_t` for the same multi-view input. This provides dense, token-level supervision, preserving direction and magnitude of the target representation.

#### 3.4. Scene Predictor (Training-Only)

The Scene Predictor generates future scene-token targets that the action expert subsequently learns to distill.
- It is a causal Transformer conditioned on the current robot state `r_t`, the current scene representation `s_p`, and the action sequence `a_t:t+H`.
- It predicts a sequence of absolute future scene latents (`s_hat_t+k_1:t+k_K`) at sparse key-frame steps (`k_1`...`k_K`) within the action chunk.
- Each key-frame query group attends causally to inputs up to its target step.
- The predicted future latents (`s_hat_t+k_i`) are then decoded using the same `g_3D` and `W_proj` from the Global Anchor, and supervised with an L1 loss (`L_pred`) against the frozen 3DFM features `Z_t+k_i` on the corresponding future multi-view frames.

#### 3.5. Joint Action–Scene Denoising

The action expert is trained to simultaneously denoise actions and future scene latents using a single flow-matching vector field.
- **During Training:**
 - Future-scene targets `z_0` are derived from the Scene Predictor's outputs (`s_hat`).
 - A shared flow-matching time `tau` is sampled, and Gaussian noise is added to both the ground-truth action chunk (`a_t:t+H`) and `z_0` to create interpolated states (`a_tau` and `z_tau`).
 - The action expert predicts velocities (`v_a_theta`, `v_s_theta`) for action and scene blocks.
 - Losses `L_actFM` and `L_sceneFM` are applied to match these predicted velocities to their respective targets. This process distills the future scene representations into the action expert.
- **During Inference:**
 - The action expert, having been trained with `L_sceneFM`, acts as the inference-time scene updater.
 - A single VLM forward pass is followed by an Euler-step denoising pass, which produces both the action chunk (`a_hat_t:t+H`) and the corresponding denoised scene tokens (`s_hat_t+k_1:t+k_K`).
 - The robot executes the action chunk. The scene token at the final key-frame offset `k_K` from this denoised output becomes the prior `s_bar_t+1` for the next chunk, effectively closing the recurrent loop without needing the separate training-only modules (Geometric Anchor or Scene Predictor).

#### 3.6. Training Objective

The full training objective combines the standard action flow-matching loss with the four scene-related losses:
`L = L_actFM + λ1*L_geo + λ2*L_rep + λ3*L_pred + λ4*L_sceneFM`.
`L_geo` and `L_rep` provide geometric grounding for current scene representations. `L_pred` trains future representations in the same coordinate system. `L_sceneFM` transfers these representations into the action expert. The model is trained end-to-end in a single stage.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of EvoScene-VLA was conducted across simulated and real-robot environments, demonstrating its performance and the efficacy of its components.

#### 4.1. RoboTwin Simulation (31 Tasks)

Experiments on the RoboTwin benchmark involved 31 diverse language-conditioned manipulation tasks. Two evaluation settings were used: "Clean," where initial object positions matched the training distribution, and "Rand," where initial positions and orientations were randomized.

- **Average Success Rates:**
 - Under the **Clean** evaluation setting, EvoScene-VLA achieved an average success rate of **89.1%**, marking a **+1.9%** improvement over the depth-augmented LingBot-VLA (LingBot-VLA*) baseline, which scored 87.2%.
 - Under the **Rand** (randomized initial conditions) evaluation, EvoScene-VLA achieved an average success rate of **88.5%**, an improvement of **+2.4%** over LingBot-VLA*'s 86.1%. The larger gain in randomized conditions suggests that the scene prior mechanism is robust to variations in initial pose and layout, which can make single-observation perception more challenging and prone to accumulating errors across chunks.

- **Qualitative Analysis (Figure 1):**
 - Qualitative examination of rollouts for tasks like "Open Microwave," "Stack Three Blocks," and "Put Object Cabinet" indicated that baseline chunked policies often failed mid-chunk due to "stale targets." This occurred because the initial observation for the chunk did not account for scene changes caused by the robot's own actions.
 - EvoScene-VLA, by contrast, leveraged its action-updated scene prior, allowing it to condition on intermediate scene states and complete the motions successfully. For example, in "Open Microwave," EvoScene-VLA continued the gripper motion through to completion, unlike the baseline that stalled.

- **Trajectory Quality (Figure 3, 5):**
 - 3D end-effector trajectory plots showed that EvoScene-VLA produced smoother paths compared to LingBot-VLA. This is attributed to the co-denoising process, which grounds each predicted action in a consistent scene context, reducing the need for abrupt corrections seen in action-only chunked baselines.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study (RoboTwin-5Task Subset)

An additive ablation study on a 5-task subset of RoboTwin (RoboTwin-5Task) progressively demonstrated the contribution of each proposed component:

- **LingBot-VLA* Baseline:** Achieved 87.8% (Clean) and 84.6% (Rand).
- **+ `L_pred` & `L_rep` (Global Anchor + Scene Predictor):** Adding the global 3DFM anchor and the Scene Predictor jointly improved performance to 89.3% (Clean) and 86.2% (Rand). This indicates the benefit of grounding the scene representation in 3D and providing future scene targets.
- **+ `L_geo` (Local Depth Anchor):** Further inclusion of the local depth anchor, which grounds observation slots in per-view geometry, raised success rates to 90.1% (Clean) and 86.5% (Rand). This complements the global anchor by providing local geometric specificity.
- **+ Prior Info at Inference (Recurrent Prior):** The final step, propagating the recurrent prior across chunks at inference (rather than reinitializing), led to the highest performance: 90.8% (Clean) and 87.8% (Rand). This isolates and confirms the direct contribution of the cross-chunk recurrence mechanism.

The ablations collectively indicate that all introduced components contribute cumulatively to the overall performance gains.

#### 4.3. Real-Robot Evaluation (Galaxea R1-Lite Platform)

EvoScene-VLA was evaluated on a physical dual-arm robot platform (Galaxea R1-Lite) across three indoor-cleaning tasks (mirror, sink, cutting-board cleaning). These tasks involve evolving surface states as the robot interacts with them.

- **Average Success Rates:**
 - EvoScene-VLA achieved an average success rate of **42.0%**, representing an improvement from LingBot-VLA*'s 37.3%. This **+4.7%** gain demonstrates consistent benefits in real-world deployment.
 - Performance varied across tasks, with EvoScene-VLA showing substantial gains on "Cutting-board" (37% to 46%) and "Sink" (49% to 51%).
The real-robot results further support the hypothesis that the method is particularly beneficial in scenarios where robot actions dynamically alter the scene state, requiring continuous updates to the robot's internal scene belief.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on EvoScene-VLA contributes to the field of robot learning and control through its novel approach to managing scene state in chunked Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Novel Mechanism for Action-Updated Scene Priors:** The work introduces a method for a robot policy to maintain an action-updated, persistent, and self-correcting scene representation (prior) across chunk boundaries. This is a direct response to the limitations of policies that re-infer scene context from each observation or discard action-conditioned predictions after immediate use.
- **Integration within the Action Decoder:** EvoScene-VLA demonstrates that the action expert itself can be trained to produce this scene prior at inference time through joint action-scene co-denoising, eliminating the need for separate online world models or auxiliary prediction modules during deployment. This integrates scene evolution directly into the control loop.
- **Two-Level Geometric Grounding:** The introduction of a two-level Geometric Anchor (local depth and global 3D foundation model) provides a robust mechanism for grounding abstract scene latent tokens in concrete 3D structure. This ensures the recurrent scene state encodes meaningful geometric information relevant to manipulation.
- **Empirical Validation Across Settings:** The consistent performance improvements observed across both highly controlled simulated environments (RoboTwin, especially under randomized conditions) and real-world physical robot deployments (Galaxea R1-Lite cleaning tasks) indicate the practical relevance and generalizability of the proposed method. The larger gains in randomized and dynamic real-world settings specifically support the core hypothesis about managing evolving scene states.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Enhanced Robustness and Reliability:** By allowing robot policies to maintain an updated belief of the scene following their own actions, EvoScene-VLA has the potential to significantly improve the robustness and reliability of robot manipulation, particularly for tasks involving dynamic object interaction, occlusion, and environmental changes (e.g., cleaning, assembly, cooking). Robots may become less prone to errors caused by stale perceptions.
- **More Fluid and Coherent Motion:** The observed smoother end-effector trajectories suggest that policies with action-updated scene priors can plan more consistently, reducing the need for abrupt corrections that stem from sudden shifts in perceived scene state. This could lead to more natural and efficient robot movements.
- **Simplified Deployment Architectures:** The design choice to distill the scene prediction capabilities directly into the action decoder means that the deployed robot controller does not require additional complex online modules for world modeling or scene prediction. This could simplify computational requirements and latency during real-time operation.
- **Foundation for Advanced Capabilities:** The concept of an internal, evolvable scene belief could serve as a foundation for more advanced robot cognitive capabilities. For example, future work might explore using the mismatch between observation slots and prior slots as an uncertainty signal for replanning or adaptive chunk execution, enabling more intelligent and responsive behavior.
- **Broader Applicability:** While demonstrated in manipulation, the principle of an action-updated scene prior could be applicable to other embodied AI domains where agents interact with and alter their environments, such as navigation or human-robot interaction.
