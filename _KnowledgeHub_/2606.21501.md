# UniviewVLA: A Unified Multiview Vision-Language-Action Model with World Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> UniviewVLA, a unified multiview Vision-Language-Action model, infers future scene evolution from multiple perspectives using only standard two-camera inputs, improving robot manipulation success rates in occluded tasks on both simulation and real-world platforms by up to 33.3 percentage points compared to two-camera baselines while maintaining efficiency.

## Problem

- Robot manipulation tasks frequently fail when critical visual cues are occluded from standard camera views, even if the underlying task logic is understood.
- Existing multiview systems require strict camera parity (number, placement, calibration) between training and inference, making them brittle and challenging to scale.
- Explicit 3D reconstruction methods are computationally intensive, provide only static scene representations, and do not inherently predict future scene evolution.

## Method

- **UniviewVLA** trains a generative world model to predict future visual states from multiple auxiliary viewpoints using only standard agent-view and wrist-view observations.
- A ==motion-informative token compression== method reduces the token count of each generated auxiliary view from 625 to 16, significantly cutting inference latency from 6-7 seconds to 0.2-0.3 seconds.
- A training-free dynamic view selection strategy, based on minimizing action entropy, identifies and utilizes the most action-informative generated viewpoint at each stage of task execution.

## Results

- UniviewVLA achieved an average chain length of **4.60** on CALVIN ABCD→D and an average success rate of **95.8%** on LIBERO, surpassing state-of-the-art methods and physical three-camera setups.
- On six customized simulation occlusion tasks, dynamic view selection improved the average success rate from **40.0%** (two-camera baseline) to **73.3%**, outperforming a fixed generated view or a physical third camera.
- In real-robot deployment, UniviewVLA using two cameras matched or slightly exceeded the performance of a three-camera setup on occlusion tasks (e.g., **53.3%** success on Oreo-to-Plate with UniviewVLA vs. **40.0%** with a physical third camera).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - World models can effectively synthesize multiview and future scene information from limited physical camera observations, providing benefits comparable to or exceeding additional physical cameras without their deployment costs.
> - Drastically compacting generated visual information to only motion-informative tokens enables efficient real-time inference, making auxiliary view generation practical for closed-loop control.
> - Dynamically selecting the most confident (lowest action entropy) generated viewpoint during inference allows for adaptive perception, mitigating stage-dependent occlusions without additional labels or training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robot manipulation systems frequently rely on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which translate visual observations and linguistic instructions into robotic movements. A fundamental limitation of these models, however, is their dependence on the visual information available through deployed cameras. In many manipulation scenarios, critical visual cues such as gripper-object relationships, precise object poses, or contact regions can be partially or entirely occluded from standard camera views, leading to task failures even if the underlying task logic is understood by the model.

Existing approaches to address this observability bottleneck fall into two main categories:
1. **Deployment of Additional Physical Cameras:** Methods employing multiple cameras have demonstrated improved manipulation performance by providing richer visual coverage. However, these systems often necessitate strict "camera parity" between training and inference, meaning the number, placement, and calibration of cameras must be identical and consistently replicated across different deployment environments. This requirement makes the solutions brittle and challenging to scale in practical applications.
2. **Explicit 3D Reconstruction:** Another line of research utilizes explicit 3D reconstruction techniques to infer the geometric structure of a scene from partial observations. While these methods can provide valuable spatial information, they are typically limited to reconstructing the current state of the scene and do not inherently predict future scene evolution. Furthermore, 3D reconstruction often involves substantial computational costs, which can hinder real-time performance in robotic tasks.

This work, UniviewVLA, positions itself by addressing the limitations of both prior approaches. It aims to overcome the need for additional physical cameras and the computational burden of explicit 3D reconstruction, while simultaneously providing both multiview information and predictive future scene evolution. It seeks to enable robust occlusion-focused manipulation using only standard two-camera observations, thereby enhancing the generality and practical applicability of VLA models in complex, occluded environments. This research contributes to the development of more adaptable and efficient robotic perception systems by leveraging world modeling to synthesize critical visual information that is not directly observed.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for UniviewVLA stems from the challenges associated with occluded tasks in robot manipulation. Standard Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which typically rely on fixed agent-view and wrist-view observations, often fail when action-critical information (e.g., precise object locations, contact points, or target regions) is visually obstructed.

The paper identifies specific limitations of current solutions:
- **Hardware Constraints of Multiview Systems:** Deploying additional physical cameras, while effective for improving visual coverage, introduces significant practical challenges. These include the requirement for exact camera parity (consistent number, placement, and calibration) between training and inference environments, which is difficult to maintain and scale across diverse deployment scenarios.
- **Computational and Temporal Limitations of 3D Reconstruction:** Explicit 3D reconstruction techniques can infer scene geometry, but they are computationally intensive and typically provide only a static representation of the current scene. They do not intrinsically model or predict the future evolution of the scene, which can be crucial for anticipating changes and planning actions in dynamic manipulation tasks.

Against this background, the central question guiding this research is whether a world model, conditioned solely on standard visual observations (agent-view and wrist-view), can generate multiview information and predict future scene evolution. The goal is to leverage this synthesized information to improve downstream action prediction, particularly for occlusion-focused manipulation, without incurring the high costs of additional sensing hardware or complex reconstruction algorithms.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of UniviewVLA are:
1. **Infer Multiview Future Scene Evolution:** To develop a unified VLA model capable of predicting how a scene will evolve from multiple viewpoints, using only standard two-camera observations. This objective aims to provide both broader spatial coverage and temporal foresight.
2. **Enhance Occlusion-Focused Manipulation:** To improve the success rate and robustness of robot manipulation tasks where critical cues are occluded from the default camera perspectives.
3. **Eliminate Extra Hardware and Explicit Reconstruction:** To achieve the benefits of multiview and future scene prediction without requiring additional physical cameras at deployment or computationally expensive explicit 3D reconstruction.
4. **Optimize Inference Efficiency:** To reduce the inference latency associated with generating auxiliary views while maintaining prediction accuracy. This involves compacting the visual information from generated views.
5. **Enable Dynamic View Selection:** To allow the model to dynamically identify and utilize the most action-informative generated viewpoint at different stages of task execution, adapting to changing occlusion patterns.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniviewVLA is designed as a unified multiview Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that integrates world modeling capabilities to infer multiview future scene evolution for action prediction. The framework is built upon the discrete-token autoregressive modeling paradigm, similar to UniVLA, employing an autoregressive transformer (Mθ) to process language instructions, visual observations, auxiliary views, and actions as token sequences.

The methodology comprises two primary training stages and a dynamic inference strategy:

#### 3.1. Generative Multiview World Model Post-training (Stage 1)

In this initial stage, the model is trained to learn the dynamics of the environment and predict future visual states from multiple perspectives.
- **Inputs:** The model receives a two-frame history of standard physical observations, consisting of the agent-view (x_a) and wrist-view (x_w), alongside a language instruction (L).
- **Prediction Task:** For a predefined set of candidate auxiliary viewpoints (V_sel), the world model is tasked with autoregressively predicting the future VQ-token sequence (c^v_{t+1}) for each auxiliary view 'v' at timestep t+1. Each full view is represented by N_tok = 25 × 25 = 625 VQ tokens.
- **Training Objective:** The training objective (L_world) is based on autoregressive visual prediction, minimizing the negative log-likelihood of predicting each VQ token in the future auxiliary view sequence, conditioned on previously generated tokens, the observation history, and the language instruction. This process equips the model with the ability to predict future multiview information and injects scene evolution knowledge into the generated view tokens.

#### 3.2. Joint Motion-Informative Token Compression and Action Fine-tuning (Stage 2)

The generation of full auxiliary views with 625 VQ tokens per view is computationally intensive, leading to high inference latency (e.g., 3125 tokens for five views per step). To address this, UniviewVLA incorporates Motion-Informative Token Compression and then fine-tunes for action prediction.

- **Motion-Informative Token Compression:**
 - **Mechanism:** For each auxiliary view 'v', the method computes a cosine-distance score (δ^v_{t,j}) for each spatial token 'j' by comparing its VQ embedding at time 't' with its embedding at time 't+1'.
 - **Rationale:** A higher δ score indicates a greater visual change between consecutive frames, suggesting higher motion relevance for that specific token.
 - **Output:** The method retains only the top K=16 tokens with the highest δ scores, forming a compact motion-informative representation (ˆo'^v_{t+1}) for each auxiliary view. This significantly reduces the token count from 625 to 16 per view, thus decreasing the overall auxiliary-view token budget (e.g., from 3125 to 80 for five views) and inference latency (from 6–7s to 0.2–0.3s per view).
- **Action Fine-tuning:**
 - **Input:** The compact motion-informative representation (ˆo'^v_{t+1}) of each candidate view is concatenated with the action-token sequence (a_t).
 - **Training Objective:** The model is then fine-tuned using an autoregressive cross-entropy loss (L_act) over this concatenated sequence. This allows action prediction to be conditioned on both compact multiview evidence and the scene evolution knowledge learned by the world model. To prevent the model from collapsing different auxiliary views into the same action-conditioned supervision, and to prepare for inference-time view selection, each demonstration is paired with a view-specific prompt prefix (e.g., "predict [view] view and [instruction]").

#### 3.3. Dynamic Test-Time View Selection via Action Entropy

During inference, UniviewVLA dynamically selects the most action-informative auxiliary view, rather than relying on a static, predetermined viewpoint. This addresses the observation that the optimal viewpoint can vary throughout the task execution.

- **Mechanism:** For each view-selection round, the model considers each candidate auxiliary view 'v'. It generates its compact representation (ˆo'^v_{t+1}) from the current observations and instruction. Subsequently, it calculates the mean action-token entropy (H_v) for the predicted action sequence (a_t) conditioned on this generated view.
- **Selection Criterion:** The view with the lowest action entropy (v^*) is identified as the most action-informative view. Lower entropy indicates a more confident and specific action prediction, suggesting that the view provides clearer, less ambiguous information for the task.
- **Dynamic Adaptation:** The selected view is dynamically updated every 30 policy steps during execution. This adaptive selection process requires no additional view-selection labels or separate training objectives, making it a training-free inference strategy.
- **Final Action Prediction:** The final action-token sequence is predicted using the standard observations, language instruction, and the dynamically selected compact motion-informative auxiliary view.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup and Data Collection

- **Benchmarks:** Evaluations are conducted on standard occlusion-free simulation benchmarks (LIBERO and CALVIN ABCD→D) and customized occlusion-focused tasks.
- **Occlusion-Focused Tasks:** Six LIBERO-style occlusion tasks and two real-robot occlusion tasks on an ALOHA platform were developed. For simulation, multiview demonstrations were collected via 3D-SpaceMouse teleoperation. For real-robot tasks, the unused left-wrist camera was repurposed as an additional physical side-view camera for comparative analysis.
- **Inference Input:** During inference, UniviewVLA consistently uses only the agent-view and wrist-view as physical camera inputs, demonstrating its ability to generate auxiliary information without extra hardware.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on UniviewVLA covered a range of benchmarks, including standard occlusion-free tasks and newly designed occlusion-focused tasks in both simulation and real-world environments.

#### 4.1. Performance on Standard Occlusion-Free Benchmarks

- **CALVIN ABCD→D:** UniviewVLA achieved an average chain length of 4.60, which was reported as the highest among the compared state-of-the-art methods (Table 1). This result suggests that the world model's generated future views provide beneficial additional visual evidence and predictive dynamics knowledge for long-horizon, multi-task manipulation, even in scenarios without explicit occlusions.
- **LIBERO:** UniviewVLA demonstrated an average success rate of 95.8% across the four standard suites, based on 500 episodes per suite. This performance surpassed GR00T N1 by 1.9 percentage points and π0-FAST by 10.3 percentage points (Table 2), indicating that the generated multiview evidence from the world model positively contributes to standard manipulation tasks beyond its specific utility for occlusions.

#### 4.2. Analysis of Generated Views Without Camera Coupling

To evaluate if world model-generated multiview evidence can replace additional physical cameras, an analysis was performed by comparing different camera configurations (Table 3):
- **Two-Camera Policy (A+W):** Achieved 93.0% average success on LIBERO and 4.42 average chain length on CALVIN.
- **Three-Camera Policy (A+W+S):** Using an additional physical side-view camera, it improved to 95.2% on LIBERO and 4.54 on CALVIN.
- **UniviewVLA (A+W):** Using only two physical cameras but leveraging its generated views, UniviewVLA achieved 95.8% on LIBERO and 4.60 on CALVIN.
These results indicate that the compact world model-generated workspace views effectively provide the performance benefits of additional viewpoint coverage, and in some cases, surpass a policy with an extra physical camera, without requiring the deployment cost of additional hardware. The entropy-based dynamic view selection further contributes by not relying on a single fixed side viewpoint.

#### 4.3. Occlusion-Focused Evaluation and Dynamic View Selection (Simulation)

On six customized LIBERO-style occlusion tasks, which hide action-critical cues from standard viewpoints (Table 4):
- **Two-Camera Policy:** Achieved an average success rate of 40.0%, with a low of 4% on Task 6 (heavily occluded switch), confirming the insufficiency of default views for occluded tasks.
- **Three-Camera Policy (physical third camera):** Improved the average success rate to 66.3%.
- **UniviewVLA (Manual fixed view):** Achieved 67.0% average success, matching the performance of a physical third camera.
- **UniviewVLA (Entropy-based dynamic selection):** Further improved the average success rate to 73.3%.
This demonstrates that world model-generated views significantly enhance performance in occluded scenarios. Furthermore, dynamic view selection based on action entropy provides additional gains by adapting to stage-dependent occlusions, outperforming a fixed generated viewpoint.

#### 4.4. Real-Robot Deployment

UniviewVLA was deployed on an ALOHA platform for two right-arm-only real-world occlusion tasks (Oreo-to-Plate and Occluded-Doll Move), each with 15 trials (Figure 6):
- **Two-Camera Policy:** Achieved 13.3% success for Oreo-to-Plate and 20.0% for Occluded-Doll Move.
- **Three-Camera Policy (repurposed left-wrist camera as side-view):** Improved success rates to 40.0% and 53.3%, respectively, validating the benefit of additional visual coverage.
- **UniviewVLA:** Achieved 53.3% for Oreo-to-Plate and 46.7% for Occluded-Doll Move, using only the two default cameras.
These real-world results confirm that UniviewVLA substantially outperforms the two-camera baseline and closely matches, or in one case slightly exceeds, the performance of a three-camera setup without the hardware and calibration costs of an extra physical camera. This suggests a favorable return on investment in terms of occlusion robustness versus deployment cost.

#### 4.5. Efficiency of Motion-Informative Token Compression

The Motion-Informative Token Compression method successfully reduced each generated auxiliary view from 625 tokens to 16 tokens. This led to a substantial reduction in per-view inference latency from 6–7 seconds to 0.2–0.3 seconds.

In summary, the main findings indicate that UniviewVLA effectively addresses the challenge of occluded manipulation by generating multiview future scene evolution from standard camera inputs. It maintains high performance on standard tasks while significantly improving success rates on occlusion-focused tasks in both simulation and the real world, without requiring additional physical cameras or complex 3D reconstruction. The proposed token compression and dynamic view selection mechanisms contribute to its efficiency and adaptability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of UniviewVLA represents a notable contribution to the field of robot learning and manipulation, particularly in addressing the persistent challenge of visual occlusion. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Robustness to Occlusion:** UniviewVLA directly tackles a fundamental limitation of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which often fail when critical visual information is occluded. By inferring multiview future scene evolution, the model provides an internal mechanism to "see around corners" and anticipate changes, making robotic manipulation more robust in complex and cluttered environments. This capability is crucial for deploying robots in real-world settings where occlusions are common and unpredictable.

2. **Reduced Hardware Dependency and Deployment Costs:** A key impact of UniviewVLA is its ability to achieve enhanced performance without requiring additional physical cameras or explicit 3D reconstruction at deployment. This significantly lowers the hardware cost, complexity, and calibration burden associated with setting up robotic systems. Eliminating the need for camera parity between training and inference makes robot policies more scalable and adaptable to diverse environments, simplifying deployment across different locations and tasks.

3. **Advancement in World Modeling for Control:** The work demonstrates a practical application of world models for improving robot control. By learning to predict future multiview visual states, UniviewVLA leverages predictive dynamics as a core component of its action policy. This moves beyond static visual perception and highlights the utility of internal scene understanding and future forecasting for more intelligent robotic behavior.

4. **Improved Efficiency for Real-time Operation:** The introduction of Motion-Informative Token Compression is critical for practical deployment. Reducing the token count per generated view from 625 to 16 dramatically cuts inference latency (from 6–7 seconds to 0.2–0.3 seconds per view). This efficiency gain makes the generation and utilization of auxiliary views viable for closed-loop, real-time robot control, which is essential for dynamic manipulation tasks.

5. **Adaptive Perception with Dynamic View Selection:** The Action-Entropy View Selection mechanism allows the robot to dynamically identify and utilize the most informative viewpoint at any given stage of a task. This adaptive capability ensures that the robot is always leveraging the clearest available "perspective" for action prediction, further enhancing its ability to handle stage-dependent occlusions without additional training or labels.

6. **Contribution to the Research Community:** The paper's contribution of six customized occlusion-focused manipulation tasks, along with an open-source pipeline for multiview data collection, processing, and evaluation, provides valuable resources for other researchers. These benchmarks can facilitate further research and development in occlusion-aware robot learning, enabling standardized assessment of future methods.

In summary, UniviewVLA offers a practical and effective framework for improving robot manipulation in occluded scenarios. Its ability to enhance perception without additional hardware, coupled with its efficiency and adaptability, has the potential to broaden the applicability of VLA models and accelerate the development of more generalist and robust robotic systems capable of operating autonomously in complex human environments.
