# MemLearner: Learning to Query Context memory for Video World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> MemLearner replaces rule-based context retrieval in video world models with learned adaptive querying: noise-initialized query tokens attend to context tokens and condition predicted tokens, run inside the pretrained video DiT itself rather than a separate module, with querying confined to 5 shallow layers plus pruned attention. Trained on a new Unreal Engine occlusion/dynamic-object dataset, it improves revisit PSNR from 17.61 to 18.57 over Context-as-Memory.

## Problem

- Video world models drift out of scene consistency over long horizons because limited context windows cannot retain enough history to reconstruct previously visited regions.
- Rule-based retrieval using FOV overlap or point-cloud surfel matching breaks under occluding walls and moving objects, since hand-coded geometry rules ignore scene state changes.
- No long-video dataset simultaneously offers precise per-frame camera poses, occlusion relations, dynamic objects, and revisit patterns, blocking training of any learned memory mechanism.

## Method

- **MemLearner**'s ==query tokens== (Q) bridge ==context tokens== (C) and predicted tokens (P): Q attends to C to extract memory, P attends to Q as generation condition, supervised only by ==diffusion loss== on P.
- Context querying runs through the ==pretrained video DiT== itself rather than a scratch-trained side module, reusing visual priors; a separate-module variant was tried and failed to learn.
- Two efficiency strategies: restrict C/Q/P interaction to 5 shallow ==Query Layers== (deep ==Generative Layers== see only Q/P), and drop ==attention patterns== where context serves as query.
- Unreal Engine dataset of 100 videos across 13 scenes (16.7 h, ~18000 frames each) with scripted collision-avoiding trajectories, plus per-dataset ==camera encoders== so unannotated real video trains with zero poses.

## Results

- On the occlusion/dynamic test set, MemLearner reaches **21.23** GT-comparison PSNR and **18.57** revisit PSNR versus **19.85**/**17.61** for Context-as-Memory and **19.59**/**17.30** for VMem, with revisit FID dropping **137.87** to **101.57**.
- Gaps shrink on the occlusion-free CaM dataset (**20.35** vs **20.22** PSNR), confirming the advantage is specifically attributable to occlusions and dynamic objects rather than general capacity.
- Ablations: removing Q-attends-to-P collapses PSNR to **17.27**; Query Layers saturate at **5**; the separate query module reaches only **9.16** PSNR; zero-shot Epic-Kitchens transfer gives **20.19** vs **19.31** for CaM.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Attaching a new capability to a pretrained backbone's own layers beats bolting on a scratch-trained module, which the frozen-prior model learns to ignore and starves of gradient.
> - What counts as correctly retrieved memory is not specifiable by hand, so end-to-end supervision from the generation loss outperforms both geometric rules and auxiliary L1 targets.
> - Generative models can learn geometric correspondence implicitly: withholding camera poses from context and query tokens barely degrades memory, questioning explicit 3D conditioning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of **World Models**, which aim to understand and simulate world dynamics by predicting future states based on historical information and actions. Among various representations for world states (language, latent representations, 3D/4D), **Video World Models** are identified as a particularly promising avenue due to their ability to photorealistically capture real-world dynamics and the abundance of internet video data.

Despite advancements in generating short video clips, Video World Models face a significant challenge: **maintaining scene consistency over extended durations**. This is primarily attributed to insufficient memory mechanisms, leading to inconsistencies in later generated scenes compared to earlier ones.

Existing approaches to address this memory issue generally fall into three categories:
1. **3D as memory:** Reconstructing 3D representations from historical frames and rendering new initial frames as conditions.
2. **Feature as memory:** Extracting semantic features from historical frames or maintaining learnable features injected into the generation model.
3. **Context as memory:** Directly using historical frames as conditions, often involving context retrieval to select relevant historical information to reduce computational overhead.

The current work focuses on the "context as memory" paradigm. Previous context retrieval methods are largely **rule-based**, relying on techniques such as Field-of-View (FOV) overlap computation or point cloud estimation and surfel matching. These rule-based approaches exhibit fundamental limitations in complex environments, particularly those involving **scene occlusions and dynamic objects**. For instance, FOV-based methods cannot account for occluding structures, and point-cloud methods struggle with accurately reconstructing moving objects. Furthermore, these hard-coded rules may not generalize well to diverse and dynamic environments with unpredictable state changes.

This paper proposes a shift from these rule-based methods to a **learning-based adaptive context querying mechanism**, aiming to overcome the limitations of prior work by enabling the model to learn dynamically how to extract relevant information from historical frames. This approach builds upon the concept of learnable query tokens, which have been successfully applied in other domains such as multimodal language models (e.g., Perceiver Resampler, Q-Former) for compressing visual context and in video understanding for adaptive frame selection. This work applies learnable query tokens to address the memory problem in video world models, using them as an information bridge for consistent video generation under challenging conditions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a critical challenge in Video World Models: the **lack of persistent memory**, which leads to generated video sequences exhibiting inconsistencies over extended durations. While existing methods have explored rule-based context frame retrieval to serve as memory, these approaches demonstrate significant limitations when applied to complex and dynamic real-world scenarios.

The key motivations and objectives of the MemLearner project are detailed as follows:

1. **Addressing Inconsistency in Long Video Generation:** Video World Models, while capable of generating short clips, struggle to maintain scene consistency—such as object appearance, spatial relationships, and background elements—across long sequences. This limitation is particularly pronounced when revisiting previously generated scenes or interacting with dynamic elements. The core objective is to develop a mechanism that enables consistent generation over extended periods.

2. **Overcoming Limitations of Rule-Based Memory Mechanisms:** Previous attempts to imbue Video World Models with memory have primarily relied on rule-based context retrieval methods. These methods, which might select context frames based on factors like camera FOV overlap or point cloud matching, are inherently brittle. They fail to generalize effectively in scenarios characterized by:
 - **Scene Occlusions:** When parts of the scene are temporarily or permanently hidden by other objects or structures (e.g., a wall between camera views), rule-based methods may retrieve irrelevant context or miss critical information.
 - **Dynamic Objects:** Moving objects are difficult for static 3D reconstruction or simple geometric matching methods to track accurately, leading to inconsistencies in their representation across frames.
 - **Lack of Generalization:** Hard-coded rules do not adapt to diverse and evolving environments, making them inflexible for generalized world modeling.

3. **Developing a Learning-Based Adaptive Context Query Method:** The central objective is to propose a novel memory mechanism that allows the network to **learn adaptively how to query information from historical frames through end-to-end training**. This paradigm shift moves away from manual rule design toward a data-driven approach. This involves:
 - **Introducing Query Tokens (Q tokens):** These tokens serve as an information bridge, designed to attend to historical context tokens (C tokens) to extract relevant memory, and then provide this extracted information as a condition for generating future predicted tokens (P tokens).
 - **Leveraging Pre-trained Visual Priors:** A key design choice is to integrate the context querying mechanism directly within the video generation model itself, rather than creating a separate, scratch-trained module. This allows MemLearner to exploit the existing visual priors embedded in the pre-trained diffusion model, thereby reducing the need for extensive additional training and potentially improving learning efficiency.

4. **Ensuring Computational Efficiency:** Long video sequences inherently present significant computational challenges. Therefore, an objective is to develop efficient strategies for context querying during both training and inference. This includes optimizing attention computations and strategically distributing the querying process across the model's layers.

5. **Collecting a Specialized Dataset and Multi-Dataset Training Strategy:** The development of a learning-based memory mechanism requires a dataset with specific characteristics: long videos, accurate per-frame camera pose annotations, explicit occlusion relationships, dynamic objects, and sufficient diversity. Recognizing that no existing dataset fully meets these criteria, the objectives include:
 - **Dataset Collection:** Creating a new rendered dataset (based on Unreal Engine) with customized scenes, dynamic objects, and automated camera trajectory generation to ensure the necessary annotations and challenging scenarios.
 - **Multi-Dataset Training:** Proposing a training strategy that can effectively combine data sources with varying levels of annotation accuracy (precisely annotated rendered data, real-world data with estimated poses, and unannotated real-world data) without mutual interference, thus leveraging their complementary strengths.

In summary, the motivation is to resolve the fundamental memory limitations of Video World Models in complex scenarios by replacing rigid, rule-based systems with an adaptive, learning-based context querying mechanism, supported by tailored data and efficient training strategies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MemLearner approach is built upon a **latent video diffusion model**, specifically leveraging a causal 3D Variational Autoencoder (VAE) and a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Base Video Generation Model

- **Latent Diffusion:** Video frame sequences (x) are compressed temporally and spatially into latent representations (z) by a 3D VAE encoder. The DiT is then trained to predict noise added to these latent representations.
- **Diffusion Transformer (DiT):** Each DiT block incorporates spatial (2D) attention, spatial-temporal (3D) attention, cross-attention, and Feed-Forward Networks (FFN).
- **Camera Control:** Interactive control, such as camera pose (R, t), is integrated by injecting embeddings from a one-layer MLP encoder into the model, specifically between the 2D and 3D attention layers. The paper notes that the learning-based context query method does not inherently rely on these camera poses.
- **Long Video Generation:** An autoregressive, chunk-by-chunk paradigm is adopted. Historical context frame latents (C) are concatenated with the currently generated latents (P) along the frame dimension and fed into the model. The diffusion loss is applied only to the predicted latents (P) to preserve generative priors.

#### 3.2. Learning to Query Context: MemLearner's Core Mechanism

The central innovation is the introduction of **learnable query tokens (Q tokens)** to bridge context and generation adaptively.
- **Token Interaction:** The video latent tokens are denoted as Z = {C, Q, P}, representing context, query, and predicted tokens, respectively.
 - **Q tokens attend to C tokens:** This allows Q tokens to dynamically extract relevant information from the historical context.
 - **P tokens attend to Q tokens:** The extracted information from Q tokens then guides the generation of the predicted frames (P tokens).
- **Adaptive Querying:** This mechanism is designed to allow different predicted frames and even different denoising stages within a single frame's generation to query distinct aspects of historical information. Empirical analysis (attention similarity) supports that early timesteps attend broadly, while later timesteps focus on fine-grained correspondences.
- **Architecture Design:** Crucially, the context querying mechanism is implemented by feeding all C, Q, and P tokens together into the *existing video generation model* (Fig. 2(c)). This approach leverages the model's pre-trained visual priors, avoiding the need to train a separate, additional context query module from scratch, which was found to be ineffective in experiments (Fig. 2(b)).
- **Training Objective:** The diffusion model input is Z_t = {C, Q, P_t}, where only P tokens are noised at the input. The training loss (L(θ)) applies only to the noise predicted on P tokens, enabling end-to-end learning of the query mechanism without explicit supervision for a dedicated querying module.

#### 3.3. Efficient Strategies for Computation

To manage the computational cost associated with potentially long context sequences, two efficiency strategies are introduced:
- **Strategy #1: Query Only in Early Layers (Layer-Split):** The diffusion transformer's layers are divided into `n` shallow "Query Layers" near the input and `m` deeper "Generative Layers" near the output (where `n << m`).
 - In Query Layers: Interactions occur among C, Q, and P tokens.
 - In Generative Layers: Only Q and P tokens are processed, and C tokens are removed. This capitalizes on the observation that information querying, like encoding, requires fewer parameters than the generative process.
- **Strategy #2: Exclude Unnecessary Computations:** The attention computations are streamlined by retaining only three essential patterns within the 3D attention blocks:
 1. Q as queries attending to P as keys/values (Q->P): Helps Q understand what information to extract based on the target prediction.
 2. Q as queries attending to C as keys/values (Q->C): Enables Q to extract information from the context.
 3. P as queries attending to P and Q as keys/values (P->{P,Q}): Allows P to utilize its own information and the queried context for generation.
 Crucially, attention computations where C tokens serve as queries are excluded, as they introduce substantial overhead with negligible benefit. This optimization is applied differently in Query Layers (C, Q, P tokens) and Generative Layers (Q, P tokens) as specified in the paper.

#### 3.4. Dataset Collection and Multi-Dataset Training

- **Dataset Challenge:** Existing datasets for long video generation often lack critical features: precise per-frame camera annotations, occlusion relationships, dynamic objects, and sufficient diversity, especially for "revisit" scenarios.
- **Customized Dataset:** The authors collected a rendered dataset using Unreal Engine. Key design features include:
 - **Customized Scenes and Dynamic Objects:** Diverse scenes (factories, streets, landscapes) and dynamic objects (humans, animals) are curated to create complex, dynamic scenarios with occlusions.
 - **Automated Trajectory Generation:** A Blueprint script in Unreal Engine automatically generates long camera trajectories with obstacle avoidance, ensuring diverse viewpoints and efficient data collection (100 videos, 13 scenes, 16.7 hours).
- **Multi-Dataset Training Strategy:** To leverage the complementary strengths of different data sources (rendered with precise poses, real-world with estimated poses, real-world without accurate poses), a specialized training strategy is proposed:
 - **Dedicated Camera Encoders:** Each type of dataset is assigned a dedicated camera encoder.
 - **Zero Camera Parameters:** For real-world data lacking reliable pose annotations, zero camera parameters are fed through its dedicated encoder.
 This isolation prevents interference between datasets with varying annotation quality, allowing the model to benefit from precise pose supervision, realism, and diversity simultaneously. During inference, only the encoder trained on precisely annotated data is used.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details (Summarized from Section 5.1)

- P tokens represent 77 video frames, Q tokens match P token count, and C tokens can be up to 9 times the length of P tokens.
- Q tokens are initialized as randomly sampled noise.
- Training involves full fine-tuning of an internal 1B-parameter text-to-video DiT with 28 layers, where 5 layers function as Query Layers.
- An open-source model (Wan2.1, T2V-1.3B) is also used for generalization verification.
- Videos are generated at 640x352 resolution, with a temporal compression ratio of 4, yielding 20-frame video latents for P and Q, and up to 180 latents for C.
- Mixed dataset configurations include rendered-only and a mix of rendered and real-world data (Sekai-real, SpatialVid).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate MemLearner's performance demonstrate its effectiveness in improving scene consistency and memory in video world models, particularly under challenging conditions.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance Comparison

- **Overall Superiority:** MemLearner consistently outperforms all baselines—DFoT, FramePack, VMem, and CaM—across all evaluated metrics (PSNR, LPIPS, FID, FVD) on the collected dataset (Table 2). This indicates both superior memory capability (higher PSNR, lower LPIPS) and better visual quality (lower FID, FVD).
- **Effectiveness in Challenging Scenarios:** While MemLearner and CaM (a rule-based method) perform comparably on the CaM dataset, which lacks occlusions and dynamic objects (Table 3), MemLearner shows a significant performance advantage on the newly collected dataset that explicitly includes these challenges. This directly validates MemLearner's robustness and effectiveness in handling complex scenes with occlusions and dynamic elements.
- **Failure of Separate Module Design:** The alternative architecture (Fig. 2(b)), which introduces a separate, scratch-trained context query module, exhibits poor performance. It fails to learn useful context conditioning, behaving akin to a text-to-video model. This finding supports the crucial design choice of integrating context querying directly within the pre-trained video generation model to leverage existing visual priors (Table 2, Fig. 11).

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

- **Complex Scene Handling:** MemLearner effectively generates consistent video sequences for diverse indoor and outdoor scenes featuring occlusions and dynamic objects (Fig. 5).
- **Real-World Generalization:** When trained with a multi-dataset strategy that includes real-world videos, MemLearner demonstrates the ability to generalize to real-world domains, preserving scene layout and object appearance across revisits (Fig. 6, Fig. 13).

#### 4.3. Generalizability Across Architectures and Datasets

- **Architecture Agnostic:** Application of MemLearner to an open-source video Diffusion Transformer (Wan 2.1) yielded consistent improvements over baselines, confirming that the approach is not specific to the internal model architecture and benefits from pre-trained priors regardless of the backbone (Table 8).
- **Real-World Transfer (SpatialVID & Epic-Kitchens):** MemLearner showed superior performance on the SpatialVID dataset, a real-world dataset with camera pose annotations (Table 9). Furthermore, it demonstrated successful zero-shot transfer to the out-of-distribution Epic-Kitchens dataset, indicating generalization to diverse real-world egocentric scenarios (Table 15).

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies Supporting Design Choices

- **Attention Computation Patterns:** Experiments confirmed the critical role of query tokens (Q) attending to predicted tokens (P) for determining what information to extract from context. Removing this pattern caused significant performance degradation. Conversely, including context tokens (C) as queries introduced substantial overhead with negligible benefits, justifying its exclusion for efficiency (Table 4, Table 11).
- **Query Layer Count:** Five query layers were identified as an optimal balance between computational cost and memory performance, with diminishing returns beyond this point (Table 6).
- **Camera Embedding:** Surprisingly, providing camera pose information only to P tokens (and not C or Q tokens) did not significantly degrade memory performance. This suggests that the video generation model can implicitly learn geometric correspondences without explicit 3D information from context (Table 5).
- **Multi-Dataset Training:** Training solely on real-world data was found insufficient for learning memory capabilities due to a lack of revisit patterns. However, incorporating real-world data effectively improved the realism of generated videos when combined with rendered datasets (Table 7).
- **Query Token Initialization:** The method of initializing Q tokens (random noise or noisy P tokens) did not significantly impact performance, indicating that the learned attention mechanisms are robust and not overly dependent on initial token values (Table 13).
- **Geometry-Based Retrieval:** MemLearner outperformed geometry-based retrieval methods like VRAG, further highlighting the advantage of learning-based over rule-based context querying (Table 14).

#### 4.5. Additional Video Quality and Consistency Metrics

- **VBench and CLIP Similarity:** Beyond pixel-level metrics (PSNR/LPIPS), MemLearner consistently outperformed baselines across various VBench dimensions (Background Consistency, Temporal Flickering, Motion Smoothness, Aesthetic Quality, Imaging Quality) and CLIP similarity, indicating a broader improvement in generated video quality and semantic consistency (Table 16).

In summary, MemLearner achieves state-of-the-art performance in video world models, particularly in complex scenarios with occlusions and dynamic objects, by leveraging a learning-based adaptive context query mechanism integrated within the generative model itself. Its robust design is supported by extensive quantitative and qualitative evaluations, including comprehensive ablation studies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MemLearner research represents a notable advancement in the field of Video World Models, primarily by introducing a learning-based approach to context memory, contrasting with previous rule-based methods. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Context Memory:** The work proposes a fundamental shift from hand-crafted, rule-based context retrieval to an adaptive, learning-based context querying mechanism. This addresses the inherent limitations of prior approaches that struggle with the complexities of real-world scenes, especially those involving occlusions and dynamic objects. This shift could inspire further research into more flexible and robust memory systems for generative models.

2. **Enhanced Scene Consistency and Memory:** MemLearner significantly improves the ability of Video World Models to maintain scene consistency and memory over extended durations. This is crucial for generating long, coherent video sequences that accurately reflect initial states and interactions, even after revisiting portions of the scene. The improved consistency has direct implications for the realism and utility of generated content.

3. **Efficiency and Leverage of Existing Priors:** By integrating the context querying directly into the pre-trained video generation model and employing efficient strategies for attention computation, MemLearner avoids the computational cost and data requirements of training entirely new modules from scratch. This approach makes the memory augmentation more practical and accessible, enabling the exploitation of existing, powerful visual priors.

4. **Dataset Contribution:** The collection of a new rendered dataset with customized occlusions, dynamic objects, accurate camera pose annotations, and revisit patterns addresses a critical gap in existing resources. This dataset provides a valuable benchmark for future research in video world models, particularly for evaluating memory capabilities in challenging scenarios. The proposed multi-dataset training strategy also offers a blueprint for effectively combining diverse data sources with varying annotation qualities.

5. **Broadened Applications of Video World Models:** Improved memory and consistency in Video World Models can unlock more sophisticated applications across various domains:
 - **Interactive Video Generation:** Enabling more coherent and controllable generation of long, interactive video content, relevant for film production, gaming, and virtual reality.
 - **Robotic Simulation and Control:** Providing more accurate and consistent simulations of environments for training and testing robotic agents, which rely on robust world models for planning and decision-making.
 - **Autonomous Systems:** Enhancing the ability of autonomous agents to understand, predict, and interact with dynamic environments over extended periods.
 - **Foundation for Advanced AI:** Advancing the core capability of "world understanding" by allowing AI models to build and maintain more accurate and dynamic representations of their environments.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper also delineates clear future research avenues, such as scaling model capacity to track numerous interacting characters, and exploring advanced memory concepts beyond mere retrieval, including context compression, summarization, updating, editing, and selective forgetting. These suggestions highlight the foundational nature of MemLearner's contribution and its potential to catalyze further innovation in intelligent memory systems.

In conclusion, MemLearner contributes to the development of more intelligent and capable Video World Models by providing a robust, learning-based memory mechanism that generalizes to complex real-world scenarios, thereby paving the way for more consistent and controllable interactive video generation and broader applications in AI.
