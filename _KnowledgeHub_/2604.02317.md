# A Simple Baseline for Streaming Video Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> SIMPLESTREAM, a minimalist baseline employing only the most recent N video frames with an off-the-shelf visual-language model, achieves state-of-the-art performance on streaming video understanding benchmarks like OVO-Bench and StreamingBench, demonstrating that complex memory mechanisms are often not necessary for competitive results.

## Problem

- Complex memory mechanisms, often involving explicit memory banks or retrieval over past observations, are commonly adopted in streaming video understanding but yield modest or inconsistent performance gains.
- There is a prevailing assumption that sophisticated memory architectures are essential for effectively managing long video histories and achieving strong performance in streaming tasks.
- Existing streaming models face challenges in efficiently integrating historical context without degrading real-time perception of current events within causal observation protocols and fixed resource budgets.

## Method

- **SIMPLESTREAM** is a minimalist baseline that processes streaming video by feeding only the most recent N frames and the query text directly into an off-the-shelf ==visual-language model (VLM)==.
- The design intentionally omits any additional ==memory banks==, ==retrieval mechanisms==, visual compression, or specialized training modules, using a ==fixed-size sliding window==.
- This approach functions purely as an ==inference-time input policy== for existing VLMs, maintaining a fixed computational and memory budget per query.

## Results

- **SIMPLESTREAM** achieved state-of-the-art performance on OVO-Bench with **67.7%** accuracy (**8.5** percentage points higher than the best prior streaming method, HERMES) and competitive results on StreamingBench at **80.59%**.
- The baseline exhibited superior efficiency, showing the lowest and most consistent peak GPU memory usage and competitive inference latency (Time-To-First-Token) across increasing stream lengths.
- While selectively re-injecting distant historical information improved memory-oriented tasks by an average of **6.6** percentage points, it concurrently reduced real-time visual perception by **4.9** percentage points, highlighting a direct trade-off.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A consistent "perception-memory trade-off" was observed, where external memory mechanisms often improve historical recall but degrade real-time perception of the current scene.
> - Performance does not always improve with more recent context; there is an optimal recent-window size, which varies depending on the VLM backbone's scale and family.
> - Current benchmarks may implicitly favor perception due to track weighting and specific task definitions (e.g., Hallucination Detection not purely measuring episodic memory), potentially masking trade-offs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of streaming video understanding has seen a growing trend towards developing complex memory mechanisms to process long video streams under causal constraints. These mechanisms typically involve explicit memory banks, retrieval over prior observations, or compression of visual and latent representations to manage bounded computational and memory budgets. This approach is often predicated on the assumption that sophisticated memory management is essential for achieving high performance in streaming scenarios where models must continuously integrate past information with new observations.

Within this landscape, existing research can be broadly categorized into three areas: proactive response and interaction (focusing on response timing and interaction policies), streaming-oriented training (addressing online generation feasibility through supervision and temporal alignment), and memory-centric context management. The latter category, which is most relevant to the current work, includes diverse strategies such as compressing redundant tokens or KV states, making past information query-addressable through retrieval, or introducing explicit external or hierarchical memory structures, and learning constant-length latent states for video prefixes.

This paper challenges the prevailing trend by introducing **SIMPLESTREAM**, a minimalist baseline that questions the necessity of such complex memory architectures. By demonstrating that a simple sliding-window approach, utilizing only the most recent N frames, can match or exceed the performance of more elaborate published streaming models, SIMPLESTREAM re-frames the discussion. It positions the recent-context baseline as a fundamental reference point, turning what might typically be an ablation study into a direct evaluation of when additional memory mechanisms genuinely yield meaningful performance gains beyond what a strong visual-language model (VLM) can achieve with readily available recent visual information. The work primarily focuses on causal online reasoning benchmarks like OVO-Bench and StreamingBench to enable a direct comparison between its simple baseline and complex streaming designs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from an observation that increasingly elaborate memory-centric designs in streaming video understanding have often yielded modest or inconsistent performance improvements. This led the authors to question a fundamental assumption: whether such complex memory mechanisms are indeed necessary for achieving strong performance in streaming video understanding tasks.

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Challenge the Dominant Paradigm**: The central objective is to challenge the prevailing research trend that assumes strong streaming performance necessitates complex memory mechanisms for managing long video histories. The authors hypothesize that the perceived need for complexity might be overestimated.
- **Introduce a Simple, Strong Baseline**: A core objective is to formalize and introduce **SIMPLESTREAM**, a deliberately minimal baseline. This baseline processes streaming video by feeding only the most recent N frames and the query text directly to an off-the-shelf VLM, without incorporating additional memory modules, retrieval systems, compression techniques, or specialized training. This minimalist design aims to isolate the performance achievable solely from recent visual context using powerful existing VLM backbones.
- **Comprehensive Performance Evaluation**: The research aims to conduct a comprehensive evaluation of SIMPLESTREAM against a broad spectrum of established offline and online/streaming video Large Language Model (LLM) baselines. This evaluation is performed on two major streaming video understanding benchmarks, OVO-Bench and StreamingBench, to demonstrate SIMPLESTREAM's competitive performance despite its architectural simplicity.
- **Analyze Performance Determinants and Trade-offs**: Beyond mere performance comparison, the authors aim to conduct controlled ablation studies to understand the factors influencing streaming performance. Specific objectives include:
 - Investigating the impact of recent-window size on accuracy.
 - Analyzing how model scale affects the utility of longer visual context, challenging the notion that more context uniformly benefits larger models.
 - Examining the effects of selectively re-injecting distant historical information through Visual-RAG augmentation.
 - Quantifying a consistent "perception-memory trade-off," where adding historical context may improve recall but potentially degrade real-time perception of the current scene.
- **Propose New Evaluation Standards**: A significant objective is to advocate for revised evaluation practices in future streaming video understanding research. This includes recommending the adoption of strong recent-context baselines (like SIMPLESTREAM), disaggregated perception-versus-memory metrics, and transparent reporting of efficiency statistics. The goal is to ensure that claimed progress from added complexity is clearly demonstrated and not merely assumed, thereby fostering a more rigorous and interpretable research landscape.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on the design and comprehensive evaluation of SIMPLESTREAM, a minimalistic baseline, followed by detailed ablation studies and analysis of its performance characteristics.

#### 3.1. SIMPLESTREAM Design

SIMPLESTREAM operates under a causal observation protocol, where a model must answer queries using only the video prefix observed up to the query time. Unlike prior methods that introduce additional mechanisms for managing long-range history, SIMPLESTREAM is defined by its simplicity:
- **Input Policy**: Given a query at time *t*, SIMPLESTREAM feeds only the most recent *N* observed frames (f*t-N+1*..., f*t*) and the query text directly to an off-the-shelf VLM.
- **Minimalist Construction**: The design intentionally omits any additional memory banks, retrieval mechanisms, visual compression, KV state compression, or specialized training modules. Frames outside the sliding window are discarded.
- **Fixed Budget**: The per-query memory and computation remain bounded by *N* frames and do not increase with the overall stream length, ensuring a fixed streaming budget.
- **Inference-Time Application**: SIMPLESTREAM is an inference-time input policy applied to existing VLMs, requiring no architectural modifications or additional training.

The specific instantiations of SIMPLESTREAM used in experiments employed two open-source VLM backbones: Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct. For each query, the visible stream was sampled at 1 frame per second (fps), and the model was fed the last N ∈ {2, 4, 8} frames.

#### 3.2. Experimental Setup

- **Benchmarks**:
 - **OVO-Bench (Li et al., 2025b)**: A benchmark containing 1,640 questions across 12 tasks, specifically focusing on its Backward Tracing and Real-Time Visual Perception categories to evaluate the trade-off between memory and real-time perception. Questions are answered under observed-only constraints, using only the video prefix.
 - **StreamingBench (Lin et al., 2024)**: The official real-time visual understanding subset, comprising 2,500 questions across ten task types, was used to complement OVO-Bench and test trend transferability.
- **Compared Models**: The study included six offline video LLMs and seven representative online/streaming video LLMs, covering various memory management paradigms. For these baselines, the best inference settings, frame budgets, or sampling rates as reported in their original papers or official implementations were used.
- **Sampling Rate**: For fair comparison with most baselines, SIMPLESTREAM typically sampled frames at 1 fps, with a maximum of 2, 4, or 8 frames fed into the VLM.

#### 3.3. Ablation Studies

To thoroughly analyze SIMPLESTREAM's behavior and the utility of context, several controlled ablations were performed:

- **Recency-Window Ablation**: The number of recent frames *N* fed to the VLM was varied (N ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16}) while keeping retrieval, prompting, and decoding consistent. This assessed the basic effect of exposing more contiguous visual context.
- **Model-Scaling Ablation**: The study investigated how the optimal recent-window size varied with VLM backbone capacity. SIMPLESTREAM was evaluated with multiple sizes of Qwen2.5-VL (3B, 7B, 32B, 72B) and Qwen3-VL (2B, 4B, 8B, 32B, 30B-A3B) across recent windows of N ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16} frames.
- **Visual-RAG Ablation**: To test the impact of selectively retrieving distant historical information, a CLIP-based index was built offline. At inference, the top-5 most similar past chunks were appended to the recent-frame input before VLM generation. This was compared to a matched recent-window baseline.

#### 3.4. Efficiency Evaluation

The efficiency of SIMPLESTREAM was assessed by measuring:
- **Peak GPU Memory**: To understand memory footprint as the stream length grows.
- **Time To First Token (TTFT)**: To evaluate inference latency across increasing observed-frame budgets.

#### 3.5. Perception-Memory Trade-off Quantification

To provide a clearer understanding of the trade-off, specific metrics were defined:
- **Real-Time Perception Change (∆P)**: Defined as RT*method* - RT*SIMPLESTREAM*, where RT is the OVO-Bench Real-Time category average.
- **Memory Gain (∆M)**: Defined as ER*method* - ER*SIMPLESTREAM*, where ER = (EPM + ASI)/2, representing the average accuracy of the Episodic Memory (EPM) and Action Sequence Identification (ASI) tracks from the Backward Tracing category. These two tracks were considered most directly tied to recalling previously observed events.

#### 3.6. Benchmark Limitations Analysis

The authors also analyzed potential limitations in current benchmark designs, specifically discussing how the Hallucination Detection (HLD) track might not be a pure measure of memory and how the macro-average scoring on OVO-Bench could inadvertently favor perception-heavy gains due to uneven track distribution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings that challenge conventional assumptions in streaming video understanding:

#### 4.1. Benchmark Performance of SIMPLESTREAM

- **State-of-the-Art Performance**: SIMPLESTREAM, despite its simplicity, demonstrated competitive performance against published streaming models.
 - On **OVO-Bench**, the optimal SIMPLESTREAM configuration (Qwen3-VL backbone with 4 recent frames) achieved an average accuracy of 67.7%, surpassing the strongest published streaming method, HERMES (59.2%), by 8.5 percentage points (pp).
 - This gain was particularly pronounced in the Real-Time Visual Perception category, where SIMPLESTREAM achieved 81.4% compared to HERMES's 69.0%, with notable improvements in OCR, ACR, and OJR tasks.
 - On Backward Tracing tasks, SIMPLESTREAM remained competitive, with its 8-frame variant reaching 54.9%, exceeding StreamForest (52.0%) and HERMES (49.4%).
 - On **StreamingBench**, SIMPLESTREAM with Qwen3-VL and 4 frames achieved 80.59% accuracy, outperforming HERMES (79.44%) and most other streaming baselines.

#### 4.2. Efficiency Observations

- **Low Peak GPU Memory**: SIMPLESTREAM-4f exhibited the lowest and flattest peak GPU memory usage among compared methods. Its memory consumption remained nearly constant as the observed stream grew, directly attributable to its fixed-window design.
- **Competitive Latency**: SIMPLESTREAM-4f demonstrated competitive Time-To-First-Token (TTFT) latency. It was the second-fastest method at 16, 64, and 256 observed frames, with only HERMES consistently faster. This suggests that low startup latency does not necessarily require persistent historical state.

#### 4.3. Longer Context Is Not Always Better

- **Recency-Window Ablation**: Performance did not monotonically improve with increasing recent context. Moving from 2 to 4 frames generally improved overall and Real-Time accuracy. However, performance often plateaued or slightly declined when the window expanded further to 8 or 16 frames. This indicated that a modest amount of recent context is beneficial, but simply increasing its length does not guarantee continuous gains.
- **Model-Scaling Ablation**: The optimal recent-window size was found to be dependent on the model's scale and backbone family, rather than increasing uniformly with parameter count. While some larger checkpoints (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B) showed a preference for wider windows (16 frames), many small and mid-sized models peaked at 4 frames, and even among larger models, the preference varied (e.g., Qwen3-VL-32B preferred 8 frames, while Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B preferred 4 frames). This suggests that a model's capacity to effectively process additional context influences the utility of longer windows.
- **Visual-RAG Ablation**: Appending retrieved historical chunks (Visual-RAG) did not yield uniform benefits. While it improved memory-oriented Backward Tracing tracks (e.g., EPM by +7.1 pp, ASI by +6.1 pp), these gains were accompanied by clear degradations in Real-Time Visual Perception tracks (e.g., OJR by -9.2 pp, OCR by -8.1 pp, ACR by -7.3 pp). Overall accuracy with Visual-RAG dropped from 66.0% to 63.7%. This demonstrated that introducing richer historical evidence, even targeted, can have a mixed outcome, often at a cost to present-scene perception.

#### 4.4. Perception-Memory Trade-off

- **Consistent Trade-off**: A consistent perception-memory trade-off was identified and quantified. Compared to SIMPLESTREAM (Qwen2.5-VL + 2f as reference), most evaluated external baselines showed a notable decrease in real-time perception (∆P < 0). This perception cost was broad, appearing across memory-bank, retrieval-based, and offline long-context methods.
- **Memory Gains with Perception Costs**: While some methods achieved positive memory gains (∆M > 0) on the combined EPM and ASI tracks, these often came with significant perception penalties. For instance, StreamForest showed a memory gain of +8.9 pp but incurred a perception penalty of -13.8 pp. HERMES gained +2.4 pp in memory but lost -6.0 pp in perception. The Visual-RAG ablation also reinforced this, improving memory-oriented tasks by 6.6 points on average but reducing real-time perception by 4.9 points.
- **Core Challenge**: This indicates that existing memory mechanisms can improve memory-oriented behaviors, but frequently at the cost of the model's ability to accurately perceive the present scene.

#### 4.5. Benchmark Limitations

- **HLD Not a Pure Memory Task**: The Hallucination Detection (HLD) track, part of OVO-Bench's Backward Tracing category, was found to measure robustness against misleading claims rather than direct episodic memory recall. This conflation can obscure true memory evaluation.
- **Macro-Average Bias**: The macro-average score on OVO-Bench was observed to be more sensitive to changes in perception-oriented tracks due to their higher weighting (6 out of 12 tracks for Real-Time Visual Perception), potentially masking trade-offs where memory gains occur at a cost to perception.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings presented in this paper carry significant implications for the future direction of research in streaming video understanding:

- **Re-evaluation of Current Paradigms**: The primary significance lies in challenging the prevailing assumption that increasingly complex memory mechanisms are necessary for strong streaming video understanding. By establishing SIMPLESTREAM as a highly competitive baseline, the paper compels a critical re-evaluation of the architectural complexity in current streaming models. It suggests that a substantial portion of performance may stem from the inherent capabilities of modern VLMs to interpret recent, undiluted visual context.
- **Guidance for Future Model Design**: The consistent perception-memory trade-off suggests a new design principle: "recent-first, history-on-demand." This implies that preserving a clear, high-quality view of the most recent frames should be prioritized by default, with historical memory accessed only when current evidence is insufficient. This could lead to more efficient and effective model architectures that optimize for both present perception and long-range recall.
- **Improved Evaluation Standards**: The paper advocates for more rigorous evaluation practices. It recommends that future streaming video understanding work should always benchmark against strong, simple baselines like SIMPLESTREAM. Furthermore, it proposes disaggregating performance metrics into distinct categories for perception, memory recall, and hallucination robustness. This will enable researchers to precisely identify where new complexities offer genuine improvements without inadvertently degrading other critical capabilities.
- **Clarifying Research Progress**: By highlighting how benchmark designs can implicitly favor certain capabilities (e.g., perception-heavy gains), the work provides a framework for interpreting reported advancements more accurately. It allows for distinguishing between methods that truly enhance long-horizon memory reasoning and those that primarily capitalize on the strengths of underlying backbones in processing recent visual information, which current benchmarks may over-reward. This helps to ensure that claims of progress are genuinely reflective of advancements in managing long-term temporal dependencies.
- **Reframing the Core Research Challenge**: The research reframes the central open problem in streaming video understanding not merely as "how to add more memory," but as "how to effectively use historical context without degrading the model’s understanding of the current scene." This emphasizes the need for memory solutions that are not only effective in recalling past events but also minimally intrusive to real-time perception.
- **Efficiency Considerations**: The demonstrated low memory footprint and competitive latency of SIMPLESTREAM underscore that high performance in streaming video understanding does not necessarily demand high computational or memory overhead. This could encourage the development of more resource-efficient models suitable for real-world deployment.
