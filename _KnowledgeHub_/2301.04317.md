# Teleoperation of Humanoid Robots: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive survey consolidates the current state of humanoid robot teleoperation systems, technologies, and methodologies, outlining their general architecture, specific components, and applications while also identifying critical challenges and future research directions.

## Problem

- Fully autonomous humanoid systems remain limited in their ability to robustly handle complex, unstructured, and hazardous environments.
- There was a recognized gap in the literature for a comprehensive, structured overview of existing humanoid robot teleoperation systems, technologies, and methodologies.
- A practical solution was needed to enable humans to control robots remotely for tasks such as disaster response, space exploration, and healthcare, leveraging human intelligence where autonomy falls short.

## Method

- The paper provides a comprehensive survey of humanoid robot teleoperation, synthesizing current research and categorizing technologies and methodologies.
- It structures the field by detailing components of the general teleoperation architecture, from human interface technologies and feedback mechanisms to retargeting, control strategies, and communication challenges.
- The survey identifies key evaluation metrics and outlines current applications, culminating in a discussion of ongoing challenges and future research directions for the field.

## Results

- A generalized architecture for humanoid robot teleoperation systems was identified, comprising a Human-Machine Interface, Retargeting and Control Algorithms, a Communication Channel, and the Robot Platform itself.
- Various human interface technologies were detailed, including motion capture systems (optical, IMU suits, exoskeletons) and physiological measurements, alongside feedback systems encompassing visual (VR), auditory, haptic, and thermal modalities.
- Current applications of humanoid teleoperation were surveyed across critical domains such as disaster response (e.g., Fukushima), industrial tasks, healthcare, and space exploration, demonstrating the practical utility of these systems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Teleoperation provides an intermediate solution that combines human cognitive abilities with robotic physical capabilities, particularly for tasks in complex or hazardous environments.
> - The effectiveness of humanoid teleoperation systems critically depends on the seamless integration and bilateral flow of information across four core components: Human-Machine Interface, Retargeting and Control Algorithms, Communication Channel, and Robot Platform.
> - Major ongoing challenges in the field include reducing operator cognitive load, enhancing system robustness, optimizing the balance in shared autonomy, and establishing standardization for interfaces and evaluation metrics.
