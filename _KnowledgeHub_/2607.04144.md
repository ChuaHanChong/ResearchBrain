# Semantic-Guided Progressive Object Removal with Gaussian Splatting

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from PICO, ByteDance Inc. and Fudan University developed a framework for 3D object removal that integrates semantic guidance and progressive refinement within 3D Gaussian Splatting. This approach yields enhanced cross-view consistency and preserves fine-grained visual details, demonstrating superior perceptual quality and geometric accuracy compared to previous methods.

## Problem

- Existing 3D object removal methods often rely on single-step completion of masked regions, frequently resulting in artifacts, blurriness, or a lack of fine detail, especially for complex geometric structures and textures.
- Many current techniques fail to effectively leverage multi-view semantic information, leading to inconsistent 3D reconstructions and a lack of geometric coherence in the regions where objects are removed.
- Generative prior-based methods, while capable of synthesizing plausible content, often produce overly smooth or visually inaccurate results due to insufficient explicit geometric guidance and difficulty preserving high-frequency details.

## Method

- The framework integrates object masking and initial inpainting using ==Score Distillation Sampling (SDS)== to generate a preliminary scene within the ==3D Gaussian Splatting== representation.
- A **Semantic-Guided Block Matching (SBM)** module is introduced, utilizing ==DINOv2== embeddings to retrieve semantically relevant content from other views and guide the diffusion model's denoising process via ==semantic-aware token injection==.
- A **Region-Wise Progressive Refinement (RPR)** strategy identifies and re-inpaints low-quality image blocks based on ==high-frequency feature extraction== (==Sobel kernels==), conditioning the denoising UNet on both semantic and frequency-aware guidance for sharper textures and consistent boundaries.

## Results

- The proposed method consistently outperformed state-of-the-art 3D object removal techniques (e.g., SPIn-NeRF, GaussianEdit, MVInpainter, InFusion) across PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS metrics, achieving superior reconstruction quality and perceptual realism (e.g., PSNR **28.7**, SSIM **0.929**, LPIPS **0.139** on Rednet dataset).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contributions of both the Semantic-Guided Block Matching (SBM) and Region-Wise Progressive Refinement (RPR) modules, with SBM significantly improving geometric alignment and RPR enhancing detail preservation and perceptual realism.
- The framework demonstrated a favorable balance of efficiency and performance, requiring approximately **7.9 hours** for training and **13 minutes** for inference with **10GB** GPU memory, which is more efficient compared to several other Gaussian-based methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging multi-view semantic information through block matching provides strong structural alignment and contextual relevance, crucial for synthesizing plausible content in occluded 3D regions.
> - A progressive, region-wise refinement approach allows for targeted improvement of localized artifacts and recovery of high-frequency details, addressing the limitations of one-shot completion strategies.
> - The integration of semantic-guided inpainting and progressive refinement within the efficient 3D Gaussian Splatting framework ensures a balance between photorealistic rendering, geometric consistency, and computational performance for object removal.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D scene reconstruction and manipulation has undergone substantial advancements with the emergence of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and, more recently, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). These representations facilitate photorealistic rendering and efficient scene representation, which are relevant for applications such as virtual and augmented reality, robotics, and autonomous driving. A critical task within this domain is 3D object removal, which involves eliminating undesirable objects from reconstructed scenes and subsequently filling the resulting gaps with geometrically plausible and visually consistent content.

Existing methodologies for 3D object removal face several challenges. Many approaches perform a single-step completion of the masked region, which can result in artifacts or lack detail, particularly when addressing complex geometric structures and textures. A common limitation is the insufficient utilization of semantic information across multiple views. For instance, some methods primarily operate on 2D inputs, leading to inconsistencies in 3D reconstruction and a lack of geometric coherence in the regions where objects are removed. Other techniques leverage generative priors, often through Score Distillation Sampling (SDS), to optimize 3D representations. While these methods can synthesize plausible content, they may produce visually inaccurate or overly smooth reconstructions due to a lack of explicit geometric guidance and difficulty in preserving high-frequency details. Furthermore, the prevalent one-shot completion strategy restricts the ability to iteratively refine suboptimal regions and correct localized artifacts.

This research positions itself within the context of 3DGS, aiming to address the aforementioned limitations by integrating multi-view semantic guidance and a progressive refinement strategy. By building upon the efficient and high-fidelity rendering capabilities of 3DGS, the work seeks to achieve more accurate and consistent object removal, particularly for large objects in complex, unbounded environments, by improving the utilization of cross-view semantic information and enabling targeted refinement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel framework for high-quality 3D object removal that maintains cross-view consistency and preserves fine-grained visual details. This is intended to overcome the limitations observed in existing methods, which often struggle with complex occlusions, intricate geometry, and the generation of coherent content across different perspectives.

The motivation for this work stems from several recognized deficiencies in the current state of 3D object removal:

- **Inadequate Use of Multi-view Semantic Information:** Current methods often do not effectively leverage semantic cues from multiple viewpoints, leading to inconsistent or implausible reconstructions, particularly in regions requiring the hallucination of previously unseen content. For example, approaches like SPIn-NeRF predominantly perform inpainting from 2D inputs, which can result in a lack of 3D geometric coherence. The integration of semantic guidance is critical to align new content with the broader scene context.
- **Challenges with Complex Geometry and Textures:** Existing techniques encounter difficulties when faced with complex geometric details and textures in the masked regions. Generative prior-based methods, while powerful, often produce overly smooth or visually inaccurate results because they lack explicit mechanisms to guide the generation towards preserving high-frequency details. There is a need for mechanisms that can inject auxiliary supervision to achieve sharper and more realistic inpainting.
- **Limitations of One-Shot Completion:** Many frameworks adopt a one-shot strategy to complete the entire masked region. This approach inherently restricts the ability to iteratively refine specific areas that exhibit poor visual quality or inconsistencies. A progressive refinement mechanism is motivated by the need to address localized artifacts and enhance visual fidelity in a targeted manner.
- **Importance within 3DGS Framework:** With the rise of 3D Gaussian Splatting as an efficient and high-fidelity 3D representation, there is an increasing demand for accurate and consistent object removal methods tailored to this framework. Such capabilities are crucial for interactive editing, digital content creation, and other downstream scene understanding tasks.

To address these motivations, the authors propose a framework that incorporates Semantic-guided Block Matching (SBM) to enhance structural alignment and texture recovery, and a Region-Wise Progressive Refinement (RPR) strategy to iteratively improve the visual fidelity of challenging regions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology integrates Semantic-guided Block Matching (SBM) and Region-Wise Progressive Refinement (RPR) within a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) pipeline to achieve 3D object removal. The process is designed to ensure multi-view consistency and fine-grained visual quality.

#### 3.1. Multi-view Representation and Initialization

The scene is represented using 3D Gaussians, where each Gaussian is characterized by its center, rotation, opacity, and RGB color (encoded via spherical harmonics).
1. **Initialization:** A sparse point cloud, estimated using Structure-from-Motion (SfM), provides the initial positions for the 3D Gaussians. Their scales and opacities are either defaulted or randomized. Adaptive densification techniques (splitting and cloning of primitives) are applied in regions with high reconstruction error to enhance scene fidelity.
2. **Object Masking and Initial Inpainting:** 2D object masks are used to identify the target object. Gaussians within the masked regions are removed and replaced with randomly initialized Gaussians. Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) is then employed, distilling knowledge from a 2D inpainting diffusion backbone (Stable Diffusion v2.1) to generate initial inpainted images and update the Gaussian parameters. This process involves adding noise to rendered images and using a latent diffusion model to predict and minimize the denoising error, guiding the 3DGS model to produce photorealistic renderings.

#### 3.2. Semantic-Guided Block Matching (SBM)

SBM is introduced to leverage cross-view contextual information, retrieve semantically relevant content, and enforce high-level alignment during diffusion-based generation.
1. **Block Decomposition and Semantic Extraction:** The initially inpainted image and its corresponding binary mask are divided into non-overlapping square blocks. Only blocks with an occlusion ratio exceeding a threshold (empirically set to 0.1) are selected for retrieval. For each selected block, a pre-trained DINOv2 encoder extracts high-level semantic embeddings.
2. **Cross-View Retrieval:** Semantic features are computed for all visible blocks in the original multi-view images. For each target block, a best-matching source block from another view is identified by computing cosine similarity between their semantic feature vectors.
3. **Semantic-guided Inpainting:** The selected best-matching source block serves as a semantic reference. It is projected into the latent space, and the denoising process of a UNet-based diffusion model is guided by injecting a semantic-aware token, derived from the matched reference block, into each layer of the UNet via cross-attention mechanisms. This ensures that the generated content within masked blocks is semantically aligned with retrieved multi-view information.

#### 3.3. Region-Wise Progressive Refinement (RPR) Strategy

RPR addresses residual issues such as blurry textures or geometric inconsistencies by selectively refining low-quality blocks.
1. **High-Frequency Feature Extraction:** Low-quality inpainted regions are identified based on their lack of high-frequency details. The reconstructed RGB image is converted to grayscale, and horizontal and vertical Sobel kernels are applied to extract high-frequency responses. A block is flagged for refinement if its average high-frequency magnitude falls below a predefined threshold (15% of the surrounding area).
2. **Re-Inpainting with Frequency Guidance:** For each identified low-quality block, a re-inpainting process is performed using a fine-tuned UNet. This step conditions the denoising UNet on both the semantic token guidance (from SBM) and a frequency-aware embedding. The frequency-aware embedding is obtained by encoding the block’s high-frequency map (derived from Sobel gradients) using a lightweight convolutional encoder. This dual conditioning aims to recover sharper textures, guided by both global semantics and local detail priors.
3. **Selective Replacement:** The refined blocks are selectively replaced within the initially completed image. This targeted approach reduces computational overhead by only updating perceptually suboptimal regions.

#### 3.4. Integration with Gaussian Splatting

The object removal framework is integrated into the 3DGS pipeline, ensuring consistent updates to the underlying 3D geometry and appearance. The Gaussian representation parameters (color and spatial) are optimized across views using a composite loss function.
- **Total Loss Function:** The total loss comprises a Gaussian reconstruction loss ($L_{GS}$) and an SDS loss ($L_{SDS}$). $L_{GS}$ combines a mean absolute error (MAE) term and a differentiable SSIM loss to balance pixel-level differences and perceptual structural similarity. The SDS loss term guides the 3DGS to produce photorealistic renderings consistent with the 2D diffusion prior.
- **Efficiency Measures:** To maintain efficiency, the number of Gaussians is limited, and a coarse-to-fine strategy is employed, starting with sparse Gaussians and progressively refining them.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework in 3D object removal.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparisons

**1. Results on Forward-facing Scenes:**
The method was compared against SPIn-NeRF and GaussianEdit on various forward-facing scenes (Rednet, Orchids, Chuyin, Kitchen).
- **Qualitative Observations:** SPIn-NeRF exhibited issues with texture discontinuities and blurry regions, particularly in complex areas like grass and nets. GaussianEdit showed improved texture fidelity but occasionally suffered from ghosting artifacts. In contrast, the proposed method generated sharper and semantically more plausible results, attributed to its multi-view semantic alignment and high-frequency supervision.
- **Quantitative Metrics:** The proposed method consistently outperformed SPIn-NeRF and GaussianEdit across PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS metrics computed over the masked inpainting regions. For instance, on the Rednet dataset, the method achieved a PSNR of 28.7, an SSIM of 0.929, and an LPIPS of 0.139, indicating superior reconstruction quality and perceptual realism.

**2. Comparison with Gaussian-based Methods:**
The method was further evaluated against MVInpainter, GaussianGroup, and InFusion, all of which are Gaussian-based approaches for 3D object removal.
- **Qualitative Observations:** In scenes such as Bear and Bicycle, the proposed method produced cleaner and more semantically aligned inpainting. MVInpainter often generated visually smooth results but struggled with detailed restoration and could introduce over-smoothed patches. GaussianGroup occasionally produced semantically irrelevant textures or inconsistent shading, especially in complex backgrounds. InFusion, while achieving promising results with 2D diffusion priors, sometimes lacked precise structural alignment in removed object areas. The proposed method's use of semantic-aware token guidance and progressive region-wise refinement contributed to visually realistic and structurally coherent results.
- **Quantitative Metrics:** The proposed approach consistently achieved the highest performance across all evaluation metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) when compared to other Gaussian-based methods. For example, on self-captured datasets, it achieved a PSNR of 29.6, an SSIM of 0.936, and an LPIPS of 0.131. In the Kitchen scene from Mip-NeRF 360, it recorded a PSNR of 27.4, an SSIM of 0.899, and an LPIPS of 0.161, surpassing the competing methods.

#### 4.2. Comparison on Runtime and Memory

An efficiency comparison was conducted on a self-captured dataset using an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU.
- The proposed method demonstrated a favorable balance between performance and efficiency, requiring 7.9 hours for training and 13 minutes for inference, with a GPU memory footprint of 10GB.
- This compared favorably to methods like MVInpainter and InFusion, which required longer training durations (120h and 24h, respectively) and more memory (20GB). GaussianEditor and GaussianGroup offered faster training but with lower accuracy as indicated by other metrics.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were performed to assess the contribution of the Semantic-Guided Block Matching (SBM) and Region-Wise Progressive Refinement (RPR) modules.
- **Effect of SBM:** Removing the SBM module resulted in a substantial performance degradation. PSNR dropped by 5.57 (self-captured) and 4.44 (public scene), SSIM decreased notably, and LPIPS increased significantly (by 0.186 and 0.218). This indicates SBM's importance for geometric alignment and overall perceptual quality.
- **Effect of RPR:** Without the RPR module, PSNR dropped by 0.78 (self-captured) and 2.19 (public scene), and LPIPS increased by 0.017 and 0.047, respectively. While SSIM degradation was less pronounced, the increase in LPIPS suggests a reduction in perceptual realism. This highlights the RPR module's role in detail preservation and boundary smoothness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for 3D object removal that addresses several challenges in the field, particularly concerning multi-view consistency, fine-grained detail preservation, and the iterative refinement of inpainted regions. By integrating semantic-guided inpainting with progressive refinement within the efficient 3D Gaussian Splatting pipeline, the work contributes to improving the realism and coherence of 3D scene manipulation.

The significance of this work lies in its ability to achieve quantitative and qualitative improvements over existing methods in perceptual quality and geometric accuracy for 3D object removal. The proposed Semantic-guided Block Matching module facilitates the effective utilization of cross-view semantic information, guiding the completion of occluded regions with contextually relevant content. The Region-Wise Progressive Refinement strategy allows for targeted enhancement of suboptimal areas, resulting in sharper textures and more consistent boundaries, which are aspects often challenging for one-shot or less guided generative approaches.

The potential impacts of this research span several application domains:
- **Augmented and Virtual Reality (AR/VR):** The ability to perform high-fidelity 3D object removal and scene completion can enhance the realism and interactivity of AR/VR experiences, allowing for more seamless integration of virtual content into real-world scenes or for modifying virtual environments.
- **Robotics:** For autonomous systems, accurate 3D scene understanding and the capability to digitally modify environments can support tasks such as path planning, obstacle removal simulations, and the generation of diverse training data by altering scene contents.
- **Digital Content Creation:** Content creators can benefit from more precise and efficient tools for editing 3D scenes, enabling the removal of unwanted elements from scanned or reconstructed environments without introducing artifacts or inconsistencies. This could streamline workflows in film production, game development, and architectural visualization.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework, by combining DINOv2 for semantic guidance and a frequency-aware progressive refinement strategy, provides a robust baseline for further exploration in 3D scene editing, multi-view generative models, and the integration of advanced semantic priors into 3D reconstruction techniques.
- **Practicality:** The demonstrated efficiency in terms of training time and GPU memory usage suggests that the approach is practical for deployment in real-world scenarios, making it accessible for applications where computational resources are a consideration.
