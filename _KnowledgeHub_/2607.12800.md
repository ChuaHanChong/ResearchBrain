# UniVR: Thinking in Visual Space for Unified Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> UniVR introduces a framework that enables AI models to learn complex reasoning, physical dynamics, and long-term planning directly from visual demonstrations, operating natively in visual space. The system improves performance on the VR-X benchmark by 18.4% in VLM score and 20.61 in JEPA compared to prior unified generative models, achieving greater logical coherence and physical consistency in generated visual sequences.

## Problem

- Contemporary AI models (LLMs, MLLMs) primarily reason within abstract linguistic spaces, limiting their ability to fully capture rich, dynamic, and spatially complex real-world visual information and physical laws.
- Existing MLLM approaches generating textual reasoning chains for visual tasks often struggle with maintaining logical coherence and physical consistency, particularly in fine-grained, long-horizon visual evolutions.
- Current video generation or unified generation models for visual reasoning frequently depend on textual guidance and extensive dense image-text datasets, posing scalability challenges for knowledge acquisition.

## Method

- **UniVR** employs a ==two-stage training pipeline==, starting with ==supervised fine-tuning (SFT)== of a pre-trained unified generative model (**Emu3.5**) on a newly constructed, diverse **VR-X** visual reasoning dataset.
- Following SFT, the model undergoes ==reinforcement learning== using **VR-GRPO**, a novel paradigm designed to refine visual reasoning through ==next-token prediction== directly within visual space.
- VR-GRPO incorporates a ==reward mechanism== that integrates a ==global reward== (assessing overall task completion and visual fidelity) with a ==step-focal reward== (identifying and correcting errors in specific sub-steps) to ensure both high-level success and fine-grained logical/physical consistency.

## Results

- UniVR achieved an **18.4%** improvement in **VLM score** and **20.61** improvement in **JEPA** (physical alignment) over the Emu3.5 baseline on the VR-X benchmark, demonstrating more coherent and physically consistent visual reasoning.
- The system attained approximately a **60%** success rate in long-term planning and **70%** in general reasoning on VR-X, with a **34B** parameter model approaching or surpassing the performance of larger MLLM+T2I pipelines like Gemini 3 Pro on several metrics.
- VR-GRPO showed pronounced gains on longer sequences (**>30 seconds**), with a **23.9%** improvement for sequences over **60 seconds** compared to Emu3.5, and demonstrated generalizability with up to **24.3%** improvement on external embodied intelligence and cognitive reasoning benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly learning complex reasoning, fine-grained physical dynamics, and long-term planning solely from visual demonstrations is effective, overcoming the limitations of text-dependent visual reasoning.
> - The combination of global and step-focal rewards within VR-GRPO is crucial for enforcing logical coherence and physical consistency throughout multi-step visual reasoning processes, mitigating reward hacking observed with global-only rewards.
> - Enhanced reasoning capabilities developed natively in visual space can transfer and provide performance benefits to multimodal understanding benchmarks that typically include textual components, indicating a synergistic relationship between visual and textual comprehension.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Contemporary artificial intelligence models predominantly acquire world knowledge through textual data, performing reasoning and planning within abstract linguistic spaces. This text-centric paradigm, common in large language models (LLMs) and multimodal large language models (MLLMs), inherently faces limitations in fully encapsulating the rich, dynamic, and spatially complex information of the real visual world, including intricate physical dynamics and underlying physical laws. In contrast, human and animal cognition frequently involves complex reasoning and simulation of task execution directly within a visual mental space, without explicit reliance on language.

Recent advancements in visual reasoning have explored two main avenues. One approach involves MLLMs, such as Gemini-3 and GPT-5, that generate textual reasoning chains which are subsequently rendered into visual traces by generative models like Nano Banana and GPT-Image. While this method leverages established linguistic capabilities, the inherent abstraction of text often limits its ability to accurately represent and translate intricate physical dynamics and spatial relationships into coherent visual sequences. Models employing this paradigm have demonstrated challenges in maintaining logical coherence and physical consistency, particularly in tasks requiring fine-grained, long-horizon visual evolution.

A second pathway has utilized video generation models or unified generation models to directly model task strategies in visual space. While these methods show promise in acquiring knowledge from visual data, their reasoning capabilities often remain dependent on textual guidance and require extensive datasets of dense image-text pairs for both training and inference. This dependency poses scalability challenges for advancing visual reasoning.

UniVR differentiates itself by investigating the simultaneous learning of complex reasoning, fine-grained physical dynamics, and long-term planning directly from pure visual demonstrations. It aims to circumvent the limitations of text-dependent or text-guided visual reasoning by operating natively within visual space, thereby seeking a more direct and scalable approach to world knowledge acquisition for AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind UniVR stems from the observed limitations of current AI systems in acquiring comprehensive world knowledge and performing nuanced reasoning from abstract textual representations. The researchers highlight that text, by its nature, cannot fully convey complex visual information such as dynamics, spatial relationships, and physical laws. This contrasts with human visual reasoning, which enables complex tasks without language by mentally simulating visual transitions and executions. Given the vast availability of visual data (e.g., internet videos), equipping AI with the ability to reason and plan directly in visual space holds potential for advancing its world modeling capabilities and improving real-world task execution.

The specific objectives of this research are multi-faceted:

- **To explore direct visual-space learning**: The core objective is to investigate the feasibility and effectiveness of simultaneously learning complex reasoning, fine-grained physical dynamics, and long-term planning solely from raw visual demonstrations, without relying on textual explanations or dense language supervision. This aims to bridge the gap between abstract textual reasoning and the direct visual processing observed in biological intelligence.
- **To develop a novel reinforcement learning paradigm**: The work seeks to introduce VR-GRPO, a reinforcement learning (RL) framework designed to enforce logical coherence and physical consistency throughout visual reasoning processes. A key motivation here is to overcome the limitations of existing RL methods that often prioritize visual fidelity or text-image alignment over the intricate logical and physical aspects of multi-step visual tasks. This paradigm aims to function without requiring task-specific heuristics or extensive image-text pairs.
- **To establish a comprehensive evaluation benchmark**: A significant objective is the construction of VR-X, a large-scale benchmark specifically designed to assess heterogeneous visual reasoning capabilities. The motivation for VR-X is to address the shortcomings of existing benchmarks, which often emphasize visual quality or text matching and are typically confined to narrow, short-term tasks. VR-X aims to provide a unified protocol for evaluating diverse capabilities, including long-horizon manipulation, spatial puzzles, and physical reasoning, under a purely visual assessment.
- **To demonstrate the broad impact of visual reasoning**: The research aims to show that enhancing reasoning capabilities directly within visual spaces can not only improve performance on purely visual tasks but also confer benefits to multimodal understanding benchmarks that typically involve textual components. This suggests a synergistic relationship between visual and textual comprehension when visual reasoning is robust.
- **To provide an open-source contribution**: A further objective is to release all code, data, and models to foster further research and development in visual reasoning and world modeling.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniVR employs a two-stage training pipeline built upon an autoregressive generation model, aiming to perform visual-space reasoning through next-token prediction.

#### 3.1. Cold Initialization (Supervised Fine-Tuning - SFT)

The initial stage involves supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of a pre-trained unified generative model.
- **Base Model**: Emu3.5 (34B parameters), a state-of-the-art unified generative model, serves as the baseline. Emu3.5 tokenizes images, text, and video into a unified discrete vocabulary. The researchers observed that Emu3.5, despite extensive pre-training on image-text pairs, exhibited limitations in capturing complex real-world physics, fine-grained action dynamics, and general visual cognitive abilities. This was attributed to its reliance on textual reasoning for world knowledge acquisition, effectively reducing vision to a rendering mechanism.
- **Data Construction (VR-X Data Generation)**: A key component of the methodology is the creation of the VR-X benchmark and its associated training data. This involved curating 1.5 million raw samples from 16 diverse sources, encompassing tasks from minute-long planning (e.g., robotic manipulation, cooking) to single-step reasoning (e.g., mazes, visual search). These raw samples were processed through a four-stage pipeline to generate 310,000 cold-initialization training samples, 3,000 RL samples, and 1,800 benchmark evaluation samples.
 1. **Video Processing**: Raw video sequences were selected, and scene-aware temporal sampling (at 0.27 FPS using PySceneDetect) was applied to retain rich temporal information. Subsequent deduplication using SigLIP2 and VLM-based filtering (using Qwen3.5-397B) removed low-quality frames, facial close-ups, and blank screens.
 2. **Question-Answer Synthesis**: Qwen3.5-397B was used to synthesize reasoning-oriented questions and corresponding textual answers (approximately 10 key steps per trajectory) from the sampled sequences. This provided textual annotations for multimodal learning.
 3. **Key-Step Frame Identification**: Based on the QA pairs, Qwen3.5 was prompted to identify the most relevant query image and key-step frames within the trajectories.
 4. **Quality Filtering**: A rigorous filter was applied to discard low-quality samples, including those with image-text mismatches, trivial questions, or poor visual quality, resulting in an 80% reduction in the candidate pool. For non-video sources (e.g., VisualCoT, ZebraCoT), the data was standardized to match the video-derived format.
 The SFT process standardizes all training samples into a query image, textual instruction, and a visual reasoning trajectory format. The formulation emphasizes direct modeling of state transitions and underlying policy dynamics in visual space, without requiring dense textual reasoning chains.
- **Optimization**: UniVR was trained with full-parameter SFT on 32 GPUs. The learning rate was set to 5e-4 with a cosine scheduler, a weight decay of 0.1, and a gradient norm clip of 5.0. Training involved 700 warm-up steps and 10,000 total steps. Input images were resized to 512 pixels on the short side, and the maximum sequence length was capped at 15,000 tokens to accommodate multi-minute reasoning trajectories.

#### 3.2. Visual Reasoning GRPO (VR-GRPO)

Following cold initialization, the model undergoes reinforcement learning using VR-GRPO, a novel paradigm designed to refine visual reasoning.
- **Motivation for RL**: SFT alone struggles to reconcile heterogeneous tasks (e.g., 2D puzzles vs. long-term human planning) due to their vast differences in temporal scale, domain knowledge, and visual appearance. Existing RL methods in generative models primarily focus on visual fidelity or cross-modal alignment, not the logical and physical consistency crucial for visual reasoning.
- **Reward Design**: VR-GRPO's reward mechanism integrates two main components:
 1. **Format Reward (R_format)**: Ensures generated image sequences adhere to structural constraints, such as uniform resolution and the number of reasoning steps specified in the instructions.
 2. **Visual Reasoning Reward (R_reason)**: This composite reward is designed to ensure both overall task completion and fine-grained reasoning coherence. It consists of:
 - **Global Reward (R_g)**: A general-purpose prompt guides an online reward model (Qwen3-VL-30B) to provide a holistic quality assessment of rollout samples against ground-truth references, considering task completion and visual fidelity. However, preliminary analysis indicated this global reward often overlooked intermediate physical violations and logical gaps in long, multi-step tasks, over-prioritizing terminal success.
 - **Step-Focal Reward (R_s)**: Introduced to address the limitations of the global reward by identifying and targeting error-prone sub-steps within a reasoning trajectory.
 - **Uncertainty-Based Sampling**: For a set of K rollout trajectories, per-frame CLIP feature embeddings are extracted. The inter-trajectory variance (σ(t)) is calculated at each timestep. High variance signifies maximum uncertainty and diverging reasoning paths.
 - **Segment Identification**: A peak uncertainty timestep (t*) is identified as argmax_t σ(t). A reasoning segment of window size W (default 4 frames) around t* is extracted ([t* - W/2, t* + W/2]). For excessively challenging samples, random sampling is used. For variable-length trajectories, they are segmented into an equal number of parts, and average CLIP features within segments are used for uncertainty analysis.
 - **Pairwise Evaluation**: Both global and step-focal rewards are obtained through a pairwise evaluation protocol (similar to), where a VLM compares two clips, mitigating scoring bias of direct scalar assessments.
 - **Reward Integration**: R_reason = R_g - λ|R_g - R_s|. This formula penalizes discrepancies between the global and step-focal assessments, encouraging the model to maintain both accurate terminal states and procedural integrity/physical coherence. The coefficient λ (default 2.0) controls the strength of this alignment.
- **Optimization**: VR-GRPO is initialized from the SFT checkpoint and trained with full parameters on 32 GPUs, using the verl framework. The learning rate is 1e-5 with a cosine scheduler, weight decay of 0.1, and gradient norm clip of 5.0, for 250 training steps. A rollout size of 8 is used. The Qwen evaluator is deployed on an additional 8 GPUs; Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B is used for efficiency, with larger variants being more accurate but slower. Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) is disabled during rollout.

#### 3.3. VR-X Benchmark Evaluation

The VR-X evaluation set comprises 1,800 high-quality reasoning trajectories curated by professional annotators from a held-out subset. Evaluation focuses on logical accuracy and adherence to physical dynamics using two metrics:
- **VLM Score**: An automated evaluation using Qwen3.5-397B, guided by a unified prompt assessing task completion, procedural coherence, visual informativeness, and image fidelity. Scores are normalized (0-100). This metric showed a Spearman correlation of approximately 0.85 with human evaluation, indicating high alignment.
- **JEPA Similarity**: This metric assesses alignment with real-world physics, addressing potential VLM hallucinations regarding physical laws. It maps image sequences into a 1280-dimensional latent space using a V-JEPA encoder and computes Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) with a polynomial kernel against ground-truth distributions. Lower scores indicate closer physical alignment. This metric is applied exclusively to long-term planning tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding UniVR's performance and the effectiveness of its components.

#### 4.1. Performance on VR-X Benchmark

UniVR demonstrated enhanced performance across the VR-X benchmark compared to existing methods:
- **LMMs with T2I models**: These models, such as Gemini 3 Pro with Nano Banana 2, achieved the highest scores (66.1% VLM score, 11.07 JEPA) among their category due to strong textual reasoning and rendering. However, they exhibited persistent visual inconsistencies in complex dynamics and planning tasks, indicating limitations in translating textual reasoning into physically coherent visual execution.
- **Unified Generation Models**: Models like Emu3.5, while generally outperforming other unified models (39.8% VLM score, 33.62 JEPA), struggled with VR-X's intricate visual dynamics, and their performance was noted to stagnate at around 35% on challenging tasks. Their training objectives often centered on entertainment rather than logical reasoning, and they showed poorer JEPA scores, indicating significant divergence from ground-truth physical dynamics.
- **UniVR Performance**: UniVR achieved a 58.2% overall VLM score and 13.01 JEPA, representing an 18.4% improvement in VLM score and 20.61 improvement in JEPA over Emu3.5. Without relying on fine-grained textual procedural annotations, UniVR attained approximately a 60% success rate in long-term planning and 70% in general reasoning. At a 34B parameter scale, UniVR approached the performance of Gemini 3 Pro and surpassed Gemini 2.5 Pro on several key metrics. This indicates that UniVR's training pipeline effectively extracts task-centric policies directly from visual signals, resulting in visually and physically more coherent generated sequences.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies provided insights into the contributions of different components:
- **Multimodal Understanding Benefit**: Integrating the UniVR visual reasoning paradigm led to significant gains across standard multimodal understanding benchmarks (e.g., MMMU, MME, MMBench, MathVista, MM-Vet), in contrast to training solely on textual reasoning chains, which yielded negligible improvements. This suggests that enhanced visual reasoning complements textual supervision and strengthens overall multimodal comprehension.
- **VR-GRPO Reward Components**:
 - The global reward alone, while improving general reasoning for short sequences, led to degradation in long-term planning and JEPA scores, indicative of reward hacking (Fig. 6).
 - Introducing the step-focal reward alone yielded positive improvements across all metrics.
 - Combining both global and step-focal rewards resulted in a synergistic effect, further enhancing overall performance.
 - Transitioning from absolute scalar VLM scoring to a pairwise comparison protocol further improved reward robustness and final results.
- **Compatibility with Text-based RL**: VR-GRPO demonstrated compatibility with other reward functions. While adding textual reasoning chains or aesthetic/vision-language alignment rewards (HPSv3, CLIP) provided limited gains for complex visual reasoning tasks, VR-GRPO itself significantly boosted visual reasoning capabilities.
- **Stabilization of Heterogeneous Knowledge Training**: During the cold-start phase, joint training across heterogeneous tasks in VR-X struggled to balance diverse knowledge, underperforming models trained on individual tasks. The introduction of VR-GRPO, however, enabled the model to autonomously explore optimal policies, leading to performance gains in both long-term planning and general reasoning, thus demonstrating its efficacy in unified visual reasoning training.
- **Generalization to External Benchmarks**: UniVR demonstrated generalizability by substantially improving baseline performance (up to 24.3%) on test data from external benchmarks focusing on embodied intelligence (WorldArena, RBench) and cognitive reasoning (Uni-MMMU), despite significant domain gaps. This indicates that VR-GRPO fosters more generalizable visual reasoning.
- **Scalability Across Temporal Horizons**: VR-GRPO showed the most pronounced gains on longer sequences (>30 seconds), with a 23.9% improvement for sequences over 60 seconds compared to Emu3.5. This was attributed to the step-level reward design, which explicitly maintains logical coherence and physical consistency at intermediate stages, mitigating compounding errors in long-range predictions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UniVR framework represents a notable investigation into the capabilities of AI models to learn complex reasoning and planning directly within visual space, without relying on dense language supervision. Its significance and potential impact can be considered in several areas:

- **Advancement in Visual Reasoning**: UniVR is presented as the first exploration into simultaneously learning complex reasoning, fine-grained physical dynamics, and long-term planning exclusively from visual demonstrations. This approach aims to overcome fundamental limitations of text-centric AI models in understanding the nuances of the visual world, such as intricate physical interactions and spatial relationships.
- **Methodological Innovation**: The introduction of VR-GRPO, an RL paradigm featuring complementary global and step-focal rewards, is a key methodological contribution. This design enforces logical coherence and physical consistency throughout the reasoning process, addressing the issue of reward hacking observed with global-only rewards. This novel reward mechanism operates without requiring task-specific heuristics or extensive image-text pairs, enhancing the scalability and applicability of the approach.
- **Comprehensive Benchmarking**: The construction of VR-X is a significant contribution to the research community. As the first large-scale benchmark designed to evaluate diverse and heterogeneous visual reasoning capabilities—spanning long-horizon manipulation, spatial puzzles, and physical reasoning under a purely visual protocol—it provides a standardized tool for assessing progress in this field and facilitating future research.
- **Enhanced Performance and Efficiency**: UniVR achieves substantial performance improvements (up to 25% on VR-X) over both text-based reasoning pipelines (MLLMs paired with text-to-image models) and existing unified generation models. Its ability to achieve strong results with a 34B parameter model, approaching or surpassing larger pipelines like Gemini 3 Pro + Nano Banana 2 on certain metrics, suggests improved efficiency in acquiring and applying visual world knowledge.
- **Broader Implications for AI World Models**: The findings underscore the potential of reasoning directly within visual spaces for developing more grounded and efficient world models. The demonstration that strengthened visual reasoning also boosts performance on various multimodal understanding benchmarks suggests that vision-native computation can serve as a powerful complement to textual abstraction, leading to more robust and comprehensive AI systems.
- **Reduced Data Dependency**: By focusing on learning from raw visual demonstrations, UniVR offers a pathway to reduce AI's dependency on meticulously curated, dense image-text pairs, potentially allowing for more scalable learning from the vast quantities of unlabeled video content available in the real world.
- **Catalyst for Future Research**: The open-sourcing of code, data, and models is intended to foster further research into visual reasoning, embodied AI, and the development of intelligent agents that can operate more effectively in complex physical environments.
