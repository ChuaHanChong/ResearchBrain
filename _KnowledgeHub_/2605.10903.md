# CapVector: Learning Transferable Capability Vectors in Parametric Space for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce CapVector, a method to extract and transfer general capabilities from advanced finetuning procedures into pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, enabling subsequent standard finetuning to achieve comparable performance with reduced computational overhead. The approach demonstrated strong out-of-distribution transferability and improved real-world robot task success rates across various VLA architectures and environments.

## Problem

- Pre-trained VLA models often lack strong generalization on complex downstream tasks, with standard supervised finetuning (SFT) proving insufficient for rapid adaptation.
- Advanced finetuning methods using auxiliary training objectives improve VLA model performance and training efficiency but introduce significant computational overhead.
- There is a trade-off between the performance benefits of advanced finetuning and the computational efficiency desired for practical VLA model deployment.

## Method

- The CapVector method extracts 'capability vectors' by computing the parameter difference between VLA models finetuned with standard SFT and auxiliary-objective SFT on the same multi-task datasets.
- These capability vectors are then merged with the original pre-trained model parameters, forming a 'capability-enhanced meta model' that serves as an improved initialization.
- A lightweight orthogonal regularization loss is applied during subsequent standard SFT on downstream tasks to preserve the transferred capabilities and prevent their degradation.

## Results

- CapVector achieved comparable or superior success rates to computationally intensive auxiliary-objective methods (e.g., Spatial Forcing) on LIBERO tasks, often requiring fewer training steps.
- Capability vectors demonstrated robust out-of-distribution transferability, improving base model performance on new environments like RoboTwin 2.0 (e.g., OpenVLA-OFT average success rate increased from 6.7% to 31.8%).
- The approach showed effective sim-to-real transfer and cross-embodiment generalization, yielding substantial improvements in real-world robot task success rates on UR3, ARX Lift 2, and AgileX Cobot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - General capabilities acquired during auxiliary-objective finetuning can be decoupled from task-specific learning and represented as transferable 'capability vectors' in the model's parametric space.
> - The quality and generalizability of extracted capability vectors are significantly influenced by the visual diversity of the capability extraction datasets, with higher diversity leading to better transferability.
> - Avoiding shortcut learning from task-relevant visual cues in extraction datasets is crucial for inducing meaningful and robust capability vectors.
