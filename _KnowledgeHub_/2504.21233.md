# Phi-4-Mini-Reasoning: Exploring the Limits of Small Reasoning Language Models in Math

> [!summary] Summary
> Microsoft researchers demonstrate that a carefully designed multi-stage training recipe combining synthetic data distillation, supervised fine-tuning, preference optimization, and reinforcement learning enables a 3.8B parameter language model (Phi-4-Mini) to outperform reasoning models nearly twice its size, achieving a 3.2-point improvement over DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B on Math-500.

## Problem

- Small language models (SLMs) struggle to match the reasoning capabilities of larger models
- Existing approaches for improving SLM reasoning lack a comprehensive, systematic training methodology
- Large reasoning-capable LLMs are computationally expensive and resource-intensive, making them impractical to deploy in resource-constrained environments despite their strong reasoning performance

## Method

- ==Four-stage training recipe== combining distillation, fine-tuning, preference learning, and reinforcement learning
- Novel implementation techniques including ==packing mode== for efficiency and ==temperature annealing== for stable RL
- ==Rollout DPO== stage builds a preference dataset by repurposing incorrect model outputs paired with correct outputs as preferred examples, without added human annotation

## Results

- Outperforms **7B-8B** parameter models on math reasoning benchmarks despite using only **3.8B** parameters, exceeding DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B by **3.2 points** and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B by **7.7 points** on Math-500
- Demonstrates stable learning curves and consistent improvements across multiple evaluation metrics
- Successfully maintains reasoning capabilities while reducing model size and computational requirements

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality Chain-of-Thought data is crucial for developing reasoning capabilities in small models
> - Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards provides more stable improvements than direct preference optimization
> - Each stage of the training recipe contributes measurably to overall performance gains

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work fits into the growing research landscape focused on improving the reasoning capabilities of language models (LMs), specifically Small Language Models (SLMs). The paper addresses a crucial challenge: while Large Language Models (LLMs) have shown remarkable reasoning abilities, they are computationally expensive and resource-intensive. SLMs offer a more efficient alternative, but their limited capacity makes it difficult to achieve comparable reasoning performance.

The paper builds upon previous work that explores various techniques to enhance SLM reasoning. Key related areas of research include:

- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting:** This technique involves prompting LMs to generate intermediate reasoning steps, which has been shown to significantly improve their ability to solve complex problems. This paper is following this general approach.
- **Distillation:** This involves training an SLM to mimic the behavior of a larger, more capable LLM. Deepseek-R1 has already demonstrated that distillation from LLM-generated synthetic data can substantially improve the reasoning ability of SLMs.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Using RL to fine-tune LMs based on reward signals that reflect the correctness of their reasoning.
- **Preference Learning:** Training LMs to align with human preferences or desired behavior by learning from pairwise comparisons of model outputs.

The authors acknowledge and build upon previous studies such as Deepseek-R1, Bespoke-Stratos-7B, OpenThinker-7B, Deepscaler, S1, and LIMO. This acknowledgment places the work within the context of ongoing efforts to improve SLM reasoning and highlights the authors' understanding of the state-of-the-art.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are:

- To develop a systematic training recipe for SLMs that can effectively enhance their reasoning capabilities.
- To validate the effectiveness of the proposed recipe by applying it to Phi-4-Mini, a compact 3.8B-parameter model.
- To demonstrate that SLMs, when trained with the right techniques, can achieve performance comparable to or even exceeding that of larger models.
- To release a concrete and detailed recipe for training SLMs to promote further research in the field.

The motivation behind this research stems from the desire to create more efficient and accessible AI systems. LLMs, while powerful, are often too large and expensive to deploy in resource-constrained environments. SLMs offer a more practical alternative, but their reasoning abilities need to be improved. This work seeks to bridge this gap by developing a training methodology that can unlock the full potential of SLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a multi-stage training recipe consisting of four key steps:

- **Large-scale Mid-Training on Diverse Distilled Long-CoT Data:** This stage involves training the base model on a large dataset of synthetic Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data generated by a larger LLM (Deepseek-R1). The goal is to equip the SLM with foundational reasoning skills. They use a packing mode, including multiple short examples in the same input sequence to increase training efficiency.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on High-Quality Long-CoT Data:** This stage involves fine-tuning the model on a smaller, more carefully curated dataset of high-quality CoT examples. They use non-packing mode in order to allow the model to decide where to stop generating.
- **Rollout DPO Leveraging a Carefully Curated Preference Dataset:** This stage utilizes Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to align the model with desired reasoning behavior. The authors create a preference dataset by repurposing incorrect outputs from the LLM-generated data, pairing them with correct outputs as preferred examples.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Verifiable Reward:** This stage involves using Reinforcement Learning (RL) to further refine the model's reasoning abilities based on a verifiable reward signal (i.e., whether the final answer is correct). This part explores different RL algorithms (PPO and GRPO) and also introduces targeted improvements such as prompt optimization, reward re-balancing via oversampling and filtering, and temperature annealing during exploration to ensure stable training.

The authors use Phi-4-Mini, a 3.8B-parameter model, as the base model for their experiments. They conduct extensive experiments to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed training recipe, comparing the resulting model (Phi-4-Mini-Reasoning) against several baseline models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main findings of the paper are:

- The proposed multi-stage training recipe is highly effective in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of SLMs.
- Phi-4-Mini-Reasoning outperforms other reasoning models nearly twice its size on math reasoning tasks, including DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B. Specifically, it exceeds DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B by 3.2 points and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B by 7.7 points on Math-500.
- Each stage of the training recipe contributes to the overall performance improvement.
- The authors' RL training technique exhibits greater stability and consistently yields meaningful improvements over the base model compared to DAPO.
- High-quality CoT data is effective to unlock strong reasoning capabilities even in resource-constrained small models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of this work lies in its demonstration that SLMs can achieve strong reasoning performance with the right training techniques. This has several potential impacts:

- **Enabling more efficient AI systems:** SLMs can be deployed in resource-constrained environments where LLMs are not feasible.
- **Democratizing access to AI:** SLMs are more accessible to researchers and developers with limited computational resources.
- **Advancing the understanding of LM reasoning:** The paper provides insights into the factors that contribute to reasoning ability in LMs and offers a blueprint for future research in this area.
- **Practical applications:** Enhanced reasoning abilities in SLMs can benefit a wide range of applications, including education, customer service, and scientific research.

The authors' release of a detailed training recipe is also significant, as it allows other researchers to replicate and build upon their work. This can accelerate progress in the field of SLM reasoning and lead to further advancements in AI technology.
