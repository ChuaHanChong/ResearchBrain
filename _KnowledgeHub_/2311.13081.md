# Learning to Fly in Seconds

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at NYU and TII developed an end-to-end deep reinforcement learning controller for quadrotors that trains in 18 seconds on a laptop, exhibiting robust zero-shot Sim2Real transfer and outperforming classical methods for agile trajectory tracking. The open-sourced framework enables reliable real-world deployment of policies trained with just 300,000 environment steps.

## Problem

- Classical quadrotor controllers require extensive, platform-specific engineering and lack generalizability.
- Deep RL policies for real-world quadrotor control suffer from a significant "Sim2Real gap" and prohibitively long training times, impeding practical deployment.
- Existing RL methods often simplify quadrotor control by using higher-level abstractions, avoiding the complexities of direct motor command (RPM) control.

## Method

- An asymmetric actor-critic deep ==reinforcement learning== architecture directly maps quadrotor states to motor RPM commands (end-to-end control).
- A custom, highly optimized C++ simulator allows for unprecedented training speed (1284 million steps/second).
- The training paradigm integrates curriculum learning, exploration noise decay, observation noise, and an action history for the actor to enhance sample efficiency and Sim2Real transfer.

## Results

- End-to-end control policies were trained in 18 seconds on a laptop, using only 300,000 environment steps, an order of magnitude faster and more sample-efficient than previous methods.
- The trained policies achieved robust zero-shot Sim2Real transfer, successfully controlling a real Crazyflie quadrotor across over 300 flights and generalizing to agile trajectory tracking tasks.
- For fast, agile trajectories, the learned controller outperformed classical PID, Geometric, Nonlinear, and INDI controllers, as well as other RL baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robust end-to-end quadrotor control, directly outputting motor commands, is achievable with deep reinforcement learning through a specialized asymmetric actor-critic architecture and action history.
> - An extremely fast, custom simulator is essential for reducing RL training times from hours to seconds, making rapid iteration and deployment feasible.
> - Curriculum learning and proper noise scheduling significantly improve training reliability and sample efficiency for real-world robotic tasks.
