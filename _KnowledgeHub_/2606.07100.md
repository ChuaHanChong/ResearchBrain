# LARA: Latent Action Representation Alignment for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The LARA (Latent Action Representation Alignment) framework jointly optimizes Latent Action Models (LAMs) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by explicitly aligning their intermediate latent representations. This approach enhances VLA model performance and generalization capabilities across various robotic manipulation benchmarks, achieving improved success rates and better adaptation to novel tasks and embodiments.

## Problem

- VLA models for robotic manipulation face challenges due to the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality labeled robot action data.
- Existing approaches decouple the training of Latent Action Models (LAMs) and VLA policies, leading to LAMs capturing spurious visual changes and VLAs being constrained by static or suboptimal LAM representations.
- This data scarcity and decoupled training limit VLA models' generalization to novel tasks or environments and can lead to overfitting.

## Method

- **LARA** proposes a joint optimization framework that explicitly aligns the intermediate latent representations of a VLA diffusion model with the continuous latent actions generated by an online Latent Action Model (LAM).
- A cosine similarity loss, often with a projection head, is used to link the VLA's internal features to the LAM's latent space, enabling reciprocal refinement during training.
- The framework employs a three-stage training pipeline: initial LAM pre-training on unlabeled videos, LARA joint pre-training on robot demonstrations for embodiment-agnostic representations, and LARA joint post-training for task-specific fine-tuning.

## Results

- LARA (full) achieved an **88.6%** average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark, an improvement of **12.1%** over OpenVLA (**76.5%**), and a **65.2%** success rate on SIMPLER-ENV, **3.8%** better than Moto-GPT (**61.4%**), using only OXE-constrained pre-training.
- As a post-training enhancement, LARA improved a state-of-the-art model, GR00T-N1.6, by **5.56%** on the real-world G1-Real benchmark (**76.0%** vs **72.0%**), demonstrating enhanced generalization to novel embodiments.
- LARA-aligned LAMs provided pseudo-labels that improved downstream Moto-GPT performance by **15.7%** on SIMPLER-ENV (**49.5%** vs **42.8%**), indicating a significant enhancement in the quality and action-centricity of latent representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Joint optimization and explicit latent action representation alignment provide bi-directional regularization, making LAMs more action-centric by grounding them with real robot actions and grounding VLA policies with forward dynamics priors.
> - Aligning the VLA's internal representations with the LAM's latent actions helps the VLA generate actions that lead to predictable and desired future states, reducing the 'hallucination' of ineffective trajectories.
> - LARA functions as a versatile, 'plug-and-play' framework, capable of enhancing existing diffusion-based VLA models, refining LAMs for improved pseudo-label generation, or serving as a comprehensive pre-training method.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is increasingly adopting Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which enable robots to predict actions directly from visual observations and natural language instructions. This paradigm shift, influenced by Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs), aims to overcome the limitations of classical task planning and control by leveraging the reasoning capabilities of advanced models. However, the performance of VLA models is contingent on the availability of large-scale, high-quality training data. Unlike visual datasets, robotic interaction data is difficult and costly to collect, and often struggles to generalize across different robot embodiments. This scarcity of real-world robotic action datasets presents a significant bottleneck for the advancement and deployment of VLA models, leading to issues such as overfitting and poor generalization to novel tasks and environments.

To mitigate this data scarcity, researchers have explored the use of abundant unlabeled human videos. Latent Action Models (LAMs) have emerged as a technique to extract valuable information from these videos by learning to predict future states and compressing visual dynamics into latent action representations. These latent representations can then serve as additional data sources or forms of supervision for VLA model learning. Prior approaches often involve multi-stage training pipelines where LAMs are either pre-trained separately and then integrated into VLA models (e.g., for pre-training or pseudo-label generation) or used to provide static representations. A key limitation of these existing methods is the decoupled nature of LAM and VLA training. This decoupling can result in LAMs learning visual changes that are irrelevant to actual robot actions (e.g., background noise), and VLA models being constrained by fixed, potentially suboptimal, LAM representations.

LARA (Latent Action Representation Alignment) is proposed to address this decoupling by introducing a joint optimization framework. It aims to create a more integrated learning process between LAMs and VLA models, allowing them to mutually benefit from each other's strengths. This work positions itself by enhancing the synergy between dynamics modeling (LAMs) and policy learning (VLAs), thereby improving action prediction quality and data efficiency in robot learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework that bridges the learning processes of Latent Action Models (LAMs) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which are typically trained separately. The motivation stems from the recognition of two principal limitations in current VLA and LAM integration:
1. **Decoupled LAM and VLA training:** Existing methods often train LAMs (for learning visual dynamics and latent actions) and VLA models (for predicting robot actions) in isolation or in sequential stages. This separation limits their ability to leverage complementary information. LAMs, when trained purely on visual dynamics, can learn to encode spurious visual changes (e.g., lighting variations, background motion) that are not causally related to effective robot actions. Simultaneously, VLA models, when constrained by frozen LAM representations, cannot influence or refine the quality of these latent actions, potentially inheriting suboptimal representations.
2. **Inefficient use of available data:** While unlabeled video data is abundant and can be leveraged by LAMs, and labeled robot action data is scarce but precise, the current methods do not fully exploit the reciprocal benefits of combining these data sources and learning paradigms. VLA models primarily focus on mapping observations and language to actions through behavior cloning, which may result in hallucinating kinematically plausible but functionally incorrect or task-irrelevant action trajectories if not properly regularized by world dynamics.

To address these issues, LARA proposes a joint optimization framework grounded in latent action representation alignment. The key objectives are:
- **Enable reciprocal benefits:** To allow LAMs to learn with the grounding of real action trajectories from VLA training, thereby focusing on control-relevant visual changes and reducing the encoding of spurious dynamics.
- **Regularize VLA learning:** To enable VLA models to incorporate forward dynamics predictions learned within LAMs, which can reduce the generation of functionally ineffective or task-irrelevant action trajectories by biasing the policy towards physically plausible and effective future states.
- **Improve data efficiency:** By jointly optimizing and aligning representations, the framework aims to make more effective use of both unlabeled video data (through LAM) and limited labeled robot data (through VLA), enhancing generalization and adaptation capabilities.
- **Develop a versatile and plug-and-play framework:** To design a method that can be flexibly integrated into various stages of VLA model deployment, including pre-training, post-training enhancement of existing models, and refinement of latent action representations in LAMs.

By achieving these objectives, LARA seeks to improve the performance, robustness, and generalizability of VLA models in robotic manipulation tasks, particularly in data-scarce real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LARA (Latent Action Representation Alignment) is a framework designed to jointly optimize Latent Action Models (LAMs) and diffusion-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models through explicit alignment of their latent representations. The methodology builds upon the principles of flow-based generative models for action prediction and existing LAM architectures.

**Background Components:**
1. **Diffusion-based VLA Models:** These models leverage VLMs to extract vision-language tokens from observations ($I_t$) and language instructions ($L$), combined with robot proprioceptive state ($s_t$), to form a conditioning input ($c_t$). A diffusion-based generative model, typically a Diffusion Transformer (DiT), then predicts a chunk of robot actions ($A_t = a_{t:t+C}$) over $C$ steps. The model optimizes a flow-matching objective ($L_{ACT}$) to predict denoising directions, enabling action generation from random noise.
2. **Latent Action Model (LAM):** LAMs are designed to learn latent action representations from unlabeled video data by encoding visual transitions between current ($I_t$) and future ($I_{t+C}$) observations. A LAM consists of three components:
 - **Inverse Dynamic Model (IDM):** Predicts a continuous latent action ($z_t$) from $(I_t, I_{t+C})$.
 - **Vector Quantizer (Quant):** Discretizes $z_t$ into a codebook token ($z_{q_t}$), following a VQ-VAE architecture.
 - **Forward Dynamic Model (FDM):** Reconstructs the future observation ($\hat{I}_{t+C}$) conditioned on the current observation ($I_t$) and the quantized latent action ($z_{q_t}$). The LAM is trained using a VQ-VAE objective ($L_{LAM}$).

**Latent Action Representation Alignment (LARA):**
LARA's core mechanism involves aligning the intermediate latent features of the diffusion VLA model with the continuous latent actions generated by the LAM.
- **Diffusion Model as Encoder-Decoder:** The flow-matching model $v_{\theta}(A^{\tau}_t, c_t)$ is conceptualized as an encoder-decoder structure, where an encoder $E_{\theta}$ extracts an intermediate latent representation $h^{\theta}_t$ (e.g., latent features between DiT layers), and a decoder $D_{\theta}$ uses $h^{\theta}_t$ to predict the target velocity.
- **Alignment Objective:** LARA introduces a representation alignment objective ($L_{LARA}$). Instead of aligning with a frozen pre-trained representation, LARA aligns the diffusion model's intermediate latent representation $h^{\theta}_t$ with the *online continuous latent action* $z^{\phi}_t$ from the LAM, *before quantization*. This is achieved through a cosine similarity loss:
 $L_{LARA}(\theta, \phi, \psi) = -E_{A_t, \epsilon, \tau} [CosSim(z^{\phi}_t, f_{\psi}(h^{\theta}_t))]$
 where $f_{\psi}(\cdot)$ is a learnable projection head.
- **Full LARA Objective:** The LARA loss is combined with both the VLA's flow-matching objective ($L_{ACT}$) and the LAM's reconstruction objective ($L_{LAM}$) to form the complete training objective:
 $L(\theta, \phi, \psi) = L_{ACT}(\theta) + w_1 L_{LARA}(\theta, \phi, \psi) + w_2 L_{LAM}(\phi)$
 where $w_1$ and $w_2$ are loss balancing hyperparameters.

**Bi-directional Regularization Effect:**
The joint optimization with this alignment loss induces complementary regularization effects:
1. **Inverse Dynamics Regularization for LAM:** By aligning LAM latent actions ($z_t$) with representations from the action policy, the LAM is encouraged to focus on control-relevant visual changes rather than irrelevant visual noise (e.g., lighting, background). This grounds the inverse visual dynamics to real actions, leading to a more action-centric LAM latent space.
2. **Forward Dynamics Grounding for Action Diffusion:** Anchoring the action-DiT representations to the LAM's forward-predictive latent actions ($z_t$) injects an explicit notion of future state evolution into the VLA policy. This biases the action model towards generating actions consistent with plausible future world states, mitigating the generation of kinematically plausible but functionally incorrect trajectories.

**Model Design (from Appendix A.1):**
- **LAM:** Adopts the Moto-GPT design, using ViT-based encoder-decoder architectures for IDM and FDM, and a 128-codebook VQ.
- **VLA Policy:** Uses a frozen Eagle-2 VLM with learnable adapters for vision-language features, embodiment-specific MLP encoders for proprioceptive states, and a 16-layer Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone. LARA alignment is typically applied at the second-to-last ($L-2$) layer of the DiT.

**LARA Training Pipeline:**
1. **LAM Pre-training:** Train the LAM on large-scale unlabeled video data (robot and internet videos) using only $L_{LAM}$ to establish a general latent action space.
2. **LARA Joint Pre-Training:** Take the pre-trained LAM, and jointly train the action diffusion model (initialized from scratch) and the LAM on robot demonstration data with action labels using the full LARA objective ($L$).
3. **LARA Joint Post-Training:** Fine-tune all models from stage 2 on target-task demonstrations for the deployment embodiment with task-specific data.

**Applications of LARA:**
LARA is designed for versatility and can be applied in different contexts:
1. **LARA Post-training Enhancement:** Apply LARA as a modular post-training procedure to an existing pre-trained diffusion-based VLA model using a pre-trained LAM for alignment.
2. **LARA for Latent Action Refinement:** The LARA-pretrained LAM can provide improved latent action tokens that serve as pseudo-labels for other latent action-based VLA frameworks (e.g., LAPA, Moto-GPT).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates LARA's efficacy across various scenarios, including full VLA training, post-training enhancement, latent action refinement, and generalization to novel tasks and embodiments.

**Experimental Settings:**
- **OXE-Constrained Comparison:** Models are pre-trained exclusively on Open-X-Embodiment (OXE) datasets, optionally with post-training on evaluation datasets.
- **Unconstrained Comparison:** No restrictions on model design or pre-training datasets.
- **Benchmarks:** LIBERO (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), SIMPLER-ENV (Pick Coke Can, Object Movement, Open & Close Drawer), GR1-Sim-24 (24 bimanual simulation tasks), and G1-Real(50) (2 real-world tasks on a Unitree G1 humanoid: Pick-n-Place, Grasp-n-Pour).

#### 4.1. LARA for Full VLA Training (OXE-Constrained)

- **LARA (full)** consistently outperformed existing VLA frameworks (OpenVLA, Octo, Moto-GPT, LAPA) when pre-trained on OXE data. It achieved an overall improvement of 12% on LIBERO and 4% on SIMPLER-ENV over the best baselines.
- Compared to a vanilla DiT baseline (LARA (DiT-only)), the full LARA pipeline achieved approximately 5% improvement on LIBERO (88.6% vs 84.4%) and 15% on SIMPLER-ENV (65.2% vs 55.8%).
- These results suggest LARA's effectiveness as a general and superior VLA model learning paradigm, demonstrating competitive performance even against larger-scale pre-trained models in the Unconstrained setting.

#### 4.2. LARA for Post-training Enhancement (Unconstrained)

- When applied as a plug-and-play post-training enhancement to the pre-trained GR00T-N1.6 model, **GR00T-N1.6-LARA** consistently improved performance across all benchmarks.
- On LIBERO, it showed a 0.6% average improvement (95.6% vs 95.0% for GR00T-N1.6).
- On SIMPLER-ENV, it showed a 1.3% average improvement (79.9% vs 78.9%).
- On GR1-Sim-24, it showed a 3.2% improvement (48.5% vs 47.0%).
- On G1-Real, it achieved a 5.56% average improvement (76.0% vs 72.0%), with notable gains in Pick-n-Place Full (84.0% vs 76.0%).
- Similar improvements were observed when LARA was applied to the 𝜋$_{0.5}$ model (Appendix B.1), with a 0.9% average improvement on LIBERO (97.8% vs 96.9%). This indicates LARA's compatibility and effectiveness across different backbone architectures.

#### 4.3. LARA for Fast Adaptation and Generalization (OXE-Constrained on Novel Embodiments)

- When LARA (full) models, pre-trained only on OXE data, were adapted to novel embodiments and tasks in GR1-Sim-24 and G1-Real(50), they showed substantial performance improvements (over ~30% on average) compared to LARA (DiT-only).
- For example, on GR1-Sim-24, LARA (full) achieved 11.4% average success rate compared to 6.4% for LARA (DiT-only). On G1-Real, LARA (full) achieved 74.0% average success compared to 56.0% for LARA (DiT-only).
- This suggests that LARA promotes learning embodiment-agnostic action representations, facilitating faster adaptation and stronger generalization to new embodiments and tasks with limited demonstration data.

#### 4.4. LARA for Latent Action Refinement

- Using the Moto-GPT framework as a testbed, LARA's ability to refine LAM latent action representations was evaluated. **LARA-LAM** (LAM refined by LARA joint training) outperformed a vanilla LAM baseline across all SIMPLER-ENV tasks, yielding an average success rate improvement of 15.7% (49.5% vs 42.8%).
- **Qualitative analysis** via attention maps (Figure 5) showed that LARA-LAM exhibited sharper focus on robot end-effectors and interaction targets, whereas the baseline LAM frequently attended to background distractors. This visual evidence supports the role of LARA as an inverse dynamics regularizer, filtering out visual noise and prioritizing task-relevant motion features.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study

- **Alignment Depth:** Aligning LARA at the second-to-last layer ($L-2$) of the DiT backbone provided the optimal balance between semantic abstraction and actionable motion for the GR00T-N1.6 model (92.5% success rate on LIBERO-Long). Earlier layers lacked semantics, while the final layer was too specialized. This optimal depth was found to be architecture-dependent, with 𝜋$_{0.5}$ performing best with alignment at the final layer ($L$).
- **Joint Optimization vs. Frozen LAM:** Jointly optimizing the LAM alongside the VLA model (92.5%) significantly outperformed using a frozen LAM as a fixed supervision target (89.0%) on LIBERO-Long. This supports the hypothesis that bidirectional information flow is critical for maximizing performance.
- **Loss Designs and Weights:** Both the LARA loss and the LAM loss were found to be essential for optimal performance, with joint training yielding the best results. Optimal loss weights ($w_1=0.01, w_2=0.02$) were identified, with larger weights leading to degraded performance.

In summary, LARA demonstrates consistent and substantial performance improvements across various robotic benchmarks and application scenarios, underscoring its utility as a framework for integrating and enhancing VLA and LAM learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LARA framework presents a novel and effective approach to addressing a critical challenge in robotic learning: the scarcity of labeled robot action data and the limitations of existing methods that decouple visual dynamics modeling from policy learning. By introducing a latent action representation alignment mechanism, LARA establishes a synergistic relationship between Latent Action Models (LAMs) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

The primary significance of this work lies in its ability to enable reciprocal benefits between these two model types. For LAMs, the joint training with VLA models grounds the learned inverse visual dynamics with actual robot action trajectories, which can help to filter out spurious visual changes (e.g., background noise, lighting variations) and focus on control-relevant features. This leads to a more robust and action-centric latent action space, as demonstrated by the improved performance of LARA-LAM and its attention maps. For VLA models, the integration of LAM's forward dynamics priors through representation alignment acts as a regularization mechanism. This regularization can mitigate the generation of kinematically plausible but functionally incorrect or task-irrelevant action trajectories, guiding the policy towards actions consistent with plausible future world states.

The versatility of LARA is a notable contribution, demonstrated by its effectiveness in three distinct application modes:
1. **Full VLA training:** LARA provides a robust training pipeline that can improve base VLA models by a considerable margin, even when pre-trained on constrained datasets. This suggests improved data efficiency.
2. **Post-training enhancement:** LARA can be integrated as a plug-and-play module to enhance the performance of already pre-trained VLA models without requiring extensive architectural modifications or full retraining. This offers a practical pathway for upgrading existing state-of-the-art models.
3. **Latent action refinement:** LARA's ability to refine the quality of latent action representations within LAMs can directly benefit other latent action-based VLA frameworks that rely on these pseudo-labels, leading to more structured and effective latent spaces.

The empirical results, including average improvements of approximately 10% for full training, 5% for post-training enhancement, and 15% for LAM refinement across simulation and real-world benchmarks, suggest the practical utility of the framework. Furthermore, the observed improvements in adaptation to novel embodiments and tasks with limited demonstration data highlight LARA's potential for enhancing generalization capabilities, which is crucial for deploying robots in diverse and unstructured environments.

The study contributes to the broader field of generalist robot learning by proposing a principled way to combine large-scale unlabeled video data with smaller, high-quality labeled robotic datasets. By facilitating a more coherent co-evolution of world models and action policies, LARA lays groundwork for future research into end-to-end learning systems that can fully leverage the vast potential of internet-scale visual data for robotic control.
