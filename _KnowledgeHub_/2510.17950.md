# RoboChallenge: Large-scale Real-robot Evaluation of Embodied Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboChallenge establishes an online, large-scale evaluation system for embodied policies, enabling reproducible real-robot testing of Vision-Language-Action models across diverse tasks. Developed by Dexmal and Hugging Face, the platform utilizes a novel "visual task reproduction" method and the Table30 benchmark to rigorously assess robot capabilities, revealing that current VLA models struggle with temporal reasoning and soft body manipulation.

## Problem

- The inherent "sim-to-real gap" often leads to robotic policies developed in simulation failing or degrading on physical robots, underscoring the necessity of real-machine testing.
- Existing real-robot evaluation systems are limited in scalability, standardization, and reproducibility, hindering comprehensive assessment of numerous models across a wide range of tasks.
- Human testers introduce significant bias and variation during real-robot evaluations, compromising the fairness and consistency of model performance assessments.

## Method

- An online "==remote robot==" paradigm with asynchronous APIs allows users to remotely control a fleet of 10 diverse real robots, decoupling model inference from robot execution.
- A "==visual task reproduction==" method standardizes initial robot states by guiding human testers to visually match a reference image, thereby reducing tester-induced variability and enhancing reproducibility.
- The **Table30** benchmark, comprising 30 varied tasks with a novel "==progress score==" (including deductions for retries), provides fine-grained performance assessment, supported by up to 1000 demonstration episodes per task.

## Results

- The Table30 benchmark effectively differentiated model performance, with `π0.5` (finetuned) consistently outperforming other evaluated VLA models across success rates and progress scores.
- Tasks requiring temporal dependence (**3%** success rate, **14%** progress score), soft body manipulation (**8%** success rate, **27%** progress score), and precise 3D localization (**18%** success rate, **38%** progress score) were identified as the most challenging for current VLA models.
- Tasks involving simple pick-and-place (**42%** success rate, **47%** progress score) and object classification/articulated object manipulation (27-**28%** success rate, 43-**44%** progress score) were found to be relatively easier for VLAs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "remote robot" paradigm coupled with "visual task reproduction" effectively enhances the accessibility, scalability, and reproducibility of large-scale real-robot evaluations for the broader research community.
> - Current state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit fundamental weaknesses in crucial capabilities such as temporal reasoning, soft body manipulation, and precise 3D localization.
> - The `π0.5` model demonstrates promising generalist capabilities, performing well even in settings with limited task-specific data, suggesting progress towards more versatile robotic agents.
