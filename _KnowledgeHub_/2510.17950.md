# RoboChallenge: Large-scale Real-robot Evaluation of Embodied Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboChallenge establishes an online, large-scale evaluation system for embodied policies, enabling reproducible real-robot testing of Vision-Language-Action models across diverse tasks. Developed by Dexmal and Hugging Face, the platform utilizes a novel "visual task reproduction" method and the Table30 benchmark to rigorously assess robot capabilities, revealing that current VLA models struggle with temporal reasoning and soft body manipulation.

## Problem

- The inherent "sim-to-real gap" often leads to robotic policies developed in simulation failing or degrading on physical robots, underscoring the necessity of real-machine testing.
- Existing real-robot evaluation systems are limited in scalability, standardization, and reproducibility, hindering comprehensive assessment of numerous models across a wide range of tasks.
- Human testers introduce significant bias and variation during real-robot evaluations, compromising the fairness and consistency of model performance assessments.

## Method

![[2510.17950-method.png]]
*Visual Task Reproduction tester UI: a reference image is superimposed on the live camera stream so the tester can adjust object positions and other factors until the live view matches, standardizing initial states across evaluations.*

- An online "==remote robot==" paradigm with asynchronous APIs allows users to remotely control a fleet of 10 diverse real robots, decoupling model inference from robot execution.
- A "==visual task reproduction==" method standardizes initial robot states by guiding human testers to visually match a reference image, thereby reducing tester-induced variability and enhancing reproducibility.
- The **Table30** benchmark, comprising 30 varied tasks with a novel "==progress score==" (including deductions for retries), provides fine-grained performance assessment, supported by up to 1000 demonstration episodes per task.

## Results

- The Table30 benchmark effectively differentiated model performance, with `π0.5` (finetuned) consistently outperforming other evaluated VLA models across success rates and progress scores.
- Tasks requiring temporal dependence (**3%** success rate, **14%** progress score), soft body manipulation (**8%** success rate, **27%** progress score), and precise 3D localization (**18%** success rate, **38%** progress score) were identified as the most challenging for current VLA models.
- Tasks involving simple pick-and-place (**42%** success rate, **47%** progress score) and object classification/articulated object manipulation (27-**28%** success rate, 43-**44%** progress score) were found to be relatively easier for VLAs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "remote robot" paradigm coupled with "visual task reproduction" effectively enhances the accessibility, scalability, and reproducibility of large-scale real-robot evaluations for the broader research community.
> - Current state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit fundamental weaknesses in crucial capabilities such as temporal reasoning, soft body manipulation, and precise 3D localization.
> - The `π0.5` model demonstrates promising generalist capabilities, performing well even in settings with limited task-specific data, suggesting progress towards more versatile robotic agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of RoboChallenge is a critical response to several evolving trends and persistent challenges in the field of robotics and artificial intelligence.

First, the rapid advancements in **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA, CogACT, and the `π` series models mentioned in the paper) have opened new avenues for developing more generalist and capable robotic policies. These models, often leveraging large-scale internet data and pre-trained foundation models, aim to allow robots to understand and execute complex instructions in diverse, open-world environments. However, their true efficacy and generalization ability can only be definitively assessed in physical, real-world settings.

Second, a longstanding challenge in robotics research is the **"sim-to-real gap."** While simulation environments offer scalability, safety, and ease of experimentation, they inherently struggle to capture the full complexity, stochasticity, and nuances of the physical world. Factors like friction, lighting variations, material properties, sensor noise, and unexpected interactions are difficult to model accurately. Consequently, policies that perform exceptionally well in simulation often fail or degrade significantly when deployed on real robots, making real-machine testing indispensable.

Third, the increasing sophistication and computational demands of VLA models necessitate **large-scale and reproducible benchmarking**. Traditional real-robot evaluation, often conducted in individual labs, is typically limited in scope, lacking standardized protocols and scalability. Existing online real-robot evaluation systems, such as RoboArena and AutoEval, while valuable, are acknowledged by the authors as typically hosting only a few tasks on a small number of machines. This limitation hinders comprehensive testing across a wide range of tasks and models, and makes fair comparisons difficult. There is an urgent need for infrastructure that can handle a "large number of models on a large number of tasks" with consistent and reproducible results.

RoboChallenge directly addresses these gaps by establishing a **large-scale, online, real-robot evaluation system**. It pioneers a scalable infrastructure that moves beyond single-robot, single-task evaluations to provide a robust platform for the systematic, real-world assessment of generalist robotic policies. By doing so, it contributes significantly to the development of embodied AI by providing the essential "reality check" needed to validate and advance VLA models, ultimately bridging the sim-to-real gap and accelerating the path towards truly capable and general-purpose robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the RoboChallenge project is to **construct and operate RoboChallenge, an online evaluation system specifically designed for large-scale, reproducible, and fair testing of robotic control algorithms on real robots.** This system aims to provide a robust infrastructure for the research community to benchmark their Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models against real-world challenges.

The motivation behind this ambitious undertaking stems from several critical needs in the field:

1. **Indispensability of Real-Machine Testing:** The authors emphasize that "testing on real machines is indispensable for robotic control algorithms." Despite advances in simulation, the real world introduces irreducible factors that digital twins cannot fully reproduce. To truly validate a robot's capabilities and its ability to generalize, real-world performance is the ultimate arbiter.

2. **Demand for Large-Scale Evaluation:** With the advent of learning-based algorithms, especially VLA models, there is an urgent need for "large-scale evaluation," meaning testing a vast number of models across a diverse and extensive set of tasks. Current real-robot testing methods are typically small-scale and bespoke, which is insufficient for the demands of modern, data-hungry AI models.

3. **Scalability and Reproducibility Challenges:** Conducting large-scale real-robot experiments in a scalable and reproducible manner is "highly non-trivial." Ensuring stable results, covering a wide range of tasks, and providing robust public access poses significant engineering and logistical hurdles that existing systems have not fully overcome. The authors highlight the difficulties in achieving fairness, consistency, and repeatability across different evaluation runs and models.

4. **Addressing Bias and Variability in Testing:** Real-robot testing is notoriously prone to variations caused by human testers, environmental conditions, and subtle differences in setup. The paper explicitly identifies how "adaptive testers" can unwittingly (or intentionally) bias results by exploiting "sweet spots" in object placement. A key motivation is to standardize the evaluation process to mitigate such biases and ensure that observed performance differences genuinely reflect model capabilities rather than testing artifacts.

5. **Advancing Generalist Robotics:** RoboChallenge aims to serve as a "necessity test" for methods aspiring to achieve general robotics. By curating a benchmark (Table30) that stresses various aspects of learning capacity, the project seeks to identify the strengths and weaknesses of current VLA models, thereby guiding future research towards developing more robust, adaptive, and truly general-purpose robotic agents.

In essence, RoboChallenge seeks to provide the essential infrastructure and standardized protocols required to accelerate the development and rigorous evaluation of next-generation robotic AI in the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RoboChallenge system is meticulously designed to offer a scalable, reproducible, and fair real-robot evaluation platform. The methodology encompasses a unique online interface, carefully selected robot platforms, and a novel evaluation protocol, along with a purpose-built benchmark.

#### 3.1. Online Interface for Serving Robots: The "Remote Robot" Paradigm

The authors depart from traditional submission paradigms (model-level, system-level, model API call) to introduce a **"remote robot" paradigm**. Instead of users submitting models or Docker images to be run on RoboChallenge's premises, users retain their models and inference logic locally. They interact with the RoboChallenge system via a set of low-level, fully asynchronous APIs.
- **Decoupled Control:** Users send "capture requests" to receive precisely timestamped observations (RGB, depth, proprioception) and "post actions" with durations to an action queue on the robot side. The robot sequentially executes actions, and the user is informed about the queue length. This allows for fine-grained, asynchronous control, supporting advanced strategies like Real-Time Action Chunking and enabling temporal alignment.
- **Accessibility and Flexibility:** This design circumvents issues like software/hardware compatibility, debugging complexity, and the need for public IP addresses, making the system accessible to a wider range of users behind Network Address Translations (NATs).
- **Job Scheduling:** The system provides APIs for job scheduling, notifying users of anticipated run times, allowing them to optimize local GPU resource allocation and pre-load models efficiently.

#### 3.2. The Robot Platforms

RoboChallenge currently operates a fleet of 10 machines across four popular robot types, chosen based on strict guidelines:
- **Durability:** Suitable for 24/7 online service (high MTBF or easily replaceable).
- **Popularity:** Established in research, with vendor support and long production lifetimes.
- **Safety:** Built-in safety features or inherently low-risk operation.
- **Performance:** Support for cyclic position control up to 100Hz and millimeter-level repeatability.
The four types include:
- **UR5:** A 6-DOF arm with a Robotiq gripper, known for industrial durability.
- **Franka Panda:** A 7-DOF arm, also with a Robotiq gripper, supporting joint and end-point control.
- **Cobot Magic Aloha:** A dual 6-DOF arm system on a moving platform, mimicking the popular Aloha system.
- **ARX-5 arm:** A 6-DOF arm.
All robots are equipped with **Intel RealSense RGBD cameras** (main, wrist, and side views) for timestamped visual and depth streams, crucial for VLA models.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Protocol

A major focus is on mitigating "dramatic variation" in real-robot test results.
- **Controlling Tester Variation:** The authors observed significant variations caused by different human testers ("experienced," "ignorant," "adaptive"). "Adaptive testers" were found to strategically place objects in "sweet spots" to maximize success rates, biasing results.
- **Visual Task Reproduction:** To standardize initial states, RoboChallenge employs a novel "controlled tester" method. Testers are presented with an augmented camera feed superimposing an initial frame from a "reference episode" (demonstration data). They adjust physical objects until the live view visually matches the reference image, ensuring consistent starting conditions across different model evaluations. This method has shown to improve stability beyond even experienced testers.
- **Background and Environmental Issues:** Acknowledging that some factors (lighting, camera intrinsics drift) cannot be perfectly controlled, the authors argue that these serve as "intended perturbations" that robust VLAs should generalize over. Empirical tests show that current VLAs are largely robust to background changes and occlusions.
- **Stability vs. Fairness:**
 - **Benchmark Protocol (Current):** Focuses on *stability* of results for individual models, measuring overall progress.
 - **Comparative Protocol (Future):** Proposed to ensure *fairness* in model comparison. This involves a post-selection procedure where the initial state is prepared, a model is randomly selected and called, and the tester oversees without knowing which model is running, thus preventing tester bias in relative model ranking. Currently, RoboChallenge serves the benchmark protocol.

#### 3.4. Table30 Benchmark

The initial benchmark, **Table30**, comprises 30 tasks designed for static armed robots operating around a fixed table.
- **Task Design Principles:** Tasks cover a range of difficulties, diverse algorithmic challenges (e.g., precise 3D localization, occlusion/multi-view, temporal dependence, multi-stage, object recognition, bimanual, soft bodies), and real-life scenarios (household, kitchen, workplace). Despite this, tasks are designed to be "simple" and "trivial" for humans.
- **Demonstration Data:** Up to 1000 episodes per task are provided (hosted on Hugging Face) for model fine-tuning.
- **Grading Protocol:** Beyond simple "success rate," a **progress score** is introduced. Tasks are divided into stages, each assigned points. Points are awarded upon completion, with deductions for retries (-0.5 points per retry). This provides a more fine-grained assessment, differentiating partial progress from full success and penalizing inefficient execution. Each task has a total of 10 progress points, and with 10 rollouts, a maximum score of 100 per task.

#### 3.5. Limitations and Known Issues

The authors transparently acknowledge limitations, including:
- **Model Verification:** The inference-on-user-side approach means RoboChallenge cannot verify if the user's claimed model matches the one actually run, or if human-in-the-loop cheating occurs. They rely on user integrity and encourage open-sourcing models.
- **Overfitting to Test Distribution:** The fixed reference test cases for visual task reproduction could potentially lead to models overfitting, though this has not been observed in practice.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The initial survey conducted using RoboChallenge's Table30 benchmark involved four popular open-source VLA algorithms: `π0`, `π0.5` (from Physical Intelligence), CogACT (Microsoft), and OpenVLA/OFT. These models were evaluated in two settings: "Task-specific" (trained on all provided demonstration data for each task) and "Generalist" (trained on ~50 samples per task, mixing data from the same machine type).

#### 4.1. Overall Model Performance

- **Clear Performance Differentiation:** The benchmark successfully differentiated between models. `π0.5` (finetuned) consistently dominated all other models across all percentiles of success rate (SR) and progress scores, demonstrating significantly stronger capabilities. This highlights that advances in foundational VLA models translate directly to better real-world robotic performance.
- **Generalist Potential:** `π0.5` showed surprisingly fair performance even in the Generalist setting (`π0.5/multi`), sometimes achieving higher scores than task-specific fine-tuned models. This indicates a promising step towards truly generalist robot models that can perform multiple tasks with limited task-specific data.
- **Distribution of Difficulties:** The cumulative distribution of SR and scores showed similar slopes across models, suggesting that Table30 tasks have a relatively even distribution of difficulties, allowing for consistent differentiation between model strengths.

#### 4.2. Analysis of Task Difficulties

The authors correlated task tags (categorizing algorithmic challenges) with averaged model performance to derive insights into current VLA limitations:
- **Most Challenging Aspects:**
 - **Temporal Dependence (SR 3%, Score 14%):** Tasks requiring models to remember past states or differentiate identical observations at different task stages proved to be extremely hard. This is attributed to the fact that all tested models are "single-frame models," lacking intrinsic temporal reasoning capabilities.
 - **Soft Body Manipulation (SR 8%, Score 27%):** Tasks involving deformable objects (e.g., folding dishcloth, wiping table) presented significant difficulty. Soft bodies deform unpredictably and often require very fine, adaptive localization of grasping points, which current models struggle with.
 - **Precise 3D Localization (SR 18%, Score 38%):** Tasks requiring precise object manipulation in 3D space (e.g., arranging flowers, putting opener in drawer) were noticeably harder than average (average SR across all tasks was 22%). This is likely due to the low input resolution (224x224) typically used by these VLAs, which limits fine-grained spatial understanding.
- **Moderate Challenges:**
 - **Bimanual, Multiview, Repeated Tasks:** Contrary to initial expectations, tasks requiring two arms, multiple camera views, or repeated skills did not show a significantly additional decrease in average performance compared to the global average. This suggests that while challenging, these factors are within the current capabilities' relative performance range or that the "global average" is already high due to the overall difficulty of the benchmark.
- **Relatively Easier Aspects:**
 - **Classification and Manipulation (SR 27-28%, Score 43-44%):** Tasks involving object classification (e.g., sorting electronic products) or manipulation of articulated objects (e.g., opening a drawer) showed slightly higher success rates than the global average. This implies that VLAs are relatively better at semantic understanding and interacting with structured environments.
 - **Simple Pick-and-Place (SR 42%, Score 47%):** As expected, tasks labeled "simple-pick" were the easiest, with strong models like `π0.5` achieving up to 90% success rates, indicating that these basic manipulation skills are largely "solved" by state-of-the-art models.

In summary, the results provided a clear hierarchy of model performance and, more importantly, illuminated fundamental weaknesses in current VLA models concerning temporal reasoning, soft body manipulation, and high-precision spatial control, while validating their capabilities in semantic understanding and basic manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboChallenge represents a significant advancement in the field of robotics and embodied AI, with far-reaching potential impacts:

1. **Standardization and Democratization of Real-Robot Evaluation:** By providing a large-scale, online platform with standardized protocols, RoboChallenge democratizes access to real-robot testing. Researchers without extensive hardware setups or engineering teams can rigorously evaluate their algorithms, fostering more equitable participation in advanced robotics research. The "Visual Task Reproduction" method is a particularly impactful contribution to standardizing test conditions, enhancing reproducibility across different evaluation runs and labs.

2. **Accelerating VLA Model Development:** RoboChallenge offers a crucial "real-world laboratory" for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The ability to quickly and reliably benchmark models against a diverse set of real-world tasks will accelerate the iterative development cycle, allowing researchers to rapidly identify strengths, pinpoint weaknesses, and iterate on model architectures and training methodologies. This direct feedback loop from real-world performance is indispensable for building truly robust and generalist robotic AI.

3. **Guiding Future Research Directions:** The detailed analysis of task difficulties in the Table30 benchmark provides invaluable insights into the persistent challenges for current VLA models. The identified hard problems—temporal reasoning, soft body manipulation, and precise 3D localization—will serve as clear targets for future research efforts, directing the community towards addressing fundamental limitations and unlocking new capabilities.

4. **Promoting Open Science and Collaboration:** The involvement of Hugging Face and the commitment to releasing demonstration data, API access, and recorded evaluation videos (via the project page `https://robochallenge.ai`) fosters a highly collaborative and transparent research environment. This open-source ethos encourages community engagement, allows for peer review of results, and facilitates the collective building of knowledge in robotics.

5. **Foundation for Robust Robotic Competitions:** The proposed "comparative protocol" lays the groundwork for future real-robot competitions focused on fairness and objective model comparison. Such competitions can act as powerful drivers of innovation, motivating researchers to push the boundaries of robotic intelligence in a standardized, real-world context.

6. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** Ultimately, RoboChallenge directly confronts the persistent sim-to-real gap. By providing a necessary platform for real-world validation, it ensures that advancements in AI translate into tangible improvements in physical robot performance, moving the field closer to deploying intelligent robots in unstructured, dynamic environments.

In conclusion, RoboChallenge is not just a benchmark; it is a critical piece of infrastructure that will shape the trajectory of embodied AI research. By making large-scale, real-robot evaluation accessible, reproducible, and informative, it empowers researchers to develop next-generation robotic policies that are not only intelligent in theory but also highly capable in practice.
