# ShareGPT4Video: Improving Video Understanding and Generation with Better Captions

> [!summary] Summary
> The ShareGPT4Video project introduces Differential Sliding-Window Captioning (DiffSW) to generate highly detailed video captions, creating a 40K video dataset and then scaling to 4.8M videos using the ShareCaptioner-Video model. This approach enhances video understanding models, achieving state-of-the-art results on multiple benchmarks, and improves the content control and temporal coherence of text-to-video generation.

## Problem

- Most existing video datasets contain brief and simplistic captions that fail to capture detailed visual information and temporal dynamics.
- Creating high-quality video captions is a time-consuming and expensive annotation process, which limits the scale of available datasets.
- Naive application of advanced AI models like GPT-4V for video captioning (e.g., by feeding multiple frames) often results in unstable outputs, temporal confusion, and loss of detail.

## Method

- Introduced ==Differential Sliding-Window Captioning (DiffSW)== using GPT-4V to efficiently capture detailed visual information and temporal dynamics by identifying changes between keyframes.
- Curated the **ShareGPT4Video** dataset comprising 40K densely captioned videos from diverse sources, employing ==semantic-based data filtering== and ==keyframe extraction==.
- Developed **ShareCaptioner-Video**, an efficient captioning model fine-tuned on the ShareGPT4Video dataset, enabling the generation of high-quality captions for 4.8 million additional videos at a significantly lower cost.

## Results

- Models trained with ShareGPT4Video data achieved state-of-the-art performance on video understanding benchmarks (TempCompass, VideoBench, MVBench), demonstrating improved temporal reasoning and detail perception.
- Text-to-video models trained on the 4.8 million video-caption pairs generated by ShareCaptioner-Video showed better content control, enhanced temporal coherence, and improved camera movement generation.
- The project created the ShareGPT4Video dataset with **40K videos**, each averaging over **300 words** in caption length, and scaled up to **4.8 million videos** using the ShareCaptioner-Video model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Capturing temporal dynamics and fine-grained visual details through a differential captioning approach is crucial for generating rich, comprehensive video descriptions.
> - The quality and depth of video captions are a critical bottleneck, and improving them directly translates to measurable advancements in both video understanding and generation tasks.
> - An efficient, scalable model can be trained to emulate complex, multi-stage LLM-based captioning processes, enabling the creation of large-scale, high-quality video-caption datasets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Video understanding and generation are rapidly evolving fields in artificial intelligence research. As Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) and Text-to-Video Models (T2VMs) continue to advance, they face a critical limitation: the quality of the video captions used for training. Most existing video datasets contain brief, simplistic captions that fail to capture the richness and temporal dynamics of video content.

The ShareGPT4Video project directly addresses this limitation by introducing a novel approach to video captioning that significantly enhances both video understanding and generation capabilities.

### 2. The Challenge of Video Captioning

Existing approaches to video captioning face several limitations:

1. **Brief and inadequate descriptions**: Most video datasets contain short captions that fail to capture detailed visual information and temporal dynamics.

2. **Challenging annotation process**: Creating high-quality video captions is time-consuming and expensive, limiting dataset scale.

3. **Ineffective AI-based captioning methods**: Naively applying models like GPT-4V to videos (by inputting multiple frames or concatenated frames) results in unstable outputs, temporal confusion, and loss of detail.

These limitations have created a significant bottleneck in advancing video-language models. The word cloud below illustrates the richness and diversity of descriptive elements needed in video captions.

### 3. Differential Sliding-Window Captioning

The core innovation of ShareGPT4Video is the Differential Sliding-Window Captioning (DiffSW) strategy. This approach efficiently captures both detailed visual information and temporal dynamics in videos:

The DiffSW process works as follows:

1. **Initial frame captioning**: Generate a detailed caption for the first keyframe using GPT-4V.

2. **Sliding window approach**: Apply a sliding window of length two to subsequent keyframes.

3. **Differential captioning**: For each window, use GPT-4V to identify changes between frames (camera movements, object movements, actions, scene transitions).

4. **Comprehensive synthesis**: Input all differential captions into GPT-4 to construct a coherent and detailed video caption.

This method provides several advantages:
- Captures fine-grained temporal changes
- Maintains spatial context across frames
- Scales efficiently to longer videos
- Produces stable, high-quality captions

The differential captions focus specifically on what has changed between frames, rather than redundantly describing unchanged elements, making the process more efficient and the final captions more informative.

### 4. ShareGPT4Video Dataset

The ShareGPT4Video dataset represents a significant contribution to the field, containing 40K densely captioned videos. The dataset features:

Key characteristics of the dataset include:

1. **Diverse sources**: Videos are collected from Panda-70M (YouTube), Pexels, Pixabay, Ego4D, Mixkit, and BDD100K, covering a wide range of content types.

2. **Semantic-based data filtering**: To ensure diversity and reduce content homogeneity, the researchers implemented a filtering strategy based on semantic embeddings.

3. **Semantic-aware keyframe extraction**: The CLIP image encoder is used to identify semantically meaningful keyframes, reducing temporal redundancy while preserving important content.

4. **Rich caption depth**: Unlike conventional datasets with 1-2 sentence captions, ShareGPT4Video captions average 300+ words per video, providing comprehensive descriptions of content, actions, and transitions.

Examples of captioned videos showcase the richness of the descriptive content.

### 5. ShareCaptioner-Video Model

To scale up video captioning beyond what would be practical with GPT-4V, the researchers developed ShareCaptioner-Video, an efficient captioning model for generating high-quality captions for arbitrary videos:

ShareCaptioner-Video is trained by fine-tuning the IXC2-4KHD model on the collected video-caption data. The model supports four key functions:

1. **Sliding Captioning**: Emulates the DiffSW strategy for new videos.
2. **Fast Captioning**: Generates captions based on all keyframes in a single pass.
3. **Clip Summarizing**: Creates concise descriptions for specific video segments.
4. **Prompt Re-Captioning**: Generates captions tailored to specific use cases (e.g., for video generation).

The model demonstrates several advantages:
- Produces captions on par with GPT-4V quality
- Operates at significantly lower cost
- Supports flexible captioning approaches
- Scales to millions of videos (4.8M in the study)

Using ShareCaptioner-Video, the researchers were able to expand their dataset dramatically, generating detailed captions for 4.8 million videos.

### 6. Applications and Results

The improved video captions directly benefit two major application areas:

#### 6.1. Impact on Video Understanding

When training Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) on ShareGPT4Video data, researchers observed consistent performance improvements across multiple benchmarks:

1. **Improved temporal reasoning**: Models showed better understanding of sequential events and cause-effect relationships.

2. **Enhanced detail perception**: Finer details in videos were more accurately identified and described.

3. **State-of-the-art results**: ShareGPT4Video-8B achieved top performance on TempCompass, VideoBench, and MVBench benchmarks.

The research demonstrates that replacing just a portion of standard training data with ShareGPT4Video data leads to significant improvements in video understanding capabilities.

#### 6.2. Impact on Video Generation

Text-to-Video Models (T2VMs) also benefit substantially from the higher-quality captions:

1. **Better content control**: Models trained with ShareGPT4Video captions demonstrate improved ability to generate content matching detailed text prompts.

2. **Enhanced temporal coherence**: Generated videos show more logical progression of actions and scenes.

3. **Improved camera movement**: Models better understand and implement camera movement descriptions (panning, zooming, etc.).

The researchers demonstrated these improvements by training a DiT-based text-to-video model on 4.8M video-caption pairs generated by ShareCaptioner-Video and comparing the results to models trained on conventional caption datasets.

### 7. Conclusion

The ShareGPT4Video project makes several significant contributions to video understanding and generation:

1. **Novel captioning strategy**: The Differential Sliding-Window Captioning approach efficiently captures both detailed visual information and temporal dynamics.

2. **High-quality dataset**: The ShareGPT4Video dataset of 40K densely captioned videos provides a valuable resource for training advanced video-language models.

3. **Efficient captioning model**: ShareCaptioner-Video enables scaling caption generation to millions of videos at reduced cost.

4. **Improved model performance**: Both video understanding and generation tasks demonstrate measurable improvements when trained with better captions.

By addressing the critical bottleneck of caption quality, ShareGPT4Video paves the way for more sophisticated video-language applications across domains like content creation, video search, education, and human-computer interaction. The approach demonstrates that the quality of training data—particularly captions—is as important as model architecture in advancing video AI capabilities.

The research team's decision to open-source their models, strategies, and annotations further ensures that their contributions will benefit the broader research community and accelerate progress in video understanding and generation.
