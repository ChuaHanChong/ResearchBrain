# PSG-Nav: Probabilistic Scene Graph Navigation via Multiverse Decision Making

> [!summary] Summary
> PSG-Nav is a navigation framework that explicitly models and leverages perception uncertainty through a 3D Probabilistic Scene Graph and a Multiverse Decision framework. The approach achieves a 12.1% increase in Success Rate over a deterministic baseline on the HM3D dataset and includes an Evidential Experience Calibrator to mitigate false positives in object goal navigation.

## Problem

- Existing embodied AI navigation systems rely on deterministic environmental representations, discarding full semantic distributions and leading to irreversible information loss from perception uncertainty.
- This loss results in inaccurate maps with potentially illogical layouts, causing high-level planners to make overconfident, unreliable decisions and leading to false positives (premature termination at incorrect goals).
- Perceptual errors from domain shift between pre-training data and real-world environments frequently cause misidentifications, hindering task success and overall reliability.

## Method

- A 3D Probabilistic Scene Graph (3D-PSG) maintains hierarchical categorical distributions for objects, groups, and rooms, preserving semantic uncertainty and rectifying illogical layouts by enumerating and pruning configurations with an LLM.
- A Multiverse Decision framework samples multiple consistent 'possible worlds' from the 3D-PSG's joint distribution, using an LLM to evaluate intrinsic uncertainty-aware exploration landmarks and marginalize perceptual noise for robust navigation decisions.
- An Evidential Experience Calibrator (EEC) acts as a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)-based module that stores past successes and failures to verify detected goal candidates and dynamically calibrate stopping confidence, mitigating false positives.

## Results

- PSG-Nav achieved a **66.1%** Success Rate (SR) and **32.1%** Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) on the HM3D dataset, an increase of **12.1%** SR over the deterministic SG-Nav baseline.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the 3D-PSG's probabilistic hierarchy and the Multiverse Decision framework's combination of spatial and semantic gain are crucial for preventing agents from getting stuck in local optima.
- The Evidential Experience Calibrator (EEC) successfully intercepted **76.9%** of false-positive stops and recovered **30.3%** of previously failed episodes on a failure-mode-specific HM3D subset, demonstrating its effectiveness in calibration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling and preserving full semantic uncertainty through a hierarchical probabilistic scene graph significantly enhances the robustness and logical consistency of environmental representations.
> - Decision-making under uncertainty benefits from considering multiple probable environmental states (a 'multiverse'), allowing for more stable, globally optimal choices by marginalizing perceptual noise through stochastic pairwise comparisons.
> - An experience-based, retrieval-augmented calibration mechanism is crucial for mitigating false positives in open-vocabulary object detection, improving navigation reliability and preventing premature task termination.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI and robotic navigation has seen advancements, particularly in open-vocabulary navigation, where agents are tasked with locating objects specified by free-form text in complex and unknown environments. This task inherently involves significant perception uncertainty, arising from semantic ambiguity in the physical world and errors within perception models.

Earlier approaches to object goal navigation (ObjectNav) frequently utilized end-to-end Reinforcement Learning (RL) or Imitation Learning (IL). While these methods allowed agents to map observations directly to actions, they often exhibited limitations in sample efficiency and real-world generalization. Modular frameworks emerged to address these issues by decoupling mapping, planning, and control components. However, many of these modular systems relied on closed-set object detectors, which restricted their ability to handle open-vocabulary tasks.

More recent advancements have integrated Foundation Models and 3D Scene Graphs (3DSGs) to enhance environmental perception and organization. Methods such as SG-Nav, ASCENT, ApexNav, and BeliefMapNav leverage these structured representations to facilitate robot reasoning about object locations and complex layouts. These methods have improved aspects like multi-floor navigation and goal-centric semantic fusion.

Despite these developments, a common characteristic among many existing frameworks is their reliance on deterministic representations. They typically discard full semantic distributions, opting instead for single, fixed labels for objects and entities within their environmental maps. This deterministic approach can lead to several challenges: information loss, inaccurate maps with potentially illogical layouts, and overconfident decisions that may result in unreliable paths or premature termination at incorrect goals (false positives). The propagation of such errors from perception to downstream planning is a known limitation.

The modeling of uncertainty in embodied decision-making is a related area of research. Traditional mapping methods have employed factor graphs or Bayesian kernels to capture metric-semantic and pose uncertainty. More recent efforts have explored Conformal Prediction for scene graph estimates and the grounding of Large Language Model (LLM) confidence in physical affordances or safety guarantees using calibration sets. Advanced methods also incorporate formal decision theory to optimize for expected utility or entropy reduction.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by addressing the challenge of unifying hierarchical probabilistic structures with open-vocabulary reasoning. It aims to overcome the limitations of deterministic scene representations by explicitly modeling and leveraging semantic uncertainty throughout the navigation process. By doing so, PSG-Nav seeks to achieve more robust and reliable navigation outcomes in the presence of perceptual ambiguity and model errors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust navigation framework that explicitly manages and leverages perception uncertainty in open-vocabulary object navigation within complex, unknown environments. The motivation stems from several limitations observed in existing approaches:

First, **perception uncertainty** is inherent in real-world scenarios due to the semantic ambiguity of objects (e.g., a sofa visually resembling a bed) and errors produced by perception models. Current navigation systems often treat noisy perceptual outputs as absolute facts, leading to deterministic labeling in maps like scene graphs. This practice results in irreversible information loss, as the full spectrum of semantic possibilities is discarded.

Second, this **loss of probabilistic depth** propagates into downstream planning. When maps are constructed with single, fixed labels, they can become inaccurate and contain illogical layouts (e.g., a "bed" appearing in a "living room" due to misclassification). High-level planners, which rely on these maps for commonsense reasoning, then make decisions based on spatially inconsistent information. This leads to unreliable decision-making and, ultimately, less effective navigation paths.

Third, the **domain shift** between pre-training datasets and real-world or simulated mesh renderings frequently causes misidentifications. These perceptual errors contribute to a high rate of **false positives**, where an agent prematurely terminates an episode near an incorrect goal object, mistaking it for the true target. This issue significantly hinders task success and overall reliability.

To address these motivations, the authors propose three core components:
1. **3D Probabilistic Scene Graph (3D-PSG):** The motivation for the 3D-PSG is to create a hierarchical environmental representation that preserves the full categorical distribution for every object node, rather than collapsing them into single labels. This probabilistic representation is intended to act as a buffer against initial perceptual noise and to rectify illogical layouts. By enumerating potential semantic combinations at group and room levels and using LLMs to prune semantically conflicting configurations, the 3D-PSG aims to maintain global semantic coherence and provide evidence for uncertainty-aware exploration.
2. **Multiverse Decision Framework:** Motivated by the need to operationalize the rich probabilistic information within the 3D-PSG, this framework aims to resolve unreliable decisions by formulating navigation as a problem of decision-making under uncertainty. It seeks to efficiently use local semantic distributions to compose and reason about optimal navigation landmarks. By sampling multiple "possible worlds" (deterministic instantiations) from the joint distribution of the 3D-PSG and evaluating landmark utility across these diverse hypotheses, the framework intends to marginalize perceptual noise and ensure decision stability.
3. **Evidential Experience Calibrator (EEC):** The primary motivation for the EEC is to mitigate false positives. Recognizing that object detection models are prone to persistent misidentifications, the EEC is designed as a verification module that enables online lifelong adaptation. By cross-validating detected goal candidates against a memory of past successes and failures, it aims to dynamically calibrate the agent's confidence, preventing premature termination at incorrect locations and promoting continued exploration when necessary.

In summary, the key objective is to enhance the robustness and reliability of open-vocabulary navigation by explicitly modeling and reasoning with perceptual uncertainty, thereby overcoming the limitations of deterministic representations and mitigating false positives through contextual verification.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Probabilistic Scene Graph Navigation (PSG-Nav) framework is designed for robust navigation under semantic ambiguity and perception uncertainty. It comprises three core components: a 3D Probabilistic Scene Graph (3D-PSG), a Multiverse Decision framework, and an Evidential Experience Calibrator (EEC).

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The work addresses the Continual Open-Vocabulary Object Navigation task. An agent is given a free-form text goal (e.g., "blue sofa") and must locate it in unseen environments. At each timestep, the agent receives a posed RGB-D observation and selects an action from a discrete action space {MOVE FORWARD, TURN LEFT, TURN RIGHT, LOOK UP, LOOK DOWN, STOP}. Success is defined as executing the STOP action within a threshold distance of a visible goal object, within a maximum time budget.

#### 3.2. 3D Probabilistic Scene Graph (3D-PSG)

The 3D-PSG represents the environment as a hierarchical multi-layered graph $G_t = (V, E)$, with three semantic tiers: Object nodes (O), Group nodes (G), and Room nodes (R). Crucially, unlike traditional scene graphs, every node in the 3D-PSG maintains a full categorical distribution $p_v \in \Delta^{C-1}$ over $C$ semantic categories.

- **Probabilistic Object Nodes:** Each object node $o_i$ has a dynamic belief represented by a count vector $n_{i,t}$. When $o_i$ is observed, the perception model's predicted label vector (e.g., one-hot encoding of the highest-confidence class) is added to $n_{i,t}$. The belief is then normalized to form $P_t(o_i = c_k)$. This "vote" accumulation strategy is used to filter transient noise and maintain full categorical distributions, avoiding issues with poorly calibrated open-vocabulary detectors.

- **Hierarchical Probabilistic Scene Graph:** To manage the combinatorial explosion of a global joint distribution, uncertainty is factorized hierarchically. Spatially proximal and semantically correlated objects are clustered into Group nodes. The probability of a specific semantic configuration $s$ for a group $g_j$ is computed as the product of the independent beliefs of its constituent objects (Eq. 2). Room-level hypotheses are formed by aggregating distributions of their child groups. This factorization (Object → Group → Room) provides a structured representation of environmental uncertainty.

#### 3.3. Multiverse Decision

This framework translates the probabilistic representations of the 3D-PSG into robust navigation actions by approximating hierarchical joint distributions via sampling discrete worlds $M = \{G^{(1)}..., G^{(K)}\}$. Each $G^{(k)}$ is a consistent, deterministic instantiation of the environment's semantic state.

- **Hierarchical Multiverse Construction:** A bottom-up process integrates probabilistic enumeration with LLM-guided logical pruning:
 - **Group-level:** Semantic configurations for each group $g_j$ are generated by enumerating constituent object-level beliefs. The top-$K_g$ most probable candidates are retained. An LLM acts as a logical filter $f_{LLM}(\cdot)$ to prune configurations exhibiting internal conflicts (e.g., "toilet in living room").
 - **Room-level:** Similar filtering is applied to combinations of refined group configurations within each room, retaining top-$K_r$ candidates and discarding those violating architectural common sense.

- **Intrinsic Uncertainty-Aware Exploration:** The environment is discretized into a candidate landmark set $L_t$, derived from the Generalized Voronoi Graph (GVG) and geometric frontiers (Appendix A.3.1). The epistemic value of each landmark $l_{i,t}$ is evaluated as $U_{gain}(l_{i,t}) = \alpha \cdot I_{spa}(l_{i,t}) + I_{sem}(l_{i,t})$.
 - **Spatial Information Discovery ($I_{spa}$):** Measures the incremental coverage of unknown space visible from $l_{i,t}$ (Eq. 4). This prioritizes landmarks that resolve geometric blind spots.
 - **Semantic Disambiguation Potential ($I_{sem}$):** Evaluates the expected reduction in semantic uncertainty at $l_{i,t}$ by aggregating Shannon entropy of proximal objects (Eq. 5). This prioritizes regions where close-range observations can effectively resolve semantic states.

- **Probabilistic Decision:**
 - **Epistemic Pruning:** A filtering threshold $\tau$ retains only high-potential landmarks $L'_t = \{l_{i,t} \in L_t \mid U_{gain}(l_{i,t}) \ge \tau\}$, focusing LLM reasoning on valuable frontiers.
 - **Stochastic Pairwise Comparison:** To address semantic uncertainty, the agent evaluates landmarks as strategic nodes within possible environmental configurations. M rounds of evaluation are performed. In each round, a deterministic scene graph $G^{(m)}$ is sampled from the multiverse M. For each landmark $l_{i,t} \in L'_t$, a context-aware descriptor $D(l_{i,t} \mid G^{(m)})$ is generated. An LLM, acting as a preference oracle $f_{LLM}(\cdot)$, compares two landmarks $l_i$ and $l_j$ to determine which is more conducive to finding the goal $g$ (Eq. 7).
 - **Pairwise Preference Oracle:** The final preference score $S(l_{i,t})$ for each landmark is derived by marginalizing these pairwise wins across the multiverse (Eq. 8). The optimal local goal $l^*$ is selected by combining this semantic preference with the intrinsic information gain: $l^* = \text{arg max}(S(l_{i,t}) + \beta U_{gain}(l_{i,t}))$.

#### 3.4. Evidential Experience Calibrator (EEC)

The EEC is a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)-based verification module designed to mitigate false positives and calibrate stopping decisions.

- **Probabilistic Context Memory:** Two persistent memory banks are maintained: a Positive Bank ($B^+$) for successful goal identifications and a Negative Bank ($B^-$) for historical false positives. Each memory entry stores a composite embedding $m = (v^{vis}_m, v^{struct}_m)$, where $v^{vis}_m$ encodes visual appearance and $v^{struct}_m = (p^R_m, p^G_m)$ encodes room-level semantic distribution ($p^R_m$) and aggregated semantic distributions of other objects within the same group ($p^G_m$).

- **Retrieval-Based Confidence Calibration:** When a candidate object $o_c$ is identified with an initial detection score $S_{det}$, its descriptor $(v_{vis}, p_G, p_R)$ is used to query the memory banks. A hybrid similarity metric (Eq. 9) combining Cosine Similarity (for visual and group features) and Jensen-Shannon Divergence (for room distributions) is used. The nearest matches from $B^+$ and $B^-$ yield $S_{pos}$ and $S_{neg}$ (Eq. 10). A calibration margin $\Delta S = S_{pos} - \gamma \cdot S_{neg}$ is computed and added to the raw confidence: $S_{final} = S_{det} + \Delta S$. The agent terminates only if $S_{final} > \delta$.

- **Diversity-Driven Update:** Memory banks are dynamically updated at the end of each episode based on ground-truth feedback. To prevent unbounded growth, a capacity limit $N_{max}$ is enforced. When capacity is reached, the entry with the highest average similarity to other samples in its bank is removed, prioritizing diversity (Eq. 11).

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

The system is evaluated in the Habitat simulator using MP3D, HM3D, and HSSD datasets. GLIP is used for open-vocabulary detection, Grounded-SAM for scene graph construction, and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct as the LLM reasoning engine. A lifelong evaluation protocol is used for EEC, updating memories across validation episodes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PSG-Nav was conducted on widely-used benchmarks: MP3D, HM3D, and HSSD, primarily focusing on the ObjectGoal Navigation task in the Habitat simulator. The experiments aimed to assess the impact of explicit semantic uncertainty modeling, individual module contributions, and real-world deployability.

#### 4.1. Main Quantitative Results

PSG-Nav demonstrated improvements over existing state-of-the-art methods in both zero-shot and lifelong object navigation settings.

- **Overall Performance:** On the HM3D dataset, PSG-Nav achieved a Success Rate (SR) of 66.1% and a Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) of 32.1%. This represents an increase of 12.1% in SR compared to SG-Nav, a deterministic baseline. On MP3D, PSG-Nav achieved 44.8% SR and 17.9% SPL. On HSSD, it obtained 67.9% SR and 33.4% SPL. These results indicate that PSG-Nav prevents agents from getting stuck in local optima.
- **Zero-Shot Performance:** A variant of PSG-Nav without the Evidential Experience Calibrator (PSG-Nav w/o EEC), representing a strict zero-shot setting, achieved competitive results. It attained 63.5% SR on HM3D, 43.3% SR on MP3D, and 66.1% SR on HSSD. This performance exceeded several competitive zero-shot methods, such as BeliefMapNav (61.4% SR on HM3D) and ApexNav (59.6% SR on HM3D), although it was slightly below ASCENT (65.4% SR on HM3D), which incorporates floor-aware exploration.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Comprehensive ablation studies were performed to quantify the contribution of each component.

- **Impact of Probabilistic Hierarchy:** The 3D-PSG and its hierarchical structure were found to be essential. Removing the 3D-PSG (reverting to deterministic labels) resulted in a notable drop in SR (e.g., -9.4% on HSSD). Furthermore, removing Group nodes (w/o Group) performed comparably poorly to the deterministic baseline (58.8% vs. 58.4% SR on HM3D). This indicates that hierarchical factorization is crucial for managing combinatorial complexity and enabling tractable reasoning over joint probabilities.
- **Ablation on Multiverse Decision:** The synergy between spatial and semantic information gain was demonstrated as fundamental. Removing both components (w/o Spa. & Sem.) led to a 4.0% drop in SR on HM3D, highlighting the importance of combining geometric exploration with semantic ambiguity resolution. The stochastic pairwise comparison strategy proved more robust than direct absolute scoring (Ranking), showing a 6.2% SR decrease on HM3D for the latter. Increasing the multiverse sampling density (K from 1 to 3) consistently improved SR (e.g., 2.8% gain on HM3D), supporting the benefit of marginalizing environmental uncertainty across multiple sampled worlds.
- **Ablation on Evidential Experience Calibrator (EEC):** The EEC significantly contributed to performance. Removing the EEC module entirely (w/o EEC) led to a decrease in SR on HM3D (63.5% vs 66.1%), affirming its role in calibration. Utilizing a pre-filled Static Memory still yielded substantial improvement, confirming the value of retrieving semantic context for filtering false positives. Quantitative analysis on a failure-mode-specific HM3D subset showed that the EEC successfully intercepted 76.9% of false-positive stops and recovered 30.3% of previously failed episodes, by correctly rejecting unreliable goal confirmations. Analysis of EEC memory capacity showed consistent gains even with small memory, with performance saturating around $N_{max}=10$.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

Qualitative experiments on a physical robot in real indoor environments further validated PSG-Nav's deployability. A visualized case showed the robot searching for a "chair." Upon misidentifying a sofa as the target, the EEC correctly rejected this false positive using past contextual experience. The robot then continued exploration and successfully located the true chair, demonstrating PSG-Nav's ability to mitigate real-world perception ambiguity and improve the reliability of stopping decisions.

#### 4.4. HM3D-OVON Benchmark

PSG-Nav also demonstrated competitive performance on the HM3D-OVON benchmark, which involves a larger set of open-vocabulary goal categories. Achieving 46.4% SR and 21.5% SPL without task-specific finetuning, it outperformed training-free baselines by over 10 absolute points in SR and was competitive with finetuned methods. This suggests that explicitly preserving semantic uncertainty is particularly beneficial in broader category spaces.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "PSG-Nav: Probabilistic Scene Graph Navigation via Multiverse Decision Making" introduces several contributions that aim to enhance the robustness and reliability of autonomous navigation systems.

A significant aspect of this work is its explicit addressing of **perception uncertainty** in open-vocabulary navigation. By proposing the 3D Probabilistic Scene Graph (3D-PSG), the authors move beyond deterministic environmental representations. This probabilistic modeling allows the agent to retain full semantic categorical distributions rather than discarding possibilities due to initial ambiguous observations. This approach can lead to more accurate and semantically coherent internal representations of the environment, which is fundamental for advanced reasoning.

The **Multiverse Decision framework** represents a novel approach to planning under uncertainty. By sampling multiple, consistent "possible worlds" from the 3D-PSG's joint distribution, the system can evaluate navigation landmarks based on their utility across diverse hypotheses. This method of aggregating decisions across a "multiverse" allows the agent to marginalize perceptual noise and make more stable, globally optimal choices, moving beyond local optimal deterministic approaches. This mechanism could have broader implications for decision-making in other AI domains where uncertainty is a significant factor.

The **Evidential Experience Calibrator (EEC)** contributes to addressing the prevalent issue of false positives in object detection and navigation. By maintaining memories of past successes and failures and using a RAG-based verification process, the EEC enables online lifelong adaptation. This self-correction mechanism reduces the likelihood of premature termination at incorrect goal locations, enhancing the trustworthiness and efficiency of autonomous agents. The ability to learn from experience and dynamically adjust confidence levels is a valuable capability for long-term robot deployment.

The experimental results demonstrate that PSG-Nav establishes new state-of-the-art performance on challenging benchmarks (MP3D, HM3D, HSSD) in both zero-shot and lifelong learning settings. The validation on a physical robot in real-world environments further supports the practical deployability of the framework, showing its potential to mitigate real-world perceptual ambiguities. The competitive performance on the HM3D-OVON benchmark also indicates the method's generalizability to broader semantic spaces and more complex open-vocabulary tasks.

Potential impacts of this research include:
- **Increased Reliability of Autonomous Agents:** By making navigation decisions more robust to perceptual uncertainty and reducing false positives, PSG-Nav contributes to the development of more reliable and safer robots for various applications, such as assistive robotics, logistics, and exploration in unknown or dynamic environments.
- **Advancements in Embodied AI:** The proposed methodology advances the understanding and implementation of uncertainty-aware planning and decision-making for embodied agents, providing a foundation for future research in areas like active perception and intrinsic motivation.
- **Foundation for Future Complex Tasks:** A more robust and semantically rich environmental understanding could enable robots to perform more complex, multi-stage tasks that require deeper reasoning about object functionality and environmental affordances.

The identified limitations, such as the reliance on 2D occupancy maps for exploration (restricting vertical navigation) and potential latency from repeated LLM-based comparisons, also point towards important avenues for future research. Extending the framework to volumetric planning and hierarchical floor-level scene graphs could further enhance its capabilities in complex, multi-level environments.
