# GlyphPrinter: Region-Grouped Direct Preference Optimization for Glyph-Accurate Visual Text Rendering

> [!summary] Summary
> GlyphPrinter improves visual text rendering by introducing Region-Grouped Direct Preference Optimization (R-GDPO) trained on a novel region-level GlyphCorrector dataset. This approach significantly enhances glyph accuracy, achieving the highest Normalized Edit Distance and Sentence Accuracy on multilingual and complex character benchmarks, with users rating glyph accuracy at 8.3084 out of 10.

## Problem

- Existing text-to-image (T2I) models struggle with high glyph accuracy, especially for complex, multilingual, or out-of-domain characters, often leading to missing or extraneous strokes.
- Reinforcement learning approaches relying on OCR/MLLMs for rewards are flawed, as these recognition models are insensitive to fine-grained glyph errors, misguiding models to accept incorrect outputs.
- Traditional image-level preference optimization is suboptimal for localized glyph errors, as it may overlook specific incorrect characters within an otherwise "good enough" image.

## Method

- A two-stage framework, **GlyphPrinter**, is developed, first fine-tuning a T2I model on text images for baseline capabilities, then optimizing it with region-level preferences.
- The GlyphCorrector dataset is constructed with fine-grained human annotations of correct and incorrect text regions, providing explicit localized preference information.
- Region-Grouped ==Direct Preference Optimization== (R-GDPO) is proposed, extending DPO to incorporate both inter-sample (across images) and intra-sample (within an image) region-level preferences, alongside Regional Reward Guidance (RRG) for inference.

## Results

- GlyphPrinter achieved the highest Normalized Edit Distance and Sentence Accuracy across both GlyphAcc-Multilingual and GlyphAcc-Complex benchmarks, outperforming several state-of-the-art methods.
- A user study rated GlyphPrinter's glyph accuracy highest at **8.3084** (out of **10**), indicating superior text fidelity with fewer extraneous or missing strokes, while maintaining competitive aesthetic and text-image alignment scores.
- Ablation studies confirmed that R-GDPO, particularly with both inter- and intra-sample preferences, and the Regional Reward Guidance (RRG) are essential for the observed performance improvements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly optimizing with fine-grained, region-level human preferences, rather than relying on insensitive text recognition models, is crucial for achieving high glyph accuracy in visual text rendering.
> - Leveraging both inter-sample and intra-sample preferences within a region-grouped optimization framework significantly enhances data utilization and model performance for localized error correction.
> - Controlling glyph accuracy during inference through a dedicated regional guidance mechanism allows for a flexible balance between text precision and overall image style without needing auxiliary networks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visual text rendering, a sub-area within image generation, aims to integrate text accurately into generated images. Current text-to-image (T2I) models have shown advancements in image synthesis but often encounter challenges in rendering text with high glyph accuracy, particularly for complex, multilingual, or out-of-domain characters. This limitation impacts the applicability of T2I models in scenarios requiring precise textual elements.

Existing methods in visual text rendering broadly fall into two categories:
1. **Prompt-guided methods**: These models are conditioned solely on text embeddings encoded by character-level or multilingual text encoders. While they can achieve a balance between stylization and glyph accuracy by training on large-scale scene text images, they often exhibit limited coverage of glyph variations. This can result in inaccurate glyphs, such as extraneous or missing strokes, especially for less common or complex characters.
2. **Glyph-image-guided methods**: These approaches leverage pre-rendered glyph images as an additional condition. While they can better handle out-of-vocabulary characters, they can sometimes lead to a rigid or "copy-and-paste" style, and still struggle with faithfully capturing intricate structural details of glyphs.

A common strategy to enhance glyph accuracy in both categories involves employing reinforcement learning (RL) techniques. These RL-based methods typically utilize text recognition systems, such as Optical Character Recognition (OCR) models or Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), to evaluate the accuracy of generated text and formulate a reward function. However, the paper identifies a significant limitation: these recognition models are often insensitive to fine-grained glyph errors. This insensitivity can lead to scenarios where images containing incorrect glyphs receive high rewards, consequently hindering the model's ability to learn and produce truly accurate text.

GlyphPrinter situates itself within this landscape by addressing the identified shortcomings. It moves away from reliance on explicit, recognition-model-dependent reward functions in RL and instead proposes a preference-based optimization approach inspired by Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). This shift aims to align generative models directly with human preferences for glyph correctness, specifically at a localized, regional level, thereby enhancing fine-grained accuracy that current recognition systems often miss. The work contributes a novel dataset and methodologies designed to optimize for region-level glyph fidelity, aiming to bridge the gap between stylized image generation and precise text rendering.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method capable of generating highly accurate glyphs for visual text rendering, especially in challenging scenarios involving complex, multilingual, or out-of-domain characters.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in existing visual text rendering techniques:

1. **Insufficient Glyph Accuracy for Complex and Diverse Characters**: Current advanced text rendering methods frequently fail to generate accurate glyphs when confronted with intricate Chinese characters, diverse multilingual texts (e.g., Japanese, Korean, Thai, Vietnamese), or non-standard elements like emojis. This deficiency restricts their practical application in many real-world contexts.
2. **Limitations of Prompt-Guided Methods**: These methods rely heavily on the training data's coverage of glyph variations. Limited coverage often results in excessive stylization that compromises glyph accuracy, leading to structural details being lost or misrepresented (e.g., extraneous or missing strokes) for less common or out-of-domain glyphs.
3. **Limitations of Glyph-Image-Guided Methods**: While these methods use glyph images to provide explicit structural guidance, they can sometimes lead to a "rigid" or "copy-and-paste" aesthetic and may not effectively integrate the text into the overall image style, nor fully capture the nuanced details of complex glyphs.
4. **Flaws in Reinforcement Learning (RL) Based Approaches**: Existing RL-based methods for text rendering typically depend on text recognition models (OCR, MLLMs) to assess text accuracy and provide rewards. However, these recognition models are often insensitive to subtle glyph errors. As illustrated in the paper, they can output identical predictions for both correct and incorrect glyphs, leading to inflated rewards for flawed outputs. This misguidance impedes the model's ability to genuinely improve glyph fidelity.
5. **Suboptimality of Image-Level Preference Optimization for Localized Errors**: Traditional DPO, while effective for overall image quality, operates at an image-level preference. In visual text rendering, glyph errors are typically localized phenomena. An image might have some correct characters and some incorrect ones. An image-level preference may deem the image "good enough" if most characters are correct, neglecting specific localized errors that still need correction. This necessitates a more granular, region-level approach to preference optimization.

To address these motivations, the research outlines the following key objectives and contributions:

- **Propose GlyphPrinter**: A novel preference-based text rendering framework designed to improve glyph accuracy by moving away from explicit, recognition-based reward models.
- **Construct GlyphCorrector Dataset**: Create a new dataset with region-level glyph preference annotations. This dataset provides fine-grained feedback on correct and incorrect glyph regions, directly supporting the learning of localized glyph correctness.
- **Design Region-Grouped Direct Preference Optimization (R-GDPO)**: Develop a region-based objective that leverages the GlyphCorrector dataset. R-GDPO optimizes for both inter-sample (preferences between regions of different images) and intra-sample (preferences between correct and incorrect regions within the same image) preferences, thereby enabling more efficient data utilization and substantial enhancement of glyph accuracy.
- **Introduce Regional Reward Guidance (RRG)**: Propose an inference-time strategy that samples from an optimal distribution with controllable glyph accuracy. This provides users with more control over the precision of the generated glyphs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GlyphPrinter framework operates as a two-stage training process followed by an inference strategy. It builds upon a pre-trained text-to-image (T2I) model, specifically Flux.1-Dev.

**Stage 1: Fine-Tuning on Text Images (Baseline Model Acquisition)**
The initial stage focuses on enhancing the T2I model's fundamental text rendering capabilities.
1. **Input Processing**: Given a prompt (P) and a set of text blocks {(T_i, P_i)} where T_i is the text and P_i is its position, each T_i is rendered into a glyph image (G_i). These G_i images are then encoded by a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) to form glyph features (F_G). The prompt embedding (F_P), noisy image feature (F_I), and F_G are concatenated to create a joint representation.
2. **Attention Mechanism**: A specialized attention mask (A) is constructed for text localization. This mask ensures that, in addition to standard prompt-image and intra-modality attentions, communication is explicitly enabled only between image features corresponding to a text region (P_i) and its respective glyph feature (F_i_G). This localizes the influence of glyph conditions to their intended visual areas.
3. **Training Data**: The model is initially trained on a large synthetic dataset, comprising multilingual characters (including Latin, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Arabic, Thai) overlaid on LAION images. Subsequently, it is fine-tuned on a smaller dataset of 30,000 high-quality realistic text images collected from the internet, which are annotated with an OCR model for text recognition and a Vision-Language Model (VLM) for image captions. Placeholders are used in captions to represent text blocks.
4. **Objective**: Training in this stage utilizes a flow matching loss, aiming to predict the velocity field for denoising the noisy image (Eq. 6). This process yields the baseline model for subsequent optimization.

**Stage 2: Region-Level Preference Optimization**
This stage addresses the suboptimality of the Stage 1 model in glyph accuracy, particularly for complex or out-of-domain characters, by leveraging human preference data.

1. **GlyphCorrector Dataset Construction**:
 - **Data Generation**: Prompt-glyph pairs are sampled from the realistic data used in Stage 1. For each pair, the original text condition is replaced with characters from a diverse pool (English letters and Chinese characters).
 - **Candidate Image Generation**: The Stage 1 baseline model is used to generate a *group* of candidate images (typically 10 per prompt-glyph pair) for each new glyph condition. Images with low overall aesthetic quality are filtered out.
 - **Region-Level Annotation**: Human annotators carefully label *correct and incorrect text regions* within each candidate image. This fine-grained annotation provides explicit region-level preference information, which is critical for learning localized glyph correctness. The dataset comprises 7,117 images from 879 prompt-glyph pairs.
2. **Region-Grouped Direct Preference Optimization (R-GDPO)**:
 - **Motivation**: Standard DPO operates on image-level preferences, which is insufficient for localized glyph errors. R-GDPO extends DPO to a region-level context.
 - **Regional RL Objective**: The denoising process is conceptualized as a multi-step Markov Decision Process, and the standard RL objective is modified to a region-level variant (Eq. 7). This leads to a regional reward function `r_m(x_0, c, M)` (Eq. 8) that evaluates the correctness of a specific region `M` within an image `x_0` under condition `c`.
 - **Training Objective**: The R-GDPO loss (Eq. 10) is designed to exploit the region-level preference pairs from GlyphCorrector. It maximizes a log-likelihood objective based on regional reward differences.
 - **Preference Masks**: To maximize data utilization, R-GDPO considers two types of preference masks for a group of images:
 - **Inter-sample preference masks (M^(+,−)_(i,j))**: Identify regions where sample `i` is correct, but sample `j` (from the same group) is incorrect. This forms winning-losing pairs across different images.
 - **Intra-sample preference masks (M^(+)_i, M^(−)_i)**: Identify correct (`M^(+)_i`) and incorrect (`M^(−)_i`) regions within the *same* image `i`, forming winning-losing pairs within a single sample.
 - The final R-GDPO objective combines these inter-sample and intra-sample losses with a weighting parameter `λ_inter`. The model from Stage 1 serves as the reference model (`p_ref`) for the DPO objective.

**Regional Reward Guidance (RRG) for Inference**
To provide greater control over glyph accuracy during inference, RRG is introduced.
1. **Conceptual Basis**: Inspired by Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), RRG adapts the guidance mechanism by treating the Stage 1 model as a reference `p_ref(x_t|c)` and the Stage 2 model as an optimized `p_θ(x_t|c)` (which implicitly represents `p(x_t|c, r)` where `r` is a "reward condition" for glyph accuracy).
2. **Guidance Formulation**: The guidance is formulated to sample from an optimal distribution `p_ref(x_0|c)exp(r(x_0,c) / (β/ω))`, where `β/ω` acts as an adjustable regularization weight controlling the desired level of glyph accuracy.
3. **Velocity Field Adaptation**: The corresponding velocity field is derived as `v*(x_t, t, c) = (1 − ω)v_ref(x_t, t, c) + ωv_θ(x_t, t, c)`.
4. **Regional Application**: To maintain background image quality, RRG applies a mask `ˆP` (derived from the overall text regions) such that the guidance `ˆv*(x_t, t, c) = ˆPv*(x_t, t, c) + (I − ˆP)v_θ(x_t, t, c)` is predominantly active in text regions, while `v_θ` (optimized for aesthetic and stylization) guides the background.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents both qualitative and quantitative results to support the efficacy of GlyphPrinter.

**Qualitative Comparisons:**
- **Simple and Complex Glyphs (Figure 4)**: For simple glyphs, most comparison methods can generate accurate characters, though AnyText2 exhibited inferior accuracy due to excessive stylization, and Glyph-ByT5-v2 tended towards rigid styles. However, for complex Chinese characters (e.g., "魑魅魍魉", "圆头耄耋", "饕餮盛宴"), methods like Qwen-Image and X-Omni struggled with accuracy, and EasyText was prone to extraneous or missing strokes. GlyphPrinter consistently demonstrated higher accuracy in preserving fine details for these complex cases.
- **Multilingual and Out-of-Domain Glyphs (Figure 5)**: When challenged with multilingual texts, emojis, and characters with stylized fonts, prompt-guided methods (X-Omni, Qwen-Image) often omitted characters or failed to render desired results when conditioned on unseen font names, attributed to limited vocabulary. Glyph-image-guided approaches (EasyText, AnyText2) showed better alignment with glyph conditions but failed to capture structural details, leading to loss of fine details in emojis and stylized text. GlyphPrinter consistently preserved glyph fidelity, indicating robust generalization ability across these challenging conditions.

**Quantitative Comparisons:**
- **Text Rendering Accuracy (Table 1)**: GlyphPrinter achieved the highest Normalized Edit Distance (NED) and Sentence Accuracy (Sen.Acc) across both the GlyphAcc-Multilingual benchmark (covering English, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, French, Vietnamese, Thai) and the GlyphAcc-Complex benchmark. This indicates superior textual correctness compared to AnyText2, EasyText, Glyph-ByT5-v2, X-Omni, and Qwen-Image.
- **User Study (Table 2)**: A user study involving 20 volunteers evaluated image aesthetic (Aes), text-image alignment (Text.Align), and glyph accuracy (Glyph.Acc) on a 1-10 scale. GlyphPrinter scored 8.3084 on Glyph.Acc, which was the highest among all methods (e.g., Qwen-Image at 7.9657, EasyText at 7.4411). This result directly supports the claim that GlyphPrinter generates more accurate glyphs with fewer extraneous or missing strokes. GlyphPrinter also achieved competitive scores for Aes (7.9143) and Text.Align (8.1692), comparable to methods built on the same base model (EasyText) and slightly lower than Qwen-Image, which is based on a different fundamental model.
- **Text-Image Alignment and Aesthetic (Table 3)**: VLM-based evaluation showed GlyphPrinter to have strong Text.Align and Aes scores (e.g., Multilingual: Text.Align 84.2992, Aes 87.3315; Complex: Text.Align 91.8969, Aes 89.5330).

**Ablation Studies (Table 4, Figure 6 & 7):**
The ablation studies systematically analyzed the contribution of key components:
- **R-GDPO Effectiveness**:
 - **Stage 1 (Baseline)**: Exhibited suboptimal performance due to limited glyph distribution in training data.
 - **SFT (Stage 2 with flow matching loss only)**: Performed comparably to Stage 1, indicating that simply adding more text data without preference optimization is insufficient.
 - **Mask-SFT (Flow matching loss only on correct text regions)**: Showed improvement over SFT but was still prone to errors (extraneous/missing strokes) due to the absence of explicit penalties for incorrect glyphs.
 - **GlyphPrinter (with R-GDPO)**: Consistently outperformed all these settings, demonstrating the effectiveness of the region-level preference optimization approach.
- **Importance of Inter- and Intra-sample Preferences**: Models trained with only inter-sample preferences (λ_inter = 1) or only intra-sample preferences (λ_inter = 0) performed worse than the full R-GDPO. This indicates that leveraging both types of region-level preference pairs contributes to more efficient data utilization and overall performance improvement, as ∆Z_m terms are not negligible for intra-sample preferences alone.
- **Regional Reward Guidance (RRG)**: The model "w/o RRG" showed subtle flaws in some small areas, while the full GlyphPrinter with RRG achieved better results, affirming RRG's role in further enhancing glyph accuracy during inference.

In summary, the main findings indicate that GlyphPrinter significantly improves glyph accuracy for visual text rendering across various challenging conditions while maintaining favorable image quality and text-image alignment, primarily due to its novel region-level preference optimization and regional guidance mechanisms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GlyphPrinter research holds significance for the field of generative AI and its applications by addressing a persistent and critical challenge: accurately rendering text in visually rich contexts.

1. **Advancement in Glyph Accuracy**: By proposing a system that can generate accurate glyphs for complex, multilingual, and out-of-domain characters, GlyphPrinter significantly improves upon the limitations of existing text-to-image models. This precision is essential for practical applications where textual fidelity is paramount, moving beyond mere stylization to exact representation.
2. **Novel Preference-Based Optimization**: The introduction of Region-Grouped Direct Preference Optimization (R-GDPO) represents a methodological advancement. It demonstrates how fine-grained, localized human preferences can be effectively incorporated into generative models, circumventing the shortcomings of reliance on insensitive text recognition systems (OCR/MLLMs) and general image-level DPO. This region-level approach can potentially be adapted to other domains requiring precise localized control in image or video generation.
3. **Introduction of GlyphCorrector Dataset**: The creation of a region-level preference dataset, GlyphCorrector, is a valuable contribution. Such datasets are crucial for training models to understand and correct localized errors, setting a precedent for similar data collection efforts in other fine-grained generative tasks.
4. **Enhanced Controllability at Inference**: Regional Reward Guidance (RRG) provides an additional layer of control during image generation. The ability to adjust glyph accuracy during inference without training auxiliary networks offers flexibility for users to balance text precision with stylistic freedom, which can be beneficial in design and creative workflows.
5. **Broadened Applicability of Text-to-Image Models**: By ensuring high glyph accuracy, GlyphPrinter expands the potential applications of T2I models. This could include generating high-quality marketing materials, personalized content, educational aids, visually consistent documents, and intricate digital art where specific text elements are crucial. The ability to render a wide array of languages and complex characters also makes it highly relevant for global applications.

**Potential Impact**: The methodology presented in GlyphPrinter could influence future research directions in aligning generative models with human perception at a sub-image level. It suggests that for tasks involving intricate details or factual correctness (e.g., generating anatomical diagrams, product labels, or historical scenes), localized preference optimization may offer a superior path to high-fidelity, controllable generation compared to purely global objectives. The framework's success highlights the importance of tailored feedback mechanisms for complex generative tasks, moving towards more reliable and human-aligned AI outputs.

**Limitations**: The authors acknowledge limitations such as reduced accuracy for very small characters due to VAE constraints and restricted ability to produce outputs with diverse aspect ratios due to fixed resolution training. These indicate areas for future research and development to further enhance the model's robustness and generalization.
