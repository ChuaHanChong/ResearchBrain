# FAST: Efficient Action Tokenization for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces a novel compression-based tokenization method for converting continuous robot actions into discrete tokens for vision-language-action models

## Problem

- Current tokenization approaches for robot actions perform poorly on high-frequency, dexterous tasks
- Simple per-dimension binning leads to highly correlated tokens that make learning difficult
- Existing methods require too many tokens to represent high-frequency action sequences

## Method

- Introduce **FAST** (==Frequency-space Action Sequence Tokenization==) based on ==discrete cosine transform (DCT)==
- Compress action sequences before tokenization to reduce correlations between tokens
- Release **FAST+**, a universal pre-trained tokenizer that works across different robots and action spaces

## Results

- **FAST** enables training of autoregressive models on high-frequency dexterous tasks that previous methods failed on
- Matches performance of state-of-the-art diffusion models while requiring **5x** less training time
- Universal tokenizer **FAST+** works effectively across diverse robot morphologies and control frequencies
- First successful zero-shot evaluation of DROID dataset policies in completely unseen environments

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robot action signals need to be compressed before training to reduce token correlations
> - Frequency-domain representation via DCT enables effective compression of smooth action trajectories
> - Simple analytical compression can outperform more complex learned compression methods

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a promising paradigm for training robotic systems that can understand natural language instructions and visual inputs to perform complex manipulation tasks. However, a critical challenge in developing effective VLA models is efficiently representing continuous robot actions as discrete tokens that language models can process. Existing approaches typically use simple binning schemes that fail to capture temporal correlations in high-frequency robot control data.

This paper introduces Frequency-space Action Sequence Tokenization (FAST), a novel action tokenization method that significantly improves VLA performance on complex manipulation tasks while dramatically increasing training efficiency. FAST leverages concepts from signal processing and compression to transform continuous robot action sequences into a more compact and learnable representation.

### 2. Background and Limitations of Current Approaches

Current Vision-Language-Action models like RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA, and π0 have demonstrated impressive capabilities in robotic control. However, they face a significant challenge in tokenizing continuous robot actions for processing by language models. Most existing approaches use naive per-dimension, per-timestep binning schemes that fail to capture temporal correlations in robot action sequences, particularly for high-frequency control data.

This limitation is clearly illustrated in a synthetic case study conducted by the researchers:

The top graph shows that as control frequency increases, naive tokenization methods experience dramatically increasing prediction errors, while frequency-based approaches maintain consistently low error rates. The bottom graphs compare predicted trajectories, demonstrating that naive tokenization methods struggle to accurately represent complex motion patterns.

### 3. The FAST Method

FAST (Frequency-space Action Sequence Tokenization) builds on the insight that robot actions are often temporally smooth and contain significant redundancy. The method compresses these action sequences using techniques from signal processing and text compression to create a more efficient representation.

The FAST tokenization process involves five key steps:

1. **Normalization**: Action sequences are normalized to a standard range
2. **Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT)**: Applied to each action dimension to convert the time series into frequency components
3. **Quantization**: The DCT coefficients are quantized to discrete values
4. **Flattening**: The matrix of coefficients is flattened into a one-dimensional vector
5. **Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE)**: The vector is further compressed using BPE to create the final token sequence

The DCT is particularly effective for this application because it concentrates most of the signal energy in the low-frequency components, allowing for efficient compression while preserving the essential characteristics of the action sequence. This approach is similar to compression techniques used in JPEG image compression.

### 4. Implementation Details

The FAST method is implemented as follows:

1. Robot action sequences are divided into fixed-length chunks (typically 1-2 seconds of data)
2. The DCT is applied to each dimension independently, producing a set of frequency coefficients
3. These coefficients are quantized to a predefined number of levels (usually 256)
4. The quantized coefficients are flattened in a specific order (prioritizing low-frequency components)
5. A trained BPE tokenizer further compresses the flattened vector

The mathematical representation of the DCT transformation for a sequence of length N is given by:

$$X_k = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x_n \cos\left[\frac{\pi}{N}\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)k\right]$$

Where $x_n$ is the input sequence and $X_k$ are the DCT coefficients.

### 5. Experimental Results

The researchers conducted extensive experiments across multiple robotic manipulation tasks to evaluate FAST's performance. The evaluation compared FAST against naive binning approaches, FSQ (a previous frequency-space quantization method), and the FAST+ universal tokenizer.

The results demonstrate that FAST consistently outperforms naive tokenization across all tasks, with particularly dramatic improvements on high-frequency control tasks like T-shirt folding and table bussing. For example, in the T-shirt folding task, the naive tokenization achieves only 1% success rate, while FAST achieves a 65% success rate.

The experiments also evaluated the compression efficiency of FAST compared to naive tokenization:

FAST achieves compression ratios ranging from 2x for simple robot arms to over 13x for more complex embodiments like OpenTeleVision humanoid robots, demonstrating its efficiency in representing action sequences.

### 6. FAST+: A Universal Action Tokenizer

Building on the success of FAST, the researchers developed FAST+, a universal action tokenizer trained on a large cross-embodied dataset of approximately one million action chunks. This universal tokenizer can be applied to new robot embodiments without task-specific training.

The reconstruction error analysis across different robot systems shows FAST+ performing competitively with task-specific tokenizers:

FAST+ provides a practical solution for researchers and practitioners who want to apply VLA models to new robot embodiments without the need for task-specific tokenizer training.

### 9. Applications and Real-World Tasks

The paper demonstrates FAST's effectiveness on a diverse set of real-world robot manipulation tasks:

Specific tasks include:

1. **T-shirt folding**: Robots must grasp and fold t-shirts of different colors and materials

2. **Table bussing**: Clearing items from a cluttered table

3. **Grocery bagging**: Placing grocery items into a paper bag

4. **Laundry folding**: Folding larger clothing items

5. **Toast retrieval**: Removing toast from a toaster

The experiments show that VLA models using FAST can successfully learn these complex manipulation tasks from demonstration data, even when the tasks require high-frequency control and dexterous manipulation.

Of particular note is FAST's ability to enable successful training on the challenging DROID dataset, which had previously been difficult to train on due to its high-frequency nature. As shown in Figure 9, the laundry folding sequence demonstrates the robot's ability to perform complex, multi-step manipulation tasks using the FAST-enhanced policy.

### 10. Conclusion

FAST represents a significant advancement in action tokenization for Vision-Language-Action models. By leveraging frequency-space transformations and compression techniques, FAST enables more efficient and effective training of VLA models for complex robot manipulation tasks.

The key advantages of FAST include:

1. **Improved performance**: Consistently outperforms existing tokenization methods across diverse tasks
2. **Training efficiency**: Enables 5x faster training compared to standard approaches
3. **Universality**: FAST+ provides a pre-trained tokenizer applicable to new robot embodiments
4. **Compression efficiency**: Achieves 2-13x compression ratios across different robot systems
5. **Real-world task capabilities**: Enables successful learning of complex dexterous manipulation tasks

FAST addresses a critical bottleneck in VLA model development, making these powerful models more practical for real-world robotic applications. The researchers have released their pre-trained universal action tokenizer, FAST+, as a HuggingFace AutoProcessor class, making it accessible to the broader research community.

By enabling more efficient and effective training of VLA models, FAST has the potential to accelerate the development of more capable and general-purpose robot systems that can understand natural language instructions and perform complex manipulation tasks in diverse environments.
