# JoyAI-RA 0.1: A Foundation Model for Robotic Autonomy

> [!summary] Summary
> JoyAI-RA 0.1 from Joy Future Academy presents a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation model designed for generalizable robotic manipulation, utilizing a multi-source, multi-level pretraining framework and a unified action space. The model achieves average success rates of 90.48% and 89.28% on the RoboTwin 2.0 Easy and Hard settings respectively, and an average success rate of 0.74 on real-world AgiBot tasks.

## Problem

- Existing robot learning datasets lack sufficient diversity to cover long-tail interactions and varied scene layouts required for robust autonomy in open-world environments.
- Significant embodiment differences across robot platforms hinder the effective transfer of learned behaviors, making it difficult to share knowledge across diverse robot types and human manipulation.
- Disparate action representations and degrees of freedom across robot embodiments and data modalities prevent directly pooling web, human-video, simulation, and real-robot data for joint policy training.

## Method

- **JoyAI-RA** uses a ==multi-source pretraining framework== that integrates diverse data: multi-modal web data, ==egocentric human manipulation videos (EgoLive)==, simulation-generated trajectories, and real-robot demonstrations.
- A ==unified camera-frame end-effector action space== is defined using a 6-DoF pose and fixed dimensionality with masking, reconciling disparate action representations across various embodiments.
- A ==multi-level pretraining strategy== involves VLM co-pretraining for visual/semantic priors, VLA co-pretraining for continuous behaviors and action-space unification, and VLA post-training for specialization to target robots.

## Results

- JoyAI-RA 0.1 achieved **90.48%** (Easy) and **89.28%** (Hard) average success rates on the RoboTwin 2.0 simulation benchmark, outperforming baselines like π0 and LingBot-VLA.
- On the RoboCasa GR1 Tabletop benchmark, the model reached an average success rate of **63.2%**, surpassing all compared prior methods (including GR00T-N1.6), with notable gains on complex, long-horizon tasks.
- On the real-world AgiBot G1 humanoid robot, JoyAI-RA attained an average success rate of **0.74** across 6 tasks, outperforming π0.5's average of **0.62**, demonstrating effective cross-embodiment knowledge transfer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating egocentric human manipulation videos (EgoLive) provides a rich source of transferable visual and behavioral priors, significantly enhancing generalization and robustness across tasks and environments.
> - The unified action space, combined with a multi-level pretraining strategy, effectively mitigates embodiment gaps, enabling knowledge transfer from heterogeneous data sources to different robot platforms.
> - Both human video and simulation data play complementary roles in the co-pretraining framework, with simulation data contributing to reducing distribution shifts and enriching cross-embodiment action diversity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic autonomy in open-world environments is currently characterized by limitations related to data diversity and generalization across different robot embodiments. Prior research has indicated that scaling data and model capacity can enhance robot performance, particularly with the advent of vision-language-action (VLA) models that integrate semantic understanding with action generation. Notable contributions in this area include systems such as RT-1, Open X-Embodiment, OpenVLA, π0, LingBot-VLA, GR00T N1, and Gemini Robotics, which have expanded the scale of robot learning datasets and explored VLA architectures.

Despite these advancements, two challenges persist: the high cost and operational constraints of collecting extensive, high-quality robot interaction data, which leads to insufficient coverage of diverse interactions and failure modes; and the "embodiment gap," which impedes effective knowledge transfer between robots with substantially different physical characteristics. Recent efforts have begun to address cross-embodiment scaling and broader pretraining, with works like RoboVLMs investigating backbone choices and VLA-Adapter focusing on efficient adaptation. Models such as Motus and ABot-M0 have explored mixture-of-transformer architectures and action manifold learning for improved policy generation.

JoyAI-RA positions itself within this landscape by directly addressing these data diversity and embodiment gap challenges. It proposes a multi-source, multi-level pretraining framework that integrates a wider array of data types, including web data, egocentric human manipulation videos, simulation data, and real-robot data. Distinct from approaches that primarily rely on scaling continuous robot control, JoyAI-RA emphasizes a structured multi-stage training recipe that includes broad multi-modal co-pretraining, cross-embodiment action alignment, and a dedicated action expert. This systematic approach aims to enhance generalizable robotic manipulation by leveraging the complementary strengths of heterogeneous data sources and explicitly unifying action representations to facilitate knowledge transfer across diverse embodiments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a vision-language-action (VLA) embodied foundation model, named JoyAI-RA, capable of generalizable robotic manipulation in open-world environments. This objective is motivated by two primary limitations in current robotic autonomy research:

1. **Insufficient Data Diversity and Task Coverage:** Existing robotic datasets are often limited in scale and scope, failing to capture the extensive variety of interactions, rare failure modes, and diverse scene layouts encountered in real-world settings. This sparsity of data restricts the breadth of behavioral priors that robotic policies can acquire, subsequently reducing their robustness when encountering novel or out-of-distribution scenarios. The goal is to overcome this by incorporating a large-scale, heterogeneous dataset that expands coverage across diverse tasks, scenes, and environments.

2. **Embodiment Gap for Multi-Source Behavior Knowledge Sharing:** Significant differences in physical characteristics and action spaces across various robot embodiments, as well as between human manipulation and robotic control, impede the effective transfer of learned behaviors. This "embodiment gap" makes it challenging to leverage scalable data sources like human videos or simulation for real-world robot deployment. The research aims to bridge this gap through explicit action-space unification and a structured multi-level pretraining strategy, enabling more effective knowledge transfer.

By addressing these challenges, JoyAI-RA seeks to enable robots to acquire a broader range of robust and generalizable manipulation skills, moving closer to achieving robust autonomy in complex, unconstrained environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

JoyAI-RA employs a multi-source, multi-level pretraining framework combined with a unified action space to enable generalizable robotic manipulation.

#### 3.1. Overview

JoyAI-RA integrates diverse data sources: real-robot demonstrations, large-scale simulation data, egocentric human videos, and multi-modal web data. The model receives multimodal inputs including language instructions, visual observations, and proprioceptive states, and predicts temporally consistent action sequences. A key aspect of the approach is the introduction of a unified action space to reconcile disparate action representations across embodiments, facilitating cross-embodiment knowledge transfer. The model architecture is designed to handle multimodal inputs and generate continuous actions, supported by a multi-stage training paradigm.

#### 3.2. Dataset

The training datasets for JoyAI-RA are organized into four complementary sources:

- **Multi-Modal Web Data:** This data constitutes a semantic layer, incorporating large-scale visual instruction-tuning data from sources like Cambrian-10M, RefSpatial, Galaxea, and Cosmos-Reason1-SFT. While lacking executable trajectories, it enhances the model’s perceptual and linguistic understanding, which is considered essential for open-world manipulation.
- **Egocentric Human Manipulation Data (EgoLive):** An in-house dataset collected with RGB cameras at 60 FPS, EgoLive provides diverse coverage with 1,969 object categories and 1,796 action categories across over 10,000 tasks (e.g., household, retail, logistics scenarios). It includes fine-grained per-frame subtask annotations and high-level task descriptions. Hand-pose estimation pipelines are used to recover human hand trajectories, which are then retargeted to multiple robot embodiments (ALOHA, Fourier, Agibot G1).
- **Simulation Data:** This serves as a foundation for robotic action learning, bridging semantic pretraining and real-world policy learning. JoyAI-RA leverages curated subsets from synthetic robotic datasets such as InternData-A1, GenieSim3.0-Dataset, and InternData-M1, providing scalable and embodiment-aware supervision.
- **Real-Robot Data (JDAgibot):** This combines open-source multi-embodiment datasets (Open-X-Embodiment, AgiBot-World, Galaxea Open-World Dataset) with an in-house self-collected dataset, JDAgibot. Real-robot trajectories capture physical deployment challenges like contact uncertainty, sensing noise, and hardware constraints, contributing to learning robust policies.

#### 3.3. Unified Action Space

To reconcile incompatible action representations from diverse data sources and embodiments, JoyAI-RA defines a unified action space:

- **Camera-Frame End-Effector Representation:** End-effector states and actions are represented as 6-DoF poses (3D translation, 3D axis-angle rotation) in the camera coordinate frame. This provides consistent physical semantics regardless of the robot's base placement or kinematic configuration and aligns with visual observations.
- **Unified Action Dimensionality:** A fixed-length action vector covers all actuator groups (e.g., left/right arms, dexterous hands, grippers) across different robot morphologies. Dimensions without a corresponding degree of freedom for a specific embodiment are masked during training, allowing a single representation to be used across heterogeneous systems.

#### 3.4. Model Architecture

JoyAI-RA features a modular design:

- **Vision-Language Model (VLM):** Handles vision-language understanding, producing spatially grounded multimodal representations from multi-view observations and language inputs. These representations encode semantic and geometric context.
- **Perception-Action Expert:** Built upon a Perceiver architecture, this component generates continuous actions. It processes the VLM's representations as contextual inputs. The model jointly predicts tokenized textual outputs (e.g., subtasks) and continuous action chunks.
- **Continuous Control Formulation:** The perception-action expert is formulated as a conditional velocity field predictor within a flow-matching framework. It learns to estimate the velocity of noisy action latents. The latent sequence is formed by concatenating proprioceptive states, learnable future tokens, and timestep-conditioned noisy action features. The expert `f_theta` predicts a velocity field over the action chunk, utilizing time-aware Perceiver attention blocks with timestep-adaptive normalization (AdaLN) to modulate perceptual features based on the denoising timestep.

#### 3.5. Training Recipe

Training is structured into three progressive phases to learn and consolidate knowledge across heterogeneous data sources:

- **VLM Co-Pretraining:** This initial stage trains the VLM on a diverse mixture of data sources (General VQA, Embodied VQA, cross-embodiment action data with discrete action generation, and human video data). The objective is to establish broad visual understanding, spatial reasoning, long-horizon planning, and initial tokenized action prediction capabilities by minimizing the negative log-likelihood of target tokens.
- **VLA Co-Pretraining:** In this phase, General VQA and Embodied VQA data are used to maintain basic competencies and spatial reasoning. For action supervision, simulated robot trajectories, real-robot demonstrations, and retargeted human videos are used, all in the unified action space. The training objective combines the autoregressive loss from the first stage with an additional flow matching loss for the action expert, balancing vision-language modeling with continuous action prediction.
- **VLA Post-Training:** This final stage specializes the pretrained policy to the target robot embodiment. Only data collected from the target robot is used. The auxiliary autoregressive objective is discarded, and the model is optimized solely with the flow matching objective over continuous target-robot actions. All model parameters are updated end-to-end to adapt visuomotor representations and action generation to specific deployment settings.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of JoyAI-RA was conducted across simulation and real-world benchmarks, with comprehensive ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Simulation Benchmark Results

- **RoboTwin 2.0:** JoyAI-RA achieved average success rates of 90.48% in the Easy setting and 89.28% in the Hard setting. This performance exceeded all comparison baselines, including π0, π0.5, Motus, and LingBot-VLA. The model demonstrated a 100% success rate on tasks such as Adjust Bottle, Grab Roller, and Place Empty Cup. These results suggest that the unified action space and multi-stage training facilitate effective modeling of diverse manipulation patterns and enhance generalization under scene randomization.
- **RoboCasa GR1 Tabletop:** JoyAI-RA established an average success rate of 63.2%, outperforming prior methods such as GR00T-N1.6, Qwen3PI, TwinBrainVLA, DualCoT-VLA, ABot-M0, and Being-H0.7. Notable improvements were observed on complex, long-horizon tasks, including CanToDrawerClose (+16.0 percentage points over the previous best), MilkToMicrowaveClose (+24.0), and TrayToPot (+18.0). These gains indicate the model's effectiveness in sequential reasoning and precise object manipulation across varied task distributions.

#### 4.2. Real-World AgiBot Benchmark Results

On the AgiBot G1 humanoid robot platform, JoyAI-RA improved the cross-task average success rate from 0.62 (π0.5) to 0.74 across six household tasks. The largest performance margins were observed on tasks requiring accurate target recognition and precise placement, such as Headphones and Remedy. Improvements were also noted on Mouse and Food Scraps. However, π0.5 maintained higher performance on Cup and Croissant tasks, indicating areas for potential further development in long-horizon visual reasoning and graded sequential manipulation.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness Analysis of EgoLive Data

Ablation studies on the RoboTwin 2.0 simulation benchmark and real-world AgiBot benchmark explored the contribution of the EgoLive egocentric human video data:

- **RoboTwin 2.0 Simulation:** Pretraining with the full EgoLive and JDAgibot datasets resulted in an 87.4% average success rate, surpassing both training from scratch (81.64%) and pretraining with only JDAgibot (77.62%). Using only 10% of EgoLive provided less benefit, suggesting that the dataset's contribution scales with its volume. The benefits were observed across tasks requiring spatial reasoning, complex interaction, and fine-grained placement.
- **Real-World AgiBot:** Integrating human egocentric data improved the real-robot performance, increasing the average score from 0.56 to 0.74. This indicates that EgoLive provides transferable priors for real-world execution, particularly for tasks where semantic grounding and accurate object-to-target interaction are critical (e.g., Headphones and Remedy).

#### 4.4. Generalization Analysis of In-Domain EgoLive Data

A comparison of models with and without in-domain EgoLive data for post-training showed that the inclusion of such data generally led to a modest overall gain in success rate across the AgiBot benchmark, with pronounced improvements on tasks like Remedy and Headphones. This was attributed to strong alignment between in-domain videos and target tasks. Conversely, on Mouse and Food Scraps, performance was sometimes lower with in-domain data, potentially due to distributional mismatches.

#### 4.5. Comparison of EgoLive and EgoDex Data

When compared with EgoDex, both EgoLive (87.16%) and EgoDex (86.88%) individually improved performance over a baseline on RoboTwin 2.0. EgoLive, characterized by long-horizon, continuous human interactions, was found to offer richer temporal structure and more realistic motion patterns. Semantic analysis indicated EgoLive had broader vocabulary coverage and richer semantic diversity, spanning a larger and more continuous region in semantic space. Combining both datasets further enhanced performance to 89.30%, suggesting their complementary nature.

#### 4.6. Validity Analysis of Co-Pretraining Framework

Ablation studies validated the two-stage co-pretraining framework:

- Both VLM Co-Pretraining (87.84%) and VLA Co-Pretraining (87.42%) independently contributed to performance improvements on RoboTwin 2.0 (Easy) compared to the VLA Post-Training baseline (81.28%).
- Combining both stages with human data yielded a 90.48% success rate.
- Removing simulation data during VLA co-pretraining resulted in a 1.14 percentage point performance drop (from 90.24% to 89.10%), underscoring the role of simulation data in aligning visual distributions and providing cross-embodiment action diversity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents JoyAI-RA as a vision-language-action embodied foundation model that addresses key limitations in achieving generalizable robotic autonomy. By introducing a multi-source, multi-level pretraining framework, the research demonstrates a method for integrating diverse data streams—ranging from web content to real-robot demonstrations—and leveraging their complementary strengths. The proposed action-space unification is a contribution to mitigating the embodiment gap, which has been a persistent challenge in transferring learned behaviors across different robot platforms.

The consistent outperformance of JoyAI-RA on both simulation (RoboTwin 2.0, RoboCasa GR1 Tabletop) and real-world (AgiBot G1) benchmarks indicates the effectiveness of the proposed methodological components. The detailed ablation studies, particularly on the EgoLive dataset, provide insights into the value of large-scale egocentric human videos and the importance of data diversity and scale for learning robust manipulation priors. The findings suggest that a structured approach to heterogeneous data integration and explicit action alignment can facilitate the transfer of manipulation knowledge.

The development of the in-house EgoLive dataset and the Real-World AgiBot Benchmark also provides new resources that could support future research in generalizable robot learning and evaluation. JoyAI-RA offers a potential trajectory toward more adaptable and capable embodied intelligence systems by enabling robots to operate effectively in complex, open-world environments.
