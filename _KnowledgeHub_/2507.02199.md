# Latent Chain-of-Thought? Decoding the Depth-Recurrent Transformer

> [!summary] Summary
> A study investigated whether depth-recurrent Transformers, exemplified by Huginn-3.5B, perform structured "latent chain-of-thought" reasoning, finding limited evidence for such a mechanism. The research revealed inconsistencies in interpretability tool behavior across the model's distinct architectural blocks and showed that increased recurrence depth yields only marginal performance improvements compared to explicit Chain-of-Thought prompting.

## Problem

- Explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, while effective for complex reasoning in LLMs, introduces verbosity and slows inference, limiting real-world application.
- The nature of "latent reasoning" in recurrent architectures, designed to enhance internal processing, remains ambiguous; it's unclear if performance gains stem from genuine structured reasoning or merely iterative computation.
- Existing interpretability techniques struggle to consistently explain the internal workings of complex, novel LLM architectures, leaving their internal reasoning mechanisms as a black box.

## Method

- Employs a systematic probing approach targeting ==Huginn-3.5B==, a depth-recurrent Transformer, focusing on arithmetic tasks where explicit Chain-of-Thought is suppressed.
- Utilizes and adapts interpretability techniques, including the standard ==Logit Lens== and a novel ==Coda Lens== specific to Huginn's architecture, to decode and analyze hidden states across unrolled recurrent layers.
- Traces the rank trajectories of intermediate and final result tokens within arithmetic problems and benchmarks model accuracy on GSM8K with varying recurrence depths against explicit CoT performance.

## Results

- Huginn's internal representations showed sharp discontinuities and periodic oscillations in decoded token rank trajectories, with interpretability varying significantly by layer and probing method.
- Analysis of token rank trajectories offered limited evidence for structured latent Chain-of-Thought, as final answers converged quickly, and no clear "phase separation" for intermediate reasoning steps was observed.
- On the GSM8K benchmark, increasing recurrence depth yielded only marginal accuracy gains (e.g., from **3.11%** to **4.93%**) when explicit CoT was suppressed, falling substantially short of performance achieved with explicit CoT (**24.87%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Interpretability methods must be carefully adapted for complex, non-standard Transformer architectures, as tools like Logit Lens can show inconsistent behavior across different recurrent blocks.
> - The specific design of recurrent blocks can lead to distinct encoding patterns, with modules like Huginn's R4 block playing a unique dual role in processing and output, requiring tailored probing.
> - Simply increasing computational depth through recurrence does not automatically induce structured, interpretable latent Chain-of-Thought reasoning that rivals the efficacy of explicit, language-based CoT.
