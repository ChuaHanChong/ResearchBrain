# Latent Chain-of-Thought? Decoding the Depth-Recurrent Transformer

> [!summary] Summary
> A study investigated whether depth-recurrent Transformers, exemplified by Huginn-3.5B, perform structured "latent chain-of-thought" reasoning, finding limited evidence for such a mechanism. The research revealed inconsistencies in interpretability tool behavior across the model's distinct architectural blocks and showed that increased recurrence depth yields only marginal performance improvements compared to explicit Chain-of-Thought prompting.

## Problem

- Explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, while effective for complex reasoning in LLMs, introduces verbosity and slows inference, limiting real-world application.
- The nature of "latent reasoning" in recurrent architectures, designed to enhance internal processing, remains ambiguous; it's unclear if performance gains stem from genuine structured reasoning or merely iterative computation.
- Existing interpretability techniques struggle to consistently explain the internal workings of complex, novel LLM architectures, leaving their internal reasoning mechanisms as a black box.

## Method

- Employs a systematic probing approach targeting ==Huginn-3.5B==, a depth-recurrent Transformer, focusing on arithmetic tasks where explicit Chain-of-Thought is suppressed.
- Utilizes and adapts interpretability techniques, including the standard ==Logit Lens== and a novel ==Coda Lens== specific to Huginn's architecture, to decode and analyze hidden states across unrolled recurrent layers.
- Traces the rank trajectories of intermediate and final result tokens within arithmetic problems and benchmarks model accuracy on GSM8K with varying recurrence depths against explicit CoT performance.

## Results

- Huginn's internal representations showed sharp discontinuities and periodic oscillations in decoded token rank trajectories, with interpretability varying significantly by layer and probing method.
- Analysis of token rank trajectories offered limited evidence for structured latent Chain-of-Thought, as final answers converged quickly, and no clear "phase separation" for intermediate reasoning steps was observed.
- On the GSM8K benchmark, increasing recurrence depth yielded only marginal accuracy gains (e.g., from **3.11%** to **4.93%**) when explicit CoT was suppressed, falling substantially short of performance achieved with explicit CoT (**24.87%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Interpretability methods must be carefully adapted for complex, non-standard Transformer architectures, as tools like Logit Lens can show inconsistent behavior across different recurrent blocks.
> - The specific design of recurrent blocks can lead to distinct encoding patterns, with modules like Huginn's R4 block playing a unique dual role in processing and output, requiring tailored probing.
> - Simply increasing computational depth through recurrence does not automatically induce structured, interpretable latent Chain-of-Thought reasoning that rivals the efficacy of explicit, language-based CoT.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of large language model capabilities, architectural innovation, and interpretability research. Its core contribution lies in scrutinizing the internal reasoning mechanisms of "depth-recurrent Transformers," a class of models designed to enhance reasoning capabilities without increasing parameter count.

**The Landscape of LLM Reasoning:**
Modern LLMs have achieved remarkable success in complex reasoning and planning tasks. A significant enabler of this success has been "Chain-of-Thought" (CoT) prompting (Wei et al., 2022), where models are explicitly guided to articulate intermediate reasoning steps in natural language. While effective, explicit CoT introduces verbosity and can slow down inference, which is a key limitation for real-world applications.

**The Quest for Latent Reasoning:**
To address the shortcomings of explicit CoT, researchers have explored methods for "latent reasoning" – where reasoning steps are performed internally within the model's hidden states, without being externalized in human-readable language. This promises greater efficiency and the potential to capture reasoning processes that might not be easily verbalized. Recurrent architectures have emerged as a promising approach for this, as they inherently allow for iterative processing of information in a continuous latent space (Hao et al., 2024).

**The Role of Depth-Recurrent Transformers:**
The Huginn-3.5B model (Geiping et al., 2025) is a prime example of a depth-recurrent Transformer that reuses layers during inference to increase computational depth per token. This architectural choice is hypothesized to facilitate more complex internal processing, potentially leading to emergent latent CoT. However, simply observing improved performance in such models does not clarify *why* it occurs – is it genuine, structured, multi-step reasoning (latent CoT), or merely a more refined iterative computation?

**The Contribution to Interpretability:**
This paper directly addresses this ambiguity. It uses established interpretability techniques, such as the Logit Lens (nostalgebraist, 2021; Geva et al., 2022; 2023) and introduces a "Coda Lens" (tailored to Huginn's architecture), to probe the internal hidden states of Huginn-3.5B. By analyzing how intermediate and final result tokens are represented and tracked across recurrent steps, the authors aim to determine if a CoT-like reasoning structure manifests latently. The work builds upon previous efforts to understand how LLMs perform multi-hop reasoning (Yang et al., 2024) but applies them to the novel context of depth-recurrent architectures.

In essence, this research scrutinizes whether a proposed architectural solution for latent reasoning (depth-recurrence) actually delivers on the promise of emergent CoT. It adds a critical voice to the ongoing debate about the nature of reasoning in LLMs, highlighting the challenges of interpreting complex internal states and pushing the boundaries of what current interpretability tools can reveal about models' "thought processes."

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate whether a depth-recurrent Transformer, specifically Huginn-3.5B, exhibits signs of "latent chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning" during inference when explicit reasoning is suppressed.

The motivation for this investigation stems from several crucial observations and open questions in the field of large language models:

- **Limitations of Explicit CoT:** While explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting has demonstrably improved LLM performance on complex tasks requiring multi-step reasoning and planning, it comes with significant drawbacks. Articulating intermediate steps in natural language leads to increased verbosity, longer inference times, and potentially limits the model's ability to express reasoning that is not easily verbalized.
- **Promise of Latent Reasoning:** The prospect of internalizing reasoning in a latent space – where models perform multi-step computations without externalizing them in language – is highly appealing. Such an approach could offer greater efficiency and potentially unlock more sophisticated, non-linguistic forms of reasoning.
- **Ambiguity in Recurrent Architectures' Success:** Depth-recurrent transformers, like Huginn-3.5B, are designed to leverage layer reuse to increase computational depth without inflating parameter counts. These models have shown improved performance on reasoning tasks. However, it remains unclear *what* precisely drives this improvement. Is it a mere iterative refinement of representations, or does it genuinely lead to the emergence of structured, CoT-like reasoning processes in their hidden states?
- **Need for Interpretability:** To understand the true nature of these models' internal workings, robust interpretability techniques are essential. Without such tools, the underlying mechanisms remain a black box, making it difficult to ascertain if the promise of latent CoT is being met or if alternative forms of computation are at play.

To address these motivations, the authors set forth the following specific objectives, framed as key contributions:

1. **Uncover Probing Inconsistencies:** To systematically examine how interpretability techniques (Logit Lens, Coda Lens) behave across the distinct blocks (Prelude, Recurrent, Coda) of Huginn's depth-recurrent architecture. This aims to reveal whether the internal representations maintain consistent semantic meaning or exhibit discontinuities.
2. **Evaluate Evidence for Latent CoT via Token Trajectories:** To trace the rank trajectories of "signature tokens" (intermediate and final result tokens) within arithmetic problems. The goal is to identify if there is a temporal separation in the emergence of these tokens, which would be indicative of a multi-step, CoT-like reasoning process.
3. **Benchmark Recurrence Depth against Explicit CoT:** To empirically assess whether increasing the number of recurrent steps in Huginn, while suppressing explicit CoT, can match the performance gains achieved by models that utilize explicit CoT prompting. This macroscopic evaluation serves as a reality check for the efficacy of latent reasoning in depth-recurrent models.

Through these objectives, the research seeks to shed light on the elusive concept of latent CoT, providing empirical evidence for its presence or absence in a state-of-the-art recurrent LLM architecture.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a systematic approach to probe the internal dynamics of Huginn-3.5B, a depth-recurrent Transformer, focusing on arithmetic tasks where explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) is suppressed.

**Model Under Investigation:**
The target model is Huginn-3.5B, characterized by its "depth-recurrent" architecture. This model comprises 2 Prelude blocks, 4 Recurrent Blocks ({R1, R2, R3, R4}), and 2 Coda blocks ({C1, C2}). The key feature is the reuse of these recurrent blocks `r` times during inference, effectively increasing computational depth without adding parameters. The authors adopt an "unrolled view" of the architecture, allowing them to track hidden states across `2 + 4r + 2` effective blocks, where `r` is the number of recurrent steps. A random vector is used to initialize the first recurrent pass.

**Decoding Hidden States (Probing Techniques):**
To interpret the hidden states (s_i) produced by each block, two primary "lens" techniques are utilized:

1. **Logit Lens:** This is a standard interpretability technique. For each hidden state, it applies RMS normalization, followed by projection through the model's unembedding matrix (W_U) to produce logits in the vocabulary space. The analysis focuses on the logits of the last token position (z_i[-1]), which reflects the model's current prediction. This method assumes that intermediate hidden states directly encode information relevant to token prediction.
2. **Coda Lens:** Unique to this study, the Coda Lens leverages Huginn's specialized Coda module. This module, consisting of two Transformer blocks, is designed to map the final recurrent block output to logits. The Coda Lens applies normalization, passes the hidden state through the entire Coda module (C), normalizes again, and then projects through the unembedding matrix. This approach aims to provide a potentially more "faithful" or semantically aligned decoding, as it uses a learned component of the model specifically designed for output generation.

**Experimental Setup for Mathematical Reasoning:**

- **Datasets:**
 - **Synthetic Arithmetic Test Data:** Primarily used for fine-grained internal analysis. This involves one-digit composite arithmetic tasks (e.g., "What is (9 + 8) * 2?"). The choice of this simple dataset is justified by Huginn's low accuracy (0.19) on it, which allows for analysis on correctly answered subsets to avoid confounding effects of errors.
 - **GSM8K:** Used for macroscopic performance evaluation, consisting of grade-school math word problems (Cobbe et al., 2021).
- **Suppressing Explicit CoT:** To encourage latent reasoning, all experiments enforce the model to output only the final answer directly. This is achieved through a system message ("You are a concise and helpful assistant. Always return only the final answer straightway.") and few-shot in-context examples that provide only the final answer without intermediate steps.
- **Recurrent Steps:** Unless otherwise stated, the number of recurrent steps (`r`) is set to 16, resulting in a total of 68 effective blocks.

**Specific Analyses Performed:**

1. **Discontinuities in Hidden State Interpretability:**
 - The authors examine the "average rank trajectory" of the final predicted tokens across unrolled layers using both Logit Lens and Coda Lens. This tests for the "smooth refinement" pattern typically observed in feedforward Transformers.
 - They also measure the "proportion of top-5 decoded tokens" that are valid signed-integer prefixes (e.g., "5", "-"). This gauges the numerical interpretability of intermediate states, expecting such prefixes if latent CoT involves arithmetic steps.
 - Manual inspection of top-5 tokens further illustrates qualitative differences.

2. **Tracing Final and Intermediate Tokens for Latent CoT Evidence:**
 - This analysis focuses on a filtered subset of 67 correctly answered single-digit composite arithmetic questions where final and intermediate results are distinct and single-digit tokens.
 - The "rank trajectory" of the final result token and the intermediate result token is tracked across recurrent steps, with a random token ("the") serving as a baseline.
 - To mitigate "blockwise inconsistency" found in the prior analysis, this tracing is restricted to R3 outputs for Logit Lens and R4 outputs for Coda Lens, as these blocks showed better alignment with final predictions and interpretability.

3. **Scaling Recurrent Steps vs. Explicit CoT Performance:**
 - Huginn's accuracy on the GSM8K dataset is evaluated for varying numbers of recurrent steps (4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256), still suppressing explicit CoT.
 - The performance is then compared against Huginn's accuracy when explicit CoT prompting is used. An 8-shot prompting format is maintained for consistency with original Huginn evaluation.

This comprehensive methodology, combining detailed architectural analysis with targeted probing and performance benchmarks, allows for a multifaceted investigation into the presence and effectiveness of latent CoT in depth-recurrent Transformers.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings, largely providing limited evidence for the presence of interpretable latent Chain-of-Thought (CoT) in Huginn-3.5B under the tested conditions.

1. **Significant Probing Inconsistencies and Discontinuities in Hidden State Interpretability:**
 - Contrary to the "smooth refinement" observed in conventional feedforward Transformers, Huginn's internal representations exhibit **sharp discontinuities and periodic oscillations** in the rank trajectories of decoded tokens across its unrolled layers (Figure 2).
 - The interpretability of hidden states heavily depends on both the **layer index (specific recurrent block)** and the **decoding method (Logit Lens vs. Coda Lens)**.
 - For the Logit Lens (Figure 2, left), R4 layers consistently show high ranks (less interpretable final prediction), while R1-R3 layers show lower ranks. Conversely, for the Coda Lens (Figure 2, right), R4 layers exhibit low ranks (more interpretable final prediction), with higher ranks in other layers.
 - This divergence is further supported by analyzing the proportion of top-5 decoded tokens that are valid signed-integer prefixes (Figure 3). Logit Lens decodes numerous numeric prefixes from R1-R3 but almost none from R4. In stark contrast, Coda Lens decodes nearly 100% numeric prefixes from R4, while R1-R2 yield virtually none.
 - Manual inspection (Tables 2-7 in Appendix) confirms this, showing that R4 decoded with Logit Lens produces uninterpretable words (e.g., {"inc", "unity", "friendships"}), while Coda Lens on the *same* R4 hidden state yields numerical tokens (e.g., {"6", "5", "1"}). The opposite pattern is observed for R1.
 - These findings suggest that different blocks encode distinct information, and a single "lens" is not universally applicable for interpretation across all layers of a depth-recurrent architecture. The unique behavior of R4 is hypothesized to be due to its dual role, feeding both the next recurrent cycle and the Coda module.

2. **Limited Evidence for Latent CoT from Token Rank Trajectories:**
 - Analysis of the rank trajectories of final and intermediate result tokens in correctly answered single-digit arithmetic problems provided **little evidence for structured latent CoT reasoning** (Figure 4).
 - Latent CoT would predict a clear "phase separation," where the intermediate token's rank drops first, followed by a delayed drop in the final token's rank, reflecting a multi-step computation. This expected pattern was not observed.
 - Instead, in both Logit Lens and Coda Lens analyses, the ranks of both final and intermediate tokens descended quickly in the early recurrent steps. The final token consistently maintained a lower rank than the intermediate token throughout, suggesting that the model was converging on the final answer without explicitly passing through an intermediate state as a distinct, low-rank prediction.
 - While an interesting "rank reversal" between final and intermediate tokens was observed around step 6 for many examples, its precise implications for latent CoT were not fully deciphered and are left for future work.
 - Analysis of other recurrent blocks (Appendix A.1) similarly showed no clear signals of latent CoT.

3. **Scaling Recurrence Depth Cannot Match Explicit Chain-of-Thought:**
 - A macroscopic performance evaluation on the GSM8K benchmark, with explicit CoT suppressed, revealed that increasing the number of recurrent steps in Huginn yielded **only marginal gains in accuracy** (Table 1).
 - Accuracy rose modestly from 3.11% (4 recurrent steps) to 4.93% (32 recurrent steps) and then plateaued, or even slightly decreased (4.62% at 256 steps).
 - This performance is **substantially lower** than Huginn's accuracy when explicit CoT prompting is enabled (24.87% strict accuracy, 38.13% flexible accuracy).
 - This indicates that even if some form of latent reasoning emerges within the recurrent loops, it is insufficient to rival the effectiveness and structured problem-solving power of explicit, language-based Chain-of-Thought reasoning.

In summary, the findings suggest that while depth-recurrence might offer some benefits, it does not straightforwardly lead to interpretable, structured latent CoT reasoning that mirrors its explicit counterpart. The model's internal states are complex, and their interpretation is highly context-dependent.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides crucial insights into the internal workings of depth-recurrent Transformers and significantly contributes to the ongoing discourse on LLM interpretability and reasoning. Its impact can be multifaceted:

1. **Challenging Assumptions about Latent Reasoning:** The most significant finding is the **limited evidence for interpretable latent Chain-of-Thought (CoT)** in Huginn-3.5B. This challenges the intuitive assumption that simply introducing recurrence will automatically induce structured, multi-step reasoning in a model's latent space that mirrors explicit CoT. It suggests that architectural changes alone, without specific training objectives or further mechanisms, may not be sufficient to unlock such complex internal reasoning processes. This finding is critical for guiding future research on designing models for efficient and robust reasoning.

2. **Advancing LLM Interpretability Methodology:** The study highlights a critical caveat for applying existing interpretability tools like the Logit Lens to novel, complex architectures. The observed "probing inconsistencies and discontinuities" across different recurrent blocks demonstrate that interpretability methods might need to be adapted or re-evaluated for non-standard transformer designs. The concept of a "Coda Lens" is a step in this direction, showcasing how architectural specifics can influence the utility and validity of probing techniques. This prompts a call for more sophisticated, context-aware interpretability frameworks for complex LLM architectures.

3. **Guiding Architectural Design for Reasoning:** The empirical results on GSM8K, showing only marginal gains with increased recurrence depth compared to the substantial improvements from explicit CoT, reinforce the value of explicit, structured guidance for reasoning. It implies that while depth-recurrent models might offer some iterative refinement, they currently fall short of inducing robust, CoT-level reasoning capabilities purely through latent mechanisms. This can inform future architectural designs, suggesting that a hybrid approach combining latent processing with more structured inductive biases or explicit guidance (even if subtle) might be necessary to achieve true latent CoT.

4. **Refining the Definition of "Latent CoT":** The paper's negative findings implicitly contribute to a deeper understanding of what "latent CoT" truly entails. If the employed probing techniques, which are well-established for detecting token-level reasoning, fail to identify it, it suggests that either latent CoT is more subtle and distributed than currently conceptualized, or it doesn't manifest in Huginn under these conditions. This encourages researchers to develop more precise definitions, theoretical frameworks, and advanced detection methodologies (e.g., activation patching, causal interventions) for uncovering hidden reasoning patterns.

5. **Setting a Benchmark for Future Work:** By systematically investigating Huginn-3.5B, the paper sets a benchmark for similar analyses on other recurrent or novel LLM architectures. It provides a methodological blueprint for probing internal states and evaluating the efficacy of latent reasoning claims. The open-sourced code further facilitates this, allowing other researchers to replicate, extend, and build upon these findings.

In conclusion, this research is significant not just for its specific findings regarding Huginn, but for its broader implications on how we understand, design, and interpret reasoning capabilities in next-generation large language models. It urges caution against assuming emergent properties and emphasizes the critical role of rigorous interpretability in uncovering the true mechanisms behind LLM performance.
