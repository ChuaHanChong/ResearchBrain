# FineVLA: Fine-Grained Instruction Alignment for Steerable Vision-Language-Action Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> FineVLA, from The University of Hong Kong and Alibaba Inc., establishes an open framework for action-aligned fine-grained vision-language-action supervision, enabling robots to follow explicit, detailed human instructions. This approach significantly improves policy compliance with execution-level factors and boosts overall task success rates in both simulated and real-world robotic manipulation.

## Problem

- Robot policies traditionally focus on high-level goals, lacking mechanisms to specify detailed execution processes (e.g., approach direction, contact region, motion path).
- Existing open-source robot datasets are heterogeneous, often redundant, and primarily annotated with coarse, goal-level language, hindering the development of steerable policies.
- There is a lack of specialized benchmarks and scalable annotators capable of assessing and generating fine-grained robotic video understanding, which is crucial for manipulation-specific details.

## Method

- Developed **FineVLA**-Tool and **FineVLA**-Data, a unified framework to aggregate, clean, cluster, and human-verify multi-aspect, fine-grained annotations for 47,159 representative robot trajectories from 10 diverse datasets.
- Created RoboFine-Bench, a fine-grained robotic video understanding benchmark with VQA and Caption tracks to evaluate VLMs' understanding of execution-level manipulation details.
- Introduced RoboFine-VLM, a specialized VLM trained on human-verified **FineVLA**-Data, to serve as a scalable annotator capable of generating temporally action-aligned, step-level fine-grained descriptions.
- Investigated **FineVLA**-Policy training strategies by systematically varying the mixture ratio of fine-grained process-level instructions and original goal-level instructions during VLA model training.

## Results

- RoboFine-VLM achieved an overall VQA accuracy of **71.0%** on RoboFine-Bench, surpassing the strongest general-purpose baseline (Gemini-3.1-Pro) by 8.9 absolute percentage points, and led in the caption track.
- Policies trained exclusively with fine-grained instructions (FG-only) improved success rates by +6.5/+4.7 points (Easy/Hard) on AlohaMix-OFT in RoboTwin simulation compared to Raw-only policies.
- The optimal mixed supervision setting (FG:Raw = 1:1) achieved **86.8%**/**82.5%** success rates on AlohaMix-OFT Easy/Hard in RoboTwin, representing gains of +15.0/+11.1 over the Raw-only baseline, and earned the highest average score (62.**7/100**) in real-world dual-arm tasks.
- In real-world evaluations, the FG:Raw = 1:1 policy demonstrated substantial improvements in compliance with fine-grained instructions for object pose (+23 points), object color (+18 points), and approach direction (+18 points).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fine-grained instructions do not compromise goal-level task success, often outperforming policies trained solely with coarse instructions.
> - A balanced mixture of fine-grained and goal-level instructions (e.g., 1:1 ratio) yields optimal policy performance, demonstrating a complementary effect between the two instruction types.
> - Fine-grained supervision directly improves steerable control by significantly increasing policy compliance with language-specified execution factors, such as object pose, color, and approach direction.
> - The benefits of fine-grained supervision are more pronounced with larger datasets and can mitigate architectural performance gaps between different VLA models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotics is progressing towards increasingly sophisticated forms of robot control. Initially focused on enabling robots to complete tasks, the emphasis has shifted towards policies that can be "steered" by human instructions. This steerability implies the capacity to execute a high-level goal in varied ways based on user-specified constraints, such as the active manipulator, approach direction, contact location, or specific motion path. Recent developments in robot foundation models, including π0.7, LingBot-VLA, GR00T N1.7, and GEN-1, have highlighted the importance of enabling robots to not only understand *what* task to perform but also *how* to perform it according to human input.

However, the existing research landscape faces several challenges in achieving truly open and steerable VLA systems. Current open-source robot datasets, while extensive (e.g., Open X-Embodiment, DROID, BridgeData V2), typically provide coarse, goal-level language descriptions (e.g., "pick up the cup") that omit critical execution details. This sparsity in annotation limits the ability to train policies that can follow fine-grained instructions. Furthermore, there is a lack of standardized infrastructure for generating action-aligned fine-grained supervision at scale, as well as benchmarks and specialized annotators capable of capturing process-level manipulation details. General video-language models often focus on scene appearance rather than robotic action specifics. Finally, the empirical effectiveness of fine-grained supervision for policy learning, and the optimal strategies for incorporating it, have not been systematically investigated.

FineVLA addresses these gaps by proposing an open framework designed to scale fine-grained VLA data, improve robotic video understanding for manipulation, and provide empirical evidence for training steerable VLA policies. It contributes infrastructure for data construction, a dedicated benchmark, a specialized annotator, and a systematic study of training recipes, thereby aiming to advance the development of robots that can execute tasks with greater human-like precision and steerability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop an open framework for action-aligned fine-grained VLA supervision to enable steerable robot policies. This overarching goal is motivated by three specific challenges identified in the current VLA research landscape:

1. **Heterogeneous Data and Missing Fine-Grained Annotation Infrastructure:** Existing robot datasets are diverse in their action and state representations, making direct unification difficult. More critically, demonstrations within these datasets are often redundant and primarily associated with coarse, goal-level descriptions. Execution-critical details like active arm choice, approach direction, contact regions, motion paths, and state transitions remain largely unannotated. The motivation here is to overcome the data bottleneck by creating an open infrastructure capable of producing fine-grained, action-aligned supervision at scale. This involves unifying disparate datasets and selectively annotating representative trajectories with detailed process-level instructions.

2. **Lack of Benchmarks and Scalable Annotators for Fine-Grained Robotic Video Understanding:** While general video-language models can describe video content, they frequently lack the specificity required for robotic manipulation, often overlooking details such as contact regions or precise motion paths. Existing embodied benchmarks primarily assess spatial reasoning or hand-object dynamics, but do not systematically evaluate a VLM's ability to comprehend process-level manipulation. Consequently, there is a need for specialized benchmarks to measure fine-grained robotic video understanding and scalable, robotics-specific annotators that can generate action-aligned captions efficiently. The motivation is to fill this gap by establishing a held-out benchmark and developing a VLM specialized in interpreting and describing fine-grained robot actions.

3. **Unknown Effectiveness and Training Recipe for Steerable Policies:** Even if fine-grained data were available, it remains unclear whether and how such detailed instructions improve policy learning. There is a lack of systematic evidence regarding the benefits of action-aligned instructions for enhancing steerable control, and what optimal mixtures of fine-grained and goal-level supervision should be used during training. The motivation is to empirically investigate the impact of fine-grained supervision on policy performance and to identify effective training strategies that yield robust steerable control, isolating the effect of language supervision from other architectural or data-scale variables.

In summary, the authors aim to transition VLA models from merely completing tasks to executing them in precisely specified ways, addressing limitations in data granularity, evaluation methods, and training methodologies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FineVLA is structured as an open framework composed of four interconnected components: FineVLA-Tool + FineVLA-Data, RoboFine-Bench, RoboFine-VLM, and FineVLA-Policy + Training Recipe.

#### 3.1. FineVLA-Tool + FineVLA-Data

The **FineVLA-Tool** addresses data heterogeneity and sparse annotation. It unifies 972,247 trajectories from 10 open-source robot datasets, which include BridgeData-V2, BC-Z, RT-1, Galaxea, RoboMIND-V1, RoboMIND-V2, RoboCOIN, RH20T, RDT, and DROID. The process involves four stages:
1. **Data Collection and Format Conversion:** Raw trajectories are converted to a unified LeRobot 2.1-style format, standardizing RGB videos, robot states, action sequences, and task metadata. Invalid or degenerate recordings are filtered.
2. **Action-State Canonicalization and Cleaning:** Action and state values, which vary in temporal reference (absolute, relative, delta) and kinematic representation (joint space, end-effector space with various rotation encodings), are canonicalized to absolute coordinates with normalized quaternion rotations. Trajectories exhibiting action-state Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) distances exceeding a dataset-specific threshold are removed as corrupted or inconsistent.
3. **Trajectory Clustering and Representative Sampling:** To reduce redundancy and maximize annotation diversity, trajectories within each task are clustered using DTW over canonicalized action sequences. Hierarchical clustering is applied to the resulting distance matrix, and two to three high-quality representatives are selected from each cluster based on proximity to the cluster medoid and trajectory quality. This process reduces the 972,247 raw trajectories to 47,159 representative samples.
4. **Fine-Grained Multi-Aspect Annotation:** Each selected representative trajectory is annotated with a ten-dimensional schema that captures control-relevant factors beyond goal-level instructions. These dimensions include action sequence, active actor, target object, initial configuration, final configuration, contact and approach, trajectory and orientation, object interaction, failure and recovery, and body motion. The annotation process involves an initial decomposition into step-level descriptions and structured slot filling by Qwen3.5-Plus, followed by human annotators reviewing the model-generated descriptions against the video to correct factual errors and verify temporal alignment. The output of this process is **FineVLA-Data**, a human-verified corpus of 47,159 trajectories, where the average instruction length increases by approximately 10.4 times (from 9.3 to 96.8 words) compared to original coarse instructions.

#### 3.2. RoboFine-Bench

**RoboFine-Bench** is a held-out benchmark designed to evaluate fine-grained robotic video understanding. It comprises 500 videos (50 from each of the 10 source datasets), covering 32 embodiments and diverse manipulation tasks. Each trajectory is paired with human-reviewed step-level annotations decomposed into 11,631 atomic facts across the ten fine-grained dimensions. These benchmark trajectories are strictly disjoint from both the RoboFine-VLM SFT training set and policy training splits.
The benchmark includes two tracks:
1. **VQA Track:** Contains 1,030 questions distributed across the ten fine-grained dimensions, aggregated into three reporting axes: Entity and Scene Grounding, Action and Motion Understanding, and Interaction and State Reasoning. Models receive video frames and questions in a single prompt, and answers are scored by deterministic matching against ground-truth labels.
2. **Caption Track:** Evaluates generative understanding by requiring models to produce action-aligned step-level fine-grained descriptions. Generated captions are assessed by an LLM against ground-truth atomic facts, yielding per-fact alignment labels (match, partial, contradiction, omission, hallucination) that are aggregated into Consistency, Coverage, and Anti-Hallucination metrics. This track includes "easy" (task instruction provided) and "hard" (vision-only) settings.

#### 3.3. RoboFine-VLM

**RoboFine-VLM** is a robotics-specialized VLM derived by fine-tuning Qwen3.5-397B-A17B on the human-verified FineVLA-Data. Its purpose is to serve as a scalable annotator for new robot trajectories, generating temporally action-aligned step-level fine-grained descriptions according to the ten-dimensional schema. The model's quality is evaluated on RoboFine-Bench. Importantly, RoboFine-VLM is trained to support future scalable annotation and open-source reproducibility, but it is not used to generate the supervision for the policy experiments in this paper; those experiments use FineVLA-Tool-generated and human-verified annotations.

#### 3.4. FineVLA-Policy + Training Recipe

**FineVLA-Policy** investigates the effect of action-aligned language supervision on steerable VLA policies. The authors employ two existing action-decoding architectures from the StarVLA codebase, both built on a Qwen3.5-4B vision-language backbone:
1. **StarVLA-OFT:** Attaches a lightweight MLP regression head for parallel prediction of continuous action chunks with an L1 objective.
2. **StarVLA-GR00T:** Utilizes a dual-system design where the VL backbone provides slow reasoning (System 2) and a DiT-based flow-matching module performs continuous action generation (System 1).
To isolate the effect of language supervision, the authors create two parallel training datasets from the same source trajectories: an **FG dataset** (47,159 representative trajectories with fine-grained process-level instructions) and a **Raw dataset** (all 972,247 source trajectories with original goal-level instructions). Crucially, for trajectories present in both datasets, the action labels and visual observations are identical, with only the paired language differing.
Seven configurations are compared based on the **FG:Raw instruction ratio** (Raw-only, 1:4, 1:2, 1:1, 2:1, 4:1, FG-only) across three (dataset, framework) combinations: RDT-OFT, RDT-GR00T, and AlohaMix-OFT (an ALOHA-compatible dual-arm mixture, 13x larger than RDT). Policies are initially pretrained (100k steps on 64 A100 GPUs) and then fine-tuned on RoboTwin simulation data (100k steps on 8 A100 GPUs), with the instruction mixture applied during fine-tuning. For real-world evaluation, simulation checkpoints are further fine-tuned on 50 demonstrations per task across 12 tabletop tasks (600 total demonstrations) on a Cobot Magic dual-arm platform.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings across VLM evaluation, simulated policy learning, and real-world steerable control.

#### 4.1. RoboFine-Bench Results (VLM Evaluation)

RoboFine-VLM demonstrated superior performance compared to five general-purpose VLMs (Qwen3-VL-Plus, Qwen3.5-Plus, Doubao-Seed-2.0-Pro, Gemini-3.1-Pro, GPT-5.4) on the held-out RoboFine-Bench:
- **VQA Track:** RoboFine-VLM achieved 68.2% overall accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline (GPT-5.4) by 8.0 percentage points. Significant gains were observed in Action and Motion Understanding (75.7% vs. 64.6%), indicating improved execution-level reasoning. Fine-tuning Qwen3.5-Plus on FineVLA-Data improved its overall accuracy from 55.9% to 68.2%, with gains across all reasoning axes.
- **Caption Track:** RoboFine-VLM led in both "easy" (task instruction provided) and "hard" (vision-only) settings. In the hard setting, it achieved 82.2% Overall score, outperforming the strongest baseline (78.0%), and ranked first across Consistency, Coverage, and Anti-Hallucination metrics. This suggests it captures execution processes intrinsically.
- **Benchmark Validity:** The caption ranking correlated strongly with human preferences (Pearson r > 0.92, Spearman ρ > 0.94 in both settings) and was robust to the choice of LLM alignment judge (GPT-5.4-Pro vs. Gemini-3.1-Pro). These results indicate that RoboFine-VLM can generate dense, action-aligned robotic descriptions, validating the proposed annotation schema's capacity to capture execution-level manipulation details.

#### 4.2. RoboTwin Simulation Results (Policy Evaluation)

Evaluation on RoboTwin, a simulation benchmark for bimanual manipulation, across three (dataset, framework) combinations (RDT-OFT, RDT-GR00T, AlohaMix-OFT) revealed three key findings regarding fine-grained supervision:
1. **No Sacrifice of Goal-Level Task Success:** Fine-grained-only (FG-only) supervision consistently improved task success rates over raw-instruction-only (Raw-only) supervision across all evaluated simulation settings. For instance, FG-only showed gains of +1.4 to +8.1 success-rate points, with the largest gain on AlohaMix-OFT (+6.5/+4.7 points on Easy/Hard splits).
2. **Complementary Nature of Fine-Grained and Raw Instructions:** Performance exhibited a consistent inverted-U trend as the FG:Raw ratio varied, peaking around 1:2 to 1:1. The strongest mixed setting, FG:Raw = 1:1, achieved 86.8%/82.5% on AlohaMix-OFT Easy/Hard, representing gains of +15.0/+11.1 over the Raw-only baseline (71.8%/71.4%). This suggests that combining both instruction types leverages their complementary strengths.
3. **Architecture and Data-Scale Effects:** Fine-grained supervision reduced the performance gap between different action-decoding architectures (e.g., StarVLA-OFT vs. StarVLA-GR00T on RDT), indicating it alleviates a supervision bottleneck. Additionally, the benefits of FG supervision were more pronounced with larger datasets (e.g., AlohaMix-OFT vs. RDT-OFT), suggesting its value increases with data scale.

#### 4.3. Real-World Steerability Results (Policy Evaluation)

Evaluation on a self-designed real-world steerability suite using a Cobot Magic dual-arm platform, designed to isolate language-conditioned controllability, reinforced the simulation findings:
- **Improved Steerable Control:** The FG:Raw = 1:1 policy achieved the highest average score (62.7/100) compared to Raw-only (49.9). The most substantial gains (over Raw-only) were observed on execution-sensitive attributes that are often unspecified by goal-level language: pose (+23), color (+18), and approach direction (+18). Gains were also observed for rotation (+10) and active arm (+4). This indicates that fine-grained supervision directly enhances compliance with language-specified execution factors.
- **Complementarity in Real World:** Consistent with simulation, the in-domain score followed an inverted-U trend, peaking at FG:Raw = 1:1. Mixed supervision also matched or exceeded Raw-only performance on general manipulation tasks (Clean Table, Stack Block), suggesting it does not interfere with routine execution.
- **OOD Probe:** On an out-of-distribution (OOD) active-arm-target binding probe ("Left hand...Place into right bowl"), the mixed model improved from 0 to 10/100, suggesting partial factor-level generalization, though full compositional generalization remains a challenge.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The FineVLA framework addresses critical limitations in the development of steerable Vision-Language-Action policies, presenting an open and comprehensive approach to enhance robot instruction following.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
- **Enhanced Data Foundation:** By unifying 10 diverse robot datasets and creating FineVLA-Data, a large-scale, human-verified dataset with fine-grained, action-aligned annotations, the work provides a crucial resource for training more capable VLA models. The 10.4x increase in instruction information density per trajectory is significant for capturing nuanced execution details.
- **Specialized Robotic Video Understanding:** The development of RoboFine-VLM, a robotics-specialized VLM, and RoboFine-Bench, a held-out benchmark, marks progress in evaluating and achieving fine-grained robotic video understanding. This addresses the current gap where general VLMs often miss execution-critical details.
- **Empirical Validation of Fine-Grained Supervision:** The policy experiments offer systematic evidence regarding the efficacy and optimal integration of fine-grained language supervision. The findings that fine-grained supervision does not compromise goal-level success, and that a complementary mixture of fine-grained and raw instructions yields optimal steerable control, provide valuable guidance for future policy training.
- **Improved Steerable Control:** The real-world evaluation demonstrates that fine-grained supervision directly improves a robot's ability to comply with language-specified execution factors, particularly those related to pose, color, and approach direction, which are underspecified by goal-level instructions. This moves robots closer to executing tasks *how* humans intend, not just *what* they intend.

**Potential Impact:**
- **More Intuitive Human-Robot Interaction:** FineVLA's approach could enable more natural and precise human-robot communication, allowing users to specify execution details beyond simple task goals, leading to robots that are more adaptable and compliant with human preferences.
- **Advancement of Robot Foundation Models:** The open-source release of FineVLA-Tool, RoboFine-VLM, RoboFine-Bench, and policy checkpoints and code provides foundational resources that can accelerate research in steerable VLA policies and robot foundation models.
- **Broadened Research Avenues:** The identified limitations, such as challenges in compositional generalization to unseen instruction combinations and the need for broader embodiment validation and integrated safety checks, highlight new research directions.
- **Increased Transparency and Control:** By explicitly linking language to execution-level choices, fine-grained supervision could enhance the transparency of robot decision-making, allowing users to understand and influence the robot's operational methods.

In conclusion, FineVLA represents a methodical effort to equip VLA models with the capacity for fine-grained instruction alignment, thereby fostering the development of truly steerable robotic systems that can operate with greater precision and responsiveness to human guidance.
