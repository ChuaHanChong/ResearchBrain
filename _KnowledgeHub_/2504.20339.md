# DRO: Doppler-Aware Direct Radar Odometry

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents a direct radar odometry framework that accurately estimates ego-motion by directly optimizing raw radar intensity data to a local map. The method continuously models sensor motion and explicitly accounts for Doppler distortion, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy of 0.26% translation error on the Boreas dataset and demonstrating robustness in feature-deprived environments through a novel GP-based Doppler integration.

## Problem

- Existing radar odometry methods (indirect/feature-based) discard significant raw intensity data and struggle with data association, while prior direct methods are computationally costly or lack generality.
- Radar data is inherently affected by motion distortion (due to sensor movement during a scan) and Doppler distortion (due to relative velocity), which are often not fully or accurately accounted for in estimation.
- Achieving robust and accurate odometry in adverse weather conditions and feature-deprived environments (e.g., tunnels) remains a challenge for traditional sensors and existing radar approaches.

## Method

- Introduces Doppler-Aware Direct Radar Odometry (**DRO**), a ==gradient-based optimization framework== for 2D (SE(2)) odometry that directly registers raw radar intensity data to a ==continuously updated local map==.
- Accounts for sensor motion and unique Doppler distortion within the estimation by modeling a continuous trajectory during each scan, correcting for these effects before intensity matching.
- Proposes a novel Doppler-based objective using ==Gaussian Process (GP) infill== to effectively leverage Doppler velocity information from triangular-modulated radars, providing robust velocity estimation in feature-sparse environments.

## Results

- On a custom automotive dataset, DRO-GD achieved **0.34%** translation error in a feature-deprived tunnel, significantly outperforming the best radar baseline (CT-RDG at **0.70%**) and a lidar baseline (**35.90%**).
- DRO-G achieved state-of-the-art performance on the Boreas public dataset leaderboard, with an average relative translation error of **0.26%** and rotation error of **0.05°/100m**, outperforming CFEAR++ (**0.51%**) and STEAM-RIO++ (**0.62%**).
- The system runs in real-time on a laptop GPU (average **89 ms** per radar frame), and ablation studies confirm the critical roles of the gyroscope and the local map for accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct radar intensity registration to a local map, combined with continuous trajectory estimation, effectively addresses motion and Doppler distortions.
> - A novel Gaussian Process (GP) based infill method allows for principled use of Doppler velocity information from triangular-modulated radars, enabling robust odometry in geometrically feature-deprived environments.
> - Integrating gyroscope measurements significantly improves orientation estimation, and robust online bias estimation for both gyroscope and Doppler lateral velocity enhances overall accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of mobile robotics is currently experiencing a "renaissance" in radar-based sensing. Historically, lidar and cameras dominated state estimation due to their high resolution and ease of interpretation. However, their limitations in adverse weather conditions (heavy rain, fog, snow, dust) and environments with occlusions (thin walls, vegetation) have spurred renewed interest in millimeter-wave (mmWave) radars, which excel in these scenarios.

This paper positions itself at the forefront of this new wave, specifically addressing the challenging problem of radar odometry. The broader landscape of radar odometry is primarily divided into:

- **Indirect (Feature-based) Methods:** These are currently the state-of-the-art (SOTA). They extract sparse features or point clouds from radar scans and perform data association and registration (e.g., SIFT, ORB, CFAR-based methods like CFEAR, HERO, STEAM-RIO++). While effective, they often discard a large amount of raw intensity data, require complex data association, and are sensitive to parameter tuning.
- **Direct Methods:** These methods, prevalent in lidar and monocular vision odometry, directly use the raw sensor intensity information for registration, avoiding explicit feature extraction and data association. For radar, direct methods have been limited. Existing approaches (e.g., Barnes et al., Weston et al., Park et al.) often rely on brute-force search, decouple rotation and translation, or use deep learning, leading to high computational costs, discrete search spaces, or limited transferability.

Furthermore, the paper addresses critical issues of motion distortion and the Doppler effect in radar data. While continuous-time trajectory estimation has been applied to lidar and cameras, its principled application to radar, specifically accounting for Doppler distortion, is less explored. The integration of Doppler velocity information, unique to radar, is a burgeoning area, with prior works often relying on strong kinematic models or requiring specific outlier rejection techniques (e.g., Kellner, Kubelka, Lisus).

"DRO" uniquely combines these aspects by introducing the first *direct* radar odometry framework that:
1. Utilizes *all* raw radar intensity information.
2. Performs *scan-to-local-map* registration, enhancing robustness.
3. Accounts for *continuous motion and Doppler distortion* in a principled, gradient-based optimization.
4. Leverages a *novel GP-based infill* to effectively utilize Doppler information, even in feature-deprived environments, without strong kinematic assumptions.
5. Avoids deep learning, promoting better transferability.

By achieving SOTA performance while addressing these fundamental limitations, DRO significantly pushes the boundaries of robust and accurate radar-based navigation, making radar a more viable standalone or primary sensor for critical autonomous applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a highly robust, accurate, and real-time capable odometry system specifically designed for spinning Frequency-Modulated Continuous-Wave (FMCW) radars. This system, termed "Doppler-Aware Direct Radar Odometry (DRO)", aims to overcome the limitations of existing radar odometry approaches.

The key objectives can be broken down as follows:
- **Direct Registration:** To formulate a novel method for scan-to-local-map registration that directly uses all the incoming radar intensity information, avoiding the need for computationally costly and information-discarding feature or point cloud extraction steps common in indirect methods.
- **Continuous Trajectory Estimation:** To perform locally continuous trajectory estimation, rather than discrete pose estimation, to accurately model and compensate for the sensor's motion during a scan.
- **Motion and Doppler Distortion Correction:** To explicitly account for both the motion-induced distortion and the unique Doppler distortion present in radar scans within the estimation process, which is critical for accuracy, especially at high speeds.
- **Leveraging Doppler Information:** For radars with specific frequency modulation patterns (e.g., triangular), to formulate an additional Doppler-based constraint that improves velocity estimates and enables robust odometry in geometrically feature-deprived environments (e.g., featureless tunnels), where other modalities or geometric methods often fail.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** To achieve and surpass the performance of existing state-of-the-art radar odometry methods across a wide range of diverse environments and conditions.
- **Practicality and Real-Time Operation:** To develop a real-time capable implementation that is publicly available, does not rely on deep learning (ensuring better transferability), and operates effectively with or without a gyroscope.

The primary motivation stems from radar's inherent robustness. Unlike cameras or lidars, radars can "see" through adverse weather conditions and vegetation, making them invaluable for all-weather autonomous navigation. However, fully harnessing radar's potential requires addressing the challenges of processing its unique data. Existing methods either sacrifice too much information (indirect methods) or suffer from computational/accuracy trade-offs (previous direct methods). By proposing a principled direct method that explicitly models radar physics (motion and Doppler distortion) and intelligently integrates auxiliary sensors like gyroscopes, the authors aim to unlock radar's full capabilities, especially in scenarios where other sensors struggle or fail.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed "Doppler-Aware Direct Radar Odometry (DRO)" method is a 2D (SE(2)) odometry approach for spinning FMCW radars, optionally aided by a gyroscope. Its core methodology revolves around a gradient-based optimization that directly registers incoming radar intensity data to a locally updated map, accounting for continuous motion and Doppler distortion.

**Inputs:** The system takes 360-degree radar scans (intensity `ψ_nm` at azimuth `α_n` and range `r_m`) and, optionally, gyroscope angular velocity measurements (`ω_i`).

**Optimization Problem:** The method estimates a finite set of state variables `S` (representing the continuous trajectory) by maximizing a combined objective function `O = O_ψ + O_d`.
- `O_ψ`: Direct intensity objective for scan-to-local-map registration.
- `O_d`: Doppler-based objective (only for radars with triangular modulation patterns).

**1. Direct Intensity Objective (`O_ψ`):**
- **Local Map (`M`):** Instead of scan-to-scan registration, DRO registers the current scan to a "local map," which is a Cartesian image-like representation of past radar data. This map is updated on-the-fly using a per-pixel low-pass filter (Equation 12), effectively combining previous scans and increasing robustness to outliers like moving objects or multipath echoes.
- **Motion and Doppler Correction:** Before registration, the incoming radar scan's points (`α_n`, `r_m`, `ψ_nm`) are transformed to correct for the sensor's continuous motion and the Doppler effect during the scan (Equations 9, 10). The Doppler shift `Δr_n` is explicitly modeled as a function of the body-centric velocity and beam azimuth, even for radars without triangular modulation, as Doppler distortion still affects range measurements.
- **Cross-Correlation:** The objective `O_ψ` is computed as the cross-correlation score between the motion- and Doppler-corrected current scan intensities and the local map intensities (Equation 11), queried via bilinear interpolation. Maximizing this score aligns the scan with the map.

**2. Doppler-Based Objective (`O_d`):** (Conditional on triangular modulation radar)
- **GP Infill:** A novel approach is used to overcome the challenge that consecutive radar beams don't observe the same environmental points due to sensor motion. The incoming radar scan is split into two "images" based on chirp direction (up-chirp `I↑` and down-chirp `I↓`). Gaussian Process (GP) regression is then used to "infill" missing rows (alternate azimuths) in both images (Fig. 4). This ensures that each corresponding row in `I↑` and `I↓` virtually observes the *same* part of the environment, making radial velocity observable via range differences.
- **Doppler Constraint:** `O_d` is a cross-correlation between `I↓` and the Doppler-rectified `I↑` (Equation 13). This objective is maximized when the estimated sensor velocity accurately predicts the Doppler-induced range shifts between the up-chirp and down-chirp measurements.
- **Lateral Velocity Bias Compensation:** An online heuristic is employed to estimate and compensate for an empirically observed lateral velocity bias in Doppler-only estimates. It uses the non-slip kinematic constraint when the vehicle is moving, effectively tracking and correcting for the bias.

**3. Motion Models:**
- **Rotation:**
 - **Constant Angular Velocity:** For gyroscope-less operation, a simple constant angular velocity `ω` is assumed for the scan duration.
 - **Gyro Preintegration:** When a gyroscope is available, a piece-wise linear model based on preintegrated gyroscope measurements (Equation 15) provides a more accurate, continuous orientation estimate. An online bias estimation strategy is used when the vehicle is static.
- **Translation:** A constant body-centric velocity `v_b` is assumed, and position is obtained by integrating this velocity over the continuous rotation (Equation 16).

**Optimization Implementation:** The problem is solved using a custom gradient ascent algorithm (Equation 17) with analytical Jacobians for efficiency. GPU acceleration (PyTorch) is leveraged, especially for the GP infill, which uses efficient convolution-like operations given the grid pattern. A robust weighting mechanism (Equation 18) is incorporated to down-weight outlier contributions from moving objects or specular noise, particularly in challenging scenarios like skyways.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper demonstrates the effectiveness and superior performance of DRO across extensive evaluations on both public and custom datasets, spanning various environments and radar configurations.

**Key Quantitative Results:**

- **Custom Automotive Dataset (Doppler-enabled, Triangular Pattern Radar):**
 - DRO-GD (Doppler + Gyroscope) consistently outperforms all other radar baselines (CT-R, DG, CT-RDG) by a significant margin. For instance, in the challenging Tunnel environment, DRO-GD achieves a 0.34% translation error, compared to 0.70% for the best radar baseline (CT-RDG) and a staggering 35.90% for CT-R.
 - In feature-deprived scenarios like Tunnel and Skyway, DRO-GD maintains low error rates (0.34% and 0.40% translation error, respectively), showcasing the critical role of the Doppler-based constraint in such conditions where geometric features are degenerate or absent.
 - The lidar baseline (CT-LG) performs poorly in these challenging scenarios, highlighting radar's advantage.
 - DRO-D (Doppler-only linear dynamics estimation) also shows a noticeable advantage over DG in challenging environments.
- **Boreas Dataset (Sawtooth Pattern Radar, No Doppler `O_d`):**
 - On the Boreas public leaderboard (13 sequences, >350 km), DRO-G (Gyroscope only) achieves state-of-the-art performance, with an average relative translation error of **0.26%** and rotation error of **0.05°/100m**. This significantly outperforms other SOTA radar-based methods like CFEAR++ (0.51% trans. error) and STEAM-RIO++ (0.62% trans. error).
 - The results show no correlation between weather conditions and translation error, validating radar's robustness in diverse weather.
 - Notably, DRO-G's orientation estimates outperform other inertial-aided methods, suggesting that radar constraints in those methods sometimes negatively impact orientation accuracy compared to pure gyroscope integration (with a robust bias estimation).
- **MulRan Dataset (Sawtooth Pattern Radar, Low-Quality IMU):**
 - DRO-G outperforms CFEAR variations on MulRan (average 1.45% translation error, 0.38°/100m rotation error), demonstrating its robustness even with lower-quality IMU data and emphasizing the soundness of the direct orientation integration.
- **Off-road Mobile Robot Dataset (Sawtooth Pattern Radar):**
 - DRO-G maintains a satisfactory level of accuracy across progressively unstructured environments (Parking, Industrial, Grass, Woods), demonstrating versatility without tedious parameter tuning. For example, in the highly unstructured Woods environment, DRO-G achieves a 0.71% RPE, outperforming CT-RG (1.30%).
 - This experiment also highlights the efficacy of the online gyroscope bias estimation for lower-quality IMUs.

**Ablation Study Findings:**

- **Gyroscope is Crucial:** Removing the gyroscope leads to the largest performance degradation, emphasizing its importance for accurate orientation estimation, especially given radar's low angular resolution.
- **Local Map Importance:** The proposed local map (scan-to-local-map registration) is vital for accuracy; replacing it with simple scan-to-scan registration degrades performance.
- **Bias Compensation:** Online gyroscope bias estimation and lateral velocity bias compensation, while smaller in impact than the gyroscope itself, contribute to overall accuracy and robustness.
- **Parameter Sensitivity:** The method is not highly sensitive to the local map update parameter `γ`.

**Computational Performance:**

- DRO-GD runs in **real-time** on a laptop GPU (average 89 ms per radar frame, well below 250 ms radar cycle). DRO-D (Doppler-only) is even faster at 34 ms/scan. CPU performance is slower but still feasible for some variants.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DRO framework represents a significant advancement in radar-based odometry, offering robust and accurate ego-motion estimation in conditions where traditional sensing modalities struggle or fail. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Radar Odometry:** DRO is the first direct radar registration method that accounts for continuous motion and Doppler distortion in a principled, gradient-based optimization without discretizing the search space or relying on deep learning. This novel approach fully leverages the dense intensity information from radar scans, a departure from the prevalent feature-based methods that discard substantial data.

2. **Enhanced Robustness to Challenging Environments:**
 - **Adverse Weather:** By directly using radar intensity, DRO fully harnesses radar's inherent ability to "see" through heavy rain, fog, snow, and dust, making it a critical component for all-weather autonomous navigation.
 - **Feature-Deprived Scenarios:** The innovative use of Gaussian Process (GP) regression for Doppler data infill, coupled with the Doppler-based objective function, allows DRO to perform robust odometry even in geometrically challenging environments like featureless tunnels or open skyways. This addresses a major limitation of lidar and vision systems.
 - **Unstructured Environments:** Demonstrated success in off-road and wooded areas further highlights its versatility beyond typical urban settings.

3. **State-of-the-Art Performance:** DRO achieves and surpasses the performance of existing state-of-the-art radar odometry methods on established public benchmarks (Boreas, MulRan) and custom datasets. Its top ranking on the Boreas leaderboard is a strong testament to its accuracy and robustness.

4. **Practicality and Generalizability:**
 - **Real-time Capable:** The GPU-accelerated implementation ensures real-time performance, crucial for autonomous systems.
 - **Model-Based:** By avoiding deep learning, DRO is more interpretable, requires no training data, and inherently generalizes better to new environments and radar models without retraining.
 - **Public Availability:** The open-source implementation fosters further research and development in the robotics community.
 - **Versatility:** Demonstrated efficacy with different radar models (Navtech RAS6, RAS3) and varying IMU qualities, showcasing its broad applicability.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:**
 - The principled integration of Doppler information opens new avenues for richer sensor fusion, combining geometric and kinematic information more effectively.
 - It lays the groundwork for more advanced radar-centric SLAM systems, enabling robust mapping and localization in scenarios where other sensors are unreliable.
 - The observed lateral velocity bias in Doppler measurements prompts further investigation into radar's physical measurement models, which could lead to even more accurate ego-motion estimation.

In conclusion, DRO significantly elevates the capabilities of radar as a primary sensing modality for autonomous navigation. Its novel direct approach, combined with sophisticated handling of motion and Doppler effects, positions it as a leading solution for developing highly robust and reliable autonomous systems that can operate effectively in any environment and weather condition.
