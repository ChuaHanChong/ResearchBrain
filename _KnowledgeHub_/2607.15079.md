# BrainPilot: Automating Brain Discovery with Agentic Research

> [!summary] Summary
> BrainPilot is an AI system designed to automate complex brain science research by integrating a multi-agent architecture with curated domain knowledge and a transparent, auditable workflow. It demonstrated comparable performance to state-of-the-art agent frameworks on neuroscientific tasks while achieving notable efficiencies in terms of time and cost.

## Problem

- Modern brain science research is complex and interdisciplinary, requiring extensive time and resources for multi-step workflows like literature review, experimental design, and data analysis.
- Existing AI agents for scientific research lack deep domain expertise, are prone to fabricating claims, can drift during multi-step reasoning, and offer insufficient points for expert intervention, particularly critical in brain science.
- The inherent difficulty in reproducing results and the sensitivity to analytical decisions in brain science necessitate a high degree of trustworthiness, transparency, and human judgment.

## Method

- A ==human-in-the-loop multi-agent system== comprising specialist agents (e.g., PI, Librarian, Experimentalist, Engineer, Writer, Auditor) coordinates and executes research tasks, with the PI agent delegating and integrating outputs.
- Agents are grounded in brain science through a comprehensive ==Retrieval Knowledge Base== (7,233 indexed items from textbooks/papers) and a modular ==Skill Library== (72 reusable methodology units) built offline.
- A ==Graph of Trace (GoT)== tool records all agent actions and dependencies as a ==directed acyclic graph==, ensuring workflow inspectability, auditability, and preventing retrospective edits, while an Auditor agent performs independent verification of claims.

## Results

- BrainPilot achieved comparable performance to state-of-the-art agent frameworks on established benchmarks (Agents’ Last Exam and BrainPilotBench-v0), sometimes outperforming matched configurations on specific tasks.
- The system demonstrated significant time and cost advantages, completing tasks in **8–63%** of the time and at **5–56%** of the cost compared to other frameworks when using matched backbone LLMs.
- **Five** end-to-end case studies showcased BrainPilot's capability to successfully execute complex brain science research pipelines, including spatial coding analysis, functional hierarchy investigation, pain decoding, and sleep staging, generating coherent and reproducible scientific outputs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A specialized multi-agent architecture, deeply grounded in domain-specific knowledge and methodology skills, is crucial for developing reliable and valid AI assistants in complex scientific fields.
> - Explicit mechanisms for traceability (Graph of Trace) and independent verification (Auditor agent) are essential to mitigate common AI agent issues like hallucination and reasoning drift, enhancing scientific trustworthiness.
> - Integrating human-in-the-loop interactions at critical junctures through an inspectable workflow is vital for preserving expert judgment and guiding AI agents in iterative scientific discovery.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of scientific research is increasingly leveraging artificial intelligence (AI) to automate and accelerate discovery. This trend is evident across various domains, including chemistry, biomedicine, and computational science, where AI agents are being developed to design experiments, execute analyses, and even generate scientific manuscripts. Examples include autonomous laboratories that combine language models with robotic platforms for chemical synthesis and general-purpose biomedical AI agents that work with extensive collections of tools and databases.

Within brain science specifically, existing AI systems have demonstrated capabilities in automating neuroethology and animal behavior analysis, orchestrating neuroimaging analysis pipelines, understanding brain signals in brain-computer interfaces, and conjecturing computational models of human behavior. These systems indicate the potential for specialized agents to handle multi-step analyses with reduced manual effort. Furthermore, large language models trained on neuroscience literature have shown promise in synthesizing prior findings and predicting experimental outcomes.

However, the authors identify several limitations in the current landscape of AI agents for scientific research, particularly for brain science. These limitations include a general lack of deep domain expertise in brain science among current agents, a propensity for fabrication or "hallucination" of claims, tendencies to drift during complex multi-step reasoning, and insufficient defined points for expert intervention. These issues are considered particularly problematic in brain science, where scientific conclusions rely heavily on laboratory-specific expertise, rigorous controls, iterative expert judgment, and careful evaluation of methodological choices and plausibility. The paper highlights that much relevant expertise is often poorly documented and therefore not readily captured by general-purpose large language models. This work positions itself to address these gaps by developing a system that integrates domain-specific knowledge, traceable workflows, and expert oversight to enhance trustworthiness and reliability in AI-assisted brain science research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the increasing complexity and interdisciplinary nature of modern brain science. Understanding the brain necessitates integrating diverse evidence across multiple scales, modalities, and scientific disciplines. This requires a coordinated sequence of operations, from comprehensive literature reviews to experimental design, data analysis, and result interpretation, all demanding significant time, resources, and expert coordination. These processes are often difficult to reproduce and sensitive to analytical decisions.

The emergence of AI agents offers a timely opportunity to accelerate these complex research workflows. However, existing AI agents, while demonstrating feasibility in scientific tasks, pose significant challenges to scientific validity. Specific issues include the potential for AI agents to fabricate claims unsupported by evidence, to deviate from the intended research path during multi-step reasoning, and to offer limited opportunities for human experts to intervene and guide the process. In brain science, where laboratory-specific expertise, stringent controls, and iterative human judgment are paramount, such shortcomings are particularly costly. Conclusions drawn in this field directly feed into downstream scientific claims and demand a high level of trustworthiness and transparency regarding the underlying evidence, assumptions, operations, and judgments.

Against this background, the key objectives of the BrainPilot system are:

1. **Accelerate Brain Science Research:** To significantly speed up the execution of complex, multi-step research workflows in brain science.
2. **Ensure Scientific Validity and Trustworthiness:** To design a system where research processes are transparent, verifiable, and resilient against fabrication or reasoning drift.
3. **Facilitate Human-in-the-Loop Interaction:** To provide clear and effective interfaces for human experts to inspect, guide, and validate the AI agent's actions and outputs at critical junctures, thus preserving essential expert judgment.
4. **Ground Agents in Domain Expertise:** To equip AI agents with curated and continuously expandable domain-specific knowledge and methodological skills tailored to brain science, moving beyond generic language model capabilities.
5. **Provide Traceable and Auditable Workflows:** To create an explicit record of the entire research process, linking subgoals, tool usage, evidence, and claims, thereby enhancing reproducibility and accountability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BrainPilot is designed as a human-in-the-loop multi-agent system structured to accelerate brain science research through a coordinated approach involving specialist agents, curated domain knowledge, and a traceable workflow.

#### 3.1. Multi-Agent System and Agent Roles

The system operates with a team of specialist agents overseen by a Principal Investigator (PI) agent. The user interacts directly with the PI agent, which is responsible for task decomposition, subtask delegation to appropriate specialists, and integration of their results. The default team comprises six agents:

- **Principal Investigator (PI) Agent:** Coordinates the overall task, clarifies user intent, delegates to specialist agents, synthesizes results, and routes deliverables through the Writer and Auditor before final delivery.
- **Librarian Agent:** Specializes in surveying literature and providing knowledge grounding. It generates structured outputs such as Key Findings, Knowledge Gaps, Suggested Hypotheses, and References, primarily utilizing search and read tools.
- **Experimentalist Agent:** Focuses on designing scientific procedures, including operationalization, control design, sample planning, step-by-step protocols, and preregistered analysis plans.
- **Engineer Agent:** Translates experimental designs into reproducible code, executes it, and reports on the execution and generated outputs. This agent emphasizes precision and reproducibility, using file and shell tools.
- **Writer Agent:** Drafts and refines scientific documents based on the outputs from specialist agents, preparing reports with an emphasis on clarity and audience awareness.
- **Auditor Agent:** Performs an independent verification of the drafts produced by the Writer. Its role is to check for fabrication by classifying numerical claims, file references, and external citations as confirmed, unverified, or disputed against the evidence present in the session's workspace. Audit reports are saved and communicated to the PI.

All agents adhere to a shared system constitution, a role-specific definition, and access a modular skill library. They operate with standardized messaging and self-recording contracts to log their actions to the Graph of Trace. A skill-router tool enables agents to query and load task-relevant modular capabilities (skills) on demand, promoting a "skills-first" approach for methodological decisions.

#### 3.2. Domain Knowledge

BrainPilot grounds its agents in brain science using two core knowledge assets built offline:

- **Retrieval Knowledge Base:** This is a unified vector index containing 7,233 indexed items, including open-source neuroscience textbooks and research papers. The corpus spans 11 disciplinary groups, with psychiatry, psychology/cognition, systems/imaging neuroscience, and AI/ML/CS being the largest. The construction process involves converting source documents to text via OCR, splitting the text into overlapping passages, and embedding each passage into a 1,024-dimensional vector using BAAI bge-m3. During runtime, an agent's query is embedded, candidate passages are retrieved by vector similarity, and then reordered by relevance using a cross-encoder reranker before being returned as evidence.
- **Skill Library:** This library contains 72 reusable methodology units (skills) across seven major research domains. Skills can be descriptive (outlining a method and its application) or executable (containing code for routines). These skills are sourced from three pipelines: an LLM-driven authoring process for general-purpose skills, a paper-derived pipeline for distilling methods from brain science publications, and a tool-derived pipeline that converts established analysis software into practical guides (e.g., DeepLabCut, MNE-Python, fMRIPrep). Meta-skills are also included to facilitate the ongoing expansion of the library from new publications, software repositories, and completed workflows.

#### 3.3. Graph of Trace (GoT)

The GoT is a central component for ensuring workflow inspectability and auditability. It is implemented as a tool accessible to the agents. Upon completing a subtask, each major agent reports its outputs to the GoT tool. This tool constructs a directed acyclic graph by converting each report into a schema-validated node containing a description, references to produced artifacts, and identifiers of parent steps. It infers dependency edges to capture causal or logical progression beyond mere temporal order. The GoT maintains an append-only record, preventing retrospective edits, cycles, or orphaned references. A frontend interface allows users to monitor the graph in real time, inspect node metadata, and review the research trajectory for potential corrections.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of BrainPilot involved quantitative assessments on established benchmarks and qualitative end-to-end case studies.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on Agents’ Last Exam (ALE)

BrainPilot was tested on three brain science tasks from Agents’ Last Exam (ALE): skull-stripping quality control (T1), ROI mask resampling (T2), and C. elegans neuron tracking (T3). The performance was compared against Codex and Claude Code harnesses, using various backbone LLMs (DeepSeek-V4-Pro, GLM-5.2, GPT-5.5, Claude-Opus-4.8), both with and without domain knowledge (DK) enabled.

- **Performance:** With domain knowledge enabled, BrainPilot generally demonstrated comparable performance to state-of-the-art agent frameworks. For instance, on T2, performance was often identical across harnesses for matched backbones, with BrainPilot and Codex reaching full credit with GPT-5.5. On T1, BrainPilot showed robustness with DeepSeek-V4-Pro, while Claude Code performed well with Claude-Opus-4.8. However, BrainPilot exhibited lower reliability on the more open-ended neuron-tracking task (T3).
- **Efficiency (Time and Cost):** BrainPilot consistently achieved a substantial time and cost advantage. Across matched backbones with DK enabled, BrainPilot completed runs in 8–63% of the time and at 5–56% of the cost required by Codex or Claude Code. The cheapest configuration was BrainPilot with DeepSeek-V4-Pro, totaling $0.08 for the three tasks, while Codex with GPT-5.5 was the most expensive at $22.53. The open-ended T3 task accounted for most of the cost and time differences.

#### 4.2. BrainPilotBench-v0

A new benchmark, BrainPilotBench-v0, was introduced specifically for brain science agents, comprising four tasks: RSC place-cell analysis (T1), TOPS-fMRI (T2), BCI Competition IV 2a (T3), and Sleep-EDF (T4).

- **Performance:** With domain knowledge enabled, BrainPilot with Claude-Opus-4.8 achieved the highest score on RSC (1.00) and BCI IV 2a (0.20), and outperformed the matched Claude Code configuration on TOPS-fMRI (0.47 vs. 0.41), while performing similarly on Sleep-EDF (0.65 vs. 0.66). BrainPilot with DeepSeek-V4-Pro successfully produced a valid RSC result where Claude Code failed. The impact of domain knowledge on scores was not uniformly positive, increasing 8 scores, reducing 14, and leaving 4 unchanged across valid cells. BrainPilot generally utilized more domain knowledge tools.
- **Efficiency (Time and Cost):** BrainPilot with DeepSeek-V4-Pro was faster (80.2 minutes) but slightly more expensive ($0.504) than the matched Claude Code configuration (91.9 minutes, $0.364). With GLM-5.2 and Claude-Opus-4.8, BrainPilot required approximately twice the runtime and incurred 2.5 to 5.8 times the cost compared to the corresponding Claude Code runs.

#### 4.3. End-to-End Case Studies

Five end-to-end case studies demonstrated BrainPilot's capability in real-world scientific research scenarios:

- **Spatial Coding in the Retrosplenial Cortex (RSC):** BrainPilot successfully translated a high-level scientific objective into a coherent and reproducible analysis pipeline. It performed place-cell screening, visualization, Bayesian position decoding, trial-bin correlation, and firing-rate dynamics analyses. The outputs included localized activity heatmaps, population maps, decoding performance metrics (e.g., mean absolute error 16.8 cm, correlation r=0.646), and stability analyses, organized into complementary levels of evidence.
- **Functional Hierarchy in the Mouse Visual System:** The system conducted a literature review to identify classical measures of sensory hierarchy and designed an analysis pipeline using large-scale Neuropixels electrophysiology data. It found positive associations between functional measures (response onset latency, intrinsic timescale, receptive-field diameter) and anatomical hierarchy scores (e.g., RF diameter ρ = +0.714, p = 0.058), even if not all reached conventional statistical significance. The case also demonstrated the agent's ability to perform intermediate diagnosis and pipeline revision, such as adding a responsiveness criterion after an initial smoke test revealed noise sensitivity.
- **Functional-Connectivity fMRI Pain Decoding:** BrainPilot implemented an experimental tonic-pain connectivity-signature analysis, training on functional connectivity data and evaluating its transfer to independent clinical pain datasets. The learned signature predicted held-out tonic pain (r=0.5791), partially generalized to clinical back-pain severity (e.g., r=0.333, p=0.0048 for subacute-back-pain spontaneous-pain condition), and classified chronic-back-pain patients from controls with AUCs of 0.793 (Japan) and 0.699 (UK). The analysis identified pain-related regions, including the ventral striatum, brainstem, dACC, aINS, and PAG.
- **EEG Motor-Imagery Decoding on BCI Competition IV 2a:** The system designed and evaluated a four-class EEG motor-imagery decoder. The MIAgentModel, combining an EEGNet backbone with a TCN, outperformed an EEGNet baseline in 7 out of 9 subjects, with mean accuracy increasing from 0.580 to 0.620 and Cohen’s kappa from 0.440 to 0.493, though these improvements were not statistically significant. Activation topomaps showed class-dependent sensorimotor patterns consistent with physiological priors.
- **Sleep-EDF Sleep Staging with Downstream Decoding and Physiological Validation:** BrainPilot performed five-class sleep staging on single-channel Fpz-Cz EEG recordings. The SleepAgentModel achieved 0.794 accuracy, 0.725 macro-F1, and 0.716 Cohen’s kappa, surpassing random baselines but slightly below DeepSleepNet references. Physiological validation through power spectra and band-power heatmaps aligned with known sleep physiology (e.g., highest delta power in N3, sigma enhancement in N2).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

BrainPilot represents an advancement in the application of AI agents to complex scientific discovery, specifically within the demanding field of brain science. Its significance lies in its structured approach to integrating AI acceleration with mechanisms for scientific validity, transparency, and human oversight. By grounding agents in curated domain knowledge through a retrieval knowledge base and a modular skill library, the system addresses the critical limitation of generic AI models lacking specialized expertise. The Graph of Trace and the dedicated Auditor agent provide an auditable record of the research process and incorporate fabrication checking, directly confronting common challenges such as AI hallucination and reasoning drift.

The potential impact of BrainPilot is multifaceted:

- **Acceleration of Discovery:** By automating complex and iterative research tasks, BrainPilot can enable researchers to allocate more time to conceptualization, hypothesis generation, and critical interpretation, potentially accelerating the pace of brain science discoveries.
- **Enhanced Reproducibility and Trustworthiness:** The auditable Graph of Trace and agent-verified results contribute to greater transparency and reproducibility in scientific workflows, which are crucial for building trust in AI-assisted science.
- **Democratization of Complex Methods:** The structured skill library and agent-guided execution could lower the barrier for researchers to adopt and correctly apply advanced analytical methods, potentially broadening access to sophisticated scientific tools.
- **Improved Human-AI Collaboration:** The explicit human-in-the-loop design, allowing for pre-execution discussions, in-process inspection, and post-analysis review, aims to foster more effective and reliable collaborations between human experts and AI systems.
- **Cost-Effectiveness:** The evaluation results indicate that BrainPilot, particularly when coupled with efficient open-source backbone models, can offer a cost-effective alternative for conducting complex scientific tasks compared to some established agent frameworks.

Despite these advancements, the authors acknowledge several limitations and outline future directions. These include the need for more comprehensive, paper-grounded, multi-step benchmarks for scientific agents, requiring community collaboration to expand BrainPilotBench-v0. Further development will also focus on continually expanding the domain knowledge and skill library with precise, laboratory-derived expertise. Enhancing human-in-the-loop capabilities to include more rigorous checks for methodological soundness and statistical validity beyond basic fabrication detection is also a priority. Future iterations are expected to support more compositional workflows, allowing specialist agents to initiate their own sub-workflows for demanding tasks like in-depth literature reviews. Finally, a significant long-term goal is to extend BrainPilot's capabilities to multimodal and cross-modal data integration, enabling it to jointly process, align, and reason over heterogeneous datasets (e.g., electrophysiology, imaging, behavior, molecular data) to address more intricate questions at the intersection of different data types.
