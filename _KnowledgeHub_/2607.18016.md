# Closing the Loop in Humanoid VLA: Persistent 3D Object Tokens for Verifiable Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed Persistent Object Tokenization (POT) and integrated it into POT-VLA, a Vision-Language-Action system for humanoid robots that addresses "object-state divergence" in long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks. The system establishes a closed-loop framework where role-indexed 3D object records consistently inform actions and enable geometric predicate-based verification, achieving 71 out of 80 successful real-world trials on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, outperforming a direct baseline (39/80).

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies implicitly encode task-relevant object states, leading to "object-state divergence" where the robot's internal object understanding deviates from physical reality.
- Humanoid loco-manipulation tasks require maintaining a persistent and verifiable understanding of objects across dynamic scenarios involving movement, contact, and occlusion, which current VLA approaches struggle with.
- Verification and replanning systems often use symbolic or skill-centric feedback decoupled from learned action streams, hindering accurate diagnosis and recovery from physical object interaction failures.

## Method

- Introduces ==Persistent Object Tokenization (POT)== to create and maintain a shared, role-indexed 3D object memory that continuously refreshes object metric evidence from RGB-D observations.
- Embeds these "==Persistent 3D Object Tokens==" into the action-head self-attention sequence of a whole-body action expert (==GR00T-N1.7==), enabling action prediction conditioned on explicit 3D object states.
- Develops a closed-loop **POT-VLA** system where the action expert and a ==geometric predicate supervisor== share the same refreshed object memory for action generation, execution, and subsequent verification and recovery.

## Results

- POT-VLA achieved **71 out of 80** successful trials across eight diverse real-world loco-manipulation task families on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, a substantial improvement over the direct baseline's **39 out of 80** successes.
- Ablation studies showed that object-token conditioning alone improved success rates to **31/40** (vs. **15/40** for baseline), with the full POT-VLA system reaching **34/40**, indicating the primary gain from explicit object state for action, with additional benefits from verification.
- The system demonstrated increased robustness against object-state shifts, including novel object instances (**9/10** successes), perturbed object layouts (**9/10** successes), and mid-execution perturbations (**8/10** successes), compared to the direct baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly conditioning the VLA action expert with persistent, role-indexed 3D object tokens provides a consistent and actionable object-level physical memory, significantly improving task execution.
> - A closed-loop system where both action generation and geometric predicate-based verification rely on a shared, refreshed 3D object memory effectively mitigates "object-state divergence" in long-horizon tasks.
> - Decomposing object interaction into persistent records, tokens for action conditioning, and geometric predicates for verification enables granular error detection and localized recovery, reducing the need for full task replanning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge within the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies for general robot control, particularly for long-horizon humanoid loco-manipulation tasks. VLA policies represent a promising direction for enabling robots to understand natural language commands and execute complex physical actions by integrating visual perception and robot state with large language models. Existing VLA policies often encode task-relevant object state implicitly within visual-language features, which can limit the robot's ability to maintain a consistent understanding of objects across dynamic scenarios involving movement, contact, occlusion, and recovery.

Previous research in generalist VLA policies has shown success in broad visuomotor generalization but often struggles with the persistence and verifiability of object states. Object-centric 3D grounding methods aim to translate language or visual goals into actionable spatial representations, providing current targets or affordances. However, humanoid loco-manipulation demands that task entities remain consistently addressable over extended periods and varied actions, a requirement not fully met by approaches focused on instantaneous targets.

In humanoid loco-manipulation systems, which combine locomotion, stance selection, reachability, manipulation, and balance, modular skills or VLM-based monitoring are sometimes integrated with high-level reasoning. While some learning-heavy whole-body policies aim for broad generalization, they often lack a mechanism for explicit, shared object-level state that is both actionable by the policy and verifiable after execution.

Execution verification and replanning systems commonly decompose instructions, select skills, and use feedback for replanning. However, their feedback state is frequently symbolic or skill-centric, and often decoupled from the learned action stream. This separation can lead to "object-state divergence," where the robot's internal understanding of an object's state for action generation differs from the state used to verify the action's physical outcome. This divergence can result in issues such as incorrect object manipulation, premature subtask transitions, or ineffective recovery.

This paper positions its contribution, Persistent Object Tokenization (POT), as a coupling representation that provides a refreshed, object-level physical memory. This memory is designed to be simultaneously actionable by the policy and verifiable after execution, thereby bridging the gap identified in current VLA, object-centric grounding, humanoid control, and verification systems. The work aims to close the perception-action-verification loop using a shared object memory to mitigate object-state divergence, a practical failure mode in long-horizon humanoid VLA.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable humanoid robots to reliably execute long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks by addressing the problem of "object-state divergence." This divergence occurs when the representation of an object's state used to generate an action differs from the state representation used to verify whether that action achieved its intended physical effect.

The motivation for addressing this problem stems from the practical challenges faced by humanoid robots in real-world scenarios. Commands like "tidy up the desk" or "bring me a drink" require robots to perceive objects, navigate complex environments, manipulate specific items, and continuously monitor task progress. Throughout such multi-step processes, the robot must maintain a persistent understanding of task-relevant entities (e.g., a specific cup, a basket, a support surface, or a handover partner), even as these objects move, are contacted, become occluded, or require recovery actions. If the object state used for planning or acting does not align with the state used for verification, the robot can fail in subtle but critical ways, such as:
- **Premature subtask transitions:** Moving to the next step before the current one is truly completed.
- **Wrong-object manipulation:** Interacting with an incorrect object due to misidentification or inconsistent state tracking.
- **Ineffective recovery:** Failing to correct errors because the system cannot accurately diagnose why an action failed in terms of physical object relations.

The underlying issue is described as physical, related to objects' metric 3D positions, contacts, containment, and visibility changes, rather than purely semantic. Therefore, the core motivation is to develop an object-centered state representation that possesses three key attributes:
1. **Persistence:** The object's identity and associated role (e.g., TARGET, DESTINATION) must be maintained across multiple action chunks and observations, even through occlusions or displacements.
2. **Actionability:** The representation must be directly consumable by the robot's action policy to guide whole-body movements and manipulations.
3. **Verifiability:** The representation must be suitable for robust, grounded geometric predicate checks to confirm whether intended physical relations have been achieved after an action.

To achieve this, the authors propose Persistent Object Tokenization (POT) as a method to lift RGB-D evidence into role-indexed 3D object records. These records are then serialized into "Persistent 3D Object Tokens" for a whole-body action expert. By implementing this as POT-VLA, the research aims to establish a closed-loop execution system where object state is both actionable and verifiable, ensuring consistent grounding of loco-manipulation actions in task-relevant physical object state.

The specific contributions outlined in the paper further detail these objectives:
- **Introduction of POT:** To provide a role-aware object-level physical memory that binds task entities to persistent role slots, refreshes their metric 3D evidence, and serializes this information into Persistent 3D Object Tokens.
- **Integration of Object Tokens:** To insert these tokens into the whole-body action-head sequence, enabling action prediction conditioned on explicit 3D object states (target, destination, support, handover).
- **Development of a Closed-Loop System:** To build POT-VLA where the action expert and predicate verifier share the same refreshed object memory, allowing grounded geometric predicates to verify physical relations and trigger recovery.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system, POT-VLA, operates as a closed-loop humanoid loco-manipulation framework. It processes natural language instructions (I), RGB-D observations, and proprioception to perform tasks. A central component is the role-indexed object memory, M_τ_i_t, which is maintained for the active typed subtask τ_i and shared between the action expert and a predicate verifier. The system follows a ground–act–refresh–verify–recover cycle, where object memory is built, tokenized for action, updated after execution, checked by predicates, and recovered if necessary.

#### 3.1. Persistent Object Tokenization (POT)

POT is responsible for creating and maintaining the shared object memory.
- **Task-role object records:** The process begins with a typed-subtask plan (Π) derived from instruction I, which specifies task-relevant entities (E_i), roles, grounding queries, and success predicates for each subtask τ_i. From current RGB-D observation (o_t) and proprioception (s_t), POT constructs a memory M_τ_i_t = {m_e_t | e ∈ E_i}. Each record m_e_t stores an entity's role, language phrase, 2D image bounding box, 3D centroid and extent, confidence, visibility, and relation features. The implementation utilizes online or cached SAM3 masks for role/query mask generation. Valid depth pixels are back-projected to 3D via camera intrinsics and a camera-to-base transform, filtered by workspace bounds, and summarized in the robot's base frame. Relation features, such as object-container distance, support height, end-effector-object offset, and alignment, are also included.
- **Persistent token schema:** The memory M_τ_i_t is tokenized into Persistent 3D Object Tokens, x_obj_t = T(M_τ_i_t). A fixed schema of K=8 slots is used, with F=33 features per slot. Slots are assigned role IDs (e.g., TARGET, DESTINATION, SUPPORT, HANDOVER_PARTNER). Unused slots are padded and masked. Slot features include visibility, confidence, normalized 2D box coordinates, 3D centroid, 3D extent, optional orientation fields, and relation features. The persistence aspect means task entities remain bound to their assigned role slots, while their metric evidence is refreshed after each action chunk. If an object becomes occluded or its confidence is low, the slot remains, but visibility and confidence fields indicate uncertainty, prompting re-observation rather than treating the object as confirmed. These tokens (x_obj_t, role_ids, token_mask) are consumed by the action head to expose role-indexed object state to both the action expert and the predicate supervisor.

#### 3.2. Object-Token Conditioned VLA Execution

The action expert is instantiated using GR00T-N1.7, maintaining the same Unitree-G1 whole-body action representation and runtime as a direct baseline.
- **Role-aware object projection:** The processor converts task-relevant object records into x_obj_t ∈ R^(K×F). Each slot k contains features like visibility (v_k), confidence (q_k), 2D box (b_k), 3D centroid (c_k), 3D extent (s_k), optional orientation (o_k), and relation features (ρ_k). All metric fields are expressed in the robot base frame and normalized before tokenization. An object-token projector embeds these features into the DiT hidden dimension, Z_obj_t = f_obj_θ(x_obj_t) ∈ R^(K×d), where f_obj_θ is a LayerNorm → Linear → GELU → Dropout → Linear projection. Each projected slot is combined with a learned role embedding and a learned context embedding. Padded slots are masked, and confidence gating can scale features based on visibility and confidence.
- **Action-head insertion:** Object tokens are inserted into the DiT action-head self-attention sequence, not into the visual-language backbone. The sequence layout is S_t = [Z_state_t, Z_obj_t, Z_action_t], where Z_state_t, Z_obj_t, and Z_action_t represent state, object, and action tokens, respectively. Visual-language backbone features (Z_vl_t) serve as encoder hidden states for cross-attention. The action expert predicts a candidate whole-body chunk ˆa_t:t+H = π_θ(S_t, Z_vl_t), encompassing navigation, reaching, grasping, carrying, placing, or handover. The executed chunk a_t:t+H undergoes runtime validation and smoothing.
- **Training and adaptation:** POT-VLA uses the same action-chunk prediction objective as the base action expert without additional object-memory or predicate losses. During object-token fine-tuning, demonstrations are augmented with object-token sidecars generated by the same RGB-D schema used at deployment. Gradients from the action loss update the object-token branch and action-head parameters.
- **Closed-loop execution and safety:** The system executes only one validated short-horizon chunk before refreshing perception. At t+ = t + H, RGB-D observations update the object memory to M_τ_i_t+, which is then used to regenerate Z_obj_t+ for continued subtask execution or recovery. The humanoid runtime includes command smoothing, joint/velocity limits, balance checks, collision constraints, and emergency stops.

#### 3.3. Predicate-Level Verification and Recovery

A geometric predicate supervisor acts as the physical consistency layer of POT-VLA.
- **Verification process:** It reads the same role-indexed object memory (M_τ_i_t) that conditioned the action policy. After an action chunk's execution, it verifies the refreshed memory (M_τ_i_t+). A subtask is deemed complete only if the intended metric relation holds in the refreshed 3D object state.
- **Predicates:** A set of physical-relation predicates Ψ_i is defined for each subtask τ_i, covering conditions like containment, support, end-effector proximity, target-destination displacement, bimanual assignment, and handover-partner distance. Each predicate is structured as p = ⟨κ, α, op, ν, n⟩, denoting predicate type, grounded object-record arguments, comparison operator, threshold/target value, and required stability window. Thresholds and stability windows are specified by the subtask and robot calibration.
- **Evaluation and status:** At verification index ξ, scalar evidence ϕ(κ, α, M_τ_i_ξ) determines predicate satisfaction. Invisible, low-confidence, or unstable records result in an "uncertain" status, unless safety is compromised. The supervisor evaluates readiness using preconditions (P_i) and completion using goals (G_i), returning statuses like {in_progress, done, blocked, failed, uncertain}.
- **Diagnostics and recovery:** The supervisor provides diagnostics (d_ξ) that localize failure modes (e.g., missing visibility, failed grasping, unstable support, incorrect placement). Based on status and diagnostics, POT-VLA selects recovery actions from {continue, retry, reobserve, reground, replan}. Local recovery mechanisms refresh or reground object records before task-level replanning receives compact feedback.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of POT-VLA was conducted on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot equipped with a Dex3-1 dexterous hand and a head-mounted RGB-D camera in a cluttered office environment. Experiments involved 10 real-world trials per entry, with success defined by satisfying final geometric predicates over a required temporal window within predefined timeouts and retry budgets, without human intervention.

#### 4.1. Main Real-World Results

The study presents two sets of results: a primary matched comparison against a direct baseline and an external reference against Being-0.

- **Matched direct baseline comparison:** Across eight real-world task families (including cart transport/placement, object-to-basket placement, cup stacking, garment handling, drawer/tray interaction, tabletop sorting, and close-range handover), POT-VLA achieved 71 out of 80 successful trials. The direct baseline, which used the same action backbone, embodiment, runtime, and task-language prompt but lacked persistent object records, object tokens, and geometric predicate supervision, achieved 39 out of 80 successes.
 - The largest performance differences were observed in tasks requiring the maintenance of object relations across multiple action chunks, such as cup stacking (POT-VLA: 8/10, Direct: 1/10), two-ball placement (POT-VLA: 9/10, Direct: 5/10), garment transport (POT-VLA: 9/10, Direct: 3/10), and drawer/tray interaction (POT-VLA: 8/10, Direct: 4/10).
 - The authors attribute direct baseline failures to metric object-state errors (e.g., inaccurate grasp targets, misaligned placements) rather than semantic misunderstandings. In contrast, POT-VLA's mechanism of verifying refreshed object memory allowed for re-observation, re-grounding, or retries upon such failures, preventing silent task progression.

- **External Being-0-aligned reference:** On a suite of five service tasks aligned with those reported in the Being-0 paper (Fetch-bottle, Deliver-basket, Grasp-bottle, Place-basket, Place-coffee), POT-VLA achieved 44 out of 50 successes. The Being-0 paper reported 37 out of 50 successes on its corresponding service tasks. This comparison serves as an external reference and not a local reproduction.

#### 4.2. Ablations and Object-State Generalization

Ablation studies and generalization tests were conducted to isolate the contributions of object-token conditioning and closed-loop verification, and to assess robustness.

- **Shared object-state loop ablations:** Performed on a subset of four tasks with significant state-divergence gaps from the main comparison (two-ball placement, cup stacking, drawer/tray interaction, and tabletop sorting), totaling 40 trials per variant.
 - The **direct baseline** (no tokens, no verifier) achieved 15/40 successes.
 - The **"Verifier only"** variant (direct action path with predicate checks and local recovery) improved success to 22/40, indicating the value of feedback.
 - The **"POT tokens only"** variant (action head conditioned on object tokens, no predicate-level recovery) achieved 31/40 successes, demonstrating that explicit role-indexed 3D state for action prediction provides a substantial gain.
 - **POT-VLA** (full system with tokens and verifier) achieved 34/40 successes, suggesting that predicate-level verification offers additional, albeit smaller, improvements on top of the gains from object-token conditioning. This aligns with the designed division of labor, where tokens address grounding errors pre-action and verification handles residual failures post-action.

- **Generalization under object-state shifts:** Evaluated robustness under various perturbations, with 10 matched trials per setting.
 - **Novel object instances:** Replacing task-role objects with unseen items (varying category, appearance, geometry) resulted in POT-VLA achieving 9/10 successes compared to 6/10 for the direct baseline.
 - **Shifted object poses/layouts:** Displacing target objects, goal containers, or altering orientations led to POT-VLA achieving 9/10 successes versus 5/10 for the direct baseline.
 - **Distractor objects:** Adding visually or functionally similar objects near the workspace resulted in POT-VLA achieving 9/10 successes, while the direct baseline achieved 8/10. Distractors had a comparatively smaller impact on both systems.
 - **Mid-execution perturbations:** Moving a target object slightly before grasping or after a partial action chunk resulted in POT-VLA achieving 8/10 successes compared to 4/10 for the direct baseline, highlighting the benefit of refreshed object records and local recovery.

- **Qualitative observations:** Role-aware 3D object tokens were observed to reduce target-search oscillation and improve contact-relevant localization. Persistent object records reduced per-chunk identity jitter, and the geometric supervisor facilitated local subtask refreshes instead of requiring full language-level replanning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Persistent Object Tokenization (POT) and its integration into a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) system, POT-VLA, representing an advancement in humanoid loco-manipulation capabilities. The core significance lies in its systematic approach to mitigating "object-state divergence," a identified practical failure mode that hinders the reliable execution of long-horizon tasks in real-world environments.

By establishing a closed-loop system where role-indexed 3D object records are consistently maintained, used to condition whole-body actions, and subsequently verified through grounded geometric predicates, the work addresses a fundamental challenge in robot autonomy. This shared object memory ensures that the robot's understanding of task-relevant entities is both actionable by its policy and verifiable after its actions, promoting physical consistency in task execution.

The quantitative results demonstrate a substantial improvement in real-world task success on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, elevating success rates from 39/80 with a direct baseline to 71/80 with POT-VLA across a diverse suite of eight manipulation and locomotion tasks. The most pronounced gains were observed in tasks demanding the maintenance of 3D object relations over multiple steps, such as stacking and detailed placement, which often expose the limitations of implicitly encoded object states.

The ablation studies further delineate the individual contributions, indicating that the explicit conditioning of the action expert with Persistent 3D Object Tokens provides the most significant performance boost, while the predicate-level verification layer offers additional gains by enabling robust recovery from partial failures or false completions. Furthermore, the generalization tests highlight POT-VLA's increased robustness against various object-state shifts, including novel object instances, perturbed layouts, and mid-execution disturbances, suggesting a more adaptable and reliable system.

The findings indicate that persistent object-centered state is a useful abstraction for verifiable humanoid VLA execution. This approach could lead to humanoid robots that are more capable of sustained, complex operations in dynamic and unstructured environments, requiring less human intervention and exhibiting improved error recovery.

Potential impacts include:
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** Enabling robots to handle more complex and long-horizon tasks with greater reliability and less susceptibility to perceptual and execution errors.
- **Improved Safety and Robustness:** The verifiable nature of the object state allows for more precise detection of physical inconsistencies, potentially leading to safer and more predictable robot behavior in human-robot collaboration scenarios.
- **Foundation for Generalist Robots:** Providing a structured, explicit representation for object interaction that could be integrated into future generalist robot platforms, allowing them to leverage broad VLA models while maintaining fine-grained control and verification over physical reality.
- **Advancements in Robot Learning:** The method of using object-token sidecars for training alongside traditional VLA data offers a pathway for integrating explicit physical understanding into learned policies without necessarily requiring a full physics engine or learned dynamics model.

The authors acknowledge current limitations related to the quality of object records, the reliability of underlying perception (SAM3/RGB-D), camera calibration, and the coverage of the action expert's embodiment. Future work plans to address these limitations by exploring active multi-view memory, richer contact modeling, enhanced dexterity, and more sophisticated human interaction, which could further broaden the applicability and impact of this research.
