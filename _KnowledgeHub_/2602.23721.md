# StemVLA:An Open-Source Vision-Language-Action Model with Future 3D Spatial Geometry Knowledge and 4D Historical Representation

> [!summary] Summary
> StemVLA, an open-source Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model, integrates future 3D spatial geometry knowledge and 4D historical spatiotemporal representations to enhance robotic manipulation. It achieves an average accuracy of 92.0% across the LIBERO benchmark, improving task success rates by incorporating explicit spatial and temporal world understanding.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models primarily rely on implicit 2D visual representations, lacking explicit 3D spatial structure and depth crucial for precise robotic manipulation.
- Current approaches often process historical observations frame-wise, inadequately capturing coherent spatiotemporal dynamics required for long-horizon tasks.
- Future state prediction methods are often computationally intensive due to full-resolution image frame generation, rather than structured 3D geometric changes relevant for robot actions.

## Method

- Introduces **StemVLA**, a ==Transformer==-based architecture that integrates 2D images, 3D spatial-geometric information, 4D spatiotemporal data, and linguistic instructions for robotic manipulation.
- Develops a ==4D Historical Spatiotemporal Representation== by using a ==hybrid encoder== to aggregate latent 3D spatial-geometric features from historical 2D images over time.
- Implements a **3D Future Spatial-Geometric World Knowledge Predictor (FSGWP)** module, which explicitly supervises the model to predict the 3D geometric structure of future environments.

## Results

- StemVLA achieved an average accuracy of **92.0%** across all LIBERO subsets, notably outperforming prior VLA models like SpatialVLA (**78.1%**) and CoT-VLA (**69.0%**).
- The 4D Historical Spatiotemporal Representation module significantly improved performance, with examples including a boost from **83.5% to 86.0%** on LIBERO-Long tasks.
- The 3D Future Spatial-Geometric World Knowledge Predictor (FSGWP) module demonstrated substantial performance gains, raising LIBERO-Long accuracy from **67.0% to 86.0%** when included.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling future 3D spatial geometry allows VLA models to anticipate scene configurations, leading to more precise and spatially rational action generation.
> - Integrating a comprehensive 4D historical spatiotemporal representation provides a richer context for dynamic perception, motion forecasting, and long-horizon action planning.
> - Fusing multi-level representations (2D visual, 3D spatial, 4D spatiotemporal) within a shared MLLM backbone creates a compact and comprehensive world understanding for robust control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

This work operates within the field of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on language-conditioned robotic manipulation. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a significant direction in this area, integrating visual perception, language understanding, and low-level control to enable robots to execute tasks based on high-level instructions.

Historically, VLA approaches have primarily relied on processing 2D visual inputs and mapping them directly to action sequences. While these methods have achieved notable results, they often lack explicit modeling of the 3D spatial structure of the environment and the temporal dynamics of object and robot motion. This limitation can hinder robust spatial reasoning and long-horizon decision-making in complex and dynamic environments.

Previous attempts to enhance world understanding include jointly modeling image prediction and action prediction within unified architectures. However, these methods frequently face challenges such as predicting redundant full-resolution future frames, modeling 3D spatial geometry only at the pixel level, and encoding historical observations frame-wise, which limits the capture of coherent spatiotemporal dynamics. Furthermore, a heavy reliance on high-level semantic embeddings can sometimes obscure the fine-grained physical details necessary for precise manipulation.

StemVLA positions itself by addressing these limitations through the explicit incorporation of both future-oriented 3D spatial-geometric knowledge and historical 4D spatiotemporal representations. It aims to evolve representational forms beyond static 2D images and even 3D "snapshots" to dynamic 4D features that capture motion and change over time. By doing so, StemVLA seeks to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the world for robotic manipulation, enhancing the robot's ability to reason about anticipated future states and integrate a rich history of motion and interaction. This contrasts with approaches that either lack structured 3D/4D information or rely on two-stage prediction-then-plan paradigms which can incur latency.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in robotic manipulation by improving their spatiotemporal comprehension and action prediction. The overarching goal is to achieve dual enhancements in both end-to-end performance and generalization capability for complex, continuous robotic manipulation tasks.

The motivation for developing StemVLA stems from several critical limitations observed in existing VLA frameworks:

1. **Lack of Explicit 3D Spatial Reasoning**: Many current VLA models rely on implicit 2D visual representations. While effective for some tasks, these representations inherently lack explicit information about object depth, geometric structure, and scene layout. Such 3D spatial knowledge is crucial for a robot to accurately perceive its environment, understand spatial relationships between objects, and execute precise manipulation actions in a physical world.
2. **Insufficient Temporal Dynamics Modeling**: Existing approaches often process historical observations in a frame-wise manner, which may not adequately capture the coherent spatiotemporal dynamics and motion patterns over time. Robust robotic control, especially for long-horizon tasks, necessitates an understanding of how the environment evolves and how objects move, requiring a representation that integrates spatial information across the temporal dimension (i.e., 4D spatiotemporal representations).
3. **Inefficiencies in Future Prediction**: Attempts to forecast future states often involve predicting full-resolution future image frames. This approach can be computationally intensive due to redundancy (many predicted pixels overlap with current observations) and may not explicitly capture structured 3D geometric changes, instead offering pixel-level extrapolations. The motivation is to shift from pixel-level future image prediction to structured 3D future spatial-geometric world knowledge prediction, which is more directly relevant for robotic actions.
4. **Loss of Fine-Grained Physical Details**: Some VLA systems, particularly those heavily relying on high-level semantic embeddings from large language models, may inadvertently abstract away fine-grained physical details necessary for precise manipulation. The objective is to retain these details by operating on implicit 3D representations extracted directly from visual inputs, thereby preserving rich geometric and physical characteristics.

To address these motivations, StemVLA aims to explicitly incorporate two key components: future-oriented 3D spatial-geometric world knowledge and historical 4D spatiotemporal representations, facilitating a more comprehensive understanding of the environment for improved robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

StemVLA proposes a unified Transformer-based architecture designed for robotic manipulation tasks by integrating 2D images, 3D spatial-geometric information, 4D spatiotemporal information, and linguistic instructions. The model generates dense, sequential action trajectories within a 4D action space.

The framework comprises four core components: Modality-Specific Inputs, a Shared Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) Backbone, Training-phase-specific 3D Future Spatial-Geometric Supervision, and Action Outputs.

**A. Modality-Specific Inputs**
StemVLA processes heterogeneous inputs through dedicated encoders:
- **Linguistic Instructions ($I_t$)**: Encoded using a frozen CLIP text encoder.
- **2D Image Observations ($O_t$)**: Processed by a Masked AutoEncoder to extract fine-grained representations (e.g., color, texture).
- **Proprioceptive States ($S_t$)**: Encoded via a combination of an attention module and fully-connected layers.
- **Historical Video Sequences ($A_h$)**: A hybrid architecture, termed the Historical Spatio Temporal Encoder, is used. This component integrates the VGGT Aggregator to extract latent 3D spatial-geometric features from historical 2D images. These 3D features are then fed into the VideoFormer (referred to as the History Aggregator) to fuse them across the temporal dimension, generating comprehensive 4D historical spatiotemporal representations ($F_{t-h:t}^{4D}$). This design captures both fine-grained spatial structures and long-range temporal dependencies.
- **Learnable Query Vectors**: Two distinct query vectors are embedded: a `<spatial-geometric>` query to enable prediction of 3D future spatial-geometric information, and an `<action>` query to aggregate task-relevant information for future action sequence prediction.

**B. Shared MLLM Backbone**
A GPT-2-based variant of an MLLM serves as the central fusion backbone. This backbone integrates information from all encoded modalities and query tokens. It aggregates low-level 2D visual features, middle-level 3D spatial-geometric features, and high-level coherent 4D historical spatiotemporal representations to construct a compact latent representation, termed the spatial embedding ($E_{spatial}$). This multi-level fusion contributes to a comprehensive world understanding.

**C. Training-phase-specific 3D Future Spatial-Geometric Supervision (FSGWP)**
This module is crucial for constraining the model to learn precise spatial-geometric representations through explicit supervision. It consists of three sub-modules:
1. **Label Generation Module**: Receives visual observation data from future timestamps ($O_{t:t+n}$). It utilizes the VGGT Aggregator to extract spatial-geometric features and generate ground-truth labels for the 3D future spatial-geometric environment ($W_{t+n}^{3D_{gt}}$). These labels specify the target geometric structure of future scenes.
2. **Synchronous Output Module**: Takes the multimodal fusion representations from the MLLM backbone, along with world knowledge embeddings, and decodes them into predicted 3D future spatial-geometric environment information ($W_{t+n}^{3D_{pred}}$). This enables explicit prediction of future scene geometric structure.
3. **3D Future Spatial-Geometry Supervision Module**: Computes the error between the predicted ($W_{t+n}^{3D_{pred}}$) and ground-truth ($W_{t+n}^{3D_{gt}}$) 3D spatial-geometric information using an L2 loss function ($L_{world}$). This supervision mechanism guides the model to learn more accurate spatial-geometric features, enhancing the spatial rationality of subsequent action generation.

**D. Action Outputs**
Conditioned on the spatial embedding ($E_{spatial}$), the MLLM processes the `<action>` query to create a latent action embedding. This compressed representation is then passed to a Denoising-Diffusion Transformer (DiT). The DiT generates the complete n-step action sequence ($A_{t:t+n}$) through an iterative refinement process, denoising Gaussian noise into an actionable trajectory. The action prediction loss ($L_{action}$) is also formulated using an L2 loss.

**E. 4D Historical Spatiotemporal Representation & 3D Future Spatial World Knowledge**
The 4D Historical Spatiotemporal Representation is generated by leveraging the VGGT Aggregator to extract 3D spatial priors from 2D images, and then employing the History Aggregator (VideoFormer) to aggregate this 3D spatial-geometric information over time. This fused representation provides dynamic perception, motion forecasting, causal reasoning, and supports long-horizon action planning. The 3D Future Spatial World Knowledge is generated by the FSGWP, guided by the `<Spatial>` query, enabling the model to actively reconstruct and externalize future geometric information.

**F. Experimental Setup**
Experiments were conducted using PyTorch on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs. The AdamW optimizer with a cosine learning rate schedule was used (VLM LR: 6e-4, Diffusion LR: 1.5e-4, Weight Decay: 1e-4). The batch size was 20, with 10 diffusion steps and a query length of 9 per modality. Loss weights were set to 0.1 for pixel-wise loss, 0.1 for 3D Future Spatial-Geometry World Loss, and 1.0 for Action Loss. The model was pre-trained on the LIBERO-90 dataset and fine-tuned/evaluated on LIBERO-Long, LIBERO-Goal, LIBERO-Spatial, and LIBERO-Object datasets for 40 epochs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of the StemVLA model was evaluated through extensive experiments on the LIBERO benchmark, a simulation platform designed for robotic lifelong learning and cross-task knowledge transfer. This benchmark includes four specialized test suites: LIBERO-Spatial, LIBERO-Object, LIBERO-Goal, and LIBERO-Long, each comprising 10 distinct tasks with 50 human teleoperated demonstration instances.

**A. Main Experiments**
StemVLA was compared against several prior approaches, including Diffusion Policy, Octo, OpenVLA, SpatialVLA, and CoT-VLA. The evaluation focused on the task success rate across the different LIBERO tracks.

- **Overall Performance**: StemVLA achieved an average accuracy of 92.0% across all LIBERO subsets. This represents a substantial improvement compared to the average success rates of previous models, such as SpatialVLA (78.1%) and CoT-VLA (69.0%).
- **Track-Specific Performance**:
 - **LIBERO-Spatial**: StemVLA achieved 96.0%, outperforming all comparative methods (e.g., SpatialVLA at 88.2%).
 - **LIBERO-Object**: StemVLA also reached 96.0%, exceeding prior models (e.g., SpatialVLA at 89.9%).
 - **LIBERO-Goal**: StemVLA demonstrated 92.0% accuracy, surpassing most methods, including CoT-VLA's 91.6% in this specific category.
 - **LIBERO-Long**: StemVLA achieved 86.0%. While CoT-VLA reported 87.6% on this specific track, StemVLA's performance on LIBERO-Long marks a significant improvement over other models like SpatialVLA (55.5%) and Diffusion Policy (50.5%), and its overall average performance is superior due to consistent high scores across other tracks.

These results indicate that StemVLA delivers superior or comparable performance across all test tracks, with its 3D future spatial-geometric world knowledge prediction mechanism being attributed to these gains.

**B. Ablation Study**
A systematic ablation study was conducted to assess the individual contributions of the two core components: 4D Historical Spatiotemporal Representation and 3D Future Spatial Geometry World Knowledge. The model was pre-trained on LIBERO-90 and fine-tuned/evaluated on the four LIBERO subsets.

- **Q1: Effectiveness of 4D Historical Spatiotemporal Representation**:
 - Removing the 4D Historical Spatiotemporal Representation resulted in a notable performance decrease across all subsets. For instance, accuracy dropped from 86.0% to 83.5% on LIBERO-Long, from 96.0% to 92.0% on LIBERO-Object, from 96.0% to 91.5% on LIBERO-Spatial, and from 92.0% to 90.5% on LIBERO-Goal.
 - These findings suggest that integrating the 4D Historical Spatiotemporal Representation, which encapsulates both spatial and temporal information, is beneficial for dynamic perception, motion forecasting, causal event sequence reasoning, and long-horizon action planning, thereby enhancing robotic manipulation performance.

- **Q2: Effectiveness of 3D Future Spatial Geometry World Knowledge**:
 - A significant reduction in performance was observed when the 3D Future Spatial-Geometric World Knowledge Predictor (FSGWP) module was removed. Accuracy dropped from 86.0% to 67.0% on LIBERO-Long, from 96.0% to 78.0% on LIBERO-Object, from 96.0% to 76.5% on LIBERO-Spatial, and from 92.0% to 72.0% on LIBERO-Goal.
 - This demonstrates that the FSGWP module, through its integration of camera parameters, depth information, point cloud data, and trajectory tracking, substantially improves robotic manipulation. The unified representation provided by FSGWP enhances the simultaneous perception of 3D spatial structure, object motion trajectories, and spatial relationships, offering richer geometric and dynamic information that supports more precise action planning and stable execution in complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

StemVLA represents a development in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation by explicitly addressing the limitations of relying on implicit 2D visual representations. Its core contribution lies in the systematic integration of both future-oriented 3D spatial geometry knowledge and historical 4D spatiotemporal representations. This approach facilitates a more comprehensive understanding of the physical world, which is crucial for robust and generalized robot control.

The research demonstrates that by moving beyond frame-wise 2D visual inputs to a more structured and dynamic comprehension of space and time, VLA models can achieve enhanced performance. The framework's ability to forecast structured 3D future geometry allows the robot to anticipate upcoming scene configurations, while the aggregation of historical latent 3D features into a 4D representation provides a richer context for understanding motion dynamics and temporal consistency. This comprehensive world knowledge is a step towards improving physical reasoning and future forecasting capabilities in embodied AI systems.

The experimental results on the LIBERO benchmark indicate that StemVLA consistently achieves higher average success rates compared to several prior state-of-the-art methods. The notable improvements, particularly in long-horizon and spatially complex manipulation tasks, underscore the significance of explicit 3D and 4D representations. The ablation studies further validate the individual contributions of both the 3D future spatial knowledge and the 4D historical spatiotemporal representation, confirming their roles in improving environmental understanding and action stability.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Robot Generalization and Robustness**: By providing a more grounded understanding of the physical world, StemVLA could enable robots to perform a wider variety of manipulation tasks with greater accuracy and robustness, particularly in dynamic and unstructured environments.
- **Improved Long-Horizon Task Execution**: The explicit modeling of future states and historical dynamics is expected to be beneficial for tasks requiring multi-step planning and sustained interaction over time.
- **Advancement in Embodied AI**: The methodology contributes to the broader field of embodied AI by demonstrating effective strategies for integrating richer, physically-grounded representations into perception-action loops.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations that provide directions for future research.
- The current model is restricted to parallel gripper manipulation and was trained in environments with limited geometric and material variability. Future work will explore incorporating demonstrations for dexterous hand manipulation, including detailed contact annotations, to expand the robot's capabilities.
- The reliance on the Denoising-Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture occasionally leads to jerky and sluggish motions, which can hinder the fluency of real-time control. Future research plans to replace DiT with more efficient Flow Matching techniques to enhance motion smoothness and real-time performance.
- To improve generalization and robustness on long-horizon tasks, future efforts will focus on scaling up data collection and integrating on-policy fine-tuning strategies.
