# Learning to Model the World with Language

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UC Berkeley developed Dynalang, an embodied agent that learns a multimodal world model to ground diverse human language by using it as a signal to predict future visual and linguistic observations. This approach allowed the agent to leverage varied language types for improved task performance across several simulated environments and enabled capabilities like grounded language generation and text-only pretraining.

## Problem

- Embodied agents struggle to utilize the full spectrum of human language beyond simple instruction following, limiting natural human-agent interaction.
- Directly mapping diverse linguistic inputs (e.g., descriptions, rules, feedback) to optimal actions is difficult for agents due to complex and indirect correlations.
- Existing language integration methods for embodied agents are often data-intensive, require explicit supervision, or struggle to bridge abstract linguistic knowledge with grounded physical interaction.

## Method

- Develops **Dynalang**, an embodied agent that learns a multimodal world model by extending ==DreamerV3== to jointly process and predict visual and linguistic observations.
- Frames language understanding as a future prediction problem, where language helps the world model predict subsequent observations, rewards, and environment dynamics.
- Trains policies by imagining future sequences of multimodal representations within the learned world model, allowing the agent to learn action selection efficiently.

## Results

- Dynalang's multimodal world model architecture consistently outperformed baselines in fusing language and vision, achieving higher task scores even with learned language embeddings.
- The agent effectively utilized diverse language (future observations, dynamics, corrections, game manuals) to improve task performance in HomeGrid and Messenger environments, while traditional methods often degraded.
- Dynalang demonstrated competitive or superior instruction following in HomeGrid and VLN-CE, and exhibited grounded language generation (e.g., question answering) and improved performance through text-only pretraining.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Grounding diverse language by using it as a signal to predict the future within a multimodal world model provides a robust self-supervised learning objective.
> - Decoupling the self-supervised multimodal world model learning from reinforcement learning-based policy learning enables more robust language understanding and better task performance.
> - A generative world model of language and vision allows for leveraging single-modality text-only pretraining and enables grounded language generation capabilities for agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence is increasingly focused on developing embodied agents that can interact with the physical world and collaborate naturally with humans. Traditionally, integrating language into embodied agents has largely centered on "instruction following," where agents execute specific commands (e.g., "bring me the apple"). While significant progress has been made in this area, the authors argue that human-agent interaction in real-world scenarios involves a much richer and more diverse range of language. This includes descriptions of world state ("we're out of milk"), general knowledge ("this button turns on the TV"), corrective feedback ("no, turn around"), and dynamic rules ("wrenches can be used to tighten nuts").

Existing approaches to language-conditioned policies often rely on:
- **Direct Policy Conditioning:** Model-free RL methods directly map language inputs to actions. However, these methods often struggle as the complexity and diversity of language increase, especially when the correlation between language and optimal actions is indirect or complex. They also typically require explicit reward signals for learning language meaning, or expensive human-annotated datasets for supervised pre-training.
- **Supervised Learning with Expert Data:** Many methods use large datasets of human demonstrations paired with language annotations. While effective for specific tasks, this approach is data-intensive, costly to scale, and limits the agent's ability to learn and adapt online.
- **Large Language Models (LLMs) as Planners/Priors:** Recent work has explored using pre-trained LLMs to generate high-level plans or provide symbolic reasoning. While powerful, LLMs often lack grounding in real-world visual observations and cannot directly take low-level actions. Bridging the gap between abstract linguistic knowledge and grounded physical interaction remains a challenge.

This paper addresses these limitations by proposing a novel perspective: instead of directly mapping diverse language to actions, agents should interpret language as a **signal that helps predict the future**. This core idea unifies language understanding with future prediction within a self-supervised learning objective. By building on "world models" – a concept popularized in model-based RL where an agent learns an internal simulation of its environment – Dynalang extends this to a *multimodal world model* that predicts future observations in both visual and linguistic modalities. This contrasts with previous work that primarily focused on language-conditioned *policies* or *video generation* from fixed text, positioning Dynalang as a significant step towards more generalized, self-improving embodied agents that can truly understand and generate diverse language in context.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable embodied agents to leverage the full spectrum of human language, moving beyond simple task instructions to incorporate diverse forms of communication like world descriptions, general knowledge, and interactive feedback. The authors are motivated by the vision of building agents that can interact naturally and collaboratively with humans in complex physical environments, such as a household robot.

The key motivations and objectives can be summarized as follows:

- **Overcoming Limitations of Instruction-Following:** Current agents are primarily limited to following basic, imperative instructions. Real-world human interaction involves richer communication, including declarative statements, questions, and contextual information, which current agents struggle to process effectively.
- **Addressing the Weak Correlation Problem:** Directly mapping diverse language to optimal actions can be an exceedingly difficult learning problem because the relationship might be complex and indirect. For example, "I put the bowls away" implies different optimal actions depending on the broader task context (cleaning vs. setting a table). The authors hypothesize that a more effective learning signal is needed.
- **Leveraging Diverse Language for Future Prediction:** The core conceptual objective is to propose and demonstrate that agents can ground diverse kinds of language by using it to predict the future. This means predicting what they will observe, how the world will behave, and which situations will be rewarded. This transforms language understanding into a powerful, self-supervised prediction problem.
- **Developing a Multimodal World Model (Dynalang):** To instantiate this idea, the paper aims to develop Dynalang, an agent that learns a joint generative model of language and vision. This model predicts future latent representations of both text and image observations.
- **Decoupling World Modeling from Policy Learning:** A specific objective is to demonstrate that by decoupling the learning of the world model (which uses self-supervised prediction objectives) from the learning of the policy (which uses reinforcement learning with task rewards), the agent can acquire a richer understanding of language and the world, leading to better task performance.
- **Enabling New Capabilities through Generative Modeling:** The authors also aim to show that learning a generative model of language and vision enables additional capabilities, such as pretraining on single-modality text-only data and generating grounded language, allowing the agent to "speak" in context.

In essence, the research seeks to develop a more robust and scalable approach to language grounding in embodied AI by reframing language understanding as a predictive task within a learned world model, thereby enhancing both perception and action capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Dynalang’s methodology centers on building a **multimodal world model** that leverages diverse language for future prediction within a model-based reinforcement learning framework. It extends the successful DreamerV3 algorithm (Hafner et al., 2023).

#### 3.1. Problem Setting and Multimodal Alignment

- **Continuous Input Stream:** Unlike prior work that often treats language as a static instruction at the beginning of an episode, Dynalang processes a continuous stream of visual (pixels, `x_t`) and textual (language tokens, `l_t`) observations at each timestep.
- **No Explicit Alignment Assumption:** Critically, Dynalang does not assume strict semantic alignment between the visual frame `x_t` and the language token `l_t` at any given timestep. It treats both as inputs to a joint prediction problem, allowing the model to learn the temporal and semantic relationships implicitly.
- **Actions:** The agent outputs motor actions and, in some settings, language tokens.

#### 3.2. World Model Learning

The core of Dynalang is its world model, illustrated in Figure 3a. It learns to compress multimodal inputs into latent representations and predict future representations given actions.
- **Components:**
 - **Multimodal Encoder (`enc`):** Maps current image (`x_t`), language token (`l_t`), and recurrent state (`h_t`) into a stochastic latent representation (`z_t`).
 - **Sequence Model (`seq`):** A recurrent neural network (GRU in this case) that predicts the next latent representation (`hat_z_{t+1}`) and recurrent state (`h_{t+1}`) from the previous latent state (`z_t`), recurrent state (`h_t`), and action (`a_t`). This is where the future prediction happens.
 - **Multimodal Decoder (`dec`):** Reconstructs the observed image (`hat_x_t`), language token (`hat_l_t`), reward (`hat_r_t`), and episode continuation flag (`hat_c_t`) from the latent representation (`z_t`) and recurrent state (`h_t`).
- **Learning Objectives:** The world model is trained to minimize a combination of losses:
 - **Representation Learning Loss (`L_repr`):** Encourages the latent `z_t` to capture information from all modalities. This includes:
 - Image reconstruction (`L_x`): L2 norm for pixels.
 - Language reconstruction (`L_l`): Categorical cross-entropy for one-hot tokens or squared error for pretrained embeddings.
 - Reward reconstruction (`L_r`): Categorical cross-entropy for a two-hot encoding of rewards.
 - Continue flag reconstruction (`L_c`): Binary cross-entropy.
 - Regularizer (`L_reg`): KL divergence between the encoded latent `z_t` and the predicted latent `hat_z_t`, regularizing representations to be predictable.
 - **Future Prediction Loss (`L_pred`):** Encourages the sequence model to accurately predict the next latent representation (`hat_z_t`) given the current state and action. This is a KL divergence between the true next latent and the predicted one. This objective is crucial for grounding language, as understanding what language means helps predict future observations and rewards.

#### 3.3. Single-Modality Pretraining

A key methodological innovation is the ability to pretrain the world model on single-modality data (e.g., text-only). By zeroing out image/action inputs and relevant decoder losses, the model can learn to represent and predict text dynamics, analogous to language modeling. This allows leveraging large offline datasets before finetuning with full multimodal experience.

#### 3.4. Policy Learning

Once the world model is learned, Dynalang trains an actor-critic algorithm to select actions.
- **Latent Imagination:** The policy (actor) and value function (critic) are trained entirely from *imagined* sequences of multimodal representations sampled from the learned world model. This means the agent "dreams" of future scenarios and learns to act optimally within these imagined futures to maximize predicted rewards.
- **Actor-Critic Loss:** The critic estimates future returns, and the actor is trained to maximize these imagined returns while also incorporating an entropy regularizer for exploration.

This integrated approach allows Dynalang to learn rich, grounded representations of the world and language, and then use these representations for efficient planning and action selection, without requiring direct supervision on language meaning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted experiments across four diverse simulated environments to validate their hypotheses.

#### 4.1. H1: Multimodal Architecture Alignment (Messenger S1)

- **Finding:** Dynalang's simple and effective architecture, which treats image frames and language tokens as single (image, token) pairs per timestep and fuses them in the world model, significantly outperforms alternative methods for incorporating language into DreamerV3.
- **Details:** Compared against baselines like language-conditioned policies (where language only affects policy, not world model), sentence embeddings, and various T5-based fusion methods (Image Adapter, Cross-Attention, Finetuned T5). Dynalang achieved substantially higher scores, indicating the effectiveness of its approach for joint multimodal representation learning. This was achieved even when language embeddings were learned from scratch, rather than relying on large pretrained models.

#### 4.2. H2: Diverse Language Benefits Task Performance (HomeGrid & Messenger)

- **Finding:** Dynalang effectively utilizes diverse language hints and game manuals to improve task performance, whereas traditional language-conditioned policies (IMPALA, R2D2) often degrade with increased language diversity.
- **HomeGrid:** Dynalang showed consistent performance improvements when provided with "future observations" (object locations), "dynamics" (how to open bins), and "corrections" (navigation feedback), compared to just "task-only" instructions. IMPALA struggled across all settings, and R2D2's performance *decreased* with more diverse language, suggesting its difficulty in correlating complex language with actions.
- **Messenger (Game Manuals):** Dynalang outperformed both model-free baselines (IMPALA, R2D2) and the task-specific EMMA architecture across all stages. Crucially, on the most complex Stage 3, where other methods failed to achieve non-trivial performance, Dynalang learned to interpret the game manuals to score, demonstrating its ability to leverage descriptive language for complex reasoning.

#### 4.3. H3: Instruction Following Performance (HomeGrid & VLN-CE)

- **Finding:** Dynalang's instruction-following capabilities are competitive with or superior to direct language-conditioned policies, despite the additional complexity of a world model.
- **HomeGrid (Task Only):** Even with only task instructions, Dynalang outperformed IMPALA and R2D2, indicating that its world modeling approach provides a stronger foundation for general task performance.
- **VLN-CE (Vision-Language Navigation):** In a visually and linguistically complex photorealistic environment, Dynalang achieved significantly higher success rates in following navigation instructions compared to R2D2. This validates its ability to ground instructions and plan in challenging, continuous environments.

#### 4.4. H4: Generative Model Capabilities (LangRoom & Text-only Pretraining)

- **Finding:** The generative nature of Dynalang's world model enables unique capabilities, including grounded language generation and effective pretraining on text-only data.
- **LangRoom (Embodied Question Answering):** By augmenting the action space with language tokens, Dynalang learned to take information-gathering actions (e.g., moving to an object) and then generate correct answers to questions about the environment (e.g., "what color is the ball?"). This demonstrates a grounded language generation capability. Furthermore, for large vocabularies (10,000 tokens), simple RL failed, but regularization towards the world model's next-token prediction successfully enabled learning, showcasing the power of the generative model for action space regularization.
- **Text-only Pretraining:** Pretraining Dynalang on in-domain (Messenger S2 manuals) and general-domain (TinyStories, 500M tokens) text-only datasets significantly improved downstream RL performance on Messenger S2. This highlights the ability to leverage vast offline text data to improve an embodied agent's understanding, bridging the gap between large-scale language models and embodied agents.

Overall, the results consistently support the authors' central hypothesis that predicting the future in a multimodal world model provides a robust self-supervised signal for grounding diverse language, leading to superior performance and novel capabilities compared to previous approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Learning to Model the World With Language" marks a significant step forward in the development of intelligent, embodied agents capable of more natural and effective interaction with humans and complex environments. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

- **Unified Language Understanding and Future Prediction:** The core conceptual contribution is reframing language understanding not as a direct command execution problem, but as a signal for future prediction within a multimodal world model. This provides a rich, self-supervised learning objective that can ground diverse types of language (instructions, descriptions, rules, feedback) to visual experience. This paradigm could lead to agents that inherently understand the *meaning* of language in terms of its implications for the world's state and dynamics, rather than just mapping commands to actions.
- **Robustness to Diverse Language:** The paper demonstrates that Dynalang's performance *improves* with more diverse language inputs, in stark contrast to traditional language-conditioned policies whose performance degrades. This is crucial for real-world deployment, where human language is rarely limited to precise, imperative commands. Agents can now benefit from "ambient" language that describes the environment or general knowledge, fostering more intelligent behavior.
- **Bridging Online and Offline Learning:** The ability to pretrain the multimodal world model on text-only data and then finetune it with interactive experience is highly impactful. This offers a scalable pathway to leverage the vast amount of existing linguistic data (like web text or books) to imbue agents with a foundational understanding of language, which can then be refined through interaction. This could accelerate learning for new tasks and environments, reducing the need for expensive, task-specific expert demonstrations.
- **Generative Capabilities for Grounded Communication:** The demonstration of embodied language generation (e.g., answering questions about observed objects) opens avenues for truly bidirectional human-agent communication. Agents can not only understand but also *articulate* their understanding of the world, providing explanations, asking clarifying questions, or reporting observations. This moves towards a more collaborative and communicative AI.
- **Advancement of Model-Based RL:** By extending DreamerV3 to a multimodal setting and showcasing its effectiveness for language grounding, the work reinforces the power and versatility of world models in reinforcement learning. It suggests that model-based approaches, with their explicit representation of environment dynamics, are particularly well-suited for integrating complex sensory modalities like language.
- **Towards Generalist Embodied AI:** By showcasing strong performance across diverse environments—from gridworlds requiring rule interpretation to photorealistic navigation and even question answering—Dynalang hints at a more generalizable approach to embodied AI. Its architecture is less task-specific and more adaptable to new linguistic and visual contexts, pushing towards the development of generalist agents that can operate across a wide range of tasks and environments.

In the long term, this research could pave the way for more intuitive and capable robotic assistants, advanced virtual agents, and more deeply integrated human-AI teams, where communication flows naturally and agents possess a richer, grounded understanding of the world and human intent.
