# PHUMA: Physically Reliable Humanoid Locomotion Dataset

> [!summary] Summary
> The PHUMA dataset, developed at KAIST, provides a large-scale collection of physically reliable human motions for training humanoid robot controllers by integrating physics-aware curation and a physics-constrained retargeting method called PhySINK. Policies trained on PHUMA achieved a 92.7% motion tracking success rate for Unitree G1 and demonstrated superior zero-shot sim-to-real transfer with reduced tracking errors on physical hardware.

## Problem

- Existing high-quality human motion datasets (mocap) for humanoid robots lack sufficient scale and diversity for comprehensive training.
- Large-scale human motion data derived from internet videos often contains severe physical artifacts such as floating, ground penetration, joint violations, and foot skating, which hinder robot stability and realism.
- These data limitations impede the development of robust, natural, and generalizable locomotion policies for humanoid robots and effective sim-to-real transfer.

## Method

- A two-stage pipeline consisting of ==Physics-Aware Motion Curation== and Physics-Constrained Motion Retargeting (PhySINK) was developed to aggregate and refine motion data.
- The curation process filters raw motion sequences based on physical criteria like root jerk, foot contact score, pelvis height, and center-of-mass stability to remove implausible segments.
- PhySINK, a novel retargeting method, adapts human motions to humanoid morphologies by optimizing a composite objective function that explicitly enforces joint feasibility, ground contact, and anti-skating constraints.

## Results

- Physics-aware curation improved the total motion tracking success rate on the PHUMA Test set from **50.6%** to **87.0%** for the Unitree G1 humanoid, despite reducing the total data volume.
- PhySINK outperformed other retargeting methods, achieving a **79.5%** total success rate on the PHUMA Test set compared to **76.2%** for SINK, **61.7%** for GMR, and **52.8%** for IK.
- Policies trained on PHUMA achieved the highest success rates (**92.7%** total for Unitree G1) across all motion categories, significantly surpassing those trained on AMASS (**76.2%**) and Humanoid-X (**50.6%**).
- Zero-shot deployment on a real Unitree G1 showed PHUMA-trained policies had lower tracking errors (**34.3** mm mean per-joint position error) compared to AMASS-trained policies (**41.0** mm).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Data quality, particularly physical reliability, is more critical than raw data quantity for achieving high-performance humanoid motion imitation.
> - Physics-constrained retargeting significantly improves motion tracking performance, especially for dynamic and balance-sensitive movements, by ensuring the generated robot trajectories are physically plausible.
> - A combination of large-scale diversity and rigorous physical quality in motion datasets is essential for training robust humanoid locomotion policies and enabling effective zero-shot transfer to real-world robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of humanoid robots in real-world environments necessitates reliable and natural locomotion capabilities, which represents a critical step towards general-purpose embodied AI. While reinforcement learning (RL) with task-oriented rewards has achieved notable advancements in quadrupedal locomotion, its application to humanoids often results in gaits that are effective but may lack human-like naturalness. Motion imitation has emerged as a promising alternative paradigm, where robot policies are trained to replicate observed human movements. This typically involves three stages: collecting human motion data, retargeting it to the robot's specific morphology, and subsequently tracking these retargeted trajectories using RL.

However, progress in motion imitation is constrained by the inherent limitations of available human motion data. High-quality motion capture (mocap) datasets, such as LaFAN1 and AMASS, offer a high degree of physical feasibility but are limited in their scale and diversity, often concentrating on simple motions like walking or reaching. To overcome this data scarcity, recent research endeavors, exemplified by Humanoid-X, have sought to leverage large volumes of internet videos. These approaches convert video content into human pose and shape representations (e.g., SMPL) using video-to-motion models, then retarget them to humanoid embodiments.

A significant challenge with video-derived motions is the prevalence of physical artifacts. Video-to-motion models frequently misestimate global translation, leading to issues such as floating above the ground or ground penetration. Furthermore, the subsequent retargeting stage often prioritizes kinematic joint alignment over physical plausibility, resulting in joint limit violations and foot skating. These artifacts hinder the stability and realism of robot motion imitation, thereby limiting the effectiveness of policies trained on such data and impeding successful sim-to-real transfer. PHUMA addresses these specific issues by focusing on developing a large-scale, diverse, and physically reliable motion dataset to advance humanoid locomotion.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind PHUMA is to overcome the limitations of existing human motion datasets used for humanoid locomotion. Current datasets either lack sufficient scale and diversity (motion capture data) or suffer from significant physical inconsistencies and artifacts (video-based data). These issues directly impede the development of robust, natural, and generalizable locomotion policies for humanoid robots.

The specific problems PHUMA aims to address are:
- **Limited Scale and Diversity of High-Quality Data:** Motion capture datasets, while providing high-fidelity, physically plausible motions, are expensive and laborious to produce, resulting in restricted content variety and duration. This limits the range of behaviors humanoid robots can learn.
- **Physical Artifacts in Scaled Data:** Efforts to scale data collection using internet videos often introduce severe physical violations. These include:
 - **Floating and Penetration:** Incorrect global translation estimates from video-to-motion models lead to the robot appearing to float above or penetrate the ground.
 - **Joint Violations:** Retargeting methods that prioritize kinematic alignment over robot mechanical limits can cause joint over-extension or unnatural poses.
 - **Foot Skating:** Inaccurate ground contact estimation or insufficient physical constraints during retargeting result in feet sliding across the ground during supposed contact phases.

The key objectives of this research are:
1. **To aggregate and scale human motion data:** Combine both high-quality motion capture data and large-scale internet video sources to create a diverse and extensive motion corpus.
2. **To enhance physical reliability through curation:** Develop a robust physics-aware curation pipeline capable of identifying and filtering out severely corrupted or physically implausible motion segments from raw data, particularly from noisy video sources.
3. **To develop a physics-constrained retargeting method:** Create a novel retargeting technique, termed PhySINK, that adapts human motions to humanoid robot morphologies while explicitly enforcing physical constraints, such as joint limits, proper ground contact, and anti-skating measures.
4. **To construct a physically reliable locomotion dataset:** Produce PHUMA, a comprehensive dataset comprising a significant duration of physically sound human-like motions suitable for training humanoid robot controllers.
5. **To validate the effectiveness of the dataset:** Empirically demonstrate that policies trained on PHUMA achieve superior motion tracking performance in simulation and exhibit effective zero-shot transfer to real-world humanoid robots, compared to policies trained on existing datasets.

By addressing these objectives, PHUMA aims to provide a foundational resource that enables humanoid robots to learn a broader spectrum of natural, stable, and human-like locomotion behaviors, thereby advancing the field of embodied AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PHUMA dataset is constructed through a two-stage pipeline: Physics-Aware Motion Curation and Physics-Constrained Motion Retargeting (PhySINK). This pipeline is designed to aggregate diverse human motion data, filter out problematic sequences, and adapt them to humanoid robots while ensuring physical reliability.

#### 3.1. Physics-Aware Motion Curation

The curation pipeline processes raw motion data, particularly from internet videos like Humanoid-X, to mitigate artifacts such as jitter, physical instability, and incorrect foot-ground contact.

- **Low-Pass Noise Filtering:** All motion sequences are resampled to 30 Hz. A zero-phase, 4th-order Butterworth low-pass filter is applied to smooth motion channels, with a 3 Hz cutoff for root translation and 6 Hz for global orientation and body pose, thereby reducing high-frequency jitter.
- **Ground Contact Information Extraction:** To establish a consistent ground plane, a subset of 88 SMPL-X foot vertices (22 from each of the left heel, left toe, right heel, and right toe regions) are identified as key indicators for ground interaction. A global ground plane is estimated using a majority-vote approach. This method collects the heights of these foot vertices across all frames and selects the optimal ground height (g*) that maximizes the duration for which foot vertices fall within a narrow band (g ± 2.5 cm). The entire motion trajectory is then shifted so that this estimated ground plane aligns with z=0.
- **Filtering Motion Data by Physical Information:** Motion sequences are segmented into 4-second clips and individually evaluated against several physical criteria. Clips are discarded if they exhibit:
 - **Excessive Root Jerk:** Indicating abrupt or unnatural motions (threshold: > 50 m/s³).
 - **Insufficient Foot Contact Score:** Measuring the consistency and sufficiency of foot-ground interactions based on a graded ground-contact ratio (threshold: < 0.6).
 - **Unnatural Pelvis Height:** Filtering out motions that are excessively crouched, lying, or floating (thresholds: min < 0.6 m, max > 1.5 m).
 - **Instability based on Center-of-Mass (CoM) Distance to Base of Support (BoS):** Evaluating the horizontal-plane projections of the pelvis and spine1 joints relative to the BoS (defined by foot and ankle projections) to detect imbalance (thresholds: pelvis distance > 6 cm, spine1 distance > 11 cm).
- **Data Augmentation:** The curated motions are augmented with data from existing sources such as LaFAN1, LocoMuJoCo, and additional self-captured videos. The final PHUMA dataset comprises 73.0 hours of physically reliable motion across 76.0K clips, with a balanced distribution across various motion types.

#### 3.2. Physics-Constrained Motion Retargeting (PhySINK)

After curation, the refined human motions are retargeted to the humanoid robot's morphology using PhySINK, a method designed to enforce physical reliability.

- **Foundation:** PhySINK builds upon Shape-adaptive Inverse Kinematics (SINK), which adapts the human body shape to the robot's kinematic structure to preserve motion style. PhySINK augments SINK by incorporating explicit physical constraints during optimization.
- **Composite Objective Function:** The PhySINK optimization minimizes a composite objective function:
 $L_{PhySINK} = L_{Fidelity} + w_{Feasibility} L_{Feasibility} + w_{Ground} L_{Ground} + w_{Skate} L_{Skate}$
 where $w$ represents weighting coefficients.
- **Loss Terms:**
 - **Motion Fidelity Loss ($L_{Fidelity}$):** Ensures the retargeted motion kinematically aligns with the source. It consists of:
 - *Global Position Matching:* Minimizes the L1 distance between global 3D joint positions of the SMPL-X body and the humanoid.
 - *Local Joint Matching:* Enforces consistency of relative joint positions and orientations between adjacent joints.
 - *Smoothness Loss:* Penalizes the second-order finite difference of joint velocities and root translations to ensure temporal smoothness.
 - **Joint Feasibility Loss ($L_{Feasibility}$):** Prevents joint angles and velocities from exceeding the robot's mechanical limits by penalizing configurations approaching or surpassing 98% of the allowable range.
 - **Grounding Loss ($L_{Ground}$):** Corrects floating and penetration by enforcing that foot regions (left heel, left toe, right heel, right toe) align with the ground plane during frames where contact is detected, using the ground-contact ratio derived from curation.
 - **Skating Loss ($L_{Skate}$):** Mitigates foot sliding by penalizing the horizontal velocity of any foot region during detected ground contact.
- **Evaluation:** PhySINK's performance is quantitatively evaluated using five metrics: Motion Fidelity, Joint Feasibility, Non-Floating, Non-Penetration, and Non-Skating, measured as the percentage of frames satisfying each physical criterion. Comparisons against standard IK, GMR, and SINK demonstrate PhySINK's superior performance across these metrics, particularly in balancing motion fidelity with physical realism.

The integration of physics-aware curation and physics-constrained retargeting allows PHUMA to provide a large-scale, diverse, and inherently more physically reliable dataset for training robust humanoid locomotion policies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents comprehensive evaluations across four main axes, demonstrating the effectiveness of PHUMA's curation, retargeting, and overall dataset quality for humanoid motion imitation.

#### 4.1. Effect of Physics-Aware Curation

An ablation study was conducted to isolate the contribution of physics-aware curation. Using Humanoid-X as the baseline and SINK for retargeting, the study showed that applying any single curation filter (jerk, foot contact, height, or base of support) improved motion tracking performance over an unfiltered baseline. Applying all curation components combined yielded a significantly larger gain in success rate (e.g., from 50.6% to 87.0% total success rate on the PHUMA Test for Unitree G1). This improvement occurred despite a substantial reduction in the total dataset volume (from 231 hours to 62 hours), indicating that data quality, as ensured by curation, is a more critical factor than raw data quantity for effective motion tracking.

#### 4.2. PhySINK Retargeting Method Effectiveness

PhySINK was compared against established retargeting baselines (IK, GMR, SINK) using the AMASS dataset to isolate retargeting performance from data curation effects. PhySINK consistently outperformed these baselines on both PHUMA Test and Unseen Video evaluation sets for the Unitree G1 humanoid. For example, on the PHUMA Test set, PhySINK achieved a 79.5% total success rate, compared to 76.2% for SINK, 61.7% for GMR, and 52.8% for IK. The most significant performance improvements were observed in angular and vertical motion categories, which require precise balance and physical stability. This suggests that physically reliable retargeting directly leads to improved imitation performance, especially for dynamic and balance-sensitive movements.

#### 4.3. PHUMA Dataset Effectiveness

Policies trained on PHUMA were compared against those trained on other prominent humanoid datasets: LaFAN1 (small-scale, high-quality), AMASS (medium-scale, moderate-quality), and Humanoid-X (large-scale, lower-quality). Policies trained on PHUMA achieved the highest success rates across all motion categories and for both Unitree G1 and H1-2 humanoids. For instance, on the PHUMA Test set for G1, PHUMA-trained policies achieved a 92.7% total success rate, significantly higher than AMASS (76.2%), Humanoid-X (50.6%), and LaFAN1 (46.1%). These results indicate that PHUMA's combination of large scale and high physical quality is crucial for superior performance, demonstrating that neither scale nor quality alone is sufficient.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiment

To validate the sim-to-real transfer capabilities, policies trained on AMASS and PHUMA were deployed zero-shot on a real Unitree G1 using the BeyondMimic framework. The PHUMA-trained policy exhibited lower tracking errors across all evaluated metrics (mean per-joint position error, DoF velocity error, DoF acceleration error) and qualitatively demonstrated more faithful motion tracking compared to the AMASS-trained policy. For example, the total mean per-joint position error for PHUMA was 34.3 mm, compared to 41.0 mm for AMASS. This demonstrates that PHUMA's advantages in dataset scale and physical reliability translate effectively from simulation to physical hardware.

#### 4.5. Additional Ablation Studies

- **Mocap vs. Video-only Data Performance:** A comparison between policies trained on MoCap-sourced PHUMA and Video-sourced PHUMA showed that the video-sourced data, due to its larger scale and broader diversity (with comparable retargeting quality), led to superior imitation performance.
- **PhySINK Robustness to Noisy Sources:** PhySINK demonstrated robust retargeting performance even when fed raw, noisy Humanoid-X motion sources, consistently producing physically reliable outputs.
- **Impact of Retargeting Quality on Policy Performance:** An analysis of various retargeting methods revealed that motion fidelity is the most critical factor influencing motion tracking performance. While addressing other physical artifacts improved physical reliability metrics, beyond a certain threshold of motion fidelity, further reductions in issues like penetration and skating yielded diminishing returns for policy learning.
- **Pelvis-Only Path Following Control:** Policies trained on PHUMA exhibited better pelvis path-following control, particularly for dynamic motions like running, compared to those trained on AMASS.

Overall, the findings consistently support that the physics-aware curation and physics-constrained retargeting pipeline of PHUMA leads to a dataset that significantly enhances the training and real-world deployment of humanoid locomotion policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PHUMA dataset and its accompanying pipeline represent a notable advancement in the field of humanoid locomotion and embodied AI. Its significance stems from addressing a core challenge: the scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and, crucially, *physically reliable* human motion data for training robot controllers.

### 6. Significance

- **Bridging the Data Quality-Quantity Gap:** PHUMA demonstrates that merely increasing the quantity of motion data from sources like internet videos is insufficient if the data quality, particularly its physical plausibility, is compromised. The research highlights the critical importance of careful curation and physics-constrained retargeting to transform noisy, real-world human observations into robust training signals for robots.
- **Enhanced Robot Learning:** By providing a dataset with both broad coverage of human behaviors and rigorous physical reliability, PHUMA enables the training of more stable, natural, and versatile humanoid locomotion policies. This is evidenced by the consistently superior performance of PHUMA-trained policies across various metrics and evaluation scenarios compared to those trained on existing datasets.
- **Improved Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful zero-shot deployment on a real Unitree G1 robot is a key indicator of PHUMA's impact. Physically reliable training data reduces the reality gap, allowing policies learned in simulation to transfer more effectively to physical hardware without extensive fine-tuning.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Humanoid Robotics Research:** PHUMA can serve as a foundational resource for researchers developing advanced control algorithms, motion generation techniques, and imitation learning frameworks for humanoid robots. Its open availability further facilitates broader research and benchmarking.
- **More Capable Humanoid Robots:** By enabling robots to learn more human-like and physically robust gaits, PHUMA contributes to the development of humanoids that can operate more effectively and safely in human-centric environments. This could include applications in logistics, assistance, and exploration.
- **Advancements in Embodied AI:** The methodology presented in PHUMA provides a blueprint for generating high-quality datasets for other complex robot skills, extending beyond locomotion to areas involving interaction with environments and objects. This could lead to more generalizable and intelligent embodied AI systems.
- **Impact on Video-to-Motion Research:** The limitations identified in current video-to-motion recovery methods (e.g., substantial filtered data) may spur further research into developing more physically accurate and robust human motion estimation techniques from unconstrained video.

**Limitations Acknowledged by the Authors:**
- The current pipeline primarily focuses on flat-terrain locomotion and does not yet encompass motions involving complex scene interaction (e.g., object manipulation, sitting, climbing) or uneven terrain. Extending to such scenarios would require integrated modeling of robot dynamics and environmental geometry.
- A significant portion of video-estimated motions was discarded during curation, reflecting the current limitations of video-to-motion recovery technology. As this technology matures, future iterations of PHUMA could potentially incorporate a wider array of behaviors.
- While PhySINK substantially reduces physical artifacts, it does not entirely eliminate them. Further refinement of the retargeting process remains an area for future work.

Despite these limitations, PHUMA represents a significant step towards creating robot learning environments grounded in physically plausible human behaviors, with demonstrable benefits for the control and deployment of humanoid robots.
