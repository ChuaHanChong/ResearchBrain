# Enhancing Reasoning through Process Supervision with Monte Carlo Tree Search

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at AI Lab, Giant Network developed an iterative self-training approach that uses Monte Carlo Tree Search to automatically generate fine-grained process supervision for large language models. This method assigns nuanced 'relative correctness' scores to intermediate reasoning steps, leading to improved mathematical reasoning performance, demonstrating gains of up to 4.85% on the MATH dataset and 5.03% on the GSM8K dataset for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) often exhibit limitations in robust, multi-step logical reasoning, particularly in complex domains such as mathematics.
- Traditional outcome-based supervision for LLMs is insufficient as models can arrive at correct final answers via flawed intermediate steps, hindering the development of genuine reasoning skills.
- While process supervision (feedback on intermediate steps) is effective, human annotation for such feedback is prohibitively expensive and prior automated methods, like MCTS-DPO, use less informative binary preference signals and discard valuable data.

## Method

- An iterative self-training framework is proposed where the LLM generates its own process supervision data using ==Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)==.
- MCTS is applied at each partial solution step to explore candidate 'next steps' and assign a nuanced =='relative correctness' score== to each step based on its average reward (success rate) compared to sibling steps.
- The LLM is fine-tuned using a ==weighted negative log-likelihood loss== that incorporates these relative correctness scores, alongside a KL divergence penalty to prevent excessive drift from the previous iteration.

## Results

- The proposed method consistently outperformed baselines (Zero-shot-CoT, RFT, Step-level DPO) on both MATH and GSM8K datasets, for instance, improving Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct's accuracy on MATH from **47.07%** to **51.92%**.
- Performance generally improved with successive iterations of the self-training process, demonstrating the cumulative benefits of the self-supervision loop, although improvements converged within a few iterations.
- The MCTS-based scoring method proved significantly superior to the 'Step-level DPO' baseline, which yielded only marginal improvements, validating the effectiveness of nuanced, relative correctness scores for training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Assigning nuanced, relative correctness scores to intermediate reasoning steps through MCTS provides a more effective supervision signal than binary preference (chosen/rejected) methods, leading to greater performance improvements.
> - An iterative self-training loop, where LLMs generate and learn from their own MCTS-evaluated reasoning steps, effectively enhances multi-step reasoning capabilities without reliance on costly human annotations or complex reinforcement learning setups.
> - The enhanced reasoning ability learned through this process demonstrates positive transferability across different mathematical reasoning datasets, indicating the acquisition of generalized problem-solving skills.
