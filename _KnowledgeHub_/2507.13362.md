# Enhancing Spatial Reasoning in Vision-Language Models via Chain-of-Thought Prompting and Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from NYU's Courant Institute systematically enhances the spatial reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by investigating structured Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting and Reinforcement Learning. The study demonstrates that Scene Graph CoT significantly improves VLM performance on spatial tasks, and fine-tuning with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) leads to superior out-of-distribution generalization compared to supervised fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often struggle with complex spatial reasoning tasks, including interpreting object locations, relative depths, and geometric relationships.
- Current VLM limitations in spatial understanding hinder their deployment in real-world applications like robotics and autonomous navigation.
- Traditional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) frequently leads to overfitting and poor generalization in VLMs, especially under out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions.

## Method

- The research develops and evaluates structured Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting strategies, specifically ==Scene Graph CoT==, to guide VLMs in explicit spatial decomposition.
- It leverages ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==, a reinforcement learning algorithm, to fine-tune VLMs for improved spatial reasoning and enhanced robustness.
- A ==two-step prompting approach== (scene graph generation followed by answer generation) is formalized, along with ==dynamic sampling within GRPO==, to encourage genuine reasoning and mitigate reward hacking.

## Results

- Structured Scene Graph CoT yielded significant gains (**5-15%**) across spatial reasoning tasks, with Llama-4-Scout-17B-16E-Instruct + 4-shot Conversational CoT achieving **77.69%** total accuracy on CVBench.
- GRPO-v2 achieved **72.38%** Pass@1 accuracy on CVBench, outperforming supervised fine-tuning (**66.79%**), and demonstrated superior robustness under out-of-distribution conditions (e.g., **+19.5%** gain on Depth OOD compared to base model, while SFT degraded).
- Fine-tuning with segmentation-aware data improved counting accuracy by **43.16%** on CV-Bench (SpaceQwenVL-2.5-3B improved from **21.05%** to **64.21%**), suggesting encoder segmentation limitations are a key factor in 'off-by-one' counting errors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Naive Chain-of-Thought prompting can be detrimental to VLM spatial reasoning, underscoring the necessity for structured, domain-specific approaches.
> - Structured Scene Graph CoT consistently improves VLM performance on spatial tasks by enabling compositional reasoning cues.
> - Reinforcement learning via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) fosters a deeper, more generalizable understanding of spatial relationships in VLMs, exhibiting superior robustness to out-of-distribution linguistic shifts compared to supervised fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) represent a significant advancement in artificial intelligence, bridging the gap between visual and linguistic understanding. They have demonstrated impressive capabilities in tasks such as image captioning, visual question answering (VQA), and image-text retrieval. However, despite these successes, VLMs often struggle with complex reasoning tasks, particularly those involving spatial understanding—the ability to interpret object locations, relative depths, geometric relationships, and spatial alignment. This limitation is a critical barrier to deploying VLMs in real-world applications like robotics, autonomous navigation, and intelligent human-object interaction, where precise spatial reasoning is paramount.

The broader research landscape for enhancing VLM reasoning capabilities has primarily focused on two main avenues:
1. **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting:** Inspired by its success in large language models (LLMs) for tasks like mathematical or logical reasoning, CoT prompting encourages models to generate intermediate reasoning steps before arriving at a final answer. For VLMs, this often involves explicit spatial decomposition or symbolic representation of visual scenes.
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Fine-tuning:** RL-based methods aim to optimize VLM behavior by providing rewards for correct or desirable outputs, allowing models to learn more robust and generalizable policies compared to traditional supervised fine-tuning (SFT). This is particularly relevant for improving out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, where models need to adapt to unseen data patterns or linguistic variations.

This paper directly addresses the aforementioned challenges by investigating both CoT prompting and RL fine-tuning for spatial reasoning in VLMs. It critically evaluates the efficacy of different prompting strategies, demonstrating that naive CoT can be detrimental, while structured CoT (specifically Scene Graph CoT) offers significant improvements. Furthermore, it champions Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) as a superior fine-tuning method over SFT for achieving better generalization and robustness in spatial tasks, especially under OOD conditions. The work contributes to a growing body of research exploring how to move VLMs beyond superficial pattern matching towards genuine, transferable understanding of complex visual-linguistic information. It builds on recent work that has applied GRPO to enhance VLM generalization and reasoning, extending its application to broader spatial reasoning benchmarks and analyzing its behavior under linguistic shifts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are multi-faceted, aiming to systematically enhance and analyze the spatial reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models:

1. **Evaluate and Refine Chain-of-Thought Prompting:** The study aims to empirically assess the impact of various CoT prompting strategies on VLM spatial reasoning. A key motivation is to understand why simple CoT formats, successful in other domains, might fail or even harm VLM performance in spatial tasks, and conversely, to identify and develop structured CoT approaches that effectively improve spatial understanding. Specifically, the paper investigates Scene Graph CoT as a promising structured alternative.
2. **Enhance Spatial Reasoning through Reinforcement Learning:** The research seeks to leverage Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), an RL algorithm, to fine-tune VLMs for improved spatial reasoning. The motivation here is to overcome limitations of traditional supervised fine-tuning (SFT), which often leads to overfitting and poor generalization, especially under out-of-distribution conditions.
3. **Investigate Generalization and Robustness:** A crucial objective is to rigorously evaluate the generalization behavior of fine-tuned VLMs under various conditions, including cross-dataset performance and linguistic polarity shifts (e.g., "closer to" vs. "farther from"). This aims to determine whether the models acquire a deeper, semantic understanding of spatial relations or merely memorize surface-level linguistic patterns.
4. **Provide Insights for VLM Development:** Ultimately, the study seeks to offer practical insights and guidance for the development of more capable and robust VLMs for real-world applications by illuminating the strengths and weaknesses of different prompting and fine-tuning methodologies for spatial reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology is structured into three primary phases: dataset selection and benchmarking, GRPO implementation and fine-tuning, and the development and evaluation of various Chain-of-Thought prompting strategies.

#### 3.1. Dataset Selection and Benchmarking

The study utilizes a diverse set of benchmarks to comprehensively evaluate VLM performance on spatial reasoning tasks:
- **CLEVR CoGenT ValB and Super-CLEVR:** Diagnostic datasets focusing on compositional language and elementary visual reasoning, and their out-of-distribution extensions.
- **Visual Spatial Reasoning (VSR) dataset:** Features 66 spatial relations from real-world images, offering higher complexity than CLEVR-based datasets.
- **CVBench:** Categorizes tasks into Spatial Relationship, Object Counting, Depth Order, and Relative Distance, providing a focused assessment of visual capabilities.
- **SAT (Spatial Aptitude Training) dataset:** Used for dynamic spatial reasoning tasks involving egocentric and object movement.
- **Pixmo-Count:** A benchmark specifically for object counting.

Baseline evaluations were conducted using CLEVR CoGenT ValB, revealing that simple prompting yielded only marginal improvements, and a "prompt-only" condition sometimes outperformed image-inclusive prompts, suggesting over-reliance on linguistic cues and limited visual reasoning.

#### 3.2. GRPO Implementation and Fine-Tuning

The Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm is applied to fine-tune the PaLI-gemma2-3B-mix-224 model on the SAT dataset. GRPO aims to optimize model behavior by integrating reinforcement learning with reward computation and gradient scaling, focusing on relative comparisons of generated responses.

- **GRPO-v1 (Initial Cross-modal Alignment):** Applies LoRA-based fine-tuning specifically to `q_proj` and `v_proj` layers (cross-modal projection) to address visual-textual misalignment.
- **GRPO-v2 (Enhanced Integration and Dynamic Sampling):** Expands LoRA-adapted modules to include `k_proj`, `o_proj`, `gate_proj`, `up_proj`, and `down_proj`, increases LoRA rank, and decreases the beta value. Crucially, it introduces a dynamic sampling strategy (inspired by DAPO) to address reward collapse, focusing learning on informative examples where responses yield different rewards.
- **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) Baseline:** A standard SFT model is trained on the same dataset for comparison, serving as a direct competitor to GRPO.

The fine-tuned models are evaluated on CVBench and under out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions (linguistic polarity shifts) to assess generalization.

#### 3.3. Scene Graph CoT Prompting Framework

A novel two-step prompting strategy is formalized:
1. **Scene Graph Generation (Step 1):** Given an image `I` and question `Q`, the model is prompted to generate a structured JSON scene graph `SG` containing relevant objects, attributes, and relationships. This step uses specific user and system prompts designed to elicit detailed scene understanding.
2. **Answer Generation Using Scene Graph (Step 2):** The model receives `I`, `Q`, and the generated `SG`, then is prompted to provide the final answer. This separation is critical to prevent "reward hacking," where models shortcut reasoning.

#### 3.4. Optical Flow CoT

For dynamic spatial reasoning tasks (Egocentric Movement, Object Movement) in the SAT benchmark, Optical Flow CoT is employed. This is treated as a special case of Scene Graph CoT where the scene graph encodes positional differences (`∆x(oi)`) across successive images, indicating left, right, or stationary movement.

#### 3.5. Few-Shot and Conversational Prompting

To further enhance in-context learning, k-shot prompting is explored, building on the two-step Scene Graph CoT:
- **Structured Few-Shot Prompting:** Examples are decomposed into two tuples: `(I, Q, R, SG)` for scene graph generation (without answer) and `(I, Q, R, SG, A)` for answer generation. This prevents the model from copying answers or skipping reasoning.
- **Conversational Few-Shot Prompting:** Few-shot examples are presented as alternating user-assistant exchanges, mimicking a chat format. This naturalistic interaction style biases the model toward structured, thoughtful behavior and improves flexibility.

#### 3.6. Other CoT Methods and Ablation Studies

The study also evaluates alternative CoT approaches for comparison and performs ablation studies:
- **Describe CoT:** A simpler two-phase prompt where the model first describes objects (color, shape, size, location, etc.) and then answers the question using this information.
- **Program-of-Thought (PoT), Chain-of-Symbols (CoS), Visualization-of-Thought (VoT):** These symbolic or visual reasoning methods were evaluated for their efficacy.
- **Depth Maps:** Integration of depth map information (generated using DepthAnythingV2) into Scene Graph prompts.
- **Confidence Scores:** Including confidence estimates for scene graph elements.
- **Visual Language Bi-Directional Alignment (VLBA):** An approach inspired by SpatialCoT for mapping coordinates to objects and vice versa.

Models used for evaluation include Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, Llama-4-Scout-17B-16E-Instruct, and PaLI-gemma2-3B-mix-224.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several key findings across counting tasks, GRPO fine-tuning, and Chain-of-Thought prompting strategies.

#### 4.1. Counting Task and Spatial Fine-tuning

- **Attention vs. Segmentation:** VLMs often attend to correct object regions but make "off-by-one" counting errors, suggesting limitations in the encoder's ability to segment tightly clustered or overlapping instances rather than misplacement of attention.
- **Impact of Segmentation-Aware Training:** Fine-tuning with a spatial VLM-generated dataset (SpaceQwenVL-2.5-3B) incorporating relative distances, depth cues, and explicit segmentation annotations significantly improved accuracy. For example, on CV-Bench, SpaceQwenVL-2.5-3B improved accuracy by 43.16% compared to the regular QwenVL2.5-3B (from 21.05% to 64.21%), supporting the hypothesis that enhanced segmentation reduces counting errors (Table I).

#### 4.2. GRPO Fine-tuning Results

- **GRPO vs. SFT:** Both GRPO and SFT improved spatial reasoning compared to the base PaLI-gemma2 model. GRPO-v2 (72.38%) outperformed SFT (66.79%) on Pass@1 accuracy across CVBench tasks (Table III), aligning with prior work suggesting GRPO improves reasoning alignment by pruning suboptimal paths. SFT, however, showed higher Pass@4 accuracy.
- **Robustness to OOD Conditions:** GRPO demonstrates superior robustness under out-of-distribution (OOD) linguistic shifts (e.g., "closer to" vs. "farther from").
 - On the Depth OOD task, SFT *degraded* performance compared to the base model (56.33% vs. 59.00%), indicating overfitting to surface-level patterns.
 - GRPO, in contrast, maintained substantial gains (70.50% from 59.00% base, +19.5%) under OOD conditions.
 - For the Distance task, GRPO experienced only a 3.17% drop from in-distribution to OOD, while SFT saw a 12.03% drop (Table IV). This confirms GRPO fosters stronger generalization.
- **Dynamic Sampling in GRPO:** GRPO-v2, which incorporated dynamic sampling to mitigate reward collapse, achieved superior accuracy compared to GRPO-v1 (72.38% vs. 54.77% Total Pass@4) (Table III).

#### 4.3. Chain-of-Thought Prompting Effectiveness

- **Simple CoT Failure:** Naive CoT prompting (e.g., "think first, then answer") not only failed to improve but sometimes degraded spatial reasoning performance in VLMs, contradicting its success in other domains.
- **Scene Graph CoT Success:** Structured Scene Graph CoT consistently yielded significant gains (5-15%) across spatial reasoning tasks (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct + SceneGraph CoT improved from 60.54% to 68.08% on CVBench; Llama-4-Scout-17B-16E-Instruct + SceneGraph CoT improved from 53.76% to 75.66% on CVBench) (Table III, V). This highlights the importance of compositional reasoning cues.
- **Two-step Prompting:** Separating scene graph generation from answer generation (two-step prompting) was crucial to prevent "reward hacking," where models shortcut reasoning for fast answer generation.
- **Optical Flow CoT for Dynamic Tasks:** Effective for dynamic reasoning in the SAT benchmark, improving accuracy for object and egocentric movements (Table VI).
- **Few-Shot and Conversational CoT:**
 - Initial flat few-shot examples led to reward hacking (model repeating prior answers).
 - Improved strategies involving split answer-free and answer-included prompts, and especially conversational formats, increased flexibility and further enhanced accuracy (e.g., Llama-4-Scout-17B-16E-Instruct + 4-shot Conversational CoT Prompting achieved the highest CVBench total accuracy at 77.69%) (Table III).

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Most alternative CoT methods (PoT, CoS, VoT) and auxiliary inputs (confidence scores, depth maps, VLBA) either underperformed Scene Graph CoT or even degraded performance compared to direct answering baselines (Table III).
- Depth Maps: Did not lead to overall improved accuracy over baseline Scene Graph CoT, though they showed marginal improvement on depth and distance tasks.
- PoT, CoS, VoT: Often introduced unnecessary complexity or misaligned reasoning steps.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This study offers profound insights into enhancing the spatial reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models, with significant implications for both research and practical applications:

1. **Challenging Naive CoT Assumptions:** The finding that simple CoT prompting can be detrimental to VLM spatial reasoning is a crucial contribution. It debunks the universal applicability of simple CoT strategies and underscores the need for domain-specific, structured reasoning approaches in multimodal contexts. This insight guides future research away from unproductive prompting techniques.

2. **Highlighting Structured Prompting's Efficacy:** The demonstrated success of Scene Graph CoT establishes it as a powerful methodology for injecting compositional reasoning into VLMs. By guiding models to explicitly decompose visual scenes into objects, attributes, and relationships, this approach enables a more robust understanding of spatial configurations, pushing VLMs beyond superficial pattern matching. Its effectiveness across various tasks, including counting and complex relations, validates structured prompting as a key strategy.

3. **Advocating for Reinforcement Learning for Generalization:** The superior OOD generalization of GRPO-fine-tuned models compared to SFT is perhaps the most impactful finding. It provides strong evidence that RL can foster a deeper, more semantic understanding in VLMs that is resilient to linguistic variations and distributional shifts. This is critical for real-world deployments where models must perform reliably in diverse and unpredictable environments, such as robotics (navigating dynamic spaces, manipulating objects), autonomous vehicles (understanding complex traffic scenes), and medical imaging (interpreting anatomical relationships). GRPO's ability to prune suboptimal response paths rather than merely memorizing training data patterns represents a significant step towards more robust and trustworthy AI systems.

4. **Informing VLM Training Paradigms:** The insights into "reward hacking" and the necessity of two-step prompting (or conversational formats) offer practical guidance for training VLMs more effectively. This ensures that models truly engage in reasoning rather than simply "cheating" by directly predicting answers from superficial cues.

5. **Benchmarking and Future Directions:** By conducting detailed evaluations across multiple benchmarks and analyzing various CoT methods and their ablations, the study provides a comprehensive landscape of current VLM performance in spatial reasoning. It identifies specific failure modes (e.g., segmentation issues in counting) and points towards promising avenues for future work, such as integrating attention-guided CoT, continuous CoT, and external spatial priors (like 3D maps or depth maps) more effectively. The extension of GRPO to video-based temporal reasoning is also highlighted as a critical next step for embodied AI.

In summary, this research not only advances the state-of-the-art in VLM spatial reasoning but also provides a principled framework for developing more intelligent, reliable, and generalizable multimodal AI systems. By meticulously dissecting the mechanisms of reasoning and generalization, it lays important groundwork for the practical deployment of VLMs in safety-critical and complex applications.
