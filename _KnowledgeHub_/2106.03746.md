# Efficient Training of Visual Transformers with Small Datasets

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Trento and Fondazione Bruno Kessler investigate the performance of advanced Visual Transformers on small datasets and introduce Dense Relative Localization (L_drloc), a self-supervised auxiliary task. L_drloc consistently boosts the accuracy of VTs across various datasets and tasks, particularly in data-limited scenarios, with negligible computational overhead.

## Problem

- Visual Transformers (VTs) often require vast amounts of training data due to their lack of inherent inductive biases compared to Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs).
- The performance and robustness of second-generation VTs (e.g., T2T, Swin, CvT) on medium-small datasets, prevalent in many real-world applications, remained underexplored.
- Existing VTs, despite strong performance on large datasets like ImageNet-1K, exhibit significant performance variability when trained from scratch on smaller datasets.

## Method

- The authors propose **Dense Relative Localization (L_drloc)**, a novel ==self-supervised auxiliary task== designed to extract supplementary spatial information from images.
- L_drloc encourages VTs to learn spatial relationships by predicting ==normalized 2D relative translation offsets== between sampled pairs of token embeddings from the final feature grid.
- This auxiliary `L_drloc` loss is combined with the standard supervised cross-entropy loss, with a small ==Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP)== used to predict relative distances from concatenated token embeddings.

## Results

- `L_drloc` consistently improved the top-1 accuracy of all tested VTs (CvT-13, Swin-T, T2T-ViT-14) across 11 diverse datasets, with gains up to over **45 percentage points** on some benchmarks (e.g., Swin-T on Quickdraw).
- The auxiliary task accelerated training, enabling models to achieve higher accuracy earlier in the schedule, while adding negligible computational overhead (**1.77%** to **2.43%** to the time per training batch).
- Benefits extended to downstream tasks: `L_drloc` pre-trained backbones improved AP on COCO 2017 for object detection/instance segmentation and mIoU/mAcc on ADE20K for semantic segmentation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Second-generation VTs show substantial performance variability when trained from scratch on medium-small datasets, contrasting with their similar performance on ImageNet-1K.
> - The proposed `L_drloc` effectively acts as a regularizer, consistently improving VT accuracy and generalization, especially under data or computational scarcity.
> - Architectures like CvT-13 demonstrate greater inherent robustness on smaller datasets compared to Swin-T and T2T-ViT-14, sometimes matching or exceeding ResNet-50 performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Visual Transformers (VTs) are emerging as an alternative architectural paradigm to Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) in computer vision. Inspired by the success of Transformers in Natural Language Processing (NLP), VTs offer the capability to model global relationships between image elements, potentially leading to a larger representational capacity compared to CNNs, whose receptive fields are inherently local.

However, a notable challenge for VTs is their data-hungry nature. Unlike CNNs, which embed local properties such as locality and translation invariance through their architectural design (inductive biases), VTs generally lack these inherent biases. Consequently, they often require extensive training data to learn these fundamental visual properties from scratch. Early VTs, such as the Vision Transformer (ViT), required colossal datasets like JFT-300M to achieve competitive performance, and they underperformed CNNs like ResNets when trained on smaller datasets like ImageNet-1K.

To address this limitation, a second generation of VTs has been developed, which integrates convolutional layers or operations within their architecture (e.g., T2T, Swin, CvT). These hybrid architectures aim to combine the strengths of both paradigms: leveraging attention for long-range dependencies while incorporating convolutions to impart local inductive biases. Empirical evaluations have shown that these second-generation VTs can be trained on ImageNet-1K and achieve performance comparable to or surpassing similar-sized ResNets.

Despite these advancements, a gap exists in the understanding of how these second-generation VTs perform when trained or fine-tuned on medium-small datasets, which are prevalent in many real-world computer vision applications where ImageNet-scale annotated data is often unavailable. This paper positions itself within this context by investigating the robustness of these advanced VTs in data-constrained environments and proposing a method to enhance their efficiency under such conditions. The work contributes a self-supervised auxiliary task, drawing inspiration from dense supervision techniques like ELECTRA in NLP, to regularize VT training and make them more robust when labeled data is limited. This approach seeks to provide VTs with additional signal to learn spatial relationships, thereby mitigating their data dependency without requiring larger supervised datasets.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The research presented in this paper is driven by several key objectives and motivations:

1. **Empirical Analysis of Second-Generation VTs on Small Datasets:**
 - **Objective:** To systematically compare the performance and robustness of various state-of-the-art second-generation Visual Transformers (T2T, Swin, CvT) when trained from scratch or fine-tuned on medium-small datasets.
 - **Motivation:** While these VTs have demonstrated strong performance on large datasets like ImageNet-1K, their behavior and relative strengths in scenarios with limited training data remain less explored. Most practical computer vision tasks do not possess datasets comparable in size to ImageNet, making it crucial to understand how VTs perform under these more common conditions. The authors hypothesize that VTs might exhibit significant performance variability on smaller datasets despite similar performance on ImageNet-1K.

2. **Development of a Self-Supervised Auxiliary Regularization Task:**
 - **Objective:** To propose a novel self-supervised auxiliary task, termed Dense Relative Localization (L_drloc), designed to extract supplementary spatial information from images. This task is intended to be computationally inexpensive and easily integratable into existing VT architectures.
 - **Motivation:** The primary motivation stems from the inherent data hunger of VTs due to their lack of convolutional inductive biases. By encouraging VTs to learn spatial relations between image patches (represented by token embeddings) through a self-supervised task, the network can acquire local and contextual information that would otherwise require more supervised samples. This regularization is aimed at improving the network's learning efficacy and generalization ability, particularly in data-scarce environments.

3. **Demonstration of Enhanced Training Robustness and Generalization:**
 - **Objective:** To empirically demonstrate that the proposed L_drloc task, when used jointly with standard supervised training, significantly improves the final accuracy and generalization ability of various VTs. The aim is to show this benefit across different datasets, training scenarios (from scratch or fine-tuning), and model architectures.
 - **Motivation:** Validating the effectiveness of L_drloc is crucial for its adoption. The authors are motivated to show that this method can mitigate the data dependency of VTs, accelerate training, and lead to more robust models, thereby making VTs a more viable option for a broader spectrum of real-world applications constrained by data availability or computational resources. The task is motivated by analogous successes in NLP, where dense self-supervised tasks have proven sample-efficient.

In summary, the core motivation is to make Visual Transformers more accessible and effective for real-world computer vision problems that typically operate with datasets smaller than those used for large-scale pre-training.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The research employed a multi-faceted approach to investigate the efficiency of Visual Transformers (VTs) with small datasets and to introduce and evaluate a novel self-supervised auxiliary task.

**A. VT Architectures and Baselines:**
The study focused on three state-of-the-art second-generation VTs, chosen for their public implementations and comparable parameter counts to a ResNet-50 (approximately 20-29 million parameters):
- **Tokens-to-Token ViT (T2T-ViT-14)**
- **Swin Transformer (Swin-T)**
- **Convolutions in Vision Transformers (CvT-13)**
For comparison, a ResNet-50 was also included as a baseline. The ViT-B/16 was additionally used in supplementary experiments for larger model evaluation.

**B. Dense Relative Localization (L_drloc) Task:**
The core of the proposed method is a self-supervised auxiliary task designed to encourage VTs to learn spatial information without requiring additional manual annotations.
- **Input:** Given an input image, the final `k x k` grid of token embeddings, denoted as `G_x = {e_i,j}`, where `e_i,j` is an embedding vector from position `(i,j)` on the grid, is utilized.
- **Pair Sampling:** For each `G_x`, `m` pairs of embeddings `(e_i,j, e_p,h)` are uniformly sampled at random.
- **Target Calculation:** For each sampled pair, the 2D normalized target translation offset `(t_u, t_v)` is computed as:
 `t_u = |i - p| / k`
 `t_v = |j - h| / k`
 These targets are continuous values in `^2`.
- **Prediction Model:** The sampled embedding vectors `e_i,j` and `e_p,h` are concatenated and passed through a small Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), denoted as `f`. This MLP consists of two hidden layers with ReLU activations and two output neurons, predicting the relative distance `(d_u, d_v)`.
- **Loss Function:** The Dense Relative Localization loss, `L_drloc`, is calculated as the L1 loss between the predicted `(d_u, d_v)` and the target `(t_u, t_v)`, averaged over the `m` sampled pairs for each image and then over the mini-batch.
- **Joint Training:** `L_drloc` is combined with the standard supervised cross-entropy loss (`L_ce`) for the main classification task using a weighting hyperparameter `λ`: `L_tot = L_ce + λ * L_drloc`.

**C. Architectural Integration and Hyperparameters:**
- **Grid Resolution Standardization:** To ensure consistency, the final embedding grids for T2T and CvT, originally `14x14`, were downsampled to `7x7` using a `2x2` average pooling operation (without learnable parameters) to match the `7x7` resolution used by Swin. This pooling only affected the input to the localization MLP, not the primary classification head.
- **Native Architectures:** Apart from the introduction of the localization MLP and grid pooling, the native VT architectures, including any existing positional embeddings, were retained without modification.
- **Hyperparameter Tuning:** Ablation studies were conducted on CIFAR-100 with Swin-T to determine optimal hyperparameters:
 - `m` (number of pairs sampled): `m = 64` was selected.
 - `λ` (loss weight): `λ = 0.1` for CvT and T2T, and `λ = 0.5` for Swin were chosen. These values were kept fixed across all subsequent experiments.

**D. Experimental Protocols:**
The evaluation involved two main training scenarios:
1. **Training from Scratch:**
 - Models were trained directly on target datasets for 100 or 300 epochs.
 - Datasets included ImageNet-100 (IN-100), CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Oxford Flowers102, SVHN, and six datasets from DomainNet (ClipArt, Infograph, Painting, Quickdraw, Real, Sketch). These datasets varied in size from small to medium.
 - ResNet-50 was trained using its standard protocol for baseline comparison. L_drloc was also applied to ResNet-50 by using its last convolutional feature map as the embedding grid.
2. **Fine-tuning:**
 - VT models pre-trained on ImageNet-1K (without `L_drloc`) were fine-tuned on the target datasets for 100 epochs. The `L_drloc` was applied only during this fine-tuning stage.
 - CvT was excluded from fine-tuning experiments due to the unavailability of publicly pre-trained ImageNet-1K models.

**E. Additional Experiments (Supplementary Material):**
- **Larger Training Budgets:** The impact of `L_drloc` was assessed with longer training schedules (300 epochs) and on larger datasets (ImageNet-1K).
- **Higher-Capacity Models:** ViT-B/16 was evaluated with `L_drloc` to observe its effect on larger VT models.
- **Transferability to Other Tasks:** The efficacy of `L_drloc` was tested on object detection (COCO 2017 with Mask R-CNN and Cascade Mask R-CNN) and semantic segmentation (ADE20K with UperNet). In these experiments, `L_drloc` was incorporated during the ImageNet-1K or IN-100 pre-training phase, and the resulting backbones were then fine-tuned on the specific tasks.
- **Loss Variants:** Alternative formulations for the localization task (e.g., negative offsets, classification-based loss, very-dense application across all transformer blocks) were explored.

All experiments were conducted using official or publicly available codebases, maintaining consistent data augmentation protocols and VT-specific hyperparameters to ensure fair comparisons.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the behavior of Visual Transformers (VTs) on smaller datasets and the effectiveness of the proposed Dense Relative Localization (L_drloc) auxiliary task.

**A. VT Performance Variability on Small Datasets:**
- A significant finding was the substantial variation in performance among different second-generation VTs when trained from scratch on medium-small datasets. For instance, the accuracy difference between CvT-13 and Swin-T reached approximately 45-46 percentage points on Quickdraw and Sketch datasets, and around 30 points on CIFAR-10. This contrasts sharply with their almost identical top-1 accuracy when trained on ImageNet-1K.
- CvT-13 generally exhibited greater robustness and higher accuracy on smaller datasets compared to Swin-T and T2T-ViT-14.
- In many cases, the best-performing VT (CvT-13) achieved classification accuracy comparable to or even better than a similarly sized ResNet-50 when trained from scratch, demonstrating that VTs can be competitive on datasets smaller than ImageNet-1K.

**B. Impact of L_drloc on Training from Scratch:**
- **Consistent Improvement:** The `L_drloc` auxiliary task consistently improved the top-1 accuracy of all tested VTs (CvT-13, Swin-T, T2T-ViT-14) across all 11 evaluated datasets when trained from scratch.
- **Significant Gains:** The performance boosts were often substantial. For example, Swin-T saw an increase of over 45 percentage points on Quickdraw and over 26 points on Sketch. T2T-ViT-14 improved by over 20 points on Sketch, and CvT-13 gained almost 4 points on SVHN.
- **Regularization Effect:** The improvements were more pronounced in scenarios with fewer training epochs (e.g., 100 epochs vs. 300 epochs on IN-100), indicating that `L_drloc` effectively acts as a regularizer that benefits learning under data or compute scarcity.
- **Effect on ResNets:** When `L_drloc` was applied to ResNet-50, it also yielded marginal but consistent accuracy improvements in 9 out of 10 datasets. The smaller magnitude of improvement compared to VTs suggests that `L_drloc` is more beneficial for architectures that inherently lack local inductive biases.

**C. Impact of L_drloc on Fine-tuning:**
- When VTs were pre-trained on ImageNet-1K (without `L_drloc`) and then fine-tuned on target datasets (with `L_drloc` applied during fine-tuning), consistent accuracy improvements were observed for both Swin-T and T2T-ViT-14 across all datasets.
- Notable gains included over 5 percentage points for Swin-T on Infograph and over 7 points for T2T-ViT-14 on Infograph.
- In the fine-tuning scenario, VTs generally outperformed ResNet-50 on most datasets, further supporting their viability in practical applications.
- `L_drloc` also provided marginal benefits to ResNet-50 during fine-tuning.

**D. Training Efficiency and Overhead:**
- Training curves showed that `L_drloc` accelerated the overall training process, enabling models to achieve higher accuracy earlier in the training schedule.
- The computational overhead introduced by `L_drloc` was found to be negligible, adding only about 1.77% to 2.43% to the time per training batch.

**E. Ablation Studies and Loss Variants:**
- Ablation studies confirmed the chosen hyperparameters (`m=64` pairs, `λ` values) as effective.
- Among various `L_drloc` variants tested (including using negative offsets, classification-based losses, and a "very-dense" loss across all transformer blocks), the original `L_drloc` formulation using L1 loss on absolute differences of normalized offsets proved to be the most effective. Notably, the "very-dense" variant performed worse than the baseline, suggesting that densely supervising intermediate, finer-resolution feature maps can be counterproductive.

**F. Transferability to Other Tasks (Supplementary Results):**
- **Object Detection and Instance Segmentation:** Swin-T models pre-trained with `L_drloc` on ImageNet-1K or IN-100 demonstrated improved AP (Average Precision) metrics on the COCO 2017 dataset for both Mask R-CNN and Cascade Mask R-CNN architectures. The improvements were more significant when `L_drloc` was used with IN-100 pre-training.
- **Semantic Segmentation:** For the ADE20K dataset, Swin-T backbones pre-trained with `L_drloc` similarly showed higher mIoU (mean Intersection over Union) and mAcc (mean class Accuracy) with UperNet, again with larger gains when pre-training on IN-100.

**G. Larger Models and Datasets (Supplementary Results):**
- Even for higher-capacity models like ViT-B/16 and when training on ImageNet-1K for extended periods, `L_drloc` provided slight but consistent accuracy improvements, although the margin was smaller compared to the data-scarce scenarios.

In summary, the research establishes that VTs, particularly CvT, can be effective on medium-small datasets, and the `L_drloc` auxiliary task provides a robust, computationally efficient, and generally applicable method to significantly boost their performance across various settings and tasks, especially when training data is limited.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research makes several contributions that hold significance for the development and application of Visual Transformers (VTs) in computer vision:

1. **Addressing a Core Limitation of VTs:** The most significant impact lies in directly addressing the data-hungry nature of VTs. By demonstrating that VTs can be effectively trained and fine-tuned on medium-small datasets, and proposing a method to significantly enhance their efficiency under these conditions, the paper helps overcome a major barrier to the wider adoption of VTs in practical applications. Many real-world scenarios lack access to colossal, ImageNet-scale annotated datasets. This work suggests that VTs are a viable choice even for such data-constrained environments.

2. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The `L_drloc` auxiliary task consistently and often substantially improves the accuracy and generalization ability of VTs. This enhanced robustness is particularly critical when training data is scarce or when computational resources limit the number of training epochs. By encouraging VTs to learn spatial relationships internally, `L_drloc` provides a stronger foundation for their visual representations.

3. **Practical Applicability:** The proposed `L_drloc` method is designed for ease of integration. It does not require modifications to the core VT architecture, introduces negligible computational overhead, and can be applied during both training from scratch and fine-tuning. This "plug-and-play" characteristic increases its practical utility for researchers and practitioners working with existing VT models.

4. **Deeper Understanding of VT Behavior:** The empirical comparison of different second-generation VTs on small datasets provides valuable insights into their varying strengths and weaknesses, which are not apparent from large-scale ImageNet benchmarks. The finding that CvT-13 generally performs more robustly with less data helps guide future architectural designs of VTs specifically optimized for data-limited settings.

5. **Paving the Way for Tailored Self-Supervision in VTs:** The success of `L_drloc` demonstrates the potential of integrating simple, yet effective, self-supervised auxiliary tasks into the VT training paradigm. This opens new avenues for research into other forms of self-supervised or multi-task learning approaches specifically designed to imbue VTs with more inductive biases or extract richer information from data without reliance on extensive manual annotations. Such developments could further accelerate the evolution of VTs beyond their current state.

6. **Broader Impact on Vision Tasks:** The supplementary results extending the benefits of `L_drloc` to object detection, instance segmentation, and semantic segmentation tasks indicate that the improved representations learned via this method are transferable. This suggests that `L_drloc` could serve as a valuable component in pre-training VT backbones for a wide array of downstream computer vision applications, enhancing performance even in complex structured prediction tasks.

In conclusion, this research significantly advances the efficiency and practicality of Visual Transformers, making them a more competitive and accessible architectural choice for a broader spectrum of real-world computer vision problems, especially those characterized by limited data availability.
