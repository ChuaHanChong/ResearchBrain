# Safety-Critical and Distributed Nonlinear Predictive Controllers for Teams of Quadrupedal Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Virginia Tech and Caltech developed a hierarchical control framework featuring a distributed nonlinear model predictive control (DNMPC) system that integrates higher-order control barrier functions (HOCBFs) for formal safety guarantees and a Lennard-Jones potential for consensus. This framework achieved a 100 Hz update rate and a 93.8% collision avoidance success rate in simulations with quadrupedal robot teams, demonstrating robust, safe, and cooperative locomotion on rough terrain and under disturbances.

## Problem

- Coordinating teams of legged robots in mission-critical tasks is challenging due to complex dynamics and the need for robust, real-time control.
- Existing Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) for multi-robot systems often lacks formal safety guarantees, and Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) are typically limited to instantaneous safety with a zero-control horizon.
- Previous distributed NMPC approaches for quadrupedal robots faced computational limitations, hindering the high-frequency control loops necessary for dynamic locomotion.

## Method

- A hierarchical control framework was designed, including a high-level path planner, a middle-level Distributed Nonlinear ==Model Predictive Control== (DNMPC), and a low-level whole-body controller.
- The middle-level DNMPC utilizes a Single Rigid Body (SRB) model and integrates Higher-Order Discrete-Time Control Barrier Functions (HOCBFs) to ensure formal safety from collisions.
- A Lennard-Jones potential function is incorporated into the DNMPC cost to facilitate a consensus protocol among agents, promoting cohesive movement while prioritizing safety.

## Results

- The framework achieved a real-time NMPC loop update rate of 100 Hz, representing a substantial improvement in computational efficiency over previous methods.
- Numerical simulations demonstrated a **93.8%** collision avoidance success rate, outperforming approaches without integrated CBFs by a margin of **27.89%** (compared to **66%** for Euclidean distance LIP-based NMPC).
- Hardware experiments with Unitree A1 robots confirmed the system's ability to safely modify trajectories, reject external disturbances, and operate robustly on rough terrain while maintaining inter-agent cohesion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct integration of Higher-Order Control Barrier Functions (HOCBFs) into a Distributed Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (DNMPC) framework enables formal safety guarantees for multi-agent legged systems without compromising real-time performance.
> - Employing a Lennard-Jones potential function within the NMPC cost effectively facilitates a consensus protocol, allowing cooperative behavior to emerge while prioritizing collision avoidance.
> - The combination of optimized DNMPC formulation, SRB dynamics, and a one-step delay communication protocol can achieve high-frequency (100 Hz) control loops crucial for dynamic and robust multi-quadrupedal locomotion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of multi-robot systems (MRSs) for mission-critical tasks, particularly those involving legged robots, is a significant area of research. Legged robots offer advantages in traversing challenging terrains, making them suitable for applications such as collaborative firefighting, unmanned rescue, disaster response, and exploration. While advancements have enhanced the agility and robustness of individual legged robots, challenges persist in coordinating teams of such robots. These challenges stem from the high dimensionality, unilateral constraints, underactuation, and hybrid nature inherent to legged robot dynamics, which differentiate them from other MRSs and can limit the applicability of established control strategies.

Model Predictive Control (MPC) has been widely adopted for real-time trajectory planning in legged robotics, often leveraging reduced-order models (templates) like the Linear Inverted Pendulum (LIP), Angular Momentum LIP (ALIP), Spring-Loaded Inverted Pendulum (SLIP), and Single Rigid Body (SRB) models. For single-agent robots, MPC based on linearized templates often results in convex Quadratic Programs (QPs), which are computationally efficient. However, ensuring collision avoidance and handling the nonlinear, nonconvex constraints associated with MRSs necessitates the use of Nonlinear MPC (NMPC). While NMPC has been employed for gait planning in single quadrupedal robots and for multi-agent quadrupedal robots using LIP models, a gap exists in formally guaranteeing safety for MRSs.

Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) offer a formal framework for ensuring safety, typically implemented through QP-based optimization problems that modify nominal controllers to satisfy barrier constraints. These CBF-based QPs often operate with a zero-control horizon, which can limit their effectiveness for extended trajectory planning, particularly for inherently unstable and underactuated legged robots. Previous work has explored hierarchical frameworks where CBFs generate reference paths for an NMPC, but this separation means CBFs do not directly operate within the NMPC loop itself. Integrating CBFs directly into real-time NMPC for complex multi-agent legged systems remains a less explored area. Some studies have attempted this integration for simple integrator dynamics or single-agent quadrupedal robots, often with limitations in update frequency or the scope of safety guarantees within a distributed context.

MPC implementations for MRSs are categorized into centralized, decentralized, and distributed approaches. Centralized control can be optimal but is often impractical due to high computational and communication demands for large systems. Decentralized MPC reduces complexity but sacrifices coordination in tightly coupled systems. Distributed MPC (DMPC) offers a balance by allowing limited inter-agent communication for collective behavior. Extending DMPC to Distributed Nonlinear MPC (DNMPC) for multi-agent quadrupedal robots faces significant hurdles due to the complex dynamics, instability, and constraints of legged locomotion. Prior DNMPC approaches for quadrupedal MRSs have addressed collision avoidance using Euclidean distance constraints but encountered computational tractability issues, limiting their update rates. This paper aims to address these identified limitations by proposing a computationally efficient, unified DNMPC framework that directly integrates CBF-based safety guarantees with a consensus protocol, enabling higher control loop frequencies and improved collision avoidance for teams of quadrupedal robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of this research is to develop computationally efficient, unified distributed nonlinear model predictive control (DNMPC) algorithms that integrate control barrier function (CBF)-based collision safety guarantees with a consensus protocol. This integration is intended to enable extended planning horizons for the safe locomotion of multi-agent quadrupedal robots in the presence of disturbances and challenging terrain conditions.

The motivation for this work arises from several identified limitations in existing research and the practical requirements for deploying multi-agent legged robot systems in complex environments:

1. **Formal Safety Guarantees for MRSs:** While NMPC can handle nonlinear and nonconvex constraints for collision avoidance, ensuring the safety of multi-robot systems requires a formal definition grounded in the concept of invariant sets. CBFs provide such a formal framework, but their integration into real-time NMPC for sophisticated MRSs, such as quadrupedal robot teams, has been underexplored.
2. **Limitations of Zero-Control Horizon CBFs:** CBFs, when typically implemented via quadratic programs (QPs) at the planning layer, offer immediate safety guarantees but operate with a zero-control horizon. This limits their effectiveness for extended trajectory planning, which is crucial for inherently unstable, underactuated, and nonlinear legged robot models that require predictive capabilities.
3. **Computational Efficiency and Real-time Operation:** Previous DNMPC approaches for multi-agent quadrupedal robots have faced computational tractability issues, leading to low real-time update rates (e.g., 5.5 Hz). High-frequency control loops are essential for dynamic and robust locomotion of legged robots in real-world scenarios.
4. **Cooperative Locomotion Challenges:** Multi-agent systems require coordination mechanisms to maintain desired formations or inter-agent distances (consensus protocol) while simultaneously ensuring safety from collisions with each other and obstacles, especially under disturbances and rough terrain. Existing work often separates safety from consensus or lacks the robustness for practical deployment.
5. **Robustness to Environmental Uncertainty:** Robots operating in unknown or partially known environments require controllers that can adapt to uncertainties, such as variations in obstacle positions or rough terrain, without compromising safety or mission objectives.

To address these motivations, the paper outlines specific contributions:
- Development of a hierarchical control framework with three layers.
- Proposal of a real-time (100 Hz) distributed NMPC algorithm for the middle layer, optimizing nonlinear Single Rigid Body (SRB) models.
- Incorporation of discrete-time CBF constraints into the DNMPC for robot-to-robot and robot-to-obstacle safety.
- Integration of a Lennard-Jones potential function into the NMPC cost for a consensus protocol, promoting cohesive behavior.
- Validation through extensive numerical simulations and hardware experiments with Unitree A1 robots under conditions involving external disturbances, rough terrain, and uncertain obstacle information.
- Demonstration of improved collision avoidance success rates and computational efficiency compared to prior methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research presents a hierarchical, safety-critical control framework designed for the cooperative locomotion of multi-agent quadrupedal robots. This framework comprises three distinct layers: a high-level path planner, a middle-level distributed nonlinear model predictive control (DNMPC) system, and a low-level nonlinear whole-body controller (WBC).

#### 3.1. High-Level Path Planner

The uppermost layer generates offline reference trajectories for the middle-layer DNMPC algorithms. This is achieved using a hybrid path planning approach that combines the A* algorithm with potential fields (PFs). The A* algorithm is utilized to generate globally optimal way-points, which are then connected by locally smooth and dynamics-informed reference paths generated by the PFs. This hybrid approach aims to mitigate the local minima issues often associated with standalone PF methods, ensuring robust initial path generation.

#### 3.2. Middle-Level: Distributed Nonlinear MPC (DNMPC) based on Higher-Order CBFs (HOCBFs)

This layer is responsible for real-time trajectory optimization and operates at 100 Hz. It addresses the nonlinear dynamics of multi-agent quadrupedal robots and integrates safety guarantees.

- **Template Model:** Each agent's dynamics are modeled using a Single Rigid Body (SRB) model. The state variables for each agent \(i\) include its center of mass (COM) position (\(p^i\)) and orientation (\(\Theta^i\)), along with their respective time derivatives (\(\dot{p}^i\), \(\omega^i\)). Control inputs (\(u^i\)) are defined as the ground reaction forces (GRFs) that generate the net force and torque around the COM. The nonlinear SRB dynamics are discretized using the Euler method.

- **Collision Safety Formulation:**
 - The framework defines global safe sets (SA for inter-agent, SO for agent-obstacle) based on Euclidean distances between the COM of agents and obstacles.
 - For each agent \(i\), local safe sets (SA\(^i\) and SO\(^i\)) are defined, comprising conditions where the distance between agent \(i\)'s COM and any other agent \(j\)'s COM, or any obstacle \(\ell\), exceeds a threshold distance \(d_{th}\).
 - These local safe sets are reformulated as a super-level set of a continuous local function \(h^i(x^i, x_{N(i)}, o) \ge 0\), where \(x^i\) is the local state, \(x_{N(i)}\) are states of neighboring agents, and \(o\) are obstacle coordinates. This function \(h^i\) contains terms like \(h_{i,j} = ||g(x^i) - g(x^j)|| - d_{th}\) and \(h_{i,\ell} = ||g(x^i) - o_\ell|| - d_{th}\). For efficiency in environments with many agents and obstacles, \(h^i\) can be expressed as the minimum of all relevant pairwise distance functions.

- **Higher-Order Discrete-Time CBF (HOCBF):**
 - For systems where the control input does not appear in the first-order difference of the CBF (i.e., relative degree \(r > 1\)), the concept of HOCBF is employed. For the local function \(h^i\), a relative degree of \(r=2\) is considered.
 - A series of functions \(\psi_a^i\) for \(a \in \{0, 1..., r\}\) are defined based on \(h^i\). The condition \(\psi_r^i(x_t^i, u_t^i) \ge 0\) (or its multi-agent equivalent) ensures the forward invariance of the intersection of corresponding sets \(S_a^i\), thereby guaranteeing safety.

- **DNMPC Formulation:**
 - For each agent \(i\), the DNMPC solves a nonlinear optimization problem over a prediction horizon \(N\). The objective is to minimize a cost function comprising a terminal cost (tracking a reference state) and a stage cost.
 - Constraints include:
 - **Prediction Dynamics:** The discretized SRB model \(x_{t+k+1|t}^i = f^i(x_{t+k|t}^i, u_{t+k|t}^i)\).
 - **HOCBF Constraints:** \(\psi_2^i(x_{t+k|t}^i, \hat{x}_{N(i),t+k|t}, u_{t+k|t}^i, \hat{u}_{N(i),t+k|t}) \ge 0\), ensuring safety.
 - **Feasibility Constraints:** Local states \(x^i\) must remain in the feasible state set \(\mathcal{X}\), and control inputs \(u^i\) (GRFs) must belong to the friction cone (FC).

- **Consensus Protocol:**
 - The stage cost includes a term \(wU^i\) for consensus, where \(U^i\) is an artificial potential function inspired by the Lennard-Jones model. This function depends on the estimated distance between the COM of agent \(i\) and its neighbors \(j\), specifically \(\rho_{i,j} = ||p_{x,y}^i - \hat{p}_{x,y}^j||\).
 - The Lennard-Jones potential encourages agents to maintain a consistent "sticking distance" by generating attractive or repulsive forces, effectively emulating soft-holonomic constraints on inter-agent distances. This promotes soft-flocking behavior, prioritizing hard safety constraints over strict adherence to consensus if conflicts arise.

- **One-Step Delay Communication Protocol (OSDCP):**
 - To address the challenge of real-time unavailability of optimal solutions from other agents for the HOCBF constraints and consensus protocol, an OSDCP is employed.
 - Agent \(i\) approximates the predicted states and inputs of neighboring agents \(j\) at time \(t+k\) using their optimal solutions computed at the previous time step \(t-1\) (\(\hat{x}_{j,t+k|t} \approx x_{j,\star,t+k|t-1}\) and \(\hat{u}_{j,t+k|t} \approx u_{j,\star,t+k|t-1}\)). This assumes a sufficiently small time step.

#### 3.3. Low-Level Nonlinear Whole-Body Controller (WBC)

Operating at 1 kHz, this layer receives the optimal reduced-order state and GRF trajectories from the middle-layer DNMPCs. The WBC imposes the full-order dynamical model of each quadrupedal robot. It is formulated as a real-time QP that tracks the prescribed trajectories within the framework of virtual constraints, solving for dynamically feasible joint-level torques.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup and Controller Synthesis

- **Hardware:** Unitree A1 quadrupedal robots equipped with NVIDIA Jetson TX2, RP-LiDAR S1 for obstacle detection, and onboard computing resources.
- **Simulation:** RaiSim physics engine for numerical simulations with up to four A1 robots.
- **Software:** Distributed algorithms run on a multi-threaded offboard desktop PC. Obstacle detection utilizes a ROS package. NLPs in DNMPC are solved using the CasADi framework with the IPOPT solver, leveraging previous solutions as initial guesses.
- **Parameters:** Control horizon \(N=10\), sampling time \(T_s=10\) ms. Collision avoidance threshold \(d_{th}=0.6\) m. Class K functions for HOCBFs are \(\alpha_1(s) = 0.1s\) and \(\alpha_2(s) = 0.05s\). Lennard-Jones potential parameters: \(\epsilon=50\), \(\sigma=0.85\) m, weighting factor \(w=1e9\). A trotting gait with a step time of 180 ms is used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed CBF-based DNMPC framework was evaluated through both extensive numerical simulations and hardware experiments with Unitree A1 robots.

#### 4.1. Computational Performance

- The proposed framework achieved a real-time nonlinear predictive control loop update rate of 100 Hz. This represents a substantial improvement in computational efficiency compared to previous work, which reported a rate of 5.5 Hz. This was facilitated by the optimized DNMPC formulation and the reformulation of the \(h^i\) function (Equation 17) to minimize inequality constraints.
- The NMPC computation time for two and four agents was approximately 4.9 ms.

#### 4.2. Hardware Experiments (Two A1 Robots)

Experiments were conducted under various challenging scenarios:
- **Modification of Trajectories by DNMPC:** When commanded to follow intentionally intersecting reference trajectories on rough terrain (wooden blocks up to 5 cm high), the CBF-based DNMPC successfully adjusted the paths of both robots to prevent collisions. This demonstrated the framework's ability to handle conflicting high-level commands and ensure safety in dynamic environments.
- **Safety with External Disturbances:** During straight-line locomotion, an external physical push was applied to one agent. The consensus protocol combined with CBFs in the DNMPC enabled the unperturbed agent to react by moving away, maintaining a constant separation distance and avoiding collision.
- **Robustness against Uncertainty and Rough Terrain:** Robots successfully navigated environments with initially known but then perturbed obstacle locations and rough terrain. The DNMPCs made real-time adjustments to account for these uncertainties, demonstrating robustness. Analysis of velocity trajectories showed deviations from the high-level planner's references, indicating the DNMPC's corrective actions.
- **Consensus Protocol Evaluation:** Experiments with and without the Lennard-Jones potential-based consensus protocol (weighted with \(w=1e9\) vs. \(w=0\)) demonstrated its efficacy. Activating the protocol improved soft-flocking behavior and inter-agent cohesion, maintaining a more stable inter-agent distance when collision avoidance was not actively constrained.

#### 4.3. Numerical Simulations (Four A1 Robots)

- Simulations involving four A1 robots navigating rough terrain with 20 randomly distributed obstacles also showed successful and safe traversal without observed infeasibility issues.

#### 4.4. Quantitative Comparison and Analysis

Extensive numerical simulations were conducted across 200 randomly generated terrains with 20 randomly positioned obstacles to quantitatively compare the proposed CBF-based DNMPC (with SRB dynamics) against two alternative distributed predictive control techniques:
1. **DNMPC without CBFs (Euclidean distance constraints for LIP dynamics):** This approach, similar to previous work, relied on geometric distance constraints for collision avoidance without formal CBFs.
2. **DNMPC without CBFs (with CBF constraints enforced at low-level WBCs):** In this scenario, the CBF conditions were applied at the low-level whole-body controller, rather than integrated into the NMPC.

The results, measured as the success rate (both agents reaching 10 m without collision and maintaining gait stability) as a function of traveled distance, indicated:
- **Proposed CBF-based DNMPC (SRB):** Achieved an overall success rate of 93.8%.
- **DNMPC without CBFs (Euclidean distance for LIP):** Achieved an overall success rate of 66%. This represents a 27.89% higher success rate for the proposed method compared to this conventional NMPC approach.
- **DNMPC without CBFs (CBF-based WBCs):** Achieved an overall success rate of 14.5%. This demonstrates a 79.39% improvement in performance for the unified CBF-based DNMPC approach compared to applying CBFs solely at the low-level.

No infeasibility issues were observed for the proposed DNMPCs during the simulations or hardware experiments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of multi-agent robotic systems, particularly for legged robots operating in complex and dynamic environments. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

- **Enhanced Safety Guarantees:** By formally integrating Higher-Order Discrete-Time Control Barrier Functions (HOCBFs) directly into the Distributed Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (DNMPC) framework, the paper provides a robust mechanism for ensuring robot-to-robot and robot-to-obstacle collision avoidance. This addresses a critical need for safety guarantees in multi-robot systems, moving beyond heuristic or less formal constraint handling. The demonstrated increase in collision avoidance success rates (from 66% to 94%) substantiates the practical benefit of this integration.

- **Improved Computational Efficiency for Complex Systems:** Achieving a real-time NMPC loop update rate of 100 Hz, a substantial increase from previously reported rates of 5.5 Hz for similar tasks, is crucial. This high control frequency is essential for dynamically stable and agile locomotion of inherently unstable legged robots, allowing for more responsive and robust control in challenging conditions. The efficient integration of HOCBFs without significantly burdening the computational budget represents a key methodological achievement.

- **Robustness to Environmental Uncertainties and Disturbances:** The validation through hardware experiments and numerical simulations under conditions involving external pushes, rough terrain, and uncertain obstacle information demonstrates the framework's robustness. This capability is vital for real-world applications where perfect environmental knowledge or disturbance-free operation cannot be guaranteed.

- **Effective Cooperative Locomotion:** The inclusion of a Lennard-Jones potential function in the NMPC cost formulation enables a consensus protocol, promoting cohesive behavior and "soft-flocking" among agents. This allows for coordinated movement while ensuring that hard safety constraints take precedence when necessary, providing a flexible and effective approach to multi-agent cooperation.

- **Broader Applicability to Mission-Critical Tasks:** The capabilities developed in this work—safe, cooperative, robust, and agile locomotion in complex environments—directly contribute to the feasibility of deploying teams of quadrupedal robots in mission-critical applications. These include disaster response, search and rescue, collaborative firefighting, and exploration in terrains inaccessible to wheeled or tracked vehicles.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper lays a foundation for future work, explicitly mentioning plans to integrate learning-based approaches for agent foot placement and to investigate scalability to larger teams and more complex environments. This suggests the proposed framework is not a terminal solution but a strong enabling technology for further advancements in intelligent and autonomous multi-robot systems.

In summary, this research advances the state of the art in multi-agent legged robot control by combining formal safety guarantees with computationally efficient distributed nonlinear predictive control, demonstrating its practical effectiveness and paving the way for more sophisticated and reliable robotic deployments.
