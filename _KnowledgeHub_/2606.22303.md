# FlowDPG: Deterministic Policy Gradient on Flow Matching Policies for Real-World Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> FlowDPG introduces an off-policy actor-critic method that applies deterministic policy gradient updates to flow matching policies for robotic manipulation without requiring backpropagation through time. The approach achieved a 92% end-to-end success rate on a complex dual-arm assembly task and improved policy robustness to unseen real-world disturbances by up to 18%.

## Problem

- Imitation learning struggles with long-horizon, contact-rich robotic tasks due to high data collection costs and performance degradation when encountering states outside the demonstration distribution.
- Integrating standard off-policy reinforcement learning (DPG) with expressive generative policies (like flow matching models that use multi-step ODEs) is computationally intensive and numerically unstable due to the necessity of backpropagating critic gradients through the entire ODE.
- Existing RL fine-tuning methods for generative policies often discard critic gradients or rely on limited auxiliary modules, which constrains policy improvement and the synthesis of novel actions beyond initial demonstrations.

## Method

- **FlowDPG** distills the critic gradient directly into the flow matching velocity field through a BPTT-free mechanism, training the policy via an L2 loss against a Q-improved clean action.
- It utilizes an ==Implicit Q-Learning (IQL)== framework with twin critics for stable value estimation, bootstrapping from a high-quantile value function to mitigate extrapolation error.
- The method employs an offline-to-online training pipeline, initially training on expert demonstrations and then fine-tuning with asynchronously collected real-world experience, guided by a stage-aware composite reward.

## Results

- FlowDPG achieved a **92%** end-to-end success rate on a complex, long-horizon dual-arm AirPods assembly task, substantially outperforming behavior cloning (**64%**) and various other RL baselines (e.g., RLT at **80%**, QAM at **80%**).
- Online reinforcement learning significantly enhanced policy robustness, increasing recovery rates for unseen disturbances (like object re-grasp or scene perturbations) by up to **18%** (e.g., from **70%** to **88%** for 'adapt grasp').
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the consistency regularizer and adaptive shift, showing that removing either component caused significant performance drops in overall success (to **80%** and **76%** respectively).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Deterministic Policy Gradient-style updates can be effectively applied to multi-step generative flow matching policies by distilling critic gradients, thereby bypassing the computational and numerical challenges of full backpropagation through time (BPTT).
> - A consistency regularizer that ensures the fidelity of projected actions and an adaptive shift that normalizes and scales critic gradients are crucial for stabilizing training and enabling robust performance in this critic-guided distillation framework.
> - Rich, well-shaped reward signals, combining dense progress terms with discrete stage-transition bonuses, are vital for FlowDPG to extract reliable and useful information from the critic, as sparse rewards can lead to noisy or counterproductive critic signals.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is progressing towards developing generalist policies capable of executing complex, long-horizon, and contact-rich tasks in real-world environments. A prevalent approach involves large-scale supervised models trained on extensive robotic datasets. However, imitation learning (IL) alone exhibits limitations, including the prohibitive cost of collecting high-quality, large-scale datasets, and the inherent degradation of policy performance when encountering states outside the demonstration distribution. In long-horizon tasks, even infrequent failures can compound, leading to overall task failure. This motivates the integration of real-world reinforcement learning (RL) to enable robots to learn from their own experience, thereby pushing policy capabilities beyond the initial data manifold.

Recent advancements in manipulation policies have leveraged complex generative models, such as flow matching and diffusion models, as action heads. These models generate expressive, multi-modal action chunks through multi-step Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) or denoising processes. A significant challenge arises when attempting to combine these generative policies with standard off-policy actor-critic RL methods, such as Deterministic Policy Gradient (DPG). The core issue lies in the necessity to backpropagate the critic gradient (∇_a Q) through the entire multi-step ODE of the generative model. This process, known as Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT), is computationally intensive, as its cost scales linearly with the number of solver steps. Furthermore, it is numerically fragile due to the compounding of Jacobians, which can lead to gradient explosion or vanishing, especially when the underlying velocity field is not yet robustly trained. Consequently, combining expressive flow policies with value-driven RL has been challenging, often requiring either the discarding of the critic gradient or reliance on coarse, one-step approximations.

Previous research efforts to fine-tune imitation-trained manipulation policies with reward signals can be broadly categorized into three families:
1. **Value-conditioning methods:** These approaches utilize only scalar value or advantage information, discarding the critic's action gradient. Examples include RA-BC, AWR, and RECAP. While these methods can improve policy performance by reweighting or conditioning on existing demonstrations, they are inherently limited to actions within the original demonstration distribution and cannot synthesize novel actions.
2. **Auxiliary-module adaptation methods:** These methods keep the base policy frozen and train a separate, lightweight adaptation module. DSRL, PLD, and RLT fall into this category. The improvement potential of these methods is typically capped by the capacity of the adapter rather than the full expressivity of the base policy.
3. **Critic-gradient methods:** This family aims to directly exploit the critic's action gradient. Q-score matching and QAM are examples. QAM, a concurrent work, converts ∇_a Q into step-wise supervision via an auxiliary adjoint flow, which can be sensitive to critic noise across the trajectory.

FlowDPG contributes to this landscape by introducing a novel off-policy actor-critic method specifically designed for flow matching policies. It addresses the computational and numerical limitations of BPTT by distilling the critic gradient into the velocity field through a BPTT-free mechanism. The work aims to combine the expressivity of flow matching policies with the effectiveness of DPG-style policy improvement, providing a transparent theoretical connection to vanilla DPG while achieving robust real-world performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a stable and computationally efficient method for applying deterministic policy gradient (DPG) style updates to flow matching policies in the context of real-world robotic manipulation. This endeavor is motivated by several critical challenges observed in the current state of robotic learning:

1. **Limitations of Imitation Learning for Complex Tasks:** For long-horizon, contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks, imitation learning (IL) based on teleoperated demonstrations alone proves insufficient. The costs associated with collecting high-quality, large-scale robotic datasets are substantial, and even with such datasets, an IL policy inherently mirrors the demonstration distribution. When deployed in novel or slightly perturbed environments not covered by the training data, performance degrades. This distribution shift is particularly problematic for long-horizon tasks, where minor errors at individual stages can cascade into catastrophic failures.
2. **Need for Self-Improvement and Exploration:** To overcome the limitations of IL, robots require the ability to learn from their own actions and experience, moving beyond the manifold of initial demonstration data. Reinforcement learning (RL) is a natural candidate for this self-improvement, enabling policies to adapt and explore beyond the initial expert behavior.
3. **Challenges of RL with Generative Action Heads:** State-of-the-art robotic manipulation policies increasingly utilize expressive generative models, such as flow matching and diffusion models, as action heads. These models generate actions through multi-step ODEs, offering enhanced expressivity for complex and multi-modal action distributions. However, integrating these generative policies with conventional off-policy actor-critic RL methods faces a fundamental technical hurdle: computing the actor update requires backpropagating the critic's action gradient (∇_a Q) through the entire multi-step ODE. This process is computationally expensive, with costs scaling linearly with the number of ODE solver steps. Moreover, it is numerically unstable, as the chain rule involves a product of Jacobians that can lead to gradient explosion or vanishing, especially in the early training phases when the velocity field is not well-trained.
4. **Inadequacy of Current RL Fine-tuning Approaches:** Existing RL fine-tuning methods for expressive manipulation policies often compromise on the direct use of critic gradients. Some approaches discard the critic gradient, relying only on scalar value information, which limits policy improvement to the existing demonstration distribution. Others employ auxiliary modules or one-step approximations, which may not fully leverage the capacity of the underlying generative policy or adequately reshape its multi-step denoising process.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the FlowDPG research are:
- To design a novel, Deterministic Policy Gradient (DPG)-style method that can stably and efficiently update flow matching policies without requiring backpropagation through the entire denoising ODE (BPTT).
- To formally establish the theoretical connection between the proposed FlowDPG update direction and the standard vanilla DPG algorithm, thereby providing a clear theoretical foundation.
- To empirically validate FlowDPG's effectiveness and robustness in a challenging, real-world robotic manipulation scenario involving a long-horizon, multi-stage, dual-arm assembly task, demonstrating its ability to achieve high success rates and adapt to out-of-distribution disturbances.

By addressing these objectives, FlowDPG seeks to bridge the gap between expressive generative action representations and effective, scalable reinforcement learning for real-world robot control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FlowDPG is an off-policy actor-critic method designed for flow matching policies, developed to overcome the computational and numerical challenges of backpropagating critic gradients through multi-step ODEs. The method's core approach involves distilling the critic gradient into the flow matching velocity field at training time, bypassing the need for Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT).

The methodology is structured around an offline-to-online training pipeline (Figure 2, Algorithm 1) comprising two main coupled components:
1. **Value Estimation with Twin-Critic IQL (Section 3.1):**
 - **IQL Framework:** The method adopts Implicit Q-Learning (IQL) with twin critics, drawing inspiration from TD3, to estimate action values. IQL is chosen to mitigate extrapolation error during value learning by replacing the conventional `max_a Q(s, a)` bootstrap target (which queries potentially out-of-distribution actions) with a value function `Vψ(s)`. This `Vψ(s)` estimates a high-quantile action-value for in-distribution actions at state `s`.
 - **Twin Critics:** Two Q-networks, `Qϕ1` and `Qϕ2`, are used to further reduce Q-value overestimation caused by out-of-distribution (OOD) actions and TD-bootstrap noise. The minimum of these twin critics is used where a single Q-estimate is required. Each critic is paired with an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) target network for stable value loss computation.
 - **Loss Functions:**
 - `Vψ` is trained via expectile regression (Eq. 1) of the minimum twin-critic estimate on dataset actions, with an expectile `τ > 0.5` to pull `Vψ(s)` towards the upper tail of the action-value distribution.
 - The critics `Qϕi` are bootstrapped from `Vψ` (Eq. 2), using a target `yQ = rt + γH Vψ(st+H)`, which avoids extrapolation to unseen actions.
 - **Reward Signal:** Chunk-level rewards `rt` are derived from SARM (Stage-Aware Reward Predictor), a pre-trained and frozen module. SARM outputs a stage label `z(ot)` and a within-episode progress `ϕ(ot) ∈`. These are combined into a composite reward (Eq. 3) consisting of three terms: a dense per-step progress term (`ϕ(ot+H) - ϕ(ot)`), a fixed bonus for predicted stage transitions (`βstage`), and a terminal success/failure bonus (`rterm`).

2. **Policy Extraction via FlowDPG (Section 3.2):**
 - **Motivation:** A standard flow matching policy transports noise (`x0`) to a feasible action (`x1`) within the demonstration support. FlowDPG aims to incorporate a critic-driven correction to steer this action towards higher value.
 - **Q-Gradient Calculation:**
 - The clean action `x1` is estimated from any intermediate noisy action `xt` by a single forward pass of the velocity predictor `vθ` (Eq. 4), resulting in `ˆa:= xt + (1 - t) ⋅ vθ(xt, t, s)`. This approximation relies on `vθ` accurately predicting the velocity field, a property encouraged by a consistency regularizer.
 - The critic gradient `g` is then computed at this projected clean action `ˆa` (with `stop-grad` on critic parameters): `g = ∇_ˆa [min(Qϕi(s, ˆa))]` (Eq. 5).
 - **Adaptive Shift:** To stabilize updates, the magnitude of `g` is normalized and scaled to the local velocity scale (Eq. 6): `∆ = α ⋅ ||ut|| / (||g|| + ε) ⋅ g`, where `α` is a base guidance scale and `ε` prevents division by zero.
 - **Distilled Flow Target:** The Q-improved clean action `a*` is formed by adding `∆` to `ˆa`: `a* = ˆa + ∆`. The corresponding velocity target `u*t` is then `u*t = a* - x0` (Eq. 7).
 - **Policy Loss:** The policy `vθ` is regressed against `u*t` (distillation loss) alongside a behavior-cloning anchor (`ut`, the original demonstration-driven velocity) via an L2 loss (Eq. 8): `L_FlowDPG(θ) = λ ⋅ ||vθ - u*t||^2 + (1 - λ) ⋅ ||vθ - ut||^2`. The weight `λ` linearly warms up from 0 to `λmax` over `Nwarmup` steps, preventing an unreliable early critic from dominating.
 - **Consistency Regularizer:** To ensure the reliability of `ˆa` (i.e., that it remains close to the true clean action `x1`), a consistency regularizer `L_cons(θ) = E[||ˆa - x1||^2]` is added (Eq. 9). This term implicitly penalizes velocity error weighted by `(1 - t)^2`, emphasizing samples where projection error is most critical.
 - **Relationship to Vanilla DPG:** The paper formally establishes that the FlowDPG update gradient (Eq. 11) approximates the vanilla DPG gradient (Eq. 10) under three explicit conditions: (i) the critic gradient is evaluated at `ˆa` instead of `x1`, effectively collapsing the ODE sensitivity matrix to the identity, with `L_cons` maintaining this fidelity; (ii) the trajectory-wide integral of DPG is approximated by a Monte Carlo estimate at a single `t`; and (iii) the un-normalized step size is replaced by the adaptive scaling of `2λα||ut||`. This derivation provides a theoretical justification for the BPTT-free approach.

**Offline-to-Online Pipeline Details:**
- **Offline Phase:** The visual backbone (DINOv2), flow matching action head, and IQL value head are jointly trained on a dataset of expert demonstrations (`D_off`) using the FlowDPG objective.
- **Online Phase:** The policy trained offline is deployed on the real-world dual-arm setup to collect new experience. These real-world rollouts are asynchronously added to an online replay buffer (`D_on`). The learner samples mini-batches from a mixture of `D_off` and `D_on` (approx. 1:1 ratio) to balance high-quality demonstrations with fresh, exploratory data. The backbone and SARM reward predictor are frozen during this phase. Updated actor and critic checkpoints are periodically synchronized to the deployed robot.

**Experimental Validation:**
- The method is evaluated on a long-horizon, contact-rich, dual-arm AirPods assembly task (8 sequential stages, millimeter precision, bimanual dexterity).
- Performance is measured by per-stage, overall, and average success rates.
- Comparisons are made against various baselines from value-conditioning, auxiliary-module adaptation, and other critic-gradient families, ensuring shared core components (backbone, action chunk horizon, reward, value head, data split).
- Ablation studies investigate the impact of the consistency regularizer, adaptive shift, and individual reward components on performance and training stability.
- Robustness to unseen disturbances (e.g., re-grasp, re-open/close, re-insert, scene perturbation) is assessed for both offline and online-tuned policies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding FlowDPG's performance, robustness, and the contribution of its individual components on a complex real-world manipulation task.

#### 4.1. Main Results (Table 1)

FlowDPG demonstrated an end-to-end success rate of 92% on the long-horizon, dual-arm AirPods assembly task, representing a substantial improvement over other methods.
- **Baseline Comparison:**
 - **Behavior Cloning (BC) base:** Achieved 64% overall success.
 - **Value-conditioning methods (RA-BC, AWR, RECAP):** These methods, which reweight or condition on existing demonstrations, showed improvements over BC (72-76% overall). Their gains were observed in stages requiring mode selection from existing data (e.g., Open/Close case) but performed similarly to BC on contact-rich insertion stages, indicating their limitation in generating actions outside the demonstration support.
 - **Auxiliary-module adaptation methods (DSRL, PLD, RLT):** These approaches, which keep the base policy frozen and train a separate adapter, achieved 68-80% overall success. RLT was the strongest in this category at 80%, but the improvements were limited by the adapter's capacity and often by discarding the multi-modal expressivity of the underlying flow policy.
 - **Critic-gradient methods (QAM, FlowDPG):** FlowDPG (offline) achieved 88% overall success, which further improved to 92% with online learning. This significantly surpassed QAM, a concurrent critic-gradient approach, which achieved 80%. The paper attributes FlowDPG's superior performance to its simpler distillation mechanism, which evaluates a single critic gradient and distills it via L2 regression, compared to QAM's adjoint matching requiring step-wise supervision and being potentially sensitive to critic noise across the trajectory. FlowDPG's direct update of the velocity field allows the full capacity of the flow policy to be leveraged for improvement beyond the demonstration manifold.

#### 4.2. Online RL Improves Robustness to Unseen Disturbances (Table 2)

The online deployment phase of FlowDPG led to an increase from 88% (offline) to 92% (overall) end-to-end success. These gains were particularly notable in contact-rich stages like Grasp/Insert R/L pod, suggesting that online data collection exposes the policy to under-represented corner cases and enables better adaptation.
To assess generalization, policies were tested under three categories of unseen disturbances:
- **Re-grasp (object replaced in tray):** Offline policy achieved 78% recovery, improved to 92% with online learning (+14%).
- **Re-open/close (case state inverted):** Offline policy achieved 82% recovery, improved to 94% with online learning (+12%).
- **Re-insert (pod dislodged):** Offline policy achieved 86% recovery, improved to 92% with online learning (+6%).
- **Adapt grasp (tray reshuffled mid-execution):** This scene perturbation, which represents a mid-execution visual transition absent from demonstrations, showed the largest gain. The offline policy achieved 70% recovery, which improved to 88% with online learning (+18%).
These results indicate that online RL significantly enhances the policy's robustness, especially to dynamic perturbations and partial-success states, by teaching the velocity field to steer such states back towards valid trajectories through critic-guided updates.

#### 4.3. Ablation: Consistency Regularizer and Adaptive Shift (Figure 4)

Ablation studies isolated the contributions of key design choices:
- **Removing the consistency regularizer (`L_cons`):** This led to the projection error (`||ˆa - x1||`) plateauing at a significantly higher level (an order of magnitude larger) and amplified the Q-value discrepancy (`|Q(s, ˆa) - Q(s, x1)|`). Consequently, the critic gradient was evaluated at an out-of-distribution action, leading to unreliable signals and a drop in Overall success from 92% to 80%.
- **Removing the adaptive shift:** This caused the training loss to oscillate instead of decreasing smoothly, as the raw `αg` update had an unbounded magnitude. This component's removal resulted in the largest single-component performance drop, from 92% to 76% Overall success.
- Removing both components together resulted in a further marginal degradation to 72%, indicating that both the consistency regularizer and adaptive shift play complementary roles in mitigating misdirected critic-gradient signals.

#### 4.4. Ablation: Reward Components (Figure 5)

The study also ablated the individual components of the SARM composite reward (Eq. 3):
- **Removing the progress term:** This resulted in Q-values collapsing to a near-constant baseline within each stage, weakening the critic gradient signal and causing a gradual erosion of performance across the sequence, leading to 80% overall success.
- **Removing the stage-transition term:** While maintaining dense progress shaping, the absence of a discrete bonus for sub-task completion meant the critic gradient did not strongly favor concluding a sub-task, leading to 76% overall success.
- **Removing both progress and stage-transition terms (leaving only terminal bonus):** This led to a severe performance drop to 60%, even below the BC baseline. In this sparse reward setting, two visually similar states (e.g., gripper holding a closed case before opening vs. after re-closing) became indistinguishable in terms of reward. The policy frequently bypassed the entire pod-handling sequence to place the case directly, significantly degrading performance at the "Open case" stage (96% → 80%). This highlights that rich, well-shaped reward signals are crucial for FlowDPG to distill useful information from the critic gradient; with sparse shaping, the critic signal can become noisy and actively degrade the policy.

Overall, the findings demonstrate FlowDPG's efficacy in improving flow matching policies for complex real-world tasks through stable critic-gradient distillation, enhanced by an offline-to-online learning paradigm, robust design choices, and carefully engineered reward signals.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FlowDPG addresses a critical technical challenge in real-world robotic manipulation by enabling the stable and efficient application of deterministic policy gradient (DPG) methods to expressive flow matching policies. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Enabling Advanced Policy Improvement for Generative Models:** Flow matching and other generative models for action generation offer high expressivity and the ability to represent multi-modal action distributions, which are crucial for complex manipulation tasks. However, the computational and numerical fragility of backpropagating critic gradients through their multi-step ODEs has historically hindered their direct fine-tuning with RL. FlowDPG provides a BPTT-free distillation framework that bypasses this bottleneck, allowing these powerful generative policies to benefit from value-driven policy improvement. This advancement opens new avenues for leveraging the full potential of such models in RL contexts.

2. **Achieving High-Performance Real-World Manipulation:** The demonstration of 92% end-to-end success on a long-horizon, contact-rich, dual-arm AirPods assembly task underscores FlowDPG's practical efficacy. This level of performance, significantly surpassing existing RL baselines, indicates a tangible step forward in developing capable robotic systems for precision assembly and other intricate real-world tasks. The ability of a single policy to handle 8 sequential bimanual stages from raw observations without stage-level resets or hand-engineered switching is particularly impactful for reducing deployment complexity.

3. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The online RL phase of FlowDPG notably improves the policy's ability to recover from various unseen disturbances, such as object re-placement, state inversion, and mid-execution scene perturbations. The significant gains in recovery rates (up to +18%) demonstrate that the policy learns to steer back towards valid trajectories even when encountering states outside the initial demonstration distribution. This enhanced robustness is vital for deploying robots in dynamic and unpredictable real-world environments, where minor variations or unexpected events are common.

4. **Theoretical Clarity and Foundation:** By establishing a formal connection between the FlowDPG update and vanilla DPG through three explicit approximations, the research provides a transparent theoretical grounding for its methodology. This clarity helps in understanding the mechanism of action and differentiates FlowDPG from other critic-gradient approaches that may rely on more abstract stochastic-control formulations. This contributes to the interpretability and reliability of the method.

5. **Practical Efficiency and Stability:** FlowDPG's distillation mechanism, which involves a single critic-gradient evaluation and L2 regression, is simpler and more computationally efficient than alternative methods like adjoint matching, which require auxiliary flows and step-wise supervision across many ODE timesteps. The inclusion of components like the consistency regularizer and adaptive shift further contributes to the numerical stability of training, a critical factor for real-world RL systems.

6. **Path Towards More Capable Generalist Robots:** The ability to effectively fine-tune expressive, generative action heads with RL, coupled with robust performance and generalization capabilities, contributes to the development of more capable and adaptable generalist robot policies. As robot learning moves towards scaling and foundation models, methods like FlowDPG that enable efficient online learning and adaptation will be crucial for pushing the boundaries of what robots can achieve in complex, unstructured environments.

While the research identifies limitations such as reliance on stage-level annotations for rewards and evaluation on a single task family, FlowDPG represents a significant advance in the integration of expressive generative policies with reinforcement learning for robust, real-world robotic manipulation. Its principles may inspire future developments in policy learning for complex, multi-modal control tasks across various robotic platforms.
