# Task-Optimized Convolutional Recurrent Networks Align with Tactile Processing in the Rodent Brain

> [!summary] Summary
> Task-optimized convolutional recurrent networks effectively model tactile processing in the rodent brain, aligning with and often exceeding the explainable variance of neural activity in the somatosensory cortex. These models highlight the necessity of recurrent architectures and modality-specific self-supervised learning for robust tactile perception.

## Problem

- Tactile sensing is less understood in neuroscience compared to vision and audition, lacking comprehensive computational models for neural processing.
- Existing robotic tactile sensors and algorithms significantly lag biological capabilities, struggling to process realistic, high-dimensional touch inputs.
- Prior goal-driven models for tactile processing used simpler architectures and lacked direct comparisons to neural data for biological validation.

## Method

- Generated a high-variation, biomechanically realistic ==tactile dataset== using a customized 3D rodent whisker simulator interacting with diverse ShapeNet objects.
- Introduced a novel Encoder-Attender-Decoder (EAD) framework to systematically explore various temporal neural network architectures, including ConvRNNs, State Space Models, and Transformers.
- Trained models using supervised object categorization and contrastive self-supervised learning, incorporating custom, tactile-specific data augmentations like flips, rotation, and temporal reversal.

## Results

- The best ConvRNN-based EAD models saturated explainable neural variance in rodent somatosensory cortex, surpassing the average inter-animal neural consistency.
- Convolutional Recurrent Neural Networks consistently outperformed purely feedforward and State Space Model encoders in supervised tactile categorization tasks.
- Contrastive self-supervised learning (SimCLR) with tactile-specific augmentations achieved neural alignment scores comparable to or better than the top supervised models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Nonlinear recurrent processing, particularly with ConvRNNs, is essential for generating general-purpose tactile representations that align with rodent somatosensory cortex activity.
> - Task optimization refines architectural inductive biases, allowing computational models to explain a substantial portion of neural variability in tactile processing.
> - Self-supervised learning, when combined with modality-specific augmentations, provides an ethologically relevant and effective approach to developing biologically plausible tactile models.
