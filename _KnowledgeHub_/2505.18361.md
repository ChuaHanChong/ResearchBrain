# Task-Optimized Convolutional Recurrent Networks Align with Tactile Processing in the Rodent Brain

> [!summary] Summary
> Task-optimized convolutional recurrent networks effectively model tactile processing in the rodent brain, aligning with and often exceeding the explainable variance of neural activity in the somatosensory cortex. These models highlight the necessity of recurrent architectures and modality-specific self-supervised learning for robust tactile perception.

## Problem

- Tactile sensing is less understood in neuroscience compared to vision and audition, lacking comprehensive computational models for neural processing.
- Existing robotic tactile sensors and algorithms significantly lag biological capabilities, struggling to process realistic, high-dimensional touch inputs.
- Prior goal-driven models for tactile processing used simpler architectures and lacked direct comparisons to neural data for biological validation.

## Method

- Generated a high-variation, biomechanically realistic ==tactile dataset== using a customized 3D rodent whisker simulator interacting with diverse ShapeNet objects.
- Introduced a novel Encoder-Attender-Decoder (EAD) framework to systematically explore various temporal neural network architectures, including ConvRNNs, State Space Models, and Transformers.
- Trained models using supervised object categorization and contrastive self-supervised learning, incorporating custom, tactile-specific data augmentations like flips, rotation, and temporal reversal.

## Results

- The best ConvRNN-based EAD models saturated explainable neural variance in rodent somatosensory cortex, surpassing the average inter-animal neural consistency.
- Convolutional Recurrent Neural Networks consistently outperformed purely feedforward and State Space Model encoders in supervised tactile categorization tasks.
- Contrastive self-supervised learning (SimCLR) with tactile-specific augmentations achieved neural alignment scores comparable to or better than the top supervised models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Nonlinear recurrent processing, particularly with ConvRNNs, is essential for generating general-purpose tactile representations that align with rodent somatosensory cortex activity.
> - Task optimization refines architectural inductive biases, allowing computational models to explain a substantial portion of neural variability in tactile processing.
> - Self-supervised learning, when combined with modality-specific augmentations, provides an ethologically relevant and effective approach to developing biologically plausible tactile models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the rapidly evolving field of "goal-driven modeling" in neuroscience, where task-optimized artificial neural networks are used to generate quantitative predictions for neural activity. This paradigm has seen significant success in explaining neural responses in more mature sensory modalities like primate vision (Yamins et al., 2014; Khaligh-Razavi and Kriegeskorte, 2014) and, more recently, audition (Kell* et al., 2018), motor control (Sussillo et al.), memory (Nayebi et al., 2021), and language (Schrimpf et al., 2021). The approach has also been extended to mouse visual cortex (Nayebi* et al., 2023; Bakhtiari et al., 2021), demonstrating its versatility across species.

Despite these advances, tactile sensing has remained a relatively underexplored area in goal-driven computational modeling. Existing work, such as Zhuang et al. (2017), provided foundational models for the rodent whisker-trigeminal system but was limited to simpler architectures and supervised learning without direct comparison to brain data. This paper directly addresses this gap by:
1. **Systematically broadening architectural exploration:** Moving beyond simple recurrent architectures to include more sophisticated Convolutional Recurrent Neural Networks (ConvRNNs), State Space Models (SSMs like S4 and Mamba), and Transformers within a novel Encoder-Attender-Decoder (EAD) framework.
2. **Incorporating diverse learning objectives:** Beyond supervised categorization, the authors explore self-supervised learning (SSL) methods, particularly contrastive SSL (SimCLR, SimSiam), adapted for tactile data, which has proven effective in other modalities for learning robust, ethologically relevant representations.
3. **Providing the first direct neural comparison:** Crucially, this work is the first to directly compare the internal representations of a wide range of these task-optimized models to neural population responses in rodent somatosensory cortex, a critical step towards biological validation.

Concurrently, the paper also addresses challenges in embodied AI and robotics. Current artificial tactile sensors and systems lag significantly behind biological capabilities, primarily due to hardware limitations (e.g., complexity in scaling, difficulty in discriminating multiple stimuli, mechanical discrepancies from biological sensors) and a lack of robust algorithms for processing realistic tactile inputs. By developing models trained on biomechanically realistic tactile data and demonstrating their alignment with biological processing, this research aims to inform the design of future robotic tactile systems, enabling them to achieve more animal-like perceptual prowess in unstructured environments. It bridges the divide between computational neuroscience and robotic perception by quantitatively characterizing the inductive biases necessary for artificial systems to process tactile information in a brain-like manner.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objectives of this research are twofold: to advance the scientific understanding of tactile processing in the brain and to improve tactile perception capabilities in artificial systems.

**From a Neuroscience Perspective:**
- **Bridge the knowledge gap:** Tactile sensing is far less understood in neuroscience compared to vision and language. The authors aim to shed light on the neural computations underlying precise tactile perception, which are currently poorly characterized by computational models.
- **Characterize inductive biases:** A primary motivation is to quantitatively characterize the underlying inductive biases—the inherent assumptions about the structure of sensory input and the computations required—that enable the rodent somatosensory cortex to process tactile information effectively.
- **Identify brain-like architectures:** The paper seeks to identify which types of temporal neural network architectures, when optimized for tactile tasks, best align with and explain neural activity in the rodent somatosensory cortex, thereby providing a mechanistic understanding of biological processing.
- **Explore ethologically relevant learning:** Investigate if self-supervised learning, particularly with label-free and tactility-specific augmentations, can serve as an ethologically more relevant proxy for shaping brain-like tactile representations compared to arbitrary supervised categorization tasks.

**From an Embodied AI and Robotics Perspective:**
- **Address hardware and algorithmic limitations:** Current robotic tactile sensors and algorithms struggle to match the performance of biological systems. The motivation is to develop robust algorithms that can handle realistic, high-dimensional tactile input sequences.
- **Inform robot design:** Identify effective architectural designs and training methodologies for artificial tactile perception systems that can robustly process touch information, similar to how animals use sensors like whiskers for navigation, foraging, and object identification in complex environments.
- **Leverage biological inspiration:** Translate insights from the rodent tactile system (e.g., hierarchical processing, active sensing, recurrence) into computational models to enhance the functionality and robustness of artificial tactile systems.
- **Achieve robust perception in unstructured environments:** Ultimately, the goal is to contribute towards robots that can interact with the world through touch as effectively and adaptively as animals do.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the desire to leverage the success of goal-driven modeling in other domains to systematically explore and validate computational models of tactile processing, thereby informing both our understanding of the brain and the development of next-generation tactile AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a systematic computational modeling approach combined with neural data analysis, built around a novel Encoder-Attender-Decoder (EAD) framework.

#### 3.1. High-Variation Tactile Dataset Generation

- **Simulator:** The authors utilized a customized biomechanical rodent whisker-array simulator, WHISKiT Physics (Zweifel et al., 2021), the first 3D simulation of the rat whisker system, adapted for a mouse whisker array (30 whiskers arranged 5x7). This simulator provides biomechanically realistic force and torque inputs, mirroring what mice receive.
- **Objects:** The models were trained on synthetic data derived from interactions with 9981 different 3D objects from the ShapeNet dataset (Chang et al., 2015), providing a high-variation dataset to strongly constrain learned representations.
- **Whisking Simulation:** Objects were "passively brushed" against the whisker array. To mimic active sensing, various "sweep augmentations" were applied, including adjustments to object sweeping speed, height, rotation, and distance relative to the whisker array (Fig. 1).
- **Datasets:** Two datasets were generated: a "High-Variation/Low-Fidelity" set with 288 sweep augmentations and 110 Hz simulation, and a "Low-Variation/High-Fidelity" set with 16 augmentations and 1000 Hz simulation. Both extracted 22 timesteps, corresponding to average rodent whisking frequency (20 Hz).

#### 3.2. Encoder-Attender-Decoder (EAD) Temporal Model Search Parameterization

- **Framework:** The core of the model exploration is the EAD architecture (Fig. 2a), designed to systematically explore hierarchical, temporal neural networks.
 - **Encoder (E):** Processes initial, temporally smooth tactile signals. Candidates include:
 - **ConvRNNs:** Specifically, variants of Zhuang's recurrent model (Zhuang et al., 2017) with different recurrent units: UGRNN, IntersectionRNN (Collins et al., 2017), LSTM (Hochreiter and Schmidhuber, 1997), and GRU (Cho et al., 2014). ConvRNNs are favored for their ability to integrate information locally over time, reducing redundancy and noise.
 - **ResNet18 (He et al., 2016):** A purely feedforward convolutional network, serving as a control.
 - **State Space Models (SSMs):** S4 (Gu et al., 2021), designed for efficiently modeling long sequences.
 - **Attender (A):** Performs higher-level temporal aggregation, selectively integrating signals across longer, potentially irregular timescales. Candidates include:
 - **Transformer (Haldar et al., 2024; GPT-like):** Attention-based architectures that dynamically weigh distinct timesteps.
 - **Mamba (Gu and Dao, 2023):** Another state-space model with selective attention mechanisms.
 - **None:** An attender-free control.
 - **Decoder (D):** The final output layer. For supervised learning, it's a classification layer; for self-supervised learning, it produces features for contrastive learning or reconstruction for autoencoding.

#### 3.3. Model Training and Tactile Augmentations

- **Training Objectives:**
 - **Supervised Categorization:** Trained to classify the 117 ShapeNet object categories.
 - **Self-Supervised Learning (SSL):** Explored SimCLR (Chen et al., 2020) and SimSiam (Chen and He, 2021) for learning robust representations by comparing augmented views of data, and autoencoding (AE) (Olshausen and Field, 1996) for reconstructing input.
- **Tactile Augmentations:** Crucially, custom tactile-specific augmentations were developed (Fig. 2b): vertical flips, horizontal flips, rotation, and temporal reversal of the force/torque input sequences. These mimic physical array manipulations. Traditional image augmentations (Gaussian blur, color jitter, grayscale) were tested but found to cause training failure.
- **Training Details:** Models were trained for 100 epochs, with specific optimizers, schedulers, and learning rates detailed in the appendix. SSL models involved a pre-training stage followed by linear probing.

#### 3.4. Neural Data Comparison

- **Neural Dataset:** Utilized a neural dataset from Rodgers (2022), recording activity from 999 neural units across 11 mice in barrel cortex (somatosensory cortex S1) as they performed a 2-object (convex/concave) classification task.
- **In-Silico Replication:** The experimental setup was replicated in simulation, generating tactile inputs for 6 specific stimuli (concave/convex objects at near/medium/far distances) using a recorded whisking trajectory (Fig. 4a).
- **Evaluation Metric: Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA):** A parameter-free approach (Kriegeskorte et al., 2008) compared the pairwise dissimilarity structure of model activations and neural population responses to the 6 common stimuli.
- **Reliability and Correction:** Only self-consistent neurons (Spearman-Brown–corrected reliability > 0.5) were included. RSA scores were corrected by neural internal consistency.
- **Inter-Animal Consistency Baseline ("a2a"):** The mean RSA score between different mice was calculated and served as a baseline, representing the inherent biological variability and providing a target for model performance (NeuroAI Turing Test, Feather* et al., 2025). Models aimed to match or exceed this score.
- **Neural Alignment Score:** The maximum, across all layers, of the median RSA score between each layer’s representation and the neural responses, averaged across mice, was reported.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

1. **Dataset Validation:** Initial SVM decoding on the simulated whisker array datasets confirmed that both the high-variation and low-variation datasets yielded discriminable object categories, exceeding chance levels and supporting their utility for training.

2. **ConvRNN Encoders Excel in Tactile Categorization:** Across the EAD architectural space, Convolutional Recurrent Neural Networks (ConvRNNs), particularly the IntersectionRNN variant, consistently demonstrated superior performance in supervised tactile categorization tasks compared to purely feedforward ResNet18 and State Space Model (S4) encoders (Fig. 3). This highlights the importance of recurrent processing for robust tactile recognition.

3. **Architectural Inductive Bias is Crucial:** Even randomly initialized networks showed a moderately positive correlation with mouse neural data (Fig. 4b), indicating that the inherent structure (inductive bias) of the chosen architectures contributes significantly to alignment with brain processing. However, training substantially improved these fits, suggesting that task optimization refines these biases towards biological realism.

4. **ConvRNN Encoders Saturate Explainable Neural Variance:**
 - **Superior Neural Alignment:** All trained EAD models significantly outperformed raw sensor inputs and untrained networks in aligning with neural recordings from rodent somatosensory cortex (Fig. 4b). This underscores the necessity of nonlinear temporal processing to capture brain-like tactile representations.
 - **Biological Plausibility:** The best ConvRNN-based EAD models saturated all of the explainable neural variance in the rodent somatosensory cortex, even when tested on novel objects under different experimental conditions (active whisking vs. passive contact during training).
 - **Exceeding Inter-Animal Consistency:** Remarkably, the neural predictivity of the best models surpassed the average inter-animal neural consistency (the "a2a" baseline in Fig. 4b), effectively passing the NeuroAI Turing Test for this dataset.
 - **Performance-Alignment Link:** A strong linear relationship (r = 0.59) was observed between supervised tactile categorization performance and neural fit for supervised models (Fig. 5b), suggesting that optimizing for a complex, diverse object classification task drives the development of brain-like representations. The layers closest to the decoder showed the best neural prediction (Fig. A2).

5. **Modest Benefits from GPT-based Attenders:** While the choice of encoder was paramount, GPT-based Attenders provided modest but consistent improvements in both supervised task categorization accuracy and neural alignment compared to Mamba-based or attender-free attenders (Fig. 5b). This suggests a potential role for attention-like mechanisms at higher levels of tactile processing.

6. **Self-Supervised Learning as an Ethologically-Relevant Proxy:**
 - **Comparable Neural Fits:** Contrastive self-supervised learning (SimCLR), when optimized with tactile-specific augmentations, achieved neural alignment scores comparable to (and sometimes marginally better than) the best supervised models (Fig. 5a). The SimCLR-optimized IntersectionRNN EAD achieved the overall best absolute neural alignment score.
 - **Importance of Tactile Augmentations:** The custom tactile-specific augmentations (flips, rotation, temporal reversal) were critical; traditional image-based augmentations (Gaussian blur, color jitter) caused models to fail to train, underscoring the modality-specific nature of effective data augmentation (Fig. 5c).
 - **Task Performance vs. Neural Fit:** Interestingly, SSL models, while achieving excellent neural alignment, showed only moderate linear probe categorization performance (Fig. 5a), suggesting that somatosensory cortex might prioritize broader, task-agnostic sensory encoding over purely categorization-driven features.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research delivers significant implications for both fundamental neuroscience and the practical development of embodied AI and robotics.

**Implications for Somatosensory Cortical Processing (Neuroscience):**
- **Nonlinear Recurrence is Key:** The work provides compelling quantitative evidence that nonlinear recurrent processing, specifically embodied in ConvRNN architectures like IntersectionRNNs, is essential for generating general-purpose tactile representations in the rodent somatosensory cortex. This is a foundational characterization of the inductive biases underlying this sensory system.
- **Task-Driven Models Explain Brain Function:** By demonstrating that task-optimized models can saturate explainable neural variability and surpass inter-animal consistency, the study validates the goal-driven modeling paradigm for tactile processing, providing a powerful tool for understanding brain computations.
- **Supervised Learning as a Proxy:** The finding that contrastive self-supervised learning (SSL) yields neural alignments comparable to supervised methods suggests that supervised object categorization, while not directly ethologically relevant, serves as a computationally effective proxy for learning biologically plausible representations. SSL, with its label-free nature and tactile-specific augmentations, offers a more ethologically relevant pathway for future neuroscience modeling.
- **Predicting Attention Mechanisms:** The modest but consistent benefits of GPT-based Attenders generate a testable prediction for the existence of attention-like mechanisms within hierarchical tactile processing pathways in the brain, opening new avenues for experimental validation through targeted perturbations.

**Implications for Embodied AI and Robotics:**
- **Design Principles for Tactile AI:** The research offers crucial design principles for developing more capable artificial tactile systems. It underscores the importance of:
 1. **Biomechanically Realistic Inputs:** Training on high-fidelity, physically realistic force and torque sequences is vital.
 2. **Temporally Recurrent Architectures:** ConvRNNs are highlighted as superior for processing sequential tactile data.
 3. **Tailored Self-Supervised Learning:** Modality-specific SSL augmentations are critical for learning robust and biologically inspired tactile representations, especially useful for systems where labeled data is scarce.
- **Overcoming Sensor Limitations:** By informing the algorithmic side, this work can help robotic systems overcome current hardware limitations related to scaling complexity, stimulus discrimination, and limited sensing surfaces, ultimately leading to more robust and nuanced environmental interactions. Future robots can be equipped with perception systems that interpret touch in a manner more akin to biological organisms, enhancing capabilities in manipulation, navigation, and object identification in complex, unstructured environments.
- **Towards Brain-Inspired Robots:** This research is a significant step towards creating robots that perceive and understand touch with similar prowess to animals, bridging the gap between current robotic capabilities and biological tactile intelligence.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations, primarily the **limited stimulus diversity in current neural datasets**. The rodent dataset used only 6 distinct object conditions, which restricts the captured neural variability and might explain the relatively low inter-animal consistency scores. Future work will require expanding neural datasets to include a much broader range of tactile stimuli and more animals to fully explore and potentially surpass the theoretical ceiling of neural predictivity.

In the longer term, **multimodal sensory integration** is highlighted as a critical future direction. Combining tactile inputs with other modalities like proprioception or vision, using biologically-inspired fusion operations that integrate signals across intermediate layers, is expected to yield stronger constraints on models and enable more robust decision-making in genuinely unstructured scenarios.

**Broader Impacts:**
On the positive side, advancements in tactile-enabled robotics, guided by biologically validated models, could profoundly enhance human safety and efficiency in various sectors such as healthcare (e.g., surgical robots, assistive devices), manufacturing (e.g., delicate assembly, quality control), and assistive technologies. However, the authors also acknowledge potential negative impacts, such as **job displacement** in sectors heavily reliant on manual labor, emphasizing the need for proactive policy-making and workforce training to manage these technological transitions thoughtfully.
