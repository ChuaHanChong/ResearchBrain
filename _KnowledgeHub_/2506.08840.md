# MoRE: Mixture of Residual Experts for Humanoid Lifelike Gaits Learning on Complex Terrains

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from CUHK, Huawei Noah's Ark Lab, and other institutions developed MoRE, a framework that enables humanoid robots to learn robust locomotion on complex terrains while simultaneously performing multiple controllable, human-like gaits. This system achieves excellent sim-to-real transfer and allows a Unitree G1 robot to traverse diverse real-world obstacles with varied, anthropomorphic movements.

## Problem

- Existing methods for human-like gaits typically operate only on flat terrains and rely on proprioception, limiting their use on complex, dynamic environments.
- Robots capable of traversing complex terrains with exteroceptive sensors often exhibit robotic, non-anthropomorphic movements lacking diversity.
- There is a challenge in seamlessly integrating robust complex terrain locomotion with natural, diverse human-like gaits within a single policy.

## Method

- A two-stage training pipeline first learns a robust base locomotion policy for complex terrains, then enhances it for anthropomorphic gaits.
- A Mixture of latent ==Residual Experts== (**MoRE**) module is integrated, which uses a multi-discriminator adversarial learning framework to acquire multiple distinct human gaits.
- Gait-specific rewards and extensive domain randomization, including simulated robot body occlusions, are used to achieve fine-grained control and enhance sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- MoRE significantly outperformed baseline methods in simulation, with 'High-Knees Gait' achieving a **0.947** success rate on Hard Step terrain and 'Walk-Run Gait' reaching **0.933** on Hard Gap terrain.
- The framework enabled direct deployment onto a Unitree G1 humanoid robot without fine-tuning, successfully traversing real-world obstacles like a 0.4m-wide gap, a 0.3m-high step, and a three-step staircase.
- Latent space analysis showed distinct clusters for each commanded gait, indicating successful capture of high-level motion characteristics, and specialized gait rewards allowed precise customization of motion attributes (e.g., target squat heights and knee lift heights were accurately achieved).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning residual policies in the latent space, rather than directly in the action space, provides a more stable and effective mechanism for modulating complex behaviors.
> - A modular Mixture of Experts design within the residual module effectively mitigates gradient conflicts when learning multiple distinct gaits, with optimal performance often achieved when experts specialize in individual or similar gait patterns.
> - The integration of exteroceptive sensing (depth camera) with human motion priors allows for proactive adaptation to terrain changes, leading to more anticipatory and efficient locomotion compared to reactive methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robust and versatile locomotion capabilities for humanoid robots is a central challenge in robotics, crucial for their deployment in human-centric environments. This paper sits at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields: humanoid robotics, reinforcement learning (RL), and motion intelligence.

**Historical Context and Current Trends:**
- **Early RL for Legged Robots:** Reinforcement Learning has revolutionized legged locomotion, enabling robots to achieve unprecedented robustness and agility on challenging terrains using primarily proprioceptive sensors (e.g., joint angles, velocities, robot orientation). Works like those cited in, have demonstrated impressive locomotion on uneven surfaces.
- **Exteroceptive Sensing:** The integration of exteroceptive sensors, such as LiDAR or RGB-D cameras, has further expanded robot capabilities, allowing them to perceive and adapt to real-time terrain variations (e.g.,). This enables more complex tasks like navigating stairs, gaps, or sparse footholds.
- **Anthropomorphic Behavior Learning:** A parallel line of research focuses on making robot motions more "human-like" or anthropomorphic. This is often achieved through motion imitation, where robots learn to track human motion data from Motion-Capture (MoCap) systems or datasets like AMASS. Approaches include direct stepwise motion tracking, or Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP), which captures the overall style of motion trajectories. AMP-based methods are particularly effective at generating natural-looking gaits.

**The Research Gap:**
Despite these advancements, a significant gap persists. Existing motion-tracking or AMP-based methods for human-like gaits typically operate on flat terrains and rely almost exclusively on proprioception. This limits their ability to traverse complex and dynamic environments while maintaining diverse, natural gaits. Conversely, robots that excel at complex terrain traversal with exteroceptive sensors often exhibit robotic, non-anthropomorphic movements, lacking diversity in their gait patterns. The challenge lies in seamlessly integrating the robustness of complex terrain locomotion with the naturalness and diversity of human-like gaits.

**MoRE's Contribution to the Landscape:**
"MoRE" directly addresses this critical gap. It aims to develop a unified framework that enables humanoid robots to:
1. **Traverse complex terrains robustly** using exteroceptive sensors (depth camera).
2. **Exhibit multiple, controllable, lifelike human gaits.**
3. **Seamlessly transition between these gaits** based on high-level commands.

By proposing a two-stage training pipeline, latent residual experts, multi-discriminator adversarial learning, and specific gait rewards, MoRE synthesizes and extends state-of-the-art techniques. It moves beyond single-gait imitation or flat-terrain humanoids, pushing towards a more adaptable and intelligent robotic agent capable of nuanced, human-like interaction with diverse environments. The use of a Mixture of Experts (MoE) architecture within the residual module also aligns with current trends in multi-skill learning and mitigating gradient conflicts in multi-task RL, showcasing a sophisticated approach to managing diverse behaviors within a single policy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable humanoid robots to achieve **robust and versatile locomotion across complex terrains with controllable, lifelike human-like gaits**, leveraging both proprioceptive and exteroceptive sensory information. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and aims to overcome specific limitations in existing humanoid locomotion research:

1. **Bridging the Gap between Robustness and Anthropomorphism:** A significant motivation is to reconcile the two major achievements in humanoid locomotion: the ability to traverse challenging terrains (often achieved by reactive, proprioception-based policies or basic exteroceptive policies) and the ability to produce human-like, natural movements (typically limited to flat ground or simulated environments). The paper seeks a solution that simultaneously provides both.

2. **Enabling Terrain-Aware, Human-like Gaits:** Current methods for human-like gaits (motion tracking, AMP) primarily learn from motion-capture data, which is usually collected on flat surfaces. This makes them unsuitable for dynamic adaptation to varied and complex terrains like stairs, gaps, or highly uneven ground. The authors aim to overcome this by incorporating exteroceptive sensing (depth camera) directly into a gait-learning framework.

3. **Learning Multiple Controllable Gaits:** Most prior work focuses on learning a single robust gait or a few predefined motions. This paper explicitly targets the challenge of learning *multiple distinct* anthropomorphic gaits (e.g., walk, run, crouch-walking, high-knees) within a single policy network, allowing for dynamic switching based on high-level commands. This diversity is crucial for adaptability in varied real-world scenarios.

4. **Improving Adaptability and Generalization:** The desire for a policy that can smoothly transition between different gaits and adapt these gaits to environmental demands (e.g., high-knees for stairs, running for gaps) motivates the novel architecture choices like the Mixture of latent Residual Experts (MoRE) and gait-specific rewards. This aims to enhance the robot's ability to generalize to unseen complex terrains and respond flexibly to different locomotion requirements.

5. **Reducing Training Complexity:** Directly training a policy to master both complex terrain traversal and multiple human-like gaits from scratch is immensely challenging. The two-stage training pipeline is motivated by the need to decompose this complex problem into more manageable sub-problems, first acquiring fundamental locomotion skills and then specializing them with human-like gaits.

In essence, the research is motivated by the vision of a humanoid robot that not only moves effectively in complex, unstructured environments but does so with the grace, efficiency, and versatility characteristic of human motion, making it more intuitive, safer, and acceptable for human interaction and deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of MoRE (Mixture of latent Residual Experts) is a novel **two-stage training pipeline** combined with a sophisticated neural network architecture designed for robust, multi-gait locomotion on complex terrains.

**A. Two-Stage Training Pipeline:**

1. **Stage 1: Base Locomotion Policy Learning:**
 - **Objective:** The initial stage focuses on training a robust base locomotion policy from scratch, primarily to enable the humanoid robot to traverse complex terrains using a depth camera. At this stage, no motion priors or gait commands are introduced.
 - **Architecture:** An asymmetric actor-critic network is employed.
 - **Actor Input:** Non-privileged observations ($o_{np_t}$), including proprioceptive information (robot angular velocity, projected gravity vector, velocity command, last action, joint angles, joint velocities) and exteroceptive data (a sequence of two depth images from a head-mounted camera).
 - **Exteroceptive Processing:** A 2D-CNN-based depth encoder processes depth images, and a 1D-CNN-based history encoder processes historical proprioception. These features, along with current proprioception, are concatenated into an actor feature ($f_t$).
 - **Critic Input:** Privileged observations ($o_{p_t}$), which include an elevation map, feet positions, ground truth velocities, and randomized physical parameters (available only in simulation for better value estimation).
 - **Rewards:** Only general locomotion rewards ($r_l$) are used, incentivizing tasks like tracking linear and angular velocities, penalizing joint acceleration/velocity, action rates, angular velocity, joint power, feet stumble, arm deviations, joint position/velocity/torque limits, feet lateral distance, feet slippage, feet force, collisions, and being stuck.
 - **Outcome:** A policy capable of basic but robust locomotion over diverse terrains such as stairs, gaps, and rough surfaces.

2. **Stage 2: Anthropomorphic Gait Acquisition with MoRE:**
 - **Objective:** In this stage, the pre-trained base locomotion policy from Stage 1 is enhanced to learn multiple anthropomorphic gaits and seamless transitions between them, while preserving its robust terrain traversal capabilities.
 - **Residual Module Integration:** A novel residual module, MoRE, is attached to the pretrained base locomotion policy. Instead of outputting residual *actions*, MoRE outputs a *latent residual* ($z'_t$). This latent residual is added to the output of the base policy's hidden layers ($z_{o_t}$), forming a combined latent feature ($z_t$) that then feeds into the base policy's actor head to predict actions.
 - **Rationale for Latent Residuals:** This approach leverages the base policy's learned mapping from latent space to action space. The latent residual is less disruptive than an action residual, allows for higher-density information transfer, and avoids the need for the residual module to learn the complex action mapping itself.
 - **Gait Command Integration:** A one-hot encoded gait command ($c_{g_t}$) is provided as an additional input to the residual module, explicitly enabling control over gait selection.
 - **Multi-Discriminator Framework for AMP:**
 - **Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP):** To instill human-like motion styles, an AMP approach is used. Unlike previous methods, MoRE employs *multiple discriminators*. Each discriminator ($D_{\phi_i}$) is specialized to a *specific* reference motion (gait) from a dataset ($M_i$), learning to distinguish between real human motion trajectories and fake robot trajectories ($G$).
 - **Trajectory Definition:** A five-step joint trajectory ($\tau$) is used for more precise discrimination compared to single state transitions.
 - **Discriminator Objective:** A least-squares GAN formulation with a gradient penalty term is used to train each discriminator.
 - **Style Reward ($r_s$):** The policy receives a style reward only from the discriminator corresponding to the currently commanded gait, effectively guiding the policy to mimic the desired motion style.
 - **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Architecture for Residual Module:**
 - **Motivation:** To efficiently learn multiple distinct gaits and mitigate gradient conflicts inherent in multi-task learning, the residual module is structured as an MoE.
 - **Components:** It consists of $N$ "experts" (MLPs) and a "gating network" (MLP).
 - **Operation:** The gating network takes the actor feature and gait command as input and predicts weights. The final latent residual ($z'$) is a weighted sum of the outputs from all experts, where weights are determined by the gating network. This allows different experts to specialize in different gait patterns or aspects of behavior.
 - **Gait Rewards ($r_g$):** Hand-crafted, gait-specific rewards are introduced to provide finer-grained control over specific motion characteristics beyond what reference motions might implicitly provide (e.g., target squat height for crouch-walking, knee lift height for high-knees gait). These rewards are only active when their corresponding gait is commanded.

**B. Training Details:**

- **Simulation Environment:** NVIDIA Isaac Gym for high-performance, GPU-accelerated physics simulation. NVIDIA Warp is used for depth image rendering to accelerate training.
- **Computational Resources:** One NVIDIA RTX 4090 for Stage 1 (10,000 iterations), four NVIDIA RTX 4090s for Stage 2 (20,000 iterations).
- **Policy Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
- **Reference Motion Data:** LAFAN1 dataset, retargeted to the Unitree G1 robot.
- **Terrain Curriculum:** An auto-curriculum mechanism progressively increases terrain difficulty (gaps, stairs, steps, roughness) based on policy performance, enabling gradual skill acquisition.
- **Domain Randomization:** Extensive randomization of physics parameters (friction, mass, CoM, motor strength/noise, PD gains, joint positions, restitution, action delay, link masses) and depth camera parameters (Gaussian noise, deviation noise, Gaussian filter, delay, position, pitch, FOV) to enhance sim-to-real transfer. Crucially, simulated robot body occlusions are introduced to mimic real-world camera limitations.
- **Action Space:** 16-dimensional action controlling shoulder, elbow, and all leg joints.
- **Real-World Deployment:** Policies are directly transferred to a Unitree G1 humanoid robot equipped with an Intel RealSense D435i depth camera. Raw depth images are filtered and downsampled before being fed to the policy. The policy runs at 50 Hz, with the camera at 10 Hz. PD controllers convert target joint positions to torques.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling simulation and real-world experimental results that validate the effectiveness and robustness of the MoRE framework.

**A. Simulation Experiments:**

1. **Locomotion Performance Comparison (Table III):**
 - MoRE consistently and significantly **outperforms baseline methods** ("Blind Locomotion" and "Base Locomotion") across various complex terrains (gaps, stairs, steps) and difficulty levels (Easy, Hard).
 - "Blind Locomotion" (no visual input) showed the poorest performance, underscoring the **critical role of exteroceptive perception** for navigating complex terrains.
 - "Base Locomotion" (Stage 1 policy) performs well but is surpassed by MoRE, demonstrating the benefits of integrating human motion priors and multi-gait capabilities.
 - MoRE's different gaits exhibit **specialized efficacy** for certain terrains: "High-Knees Gait" is highly effective on stairs and tall steps (e.g., 0.947 success rate on Hard Step terrain), while "Walk-Run Gait" is well-suited for traversing wide gaps (e.g., 0.933 success rate on Hard Gap terrain). This highlights the framework's ability to learn and leverage diverse strategies.

2. **Policy Component Ablations (Figure 3):**
 - **Expert Number:** The ablation study on the number of experts confirmed that **three experts (MoRE)** performed optimally for three target gaits (walk&run, high-knees, squat), achieving the highest mean reward during training. Fewer experts (MoRE2) led to experts handling multiple disparate gaits, while more experts (MoRE4) resulted in overfitting to minor variations and reduced generalization, indicating that a modular design mapping one expert to one primary gait (or similar gaits) is most effective.
 - **Residual Fusion Dimension:** Crucially, the latent-space fusion of the residual (MoRE) was essential for stable training and convergence. An attempt to apply residuals directly in the action space (MoRE-A) resulted in **unstable training and complete failure to converge**, validating the design choice of latent residual experts.
 - **Policy Initialization Strategy:** Directly training the residual policy from scratch in a one-stage approach (MoRE-OS) led to **complete training failure**, confirming the vital role of the two-stage training pipeline and the pre-trained base locomotion policy in providing fundamental robust locomotion priors.

3. **Residual Latent Analysis (Figure 4):**
 - t-SNE visualization of the residual latent space revealed **clear and distinct clusters for each gait pattern**, even when samples were collected from different terrains. This indicates that the latent space successfully captures high-level, semantically meaningful motion characteristics rather than being confounded by terrain variations.
 - It was observed that even within a single expert, similar motion modes (e.g., walk and run combined) could form distinct sub-clusters.
 - Intriguingly, during challenging terrain traversal (e.g., stair climbing while commanded to walk), the latent outputs might cluster closer to the "run" gait region, suggesting that the latent space dynamically adapts to locomotion demands, reflecting an implicit shift towards more vigorous motions when needed, rather than strictly adhering to the reference gait label.

4. **Gait Reward Modulation (Table IV):**
 - Experiments demonstrated that the specialized gait rewards effectively allow for **fine-grained, interpretable control over specific motion attributes** beyond what is strictly dictated by the reference motion.
 - For instance, target squat heights (e.g., 0.600m or 0.550m) and knee lift heights (e.g., 0.580m or 0.480m) were successfully achieved, with the robot's actual mean values closely matching the targets, showcasing the ability to customize gait behaviors precisely.

**B. Real-World Experiments:**

- The trained MoRE policies were **directly deployed onto a Unitree G1 humanoid robot without any fine-tuning**, demonstrating excellent sim-to-real transfer capabilities.
- The robot successfully traversed **diverse and challenging real-world terrains**, including a 0.4m-wide gap, a 0.3m-high step, a three-step staircase (0.15m per step), and a complex composite terrain combining these obstacles.
- It exhibited **robust and stable behavior**, maintaining posture and accurate foot placement even when subjected to intentional external disturbances (e.g., pushes).
- The policy enabled **smooth and seamless transitions** between different commanded gait patterns (walk-run, high-knees, squat) during traversal.
- Crucially, the **visual sensing enabled proactive adaptation** to upcoming terrain changes, contrasting with reactive, proprioception-only methods, leading to more anticipatory and efficient locomotion.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that MoRE achieves its key objectives, delivering a humanoid robot controller capable of robustly navigating complex environments with diverse, controllable, and human-like gaits, successfully bridging a critical gap in the field.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MoRE framework represents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robot locomotion, addressing a long-standing challenge of combining robust terrain traversal with diverse, anthropomorphic gait generation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling True Humanoid Agility and Versatility:** By successfully integrating exteroceptive sensing for complex terrain navigation with multiple controllable human-like gaits, MoRE moves humanoid robots closer to achieving the versatility and agility needed for deployment in unstructured human environments. This is a crucial step towards general-purpose humanoid robots that can fluidly adapt their movement style to environmental demands and task requirements.

2. **Novel Methodological Synthesis:** The paper introduces a highly effective methodological synthesis:
 - **Two-Stage Training:** Decomposing the complex learning problem into foundational locomotion and subsequent gait specialization is an elegant solution for improving training efficiency and stability.
 - **Latent Residual Experts:** The concept of residual learning in the latent space, rather than directly in the action space, provides a more stable and information-rich modulation mechanism for policy adaptation, which could have implications for other multi-skill learning tasks.
 - **Multi-Discriminator AMP with MoE:** The use of multiple discriminators for Adversarial Motion Prior, combined with a Mixture of Experts architecture, is a sophisticated approach to learning and arbitrating between multiple distinct motion styles within a single, unified policy, mitigating gradient conflicts often seen in multi-task RL.
 - **Gait-Specific Rewards:** The incorporation of explicit, hand-crafted gait rewards offers a powerful tool for fine-tuning specific motion characteristics, allowing researchers to customize behaviors beyond the constraints of reference motion data.

3. **Enhanced Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The robust performance in real-world experiments without additional fine-tuning highlights the effectiveness of the extensive domain randomization strategies employed, particularly for the depth camera and robot body occlusions. This enhances the practicality and deployability of learned policies.

4. **Proactive Environmental Interaction:** The demonstration that visual sensing enables *proactive* adaptation to terrain changes is critical. Unlike reactive methods that respond *after* an interaction (e.g., a foot slip), MoRE allows the robot to anticipate and prepare for obstacles, leading to smoother, safer, and more efficient locomotion.

5. **Broadening Application Scope:** Robots capable of both complex terrain traversal and diverse, human-like gaits have a wider range of potential applications. These include:
 - **Assistance Robotics:** Operating naturally and safely alongside humans in homes, hospitals, or workplaces.
 - **Logistics and Industrial Tasks:** Navigating varied factory floors or construction sites.
 - **Disaster Response and Exploration:** Traversing rubble or uneven natural terrains while maintaining stable, efficient movement.
 - **Entertainment and Education:** Creating more engaging and believable robotic figures.

Looking forward, the framework sets a strong foundation for future research. The authors suggest extending the framework to learn even more challenging and lifelike gaits and achieving even smoother gait transitions. This work opens avenues for exploring more sophisticated environmental interactions, integrating higher-level reasoning, and pushing humanoid robots closer to truly autonomous, versatile agents capable of navigating and interacting with the world in a human-like manner.
