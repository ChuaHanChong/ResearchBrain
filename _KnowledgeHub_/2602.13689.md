# Symmetry-Aware Fusion of Vision and Tactile Sensing via Bilateral Force Priors for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from DexAI, Analog Devices Inc., developed a visuo-tactile fusion framework that integrates a Cross-Modal Transformer with a bilateral force symmetry regularization for robotic insertion tasks. The framework improved insertion success rates to 96.59% by enabling more stable and precise manipulation, demonstrating the value of physics-informed inductive biases and outperforming vision-only and naive fusion methods.

## Problem

- Vision-only robotic systems struggle with contact-rich tasks like insertion due to occlusion, lighting variations, and inability to perceive subtle physical interactions (e.g., micro-slippage, compliance).
- Effectively fusing heterogeneous vision and tactile data streams is challenging, as simplistic approaches often dilute modality-specific cues and yield minimal or negative performance gains.
- Existing multimodal fusion methods often lack a structured integration of physical principles to enhance stability and precision directly within policy learning for contact-rich manipulation.

## Method

- A ==Cross-Modal Transformer (CMT)== architecture was developed, employing ==hierarchical self-attention== for intra-tactile consistency and ==cross-attention== to integrate global visual context with local tactile feedback.
- A physics-informed ==bilateral force symmetry regularization== loss was introduced, encouraging stable and balanced forces in tactile embeddings, inspired by human motor control principles to reduce lateral misalignments.
- Tactile inputs are processed as ==residual forces== (deviations from pre-insertion reference forces) to allow the policy to learn corrective actions based on subtle contact state changes.

## Results

- The CMT with symmetry regularization achieved the highest insertion success rate of **96.59%**, surpassing vision-only (**93.23%**), tactile-only (**91.41%**), naive fusion (**92.97%**), and gated fusion (**94.53%**) baselines.
- Qualitative analysis showed the symmetry-aware CMT maintained balanced force distributions, leading to smoother, straighter insertion trajectories and a **2.83%** reduction in average steps to succeed (**108.48 steps** vs. **111.63** for naive fusion).
- The proposed framework operates within real-time constraints (**153.37 fps** throughput) with a marginal **20%** increase in latency compared to naive fusion, a trade-off justified by its substantial performance improvements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured visuo-tactile fusion through a Cross-Modal Transformer effectively combines complementary visual and tactile information, significantly outperforming simpler fusion strategies.
> - Embedding physics-informed inductive biases, such as bilateral force symmetry, into tactile representations enhances policy stability and precision, mitigating common issues like jamming during insertion.
> - High-resolution tactile sensing provides critical information for precise alignment and contact-rich manipulation, demonstrating strong performance (e.g., 100% success in screw tasks) even as a primary modality when contact geometry and force details are paramount.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation, particularly in contact-rich tasks such as insertion, presents significant challenges for autonomous systems. Historically, vision-based policies have been prominent due to the advancements in deep learning for visual perception. These methods excel at global scene understanding and object localization. However, vision-only approaches often encounter limitations in insertion tasks, including sensitivity to occlusion, lighting variations, incomplete geometry, and the inability to discern subtle physical interactions such such as micro-slippage, compliance, or precise misalignments during contact.

Tactile sensing emerges as a complementary modality by providing direct measurements of local contact states, including surface deformations, slip, and force distributions. Sensors like GelSight, DIGIT, and TacTip offer high-resolution feedback that has demonstrated utility in various applications, including grasp stability, slip detection, and dexterous in-hand manipulation. For insertion tasks, tactile feedback is particularly critical for signaling misalignments and interactions with the socket that visual input alone cannot infer. Previous research, including the TacSL benchmark, has indicated that augmenting visual or low-dimensional state policies with tactile input generally improves insertion success rates, underscoring the value of contact feedback.

Despite the intuitive benefits of integrating vision and touch, effectively fusing these heterogeneous data streams remains a research challenge. Naïve fusion strategies, such as direct feature concatenation, have often yielded negligible or even negative performance gains, suggesting that these methods can dilute modality-specific cues or struggle with synchronization. This has motivated the development of structured fusion approaches. Recent works have explored attention mechanisms, including self-attention for intra-modal correspondences and cross-modal attention for alignment. Other adaptive methods have utilized predictive force cues to dynamically reweight modalities, and some have even integrated language to guide fusion and improve generalization.

Parallel to these developments, the integration of physical principles and inductive biases into machine learning models has gained prominence. Physics-informed machine learning seeks to embed known physical constraints directly into neural network architectures to improve stability, generalization, and data efficiency. In robotics, studies have explored leveraging symmetry priors for tasks like object reconstruction and dual-arm manipulation, inspired by principles observed in human motor control, where bilateral force balance contributes to stability and coordinated movements.

This work positions itself at the intersection of these fields. It acknowledges the limitations of vision-only systems and the challenges of naïve multimodal fusion. It builds upon the advancements in attention-based fusion by proposing a Cross-Modal Transformer for structured integration of visual and tactile features. Critically, it extends beyond prior adaptive fusion mechanisms by introducing a physics-informed regularization specifically for tactile embeddings. This regularization enforces bilateral force symmetry, inspired by human motor control, and differentiates itself from earlier work that applied symmetry for state estimation or object modeling. By combining structured attention with a policy-driven, physics-informed inductive bias, the paper aims to develop a more robust and stable visuo-tactile fusion framework for contact-rich robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust visuo-tactile fusion framework that enables precise and stable robotic manipulation, specifically focusing on insertion tasks.

The motivation for this work stems from several key observations and challenges in the field of robotic manipulation:

- **Limitations of Vision-Only Perception:** In contact-rich tasks like insertion, vision-based policies, while effective for global scene understanding, struggle with fine-grained local interactions. They are susceptible to issues such as occlusion of the contact area, variations in lighting, depth ambiguity, and the inherent inability to directly perceive physical properties like contact forces, material compliance, or micro-slippage. These limitations often lead to imprecise alignments and unstable behavior during close-contact operations.
- **Indispensability of Tactile Feedback:** Tactile sensing directly measures local contact states, providing crucial information that complements vision. It can detect subtle misalignments, frictional dynamics, and the precise timing and magnitude of contact forces, all of which are critical for successful insertion. Empirical studies have consistently demonstrated that augmenting policies with tactile signals improves performance in contact-rich scenarios.
- **Challenges in Effective Multimodal Fusion:** Despite the complementary nature of vision and touch, their effective integration is non-trivial. Previous research, particularly on the TacSL benchmark, has shown that simplistic approaches like direct concatenation of visual and tactile features often yield minimal or even negative gains. This failure is attributed to difficulties in synchronizing heterogeneous representations and the risk of diluting modality-specific information, highlighting the need for structured and principled fusion strategies.
- **Inspiration from Human Motor Control and Physical Priors:** Human manipulation often relies on internal models that incorporate physical principles, such as maintaining bilateral force balance during grasping and insertion to ensure stability and minimize jamming. The authors are motivated to incorporate similar physics-informed inductive biases into robotic learning algorithms. Specifically, principles of bilateral symmetry in force application, observed in human motor control, suggest a mechanism to stabilize grasps and reduce misalignments.
- **Gap in Existing Fusion Methods:** While attention mechanisms have been explored for structured visuo-tactile fusion, many existing approaches treat tactile signals as features to be adaptively weighted or use symmetry for state estimation rather than directly integrating physical principles into the policy learning process. There is a recognized need for a framework that embeds explicit physical constraints into tactile representations to enhance stability and task performance directly within a policy-driven learning context.

Based on these motivations, the specific objectives are:
1. To propose a novel **Cross-Modal Transformer (CMT)** architecture that employs hierarchical self- and cross-attention mechanisms to effectively integrate global visual context with local tactile feedback, addressing synchronization and representation challenges.
2. To introduce a **physics-informed symmetry regularization** loss that encourages bilateral force balance in tactile embeddings, drawing inspiration from human motor control principles to stabilize grasps and reduce lateral misalignments during insertion.
3. To **experimentally validate** the proposed symmetry-aware visuo-tactile fusion framework, demonstrating its superior performance in robotic insertion tasks compared to vision-only, tactile-only, naïve fusion, and gated fusion baselines, and to analyze its computational efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on a symmetry-aware, physics-informed visuo-tactile fusion framework for robotic insertion, leveraging a Cross-Modal Transformer (CMT) architecture with an auxiliary bilateral force symmetry regularization loss. The approach formulates insertion as a partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP), where the policy aims to maximize discounted return based on multimodal observations and continuous robot actions.

The framework integrates three main components:

**A. Residual Tactile Encoding with Symmetry Priors**
- **Residual Force Calculation:** To account for potential object asymmetry and variations in grasp pose, raw tactile forces from the left ($\bar{f}^L_t$) and right ($\bar{f}^R_t$) gripper fingers are converted into residual forces ($f^L_t, f^R_t$). This is achieved by subtracting calibrated reference forces ($f^L_{ref}, f^R_{ref}$) obtained during a pre-insertion grasping step. For symmetric objects, these reference forces can be zero. This residual formulation is designed to allow the policy to learn corrective actions based on deviations from a nominal contact state rather than absolute force values.
- **Intra-Modal Self-Attention:** The residual tactile forces are encoded into embeddings ($h^L_t, h^R_t$) using a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) backbone. To capture bilateral consistency and enforce a coherent intra-modal structure, self-attention is applied over the concatenated left and right tactile feature embeddings ($[h^L_t; h^R_t]$). This process, represented as $z^T_t = \text{Attn}(W_q [h^L_t; h^R_t], W_k [h^L_t; h^R_t], W_v [h^L_t; h^R_t])$, yields a residual-aware tactile representation.

**B. Visuo-Tactile Cross-Attention**
- **Visual Encoding:** RGB wrist camera observations ($v_t$) are encoded into a visual embedding ($z^V_t$) using a separate CNN backbone.
- **Cross-Modal Fusion:** A cross-attention mechanism integrates the visual and tactile embeddings. The design choice is for vision to act as the query (guiding global alignment) and tactile sensing to provide the key and value (supplying fine-grained local corrective feedback). This is formulated as $z^{VT}_t = \text{Attn}(W^V_q z^V_t, W^T_k z^T_t, W^T_v z^T_t)$. By stacking the intra-tactile self-attention and the visuo-tactile cross-attention, the CMT achieves hierarchical fusion, explicitly modeling the distinct but complementary roles of each modality.

**C. Physics-Informed Symmetry Regularization**
- **Bilateral Force Balance:** Inspired by human motor control principles, an auxiliary loss, $L_{sym}$, is introduced to enforce bilateral force balance. This regularization encourages vertical symmetry between the left and right finger forces.
- **Implementation:** The right tactile map is vertically flipped and then encoded as $\tilde{h}^R_t$. This flipped and encoded representation is compared against the embedding of the left tactile map ($h^L_t$) using a mean squared error (MSE) loss: $L_{sym} = E_{t \sim D} \left\|h^L_t - \tilde{h}^R_t\right\|^2_2$.
- **Functionality:** This auxiliary objective serves two purposes: (i) pre-insertion, it stabilizes initial contact by suppressing asymmetric grasp forces, and (ii) during insertion, it reduces lateral misalignments that can lead to jamming. This acts as a physics-informed inductive bias, promoting physical consistency in the learned policy.

**D. Policy Optimization with PPO**
- **Overall Objective:** The total training objective is a combination of the clipped surrogate objective of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) ($L_{PPO}$) and the physics-informed symmetry regularization loss ($L_{sym}$), weighted by a coefficient $\lambda_{sym}$: $L = L_{PPO} + \lambda_{sym} L_{sym}$.
- **Action Space and Stability:** The stochastic policy outputs Gaussian actions with bounded variance, ensuring training stability.

**E. Experimental Setup**
- **Environment:** Experiments are conducted on a robotic insertion task within the IsaacGym simulation environment, following the TacSL benchmark framework. This involves inserting a peg held by a gripper into a socket.
- **Randomization:** To ensure robustness and generalization, the environment includes extensive randomization (Table III in Appendix VI), covering initial end-effector pose, peg-in-gripper offsets, socket position, contact parameters (stiffness, damping), joint damping, and observation noise.
- **Sensor Configuration:** Observations include an RGB wrist camera image (64x64x3) and tactile force fields from two gripper-mounted tactile sensors (32x32x3 for each finger, with 3 channels for $f_x, f_y, f_z$).
- **Architecture:** A lightweight 3-layer CNN encoder with a Spatial SoftArgMax layer processes each modality. The fusion module (naïve, gated, or CMT) combines these embeddings, followed by a recurrent (2-layer LSTM) and feedforward MLP policy head (Table IV).
- **Baselines:** Performance is compared against vision-only, tactile-only, privileged settings (compact state features, wrist camera + contact force), naïve early fusion (feature concatenation), and gated fusion (with and without symmetry regularization).
- **Metrics:** Primary metrics include insertion success rate, average steps to succeed, inference latency, memory usage, and throughput.
- **Training Details:** All policies are trained using PPO with hyperparameters adapted from TacSL (Table V), across three different random seeds for statistical robustness.
- **Additional Task:** An extended evaluation on a screw task (grasping and screwing a nut onto a bolt) is performed to further assess tactile sensing's role.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the efficacy of the symmetry-aware visuo-tactile fusion framework through quantitative and qualitative analyses, along with a computational assessment.

**A. Quantitative Analysis (Insertion Success Rates)**
Table I presents the insertion success rates across various sensory modalities and fusion strategies.
- **Indispensability of Contact Forces:** The results consistently show that incorporating contact force sensing improves performance. In the privileged setting with compact state features, adding contact forces increased the success rate from 96.74% to 98.96% (+2.22%). Similarly, augmenting the wrist camera with contact forces raised success from 93.23% to 96.09% (+2.86%). These findings indicate that contact feedback provides crucial information (e.g., micro-slippage, compliance) that vision alone cannot supply.
- **Robustness of Tactile-Only Sensing:** A tactile-only policy achieved a success rate of 91.41%. This demonstrates that high-resolution tactile signals alone contain sufficient geometric and force-distribution information to enable robust task execution, even without visual context, suggesting resilience under visually degraded or occluded conditions.
- **Superiority of Structured Fusion (CMT):** The proposed Cross-Modal Transformer (CMT) without symmetry regularization achieved a 96.22% success rate, significantly outperforming both the naïve concatenation (92.97%) and gated fusion (94.53%) baselines. This performance approaches that of the privileged "wrist + contact force" configuration (96.09%), indicating that structured cross-modal attention effectively extracts complementary benefits from both modalities.
- **Benefits of Symmetry Regularization:** The addition of the physics-informed symmetry regularization further enhanced performance. For gated fusion, the success rate increased from 94.53% to 95.05%. For the CMT architecture, the success rate rose from 96.22% to 96.59%. These gains, though marginal in percentage, suggest that symmetry-aware balancing acts as a stabilizing inductive bias, contributing to improved consistency and reducing variability in policy performance. The CMT with symmetry regularization achieved the highest success rate among all tested fusion methods.

**B. Qualitative Analysis (Bilateral Force Fields and Insertion Trajectories)**
- **Naïve Fusion Behavior:** Figure 4 illustrates that na¨ıve fusion strategies often lead to imbalanced force distributions during contact with the socket walls. This imbalance manifests as asymmetric flow patterns on the tactile arrays, frequently triggering unstable corrective actions, attitude oscillations, and occasional re-grasping events.
- **Symmetry-Aware CMT Behavior:** In contrast, the proposed CMT with symmetry regularization maintained balanced force distributions throughout the insertion process. By learning bilateral consistency, the model facilitated straighter, smoother insertion trajectories with minimal net lateral torque, resembling a clean, pre-aligned insertion motion. This qualitative stability is directly linked to the quantitative performance improvements.
- **Efficiency in Steps to Succeed:** Figure 5 further quantifies these qualitative observations by showing the distribution of steps required for successful insertion. The na¨ıve fusion policy averaged 111.63 steps, whereas the CMT-based fusion reduced this to 108.48 steps, a decrease of 2.83%. This reduction in steps is attributed to the balanced force distributions and reduced corrective oscillations, leading to more efficient execution.

**C. Computational Analysis (Latency, Memory, Throughput)**
Table II details the computational efficiency of the different fusion models.
- **Efficiency of Baselines:** The na¨ıve model demonstrated the lowest latency (5.42 ms) and highest throughput (184.5 fps). The gated model had comparable latency (5.51 ms, 181.5 fps) and the smallest memory footprint (17.43 MB).
- **CMT Performance:** The proposed CMT, with its Transformer layers, incurred slightly higher latency (6.52 ms) and memory usage (21.45 MB), resulting in a throughput of 153.37 fps.
- **Trade-off Justification:** Despite the marginal increase in computational cost (approximately 20% higher latency than naïve), the CMT's substantial improvement in success rate (over 3.6% absolute gain) justifies this trade-off. All models, including CMT, operate well within real-time control requirements (above 60 Hz), indicating that performance differences are primarily driven by the effectiveness of multimodal structure exploitation rather than computational bottlenecks.

**D. Tactile Sensing for Screw Tasks (Appendix VII)**
An extension of the evaluation to a screw task revealed that a tactile-only policy achieved a 100% success rate, outperforming both the privileged contact-force baseline and the wrist-camera policy (Figure 6). This suggests that for tasks dominated by fine-grained contact interactions, high-resolution tactile feedback can function as a primary modality, potentially even substituting for vision in specific scenarios where rich contact geometry and force details are paramount.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by advancing visuo-tactile fusion strategies and clarifying the role of tactile sensing in contact-rich tasks. The significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancement in Multimodal Fusion:** The introduction of the Cross-Modal Transformer (CMT) with hierarchical self- and cross-attention provides a structured and principled approach to integrate heterogeneous visual and tactile information. This method demonstrates that overcoming the limitations of naïve fusion strategies is possible, leading to performance that closely matches or even surpasses privileged sensing configurations. This finding is significant for developing more capable robotic systems that can robustly interpret and act upon complex sensory inputs.
- **Validation of Physics-Informed Learning:** The bilateral force symmetry regularization highlights the value of embedding physics-informed inductive biases directly into policy learning. By encouraging stable grasp forces and reducing lateral misalignments, this regularization enhances policy stability and precision, offering a pathway to mitigate common challenges like jamming during insertion. This approach demonstrates a method for leveraging domain knowledge to improve data efficiency and robustness in robotic learning.
- **Reaffirming the Role of Tactile Sensing:** The study empirically establishes tactile sensing as an indispensable modality for precise alignment and contact-rich manipulation. The strong performance of tactile-only policies, particularly in tasks like screw insertion, suggests its potential as a primary sensory input in specific scenarios where fine-grained contact interactions are dominant, and as a robust alternative when visual information is limited or occluded. This could lead to a re-evaluation of sensor priorities for certain robotic applications.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Precision in Robotic Manipulation:** The combined framework of structured attention and physics-informed regularization results in policies that achieve high success rates and smoother, more efficient insertion trajectories. This increased robustness and precision are critical for real-world deployment of robots in industrial assembly, medical procedures, or other tasks requiring delicate object interaction.
- **Generalizability for Diverse Manipulation Tasks:** While validated on symmetric plug insertion, the residual tactile force formulation and the general principles of symmetry-aware fusion are designed to generalize to asymmetric objects through calibration. This foresight suggests a broader applicability for the framework across a wider range of manipulation tasks and object geometries, moving towards more versatile and adaptive robotic systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** By open-sourcing the code and task configurations, the research promotes reproducibility and provides a robust baseline for future investigations into visuo-tactile fusion. This includes exploring extensions to more complex, multi-step assembly tasks where vision might provide global context and tactile sensing ensures local precision, and further exploring how different physical priors can be effectively integrated into deep reinforcement learning.
