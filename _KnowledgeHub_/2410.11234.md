# Bayes Adaptive Monte Carlo Tree Search for Offline Model-based Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The University of Hong Kong, Tsinghua University, and Carnegie Mellon University developed Bayes Adaptive Monte Carlo Tree Search (BA-MCTS), an offline model-based reinforcement learning algorithm that leverages Bayes Adaptive MDPs to handle model uncertainty in continuous state and action spaces. The method achieves state-of-the-art performance on D4RL MuJoCo benchmarks and demonstrates superior control in challenging tokamak fusion tasks.

## Problem

- Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) from static datasets faces significant challenges due to model uncertainty, as multiple underlying MDPs can be consistent with observed data, leading to unreliable predictions in out-of-distribution regions.
- Existing Bayes Adaptive Monte Carlo Planning (BAMCP) methods for principled uncertainty handling are typically restricted to discrete state/action spaces, often rely on a ground-truth world model, and do not produce generalizable policy functions.
- Current offline Model-Based RL (MBRL) often uses ensemble models but treats members uniformly, failing to dynamically adapt to local model reliability or robustly handle stochastic transitions and continuous spaces.

## Method

- A novel ==Bayes Adaptive Monte Carlo Tree Search== (**BA-MCTS**) framework is introduced, which integrates deep ensembles for world model estimation, a pessimistic Bayes-Adaptive MDP (P-BAMDP) construction, and a Continuous Bayes Adaptive Monte Carlo Planning (Continuous BAMCP) algorithm.
- Continuous BAMCP extends Bayes-adaptive planning to continuous, stochastic environments by incorporating Double Progressive Widening (DPW) for actions and states, dynamically updating beliefs over ensemble models, and using a penalized reward to manage uncertainty.
- The framework operates within a policy iteration structure where actors use Continuous BAMCP for planning and data generation, while a learner distills these planning outcomes into a deployable parameterized policy using an off-policy RL algorithm like SAC.

## Results

- BA-MCTS achieved an average score of **80.25** across D4RL MuJoCo tasks, outperforming several state-of-the-art model-based and model-free offline RL baselines, with a statistically significant performance difference.
- Belief adaptation improved world model prediction accuracy in **10** out of **12** D4RL tasks and led to higher average policy performance compared to uniform ensembles.
- The method demonstrated superior performance on challenging, high-dimensional, and stochastic tokamak control tasks (Ion Rotation, Electron Temperature, βn), validating its applicability to complex real-world problems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bayesian belief adaptation over an ensemble of world models significantly enhances model prediction accuracy and improves policy performance by dynamically adjusting confidence in individual ensemble members.
> - The Continuous BAMCP algorithm effectively extends Bayes-adaptive planning to complex continuous and stochastic domains, providing theoretical consistency guarantees for its value estimation.
> - Integrating MCTS-based planning with a deep RL policy iteration framework (an "RL + Search" paradigm) enables the distillation of superior planning outcomes into a generalizable policy, outperforming approaches without such explicit planning.
