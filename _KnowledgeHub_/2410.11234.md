# Bayes Adaptive Monte Carlo Tree Search for Offline Model-based Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The University of Hong Kong, Tsinghua University, and Carnegie Mellon University developed Bayes Adaptive Monte Carlo Tree Search (BA-MCTS), an offline model-based reinforcement learning algorithm that leverages Bayes Adaptive MDPs to handle model uncertainty in continuous state and action spaces. The method achieves state-of-the-art performance on D4RL MuJoCo benchmarks and demonstrates superior control in challenging tokamak fusion tasks.

## Problem

- Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) from static datasets faces significant challenges due to model uncertainty, as multiple underlying MDPs can be consistent with observed data, leading to unreliable predictions in out-of-distribution regions.
- Existing Bayes Adaptive Monte Carlo Planning (BAMCP) methods for principled uncertainty handling are typically restricted to discrete state/action spaces, often rely on a ground-truth world model, and do not produce generalizable policy functions.
- Current offline Model-Based RL (MBRL) often uses ensemble models but treats members uniformly, failing to dynamically adapt to local model reliability or robustly handle stochastic transitions and continuous spaces.

## Method

- A novel ==Bayes Adaptive Monte Carlo Tree Search== (**BA-MCTS**) framework is introduced, which integrates deep ensembles for world model estimation, a pessimistic Bayes-Adaptive MDP (P-BAMDP) construction, and a Continuous Bayes Adaptive Monte Carlo Planning (Continuous BAMCP) algorithm.
- Continuous BAMCP extends Bayes-adaptive planning to continuous, stochastic environments by incorporating Double Progressive Widening (DPW) for actions and states, dynamically updating beliefs over ensemble models, and using a penalized reward to manage uncertainty.
- The framework operates within a policy iteration structure where actors use Continuous BAMCP for planning and data generation, while a learner distills these planning outcomes into a deployable parameterized policy using an off-policy RL algorithm like SAC.

## Results

- BA-MCTS achieved an average score of **80.25** across D4RL MuJoCo tasks, outperforming several state-of-the-art model-based and model-free offline RL baselines, with a statistically significant performance difference.
- Belief adaptation improved world model prediction accuracy in **10** out of **12** D4RL tasks and led to higher average policy performance compared to uniform ensembles.
- The method demonstrated superior performance on challenging, high-dimensional, and stochastic tokamak control tasks (Ion Rotation, Electron Temperature, βn), validating its applicability to complex real-world problems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bayesian belief adaptation over an ensemble of world models significantly enhances model prediction accuracy and improves policy performance by dynamically adjusting confidence in individual ensemble members.
> - The Continuous BAMCP algorithm effectively extends Bayes-adaptive planning to complex continuous and stochastic domains, providing theoretical consistency guarantees for its value estimation.
> - Integrating MCTS-based planning with a deep RL policy iteration framework (an "RL + Search" paradigm) enables the distillation of superior planning outcomes into a generalizable policy, outperforming approaches without such explicit planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) typically relies on extensive interaction with an environment, which can be impractical, unsafe, or costly in real-world applications. Offline RL emerges as an alternative, aiming to train policies from static datasets of pre-collected transitions.

Within offline RL, two primary paradigms exist:
- **Model-Free Offline RL:** These methods, such as those that constrain the learned policy to remain close to the behavior policy that generated the data, aim to mitigate issues like overestimation for out-of-distribution (OOD) states. A limitation of this approach is the potential need for large volumes of high-quality, near-optimal demonstrations.
- **Offline Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL):** This approach explicitly learns dynamics models from offline data. These models then serve as surrogate simulators, allowing for policy optimization through imaginary rollouts. A key advantage of offline MBRL is its potential for improved data efficiency and the ability of learned policies to generalize beyond the immediate support of the dataset, even from suboptimal or exploratory data, provided there is adequate state-action space coverage.

A significant challenge in offline MBRL is model uncertainty. Given a static dataset, multiple underlying Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) can exhibit identical behavior within the observed data. However, their dynamics and reward functions may differ substantially in OOD regions. Current MBRL methods often address this by learning an ensemble of world models, frequently treating each member uniformly, which may not adequately capture or adapt to local model reliability.

The Bayes Adaptive Markov Decision Process (BAMDP) framework offers a principled approach to address model uncertainty by treating the true MDP as unknown and maintaining a belief distribution over possible MDPs, which is updated based on observed experience. Bayes Adaptive Monte Carlo Planning (BAMCP) has been developed to solve BAMDPs using Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS). However, existing BAMCP methods face several limitations: they often rely on a ground-truth world model, are typically restricted to discrete state and action spaces, and produce an action choice for a specific state rather than a generalizable policy function.

This work situates itself by aiming to bridge these gaps. It proposes to integrate the principled uncertainty handling of BAMDPs into the offline MBRL setting, specifically addressing the challenges of continuous state and action spaces and stochastic transitions. The paper aligns with the "RL + Search" paradigm exemplified by systems like AlphaZero, seeking to enhance existing offline MBRL methods by incorporating sophisticated search-based planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an offline Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) algorithm that robustly handles model uncertainty in environments with continuous state and action spaces and stochastic transitions, leveraging the principled framework of Bayes Adaptive Markov Decision Processes (BAMDPs).

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges and opportunities in the field of reinforcement learning:

1. **Addressing Data Scarcity and Safety in Real-World RL:** Traditional online RL methods necessitate extensive interaction with the environment, which is often infeasible, costly, or unsafe in real-world applications (e.g., robotics, autonomous driving, industrial control, nuclear fusion). Offline RL offers a compelling alternative by enabling policy learning from pre-collected, static datasets, thus avoiding direct interaction during training.

2. **Overcoming Limitations of Model-Free Offline RL:** While model-free offline RL methods attempt to learn policies from static data, they frequently employ mechanisms to prevent overestimation of returns for out-of-distribution (OOD) states by constraining the learned policy to remain close to the behavior policy. This approach, however, often requires large volumes of high-quality demonstration data and may limit the policy's ability to generalize or discover novel, superior behaviors.

3. **Enhancing Offline MBRL through Principled Uncertainty Quantification:** Offline MBRL holds promise for improving data efficiency and enabling generalization beyond the dataset support. However, its effectiveness is critically dependent on the accuracy of the learned world models. A fundamental challenge is that a static offline dataset can be consistent with numerous possible underlying MDPs, leading to significant uncertainty about the true environment dynamics, particularly in unobserved regions. Existing ensemble-based MBRL methods often treat individual model predictions uniformly, which may not adequately address this uncertainty or adapt to the reliability of specific ensemble members in different state-action regions. The paper is motivated by the need for a more principled approach to model this ambiguity.

4. **Extending BAMDPs to Continuous, Stochastic Environments for Policy Learning:** The BAMDP framework offers a theoretically sound method for handling model uncertainty by explicitly incorporating a belief distribution over possible MDPs. While existing Bayes Adaptive Monte Carlo Planning (BAMCP) methods apply MCTS to BAMDPs, they are limited by:
 - **Reliance on Ground Truth:** Some variations require access to a ground-truth model for planning.
 - **Discrete State and Action Spaces:** They are typically designed for discrete environments, making them unsuitable for continuous control tasks prevalent in many real-world applications.
 - **Action Output vs. Policy Function:** They primarily output an optimal action for a given state-belief pair, rather than a generalizable policy function that can be deployed in real-time.

By addressing these motivations, the key objectives are:
- To **firstly apply BAMDPs** to explicitly model and manage the uncertainties inherent in world models learned from offline datasets.
- To **propose a novel planning algorithm, Continuous BAMCP**, that can solve BAMDPs in continuous state and action spaces with stochastic transitions, and to provide theoretical grounding for its consistency.
- To **develop an integrated "RL + Search" framework** that successfully combines Bayesian RL, offline MBRL, and deep MCTS-based planning for data-driven continuous control, enabling the distillation of planning outcomes into an executable policy function.
- To **demonstrate state-of-the-art performance** on challenging benchmarks, including D4RL MuJoCo tasks and real-world inspired tokamak control problems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed Bayes Adaptive Monte Carlo Tree Search (BA-MCTS), integrates several components: deep ensembles for world model estimation and uncertainty quantification, a novel Continuous Bayes Adaptive Monte Carlo Planning (Continuous BAMCP) algorithm, and a policy iteration framework for distilling planning results into a deployable policy.

#### 3.1. The Key Role of Deep Ensembles

- **World Model Estimation:** From a static offline dataset $D_\mu$, an ensemble of $K$ dynamics models $\{P^i_\theta\}$ and reward models $\{R^i_\theta\}$ is learned. Each model is a deep neural network, trained by minimizing supervised learning losses (Equation 3) on the dataset. This ensemble is treated as a finite approximation of the true distribution of world models, $P(M_\theta|D_\mu)$.
- **Belief Representation and Update:** The belief over these world models is represented as a mass function, $b(\theta)$, where each element $b(\theta)(i)$ denotes the probability of the $i$-th ensemble member being the true MDP. Initially, this belief is uniform (e.g., $[1/K, \ldots, 1/K]$). After observing a transition $(s, a, r, s')$, the belief is updated via a Bayesian posterior update rule (Equation 4), allowing the agent to dynamically adapt its confidence in each ensemble member based on experience.
- **Pessimistic Bayes-Adaptive MDP (P-BAMDP) Construction:** To guard against over-optimism due to potential inaccuracies in all ensemble members, a pessimistic approach is adopted. A penalized reward, $\tilde{r}$, is calculated (Equation 5) by subtracting a term proportional to the standard deviation of one-step lookahead Q-value targets across the ensemble. This penalty discourages exploration in regions of high model disagreement, thereby mitigating the exploitation of unreliable model predictions.

#### 3.2. Continuous BAMCP

- **Motivation:** Standard BAMCP is limited to discrete state and action spaces and does not directly handle stochastic transitions. The objective is to extend Bayes-adaptive planning to continuous and stochastic control.
- **Double Progressive Widening (DPW):** The proposed Continuous BAMCP (Algorithm 1) incorporates DPW, a technique to adapt MCTS for continuous spaces.
 - **Action Widening (ACTIONPW):** Instead of exploring all continuous actions, new actions are incrementally added to a finite list of options for a given decision node $(s, h)$ based on its visitation count $N((s, h))$ and a hyperparameter $\alpha$. If the widening condition `floor(N((s, h))^α) >= |C((s, h))|` is met, a new action is sampled from the current policy $\pi$; otherwise, an existing action is selected using the UCT (Upper Confidence bound for Trees) rule.
 - **State Widening (STATEPW):** For stochastic transitions, new next states $s'$ are added to the children set of a state-action pair $(s, h), a$ based on its visitation count $N((s, h), a)$ and a hyperparameter $\beta$. If the widening condition `floor(N((s, h), a)^β) >= |C((s, h), a)|` is met, a new state is sampled; otherwise, the least visited child is selected.
- **Integration with BAMDP:**
 - The planning operates in the augmented information state space $S^+ = (s, b(\theta))$, where $b(\theta)$ is the belief vector.
 - In the `STATEPW` procedure, rewards $r$ and next states $s'$ are sampled from the belief-weighted distributions across the ensemble members (representing $R^+$ and $P^+$ in the BAMDP).
 - After each transition, the belief $b(\theta)$ is updated to $b'(\theta)$ using Equation 4, thus progressing in the information state space.
 - The `SIMULATE` procedure uses the penalized reward $\tilde{r}$ (Equation 5) to incorporate pessimism during planning.
- **Theoretical Consistency:** Theorem 4.1 establishes the consistency of Continuous BAMCP. It proves that the estimated value of the root node $\hat{V}(z_0)$ converges exponentially surely to a ball of radius $\gamma^{d_{\max}} \epsilon_V$ around the P-BAMDP's optimal value $V^*_p(z_0)$, where $\epsilon_V$ is the value function approximation error at leaf nodes and $\gamma$ is the discount factor. This theoretical guarantee supports the reliability of the planner.

#### 3.3. Overall Framework: BA-MCTS

- **Policy Iteration Structure (Algorithm 2):** BA-MCTS operates within a policy iteration framework with parallel execution of a learner and multiple actors. The actors gather data by interacting with the learned world models using the Continuous BAMCP, and this data is then used by the learner to update policy and value networks.
- **Actor Operations:**
 - Actors sample starting states from the original offline dataset $D_\mu$.
 - For each starting state, an initial belief $b(\theta)$ is constructed by processing the corresponding offline trajectory in $D_\mu$.
 - Actors perform short imaginary rollouts of length $H$ within the learned P-BAMDP.
 - At each decision node $(s, h)$ during these rollouts, the `SEARCH` procedure (Continuous BAMCP) is invoked to determine an improved, non-parametric policy $\pi_{\text{ret}}$ and value $v_{\text{ret}}$.
 - Actions are sampled from $\pi_{\text{ret}}$, and the P-BAMDP dynamics are used to generate transitions and penalized rewards $\tilde{r}$.
 - The collected trajectories (including $(s, h, \pi_{\text{ret}}, \tilde{r}, s', h')$) are stored in a replay buffer $D$.
- **Learner Operations:**
 - The learner periodically samples mini-batches from the replay buffer $D$.
 - It updates the parameterized policy $\pi$ (actor network) and value function $Q_\psi$ (critic network) using an off-policy RL algorithm, Soft Actor-Critic (SAC). The goal is to distill the superior planning outcomes $\pi_{\text{ret}}$ into the parameterized policy $\pi$.
 - **BA-MCTS-SL (Ablation):** An alternative policy distillation approach where the policy network is updated via supervised learning (cross-entropy loss) to directly mimic the planner's output distribution $\pi_{\text{ret}}$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of BA-MCTS involved several aspects, including the benefits of belief adaptation, comprehensive benchmarking on D4RL MuJoCo tasks, ablation studies, and an application to tokamak control.

#### 4.1. The Benefit of Belief Adaptation

- **Improved Model Accuracy:** Empirical results indicate that Bayesian belief adaptation enhances the accuracy of world model predictions. Table 1 shows that the adaptive ensemble yields more accurate predictions for next states and rewards in 10 out of 12 D4RL MuJoCo benchmarking tasks compared to a uniform ensemble. Table 8 further supports this by demonstrating that real-world offline transitions have higher average likelihoods under an adapted belief distribution.
- **Enhanced Policy Performance:** This improved model accuracy translates to better performance of the final learned policy. The ablation study shows that BA-MBRL (which includes belief adaptation but no MCTS) significantly outperforms the Optimized baseline (without belief adaptation), supporting the efficacy of the BAMDP framework for calibrating learned world models.
- **Dynamic Belief Adjustment:** Qualitative analysis (Figure 1) illustrates that the belief over individual ensemble members dynamically shifts during both offline and imaginary rollouts, with different models gaining dominance as the trajectory unfolds.

#### 4.2. Benchmarking Results on D4RL MuJoCo

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** BA-MCTS demonstrates competitive performance across a range of D4RL MuJoCo tasks.
 - **Against Model-Based Methods:** In Table 2, BA-MCTS achieves a high average score of 80.25 across all twelve tasks, outperforming several state-of-the-art model-based baselines such as MOBILE, CBOP, RAMBO, APE-V, MAPLE, and Optimized.
 - **Against Model-Free Methods:** Table 9 indicates that BA-MCTS generally outperforms various model-free offline RL methods (e.g., CQL, BEAR, BRAC-v, SAC, BC), highlighting the benefits of model-based learning in these environments, particularly where data quality might be varied.
 - **Comprehensive Comparison:** Table 3 summarizes a comparison against 19 diverse offline RL baselines, where BA-MCTS obtains the highest overall average score. Statistical analysis using Cohen's d suggests a statistically significant performance difference (d > 1.8 in all cases where standard deviations were available) compared to most baselines.
- **Comparison with MuZero Variants:** When evaluated against Sampled EfficientZero (a MuZero variant adapted for offline learning), BA-MCTS shows superior performance (Figure 2), despite requiring less training time (Table 10). This is attributed to the challenges of MuZero's single-stage model/policy learning and its lack of explicit uncertainty handling in the offline setting.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Contribution of Belief Adaptation:** Comparing BA-MBRL (with belief adaptation) to the Optimized baseline (without belief adaptation) in Table 11 reveals that BA-MBRL achieves higher average performance and outperforms Optimized on 8 out of 12 tasks, directly demonstrating the positive impact of Bayesian model adaptation.
- **Contribution of MCTS-based Planning:** The comparison between BA-MCTS (with MCTS planning) and BA-MBRL (without MCTS planning, only belief adaptation) in Table 11 shows that BA-MCTS further improves performance. This result underscores the effectiveness of Continuous BAMCP as a policy improvement operator within the "RL + Search" paradigm.
- **Policy Distillation Mechanisms:** The study comparing BA-MCTS (SAC-based policy distillation) and BA-MCTS-SL (supervised learning-based policy distillation) shows comparable average performance (Table 11). BA-MCTS-SL, however, exhibits smoother learning curves (Figure 3), suggesting greater training stability, though it may require a warm-up phase on certain complex tasks (e.g., Walker2d) to establish an initial effective policy.
- **Necessity of Reward Penalty:** An ablation of the reward penalty (setting $\lambda=0$ in Equation 5) generally leads to a drop in average performance across all variants (Table 12). This indicates that the pessimistic reward penalty is crucial for preventing the agent from exploiting inaccuracies in the learned world models. BA-MCTS-SL was found to be less sensitive to the absence of this penalty compared to policy-gradient methods.

#### 4.4. Application to Tokamak Control

- **Real-World Applicability:** BA-MCTS was evaluated on three challenging and stochastic target tracking tasks in tokamak control (Ion Rotation, Electron Temperature, and $\beta_n$), which involve high-dimensional continuous state and action spaces.
- **Superior Performance:** Table 4 shows that BA-MCTS consistently outperforms all baselines (BA-MCTS-SL, BA-MBRL, CQL, Optimized) on these tasks, demonstrating the algorithm's potential for complex, data-driven control problems in scientific or industrial domains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of reinforcement learning by proposing a principled and effective framework for offline model-based reinforcement learning in continuous control tasks. The significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

- **Principled Uncertainty Management in Offline MBRL:** The work introduces the Bayes Adaptive Markov Decision Process (BAMDP) framework to offline MBRL in continuous state and action spaces, providing a theoretically grounded method for handling inherent model uncertainties when learning from static datasets. This approach allows for dynamic adaptation of belief over an ensemble of world models, which is a more sophisticated treatment of uncertainty than typically employed in previous ensemble-based methods.
- **Advancement in Bayes-Adaptive Planning:** The development of Continuous BAMCP extends the applicability of Bayes-adaptive planning to complex, high-dimensional, and stochastic environments. By integrating Double Progressive Widening with BAMCP and providing a consistency proof, the algorithm overcomes previous limitations related to discrete action/state spaces and assumptions about ground-truth models. This expands the scope of problems that can be addressed by such principled planning methods.
- **Enhanced Policy Learning through RL + Search:** The integration of deep search (Continuous BAMCP) into a policy iteration framework for offline MBRL establishes a robust "RL + Search" paradigm. This allows the parameterized policy to distill knowledge from a computationally intensive, yet more accurate, non-parametric planner, leading to superior policies. This approach is demonstrated to achieve state-of-the-art performance on widely-used benchmarks, suggesting a powerful general strategy for offline policy learning.
- **Improved Performance on Standard Benchmarks:** The consistent outperformance of BA-MCTS against numerous state-of-the-art model-based and model-free offline RL methods on the D4RL MuJoCo benchmark indicates a significant advancement in the field's empirical capabilities. The quantified statistical significance further reinforces the robustness of these improvements.
- **Demonstrated Real-World Applicability:** The successful application of BA-MCTS to complex tokamak control tasks, a domain critical for nuclear fusion research, highlights the algorithm's potential for impact in challenging scientific and engineering fields. This demonstration validates the framework's ability to handle highly stochastic dynamics, continuous high-dimensional spaces, and critical decision-making under uncertainty, which are common characteristics of many real-world control problems.
- **Foundational for Future Research:** This work lays a foundation for future research in several directions:
 - **Exploration in Offline Settings:** The principled treatment of model uncertainty could inform more effective exploration strategies in online settings or in semi-offline scenarios where limited additional data collection is permitted.
 - **Robustness to Model Mismatch:** The insights gained from belief adaptation and pessimistic planning could lead to more robust RL algorithms that are less susceptible to model inaccuracies.
 - **Architectural Innovations:** The interplay between planning and learning, as well as different policy distillation mechanisms, suggests avenues for further architectural and algorithmic innovations in deep RL.
 - **Domain-Specific Adaptations:** The success in tokamak control opens possibilities for tailoring this framework to other high-stakes domains requiring data-driven decision making under uncertainty.

In summary, this research advances offline model-based reinforcement learning by introducing a theoretically grounded and empirically validated approach to handle model uncertainty in continuous control, with promising implications for complex real-world applications.
