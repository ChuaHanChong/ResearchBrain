# Dolphin: Moving Towards Closed-loop Auto-research through Thinking, Practice, and Feedback

> [!summary] Summary
> DOLPHIN is a closed-loop LLM framework for automated research. It retrieves papers and ranks them by topic and task-attribute relevance, generates ideas and filters redundant or non-novel ones, implements them with traceback-guided debugging, then feeds experiment outcomes into the next round. It reaches accuracy comparable to state-of-the-art human methods on 3D point classification (93.9% overall accuracy on ModelNet40).

## Problem

- Prior idea-generation work judges ideas by LLM or human novelty ratings, which do not reflect whether an idea actually works when validated experimentally.
- Existing auto-research systems like AI-Scientist validate on simple self-built datasets, so results cannot be compared meaningfully with established methods in the same field.
- Earlier frameworks lack a feedback path from experimental results back to idea generation, so ideas are not refined iteratively the way human researchers refine them, and duplicates recur.

## Method

- ==Task-attribute-guided paper ranking==: an LLM extracts task attributes such as inputs and outputs, scores retrieved papers 1-10 on topic and attribute relevance, and keeps only those scoring 8 or higher as references.
- Idea filtering: summary embeddings are compared by ==cosine similarity== against an ==idea bank== (threshold 0.8) to drop redundant ideas, followed by an LLM ==novelty check== against searched literature.
- ==Exception-traceback-guided debugging==: custom-code function names, lines and code are extracted from the traceback, the LLM builds a ==local code structure== from them, then edits code, up to 5 attempts. Results are labeled improve, maintain or decline, and feed back into the idea bank and prompts.

## Results

- On ModelNet40, DOLPHIN's best idea, PointNet-CSR, reaches **93.9%** overall accuracy versus **91.0%** for PointNet and **93.8%** for GPSFormer, and runs over **3x** faster than DGCNN.
- Task-attribute paper ranking raises novel ideas from **8/20** (naive generation) and **13/20** (naive retrieval) to **19/20**. Traceback-guided local code structure lifts execution success from **4/15** to **7/15**.
- Idea improvement rate rises across loops from **2/7** to **3/6** to **4/8**. Over two loops, **5-6 of 40** ideas per task beat the baseline, and MLE-bench scores rise, e.g. **81.0** to **84.7** and **94.7** to **97.2**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Closing the loop from experimental outcomes back to idea generation both prunes repeated failures and steers toward productive directions, so idea quality can improve across iterations without model retraining.
> - Filtering retrieved literature by task attributes matters more than retrieval alone, because topically adjacent but structurally different papers mislead the LLM into non-novel or irrelevant ideas.
> - Debugging LLM-written code works better when the model sees a compact structure of only the custom code implicated by the traceback, not raw tracebacks cluttered with library frames.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the forefront of the evolving scientific research paradigm, aiming to transition from human-driven and AI-assisted models to fully automated scientific discovery. The paper explicitly identifies four stages in this evolution:
1. **Entirely Human-driven Research:** Traditional scientific inquiry.
2. **AI-assisted Research:** Researchers use AI tools (e.g., LLM-based assistants like GPT-researcher) to enhance efficiency in tasks like data analysis, computation, and idea generation.
3. **Semi-automatic Research:** Automation of specific processes within the research cycle (e.g., LLMs generating ideas based on literature, as seen in works by Si et al. (2024) or Li et al. (2024)).
4. **Auto-research:** The ultimate goal where AI autonomously handles the entire research process from ideation to experimental validation and reporting, as exemplified by recent efforts like AI-Scientist (Lu et al., 2024).

DOLPHIN directly contributes to the "auto-research" stage, striving for a comprehensive, closed-loop framework. The authors highlight two key limitations of existing auto-research efforts that DOLPHIN seeks to overcome:
- **Accurate Assessment of AI-generated Ideas:** Many prior works evaluate ideas based on human or LLM judgment, lacking rigorous experimental validation on real-world, comparative datasets. Simple, self-constructed datasets in works like AI-Scientist limit meaningful comparisons. DOLPHIN addresses this by validating ideas on commonly used public benchmarks.
- **Absence of a Feedback Mechanism:** A crucial iterative feedback loop, fundamental to human research (where experimental outcomes inform subsequent ideas), is often missing in previous AI-driven research frameworks. DOLPHIN explicitly incorporates this closed-loop feedback, allowing for continuous refinement and improvement of ideas.

By integrating robust experimental verification on diverse, public benchmarks and establishing a critical feedback loop, DOLPHIN aims to push the boundaries of automated scientific discovery beyond mere idea generation, moving towards a more complete and effective auto-research paradigm. It stands out by emphasizing the *practice* and *feedback* elements, which are often underdeveloped in AI systems focused primarily on *thinking* (idea generation).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of DOLPHIN is to introduce and validate a **closed-loop LLM-driven framework for enhancing the automation level of scientific research**. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations stemming from the limitations of current AI-assisted and semi-automatic research systems:

- **Bridging the Gap to Automatic Scientific Research:** The paper is motivated by the "profound transformation" of the scientific research paradigm due to AI. While AI has advanced data analysis, computation, and idea generation, the ultimate goal of "automatic scientific research" remains elusive. DOLPHIN aims to bridge this gap by creating a more comprehensive and autonomous system.
- **Ensuring Practical Effectiveness of AI-Generated Ideas:** A key motivation is to move beyond mere novelty or human-judged quality of AI-generated ideas. The authors argue that an idea's true effectiveness can only be confirmed through experimental validation. DOLPHIN seeks to implement this crucial empirical verification, addressing the uncertainty regarding the practical utility of AI's creative output.
- **Incorporating Iterative Feedback:** Human researchers continuously refine their hypotheses and methods based on experimental outcomes. This iterative learning process is vital for improving research quality. DOLPHIN is motivated to replicate this fundamental aspect of scientific inquiry, creating a feedback mechanism where experimental results directly inform and improve subsequent idea generation.
- **Improving Efficiency and Success Rate of Auto-research:** Recognizing that AI-generated code often contains errors and that irrelevant retrieved papers can misguide idea generation, DOLPHIN aims to enhance the efficiency of the research cycle. This involves filtering mechanisms for retrieved papers and robust debugging strategies for experimental code, ensuring a higher successful execution rate and better quality ideas.
- **Achieving Performance Comparable to Human-Designed Methods:** Ultimately, a core motivation is to demonstrate that an AI-driven auto-research system can not only automate processes but also generate novel methods that are competitive with, or even surpass, state-of-the-art human-designed approaches on established benchmarks.

In summary, DOLPHIN's key objective is to realize a more complete and effective auto-research system by establishing a closed loop of thinking, practice, and feedback, thereby addressing critical gaps in experimental validation and iterative refinement that characterize existing AI-driven scientific discovery efforts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DOLPHIN operates as a **closed-loop auto-research framework**, composed of three interconnected key stages that form a continuous research cycle: **idea generation, experimental verification, and results feedback**.

#### 3.1. Idea Generation Process

This initial stage aims to produce high-quality, novel research ideas, mimicking human researchers' approach of literature review and experience-based ideation.
- **Paper Retrieval and Ranking:**
 - Given an input research topic (e.g., "3D classification"), DOLPHIN uses the Semantic Scholar API to retrieve relevant papers.
 - To filter out irrelevant papers and focus on the specific task, a **task-attribute-guided paper ranking process** is employed. An LLM first extracts task attributes (e.g., model inputs, outputs) of the input topic. Then, it scores each retrieved paper (1-10) based on its relevance to the input topic and the alignment of its task attributes. Papers with scores below 8 are filtered out, serving as high-quality references.
- **Ideas Generation and Filtering:**
 - Using the filtered relevant papers and optionally previous experimental feedback, the LLM generates *N* novel and non-redundant ideas, each with a title, brief experiment plan, and summary.
 - A **further idea-filtering procedure** is applied to ensure novelty and non-redundancy, crucial for research efficiency.
 - **Independence Check:** Sentence-level embeddings of ideas are extracted. An idea bank *B* (initialized with previous *ineffective* ideas) stores embeddings of ideas checked for independence. A new idea is discarded if its cosine similarity with any idea in *B* exceeds a threshold (0.8), preventing redundant verification of similar ideas.
 - **Novelty Check:** The LLM is prompted to determine if the remaining ideas are novel based on searched papers, ensuring genuine newness. Only ideas deemed novel and independent proceed.

#### 3.2. Experimental Verification Process

This stage empirically validates the effectiveness of the generated ideas.
- **Plan Generation and Code Modification:** For a selected idea and reference code, the LLM generates detailed experimental plans and modifies the reference code accordingly.
- **Exception-Traceback-Guided Debugging:** Recognizing the low success rate of LLM-generated code, a specialized debugging process is implemented:
 - DOLPHIN extracts information (function name, line, code) from exception tracebacks, focusing on custom code errors rather than library calls.
 - The LLM is prompted to generate a **local code structure** guided by this extracted traceback information, which helps it understand the nested relationships and context around the error.
 - The LLM then analyzes the exception traceback and the generated local code structure to propose necessary modifications.
 - This debugging process iterates (up to a maximum of 5 attempts) until successful execution or the maximum attempts are reached.
- Successfully executed experiments lead to comprehensive analysis in the next phase.

#### 3.3. Results Feedback Process

This crucial stage closes the loop, allowing the system to learn from past experiments and improve future idea generation.
- **Result Categorization:** Experimental outcomes are categorized into three groups based on performance compared to the reference code: *improvement*, *maintenance*, or *decline*.
- **Feedback Integration:**
 - Embeddings of summaries from ideas that *maintained* or *improved* performance are incorporated into the idea bank *B* (from Sec. 3.1). This ensures that future idea generation avoids proposals similar to past ineffective ones.
 - Performance-enhancing ideas are explicitly incorporated into the prompt for the next round of idea generation, guiding the LLM towards more promising directions.

The system utilizes `gpt-4o-2024-08-06` for idea generation and feedback, and `deepseek-v2.5` (via Ollama) as the code agent for experimental verification, with `aider` as the framework for calling LLM agents during debugging.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DOLPHIN's effectiveness is demonstrated through extensive experiments on various benchmark datasets and tasks, showcasing its ability to generate high-quality, verifiable ideas and execute them.

- **Performance on Benchmark Tasks:**
 - **3D Point Classification (ModelNet40):** Using PointNet as a baseline, DOLPHIN generated ideas achieved an average improvement of +1.0% OA and a maximum improvement of +2.9% OA, reaching 93.9% OA. This performance is notably comparable to human-designed state-of-the-art (SoTA) methods like GPSFormer (93.8% OA), highlighting AI's capability to propose competitive solutions.
 - **2D Image Classification (CIFAR-100):** With WRN-28-10 as the baseline, DOLPHIN showed average improvements of +0.6% and a max of +0.8%, with the best idea achieving 82.0% accuracy, comparable to human-crafted methods like ResNeXt (82.2%).
 - **Sentiment Classification (SST-2):** Fine-tuning BERT-base, DOLPHIN achieved a maximum improvement of +1.5%, reaching 92.5% accuracy. This reduced the performance gap between BERT-base and BERT-large (93.1%), indicating effectiveness across different modalities.

- **Results on MLE-bench:** DOLPHIN successfully integrated with code generation frameworks like AIDE and demonstrated capability for version updates. For instance, it improved insult detection performance from 81.0% to 84.7% (achieving "gold medal" level on Kaggle) and enhanced tabular playground performance from 95.3% to 96.2%. This shows its adaptability to real-world ML engineering tasks and existing codebases.

- **Analysis of Ideas Generation Process:**
 - Ablation studies confirmed the efficacy of the proposed components. Naive idea generation (without retrieved papers) yielded only 8/20 novel ideas. Adding naive paper retrieval improved this to 13/20.
 - The **task-attribute-guided paper ranking** significantly boosted novelty, resulting in 19/20 novel ideas. This highlights the importance of filtering relevant reference papers to guide the LLM effectively, reducing misleading information and improving idea quality.

- **Analysis of Experimental Verification Process:**
 - The **exception-traceback-guided debugging** mechanism proved crucial for code execution success. Directly feeding tracebacks to LLMs resulted in a low success rate (e.g., 4/15 in Loop 1).
 - While simply adding local code structure without guidance didn't significantly improve results, guiding LLMs to generate code structures *with information extracted from the traceback* substantially increased the success rate (e.g., from 33.3% to 50.0%). This demonstrates that targeted context helps LLMs in complex code debugging.

- **Analysis of Results Feedback Process:**
 - The closed-loop framework exhibited progressive improvement in idea quality. The "improvement rate" (ratio of ideas improving performance to successfully executed ideas) increased from 2/7 in Loop 1 to 4/8 in Loop 3.
 - This indicates that the feedback mechanism successfully guides the LLM to generate more effective ideas and avoid redundant verification of previously ineffective concepts, thereby improving overall research efficiency.

- **Case Studies (PointNet-CSR):** A specific AI-generated method, PointNet-CSR, for 3D point classification, achieved 93.9% OA and 91.1% mAcc on ModelNet40, outperforming most human-designed methods (e.g., DGCNN's 92.9% OA). Furthermore, PointNet-CSR featured a more concise architecture and faster execution (∼6.12s/epoch vs. DGCNN's ∼20.86s/epoch), showcasing that AI can not only achieve comparable or better performance but also do so with more efficient implementations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DOLPHIN represents a significant step forward in the quest for automated scientific discovery, offering several crucial implications and potential impacts:

- **Advancement Towards Closed-Loop Auto-research:** The primary significance lies in its successful implementation of a robust closed-loop framework. By integrating idea generation, experimental verification, and results feedback, DOLPHIN provides a blueprint for a more autonomous research cycle, moving beyond fragmented AI-assisted tools. This brings the scientific community closer to the vision of truly self-driving research.
- **Empirical Validation of AI-Generated Ideas:** DOLPHIN addresses a critical gap in current AI research automation by emphasizing rigorous experimental validation on public benchmarks. This shifts the focus from theoretical novelty to practical effectiveness, providing a more reliable measure of AI's creative capabilities in scientific contexts.
- **Enhanced Research Efficiency:** The proposed mechanisms—task-attribute-guided paper ranking for better idea quality and traceback-guided debugging for higher code execution success—directly contribute to streamlining the research process. By reducing the generation of irrelevant ideas and minimizing debugging efforts, DOLPHIN significantly improves the overall efficiency of AI-driven research.
- **Discovery of Novel and Efficient Solutions:** The case study of PointNet-CSR (an AI-generated method comparable to human-designed SoTA but with a more concise and faster architecture) highlights the potential for AI to not only automate but also innovate. This suggests that AI systems like DOLPHIN could uncover non-obvious solutions or design more efficient architectures that human researchers might overlook.
- **Accelerating Scientific Progress:** By automating iterative research cycles, DOLPHIN has the potential to accelerate the pace of scientific discovery across various domains. It could enable rapid exploration of hypotheses, optimization of models, and generation of new knowledge, freeing up human researchers to focus on higher-level conceptualization and interpretation.
- **Foundation for Future AI-driven Research:** DOLPHIN serves as an important evaluation protocol for future works focusing on automatic idea generation. Its closed-loop design can be extended and combined with other advanced AI models, pushing the frontier of multi-disciplinary knowledge integration and complex problem-solving.

**Limitations and Future Outlook:**
Despite its advancements, DOLPHIN has acknowledged limitations. LLMs may suffer from "knowledge leakage" from their training data, potentially generating ideas that are not truly novel in principle, even if filtered as such. The reliance on abstracts and titles for paper ranking might limit the LLM's understanding of deep technical details. Furthermore, the current code capabilities of LLMs are insufficient for handling complex, project-level codes, restricting the complexity of tasks DOLPHIN can verify.

The authors envision future work addressing these limitations by developing more powerful code models capable of understanding and modifying large-scale projects, and by enhancing paper retrieval to incorporate multi-disciplinary knowledge, potentially leading to groundbreaking cross-domain scientific discoveries.

In conclusion, DOLPHIN is a pioneering effort in closed-loop auto-research, demonstrating AI's capacity for iterative scientific inquiry from ideation to empirical validation, with the potential to significantly transform the landscape of scientific discovery.
