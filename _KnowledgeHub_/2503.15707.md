# Safety Aware Task Planning via Large Language Models in Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called SAFER is introduced, which integrates multi-LLM collaboration for task planning with Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) for low-level safety guarantees in robotics. This approach reduces safety violations by an average of 77.5% in simulations and demonstrates robust, safe execution in real-world multi-robot tasks.

## Problem

- LLMs, when used for robotic task planning, tend to prioritize task completion and efficiency, often overlooking critical safety hazards like spatial conflicts or incomplete action sequences.
- Traditional safety methods are often insufficient for the dynamic and open-ended nature of LLM-generated plans, while single-LLM approaches face context window limitations when balancing task history and safety guidelines.
- There is a significant gap in systematically embedding safety awareness throughout the LLM-driven planning pipeline, especially in integrating high-level LLM planning with low-level robot control under provable safety guarantees.

## Method

- ==The SAFER== framework employs a multi-LLM architecture, decoupling task planning from safety auditing with a dedicated "Safety Planning LLM" providing feedback to the "Task Planning LLM."
- It seamlessly integrates Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) at the control policy level to provide theoretical guarantees for real-time safety enforcement, minimally modifying nominal robot control.
- A novel "LLM-as-a-Judge" module objectively evaluates and quantifies safety violations against 15 predefined risk criteria, feeding corrective feedback to the planning module for real-time adaptation and plan refinement.

## Results

- SAFER reduced Average Safety Violations (ASV) by an average of **77.5%** using DeepSeek-r1 and **47%** using GPT-4o on the COHERENT benchmark, while maintaining competitive task efficiency with minimal latency overhead.
- The framework was successfully deployed on real robotic hardware, demonstrating robust task execution, dynamic constraint handling (e.g., human interventions), and completion of complex, long-horizon multi-robot tasks while adhering to safety standards.
- Observations confirmed that advanced LLM reasoning improves plan safety, and the "LLM-as-a-Judge" provided fair and quantifiable safety assessments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs with stronger inherent reasoning capabilities generate plans with significantly fewer safety violations, indicating that advanced reasoning can proactively mitigate risks.
> - The "LLM-as-a-Judge" demonstrated objectivity in safety assessment due to the rule-based nature of safety checks, countering previous findings of LLMs favoring their own outputs.
> - Without explicit safety instructions, LLMs naturally prioritize task efficiency over safety, but the multi-LLM framework balances the two, achieving a substantial violation reduction without excessive execution steps.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into robotic task planning has rapidly transformed the field, offering unprecedented capabilities in interpreting natural language, reasoning about complex scenarios, and generating sophisticated action sequences for single and heterogeneous multi-robot systems. LLMs have shown superior performance over traditional logic-based planners for long-horizon and dynamic tasks, effectively bridging the gap between high-level human commands and low-level robotic execution.

However, a critical and widely acknowledged limitation of LLM-driven robotics is the inherent challenge of ensuring safety. LLMs, by design, tend to prioritize task completion and efficiency, often overlooking potential hazards such as spatial conflicts, incomplete action sequences, or unstable object handling. Traditional safety approaches, relying on rigid constraints or predefined rules, are often insufficient for the dynamic and open-ended nature of LLM-generated plans. While some prior work has attempted to introduce safety-aware LLM planning, these often face limitations due to the LLM's context window, forcing a trade-off between retaining task history for coherence and incorporating extensive safety guidelines.

This paper directly addresses this significant gap by introducing a novel framework that systematically embeds safety awareness throughout the task planning and execution pipeline. It differentiates itself from existing work in several key ways:

- **Multi-LLM Collaboration for Decoupled Planning and Safety:** Unlike single-LLM approaches that struggle with context window limitations when trying to balance task planning and safety checks, SAFER proposes a multi-LLM architecture. This decouples the roles, allowing a dedicated "Safety Planning LLM" to audit and provide feedback on plans generated by the "Task Planning LLM," thus overcoming the inherent trade-off. This is a significant advancement over methods that rely solely on Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) or Few-Shot Learning (FSL) to inject safety rules, as these still operate within the constraints of a single LLM's context.
- **Integration of Formal Safety Guarantees (CBFs) at the Control Level:** A crucial contribution is the seamless integration of Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) with LLM-based task planning. While previous research has explored safety in LLM-robotics through methods like Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) or general motion policy safety, this is presented as the "first work that incorporates safety into an LLM-based task planner while integrating CBFs to ensure safety at the level of the robotic control policy." CBFs provide strong theoretical safety guarantees by minimally modifying the nominal controller, offering a robust solution that is superior to older methods like Artificial Potential Fields (APFs) which can continuously affect control output. This bridges the gap between high-level cognitive planning and low-level physical execution with provable safety.
- **Quantifiable Safety Evaluation (LLM-as-a-Judge):** The paper formalizes "LLM-as-a-Judge" as a metric for safety violations. This leverages LLMs not just for planning, but also for objective evaluation of plan safety against predefined criteria, providing interpretable safety reports and enabling continuous system improvement. This extends the emerging "LLM-as-a-Judge" paradigm to the critical domain of robotic safety.

In essence, this work advances the state of the art by proposing a comprehensive, modular, and robust framework that systematically addresses the safety vacuum in LLM-driven robotics, leveraging multi-LLM collaboration for planning and formal control theory for execution, while also introducing a novel evaluation metric.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this research stems from the critical need to address safety challenges in LLM-driven robotic task planning. While LLMs have greatly enhanced robotic reasoning and task execution capabilities, their tendency to prioritize task completion over risk mitigation makes them prone to generating unsafe plans, especially in complex, long-horizon, and multi-agent scenarios. This inherent lack of safety awareness in LLMs can lead to catastrophic failures in real-world deployments.

The key objectives of this paper are:

1. **Develop a Safety-Aware Task Planning Framework:** To design and implement a novel framework that systematically embeds safety awareness into the entire robotic task planning and execution pipeline, moving beyond reactive safety measures to proactive risk mitigation.
2. **Mitigate Safety Violations in LLM-Generated Plans:** To significantly reduce the occurrence of safety violations (e.g., spatial conflicts, invalid action sequences, incomplete handoffs) in task plans generated by LLMs, particularly for complex, long-horizon tasks involving heterogeneous robotic agents.
3. **Maintain Task Efficiency:** To achieve enhanced safety without unduly compromising the efficiency or performance of the robotic system, seeking an optimal balance between safety and task completion.
4. **Decouple Task Planning and Safety Responsibilities:** To overcome the context window limitations of single LLMs by introducing a multi-LLM collaboration where a dedicated "Safety Planning LLM" provides feedback and enforces generalized safety rules alongside the primary "Task Planning LLM."
5. **Integrate High-Level Planning with Low-Level Control Guarantees:** To bridge the gap between high-level LLM-generated plans and low-level robot control by incorporating Control Barrier Functions (CBFs), thereby ensuring real-time safety enforcement and theoretical guarantees during physical execution.
6. **Establish a Quantifiable Safety Evaluation Metric:** To formalize a novel metric, "LLM-as-a-Judge," which leverages LLMs themselves as evaluators to quantify safety violations against a set of risk criteria, providing transparent and interpretable safety reports.
7. **Validate on Complex Multi-Agent Robotics:** To empirically validate the framework's effectiveness through extensive simulation experiments on a heterogeneous multi-robot task planning benchmark and through real-world hardware experiments involving multiple robots and human interaction.

In essence, the authors are motivated to make LLM-driven robotics not just capable, but also reliably safe, addressing a critical bottleneck for their widespread adoption in human-centric environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **SAFER (Safety-Aware Framework for Execution in Robotics)**, employs a multi-LLM collaborative approach combined with formal control methods to embed safety into robotic task planning and execution. SAFER is composed of four main modules: the Planning Module, Execution Module, LLM-as-a-Judge, and Feedback Module.

#### 3.1. A. Planning Module

This module is central to SAFER's safety-aware planning. Instead of a single LLM handling both task planning and safety, it uses a decoupled, collaborative approach:

- **Task Planning LLM:** This is the primary orchestrator. Given a high-level task description, agent capabilities, and environmental observations, it decomposes the goal into executable subtasks and generates an initial action sequence.
- **Safety Planning LLM:** Operating in parallel, this specialized LLM audits the initial plan from the Task Planning LLM. It identifies potential hazards (e.g., spatial conflicts, invalid action dependencies, omitted preconditions) and generates safety-related feedback. This feedback is then integrated back into the Task Planning LLM, which refines the plan to adhere to safety constraints.
- **Multi-LLM Rationale:** The authors argue that this multi-LLM approach overcomes the context window limitations of single LLMs, which struggle to retain long-horizon task history *and* extensive safety guidelines simultaneously. By specializing LLMs, SAFER ensures robust safety integration without sacrificing long-term coherence.

#### 3.2. B. Execution Module

This module translates the refined, safety-aware task plans into actionable commands for heterogeneous robotic agents (e.g., robot arms, quadrotors, robotic dogs).

- **Robot Execution LLM:** Each robot agent is equipped with a specific Execution LLM. This LLM verifies the feasibility of assigned actions in real-time before execution. This provides an additional layer of runtime checking.
- **Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) Integration:** To ensure safety guarantees at the low-level control policy, SAFER integrates a CBF-based control framework.
 - **Safe Set Definition:** Safety constraints (joint position/velocity/torque limits, obstacle avoidance, operational space limits, singularity avoidance, inter-robot collision avoidance) are defined in terms of the robot's state (joint, task, or operational space) to create a "safe set."
 - **Barrier Functions:** A barrier function `h(x) ≥ 0` ensures that if the system starts within the safe set, it remains there.
 - **Minimally Invasive Control:** CBFs minimally modify a nominal controller (`u_nom`) by solving a Quadratic Programming (QP) optimization problem (`u* = arg min ||u - u_nom||^2`) subject to the barrier function inequality. This ensures that safety is prioritized only when necessary, preventing continuous unnecessary influence on the controller. The CBFs provide theoretical guarantees for forward invariance of the safe set.
 - **Parsing:** Outputs from the LLMs (instructions and constraints) are parsed into control goals (e.g., desired velocity, end-effector pose) and barrier functions `h(x)` for the CBF optimizer to consider.

#### 3.3. C. LLM-as-a-Judge

This module serves as a dedicated evaluation mechanism for the generated task plans.

- **Safety Judge LLM:** A separate LLM acts as a "judge" that evaluates the final task plan against a predefined set of 15 risk criteria. It quantifies safety violations (e.g., invalidated handoffs, proximity hazards, incomplete action sequences), providing an interpretable safety metric. This approach aims for objective assessment, unlike previous LLM-as-a-Judge applications that sometimes showed bias towards their own generations.

#### 3.4. D. Feedback Module

This module processes the outcomes of the execution phase and provides corrective feedback to the Planning Module, enabling real-time adaptation.

- **Failure Feedback:** If an action fails (due to environmental constraints, insufficient resources, or incorrect sequencing), the module analyzes the failure and sends detailed feedback to the Planning Module for plan refinement.
- **Success Feedback:** Upon successful completion of a subtask, it issues feedback to allow the system to transition to the next phase, maintaining coherence in long-horizon tasks.

In summary, SAFER uses a sophisticated feedback loop involving multiple specialized LLMs for high-level reasoning and a robust CBF-based control system for low-level execution, all monitored and evaluated by a dedicated LLM-as-a-Judge, to achieve truly safety-aware robotic operations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research validated SAFER through extensive experiments, including simulations using the COHERENT benchmark for multi-agent robotic task planning and real-world hardware deployments.

#### 4.1. A. Simulation Results on COHERENT Benchmark

- **Significant Reduction in Safety Violations:**
 - SAFER demonstrated a substantial reduction in Average Safety Violations (ASV) across all five scenes of the COHERENT benchmark compared to non-safety baselines.
 - Using the DeepSeek-r1 model (S-r1), SAFER achieved an average reduction of **77.5%** in ASV.
 - Using the GPT-4o model (S-4o), SAFER reduced ASV by an average of **47%**.
 - This highlights SAFER's effectiveness in enforcing safety rules and its generalizability across different LLMs.
- **Maintenance of Task Efficiency:**
 - While enforcing safety constraints can sometimes lead to increased steps, SAFER largely maintained competitive task efficiency. The increase in Average Steps (AS) was minimal compared to the significant reduction in ASV.
 - For instance, in S-r1, while AS slightly increased, the ASV plummeted, demonstrating a successful trade-off.
- **Latency and Cost Overheads:**
 - The framework introduced only a minimal increase in computational time (latency) and cost per task.
 - The overhead was consistent across models, primarily due to SAFER introducing two API calls per step (one for the Task Planning LLM and one for the Safety Planning LLM).
 - DeepSeek-r1 exhibited higher latency due to its sensitivity to load, but the *relative* overhead introduced by SAFER remained low.

#### 4.2. B. Key Observations (OB❶-OB❹)

The authors provided several insightful observations from their experimental analysis:

- **OB❶: Stronger Reasoning Abilities Lead to Better Safety:** LLMs with superior reasoning capabilities (e.g., DeepSeek-r1 vs. GPT-4o) inherently generated plans with significantly fewer safety violations. This suggests that advanced reasoners can create more structured and foresighted plans, proactively anticipating and mitigating risks.
- **OB❷: LLM-as-a-Safety-Judge Exhibits Fairness:** Contrary to previous research where LLMs acting as judges tended to favor their own responses, the "LLM-as-a-Judge" in SAFER showed objectivity in safety assessment. This is attributed to the safety assessment involving more explicit, rule-based checks, which reduces bias compared to subjective answer validation tasks.
- **OB❸: Multi-LLM Framework Achieves Efficiency-Safety Balance:** SAFER effectively balanced efficiency and safety, significantly reducing violations without introducing excessive execution steps. The multi-LLM approach allowed for informed adjustments that optimized both aspects, rather than sacrificing one for the other.
- **OB❹: LLMs Prioritize Efficiency Over Safety by Default:** Without explicit safety instructions, LLMs naturally generate plans that are quick and efficient but often overlook critical safety details. This underscores the crucial need for frameworks like SAFER to explicitly incorporate safety directives in environments where risk management is paramount.

#### 4.3. C. Real Robot Deployment

- **Successful Hardware Implementation:** SAFER was successfully deployed on a real robotic system involving two mobile manipulators (Kuka IIWA LBR 14 arms on Clearpath Ridgeback mobile bases) and a human.
- **Robust Task Execution with Dynamic Safety:** The system effectively executed a complex task: "Safely transport a cylinder using two collaborating robots while handling human interventions and adjusting speed for non-technical personnel, and place the cylinder in the box."
- **Dynamic Constraint Handling:** The integration of CBFs allowed for real-time handling of dynamic safety constraints, such as maintaining a safe distance from approaching non-technical personnel, and static constraints like avoiding collisions with tables.
- **Seamless LLM-to-Robot Translation:** Custom parsers translated natural language outputs from the LLMs into structured control and safety goals, bridging the gap between high-level plans and low-level robotic actions with high accuracy.
- **Demonstrated Adaptability:** The hardware experiments showcased the framework's ability to adapt to human interventions and successfully complete long-horizon tasks while adhering to safety standards, validating its practical effectiveness.

In summary, the results conclusively demonstrate that SAFER significantly enhances safety in LLM-driven robotics, maintaining efficiency, incurring minimal overhead, and proving robust in both simulated and real-world complex multi-robot scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a highly significant advancement in the field of robotics, particularly at the intersection of artificial intelligence and physical systems. The potential impacts are far-reaching:

- **Enhanced Safety for Autonomous Systems:** The most direct and critical impact is the significant improvement in safety for LLM-driven robotic systems. By systematically embedding safety awareness and formal guarantees, SAFER addresses a major barrier to the widespread deployment of autonomous robots in complex, dynamic, and human-centric environments (e.g., manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, personal assistance). This could drastically reduce accidents and build greater public trust in robotic technologies.
- **Robustness in Complex, Long-Horizon Tasks:** SAFER's multi-LLM architecture and comprehensive feedback mechanisms enable robust planning and execution for complex, long-horizon tasks involving heterogeneous robots. This extends the capabilities of LLM-driven robotics beyond simpler, predefined scenarios, unlocking new possibilities for automation in challenging real-world applications.
- **Bridging High-Level AI and Low-Level Control:** The seamless integration of high-level LLM reasoning with low-level Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) is a crucial methodological contribution. This effectively unifies cognitive planning with provable safety guarantees at the physical control layer, a long-standing challenge in robotics. This approach sets a new standard for how AI-generated plans can be rigorously enforced for safety.
- **Novel Evaluation Paradigm (LLM-as-a-Judge for Safety):** Formalizing "LLM-as-a-Judge" for quantifying safety violations offers a transparent and interpretable metric for evaluating robotic task plans. This could become a standard tool for auditing and continuously improving the safety profiles of autonomous systems, providing insights that go beyond simple pass/fail criteria.
- **Foundation for Future Safety Research:** The findings, particularly the observations (e.g., LLMs prioritizing efficiency, the fairness of LLM-as-a-Judge for safety, the benefits of multi-LLM architectures), provide valuable insights that can guide future research. This includes exploring more dynamic environments, adaptive safety feedback mechanisms, and refining LLM-based evaluation tools.
- **Industrial and Societal Applications:** By making LLM-driven robots safer and more reliable, this work paves the way for their adoption in industries requiring stringent safety standards. This includes collaborative robots working alongside humans, autonomous vehicles, and critical infrastructure maintenance, ultimately contributing to more efficient, adaptable, and safer automated solutions that benefit society.

In conclusion, "Safety Aware Task Planning via Large Language Models in Robotics" offers a pioneering framework that not only pushes the boundaries of LLM capabilities in robotics but also fundamentally tackles the critical issue of safety. Its comprehensive approach, combining multi-LLM intelligence with formal control theory, represents a significant step towards the practical, reliable, and safe deployment of advanced autonomous systems.
