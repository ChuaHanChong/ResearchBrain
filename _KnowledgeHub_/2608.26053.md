# $R^3$: Training Robots to Reason in Natural Language via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> R3 is a two-stage post-training recipe that turns an off-the-shelf 4B VLM into a robotic reasoner steering a frozen language-conditioned low-level policy. It mid-trains on expert reasoning traces, then applies single-step rubric-judged RL (Dr.GRPO) over instruction-only offline data. Free-form language reasoning acts as test-time compute, beating instruction-only imitation on all five held-out Language Table tasks and lifting bimanual grocery-packing success from 38.0% to 47.9%.

## Problem

- Generalist VLA policies inject reasoning only as structured training-time supervision (object boxes, subtask labels, depth tokens), so generating reasoning at test time adds little measured benefit.
- Chain-of-thought gains for VLMs are validated on static VQA, which need not transfer to long-horizon embodied control under partial observability induced by a noisy low-level controller.
- Language-based robot systems lean on hand-designed symbolic plans, code, or templates, while online multi-turn RL for reasoning needs costly rollouts and hard long-horizon credit assignment.

## Method

- Stage I mid-training: next-token ==SFT== on expert-VLM reasoning traces, including unsuccessful trajectories, to initialize reasoning style, progress tracking, constraint use, and recovery behaviors.
- Stage II single-step ==RL== with ==Dr.GRPO== over instruction-only offline data, rewarded by a rubric-guided ==VLM-as-a-judge== scoring semantic match to the expert instruction; exact-match reward for packing.
- Hierarchical policy: a Qwen3.5-4B reasoner conditioned on goal, image, and previous response emits an instruction to a frozen low-level actor, plus repetitive-step filtering and reasoning-context imputation, together forming **R3**.

## Results

- On Language Table, R3 beats instruction-only imitation on all five out-of-distribution tasks (diag_line **+14.2**, iL **+9.9**, mid **+8.7** points) and on V by **+28.3** points.
- Truncating the same checkpoint's reasoning isolates test-time compute: V success falls from **69.2%** to **26.6%** at 50 tokens and **20.3%** with reasoning removed.
- Bimanual grocery packing reaches **47.9%** mean success and **73.1%** progress versus **38.0%** and **65.4%** for imitation; ECoT-style structured traces slightly degraded performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Free-form language reasoning functions as genuine test-time compute for control, not merely auxiliary training supervision: removing it at inference collapses performance that pre-training cannot recover.
> - Mid-training aligns the behavior prior with the expert so RL makes localized refinements; RL from a weak prior instead shifts toward a different, worse mode.
> - Structured visual chain-of-thought helps little when tasks hinge on progress tracking and replanning rather than grounding, and cheap instruction labels suffice to reward reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of natural language reasoning into artificial intelligence models, particularly large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs), has gained attention for its ability to enable models to dedicate increased computational resources to complex problems. This approach, often termed "chain-of-thought" (CoT) reasoning, allows models to decompose tasks, track constraints, and predict future consequences, leading to improved performance across various domains, including visual perception and complex question answering.

In the field of robotic manipulation, several recent developments have explored intermediate representations to enhance robot policy capabilities. These include generalist robot policies and vision-language-action models such as ECoT, SteerVLA, and MolmoAct, which incorporate elements like object-centric annotations, short plans, depth-aware perception tokens, or image-space trajectories. Other complementary approaches expose generalist policies through semantic interfaces, allowing them to be steered at inference time with subtask instructions, visual subgoals, or high-level language commands.

However, a recurring observation in much of this prior work is that while structured reasoning supervision can improve grounding and perception during training, the explicit generation of reasoning at test time often provides limited additional benefit. This suggests that the primary utility of such structured reasoning has been as an *auxiliary training signal* for representation learning, rather than as an active, flexible mechanism for *inference-time compute*. Consequently, there has been an open question regarding whether free-form natural language reasoning, similar to the effective CoT strategies observed in LLMs, could serve as a dynamic test-time computation mechanism for robotic manipulation, and how such a capability could be learned.

This research paper positions itself within this landscape by directly addressing this question. It focuses on training VLMs to generate *free-form natural language reasoning* to guide low-level manipulation policies at test time. The work aims to bridge the gap between effective language reasoning in abstract domains and its practical application for embodied robotic tasks, particularly those requiring long-horizon planning, tracking partial progress, reasoning about object relations, recovering from mistakes, and steering noisy low-level policies. The proposed method, ℛ3, seeks to operationalize natural language reasoning as a dynamic inference-time mechanism, moving beyond its role as a static training-time supervisory signal.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether Vision-Language Models (VLMs) can be trained to reason directly in free-form natural language to guide low-level robotic manipulation policies. This overarching goal is driven by several motivations and is broken down into specific objectives:

**Core Motivation: Leveraging Language Reasoning for Robotic Manipulation:**
The authors observe that natural language reasoning provides a mechanism for models to allocate more computational resources to challenging problems, aiding in decomposition, constraint tracking, and prediction. They hypothesize that this mechanism could be similarly beneficial for robotic manipulation, a domain that inherently requires complex cognitive functions such as interpreting scenes, understanding physical constraints, anticipating action effects, and adapting to unforeseen circumstances. The motivation is to enable robots to "spend test-time compute" on problem instances by engaging in explicit language-based deliberation.

**Addressing the Gap in Existing Robotic Reasoning Methods:**
Prior work in robotic reasoning often employs structured intermediate representations (e.g., object-centric annotations, plans) or uses reasoning-like signals primarily as auxiliary supervision during training. A significant observation from some of these methods is that generating reasoning at test time offers little additional benefit after the model has been trained with reasoning supervision. This paper is motivated to address this gap by exploring whether *free-form language reasoning* can serve as a *test-time compute mechanism* that dynamically guides action, rather than merely improving training-time representations.

**Developing a Practical and Data-Efficient Training Recipe:**
The development of a practical training methodology is a key objective. The authors aim to create a recipe that can turn off-the-shelf VLMs into robotic reasoners without requiring extensive online robot interaction, which is often expensive and time-consuming. This involves:
- **Minimizing Reliance on Expert Reasoning Traces:** While some expert-generated reasoning data is used for initialization, a motivation is to develop a system that can improve from more readily available offline action data, where only expert instructions (without explicit reasoning traces) are present. This addresses the practical constraint that high-quality, human-annotated reasoning traces are costly to obtain at scale.
- **Leveraging Existing Models:** The approach is designed to be a "post-training recipe" that builds upon existing, off-the-shelf VLMs, making it more accessible and applicable to a wider range of foundation models.

**Enabling Robustness and Generalization in Long-Horizon Tasks:**
Robotic manipulation tasks, especially long-horizon ones, necessitate capabilities such as tracking partial progress, reasoning about object relations, recovering from mistakes, and effectively steering noisy low-level policies. The motivation is to imbue VLMs with these capabilities through language reasoning, thereby improving exploration, generalization across unseen tasks, and overall task success.

**Investigating Key Design Choices for Robotic Reasoning:**
Beyond developing a functional system, the research is motivated to empirically study critical design choices that underpin robotic reasoning systems. These include:
- Optimal data collection strategies for reasoning.
- Effective parameterization of the reasoning process.
- Methods for initializing or warm-starting the reasoner.
- Objectives that can improve reasoning capabilities beyond simple memorization of expert behaviors, particularly from action-only supervision.

In summary, the key objectives are to train VLMs for free-form language reasoning in robotics, demonstrate its utility as a test-time compute mechanism, develop a practical and data-efficient training methodology, enhance robotic task performance and generalization, and provide empirical insights into the design of robotic reasoning systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, ℛ3 (Robotic Reasoners via Reinforcement Learning), employs a two-stage post-training recipe to enable Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to reason in natural language and guide a fixed low-level robot policy.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Policy Architecture

The system operates with a hierarchical architecture:
- **Low-level Policy ($\pi_{lo}$):** A pre-trained language-conditioned policy that takes visual and proprioceptive information ($s_t$) and a short-horizon subtask instruction ($u_t$) to output a robot action ($a_t$). This policy remains fixed during ℛ3 training.
- **High-level VLM Reasoner ($\pi_{\theta}$):** A VLM responsible for generating a free-form natural language reasoning trace ($z_t$) and a short-horizon instruction ($u_t$) based on the current observation, a long-horizon goal ($g$), and the interaction history ($x_t$). The goal of ℛ3 is to train this VLM.

#### 3.2. Problem Setup and Environments

Tasks are modeled as decision processes with a textual long-horizon goal ($g$). The methodology is evaluated on two distinct simulation environments:
- **Language Table:** A 2D tabletop environment involving arranging 8 blocks into various spatial patterns. It features cluttered scenes and somewhat imperfect low-level policies, necessitating deliberate reasoning. Tasks are designed to test relational transfer, compositional generalization, and increasing geometric difficulty.
 - **Data Collection:** To obtain diverse behaviors, including recovery from mistakes and imperfect attempts, an expert VLM reasoner (Gemini 3 Flash) is used. It generates reasoning traces and instructions for 14 types of long-horizon block arrangement tasks across multiple scenes. This process yields two data subsets: one with expert reasoning traces (for Stage I) and one with only expert instructions (for Stage II). Interaction history (previous VLM response) is incorporated as input to the VLM to improve performance.
- **Bimanual Grocery Packing:** A simulated dual-arm task involving packing YCB objects into trays. Data for this environment is collected from human operators via teleoperation, with segments labeled with short-horizon instructions. Reasoning traces are not explicitly collected for this environment.

#### 3.3. Stage I: Mid-Training Reasoning Behaviors into the VLM

This stage addresses the observation that off-the-shelf VLMs (even strong open-source ones) often produce shallow or unreliable reasoning for manipulation tasks.
- **Purpose:** To imbue the VLM with a desired reasoning style, including decomposition, constraint tracking, and self-correction. This serves as a warm-start or initialization phase.
- **Data:** A limited subset of expert-generated trajectories from Language Table, which includes explicit reasoning traces ($z_t$) alongside instructions ($u_t$). Both successful and unsuccessful trajectories are used to provide comprehensive supervision on progress tracking, mistakes, and recovery.
- **Objective:** Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) using a standard next-token prediction objective. The VLM learns to predict the concatenated sequence of reasoning and instruction ($y_t = (z_t, u_t)$) given the interaction context ($x_t$) and goal ($g$).
- **Applicability:** This stage is primarily applied to Language Table. For grocery packing, it is skipped as the base VLM's initial reasoning capabilities are considered sufficient.

#### 3.4. Stage II: Rubric-Based Single-Step RL with Offline Data

This stage refines the VLM's reasoning to produce effective instructions, optimizing directly for action outcomes without requiring expensive online robot rollouts.
- **Purpose:** To improve the reasoner's ability to generate instructions that are semantically consistent with expert actions, leveraging a broader dataset of instruction-only demonstrations.
- **Data:** Offline datasets containing interaction history ($x_t$), goal ($g$), and corresponding expert instructions ($u^*_t$), but *without* expert reasoning traces.
- **Process:**
 1. The VLM (initialized from Stage I or the base model) samples its own free-form reasoning ($z_t$) and instruction ($u_t$).
 2. A reward function ($R$) evaluates the quality of the generated ($z_t, u_t$) pair.
 - **Reward Function for Language Table:** A "VLM-as-a-judge" (Qwen3.5-35B-A3B) is used. It evaluates the generated instruction's semantic consistency with the expert's instruction based on rubrics (linguistic match, adverb mismatch, semantic match). A negative reward term is applied to overly short responses to encourage explicit reasoning. The judge is validated against human labels and other VLMs.
 - **Reward Function for Grocery Packing:** A simpler exact-string-match reward is used, as instructions are from a finite, unambiguous set.
- **RL Algorithm:** Dr.GRPO is employed. It optimizes a policy gradient loss by sampling a group of responses, scoring them with the reward function, and computing an advantage.
- **Design Choices and Mitigations:**
 - **Reasoning Context Imputation (Language Table):** Since RL data lacks expert reasoning, the previous reasoning trace (part of $x_t$) is imputed by sampling multiple responses from the mid-trained model. If a sampled response yields the expert's previous instruction, it's used; otherwise, only the previous instruction is provided.
 - **Filtering Repetitive Steps:** Repetitive instruction steps are removed from the RL training data to prevent the VLM from learning reward shortcuts associated with instruction repetition.

This two-stage approach allows for efficient training by leveraging limited, high-quality reasoning traces for initial style guidance (Stage I) and then scaling improvement using more abundant instruction-only data with a practical reward mechanism (Stage II).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of ℛ3 was conducted on both Language Table and a bimanual grocery packing environment, comparing various ℛ3 variants against instruction-only imitation learning baselines.

#### 4.1. Performance on Language Table

- **Overall Performance:** ℛ3 significantly outperformed instruction-only imitation learning (IL) baselines on both in-distribution (mid-training tasks 𝒯M and RL tasks 𝒯R) and out-of-distribution (OOD) held-out tasks (𝒯O).
- **Impact of RL Post-training:** Even without prior mid-training, ℛ3 (RL only) demonstrated substantial performance improvements over the base model across most tasks, including OOD tasks. This indicates that RL, even from instruction-only data, can reinforce beneficial reasoning behaviors.
- **Impact of Mid-training:** Mid-training (Stage I) served as an effective warm-start. Full ℛ3 (mid-training + RL) consistently outperformed ℛ3 (RL only) and the base model across nearly all tasks. A notable finding was that even a quarter of the mid-training data (ℛ3 (1/4th mid)) could yield performance comparable to or exceeding full ℛ3 on OOD tasks, suggesting that a modest amount of reasoning data can provide substantial benefit for generalization.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:** ℛ3 demonstrated superior generalization to OOD tasks compared to instruction-only IL. While IL yielded only minor gains or even degraded performance on several OOD tasks, ℛ3 consistently outperformed IL across all five held-out OOD tasks. This indicates that ℛ3 learns more adaptable reasoning behaviors rather than merely memorizing in-distribution strategies.

#### 4.2. Significance of Inference-Time Reasoning

The research provided three lines of evidence indicating that explicit inference-time reasoning, rather than merely improved representations from reasoning supervision, contributes to performance gains.
- **VQA Diagnostics:** ℛ3 improved performance on Visual Question Answering (VQA) tasks, particularly for relative-position and distance questions (perception) and instruction inference (action-oriented reasoning). However, VQA performance alone did not fully explain manipulation gains; ℛ3 models, despite lower VQA scores than the expert Gemini, achieved comparable manipulation success on many tasks. This suggests that improved static perception is not the sole driver of success, and how the model uses language reasoning to steer the policy is critical.
- **Comparison with Non-Reasoning Policies (Auxiliary Supervision):** Instruction-only imitation learning policies that received reasoning supervision during training (via pre-training or co-training with reasoning traces) but did not generate reasoning at test time generally underperformed full ℛ3 on OOD tasks. This outcome suggests that the benefits of reasoning cannot be fully absorbed as representation improvements and discarded at inference; explicit test-time deliberation is beneficial.
- **Inference-Time Reasoning Budget:** For a fixed ℛ3 checkpoint, increasing the inference-time reasoning budget (e.g., full reasoning vs. truncated reasoning or no reasoning) consistently led to performance gains across several tasks. Furthermore, the model tended to generate longer reasoning traces on more challenging tasks, indicating a dynamic allocation of compute based on task difficulty. These findings provide causal evidence that explicit reasoning at inference time contributes substantially to task success.

#### 4.3. Learned Reasoning Behaviors

Qualitative analysis of reasoning traces revealed specific behaviors learned by ℛ3:
- **Action-Oriented Planning:** The trained reasoner compared multiple alternatives, performed self-correction, re-examined scenes to resolve visual and historical uncertainty (e.g., when objects were occluded), and tracked task progress more effectively.
- **Evolution of Reasoning:** The base model's reasoning was often exploratory but unreliable, with formatting issues and hallucinations. Mid-training largely resolved these interface-level failures, producing terse but reliable reasoning. Subsequent RL refined this into more deliberate, state-aware planning, including restating task constraints, tracking progress, and incremental step selection.
- **Instruction Distribution Alignment:** Mid-training aligned the model's instruction primitive distribution with the expert's, providing a strong behavioral prior. RL then refined this distribution, concentrating on higher-reward strategies and reducing the usage of infrequent or misaligned instruction types.

#### 4.4. Comparison with Structured CoT Templates (ECoT)

- Augmenting free-form language reasoning with structured, vision-centric information (like bounding boxes and object coordinates, as in ECoT) did not provide additional benefits in this setting, sometimes slightly degrading performance. This suggests that for tasks requiring long-horizon progress tracking, error recovery, and closed-loop replanning, free-form language reasoning is more beneficial than explicit low-level visual grounding information.

#### 4.5. Experiments on Bimanual Grocery Packing

- **Generality:** R3 (RL only), initialized directly from the base VLM (skipping mid-training), achieved substantially higher overall success and normalized task progress compared to instruction-only imitation learning. This demonstrates the generality of the ℛ3 recipe to a different, bimanual, long-horizon manipulation task and supports the finding that Stage I can be omitted if the base VLM's initial reasoning is already functional. Qualitative examples showcased similar action-oriented behaviors, such as tracking progress and correcting object localization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces ℛ3, a novel methodology for training Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to employ flexible natural language reasoning as an active computational mechanism at inference time for robotic manipulation. The significance and potential impact of this work can be delineated as follows:

- **Advancing Robotic Generalization and Robustness:** The study provides empirical evidence that training VLMs to reason in free-form natural language significantly enhances generalization, particularly to out-of-distribution tasks, compared to traditional instruction-only imitation learning. This suggests a pathway towards more robust and adaptable robotic policies capable of handling novel scenarios, environmental variations, and unexpected challenges, which is crucial for real-world deployment.

- **Operationalizing Language as Test-Time Compute:** Unlike prior approaches that primarily utilized structured reasoning as an auxiliary training signal to improve learned representations, ℛ3 demonstrates that explicit, free-form language reasoning can serve as a dynamic test-time compute mechanism. This allows robots to deliberate, plan, and self-correct online, allocating computational effort commensurate with task complexity and uncertainty. This paradigm shift could unlock new levels of cognitive ability for embodied AI.

- **Practical and Data-Efficient Training:** The two-stage training recipe of ℛ3 offers practical advantages. By combining initial mid-training on limited expert reasoning traces with subsequent reinforcement learning from more abundant offline action-only data, the method reduces reliance on expensive, labor-intensive annotation of reasoning for every training instance. This makes the process more scalable and applicable to broader datasets.

- **Enhanced Interpretability and Debuggability:** The generation of natural language reasoning traces provides an interpretable window into the robot's decision-making process. This transparency can be invaluable for human operators to understand why a robot chooses a particular action, diagnose failures, and build trust in autonomous systems. Such interpretability is a significant step towards more transparent and accountable AI in robotics.

- **Unlocking Complex Cognitive Behaviors:** The qualitative analysis of learned reasoning behaviors (e.g., comparing alternatives, self-correction, resolving visual ambiguity, dynamic progress tracking) indicates that ℛ3 enables VLMs to exhibit more sophisticated cognitive functions relevant to long-horizon, open-world manipulation. These capabilities are fundamental for truly intelligent robotic agents.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays a foundation for several promising research directions:
 - **Real-World Deployment:** Extending ℛ3 to real robots would test its robustness against real-world complexities such as noisy perception and physical uncertainties.
 - **Online and Multi-Turn Reinforcement Learning:** Moving beyond single-step offline RL to multi-turn online RL with feedback from task completion or human preferences could further optimize long-horizon behaviors and close the performance gap with surrogate objectives.
 - **Joint Training of Reasoning and Action:** Investigating co-training of the high-level reasoner and low-level policy could improve coordination and potentially enable reasoning to directly influence low-level action generation, moving beyond fixed low-level controllers.
 - **Qualitative Exploration:** Free-form reasoning could provide a natural mechanism for enabling robots to engage in qualitatively broader exploration, adapting to novel objects, embodiments, and failure modes not encountered during initial training, thereby pushing the boundaries of autonomous learning.

In conclusion, ℛ3 represents a contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by demonstrating the efficacy of training VLMs for inference-time natural language reasoning. Its potential to improve generalization, robustness, interpretability, and cognitive capabilities positions it as a significant step toward more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems.
