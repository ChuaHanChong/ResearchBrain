# ManiSkill-HAB: A Benchmark for Low-Level Manipulation in Home Rearrangement Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> MANISKILL-HAB is a new benchmark that offers a GPU-accelerated simulation platform for low-level mobile manipulation in home rearrangement tasks. It achieves a 3.08x simulation speedup over Habitat 2.0 while enabling realistic contact-rich interactions and introduces a novel automated system for scalable, controlled demonstration data generation.

## Problem

- Existing embodied AI benchmarks often faced trade-offs between simulation speed, physical realism (e.g., using 'magical grasping'), and environmental complexity.
- Developing realistic low-level control policies for complex, home-scale manipulation was hindered by the lack of high-fidelity, fast simulators that avoided abstracted grasping.
- Generating large-scale, high-quality demonstration datasets for robot learning was either too costly, prone to artifacts, or limited in its ability to enforce specific behaviors without manual effort.

## Method

- Implemented a ==GPU-accelerated== version of Habitat's Home Assistant Benchmark (HAB) using the **ManiSkill3** framework, replacing ==magical grasp== with ==realistic low-level control== for a Fetch mobile manipulator.
- Provided a comprehensive set of three ==long-horizon== home rearrangement tasks (TidyHouse, PrepareGroceries, SetTable) decomposed into low-level manipulation subtasks like Pick, Place, Open, and Close.
- Developed an automated, ==rule-based trajectory filtering== system that leverages privileged simulator information to generate large, high-quality demonstration datasets with customizable behavioral biases.

## Results

- MS-HAB achieves **4299.18 samples per second (SPS)** during interaction, a **3.08x** speedup over Habitat 2.0, while using **32% less** GPU memory.
- Per-object Reinforcement Learning policies demonstrated **10-15%** higher success rates for Pick subtasks and up to **7%** higher for Place subtasks compared to all-object policies.
- Long-horizon task progressive completion rates remain low, and even simplified tasks with relaxed collision/placement requirements achieved **less than 20%** success, underscoring the challenge of fundamental low-level whole-body control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Realistic low-level mobile manipulation for home rearrangement, even with extensive training, results in low overall task success rates, indicating the substantial difficulty of whole-body control in cluttered environments.
> - Training per-object policies significantly improves subtask success rates for pick and place compared to all-object policies, demonstrating the benefit of object-specific geometric knowledge for robust manipulation.
> - The automated trajectory categorization system is effective in filtering demonstrations to bias the behavior of imitation learning policies, providing a scalable method for controlling data characteristics.
