# ManiSkill-HAB: A Benchmark for Low-Level Manipulation in Home Rearrangement Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> MANISKILL-HAB is a new benchmark that offers a GPU-accelerated simulation platform for low-level mobile manipulation in home rearrangement tasks. It achieves a 3.08x simulation speedup over Habitat 2.0 while enabling realistic contact-rich interactions and introduces a novel automated system for scalable, controlled demonstration data generation.

## Problem

- Existing embodied AI benchmarks often faced trade-offs between simulation speed, physical realism (e.g., using 'magical grasping'), and environmental complexity.
- Developing realistic low-level control policies for complex, home-scale manipulation was hindered by the lack of high-fidelity, fast simulators that avoided abstracted grasping.
- Generating large-scale, high-quality demonstration datasets for robot learning was either too costly, prone to artifacts, or limited in its ability to enforce specific behaviors without manual effort.

## Method

- Implemented a ==GPU-accelerated== version of Habitat's Home Assistant Benchmark (HAB) using the **ManiSkill3** framework, replacing ==magical grasp== with ==realistic low-level control== for a Fetch mobile manipulator.
- Provided a comprehensive set of three ==long-horizon== home rearrangement tasks (TidyHouse, PrepareGroceries, SetTable) decomposed into low-level manipulation subtasks like Pick, Place, Open, and Close.
- Developed an automated, ==rule-based trajectory filtering== system that leverages privileged simulator information to generate large, high-quality demonstration datasets with customizable behavioral biases.

## Results

- MS-HAB achieves **4299.18 samples per second (SPS)** during interaction, a **3.08x** speedup over Habitat 2.0, while using **32% less** GPU memory.
- Per-object Reinforcement Learning policies demonstrated **10-15%** higher success rates for Pick subtasks and up to **7%** higher for Place subtasks compared to all-object policies.
- Long-horizon task progressive completion rates remain low, and even simplified tasks with relaxed collision/placement requirements achieved **less than 20%** success, underscoring the challenge of fundamental low-level whole-body control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Realistic low-level mobile manipulation for home rearrangement, even with extensive training, results in low overall task success rates, indicating the substantial difficulty of whole-body control in cluttered environments.
> - Training per-object policies significantly improves subtask success rates for pick and place compared to all-object policies, demonstrating the benefit of object-specific geometric knowledge for robust manipulation.
> - The automated trajectory categorization system is effective in filtering demonstrations to bias the behavior of imitation learning policies, providing a scalable method for controlling data characteristics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI and robotics is rapidly advancing, with a long-term goal of creating intelligent agents that can perform complex tasks in human-centric environments. A critical bottleneck in this progress is the availability of high-quality benchmarks and scalable simulation platforms that can support realistic robot-environment interactions. Existing benchmarks often face trade-offs between simulation speed, physical realism, environmental complexity, and data generation capabilities.

Earlier simulators like AI2-THOR and iGibson focused on navigation and scene-level interaction, sometimes incorporating kinematic object state transitions or diverse scene randomization. However, these often came at the cost of simulation speed or lacked detailed low-level control. Habitat 2.0 (Szot et al., 2021) achieved best-in-class single-process simulation speed for rigid-body dynamics and articulations, but critically simplified manipulation through "magical grasping"—a teleportation-like mechanism that abstracts away the complexities of real-world contact-rich manipulation. This limitation severely hinders research into realistic low-level control policies. Other simulators, such as RoboCasa, offer physical simulation, diverse assets, and human demonstrations but still struggle with high simulation speeds while rendering, and lack mechanisms for behavior-specific data filtering.

On the data front, real-world robot datasets (e.g., RT-1, DROID) are invaluable for direct deployment but are inherently limited by the immense human effort and cost required for collection and scaling. Generative interactive world models offer some interactivity but suffer from artifacts and long-term memory issues, making them unsuitable for home-scale rearrangement and high-frequency low-level control. Classical physical simulation provides scalable data generation but often on smaller-scale continuous control tasks.

MS-HAB steps into this landscape by aiming to unify several critical features: high-speed, GPU-accelerated simulation, realistic low-level control (eschewing magical grasping), complex home-scale rearrangement tasks (from Habitat's Home Assistant Benchmark), extensive baselines, and a novel method for scalable, controlled data generation. By addressing the limitations of prior work—particularly Habitat 2.0's magical grasp and other simulators' speed-realism trade-offs—MS-HAB provides a more holistic and challenging platform that is essential for advancing research in truly embodied, adaptable robotic agents. It pushes the frontier by enabling researchers to train and evaluate policies that can handle the nuanced physical interactions crucial for real-world deployment, while maintaining the scalability necessary for deep reinforcement learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind MS-HAB is to bridge existing gaps in embodied AI benchmarks by providing a unified platform that supports realistic and efficient research into complex robotic manipulation. The authors identify a critical need for benchmarks that combine fast simulation, intricate test environments, and large, controllable demonstration datasets, especially as frontier tasks in robotics become more advanced.

The key objectives of MS-HAB are four-fold:

1. **Fast Simulation with Realistic Physics and Low-Level Control:** To implement a GPU-accelerated version of the Home Assistant Benchmark (HAB) using ManiSkill3, enabling significantly faster simulation speeds compared to prior CPU-based implementations. A crucial aspect is to replace HAB's "magical grasp" with realistic low-level control for grasping, manipulation, and interaction, which is vital for developing policies transferable to the real world.
2. **Home-Scale Manipulation Tasks:** To provide a comprehensive set of complex, long-horizon object rearrangement tasks within realistic, apartment-scale environments, inherited from the HAB. This ensures that the benchmark addresses practical challenges faced by household robots.
3. **Extensive Baselines:** To train and provide extensive Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Imitation Learning (IL) baselines. These baselines serve as reference points for future research, allowing new methodologies to be rigorously compared against established performance levels.
4. **Scalable, Controlled Data Generation:** To develop an automated, rule-based trajectory filtering system that leverages privileged simulator information. This system aims to efficiently generate large, high-quality demonstration datasets, where specific criteria for robot behavior and safety can be enforced without manual labor, thus accelerating data-driven learning for complex robotic skills.

In essence, the authors are motivated to create a benchmark that not only poses challenging problems (home rearrangement with low-level control) but also provides the computational efficiency and data generation tools necessary for the community to effectively tackle these problems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of MS-HAB is built upon integrating and extending high-performance simulation with advanced robot learning techniques and novel data generation tools.

#### 3.1. Environment Design and GPU Acceleration

The core of MS-HAB involves a GPU-accelerated implementation of Habitat's Home Assistant Benchmark (HAB) using the ManiSkill3 framework. This enabled a significant speedup in simulation, achieving over 4300 samples per second (SPS) while rendering two 128x128 RGB-D images and actively colliding with multiple dynamic objects. This is notably 3x faster than Habitat 2.0's peak performance, with 32% less GPU memory usage. Key design choices include:
- **Realistic Low-Level Control:** Unlike Habitat 2.0's "magical grasp," MS-HAB supports full low-level control of the Fetch mobile manipulator, including its arm, torso, and head joints, and base linear/angular velocities. This necessitates learning realistic contact-rich grasping and manipulation.
- **Environment Variants:** Two types of environments are provided: `SequentialTask` for evaluation (supports parallel execution of subtasks, physics, and rendering) and `{SubtaskName}SubtaskTrain` for training (adds dense rewards, randomization, and specific features for learning skills).
- **Observation and Action Space:** Policies receive target object pose, goal position, TCP pose, grasped indicator, 128x128 depth images from head and arm cameras, and robot proprioception. The action space is continuous and normalized for PD joint delta position control for arm/torso/head and velocity control for the base.
- **ReplicaCAD Integration:** The ReplicaCAD dataset is used for apartment scenes. Crucially, original collision meshes for articulated objects (e.g., fridge, drawers) were modified to include handles to support realistic grasping, and navmeshes were generated.

#### 3.2. Task and Subtask Decomposition

MS-HAB utilizes the three long-horizon rearrangement tasks from HAB: TidyHouse, PrepareGroceries, and SetTable. These are decomposed into a sequence of parameterized subtasks: Pick, Place, Open, Close, and Navigate. For the scope of this work, navigation is simplified to a teleport function to focus on low-level manipulation. Each subtask has specific success/failure conditions and a cumulative force threshold to enforce safety.

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Learning (RL) Baselines

The authors train individual mobile manipulation skills using RL, which benefits from the fast simulation without requiring prior demonstration data.
- **Algorithms:** SAC (Soft Actor-Critic) is used for Pick and Place policies, while PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization) is used for Open and Close policies. This choice was justified by ablation studies showing SAC's superior performance for complex manipulation tasks requiring a large replay buffer.
- **Policy Types:** To handle diverse object geometries and tight manipulation tolerances, "per-object" Pick and Place policies are trained (overfitting to a specific object's geometry), alongside "all-object" policies for comparison. Open and Close policies are trained per articulation (fridge/drawer).
- **Training Details:** Policies are trained across multiple seeds, with extensive environment samples (1.83 billion samples for 150 policies). Dense, hand-engineered rewards are provided. Place subtasks are initialized by sampling grasp poses from trained Pick policies, addressing the lack of "magical grasp" for initial conditions.
- **Metrics:** Subtask policies are evaluated by "success once rate" (achieving success at least once within 200 timesteps). Long-horizon tasks are evaluated by "Progressive Completion Rate," where each subtask's success depends on the preceding ones.

#### 3.4. Automated Trajectory Categorization and Dataset Generation

A novel rule-based system is developed to filter demonstrations without manual labor, ensuring scalable and controlled data generation for imitation learning.
- **Event Labeling:** Privileged information from the simulator is used to distill trajectories into chronologically ordered lists of events (e.g., `Contact`, `Grasped`, `Dropped`, `Excessive Collisions`).
- **Success/Failure Modes:** Mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive success and failure modes are defined based on these event lists (e.g., "straightforward success" for Pick requires `(Contact, Grasp, Success)` without `Dropped` or `Excessive Collisions`).
- **Dataset Filtering:** This system allows users to filter generated demonstrations based on specific behavioral biases or safety constraints (e.g., generating datasets only with "place in goal" successes, excluding "drop to goal").
- **Imitation Learning (IL) Baselines:** Behavior Cloning (BC) is used to train IL policies on these filtered datasets, demonstrating how data filtering influences learned behavior.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several key findings across simulation performance, policy capabilities, and data generation.

#### 4.1. Simulation Performance

- **Speed and Efficiency:** MS-HAB significantly outperforms Habitat 2.0 in the `Interact` benchmark. It achieves **4299.18 SPS** (samples per second) with 15.35 GB VRAM usage, representing a **3.08x speedup** compared to Habitat's 1397.65 SPS at 22.60 GB VRAM. This efficiency is crucial for large-scale RL training and data generation.
- **Realistic Rendering:** With ray-tracing enabled, MS-HAB achieves **69.90 SPS** while rendering, which is 3.51x faster than Behavior-1K (19.92 SPS) at comparable visual fidelity and lower GPU memory usage.

#### 4.2. Reinforcement Learning Baselines

- **Low Overall Success Rates:** Despite extensive training, the progressive completion rates for long-horizon tasks remain low (e.g., TidyHouse, PrepareGroceries, SetTable). This indicates the inherent difficulty of low-level, whole-body mobile manipulation with realistic physics and collision constraints in cluttered home environments.
- **Per-Object vs. All-Object Policies:** Per-object RL policies demonstrate **notably improved subtask success rates** compared to all-object policies. This advantage is most pronounced in Pick tasks involving many diverse objects (e.g., +10-15% for TidyHouse/PrepareGroceries Pick) and Place tasks with tight tolerances (e.g., +7% for PrepareGroceries Place). This suggests that overfitting to specific object geometries is beneficial for robust manipulation. Ablation on the Cracker Box confirms per-object policies are significantly better at avoiding collisions and grasping failures.
- **Generalization Challenges:** The Close Fridge policy completely fails on validation scenes (0% success) because the environment setup places the fridge door against a wall, preventing the robot arm from reaching the handle. This highlights a need for greater scene diversity or more robust policies capable of handling unforeseen environmental constraints, which were not an issue with magical grasping.
- **Impact of Collision Thresholds (Appendix A.4.4):** Evaluating policies under stricter (real-world relevant) cumulative collision thresholds (e.g., 1400 N, an industry safety standard) reveals a **5-20% drop in success rates**. This underscores the challenge of developing safe and reliable policies for real-world deployment.

#### 4.3. Imitation Learning Baselines

- **Performance Gap with RL:** IL policies (trained with Behavior Cloning) perform notably worse than their RL counterparts, especially for Pick and Place subtasks. This suggests that simple BC struggles with the multimodal nature of expert demonstrations and the complexities of low-level control.
- **Effectiveness of Data Filtering:** Experiments demonstrate that filtering demonstrations using the automated trajectory categorization system can effectively **bias IL policy behavior**. For instance, training with "place in goal only" demonstrations leads to policies that prioritize placing within the goal, though some opposite behaviors still manifest. This proves the utility of the filtering system for controlling data characteristics.
- **Dataset Size (Appendix A.4.1):** Increasing the number of demonstrations per object from 1 to 1000 consistently improves IL policy performance, highlighting the importance of scalable data generation.

#### 4.4. Task Simplification (Appendix A.4.2)

Even when long-horizon tasks are simplified by removing all collision requirements and relaxing Place subtask success conditions (allowing placement anywhere on the target receptacle), overall task success rates remain below 20%. This critically emphasizes that the largest challenge lies in fundamental low-level whole-body control rather than just collision avoidance or precise placement.

#### 4.5. Diffusion Policy Baselines (Appendix A.4.6)

Diffusion Policy (DP) baselines, despite being a more advanced IL method, surprisingly perform worse than BC baselines, especially in Pick tasks. This suggests that existing DP architectures or default hyperparameter tuning might not be sufficient for the complex, contact-rich manipulation and strict safety constraints of ManiSkill-HAB, indicating a need for more tailored approaches (e.g., diffusion transformers, task-specific tuning, online fine-tuning).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MANISKILL-HAB represents a significant contribution to the embodied AI and robotics research community due to its comprehensive and forward-looking approach to benchmarking.

#### 5.1. Advancing Realistic Embodied AI

- **Bridging the Reality Gap:** By replacing "magical grasping" with realistic low-level control and physics, MS-HAB forces policies to learn contact-rich interactions crucial for real-world robotics. This is a vital step toward developing deployable solutions that can handle the complexities of physical environments.
- **Holistic Challenge:** It combines high-fidelity simulation, complex home-scale tasks, and realistic manipulation, presenting a holistic challenge that pushes the boundaries of current robot learning methods. The low baseline success rates, even with extensive training, highlight the significant room for improvement and point to critical areas for future research in whole-body control, skill chaining, and navigation in cluttered spaces.

#### 5.2. Enabling Scalable Research

- **Unprecedented Simulation Speed:** The GPU-accelerated implementation provides researchers with a platform that can generate billions of environmental samples in fast wall-clock time, accelerating the iterative development and training cycles for deep reinforcement learning and imitation learning.
- **Controlled Data Generation:** The automated trajectory categorization and filtering system is a game-changer for data-driven robotics. It allows for the scalable generation of high-quality, behavior-specific datasets without tedious manual labeling. This capability will empower researchers to investigate how different demonstration characteristics influence policy learning and safety.

#### 5.3. Foundational Benchmark and Resource

- **Extensive Baselines:** The provided RL and IL baselines serve as a strong starting point for the community, offering benchmarks for comparison and illustrating the current state-of-the-art on these challenging tasks. The detailed ablation studies offer insights into the difficulty of specific manipulation problems and the utility of techniques like per-object policy training.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The commitment to open-sourcing all code, environments, models, and data (including ray-tracing capabilities and comparisons with other visual simulators) fosters transparency, reproducibility, and collaborative advancement within the community.

#### 5.4. Identification of Research Frontiers

The results clearly delineate current limitations, such as the struggle of IL with multimodal data, the challenge of generalization in diverse scene configurations (e.g., Close Fridge failure), and the performance drop under strict safety constraints. These findings directly inform future research directions, encouraging the development of more robust learning algorithms, advanced policy architectures (e.g., diffusion transformers with online fine-tuning), better reward design, and improved data augmentation strategies.

**Limitations Acknowledged:** The authors are transparent about current limitations, stating that they do not claim transfer to real robots and acknowledge significant room for improvement in their baselines. This sets realistic expectations and invites further community engagement.

In conclusion, MANISKILL-HAB is not merely another benchmark; it is a meticulously crafted platform designed to accelerate fundamental research in realistic embodied AI. Its unique combination of speed, fidelity, task complexity, and intelligent data generation capabilities positions it as a critical resource for the next generation of robotic learning algorithms.
