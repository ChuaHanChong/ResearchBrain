# Stepwise Credit Assignment for GRPO on Flow-Matching Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Carnegie Mellon University and Adobe Research introduced Stepwise-Flow-GRPO, an approach that refines reinforcement learning for text-to-image flow-matching models by using stepwise, gain-based credit assignment. This method, combined with an improved DDIM-style stochastic differential equation for sampling, demonstrates superior sample efficiency and faster convergence, leading to enhanced generative quality, particularly in compositional understanding.

## Problem

- Applying reinforcement learning to text-to-image (T2I) flow-matching models faced limitations due to uniform credit assignment, where the final image reward was applied equally to all denoising steps, ignoring the temporal generation hierarchy.
- This uniform approach often reinforced suboptimal intermediate decisions, leading to inefficient learning as early steps determine low-frequency composition and later steps refine high-frequency details.
- The stochastic differential equations (SDEs) used in prior flow-matching RL methods produced noisy intermediate samples, degrading the quality and reliability of reward signals from pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs).

## Method

- Developed **Stepwise-Flow-GRPO**, which assigns credit based on '==stepwise gains=='—the marginal reward improvement achieved at each denoising step—rather than solely on the final image's reward.
- Introduced an improved DDIM-style stochastic differential equation (SDE) for sample generation that produces less noisy intermediate images while preserving necessary stochasticity for policy gradient exploration.
- Utilized Tweedie's formula to estimate clean intermediate images, optionally refined by a few ODE substeps, to enable accurate evaluation of stepwise rewards for gain computation.

## Results

- Stepwise-Flow-GRPO achieved higher rewards per training iteration and faster convergence in total wall-clock time compared to Flow-GRPO across various reward functions and datasets.
- On the GenEval benchmark, the method surpassed Flow-GRPO, particularly in compositional understanding tasks like counting and spatial positioning, demonstrating improved generative quality at higher classifier-free guidance scales.
- Demonstrated enhanced training stability and superior final performance, notably on challenging OCR text rendering tasks, and trained stably with complex VLM-based reward models (e.g., UnifiedReward) where Flow-GRPO diverged.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Assigning credit based on the marginal reward improvement at each denoising step significantly enhances sample efficiency and training stability compared to uniform credit assignment, especially for hierarchical generative tasks.
> - Joint normalization of stepwise gains across all steps and trajectories within a batch proves more effective for convergence speed than per-step normalization, as it preserves the natural magnitude differences of gains at different temporal stages.
> - A carefully designed DDIM-inspired SDE that produces higher-quality, less noisy intermediate samples contributes complementarily to performance by providing a more stable and informative reward signal for the RL agent.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of generative artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on the application of reinforcement learning (RL) to text-to-image (T2I) generation models, particularly those based on flow-matching techniques.

Reinforcement learning has been successfully applied to enhance the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs). However, its adaptation to T2I flow-matching models, such as SD-3.5-Medium, presents distinct challenges. Previous efforts, including Flow-GRPO and Dance-GRPO, demonstrated the feasibility of applying policy gradients to these models by converting deterministic flow ordinary differential equations (ODEs) into stochastic differential equations (SDEs) with matched marginals. These methods, however, employ a uniform credit assignment mechanism, where the advantage—derived solely from the reward of the final generated image—is applied equally to all steps in a denoising trajectory.

The paper identifies a limitation in this uniform credit assignment: it disregards the inherent temporal structure of the diffusion generation process. Early denoising steps are primarily responsible for establishing low-frequency information, such as composition and layout, while later steps refine high-frequency details and textures. Assigning uniform credit based on the final image quality can inadvertently reinforce suboptimal intermediate decisions if those errors are subsequently corrected later in the diffusion process. This can lead to inefficient learning and potentially hinder the model's ability to achieve optimal performance in terms of image-text alignment and aesthetic quality.

The research aligns with ongoing efforts to improve T2I models by addressing issues such as precise image-text alignment and aesthetic fidelity, which existing methods like attention manipulation, contrastive learning, or asynchronous diffusion have partially addressed. RL has emerged as a promising direction, leveraging pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) for reward assessment.

This work directly builds upon and seeks to improve Flow-GRPO by tackling its credit assignment shortcomings. It also acknowledges concurrent research, such as TempFlow-GRPO and Granular-GRPO, which similarly recognize the disproportionate impact of early denoising steps but propose different solutions, such as noise-level weighting or optimizing only a subset of steps. The current work differentiates itself by proposing a data-driven approach based on telescoping reward gains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and underlying motivations of this research are detailed as follows:

- **To address the limitations of uniform credit assignment in reinforcement learning for flow-matching models:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing methods, like Flow-GRPO, assign the same advantage to all denoising steps based solely on the final generated image. This approach overlooks the temporal hierarchy inherent in diffusion generation. Early steps dictate broad compositional elements and low-frequency structures, while later steps resolve intricate details and high-frequency textures. Figure 1 visually illustrates this, showing non-monotonic reward curves for intermediate steps that uniform credit assignment would ignore. Consequently, this uniform approach can inadvertently reward or penalize an entire trajectory, including suboptimal intermediate decisions that might have been corrected later, leading to inefficient learning and potentially reinforcing undesirable behaviors.
- **To improve sample efficiency and accelerate convergence of RL fine-tuning for flow models:**
 - **Motivation:** By introducing a more granular and accurate credit assignment mechanism, the researchers aim to provide finer-grained guidance to the policy. This more precise feedback at each denoising step is hypothesized to enable the model to learn effective generation strategies more rapidly. Faster convergence and superior sample efficiency would translate into reduced computational costs and quicker development cycles for high-quality T2I models.
- **To enhance the quality of the reward signal by producing less noisy samples during stochastic generation:**
 - **Motivation:** The Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) used in Flow-GRPO, while ensuring marginal matching with the deterministic ODE, can generate visually noisy intermediate images during the sampling process. Since reward models are typically trained on clean, high-quality images, evaluating rewards on these noisy samples can degrade the informativeness and reliability of the reward signal. This degradation can impede the RL optimization process. The objective is to introduce an improved SDE that yields higher-quality, less noisy samples while maintaining the necessary stochasticity for policy gradient exploration, thereby providing a more accurate and stable reward signal.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Stepwise-Flow-GRPO, addresses the identified problems through two main innovations: a stepwise credit assignment mechanism and an improved SDE for sample generation.

**Stepwise Credit Assignment:**
The core of Stepwise-Flow-GRPO lies in assigning credit based on the reward improvement at each denoising step rather than solely on the final image reward.
1. **Intermediate Reward Estimation:** Direct evaluation of reward models on noisy intermediate states ($x_t$) is not practical. To overcome this, the method uses Tweedie's formula to estimate the underlying clean image, $\hat{x}_0(t) := \mathbb{E}[x_0|x_t]$. This formula provides a prediction of the clean image based on the noisy state $x_t$ and the predicted noise $\hat{x}_1$ (which is already computed during the generation process). For higher-quality estimates, the researchers denoise from $x_t$ towards $x_0$ over a small number of ($T'$) substeps (default $T'=5$) using the flow ODE.
2. **Stepwise Rewards:** The reward for each intermediate clean image estimate is then calculated: $r_t := R(\hat{x}_0(t), \text{prompt})$. These rewards quantify the quality of the image at different stages of the denoising process.
3. **Gain-Based Advantages:** Instead of optimizing these stepwise rewards directly, the method computes stepwise gains, $g_t := r_{t-1} - r_t$. These gains measure the marginal reward improvement achieved by each denoising step. A positive gain indicates an improvement, while a negative gain indicates a degradation in reward. A crucial property of these gains is that they telescope: $\sum_{t=1}^T g_t = r_0 - r_T$, implying that maximizing stepwise gains is equivalent to maximizing the overall improvement from the initial noise to the final image.
4. **Joint Normalization:** To compute group-relative advantages for GRPO, the gains are transformed. A key design choice is joint normalization, where the mean and standard deviation are calculated across all steps and trajectories within a group. This preserves the relative magnitudes of gains across different steps (e.g., larger gains in early compositional steps), rather than normalizing them away, which an ablation study indicated was beneficial for convergence speed. The advantage at step $t$ of trajectory $i$ is denoted as $\tilde{A}^i_t$.
5. **Policy Optimization:** The policy is updated using a modified GRPO objective function. The main difference from standard Flow-GRPO is that the uniform advantages ($A^i$) are replaced with the stepwise advantages ($\tilde{A}^i_t$). The objective includes propensity ratio clipping and KL regularization to stabilize policy gradient optimization, anchoring the learned policy to an initial reference policy.

**Improved Sampling via DDIM-Style Updates:**
The paper also introduces a new SDE to address the issue of noisy samples generated by Flow-GRPO's original SDE, which can degrade reward signal quality.
1. **DDIM-Inspired SDE:** A DDIM-style sampling strategy is adopted, which interpolates between deterministic ODE sampling and stochastic exploration. The update rule for $x_{t-\Delta t}$ is formulated to match the marginals of the rectified flow, while allowing for controlled stochasticity.
2. **Variance-Preserving Property:** This improved SDE formulation is designed to preserve the marginal variance at each step, unlike Flow-GRPO's SDE which can inflate it, leading to noisier samples.
3. **Adaptive Noise Schedule:** The stochasticity is controlled by a noise schedule $\sigma_t = \eta(t-\Delta t)\sqrt{1-t}$, where $\eta$ controls the exploration strength. This schedule injects maximum stochasticity at $t=1$ (initial heavy noise) and smoothly anneals to $\sigma_t \rightarrow 0$ as $t \rightarrow 0$ (deterministic ODE recovery), ensuring controlled noise injection throughout.
4. **Gaussian Policy:** This SDE results in a Gaussian policy, $\pi_\theta(x_{t-1}|x_t, c) = \mathcal{N}(x_{t-1}; \mu_t, \sigma_t^2 I)$, allowing for the computation of importance ratios and KL penalties required for GRPO. This approach aims to produce higher-quality samples for reward evaluation, providing a cleaner signal for optimization.

**Connection to Submodular Optimization:** The authors also draw a connection between their greedy gain maximization and adaptive submodular optimization, suggesting that under certain submodularity assumptions on reward gains, such a greedy approach can lead to provably near-optimal policies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented several findings demonstrating the efficacy of Stepwise-Flow-GRPO:

- **Superior Sample Efficiency:** Stepwise-Flow-GRPO consistently achieved higher rewards per training iteration compared to standard Flow-GRPO across all evaluated reward functions (PickScore, ImageReward, UnifiedReward) and datasets (GenEval, PickScore Dataset). This indicated faster convergence and, in 3 out of 4 settings, superior final performance.
- **Improved Wall-Clock Efficiency:** Despite an increased computational cost per iteration due to intermediate denoising and reward evaluation (approximately 1.8-2.4 times more generation time and varying reward evaluation overheads depending on the reward model, e.g., 10x for ImageReward), the enhanced sample efficiency compensated for this. The method converged faster in total wall-clock time, achieving target reward values with less overall computation.
- **Enhanced Generative Quality (GenEval Benchmark):**
 - On the GenEval benchmark, models trained with Stepwise-Flow-GRPO matched Flow-GRPO at a classifier-free guidance (cfg) scale of 1.0.
 - At `cfg=4.5`, Stepwise-Flow-GRPO outperformed Flow-GRPO across most categories, notably in compositional understanding tasks such as counting and spatial positioning.
 - Qualitative evaluations (Figure 3) demonstrated improvements in spatial reasoning, attribute binding, and counting capabilities, producing images that appeared more realistic and physically plausible. Examples highlighted issues in Flow-GRPO like object merging or unrealistic placement, which Stepwise-Flow-GRPO mitigated.
- **Complementary Improvements from Improved SDE:** When both methods utilized the newly introduced DDIM-inspired SDE (which produces less noisy samples), Stepwise-Flow-GRPO maintained its sample efficiency advantage over Flow-GRPO. This indicated that the benefits from stepwise credit assignment and cleaner sample generation were complementary, each contributing to overall performance.
- **Robustness to Design Choices:**
 - **Number of Denoising Substeps ($T'$):** Ablation studies showed that Stepwise-Flow-GRPO's performance was robust to the number of ODE substeps ($T'$) used for intermediate clean image estimation (e.g., $T' \in \{2, 5, 8\}$), with similar convergence speeds and final performance.
 - **Gain Normalization Strategy:** Joint normalization of gains (across all steps and trajectories) significantly outperformed per-step normalization in convergence speed, underscoring the importance of preserving the natural temporal magnitude structure of gains.
- **Extended Training and Stability Benefits:**
 - **OCR Text Rendering:** On a challenging OCR text rendering task, Stepwise-Flow-GRPO substantially outperformed Flow-GRPO, which diverged after approximately 500 steps. This demonstrated enhanced stability and superior final performance in hierarchical compositional tasks.
 - **Long-Term GenEval Performance:** Over extended training (400 GPU hours), Stepwise-Flow-GRPO achieved an overall GenEval score of 0.87, which surpassed both Flow-GRPO (0.72) and state-of-the-art autoregressive models like GPT-4o (0.84 at the time). The performance gap widened with prolonged training, particularly in difficult categories like counting (0.89), spatial positioning (0.73), and attribute binding (0.80).
 - **UnifiedReward Training Stability:** When trained with the complex UnifiedReward-7b-v1.5 model, Stepwise-Flow-GRPO trained stably, achieving a 0.74 GenEval score after 60 GPU hours. In contrast, Flow-GRPO consistently diverged with this reward function, indicating that stepwise credit assignment provides not only efficiency but also fundamental stability advantages with complex, VLM-based reward models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on Stepwise-Flow-GRPO contributes to the field of generative AI with several potential significances and impacts:

- **Advancement in RL for Text-to-Image Generation:** The work presents a refined approach to applying reinforcement learning for fine-tuning text-to-image flow-matching models. By addressing the uniform credit assignment problem, it offers a more principled and effective method for improving model performance.
- **Enhanced Learning Efficiency:** The demonstrated superior sample efficiency and faster convergence directly translate to reduced computational resources and time required for training high-quality generative models. This has practical implications for researchers and developers, allowing for quicker iteration cycles and more accessible development of advanced T2I systems.
- **Improved Generative Capabilities:** The method's ability to achieve better compositional understanding, spatial reasoning, counting, and attribute binding indicates a fundamental improvement in the fine-grained control and accuracy of image generation. This can lead to T2I models that are more capable of adhering to complex prompts and generating visually coherent and contextually accurate images.
- **Increased Training Robustness:** The observed training stability, especially when utilizing complex, VLM-based reward models like UnifiedReward, is a significant practical advantage. Many advanced reward functions can introduce noisy gradients, and a method that maintains stability under such conditions is more reliable for real-world applications.
- **Foundational for Future Research Directions:** The concept of stepwise, gain-based credit assignment opens several avenues for future work. These include:
 - **Curriculum Learning:** Using per-prompt gain variance as a difficulty signal to guide a curriculum learning strategy, allowing models to progressively tackle more challenging aspects of image generation.
 - **Adaptive Step Weighting:** Dynamically weighting denoising steps based on their gain variance could enable optimization to focus on high-information or problematic regions of the trajectory, further enhancing efficiency.
 - **Self-Correcting Diffusion Models:** The ability to assign credit to individual steps could form the basis for models that can detect and autonomously correct suboptimal intermediate decisions during the generation process, leading to more robust and fault-tolerant systems.
- **Broader Applicability of Gain-Based RL:** By extending adaptive gain maximization to off-policy GRPO and flow models, the principles elucidated in this research could potentially be applied to other sequential decision-making tasks in various domains where precise temporal credit assignment is crucial.
