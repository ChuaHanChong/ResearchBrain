# WorldSample: Closed-loop Real-robot RL with World Modelling

> [!summary] Summary
> A closed-loop framework, WorldSample, enhances real-robot reinforcement learning by integrating physically grounded data augmentation and Policy-Paced Learning to minimize real-world interaction costs. This approach achieved an 82% average policy success rate, a 26 percentage point improvement, and cut physical training steps by 59% to 23K across five diverse manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Imitation Learning struggles with out-of-distribution states, leading to compounding errors in robot manipulation tasks.
- Deploying Reinforcement Learning on real robots is prohibitively expensive due to high physical interaction costs, hardware wear, and extensive human intervention.
- Existing world models for generating synthetic experience often produce visual hallucinations or dynamic artifacts, introducing inaccuracies that destabilize policy learning.

## Method

- **WorldSample** uses a ==real-synthetic data loop== that continuously grounds an ==action-conditioned world model== with physical robot rollouts and generates diverse, physically feasible ==counterfactual trajectories==.
- ==Asynchronous scheduling== enables parallel execution of robot learning and world model generation, with online rollouts dynamically adapting the world model to current policy-induced state distributions.
- **Policy-Paced Learning (PPL)** safely integrates synthetic data into policy training using ==Q-aware sample selection== to balance value signals and ==uncertainty-guided scheduling== to modulate synthetic data usage based on policy entropy.

## Results

- Increased average policy success rate from **56%** to **82%** (a **26 percentage point** improvement) and reduced average physical training steps by **59%** (from **56K** to **23K**) across five real-world manipulation tasks.
- Achieved a **23%** reduction in average wall-clock training time, decreasing it from **83 minutes** to **64 minutes** compared to the HIL-SERL baseline.
- Ablation studies confirmed that Policy-Paced Learning components (Q-aware sample selection and uncertainty-guided scheduling) are essential, with their removal leading to significant drops in success rate on the insertion task (to **76%** and **61%** respectively).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adapting the world model with diverse online robot rollouts, including failed attempts, is crucial for improving its prediction fidelity and enabling it to generate realistic and accurate synthetic trajectories.
> - Unregulated injection of synthetic data from world models can lead to unstable policy training and suboptimal performance, even when the synthetic data is generally useful.
> - A closed-loop system that continuously grounds a world model with real interaction while generating physically feasible synthetic experience can drastically reduce real-robot training costs without sacrificing performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Robot learning has seen advancements through two primary paradigms: Imitation Learning (IL) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). Imitation learning facilitates the acquisition of complex manipulation skills by leveraging large datasets of human demonstrations. However, IL is constrained by the coverage of its training data. Policies trained with IL may encounter out-of-distribution states not present in the demonstration set, leading to compounding errors and task failure.

Reinforcement learning provides a complementary approach by enabling robots to improve through trial-and-error interactions and reward signals, allowing policies to move beyond the behaviors observed in demonstrations. Despite this potential, deploying RL on physical robots faces a significant challenge: the high cost of real-world interaction. Physical rollouts are time-consuming, can lead to hardware wear, may necessitate human intervention for resets or safety, and each rollout provides only a single action-outcome path for learning.

Existing research efforts to mitigate this rollout cost can be broadly categorized:
- **Demonstration-based and human-in-the-loop methods:** These approaches utilize expert trajectories, corrective human interventions, and pre-existing offline data to enhance initial policy quality and stabilize online training. While effective, they still depend on substantial human effort and physical interactions.
- **Traditional data augmentation methods:** These techniques typically involve perturbing image observations or action sequences to improve policy robustness and introduce data variation. However, such unguided perturbations often neglect the physical feasibility, reachability, or realistic dynamics of the robot and environment.
- **World-model-based methods:** These methods aim to synthesize action-conditioned experience by predicting future observations, rewards, or complete rollout trajectories. When employed as standalone simulators or "dreaming" environments, current world models can exhibit limitations. They may drift from the true physical data distribution, generating visual hallucinations or artifacts in contact dynamics, which can introduce noisy or inaccurate supervision for policy learning.

WorldSample positions itself within this landscape by addressing the limitations of existing world-model-based methods for real-robot RL. It proposes a physically grounded data augmentation framework designed to expand action-conditioned experience while maintaining anchors to real robot rollouts. This approach seeks to provide trajectory-level augmentation that is both diverse and physically feasible, thereby improving sample efficiency and stability in real-world robot learning, without fully replacing physical interaction or introducing significant visual and dynamic inaccuracies.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework that reduces the interactive cost associated with deploying reinforcement learning on real-world robots for manipulation tasks. This overarching goal is driven by several detailed motivations arising from the limitations of current robot learning paradigms:

- **Addressing the "Demonstration-Coverage Limitation" of Imitation Learning:** While IL has shown success in learning from large-scale human demonstrations, it inherently struggles when the robot encounters states or scenarios not adequately represented in its training data. Small deviations can cascade into larger errors, highlighting the need for methods that allow policies to improve beyond initial demonstrations.
- **Mitigating the "Rollout-Cost Bottleneck" in Real-World RL:** Reinforcement learning, with its trial-and-error approach, offers a pathway to overcome IL's limitations. However, its direct application to real robots is severely hampered by the high cost of physical interaction. Each physical rollout consumes time, can contribute to hardware wear, may require human intervention for resets or safety, and provides only a single trajectory of experience. These factors make learning from scratch on real robots prohibitively expensive and slow.
- **Improving upon Existing Data Augmentation Strategies:**
 - **Costly Human Effort:** Current demonstration-based and human-in-the-loop methods, while effective, still rely on significant human involvement and physical rollouts, which are expensive resources.
 - **Lack of Physical Grounding:** Traditional data augmentation techniques, such as image or action perturbations, often lack an understanding of physical constraints and reasonable robot dynamics. This can lead to the generation of unrealistic or unhelpful synthetic data.
 - **World Model Imperfections:** While world models offer the promise of generating synthetic experience, current implementations often suffer from visual hallucination and contact-dynamics artifacts. When policy learning relies heavily on these imagined rollouts, the discrepancies between the synthetic and real environments can introduce noisy supervision, leading to unstable or suboptimal policy learning.

Motivated by these challenges, WorldSample aims to provide a solution that leverages the generative power of world models while ensuring the synthetic data remains physically grounded and suitable for stable policy improvement. The core objectives are to:
1. Develop a framework for physically grounded data augmentation that expands real-robot rollouts into multiple task-relevant synthetic trajectories.
2. Formulate a "real-synthetic data loop" where online physical interactions continually adapt the world model, and the adapted model, in turn, generates high-fidelity synthetic experience.
3. Introduce a mechanism, Policy-Paced Learning (PPL), to regulate the integration of this synthetic data into policy training. This mechanism specifically aims to mitigate issues such as Q-value overestimation and noise induced by world model artifacts, ensuring that the augmentation is useful and stable.
4. Demonstrate that this framework can achieve faster convergence, higher success rates, and reduced physical interaction costs in real-world robot manipulation tasks compared to existing baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WorldSample is presented as a physically grounded, closed-loop data augmentation framework for online real-world reinforcement learning. Its design integrates two main components: a real-synthetic data loop and Policy-Paced Learning (PPL).

#### 3.1. Real-Synthetic Data Loop

This component focuses on generating high-fidelity synthetic transitions grounded in real-world data and asynchronously integrating them into the learning process.

- **Physical Robot Rollouts:** Online interactions with the physical robot environment serve a dual purpose. They provide direct experience for policy learning and act as grounding data for the action-conditioned world model. These real rollouts are continuously collected and stored in a real replay buffer.
- **Counterfactual Trajectory Generation:** WorldSample generates synthetic transitions by sampling counterfactual trajectory segments. Instead of generating actions randomly, these segments are sampled with local perturbations around physically executed real action sequences. Given a real rollout segment $\tau = (o_0, A_{0:T-1}, O_{1:T}) \sim D_{\text{real}}$, a perturbed action sequence $\tilde{A}_{0:T-1}$ is sampled from a scale-randomized local perturbation distribution $q_{\sigma,s}$ centered on the real action sequence. This process is designed to preserve the temporal structure and action statistics of the robot's policy, aiming for physically feasible generated trajectories. The world model then predicts the visual future ($\tilde{O}_{1:T}$), and a reward model assigns task feedback ($\tilde{r}_{0:T-1}$). The resulting tuple $(o_0, \tilde{A}_{0:T-1}, \tilde{O}_{1:T}, \tilde{r}_{0:T-1})$ constitutes a labeled synthetic trajectory. The grounding in real visual and action conditions is intended to maintain fidelity to physical dynamics and geometry while expanding data coverage.
- **Asynchronous Scheduling:** To prevent the potentially slow process of video generation from impeding real-time robot control, WorldSample employs asynchronous execution. The robot's learning stream continues collecting physical rollouts and updating the policy in parallel with the world model generating synthetic trajectories. The real-synthetic loop is closed by asynchronously adapting the world model using online rollout data. As new physical rollouts are collected, they are incorporated into the world model's post-training process, ensuring that the generation capabilities of the model align with the increasingly diverse and policy-induced state distribution. This continuous adaptation is intended to provide higher-quality synthetic experience for subsequent policy learning.

#### 3.2. Policy-Paced Learning (PPL)

PPL is introduced to manage the integration of generated synthetic data into policy learning, specifically addressing potential issues like Q-value overestimation and instability caused by inherent imperfections in world models. Naive injection of synthetic transitions, which carry residual errors ($\epsilon_{\text{syn}}$), can lead to compounding errors in Bellman backups and destabilize critic learning. PPL regulates synthetic data usage through two mechanisms:

- **Q-Aware Sample Selection:** This mechanism controls the composition of generated transitions to maintain a balanced value signal. Each generated trajectory is labeled by an independent reward model as either positive (successful, $D^+_{\text{syn}}$) or negative (failed, $D^-_{\text{syn}}$). PPL aims to select synthetic data such that the average Q-value of selected positive and negative synthetic samples, combined, does not significantly shift the overall value contrast from that observed in real data (Equation 5). This approach is designed to prevent the critic from converging to uniformly optimistic or overly conservative value estimates by balancing the positive signals that propagate successful outcomes with negative signals that constrain visually plausible but unsuccessful behaviors.
- **Uncertainty-Guided Data Scheduling:** This component determines the quantity of synthetic data introduced during RL training based on the policy's uncertainty. Policy uncertainty ($H_t$) is quantified by the actor's entropy on real states (Equation 6). High actor entropy signifies that the policy is uncertain on real states, making actor-critic learning more susceptible to biases from synthetic targets. PPL employs an entropy-paced schedule, $\rho(H_t)$, to modulate the influence of synthetic data. This schedule actively suppresses synthetic data's contribution when the policy exhibits high uncertainty and gradually increases its usage as the policy becomes more concentrated and stable (Equations 8 and 9). The intention is to leverage synthetic data conservatively in early, uncertain training phases and more extensively as the policy matures, thereby mitigating synthetic noise and preventing instability.

Collectively, the real-synthetic data loop provides a source of physically grounded and diverse experience, while Policy-Paced Learning ensures this experience is incorporated into the policy training safely and effectively, without replacing real interaction or violating real-time constraints.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of WorldSample focused on its ability to improve online real-robot RL efficiency, enhance world model fidelity, and validate the necessity of Policy-Paced Learning (PPL). Experiments were conducted on a Galaxea A1X robotic arm across five real-world manipulation tasks: Pushing, Insertion, Sorting, Pick & Place, and Assemble, which varied in contact complexity, precision, and action-space difficulty.

#### 4.1. Real-World Experiments: RL Efficiency and Policy Capability

- **Comparison with HIL-SERL (Real-Robot RL Baseline):** WorldSample demonstrated improved performance metrics across all five tasks.
 - **Success Rate:** Increased the average policy success rate from 56% (HIL-SERL) to 82% (WorldSample), an improvement of 26 percentage points.
 - **Training Steps:** Reduced the average number of physical training steps from 56K (HIL-SERL) to 23K (WorldSample), representing a 59% reduction in interaction cost.
 - **Training Time:** Decreased the average wall-clock training time from 83 minutes (HIL-SERL) to 64 minutes (WorldSample), a 23% reduction.
 - **Task-Specific Improvements:** Notably, on contact-rich tasks like Insertion and Sorting, WorldSample achieved 95% success with 50% and 33% fewer real interaction steps, respectively. The results indicated that WorldSample generally reached higher success rates earlier in training, suggesting that generated transitions provided useful learning signals under limited real-world interaction.

- **Comparison with Model-Based Baselines (VLAW and WMPO):**
 - WorldSample achieved substantially higher success rates across all tasks compared to VLAW (average 64%) and WMPO (average 69%).
 - The performance gap was particularly pronounced on tasks requiring high precision or complex contact dynamics, such as Insertion and Assemble, where the model-based baselines were more constrained by the quality of their generated trajectories. For instance, WorldSample achieved 95% success on Insertion compared to VLAW's 47% and WMPO's 82%, and 42% on Assemble compared to VLAW's 32% and WMPO's 23%.
 - On tasks with relatively simpler dynamics and visual distributions (e.g., Pushing and Pick&Place), the performance gap between WorldSample and other model-based methods was smaller.

#### 4.2. World Model Adaptation

The study evaluated the impact of different data sources on the world model's prediction quality.
- **Pretrained Model:** The generic pretrained model exhibited poor performance (PSNR: 8.50, SSIM: 0.334), indicating a significant domain gap.
- **Demonstration-Only Finetuning:** Adapting the model solely with human demonstrations showed limited improvement (PSNR: 10.27, SSIM: 0.428).
- **Online Rollout Adaptation:** Finetuning with online RL rollouts resulted in substantial improvements in prediction quality (PSNR: 28.43, SSIM: 0.906, LPIPS: 0.034). This suggests that the diverse state-action outcomes, including failures and recovery behaviors from online RL data, are critical for the world model's generalization.
- **Combined Data and Dual-View Model:** Combining human demonstrations with online rollouts further improved PSNR (29.66) and SSIM (0.910). The dual-view model, utilizing both side-view and wrist-mounted cameras, achieved the best structural fidelity (PSNR: 29.89, SSIM: 0.925).
- Conclusion: The findings confirm that online rollout adaptation is crucial for grounding the world model in physical dynamics and enabling it to generate realistic and accurate videos.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies on Policy-Paced Learning (PPL)

Ablation studies on the insertion task, known for its sensitivity to data quality, evaluated the necessity of PPL's components.
- **Naive Strategy (w/o PPL):** Removing PPL entirely allowed the policy to eventually reach 86% success, but required 32K steps and 69 minutes, indicating that while synthetic data is useful, it is inefficient without PPL's control. This is significantly less efficient than WorldSample's 95% success with 10K steps and 30 minutes.
- **Without Uncertainty-Guided Scheduling:** Removing the scheduling component led to a significant drop in success rate from 95% to 61%. This suggests that introducing synthetic data without appropriate scheduling can cause unstable training and suboptimal policy performance, likely due to visual hallucinations and noisy targets during early, uncertain policy stages.
- **Without Q-Aware Sample Selection:** Removing Q-aware sample selection resulted in a degradation of performance to 76% success and an increase in the intervention rate to 42% (compared to 24% for WorldSample). This indicates that the balanced composition of generated outcomes (positive vs. negative samples) is important for preventing unstable value learning and maintaining policy stability.
- Conclusion: The ablation studies validate that both Q-aware sample selection and uncertainty-guided scheduling are necessary components of PPL for the stable and effective utilization of synthetic trajectories in real-robot reinforcement learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The WorldSample framework addresses a core challenge in real-robot reinforcement learning by providing a method to significantly reduce the cost of physical interaction while enhancing policy performance. This work demonstrates the utility of a closed-loop real-synthetic data pipeline where world models are dynamically improved by online robot rollouts, and in turn, generate grounded synthetic experience for policy training.

The significance and potential impact of this research include:

- **Increased Accessibility and Efficiency of Real-Robot RL:** By substantially reducing the number of required physical training steps and wall-clock time, WorldSample makes it more feasible to apply reinforcement learning directly on real robotic hardware. This reduction in interaction cost can mitigate hardware wear and tear, decrease the need for human intervention, and accelerate the development cycle for new robot skills.
- **Enhanced Policy Robustness and Success Rates:** The framework's ability to achieve higher success rates, particularly in complex contact-rich and precision manipulation tasks, indicates its potential to develop more capable and reliable robot policies. This is crucial for deploying robots in real-world applications where performance and robustness are paramount.
- **Advancement in World Model Integration for RL:** WorldSample provides a method for safely and effectively integrating imperfect world models into online learning systems. By anchoring synthetic generation to real data and employing Policy-Paced Learning, it mitigates common issues like visual hallucination and value overestimation, which have historically limited the practical application of world-model-generated data in real-robot contexts. The demonstration of how online rollout data significantly improves world model fidelity is a key contribution to this area.
- **Foundation for Scalable Robot Learning:** The approach of expanding real-world experience through physically grounded synthetic trajectories offers a pathway toward more data-efficient and scalable robot learning systems. This could contribute to the development of more generalist robot policies that can adapt to novel situations with less reliance on extensive, task-specific real-world data collection.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The identified limitations, such as focusing on single-task and fixed-scene distributions, highlight important avenues for future work in extending the framework to instance-, task-, or scene-level generalization, further advancing the capabilities of learned robot behaviors.
