# PROPA: Toward Process-level Optimization in Visual Reasoning via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called PROPA is introduced by the University of Melbourne to enhance complex visual reasoning in Vision-Language Models by combining Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) for dense, process-level reward generation with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and an interleaved Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) scheme. This approach achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across seven benchmarks, demonstrating up to a 21.0% gain on out-of-domain tasks, by specifically optimizing intermediate reasoning steps.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with complex, multi-step visual reasoning tasks, where errors in early stages can propagate and lead to incorrect final answers.
- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) methods for reasoning require costly, dense step-level human annotations, or suffer from error propagation and overfitting when using auto-generated data.
- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) approaches typically use sparse, outcome-level feedback, making it difficult to pinpoint errors in intermediate steps and leading to unstable training and inefficient exploration.

## Method

- PROPA uses ==MCTS== to auto-generate dense, ==process-level reward estimates== per reasoning step, then refines the policy via ==GRPO== on these rewards, using group-level filtering and a ==log-based nonlinear reward transformation== for sharper differentiation.
- An ==interleaved training scheme== dynamically combines GRPO updates (on successful MCTS trajectories) with SFT updates (on trajectories where MCTS fails or for initial stabilization) to address the cold-start problem and maintain robust learning.
- A dedicated ==Process Reward Model (PRM)== is trained to predict node scores, which then guides MCTS efficiently during test-time inference, replacing computationally expensive simulations.

## Results

- PROPA consistently achieves the highest accuracy across all **seven** visual reasoning benchmarks (both in-domain and out-of-domain) and four VLM backbones, outperforming competitive baselines like Reason-RFT.
- The framework demonstrates substantial quantitative gains, including an average accuracy improvement of **7.9%** and **10.1%** on Qwen2.5-VL-3B and Intern2.5-VL-2B backbones, respectively, over Reason-RFT, with a peak gain of **21.0%** on the TranceL (Out-of-Domain) benchmark.
- Ablation studies confirm the critical role of the interleaved training scheme, the log-based nonlinear reward transformation, and the Process Reward Model in PROPA's superior performance, stability, and effective test-time search.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Automatically generated, dense process-level rewards move beyond sparse outcome feedback; non-linear transformations of these rewards amplify disparities, letting GRPO better differentiate high- vs low-quality reasoning paths.
> - An interleaved training strategy, blending reinforcement learning with supervised fine-tuning, stabilizes the learning process and effectively mitigates the cold-start problem often associated with pure RL approaches.
> - A learned Process Reward Model can accurately guide efficient MCTS at inference time, aligning test-time search with the optimized training signal without incurring high computational costs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has witnessed remarkable progress in tasks such as visual question answering and image captioning, largely driven by advancements in deep learning and large language models (LLMs). However, a critical challenge remains: enabling VLMs to perform *complex, multi-step visual reasoning*. Unlike simpler recognition or descriptive tasks, visual reasoning demands an understanding of intricate relationships, comparisons, and inferences drawn through a structured, often sequential, thought process. Errors in early reasoning steps can cascade, leading to incorrect final answers, a problem that current VLMs frequently encounter.

The existing landscape of post-training paradigms for enhancing VLM reasoning can be broadly categorized into two main approaches:

1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)-based methods:** These approaches leverage annotated reasoning chains (Chain-of-Thought, CoT) to train models to generate multi-step explanations. While effective, SFT suffers from several limitations. It often relies on costly, dense, step-level human annotations, which are difficult and expensive to acquire at scale. When intermediate reasoning steps are automatically generated, they can introduce inaccuracies, leading to error propagation. Furthermore, SFT tends to overfit to specific training trajectories, limiting its generalization to diverse reasoning paths. Previous works like LLaVA-CoT, Mulberry, SDRT, and A\*Star fall into this category, with some exploring tree search for data synthesis or inference-time exploration but still relying on the underlying SFT principle.

2. **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods:** Increasingly popular, these methods, exemplified by Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), aim to improve reasoning by rewarding trajectories that lead to correct final answers. Approaches like Reason-RFT, Visual-RFT, R1-V, MM-Eureka, and MMR1 have demonstrated stronger reasoning and generalization capabilities. However, a significant drawback of most RLVR methods is their reliance on *sparse, outcome-level feedback*. The model only receives a reward at the very end of a reasoning chain, making it difficult to pinpoint errors in intermediate steps and leading to unstable training, a phenomenon often referred to as reward sparsity. This limited signal hinders effective exploration and optimization of the vast search space of possible reasoning paths.

PROPA directly addresses the limitations of both SFT and RLVR. It identifies a crucial gap: the need for *dense, process-level reward signals without requiring explicit human step-level annotations* for RL-based optimization. By integrating Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) with GRPO, PROPA aims to automatically generate these dense, intermediate rewards. This approach moves beyond the sparse outcome-based rewards of traditional RLVR and avoids the annotation burden and error propagation issues of SFT, carving out a novel and highly impactful niche in the pursuit of more robust and generalizable visual reasoning models. The work is thus positioned at the forefront of efforts to make VLMs truly capable of complex, human-like reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind PROPA stems from the inherent difficulties VLMs face when confronted with complex visual reasoning tasks. These tasks often require a sequence of logical steps, where errors made early on can fatally compromise the final outcome. The current landscape offers two main paradigms for VLM post-training, both with significant shortcomings:

1. **Cascading Errors in Multi-step Reasoning:** Conventional VLMs, even with chain-of-thought prompting, struggle when reasoning tasks demand intricate, multi-step problem-solving. An incorrect observation or inference at an intermediate stage propagates errors throughout the reasoning chain, leading to an incorrect final answer. This is particularly evident when the model has to perform sequential operations like counting, comparing, or inferring spatial relationships.

2. **Limitations of SFT-based Approaches:**
 - **Costly Annotations:** Generating high-quality, step-by-step reasoning annotations is immensely expensive and labor-intensive, making large-scale datasets for SFT challenging to create.
 - **Error Propagation with Auto-generated Data:** While some methods attempt to synthetically generate intermediate steps, these are often noisy and can introduce or amplify inaccuracies, leading to models that learn from flawed supervision.
 - **Overfitting:** SFT models tend to overfit to specific reasoning trajectories present in the training data, limiting their ability to generalize to novel or diverse reasoning paths.

3. **Challenges with RLVR-based Approaches:**
 - **Reward Sparsity:** Most existing RLVR methods provide only an outcome-level reward (e.g., correct/incorrect final answer). This sparse signal offers little guidance for optimizing individual intermediate reasoning steps, making effective learning difficult and often unstable.
 - **Exploration Depth:** Without dense rewards, the model struggles to explore the vast search space of possible reasoning paths efficiently, often getting stuck in local optima or failing to discover optimal reasoning strategies.

Against this backdrop, PROPA sets forth several key objectives:

- **To generate dense, process-level rewards automatically:** The core objective is to move beyond sparse, outcome-based rewards by providing feedback for each intermediate reasoning step, thereby guiding the model more effectively. Crucially, this must be achieved *without* relying on costly human step-level annotations.
- **To optimize reasoning at each intermediate step:** By obtaining process-level rewards, the framework aims to enable fine-grained optimization of the VLM's policy, ensuring that individual reasoning steps are more robust and accurate, thus preventing error propagation.
- **To address the cold-start problem in RL-based training:** Untrained policies often explore uninformative paths, making initial RL training difficult. PROPA aims to stabilize learning from the outset.
- **To align test-time search with the training signal:** For efficient inference, the model needs a mechanism to effectively explore the reasoning space at test time, guided by the knowledge gained during training. This involves creating a heuristic that approximates the learned process rewards.
- **To achieve superior reasoning and generalization capabilities:** Ultimately, the framework seeks to outperform existing state-of-the-art SFT and RLVR methods on both in-domain and out-of-domain visual reasoning tasks, demonstrating enhanced stability and robustness.

By tackling these challenges, PROPA aims to pave the way for more sophisticated and reliable visual reasoning in VLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PROPA (Process-level Reasoning Optimization with interleaved Policy Alignment) is a novel framework designed to enhance visual reasoning in VLMs by integrating Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), complemented by an interleaved training scheme and a Process Reward Model (PRM). The methodology is structured around three core components:

#### 3.1. MCTS-Guided GRPO Training

The cornerstone of PROPA is the use of MCTS to generate dense, process-level rewards, which are then used to optimize the VLM's reasoning policy via GRPO.

- **MCTS for Process Reward Generation:** For each training instance (image and question), PROPA constructs a search tree. Each node in this tree represents a reasoning step. The MCTS procedure involves four stages:
 1. **Selection:** Starting from the root, the algorithm traverses the tree by selecting nodes with the highest Upper Confidence Bound (UCT) score, balancing exploration (of less-visited nodes) and exploitation (of promising nodes).
 2. **Expansion:** From the selected node, the current policy model ($\pi_\theta$) samples *k* child nodes, representing potential next reasoning steps.
 3. **Simulation (Rollout):** For each newly expanded child node, a Monte Carlo rollout is performed. This involves sampling subsequent reasoning paths until a final answer is reached. A simulation reward $r(s) = 1$ is assigned if the final answer is correct, and $0$ otherwise.
 4. **Backpropagation:** The simulation reward is backpropagated up the path from the newly expanded node to the root. The total accumulated reward ($W(s)$) and visit count ($N(s)$) for each node along this path are updated.
 After multiple MCTS iterations, the average simulation reward $Q(s) = W(s) / N(s)$ for each node serves as a *dense, process-level reward estimate*. This is crucial because it provides feedback on the quality of *intermediate* reasoning steps, without requiring human annotations for each step.

- **Localized GRPO with Tree Node Filtering:** The MCTS-generated rewards are then leveraged for GRPO updates.
 - For each parent node in the MCTS tree, its expanded child nodes form a "group" of completions. The $Q(s)$ value of each child node serves as its GRPO reward.
 - The GRPO loss is computed based on the *advantage* of each child node's reward relative to the group mean, normalized by standard deviation. This encourages the policy to favor actions leading to higher $Q(s)$ values.
 - To ensure meaningful learning signals, **group-level filtering** is applied: groups with very low variance in child node $Q(s)$ values (i.e., $\Delta_{G_c} < \tau$) are discarded, as they offer weak differentiation.
 - A **log-based nonlinear transformation** is applied to the $Q(s)$ values, amplifying larger values and suppressing smaller ones, further enhancing the contrast among reasoning paths and allowing GRPO to better distinguish high-quality steps.

#### 3.2. Interleaved GRPO and SFT Training Scheme

To mitigate the "cold-start" problem often encountered in pure RL approaches and ensure robust learning, PROPA employs an interleaved training strategy:

- **SFT Activation Stage:** Initially, the base VLM ($\pi_\theta$) undergoes Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) using a dataset ($D_{act}$) of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) annotations generated by a powerful teacher model (e.g., GPT-4.1). This stage teaches the model basic reasoning structure and appropriate termination tokens, providing a stable starting point for RL.
- **Dynamic Data Partitioning:** During the main training loop, after constructing an MCTS tree for each instance, the training data is dynamically partitioned:
 - $D_{grpo}$: Contains instances where MCTS successfully finds at least one correct terminal node. These instances are used for GRPO updates.
 - $D_{sft}$: Contains instances where *all* MCTS simulations fail (i.e., no correct answer is found). For these, the model reverts to SFT using the corresponding entries from the initial $D_{act}$ dataset.
- **Interleaved Updates:** GRPO updates on $D_{grpo}$ (for refinement of successful paths) and SFT updates on $D_{sft}$ (for recovering from failures and preserving basic capabilities) are executed periodically every $\lambda$ iterations. This dynamic balancing ensures continuous refinement while preventing catastrophic forgetting or prolonged cold-start issues.

#### 3.3. Test-Time MCTS with a Process Reward Model (PRM)

Performing full MCTS simulations at inference time is computationally prohibitive. To enable efficient and effective exploration during testing, PROPA introduces a specialized PRM:

- **PRM Training:** A Process Reward Model ($P_\phi$) is trained by extending the policy model ($\pi_\theta$) with a linear layer that directly predicts node scores (i.e., the $Q(s)$ values). This PRM is optimized using Mean Square Error (MSE) loss, learning from the (state, $Q(s)$) pairs collected from all successful MCTS search trees generated during the *final epoch* of interleaved training.
- **Test-Time MCTS:** During inference, the PRM replaces the computationally expensive simulation step in MCTS. When a new node is expanded, the PRM directly estimates its $Q(s)$ value. This predicted reward is then backpropagated to update parent nodes, guiding the MCTS exploration efficiently.
- **Best Sequence Selection:** After MCTS completes its iterations at test time, the reasoning path (from all terminal nodes) with the highest *average* $Q(s)$ value (as estimated by the PRM) is selected, and its embedded final answer is outputted.

By combining these innovative components, PROPA creates a powerful, self-improving loop for visual reasoning, enabling fine-grained optimization of reasoning processes without extensive manual supervision.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PROPA demonstrates significant advancements in visual reasoning, consistently outperforming existing state-of-the-art baselines across a comprehensive set of benchmarks. The key findings are as follows:

- **Superior Overall Performance:** Across all seven benchmarks (including both in-domain and out-of-domain tasks) and four VLM backbones (Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B, Intern2.5-VL-2B/8B), PROPA consistently achieves the highest accuracy. This indicates its robust structural reasoning and generalization capabilities.

- **Significant Quantitative Gains:**
 - PROPA registers an average accuracy improvement of 7.9% and 10.1% on the Qwen2.5-VL-3B and Intern2.5-VL-2B backbones, respectively, compared to Reason-RFT (RFT), the most competitive baseline.
 - The largest observed gain is a remarkable **21.0%** on the TranceL (Out-of-Domain) benchmark when using the Intern2.5-VL-2B backbone, highlighting its strong cross-domain generalization.
 - Even with larger backbones (Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Intern2.5-VL-8B), PROPA maintains its lead, outperforming RFT by approximately 4.4% and 4.1%, respectively.
 - It also consistently outperforms the teacher model used for SFT activation, benefiting from the subsequent RL optimization.

- **Improved Convergence and Stability:** Analysis of accuracy trends over epochs (Figure 3 in the paper) shows that PROPA generally outperforms baselines throughout training, demonstrating improved convergence and stability. A clear performance surge is observed after the initial SFT activation stage, confirming the effectiveness of the GRPO-based refinement.

- **Effectiveness of the Interleaved Training Scheme (Ablation Study):**
 - The interleaved GRPO and SFT approach significantly outperforms both "GRPO only" and "SFT only" variants. This validates the design choice to address the cold-start problem (GRPO only) and error propagation (SFT only).
 - The log-based nonlinear reward transformation is crucial; removing it leads to decreased performance, indicating that amplifying reward disparities is essential for GRPO to effectively distinguish high-quality reasoning paths.

- **Effectiveness of the Process Reward Model (PRM) at Test Time (Ablation Study):**
 - MCTS + PRM generally outperforms greedy search, bestN search, and even MCTS guided by a strong external VLM judge (e.g., GPT-4.1) during inference. This confirms the PRM's effectiveness in guiding efficient exploration and aligning test-time search with the training signal.
 - An interesting exception is the Trance dataset, where greedy search sometimes performs better. This is attributed to the high lexical and semantic similarity among tokens describing object transformations, which can make fine-grained discrimination challenging for the PRM.

- **Dynamic Data Transition during Interleaved Training:** Figure 4 (in supplementary material) illustrates that as training progresses, the proportion of GRPO-generated data gradually increases, while reliance on SFT data decreases. This confirms that the model effectively transitions from initial supervised learning to self-improving reinforcement learning, as designed.

- **Qualitative Analysis and Error Reduction:** Case studies and a manual annotation study (on 100 GeoMath examples) reveal that PROPA significantly reduces both perceptual and logical errors in intermediate reasoning steps compared to baselines. This demonstrates that PROPA's performance gains are not just in final answer accuracy but also in the underlying quality and reliability of the reasoning process. Baselines frequently exhibit fundamental perceptual errors or logical inconsistencies that propagate, whereas PROPA manages to optimize these intermediate steps effectively.

- **Computational Considerations:** While PROPA's training time is approximately twice that of RFT variants due to the MCTS exploration tree construction, this overhead is justified by the substantial performance improvements and the value of automatically generated dense rewards.

In summary, PROPA successfully addresses critical limitations in current visual reasoning paradigms by providing a robust framework for process-level optimization. Its consistent outperformance, strong generalization, and demonstrated stability mark a significant step forward in enabling VLMs to tackle complex, multi-step visual reasoning with greater accuracy and reliability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PROPA framework represents a substantial step forward in the field of visual reasoning with Vision-Language Models, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

- **Bridging Key Gaps in VLM Reasoning:** PROPA directly addresses two long-standing challenges in VLM post-training: the **reward sparsity** prevalent in reinforcement learning methods and the issues of **costly annotations and error propagation** in supervised fine-tuning. By providing dense, process-level rewards *without human annotations* and integrating an interleaved SFT-GRPO scheme, it offers a more robust and scalable solution for training reasoning-capable VLMs.

- **Enabling Truly Multi-step Reasoning:** By optimizing at the intermediate reasoning step level, PROPA moves beyond superficial pattern matching or direct answer generation. It encourages the VLM to develop a more stable and accurate "thought process," making it less susceptible to cascading errors that plague current models on complex, multi-stage reasoning tasks. This is crucial for problems requiring sequential logical deductions, mathematical calculations, or spatial transformations.

- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The consistent outperformance on out-of-domain (OOD) tasks, with gains up to 21.0%, is a critical indicator of PROPA's strength. This suggests that the process-level optimization helps the model learn more fundamental reasoning principles rather than overfitting to specific task formats or datasets. Improved generalization is paramount for deploying VLMs in real-world scenarios where unseen variations are common.

- **Reduced Reliance on Costly Human Supervision:** The ability to automatically generate dense, process-level rewards through MCTS obviates the need for expensive and labor-intensive human annotations of intermediate reasoning steps. This significantly lowers the barrier to developing and refining complex reasoning models, making the approach more scalable and applicable to a wider range of challenging datasets.

- **Foundation for More Robust AI Systems:** By fostering more accurate and reliable intermediate reasoning, PROPA contributes to building more trustworthy and explainable AI systems. While not explicitly an objective of interpretability, a model that systematically reduces perceptual and logical errors in its steps inherently offers a more coherent and verifiable reasoning chain.

- **Catalyst for Future Research:** PROPA opens several avenues for future research:
 - **Advanced Tree Search Algorithms:** Exploring other sophisticated tree search algorithms or hybrid approaches could further enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of process-level reward generation.
 - **Improved Reward Shaping:** Investigating more nuanced reward functions beyond binary correct/incorrect outcomes in MCTS simulations could provide even richer signals.
 - **PRM Enhancements:** Further developing the Process Reward Model to handle highly similar semantic spaces (as seen in the Trance dataset challenge) would be beneficial for universal test-time search efficiency.
 - **Integration with Planning:** The structured reasoning process learned by PROPA could serve as a powerful foundation for integrating VLMs with higher-level planning and decision-making systems.

- **Potential Practical Applications:** The improved reasoning capabilities offered by PROPA have broad implications for various application domains:
 - **Scientific Discovery:** Assisting researchers in analyzing complex visual data (e.g., charts, graphs, scientific images) and deriving logical conclusions.
 - **Education:** Developing intelligent tutoring systems that can not only answer questions but also demonstrate step-by-step problem-solving in visual-mathematical or scientific contexts.
 - **Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** Enabling agents to understand and reason about complex visual environments and make sequential decisions.
 - **Medical Imaging:** Improving diagnostic accuracy by providing more robust reasoning over medical scans and related patient data.

In conclusion, PROPA is a highly significant contribution that pushes the boundaries of VLM capabilities in complex visual reasoning. By cleverly combining MCTS and GRPO with an adaptive training strategy, it establishes a new paradigm for optimizing reasoning processes, promising more capable, generalizable, and reliable AI systems for a future where sophisticated multimodal understanding is paramount.
