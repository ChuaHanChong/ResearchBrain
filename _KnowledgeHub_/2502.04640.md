# Building Rome with Convex Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Harvard's Computational Robotics Group developed XM, a scalable and initialization-free solver for global Bundle Adjustment that achieves certifiable optimality. By integrating learned monocular depth with a CUDA-accelerated Semidefinite Programming relaxation, XM processes large-scale datasets, demonstrating up to 1000x faster computation than existing methods while maintaining comparable reconstruction accuracy.

## Problem

- Bundle Adjustment (BA) is a non-convex optimization problem essential for 3D reconstruction, suffering from initialization dependence and convergence to local minima.
- Traditional BA solvers face significant scalability issues, becoming computationally prohibitive for large datasets with thousands of images.
- Existing BA methods lack global optimality guarantees, making it impossible to certify the best possible solution.

## Method

- Reformulating Bundle Adjustment as ==Scaled Bundle Adjustment (SBA)== by incorporating learned monocular depth to lift 2D keypoints into approximate 3D points, simplifying the objective.
- Designing an empirically tight convex ==Semidefinite Program (SDP) relaxation== for the SBA problem, which can be solved to certifiable global optimality.
- Developing a highly optimized, custom C++/CUDA-based Riemannian optimizer (**XM**) that leverages the ==Burer-Monteiro factorization== and a "Riemannian Staircase" algorithm for efficient and scalable solution of the large-scale SDP.

## Results

- The XM solver consistently achieves certifiable global optimality on Bundle Adjustment problems without initialization, solving large instances (over **10,000 cameras**) in minutes and exhibiting up to **100x** speedup over CPU-based methods.
- The XM-SfM pipeline delivers **100 to 1000 times** faster processing speed than state-of-the-art SfM pipelines like COLMAP and GLOMAP on diverse datasets, enabling large-scale reconstructions (e.g., a **2000-frame** dataset in **10 seconds**).
- Reconstruction accuracy remains comparable to existing methods, even when utilizing noisy learned monocular depth, demonstrating the robustness of the framework.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating learned monocular depth into Bundle Adjustment significantly simplifies the problem by transforming 2D reprojection errors into 3D distance errors, eliminating a major source of non-convexity.
> - Custom-built, GPU-accelerated Riemannian optimization of Burer-Monteiro factorizations can make large-scale Semidefinite Programs (SDPs) practically solvable for geometric perception problems.
> - The "Riemannian Staircase" algorithm provides a reliable mechanism to escape local minima in Burer-Monteiro factorizations, ensuring convergence to the global optimum of the underlying SDP.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a central and long-standing problem in computer vision and robotics: Bundle Adjustment (BA). BA is the process of simultaneously refining camera poses and 3D landmark positions from a set of 2D image measurements, forming the core of Structure from Motion (SfM) and Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) pipelines.

Historically, BA is formulated as a large-scale, highly non-convex optimization problem due to the non-linear reprojection function and the non-convex constraint of camera poses belonging to the Special Euclidean Group SE(3). This non-convexity leads to several critical challenges:
1. **Initialization Dependence:** Traditional local optimization methods (e.g., those in CERES, GTSAM) are highly sensitive to their initial guess and often get stuck in poor local minima if not properly initialized.
2. **Scalability Issues:** While incremental SfM pipelines like COLMAP ensure stable initialization by building the reconstruction sequentially, they become extremely slow and difficult to scale to large datasets (e.g., thousands of images). Global SfM approaches like GLOMAP attempt to mitigate this by decoupling rotation and translation estimation, but their initialization can still be time-consuming.
3. **Lack of Global Optimality Guarantees:** Without convex formulations, it is impossible to certify whether a found solution is truly the best possible, or merely a local optimum.

The broader research landscape has seen a growing trend towards applying *convex relaxation techniques*, particularly Semidefinite Programming (SDP), to geometric perception problems in robotics. This "growing family of SDP-enabled certifiable algorithms" aims to address the non-convexity challenge by formulating problems as convex programs, which can be solved to *certifiable global optimality*. Previous works in this lineage have successfully tackled problems such as rotation averaging (SE-Sync), point cloud registration (TEASER), and pose-graph optimization.

This paper positions itself at the intersection of these trends by introducing two key innovations:
- **Leveraging Learned Depth:** Instead of relying solely on 2D keypoint measurements, the authors integrate modern monocular depth prediction models (e.g., UNIDEPTH) to lift 2D keypoints into approximate 3D keypoints. This crucial step *removes the non-convex reprojection function* (π(·)) from the objective, simplifying the problem significantly.
- **Extending Certifiable Optimization to Full BA:** While previous SDP-based methods tackled subproblems of SfM (like rotation synchronization or point cloud registration), this work extends the framework to the *full Bundle Adjustment problem*, including joint estimation of camera poses, 3D landmarks, *and* per-frame scaling factors to correct for errors in learned depth. This is a significant leap, as it aims to provide a certifiably optimal solution for the entire BA pipeline, which has traditionally been elusive.

Furthermore, the paper tackles the scalability limitation that often plagues SDPs by employing the Burer-Monteiro (BM) factorization and, crucially, developing a highly optimized, custom C++/CUDA-based Riemannian optimizer. This practical engineering effort is vital for moving certifiable methods from theoretical demonstrations to real-world applicability for large-scale problems.

In essence, this work seeks to revolutionize BA by offering an initialization-free, highly scalable, and globally certifiable solution, drawing upon advancements in both deep learning (for depth prediction) and convex optimization theory, all while pushing the boundaries of computational efficiency with GPU acceleration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this research is encapsulated in the question posed by the authors: "Can we design an algorithm that solves the global bundle adjustment problem (1) without initialization and at scale?" This question highlights the two primary limitations of conventional BA: its dependence on good initialization and its computational burden for large datasets.

The key objectives of the paper are to:

1. **Develop a Scaled Bundle Adjustment (SBA) Formulation:** The authors aim to simplify the notoriously non-convex BA problem by integrating external information. Their solution is to use *learned monocular depth prediction* to lift 2D keypoint measurements into noisy 3D keypoints. This transforms the original 2D reprojection error, which is highly non-linear, into a 3D distance error, which is more amenable to optimization. To account for inaccuracies in predicted depth, the formulation introduces per-frame scaling factors (`s_i`), leading to the "Scaled Bundle Adjustment" (SBA) problem (Eq. 3).
2. **Design an Empirically Tight Convex Semidefinite Program (SDP) Relaxation:** Building on the simplified SBA formulation, the next objective is to "convexify" the remaining non-convexities. This involves a sequence of mathematical manipulations, starting from marginalizing out translations and landmarks to obtain a problem solely involving scaled rotations, and then applying Shor's semidefinite relaxation. The goal is to obtain a convex SDP (Eq. 11) whose solution can provide a certifiable global optimum for the original non-convex SBA problem. The emphasis is on "empirical tightness," meaning that in practice, the relaxation yields the true global optimum.
3. **Develop a Scalable Solver for the Convex SDP:** A major bottleneck for SDPs is their computational cost, especially for large problem instances typical in BA. The authors aim to overcome this by:
 - Leveraging the **Burer-Monteiro (BM) factorization**, which exploits the low-rank nature of optimal SDP solutions to transform the SDP into a lower-dimensional, non-convex Riemannian optimization problem (Eq. 19).
 - Designing a **CUDA-based trust-region Riemannian optimizer (XM)**, which is a highly optimized C++/CUDA implementation of the Riemannian trust-region algorithm. This is critical for achieving extreme speed and scalability, far beyond what off-the-shelf SDP solvers or CPU-based manifold optimization toolboxes (like MANOPT) can provide. The "Riemannian Staircase" algorithm (Algorithm 1) ensures global convergence for the BM factorization, even though it's non-convex.
4. **Construct and Evaluate a Full Structure from Motion (SfM) Pipeline (XM-SfM):** The ultimate objective is to integrate the novel XM solver into a complete SfM pipeline. This involves incorporating standard SfM components like feature detection/matching (from COLMAP, GLOMAP), view graph creation, and a final refinement step using a local BA solver (CERES) to address any residual noise from learned depth. The pipeline's performance (quality, speed, scalability, initialization-freeness) is then rigorously compared against state-of-the-art SfM pipelines like COLMAP and GLOMAP across various real-world and synthetic datasets.

In essence, the motivation is to tackle the "holy grail" of global BA by combining insights from learned perception and advanced convex optimization, and crucially, making these theoretically powerful methods practical for real-world, large-scale applications through high-performance computing.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of "Building Rome with Convex Optimization" is a sophisticated blend of problem reformulation, convex optimization theory, and high-performance computing.

#### 3.1. Scaled Bundle Adjustment (SBA) Formulation

The fundamental idea is to simplify the classical BA problem (Eq. 1) by eliminating the problematic 2D reprojection function `π(·)`. This is achieved by:
- **Lifting 2D Keypoints to 3D:** For each 2D keypoint measurement `u_ik` observed by camera `i` corresponding to 3D point `k`, the authors leverage off-the-shelf pre-trained monocular depth prediction models (e.g., UNIDEPTH) to obtain a predicted depth `d_ik`. This allows the 2D keypoint to be lifted into an approximate 3D keypoint `~u_ik = d_ik * [u_ik; 1]`.
- **Introducing Per-Frame Scaling:** To compensate for potential scale inaccuracies in the learned depth predictions, a positive scaling parameter `s_i` is introduced for each camera `i`.
- **New Objective:** The problem is reformulated as minimizing the 3D distance between the transformed scaled 3D keypoint (`R_i(s_i ~u_ik) + t_i`, where `R_i` and `t_i` now transform from camera to global frame) and the 3D landmark `p_k` (Eq. 3). This effectively removes the non-convex reprojection.
- **Anchoring:** To resolve inherent ambiguities (global scale, global pose), the first camera's pose and scale are fixed (`R_1 = I_3, t_1 = 0, s_1 = 1`).

#### 3.2. QCQP and Convex SDP Relaxation

Despite removing the reprojection term, the SBA problem (Eq. 3) remains non-convex due to the `SE(3)` constraints and the product terms `s_i R_i`. The authors tackle this with a two-step relaxation:
- **Marginalization and QCQP Transformation:** They first analytically marginalize out the translation `t_i` and landmark `p_k` variables (Proposition 2). This reduces the problem to only optimizing over `U = [I_3, s_2 R_2, ..., s_N R_N]`. By defining a new variable `~R_i = s_i R_i`, belonging to the "scaled orthogonal group" `sO(3)`, the problem is cast as a Quadratically Constrained Quadratic Program (QCQP) (Eq. 8). While `sO(3)` itself is non-convex, it can be described by simple quadratic equality constraints (Eq. 7).
- **Shor's Semidefinite Relaxation:** To "convexify" the QCQP, Shor's semidefinite relaxation is applied. This involves introducing a larger matrix variable `X := U^T U` and reformulating the objective and constraints linearly in `X`, plus adding a convex Positive Semidefinite (PSD) constraint `X ⪰ 0` (Eq. 11). This results in a convex Semidefinite Program (SDP), whose global optimum can be found efficiently (in theory). Crucially, if the optimal solution `X*` of the SDP has a rank of 3 (the intrinsic dimension of rotations), the relaxation is "tight," meaning its solution directly corresponds to the global optimum of the original non-convex QCQP (Proposition 5). The suboptimality gap `η` (Eq. 16) allows for certification of global optimality.

#### 3.3. Burer-Monteiro Factorization and CUDA-based Riemannian Optimization (XM Solver)

Solving large-scale SDPs directly is computationally prohibitive. The authors address this by:
- **Burer-Monteiro (BM) Factorization:** This technique exploits the empirically observed low rank (typically rank 3) of the optimal `X*` in many SDP relaxations. Instead of optimizing `X` directly, it factorizes `X = U^T U`, where `U` is a `r x 3N` matrix (with `r` being the rank, typically chosen as 3 or 4). This transforms the SDP back into a non-convex optimization problem (Eq. 19), but in a lower-dimensional manifold.
- **Riemannian Staircase Algorithm:** To solve the non-convex BM factorization, a "staircase" approach (Algorithm 1) is employed. It starts with the lowest possible rank `r=3` and attempts to find a local optimum using Riemannian optimization. It then checks for global optimality of the SDP using the *dual optimality certificate* (Theorem 8). If the local optimum found is *not* globally optimal for the SDP (indicated by a negative eigenvalue in the dual matrix `Z(y)`), the algorithm "escapes" the local minimum by increasing the rank `r` (Theorem 9) and continuing optimization. This iterative process guarantees convergence to the global optimum of the SDP (Theorem 10).
- **C++/CUDA-based Riemannian Optimizer (XM):** The non-convex BM factorization problem (Eq. 19) can be interpreted as an unconstrained Riemannian optimization problem on a product manifold of positive numbers (for scales) and Stiefel manifolds (for rotations) (Proposition 11). The authors implement the Riemannian trust-region algorithm with truncated conjugate gradient (Rtr-tCG) directly in C++/CUDA. This highly optimized GPU implementation, leveraging `cuBLAS` for matrix operations and custom CUDA kernels for manifold-specific operations (like retraction and Hessian-vector products), provides significant speedups (up to 100x compared to CPU-based MANOPT), making large-scale problem instances solvable in seconds.

#### 3.4. XM-SfM Pipeline

The XM solver forms the optimization engine of a full SfM pipeline:
- **View Graph Construction:** Utilizes COLMAP's feature extraction (SIFT) and exhaustive matching, followed by GLOMAP's track establishment to build the 2D view graph.
- **Depth Estimation:** Employs UNIDEPTH (or other models, as explored in Appendix B) to lift 2D keypoints to 3D, along with confidence weights.
- **Outlier Filtering:** An initial filtering step based on two-view relative pose estimation removes 3D landmarks with large Euclidean distance errors. An iterative refinement (XM²), re-running the solver after removing outliers with largest residuals, further enhances robustness.
- **XM Solver:** The core optimization step, solving the SBA problem (Eq. 11) using the CUDA-accelerated Riemannian staircase approach.
- **CERES Refinement:** Since learned depth can be noisy, the poses and landmarks estimated by XM are used as a warm-start for CERES to perform a final local optimization of the original non-convex BA problem (Eq. 1). This combines the global robustness of XM with the local accuracy of CERES.
- **Scale Regularization:** To prevent issues with noisy depth causing scales to collapse, a quadratic regularization term (Appendix D) is added to the SDP objective.

This comprehensive methodology ensures a robust, scalable, and globally consistent 3D reconstruction pipeline, moving beyond the limitations of prior art.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of XM and the XM-SfM pipeline is comprehensive, demonstrating their superior performance across various benchmarks.

#### 4.1. XM Solver Performance (BAL Dataset)

- **Initialization-Free Global Optimality:** On the BAL dataset, XM consistently achieves global optimality without requiring any initialization, a significant advantage over local solvers like CERES. Standard CERES fails catastrophically without a good initial guess, highlighting the susceptibility of non-convex BA to local minima.
- **Extreme Speed and Scalability:** XM demonstrates a remarkable speed advantage, being up to 100 times faster than MANOPT (a state-of-the-art CPU-based Riemannian optimization toolbox) on the same problem. For hundreds of camera frames, XM's solver time is sub-second, and it successfully scales to over 10,000 camera frames (BAL-10155), solving it in minutes, a scale previously beyond reach for certifiable methods. This validates the effectiveness of the custom C++/CUDA implementation.
- **Certifiable Results:** The suboptimality gaps are consistently very small, and the minimum eigenvalue of the dual matrix `Z(y)` is close to zero, providing numerical certification of global optimality for most tested instances.

#### 4.2. XM-SfM Pipeline Performance (Replica, Mip-Nerf/Zip-Nerf, IMC, TUM, C3VD Datasets)

- **Comparable Accuracy:**
 - **With Ground Truth Depth (Replica):** When provided with ground truth depth, XM-SfM (with minor filter refinement) achieves accuracy comparable to, and often slightly better than, COLMAP and GLOMAP.
 - **With Learned Depth (Mip-Nerf, IMC, TUM, C3VD):** Even with noisy learned depth, XM + CERES refinement delivers reconstruction quality comparable to COLMAP and GLOMAP. The paper shows that minor differences in ATE/RPE do not significantly impact downstream applications like 3D Gaussian Splatting for novel view synthesis.
- **Massive Speedup and Scalability for SfM:** The most striking result is the substantial speed advantage of XM-SfM. Its solver time is consistently 100 to 1000 times faster than COLMAP and GLOMAP. For a 2000-frame Replica dataset, XM solves it within 10 seconds, while COLMAP takes over 20 hours. This shifts the computational bottleneck from the BA solver to preprocessing steps like feature matching, indexing, and depth estimation, paving the way for significantly faster and larger-scale reconstructions.
- **Robustness to Learned Depth Noise:** XM-SfM shows robustness to inaccuracies in learned depth, tolerating noise levels up to `ϵ=1.5` before significant degradation in accuracy. The underlying SDP relaxation itself is even more robust, breaking only at `ϵ=5`. The pipeline is also shown to be robust to the choice of different depth estimation models (Appendix B).
- **Effectiveness of Riemannian Staircase:** The paper provides a clear demonstration (Fig. 10) of how the Riemannian Staircase algorithm successfully escapes poor local minima. On a Mip-Nerf dataset, directly solving the rank-3 BM factorization led to a collapsed, messy reconstruction (a local minimum), while increasing the rank to 4 allowed the solver to find the globally optimal solution. This underscores the theoretical guarantees and practical utility of the staircase method.

In summary, the findings strongly support the claim that XM offers a faster, more scalable, and initialization-free solution for global bundle adjustment, while maintaining high accuracy comparable to existing state-of-the-art pipelines, even when relying on learned monocular depth.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Building Rome with Convex Optimization" paper represents a significant advancement in the field of 3D reconstruction and robotics, with several layers of impact:

#### 5.1. Revolutionizing Bundle Adjustment

- **Initialization-Free Global Optimality:** By providing an algorithm that can solve global BA to certifiable optimality without requiring a good initial guess, XM fundamentally changes how large-scale BA problems can be tackled. This eliminates a major headache for practitioners and enables robust reconstruction in challenging scenarios where good initializations are hard to obtain.
- **Scalability for Large-Scale Reconstruction:** The demonstrated ability to process thousands, even tens of thousands, of camera frames in minutes or hours (compared to days for traditional methods) is a game-changer. This unlocks the potential for rapid, high-quality 3D reconstruction of entire environments, cities, or long-duration robot trajectories, moving beyond the limitations of incremental or less precise global methods.
- **Bridging Deep Learning and Optimization:** The work effectively integrates modern deep learning-based monocular depth prediction with classical geometric optimization, showing how noisy, learned perception can be effectively harnessed and refined by robust mathematical optimization frameworks to achieve superior performance.

#### 5.2. Advancing Certifiable Optimization in Robotics

- **Practical Viability of SDPs:** The most critical impact on the broader field of certifiable optimization is demonstrating that complex SDP relaxations, previously often considered too computationally expensive for real-world applications beyond toy examples, can indeed be made *practically viable* at extreme scales. The custom C++/CUDA implementation of the Riemannian trust-region algorithm is a major engineering feat that proves this point. This paves the way for wider adoption of certifiable methods in robotics, where guaranteed optimality and robustness are paramount for safety-critical applications.
- **Generalizable Solver:** The core XM solver (BM factorization with Riemannian staircase) is not specific to BA. Its successful application here suggests its potential for other challenging non-convex geometric problems in robotics that can be formulated as low-rank SDPs, such as generalized pose graph optimization, sensor calibration, or simultaneous localization and mapping in highly cluttered or dynamic environments.

#### 5.3. Impact on Future Robotics and Vision Systems

- **Faster and More Reliable SfM/SLAM:** The significant speedup and reliability offered by XM-SfM can accelerate research and development in various areas dependent on 3D reconstruction. This includes rapid mapping for autonomous vehicles, large-scale augmented reality, creating digital twins of cities, and precise scene understanding for robotic manipulation.
- **Foundation for Real-time Global Systems:** By shifting the computational bottleneck from the solver to preprocessing, the paper opens up exciting avenues for achieving *real-time global reconstruction* for massive datasets. Further GPU acceleration of feature matching, indexing, and data matrix construction, as outlined in future work, could make this a reality.
- **Exploration of Challenging Domains:** The framework's ability to handle noisy learned depth and its robust performance on diverse datasets (including medical images) suggests its potential for applications in data-scarce or unique environments (e.g., underwater, space, medical endoscopy) where precise ground-truth measurements or training data are hard to come by.

#### 5.4. Limitations and Future Work

The authors acknowledge several limitations that also point to exciting avenues for future research:
- **Outlier Handling:** While an outlier filtering step exists, more sophisticated robust estimation techniques (beyond `ℓ2` based filtering) could further improve performance in very noisy scenarios.
- **Sparse Structure Exploitation:** The current CUDA solver leverages dense matrix operations. Extending it to efficiently handle sparse patterns, common in SLAM camera sequences, would significantly broaden its applicability.
- **Preprocessing Bottlenecks:** The paper explicitly identifies feature matching, indexing, and matrix construction as current bottlenecks. Further porting these components to CUDA would likely unlock another order of magnitude in speedup for the overall pipeline.
- **Reliance on Depth Prediction Models:** While robust to different models, the quality of reconstruction ultimately depends on the quality of the learned depth. This remains a challenge in data-scarce domains, suggesting a need for more generalizable or self-supervised depth estimation techniques.

In conclusion, "Building Rome with Convex Optimization" is a landmark paper that pushes the boundaries of large-scale 3D reconstruction by successfully integrating advanced convex optimization, high-performance computing, and learned monocular depth. Its contribution lies not just in theoretical elegance but, crucially, in demonstrating the practical feasibility and superior performance of a certifiable global optimization approach to a fundamental computer vision problem. This work is poised to significantly impact future developments in robust and scalable robotic perception and mapping.
