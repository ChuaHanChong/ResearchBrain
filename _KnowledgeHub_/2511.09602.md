# ScaleADFG: Affordance-based Dexterous Functional Grasping via Scalable Dataset

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from CASIA, CasiaHand Robotics, and SenseTime developed ScaleADFG, an affordance-based framework for dexterous functional grasping that includes a large-scale, multi-hand, scale-variant dataset. This approach automates data generation using foundation models and demonstrates robust zero-shot transfer to real-world objects, achieving a 76.0% grasp success rate on a real robot.

## Problem

- Existing methods for generating dexterous functional grasp datasets are labor-intensive, resulting in limited data volume and diversity, which hinders the training of robust deep learning models.
- Robotic grasping solutions lack generalizability to the wide range of object scales encountered in real-world environments, often failing for objects outside a narrow size range due to data scarcity in scale variations.
- Datasets built from human demonstrations inherit biases toward human hand size and kinematics, producing grasps that are imprecise or ill-suited for robotic hands with different proportions.

## Method

- An automated pipeline was developed to generate a large-scale, multi-hand, and scale-variant dataset by leveraging pre-trained foundation models for 3D asset creation and affordance retrieval.
- An affordance-based optimization method, incorporating an axis-alignment initialization strategy, synthesizes functional grasps that adapt to various object scales for multiple dexterous hands.
- ScaleADFG-Net, a lightweight Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE), was designed for efficient, generalizable, and scale-adaptive grasp prediction from the generated data.

## Results

- The ScaleADFG dataset comprises over 60,000 functional grasps per hand across 5 object categories, each with 15 scale variations, demonstrating high average stability (91.60%) and functional ratio (81.20%) in human evaluations.
- ScaleADFG-Net, trained on the synthetic dataset, achieved a 94.58% functional grasping success rate in comparative benchmarks and showed robust zero-shot transfer capabilities to real-world objects.
- Real robot experiments with an Allegro Hand yielded an overall grasp success rate of 76.0% and a functional success rate of 58.7% across 15 novel objects, maintaining consistent performance across small, medium, and large object scales.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Utilizing pre-trained foundation models for 3D asset generation and affordance retrieval provides an effective and scalable solution for constructing large, diverse datasets for robotic manipulation.
> - Explicitly incorporating wide object scale variations into training data is crucial for developing grasp models that exhibit robust generalization to unseen object sizes in real-world scenarios.
> - An affordance-based grasp synthesis approach, combined with efficient optimization and a lightweight prediction network, enables strong zero-shot transfer from synthetic training data to real-world functional grasping tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The ability to perform dexterous functional grasping is a critical capability for advanced robotic manipulation, underpinning a vast array of applications from domestic service robots to precision industrial tasks. This paper addresses a core challenge in this field: enabling robots to grasp objects not just for stability (universal grasping) but specifically to perform a subsequent task or manipulation (functional grasping).

Historically, the development of robotic grasping has progressed along two main lines: analytical methods, which rely on geometric and physical models to synthesize grasps, and data-driven methods, which leverage large datasets and machine learning to learn grasping strategies. Recent advancements in deep learning have significantly improved the generalizability of data-driven approaches. However, a major bottleneck, particularly for dexterous *functional* grasping, has been the scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and semantically rich datasets.

Existing methods for generating dexterous grasp datasets often suffer from several limitations:
1. **Reliance on human demonstrations:** Approaches like directly imitating human hand poses or retargeting human contact maps are labor-intensive, yield limited data, and often introduce biases related to human hand size and kinematics. This typically leads to datasets skewed towards larger objects and less precise functional grasps for robotic hands, which may have different kinematic properties or relative sizes to objects.
2. **Limited scalability and diversity:** Many datasets rely on existing object repositories or manual 3D scanning, which restricts their ability to scale to the vast diversity of real-world objects and their varying shapes and sizes.
3. **Lack of scale variability:** Most datasets do not explicitly account for the wide range of object scales found in real-world environments, or the significant size mismatch between large robotic hands and common everyday objects. This leads to poor generalization when robots encounter objects of different sizes than those seen in training.

This paper positions itself by tackling these critical limitations directly. It proposes an **affordance-based grasp synthesis** approach as a promising alternative. Affordances, first conceptualized by Gibson, describe the actionable properties of objects that suggest potential interactions. By focusing on object affordances (e.g., the handle of a drill, the button of a spray bottle), robots can develop more intuitive, adaptive, and generalizable grasping strategies that align more closely with human cognitive processes for interaction, rather than merely imitating specific human hand postures.

Crucially, the ScaleADFG framework leverages the power of **pre-trained foundation models** – a significant trend in modern AI. By utilizing advanced vision models for image segmentation (like Grounded SAM 2) and generative models for 3D reconstruction (like InstantMesh, TripoSR, TRELLIS), the framework automates the creation of vast numbers of 3D object assets and the efficient identification of their affordance parts. This addresses the scalability and diversity challenges that plague traditional dataset generation methods.

In summary, ScaleADFG advances the field by:
- **Filling the data gap:** Creating a large-scale, multi-hand, *functional* grasp dataset that explicitly incorporates *scale variation*, which is a novel and critical contribution.
- **Automating data generation:** Moving away from human demonstrations and manual labeling towards an efficient, automated, and scalable pipeline using state-of-the-art generative AI.
- **Enhancing generalizability:** Developing a method that inherently handles object scale variations and offers zero-shot transfer capabilities to real-world objects, a key requirement for practical robot deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation behind the ScaleADFG research is to overcome the significant challenges currently preventing dexterous functional tool-use grasping from becoming widely deployable in robotic systems. The ability for a robot to effectively pick up and utilize a tool for a specific task (e.g., pressing a button on a drill, squeezing a spray bottle) is fundamental for advanced autonomy in both domestic and industrial settings.

The authors identify two primary limitations in existing approaches that their work aims to address:
1. **Inefficient construction of large-scale datasets:** Current methods for generating dexterous functional grasp datasets are labor-intensive, slow, and constrained by the quantity and biases inherent in human demonstrations. This scarcity of high-quality, diverse data severely hampers the training of robust deep learning models for grasping.
2. **Lack of generalizability to everyday object scales:** A significant practical hurdle is the size mismatch between many robotic hands (which tend to be larger than human hands) and the wide variety of object scales encountered in the real world. Existing datasets often overlook or inadequately represent this scale diversity, leading to robotic grasps that are unstable or non-functional for objects outside a narrow size range.

To address these limitations, the ScaleADFG framework sets out several key objectives:

- **To propose a novel, fully automated dataset construction pipeline:** This pipeline is designed to synthesize diverse tool-use grasp configurations without requiring expert human demonstrations. The core idea is to leverage object affordances rather than imitation, making the process scalable and less biased.
- **To create a comprehensive, large-scale dexterous functional grasp dataset (ScaleADFG-Dataset):** This dataset is a central contribution, specifically designed to:
 - Allow flexible object-hand size ratios, enabling larger robotic hands to effectively grasp a broad range of everyday objects, including smaller ones.
 - Leverage pre-trained foundation models (for 3D asset generation and affordance retrieval) to generate extensive 3D assets and efficiently identify object affordances.
 - Feature unprecedented diversity, including over 1,000 unique shapes per category, each presented in 15 distinct scale variations, supporting two different dexterous robotic hands (Allegro and Shadow Hands). The filtered dataset is expected to contain over 60,000 grasps per hand.
- **To develop a lightweight and generalizable grasp generation network (ScaleADFG-Net):** This network aims to demonstrate the efficacy of the large-scale dataset. Key objectives for the network include:
 - Being a single-stage network with a simple loss design, eliminating the need for complex post-refinement steps.
 - Exhibiting strong adaptability and generalization to objects of varying scales.
 - Enhancing functional grasp stability, diversity, and overall generalizability.
 - Achieving effective zero-shot transfer from synthetic training data to real-world objects, demonstrating practical applicability.

By achieving these objectives, the ScaleADFG project aims to provide a fundamental advancement in robotic grasping, paving the way for more capable and autonomous robots in real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ScaleADFG framework is meticulously designed with two primary components: a fully automated, scalable dataset construction pipeline (ScaleADFG-Dataset) and a lightweight, generalizable grasp prediction network (ScaleADFG-Net).

#### 3.1. ScaleADFG-Dataset Construction Pipeline

The pipeline for generating the ScaleADFG-Dataset is built to automate the creation of high-quality, diverse, and scale-variant dexterous functional grasps.

1. **Generation of Large-Scale 3D Assets:**
 - **Source Data:** A vast collection of 2D images is gathered through extensive internet searches using relevant keywords.
 - **Image Processing:** Grounded SAM 2, an advanced segmentation model, is employed to segment and filter these 2D images, isolating individual objects.
 - **3D Reconstruction:** Multiple pre-trained generative models (InstantMesh, TripoSR, and TRELLIS) are utilized to convert the segmented 2D images into 3D object assets. Using diverse generative models ensures a wider variety of mesh structures and mitigates biases from any single model.
 - **Quality Control:** The generated 3D meshes undergo a multi-stage filtering process:
 - Manual screening of segmented images for irrelevance.
 - Automated filtering based on mesh file size (1M–10M) to remove degenerate geometries.
 - Final filtering based on grasp quality metrics (e.g., dG, dF, dIP, dSP thresholds, defined later) after grasp synthesis. Notably, even abnormal but graspable meshes are retained to enhance the dataset's robustness to real-world imperfections.

2. **Affordance Retrieval for Category Objects:**
 - **Feature Extraction:** The PTv3-object model from SAM-Part3D (which distills features from FeatUp-DINOv2) is used as a robust 3D feature extractor for object point clouds. These models are chosen for their ability to learn highly generalized and semantically consistent feature representations, making them robust to intra-category shape variations.
 - **Template Annotation:** A single, randomly selected object template for each category is manually annotated with functional and grasping part labels. This minimal manual effort is crucial for scalability.
 - **Affordance Propagation:** Dense feature matching is performed to transfer these affordance labels from the annotated template to all other instances within the same object category. The robustness of DINOv2-derived features ensures reliable correspondence despite significant shape or rotational differences between objects, minimizing template-specific bias.

3. **Dexterous Functional Grasp Optimization:**
 - **Affordance-based Strategy:** Grasps are synthesized by encouraging close proximity between the identified functional and grasping parts on both the object and the robotic hand. Physical plausibility is enforced by minimizing hand-object and hand self-penetration.
 - **Scale Variance Handling:** The method explicitly addresses object scale. For each category, 15 distinct scales, sampled within a defined real-world range (S_low to S_high), are used. The algorithm adapts by changing hand gestures for smaller objects, which is critical for accommodating the relatively large size of robotic hands compared to human hands and avoiding self-collisions.
 - **Axis Alignment Initialization:** To counter the sensitivity of optimization-based grasp synthesis to initial configurations, an innovative axis alignment strategy is introduced.
 - **Axes Definition:** Both hand and object are assigned Grasp-to-Functional Axes (GF_i) and Force-Application Axes (FA_i), derived from relative positions of contact regions, functional parts, and fingertips. For the thumb, three GF axes are defined due to its high mobility.
 - **Alignment Process:** Primary axes (GF_i) are aligned first, followed by secondary axes (FA_i) within the orthogonal plane.
 - **Finalization:** The hand is translated to initial collision-free contact, and finger joints are set to a slightly flexed posture to aid optimization. This initialization significantly improves efficiency and grasp quality, leading to convergence in <200 steps and synthesis of 150 grasps in <30 seconds.
 - **Affordance-based Optimization (Loss Functions):** The optimization minimizes a total loss (L_total) comprising weighted sums of:
 - **Functional Part Distance Loss (L_F):** Chamfer Distance between hand anchor points (A_H_F) and object functional part (A_O_F).
 - **Grasping Part Distance Loss (L_G):** Chamfer Distance between hand grasping anchor points (A_H_G) and object grasping part (A_O_G).
 - **Force Closure Loss (L_FC):** Ensures grasp stability by minimizing deviations from ideal force-torque equilibrium and friction constraints.
 - **Hand-Object Interpenetration Loss (L_IP):** Penalizes negative Signed Distance Field (SDF) values from hand mesh points to the object.
 - **Hand Self Penetration Loss (L_SP):** Penalizes negative SDF values between different parts of the robotic hand.
 - **Refinement and Filtering:** Post-optimization, grasps are filtered based on quality metrics and validated in a simulation environment to remove collisions and ensure success.

#### 3.2. ScaleADFG-Net (Grasp Prediction Network)

The ScaleADFG-Net is a lightweight, single-stage network designed for efficient grasp prediction on novel objects and scales.

- **Architecture:** It employs a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE) architecture, known for its ability to generate diverse outputs conditioned on input.
- **Feature Processing:** The network uses the pre-trained PTv3 Object model (from SAMPart3D) and PointNet for robust feature extraction from object point clouds.
- **Input & Encoder:** Object point clouds, along with the first three principal components of their extracted features, are fed into the encoder, which maps them to a latent space (μ, Σ).
- **Decoder:** The decoder then predicts the grasp configuration (joint angles, palm pose) from this latent space.
- **Differentiable Forward Kinematics:** This component converts the predicted grasp configuration into a specific hand pose in 3D space.
- **Loss Functions:**
 - **Reconstruction Loss (L_Rec):** Chamfer Distance between the point cloud of the predicted hand pose and the ground truth hand pose. This point-cloud-based loss implicitly constrains all aspects of the grasp (joint angles, rotation, translation) efficiently.
 - **KL Divergence (L_KLD):** Regularizes the latent space to a standard Gaussian distribution, promoting generalizability and enabling diverse grasp generation.
- **Inference:** During inference, the encoder is removed. Latent codes are sampled from a standard Gaussian distribution, and the decoder generates functional grasps for previously unseen objects and scales, demonstrating zero-shot transfer capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across dataset quality, model performance, and real-world applicability.

#### 4.1. ScaleADFG Dataset Statistics and Quality

- **Scale and Diversity:** The ScaleADFG dataset comprises 5 object categories, each containing over 1,000 unique shapes. For each shape, 15 distinct scale variations are generated, covering common real-world sizes. After filtering, the dataset includes over 60,000 functional grasps for each of the two dexterous robotic hands (Allegro Hand and Shadow Hand).
- **Dataset Comparison (Table I):** A comparison with existing dexterous grasp datasets (DexGraspNet, ContactDB, OakInk, DexFG, etc.) highlights that ScaleADFG is unique in offering a large-scale, functional, multi-hand dataset with significant *scale variety*. This addresses a critical gap in the research landscape.
- **Dataset Quality (Table II):** A human study involving 50 objects per category (3 participants) confirmed the high quality of the dataset:
 - Low average abnormal shape ratio (5.60%), indicating effective 3D asset generation and filtering.
 - Low error ratios for functional part (7.20%) and grasping part (2.00%) annotations, demonstrating high accuracy of affordance retrieval.
 - High average stable ratio (91.60%) and functional ratio (81.20%) for the synthesized grasps, confirming the efficacy of the affordance-based optimization.
 - High affordance accuracy (Functional Part: 91.12%, Grasping Part: 87.69%) further validates the quality of the automated affordance labeling.

#### 4.2. Effect of Object Scale Diversity and Dataset Volume on Grasp Prediction

- **Effect of Dataset Volume (Table III):** Experiments showed a direct correlation between the volume of training data and the performance of the grasp prediction network. As the number of grasps per category increased (from 0.1k to 10k), there was a significant improvement across all metrics: stability (SR ↑), contact (dG ↓), functionality (dF ↓), and penetration (dIP ↓, dSP ↓). This empirically validates the necessity of large-scale datasets for training robust and efficient grasp models.
- **Effect of Object Scale Diversity (Fig. 4):** The model demonstrated strong generalization to previously unseen scales, both interpolated (e.g., 1.5x) and extrapolated (e.g., 14.5x). Crucially, increasing the diversity of training scales consistently enhanced grasp quality for both seen and unseen objects. The performance on unseen objects remained comparable to that on seen objects, confirming the robust scale generalization capabilities of the ScaleADFG framework.

#### 4.3. Evaluation of ScaleADFG Net on Transferring from Generated to Real Objects

- **Zero-Shot Transfer (Table IV):** The ScaleADFG-Net, trained exclusively on the synthetic ScaleADFG dataset, exhibited strong zero-shot transfer capabilities to real-world objects. When tested on the DexFG dataset (comprising real objects), the model's performance was largely comparable to its performance on its own generated synthetic test set, although with a slight decrease in some metrics. This finding is critical for the practical deployment of robots, as it reduces the need for extensive real-world data collection.

#### 4.4. Comparison with Other Methods

- **Grasp Synthesis Across Varying Scales (Fig. 5):** ScaleADFG demonstrated superior adaptability to different object scales (0.75x, 1x, 1.25x) compared to DexFG. ScaleADFG achieved more consistent contact, lower hand-object interpenetration, and better functional contact across scales. DexFG, being reliant on rigid contact maps, showed limited adaptability, especially for smaller objects where rigid contact constraints became problematic. ScaleADFG's ability to adapt hand gestures to object scale proved advantageous.
- **Grasp Prediction Quality (Table V):** When compared with DexFuncGrasp and FunctionalGrasp on common categories (using their respective test datasets and object scales), ScaleADFG-Net achieved significantly higher average success rates (94.58%) for functional grasping. This highlights the superior generalizability and grasp quality offered by the ScaleADFG framework and its underlying dataset.

#### 4.5. Experiments on Real Robot

- **Setup:** Real-world experiments were conducted using an Aubo I5 robotic arm, an Allegro Hand, and a Mech-Eye PRO M camera, with a diverse set of 15 novel objects varying in category and scale (Fig. 7). ICP registration with offline-generated object models and Grounded-SAM 2 based 3D reconstruction were used for object pose estimation.
- **Performance (Table VI):** The system achieved an overall Grasp Success Rate (SR) of 76.0% and a Functional Success Rate (SR_func) of 58.7% across all objects.
- **Scale Invariance:** Crucially, performance remained consistent across small, medium, and large object scales within each category, confirming the strong generalization and scale-invariance of the ScaleADFG framework in real-world scenarios.
- **Failure Analysis (Fig. 6):** The authors also identify common failure modes:
 1. Difficulties in synthesizing collision-free grasps during optimization for objects with narrow or complex geometries (e.g., two closely spaced handles).
 2. Slight collisions introduced by the network's predicted grasps during inference.
 3. Functional finger contact slippage on the real robot due to object motion or imprecise force application during grasping.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ScaleADFG framework represents a significant step forward in the field of robotic dexterous manipulation, with profound implications for both fundamental research and practical applications.

1. **Bridging the Data Gap for Dexterous Functional Grasping:** The most immediate and critical impact is the creation of a truly large-scale, diverse, and semantically rich dataset for dexterous functional grasping. By specifically addressing object scale variations and providing multi-hand support, ScaleADFG fills a long-standing gap that has hindered the development of robust, generalizable grasping solutions for high-degree-of-freedom robotic hands. This dataset will serve as a vital resource for the research community, enabling the training of more sophisticated and capable grasping policies.

2. **Advancing Automated Data Generation with Foundation Models:** The methodology for automatically generating 3D assets and affordance labels using pre-trained foundation models (segmentation, generative 3D, feature extractors) is a paradigm shift. It significantly reduces the reliance on laborious human demonstrations and manual annotations, accelerating the pace of research in data-driven robotics. This automated pipeline can be adapted for creating diverse datasets in other areas of robotic interaction, demonstrating the power of integrating state-of-the-art AI models into robotics.

3. **Enhancing Generalizability and Robustness for Real-World Deployment:** The demonstrated adaptability to varying object scales and the strong zero-shot transfer capabilities to real-world objects are crucial for practical robotic deployment. Robots operating in unstructured environments will encounter objects of various sizes and shapes. ScaleADFG's ability to handle this variability, confirmed by extensive simulation and real-robot experiments, makes it a highly promising approach for robust and reliable manipulation.

4. **Validating Affordance-Based Grasping:** The success of the ScaleADFG framework reinforces the efficacy of affordance-based grasp synthesis. By reasoning about the functional properties of objects rather than relying on rigid contact maps or direct imitation of human kinematics, robots can develop more intuitive, adaptive, and fundamental grasping strategies. This approach inherently offers greater flexibility and generalizability compared to methods sensitive to hand-object scale ratios.

5. **Impact on Robotic Applications:**
 - **Tool Use in Diverse Settings:** The framework directly benefits applications requiring tool manipulation, from industrial assembly and factory automation to domestic service robots (e.g., using a spray bottle, operating kitchen appliances) and even assistive robotics.
 - **Dexterous Hand Utilization:** It unlocks the full potential of advanced multi-finger robotic hands, providing them with the intelligence to perform complex functional tasks effectively.
 - **Reduced Development Costs:** By enabling training on synthetic data and achieving zero-shot transfer, ScaleADFG can significantly reduce the cost and time associated with real-world data collection and model fine-tuning for new tasks or environments.

6. **Paving the Way for Future Research:** The work lays a solid foundation for addressing even more complex challenges in dexterous manipulation. The authors identify clear future directions, including extending the framework to deformable objects, more complex articulated tools (e.g., scissors), and cluttered real-world scenes with heavy occlusion. Furthermore, moving beyond grasp generation to fine-grained post-grasp manipulation is a natural and impactful next step.

In conclusion, ScaleADFG represents a comprehensive solution to fundamental problems in dexterous functional grasping. By integrating advanced AI models for data generation and proposing an innovative affordance-based optimization strategy, it delivers a highly scalable, robust, and generalizable framework that promises to accelerate the deployment of intelligent robotic systems capable of complex interactions with the world.
