# SoftGym: Benchmarking Deep Reinforcement Learning for Deformable Object Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> SoftGym introduces an open-source benchmark featuring simulated environments for deep reinforcement learning (RL) on deformable object manipulation tasks, addressing the lack of standardized platforms in this complex domain. The evaluation of various RL algorithms demonstrates that current image-based methods struggle considerably with the high-dimensional states and intricate dynamics characteristic of deformable objects.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation of deformable objects, such as cloth or fluid, is challenging due to their high-dimensional state representation and complex, non-linear dynamics.
- A lack of standardized benchmarks for deformable object manipulation hinders systematic comparison and reproducible research in reinforcement learning.
- Existing RL benchmarks primarily focus on rigid objects, often assuming low-dimensional, fully observable state representations that are not applicable to deformable objects.

## Method

- The paper introduces SoftGym, an open-source physics-based simulation benchmark built on Nvidia FleX, providing a unified OpenAI Gym API for deformable object manipulation tasks.
- SoftGym includes diverse tasks across categories like SoftGym-Medium, SoftGym-Hard, and SoftGym-Robot, involving cloth, rope, and fluids with abstract or robotic manipulator action spaces.
- The benchmark evaluates state-of-the-art RL algorithms across full state oracle, reduced state oracle, and image-based observation settings to systematically assess their performance.

## Results

- The Dynamics Oracle (CEM), leveraging ground-truth state and dynamics, achieved the highest performance across most tasks, establishing an empirical upper bound.
- Image-based RL methods (PlaNet, CURL-SAC, DrQ) showed significantly lower success rates, particularly in tasks like StraightenRope, SpreadCloth, and FoldCloth, struggling to learn from high-dimensional visual observations.
- Qualitative comparisons indicated that SoftGym's simulated cloth behavior resembles real-world cloth manipulation by a robot, suggesting the benchmark's relevance for real-world applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Existing image-based reinforcement learning algorithms perform substantially worse than oracle methods on deformable object manipulation tasks, indicating a significant gap in visual perception and dynamics modeling.
> - Simply providing raw, high-dimensional ground-truth particle positions to standard MLP policies (Full State Oracle) does not effectively enable learning, suggesting the need for specialized architectures or state encoding.
> - The effectiveness of a reduced state oracle heavily depends on the chosen low-dimensional features, highlighting the challenge of deriving adequate state representations for deformable objects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation of deformable objects, such as cloth, rope, or fluids, presents a significant challenge in the field of robotics. These tasks are prevalent in daily life and industrial applications, including laundry folding, food preparation, or handling cables. The complexity arises from the high-dimensional state representation required to describe deformable objects and their intricate, non-linear dynamics. Traditional methods, often relying on analytical models and motion planning, face difficulties due to the vast configuration spaces involved.

Recent advancements in deep reinforcement learning (RL) have shown promise in enabling robots to learn complex manipulation skills through data-driven approaches. Many successful RL benchmarks exist for rigid or articulated objects, often assuming direct observability of a low-dimensional state that fully characterizes the environment's dynamics, such as joint angles or object positions. However, such low-dimensional, sufficient state representations are often difficult or impossible to perceive or even define for deformable objects. Consequently, for deformable object manipulation, robots typically rely on high-dimensional observations, such as camera images.

Existing research exploring learning-based methods for deformable object manipulation frequently evaluates algorithms on different task variants, using diverse simulators or robot setups. This lack of standardization makes direct comparison and reproducible research challenging, hindering systematic progress in the domain. There is a recognized absence of a unified benchmark for evaluating and comparing different approaches for deformable object manipulation within the RL community. While some recent simulators, like those built on Nvidia PhysX, support deformable objects, they often do not provide pre-defined tasks or assets for their manipulation. This paper addresses this gap by introducing SoftGym, a standardized benchmark designed to facilitate the development and comparison of RL algorithms for deformable object manipulation, thereby aiming to accelerate research in this complex area.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce SoftGym, an open-source collection of simulated benchmark environments specifically designed for the manipulation of deformable objects using deep reinforcement learning. SoftGym aims to provide a standardized platform with a common OpenAI Gym API and a Python interface to enable reproducible research in this domain.

The motivation behind developing SoftGym is multifaceted:

- **Addressing the lack of standardized benchmarks:** The authors identify a significant gap in the current RL landscape, where existing benchmarks primarily focus on rigid or articulated objects and lack environments that incorporate the challenges of deformable objects. This absence makes it difficult to compare and evaluate the performance of different RL algorithms systematically.
- **Facilitating reproducible research:** By offering a standardized set of tasks and environments, SoftGym intends to enable fair comparisons between different algorithmic approaches for deformable object manipulation. This is crucial for identifying effective strategies and fostering cumulative progress in the field.
- **Highlighting challenges for current RL algorithms:** The benchmark is designed to expose fundamental difficulties encountered by contemporary RL algorithms when dealing with deformable objects. These challenges include:
 - **High-dimensional state representation:** Deformable objects inherently possess a large number of degrees of freedom, leading to state spaces that are orders of magnitude larger than those for rigid bodies. This makes both state estimation and policy learning challenging.
 - **Complex dynamics:** The non-linear and often chaotic dynamics of deformable objects are difficult to model and predict, complicating model-based RL approaches and exploration strategies.
 - **Partial observability from visual input:** In real-world scenarios, robots primarily rely on visual observations (e.g., camera images). Inferring the underlying high-dimensional state of a deformable object from these pixel-based inputs, which are often partially observable, presents a significant perception challenge.
 - **Varying object properties and configurations:** Robotic tasks often require agents to generalize to objects of different sizes, shapes, and initial configurations, adding another layer of complexity.
- **Motivating new algorithmic development:** By systematically evaluating a range of existing algorithms, SoftGym aims to pinpoint their strengths and limitations in the context of deformable object manipulation. The expected suboptimal performance of current methods, particularly those relying solely on visual observations, is intended to motivate the development of novel RL algorithms specifically tailored to overcome these challenges.
- **Decoupling low-level control from high-level planning:** SoftGym offers abstract action spaces in many tasks to allow researchers to focus on high-level planning and reasoning about deformable object behavior, rather than spending effort on learning low-level grasping skills.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SoftGym is built upon the Nvidia FleX physics simulator, which models deformable objects using a particle and position-based dynamical system. Objects are represented by a collection of particles and internal constraints. Each particle has attributes such as position, velocity, and inverse mass. Physical properties like density, stiffness, and bending resistance are characterized by these constraints. FleX computes predicted particle positions and then projects them onto feasible sets defined by constraints to update their states. Fluids are modeled with constant density constraints, ropes as sequences of particles connected by springs, and cloth as a grid of particles with stretching and bending constraints, alongside self-collision handling.

SoftGym comprises three categories of tasks: SoftGym-Medium, SoftGym-Hard, and SoftGym-Robot. SoftGym-Medium includes six tasks evaluated extensively, while SoftGym-Hard features four more challenging tasks. SoftGym-Robot offers tasks with robotic manipulators (Sawyer or Franka) as the action space, in contrast to the abstract action spaces used in SoftGym-Medium and SoftGym-Hard.

**Action Space:**
For tasks in SoftGym-Medium and SoftGym-Hard, abstract action spaces are employed to separate high-level planning challenges from low-level grasping.
- **Rope and Cloth Manipulation:** Utilizes "pickers," simplified grippers modeled as spheres. An agent's action is a 4n-vector (where n is the number of pickers), specifying 3D movement (dx, dy, dz) and a binary activation state (p) for each picker. If activated, the closest particle on the object attaches to the picker and moves with it.
- **Fluid Tasks:** The agent directly controls the position and rotation of the cup holding the fluid. For instance, in PourWater, actions are (dx, dy, dθ) for displacement and rotation.
- **SoftGym-Robot:** Employs Cartesian control of the end-effector of a simulated Sawyer or Franka robot.

**Tasks:**
SoftGym-Medium includes:
- **TransportWater:** Move a cup of water to a target position without spilling. Action: scalar displacement (dx). Reward: negative distance to target, penalty for spilled water.
- **PourWater:** Pour water from one cup into a target cup. Action: (dx, dy, dθ) for cup position and rotation. Reward: fraction of water poured into the target cup.
- **StraightenRope:** Straighten a crumpled rope using two pickers. Reward: negative absolute difference between current endpoint distance and straightened rope length.
- **SpreadCloth:** Spread a crumpled cloth on the floor using two pickers. Reward: area covered by the cloth.
- **FoldCloth:** Fold a flattened cloth in half using two pickers. Reward: distance between corresponding particles of the two halves, penalty for cloth displacement.
- **DropCloth:** Lay a cloth flat on the floor, starting with two corners held by pickers. Requires swinging and dropping motions. Reward: mean particle-wise L2 distance to a flat target configuration.

SoftGym-Hard includes:
- **PourWaterAmount:** Similar to PourWater, but requires pouring a specific amount of water.
- **FoldCrumpledCloth:** Similar to FoldCloth, but the cloth is initially crumpled.
- **DropFoldCloth:** Similar initial state to DropCloth, but requires folding the cloth instead of just laying it flat.
- **RopeConfiguration:** Manipulate a rope into a specific target configuration (e.g., letters). Reward: minimum bipartite matching distance between current and goal particle positions.

**Task Variations:** To ensure robustness and generalization, each task incorporates variations in object properties, such as cup size, water volume, cloth size, and initial configurations (e.g., crumpled cloth, twisted rope). These variations are pre-computed for training (800) and evaluation (200).

**Observation Spaces Evaluated:** The study evaluates algorithms under three observation settings:
- **Full State Oracle:** Access to ground-truth positions and velocities of all particles of the deformable object, plus proprioception. Used with gradient-free optimization (e.g., Cross Entropy Method - CEM) and model predictive control (MPC). This serves as a theoretical upper bound without learned parameters. For SAC policies, it provides the full particle positions as input.
- **Reduced State Oracle:** Access to a hand-defined, low-dimensional subset of the full state (e.g., positions of four cloth corners, ten keypoints on a rope, cup dimensions). This assumes perfect state estimation of relevant features and is fed into a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) policy, representing an upper bound for systems with effective state estimators.
- **Image-Based Observations:** Policies directly process RGB camera images (128x128 pixels). This simulates real-world conditions where ground-truth state is unavailable.

**Algorithms Evaluated:**
- **Dynamics Oracle (CEM):** A random shooting algorithm using CEM for trajectory optimization, assuming full ground-truth state and dynamics access. MPC is used for execution.
- **State Oracle (SAC):** Uses the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm with Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) policies. Two variants:
 - *Full State Oracle:* Takes all ground-truth particle positions as input.
 - *Reduced State Oracle:* Takes the hand-defined reduced state as input.
- **Image-Based Algorithms:**
 - **CURL-SAC:** A model-free approach that uses contrastive unsupervised representations for reinforcement learning from pixels.
 - **DrQ:** Applies data augmentation and regularization techniques to standard RL from pixels.
 - **PlaNet:** A model-based method that learns a latent state space dynamics model for planning from pixel observations.
 - **Wu et al. (2020):** A model-free pick-and-place policy specifically for SpreadCloth and FoldCloth tasks, leveraging a segmentation map during training.

**Experimental Setup:** Algorithms are evaluated on a normalized performance metric (0-1 range) derived from task-specific reward functions and bounds. Each algorithm is run with 5 random seeds on 800 training and 200 evaluation task variations. Most algorithms are trained for 10^6 time steps, while PlaNet is trained for 5x10^5 steps due to computational cost.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of various algorithms on SoftGym environments yielded several key findings regarding the challenges and current limitations of deep reinforcement learning for deformable object manipulation:

- **Dynamics Oracle Performance:** As anticipated, the Dynamics Oracle (CEM) demonstrated the highest performance across most tasks. This method, leveraging ground-truth particle positions, velocities, and dynamics, serves as an empirical upper bound for what can be achieved, assuming perfect information. Its success underscores the inherent potential if accurate state and dynamics models are available. However, its performance could still be influenced by the exploration strategy of CEM, as indicated by sensitivity to planning horizon in some tasks (e.g., TransportWater, FoldCloth, DropCloth).

- **Reduced State Oracle Effectiveness:** The Reduced State Oracle (SAC with hand-defined low-dimensional state) performed well on tasks where the selected reduced state sufficiently captured the critical information for the task, such as StraightenRope, TransportWater, and FoldCloth. However, its performance was suboptimal on tasks like SpreadCloth, where relying solely on the four cloth corners proved insufficient to reason about the full configuration of a crumpled cloth. This highlights the dependency on an adequate and estimable reduced state representation for effective policy learning.

- **Challenges for Image-Based Methods:** A significant finding was the markedly lower performance of state-of-the-art image-based reinforcement learning algorithms (PlaNet, CURL-SAC, DrQ) compared to the oracle baselines on numerous SoftGym tasks. This performance gap was particularly pronounced in tasks such as StraightenRope, SpreadCloth, and FoldCloth, where learning curves suggested limited improvement even with extended training. These results indicate that current methods for learning from high-dimensional visual observations struggle substantially with the complexities of deformable object dynamics and perception, pointing to a critical area for future algorithmic development.

- **Limitations of Full State Oracle:** The Full State Oracle (SAC using raw ground-truth positions of all particles) exhibited poor performance across all tasks. This outcome suggests that simply concatenating a large, variable number of particle positions as input to a standard MLP policy does not effectively enable the network to learn the underlying geometric structure and dynamics. This highlights the challenge of dealing with inherently high-dimensional and variable state representations for generic RL architectures. The authors suggest that specialized architectures, such as graph neural networks, might be more suitable for capturing the connectivity and structure of particles.

- **Comparison to Prior Work (Wu et al. 2020):** On the SpreadCloth task, the method by Wu et al. (2020), which employs a model-free pick-and-place policy leveraging a segmentation map for exploration, showed better performance than other image-based baselines. This was attributed to the use of a segmentation map, which aids in selecting valid pick locations, and an action space more conducive to exploration. However, even this specialized method did not achieve optimal performance, and it performed less effectively on FoldCloth, further indicating the challenges that persist even with task-specific enhancements.

- **Difficulty in Future Prediction:** Analysis of PlaNet's predictions revealed its inability to accurately forecast future observations in deformable manipulation tasks, such as spilled water or the precise shape of a deforming cloth. This provides evidence that learning accurate visual dynamics models for deformable objects remains a difficult problem, contributing to the suboptimal planning performance of model-based image-based methods.

- **Relevance to Real-World Robotics (Reality Gap):** The paper included a qualitative demonstration comparing cloth manipulation in SoftGym with a real-world Sawyer robot. The simulated cloth exhibited behavior similar to its real-world counterpart under pick-and-place actions. This suggests that the FleX-based simulation environment can capture relevant aspects of real-world deformable object dynamics, and that algorithmic improvements in SoftGym are likely to translate into potential improvements for real robot applications, even though direct sim-to-real policy transfer would still involve additional challenges.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of SoftGym carries several significant implications and potential impacts for the fields of robotics and deep reinforcement learning:

- **Standardized Benchmarking:** SoftGym fills a critical void by providing the first open-source, standardized benchmark specifically for deformable object manipulation. This standardization is crucial for enabling direct and fair comparisons between different deep reinforcement learning algorithms, which was previously challenging due to varied task setups and simulators. This will facilitate more objective evaluation of progress in the field.

- **Accelerated Research and Development:** By offering a readily accessible platform with an OpenAI Gym API and Python interface, SoftGym lowers the barrier to entry for researchers to experiment with and develop new algorithms. This is expected to accelerate the pace of algorithmic development for deformable object manipulation. The reproducible nature of the benchmark ensures that new methods can be systematically validated against existing approaches.

- **Identification of Key Challenges:** The comprehensive benchmarking results highlight the substantial difficulties that current state-of-the-art reinforcement learning algorithms face when operating with high-dimensional visual observations and complex, high-dimensional deformable object states. The poor performance of image-based methods, in particular, points to a clear need for novel algorithmic advancements in areas such as robust visual perception of deformable states, learning predictive models for complex dynamics, and effective exploration strategies in high-dimensional action and observation spaces.

- **Guidance for Future Algorithmic Design:** The detailed analysis of algorithm strengths and limitations provides insights that can guide the design of future methods. For instance, the poor performance of the Full State Oracle suggests that simple concatenation of particle positions is insufficient, encouraging exploration of structured representations (e.g., graph neural networks) or more sophisticated state encoding techniques. The difficulties in dynamics prediction for image-based methods motivate research into more robust and generalizable latent dynamics models.

- **Bridge to Real-World Applications:** The qualitative demonstration of similar cloth behavior between SoftGym and a real robot suggests that the simulation environments are sufficiently realistic to reflect real-world challenges. While sim-to-real transfer remains an independent research area, the relevance of SoftGym's dynamics implies that algorithms showing strong performance in this benchmark are more likely to be applicable and adaptable to real-world robotic tasks involving deformable objects. This positions SoftGym as a valuable tool for developing fundamental skills that could eventually transfer to practical applications like manufacturing, healthcare, and domestic robotics.

In summary, SoftGym is positioned to be a foundational tool that not only allows for systematic evaluation of existing methods but also actively drives the development of next-generation reinforcement learning algorithms capable of effectively tackling the intricate problem of deformable object manipulation.
