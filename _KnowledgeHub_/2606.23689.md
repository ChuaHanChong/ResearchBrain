# AutoDex: An Automated Real-World System for Dexterous Grasping Data Collection

> [!summary] Summary
> AutoDex is an automated real-world system that collects physically validated data for multi-finger dexterous grasping, achieving a 4.8x higher throughput than human teleoperation. It uses dense multi-camera perception and active grasp-based object resetting, demonstrating that physically validated grasps achieve 76% real-world success compared to 34% for simulation-screened candidates.

## Problem

- Learning robust dexterous grasping policies is challenged by the lack of large-scale, physically validated real-world data, as simulation often fails to capture complex contact mechanics.
- Existing data collection methods for dexterous grasping are either slow and prone to human bias (teleoperation) or suffer from significant 'sim-to-real' gaps (simulation-based synthesis).
- Fully automated data collection for multi-finger dexterous grasping is difficult due to requirements for robust object tracking under occlusion, reliable success/failure labeling, and efficient inter-trial scene reset.

## Method

- **AutoDex** integrates a grasp candidate generator with an automated real-world execution system, featuring a ==20-camera multi-view perception== system for robust object pose estimation and tracking.
- The system performs collision-monitored grasp execution with a multi-finger hand, automatically labeling grasp success or failure post-hoc based on 6D object pose tracking.
- An active, grasp-based reset module precisely reorients objects to different stable poses, enabling comprehensive testing of candidates across various object orientations.

## Results

- AutoDex achieved a **4.8x** higher throughput for data collection compared to human teleoperation, gathering 500 grasp trajectories in **10.3** hours.
- Real-world success rates for physically validated grasps from AutoDex reached **76%**, substantially higher than the **34%** success rate for simulation-screened grasps.
- The active 'Stable Reorient Placement' reset method consistently achieved near **100%** success in transitioning objects to target stable poses, proving reliable for unattended operation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physical validation of grasp candidates on real hardware is crucial to overcome the sim-to-real gap, as simulation-based screening alone poorly predicts real-world dexterous grasp success.
> - Dense, multi-camera perception is critical for reliable 6D object pose estimation and tracking during dynamic dexterous manipulation, especially when the robot hand heavily occludes the object.
> - Active, grasp-based object resetting is a reliable and efficient method for inter-trial scene reconfiguration, enabling a wider exploration of grasp candidates across stable object poses compared to passive dropping.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Learning robust dexterous grasping requires empirical data that records the physical outcomes of grasp attempts on real hardware. Such data is critical because multi-finger grasps involve high-dimensional hand configurations and complex contact mechanics, where minor inaccuracies in pose, friction, compliance, or force distribution can lead to failure. Geometric feasibility alone, often derived from simulation, is thus an insufficient predictor of real-world grasp validity.

Existing data collection paradigms primarily fall into two categories:
- **Human Teleoperation:** Methods like those in produce real contact outcomes, ensuring physical validity. However, this approach is inherently slow, limited by human fatigue and attention, and introduces operator bias in terms of chosen approach directions and finger configurations.
- **Simulation-based Grasp Synthesis:** Approaches detailed in can generate large sets of candidate hand-object configurations efficiently. These methods can check many aspects of geometric feasibility, such as joint limits, inverse kinematics solvability, collision avoidance, and motion planning. However, they struggle to reliably certify stability under real contact dynamics, which depend on complex physical phenomena difficult to accurately simulate (e.g., friction, slip, finger-pad deformation, actuator behavior). Consequently, a grasp candidate that appears feasible in simulation may still fail in the physical world.

A recognized solution involves combining scalable candidate synthesis with real-world verification. This hybrid approach proposes executable candidates using simulation and planning tools, then tests their physical success on actual hardware. The challenge, however, is that this real-world validation process must be fully automated to achieve scale. Any manual intervention in steps such as initial object pose estimation, object tracking during execution (especially under occlusion), candidate selection, safety monitoring, success/failure labeling, or scene reset reintroduces the human bottleneck characteristic of teleoperation.

Within the broader landscape of autonomous data collection, prior work has largely focused on parallel-jaw grippers. For multi-finger dexterous hands, direct on-robot exploration to propose useful candidates is inefficient due to the high-dimensional action space. Additionally, success labeling for multi-finger grasps is more complex due to significant hand-object occlusion during execution, necessitating dense, execution-time tracking. Inter-trial reset for dexterous grasping also presents unique challenges, as it cannot rely on passive settling alone; objects must be actively reoriented to specific stable poses to evaluate diverse candidates. Recent autonomous pipelines that attempt this direction often still restrict themselves to simpler parallel-jaw grippers.

AutoDex addresses these existing gaps by presenting an end-to-end automated system specifically designed for multi-finger dexterous grasping. It integrates existing candidate generation methods (using BODex in this implementation) with a real-world execution stage to provide ground-truth physical outcome labels. The system incorporates dense multi-camera perception for robust tracking under occlusion and an active, grasp-based reset module for efficient transitions between various object stable poses, thereby enabling fully autonomous and scalable data collection for dexterous grasping.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and implement an automated real-world system, AutoDex, capable of collecting large-scale, physically labeled data for dexterous grasping. This system aims to overcome the limitations of current data collection paradigms by enabling continuous, unattended execution of grasp trials on physical hardware.

The motivation for AutoDex stems from several critical challenges in advancing dexterous manipulation research:
- **Need for Physically Valid Data:** Learning robust dexterous grasping policies requires data that not only describes proposed hand-object configurations but also records their actual physical outcomes (success or failure) on real hardware. Geometric or kinematic feasibility, typically verified in simulation, is often an unreliable indicator of physical stability due to complex real-world contact physics, including friction, compliance, and dynamic interaction forces that are difficult to model accurately.
- **Limitations of Teleoperation:** Human teleoperation produces ground-truth physical outcomes but is inherently slow, resource-intensive, and susceptible to operator bias and fatigue. This limits the scale and diversity of collected datasets.
- **Limitations of Simulation:** While simulation-based methods can generate vast quantities of grasp candidates at low cost, their ability to certify real-world contact validity is compromised by the inherent inaccuracies of physics engines in modeling complex multi-finger contact. This leads to a significant "sim-to-real" gap, where many simulation-valid grasps fail physically.
- **Automation as a Scaling Solution:** The most promising approach to combine the strengths of both paradigms (scalable synthesis and physical validity) is to generate candidate grasps and then verify them on real hardware. However, this only scales if the *entire* data collection loop can operate without human intervention. This full automation requires addressing several technical challenges:
 - **Accurate Object Pose Estimation:** Precisely locating the object's initial 6D pose in the workspace.
 - **Robust Object Tracking under Occlusion:** Maintaining accurate object pose estimation during grasp execution, especially when the robot hand heavily occludes the object from camera views.
 - **Automated Success/Failure Labeling:** Objectively determining the outcome of a grasp attempt (e.g., successful lift and hold) without human judgment.
 - **Collision-Monitored Execution:** Ensuring safe robot operation during unattended trials to prevent damage.
 - **Active Scene Reset:** Efficiently reconfiguring the object's pose between trials to allow testing of candidates for various stable object orientations, rather than relying on passive settling.

By addressing these motivations and challenges, AutoDex aims to produce a reusable database of physically labeled grasp trials. This database would enable downstream systems to retrieve and filter feasible grasps based on empirical evidence, significantly accelerating the development of dexterous manipulation capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AutoDex operates as an automated real-world system designed to collect physically labeled dexterous grasping data. The system takes generated grasp candidates and autonomously executes, labels, and resets trials.

#### 3.1. Grasp Candidate Generation

AutoDex utilizes a modular candidate generator, instantiated with BODex. This generator receives an object model and scene specifications (e.g., planar constraints like walls, shelves, or boxes with varying gaps) and outputs a set of candidate grasps, each defined by a wrist pose relative to the object and a hand configuration. The generator's role is to propose kinematically and geometrically feasible candidates; it is not assumed to guarantee stability under real contact dynamics. AutoDex precomputes a set of stable tabletop poses for each object and aggregates candidates for each pose and scene primitive. A simulation sanity pre-filter (MuJoCo-based) is applied to remove obviously unstable candidates (e.g., those failing under a 0.1 N gravitational perturbation) before real-world execution.

#### 3.2. AutoDex Workcell

The system is built around a calibrated robot-capture workcell comprising:
- **Robot Arm and Hand:** A 6-DoF xArm with swappable multi-finger hands (16-DoF Allegro Hand or 6-DoF Inspire Hand).
- **Perception System:** A 1.5x1.5x2.5 m LED-lit capture cell housing 20 synchronized RGB cameras, each recording at 30 FPS with 2048x1536 resolution. This dense camera array provides redundant views for robust object pose estimation, especially during execution under hand-object occlusion.
- **Calibration and Synchronization:** Camera intrinsics are calibrated using a ChArUco board, and per-session extrinsics are recovered via global bundle adjustment (COLMAP), yielding sub-millimeter multi-view pose self-consistency. Hand-eye calibration and a robot-camera timestamp offset ensure temporal alignment of robot state data (120 Hz streaming of joint positions, velocities, torques, etc.) with visual observations.
- **Safety Mechanism:** A learned residual-torque monitor is implemented to detect unexpected contacts, as the robot arm’s built-in collision detector proved unreliable with varying end-effector loads from grasped objects. This monitor uses a small MLP trained on collision-free motions to predict nominal free-space torques. A sustained residual torque above a threshold (30 N·m on shoulder joints for 0.5 s) triggers an abort, and the robot recovers by planning a trajectory to a home configuration with monotonically increasing end-effector height. The monitor is active primarily during contact-critical downward motions.

#### 3.3. Autonomous Data Collection Loop

The core of AutoDex is an autonomous loop that validates grasp candidates:
1. **Object Pose Estimation:** At the start of each trial, a multi-view pipeline (Fig. 7a) uses SAM3 for object masks and FoundPose for initial 6D pose estimation from the 20 RGB views. The pose is refined by cross-view silhouette-mask agreement and silhouette optimization.
2. **Candidate Selection and Execution:** The system identifies the current stable pose, transforms object-relative candidates into the robot frame, and discards those without an arm IK solution or with arm/hand collisions. An unattempted feasible candidate is selected. The robot plans a collision-free arm trajectory, approaching with an optimized open-hand configuration (`q_open`) to maximize clearance, then closing through pre-grasp (`q_pre`) and grasp (`q_grasp`) configurations. A final "squeeze" (`q_sqz`) over-closes the fingers to resist slip. The robot then lifts the object and holds it for a fixed duration.
3. **Automatic Labeling:** Post hoc, 6D object pose tracking is run on the recorded multi-view stream using GoTrack. A trial is labeled successful if the tracked object remains at least 5 cm above its initial height throughout a 3-second hold phase, and a failure otherwise. This avoids adding real-time tracking latency to the control loop.
4. **Data Recording and State Update:** The trial record (executed plan, multi-view recordings, tracked poses, calibration, metadata, label) is saved, and the candidate is marked as attempted for the current stable pose.
5. **Object Placement and Reset:** After labeling, the robot places the object back on the table. If unattempted feasible candidates remain for the current object pose, the next trial begins. Otherwise, the reset module is invoked to move the object to another stable pose with remaining candidates.

#### 3.4. Reset

When no feasible candidates remain for the current stable pose, the reset module reorients the object to a different target stable pose. It generates a "reset grasp" that is collision-free within a combined pickup-placement scene.
- **Direct Placement:** For many pose pairs, the robot directly grasps the object, lifts, reorients, places it at the target pose, releases, and retracts.
- **Height-Relaxed Placement:** For flat objects where direct placement is difficult due to limited space, the object can be released from a height `h` above the target pose. To ensure the object descends correctly without finger contact, virtual support pillars are added during reset-grasp generation to exclude grasps where fingers intrude into the descent region. Reset grasps are cached for reuse.

#### 3.5. AutoDex Database and Retrieval-Based In-the-Wild Execution

AutoDex collects a database of physically executed and labeled grasp trials. For deployment, a robot observes a workspace (using a sparser four-camera rig) to estimate the target object's 6D pose and obstacle geometry. Successful grasps from the AutoDex database are retrieved, transformed into the workspace frame, and filtered by collision-free arm planning. The first feasible grasp is selected for execution, enabling in-the-wild operation without further policy training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of AutoDex focused on its throughput, the impact of physical validation on dataset quality, the efficacy of the reset strategy, and the influence of camera density on pose reliability.

#### 4.1. Autonomous Data Collection Throughput and Bottlenecks

- **Throughput:** In a matched 500-trajectory collection study, AutoDex required 10.3 hours. In contrast, human teleoperation in the same workcell required 49.4 hours for the same number of trials, demonstrating a 4.8x improvement in effective throughput for AutoDex (Figure 4, left). This gain is attributed to AutoDex's ability to operate autonomously and continuously, eliminating human idle time and fatigue, despite individual robot executions having comparable durations in both settings.
- **Runtime Composition:** An analysis of 500 autonomous trials revealed a mean grasp-execution loop duration of 48.2 seconds (Figure 3, right). Physical robot motion (execution and retract phases) accounted for the majority of this time (51.5% for execution, 24.6% for retract), totaling 76.1%. Perception and motion planning constituted 16.1% and 7.8% of the loop, respectively. This indicates that physical robot movement is the dominant bottleneck in the cycle.

#### 4.2. Impact of Physical Validation on Dataset Quality

- **Grasp Success Rate:** The study compared two databases: a model-screened database (generated candidates screened only by simulation pre-filter) and the AutoDex-validated database (candidates that passed real-world lift-and-hold tests). Across 20 objects and 515 trials with various scene types, grasps retrieved from the AutoDex-validated database achieved 76% real-world success. In contrast, grasps from the model-screened database achieved only 34% real-world success (Figure 4, right).
- **Consistency Across Categories:** This improvement was consistent across different material categories, scene types (tabletop, wall, cluttered), and object weight bins. The most significant gap was observed in cluttered scenes, where model-screened grasps frequently failed, while AutoDex-validated grasps maintained 80-90% success. These results indicate that kinematic and collision feasibility checks alone are insufficient for predicting real-world dexterous grasp success, and physical validation is crucial for filtering out grasps sensitive to real-world contact effects like slip and compliance.

#### 4.3. Reset Strategy Evaluation

- **Performance Comparison:** AutoDex's "Stable Reorient Placement" reset method was compared against two passive-drop baselines: "Naive Drop" and "Reorient + Passive Release." Reset success was evaluated against the passive transition probability, which quantifies the difficulty of reaching a target pose through uncontrolled settling.
- **Results:** (Figure 5, left) Naive Drop's success rate matched the passive transition probability. Reorient + Passive Release showed minor improvements but largely remained close to the passive baseline. AutoDex's Stable Reorient Placement method consistently achieved high success rates (close to 100%) across the entire difficulty range, including transitions with very low passive transition probabilities (i.e., poses rarely reachable by passive settling).
- **Failure Modes:** Reorient + Passive Release exhibited a failure mode incompatible with unattended operation, ejecting objects from the workspace in 5.3% of attempts. AutoDex's method caused no such failures, highlighting the necessity of explicit, grasp-based placement for reliable unattended collection.

#### 4.4. Impact of Camera Density on Pose Reliability

- **Pose Accuracy:** The reliability of object pose estimation, crucial for unattended operation, was assessed by varying the number of cameras (`k`) used for silhouette refinement. The pose refined from all 20 cameras served as the reference.
- **Results:** (Figure 5, right) The mean ADD-S (Average Distance of Nearest Surface Points) error between the reference pose and `k`-camera subset poses decreased from 14.3 mm at `k=2` to 0.5 mm at `k=16`. The largest improvement in accuracy occurred between `k=2` and `k=4`. Catastrophic pose failures, common with `k=2` on textureless or thin objects, were largely eliminated by `k=8`. This demonstrates that dense multi-view redundancy significantly enhances object pose reliability, which is essential for accurate candidate selection, collision checking, and grasp execution in an autonomous system.

#### 4.5. Safety Monitor Performance (Supplementary Material)

The learned residual-torque collision detector proved effective. In an earlier run without the monitor, two collisions caused damage. After implementation, no further adapter damage occurred. The monitor halted the arm during downward approach in 2.7% of all trials and during placement in 8.9% of all trials, with visible contacts accounting for a portion of these triggers. This conservative abort strategy is beneficial for unattended operation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AutoDex represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic dexterous manipulation by providing an automated, scalable solution for collecting real-world physically labeled grasp data. This work addresses a critical bottleneck in learning robust dexterous policies, which have historically been limited by the scarcity of high-quality, real-world data reflecting complex contact physics.

The key significance and potential impact include:

- **Enabling Large-Scale, High-Quality Data Collection:** By fully automating the entire data collection loop—from perception and candidate execution to labeling and scene reset—AutoDex achieves a 4.8x throughput improvement over traditional teleoperation. This allows for the generation of substantially larger and more diverse datasets, which are crucial for training data-driven models for dexterous grasping.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** The research empirically demonstrates the inadequacy of simulation-only validation for dexterous grasping. The AutoDex-validated database yields a 76% real-world grasp success rate compared to 34% for simulation-screened candidates, highlighting the necessity of physical validation. This finding emphasizes that accurate contact physics remain a challenge for simulators, making real-world testing indispensable for reliable grasping.
- **Improving Dataset Reliability and Transferability:** The collected database of physically labeled successes and failures provides a reusable resource that directly informs which grasps are robust in the real world. Downstream systems can retrieve these validated grasps and efficiently filter them for feasibility in new scenes, reducing the need for extensive online policy training or additional real-world trials during deployment. This capability can accelerate the development and deployment of dexterous robots in various applications.
- **Advancements in Autonomous System Design:** The AutoDex system integrates several technical innovations crucial for unattended operation:
 - **Dense Multi-View Perception:** Crucial for robust 6D object pose estimation and tracking, particularly under severe hand-object occlusion.
 - **Active Grasp-Based Object Reset:** A novel mechanism that reliably reorients objects to diverse stable poses, enabling the exhaustive evaluation of grasp candidates across different object configurations, a capability lacking in passive reset methods.
 - **Learned Safety Monitoring:** A robust collision detection system essential for the safe, continuous operation of robots in unsupervised environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The AutoDex database, with its physically labeled trials, synchronized multi-view observations, and robot-state trajectories across 100 diverse objects, provides a valuable public resource for the research community. This can stimulate further research in areas such as learning from real-world successes and failures, developing more accurate grasp metrics, and improving sim-to-real transfer.

**Limitations identified by the authors include:**
- **Scope:** AutoDex currently focuses on stable grasps for a fixed arm-hand setup. Complex manipulation tasks like bimanual coordination, mobile manipulation, finger-rolling regrasps, or functional grasps (e.g., tool use) are outside its current scope.
- **Generator Dependency:** The system inherits potential blind spots from its upstream grasp generator, meaning it might miss effective grasps that require dynamic finger motions not considered by the generator.
- **Hardware Dependency:** The highest throughput and reliability are achieved through a specialized multi-camera workcell, which may pose a deployment challenge for systems with lighter sensing footprints.

Despite these limitations, AutoDex significantly contributes to scaling dexterous manipulation research by providing a validated, efficient, and reliable method for collecting essential real-world data.
