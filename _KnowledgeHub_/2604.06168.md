# Action Images: End-to-End Policy Learning via Multiview Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Action Images reformulates robot policy learning as multiview video generation, converting 7-DoF robot actions into pixel-grounded images. This approach enables a unified world model to achieve superior zero-shot policy generalization on unseen tasks and environments, alongside improved video-action joint generation quality.

## Problem

- Existing robotics world models struggle to translate strong video generation capabilities into robust policy generalization, especially in novel environments.
- Current action representations are often detached from visual space or rely on separate policy heads, limiting the direct leverage of rich, pre-trained video knowledge for control and hindering transferability.
- Many generalist robot policy models face challenges with zero-shot transfer to entirely new environments, often failing to adapt beyond training scenes.

## Method

- Robot 7-DoF actions (position, orientation, gripper) are converted into ==multi-view pixel-grounded action images== by projecting semantic 3D points (end-effector position, up, normal) into ==2D Gaussian heatmaps== across multiple camera views.
- A large pre-trained ==video generator== is fine-tuned to jointly model multi-view robot observation videos and the newly created multi-view action videos within a ==unified video-space==.
- Diverse ==masking strategies== are employed during training to enable a single model to perform multiple tasks, including video-action joint generation, action-conditioned video generation, and video-to-action labeling.

## Results

- **Action Images** achieved superior zero-shot policy success rates on unseen tasks, for example, up to **60%** for 'reach target' on RLBench and **45%** for 'close drawer' on real-world tasks, outperforming baselines which often showed **0-20%** success.
- Demonstrated improved video-action joint generation quality, outperforming baselines like TesserAct in video quality (PSNR **23.48** vs **20.83**, SSIM **78.62%** vs **59.20%**).
- Outperformed specialized baselines in action-conditioned video generation (PSNR **31.35** vs. Tora's **19.76**) and video-to-action labeling (trajectory error **5.785** vs. TAPIR's **14.80**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Grounding robot actions directly in the pixel space as visual signals allows large video backbones to inherently leverage their extensive visual knowledge for action reasoning and control.
> - Representing actions as multi-view images resolves 3D ambiguities and integrates action directly into the visual prediction problem, enabling the video backbone itself to function as a zero-shot policy without separate modules.
> - A unified video-space representation of observations and actions enables a single generative model to perform diverse robot learning tasks, simplifying architecture and training across different objectives.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of robotic policy learning, specifically focusing on **World Action Models (WAMs)**. WAMs leverage video backbones to predict future states, aiming to enable robots to understand and interact with their environments. The paper identifies a significant challenge in this area: while video generation models have advanced in predicting future observations, effectively translating this predictive ability into generalizable policies for robot control remains difficult.

Current approaches to integrating action into world models typically fall into two categories. Some methods, such as those by, utilize a separate policy head or action module layered on top of a world model. This approach requires an additional network to decode control signals from learned video features, which can shift the burden of generalization to a specialized control component, potentially limiting transferability across diverse settings. Other approaches, exemplified by, adapt video models for action generation but employ action representations that are not spatially grounded in image space. In these cases, the world model's predictive knowledge is only indirectly connected to the act of controlling the robot.

The paper also relates to the broader endeavor of developing **Generalist Robot Policy Models**, which aim for policies capable of succeeding across varied tasks and adapting to new environments with minimal fine-tuning. While advancements in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, Diffusion Policy, and Reinforcement Learning have improved generalizability, zero-shot transfer to novel environments often remains a limitation. This work specifically seeks to bridge the gap between strong video prediction and transferable control by making action "native" to the video space.

Furthermore, the research touches upon **4D Generation Models** (3D plus time), noting that while there have been advances in generating complex dynamic scenes, many robotics applications in this domain are limited to a fixed single view. Even multi-view input approaches have not consistently demonstrated broad generalization beyond their training scenes. The proposed method utilizes multi-view generation to enhance its understanding of 3D robot actions, distinguishing it from prior work that may lack geometry-consistent supervision or detailed 7-DoF action representation in a pixel-grounded format.

By representing actions as pixel-grounded multi-view images encoding full 7-DoF control, this work differentiates itself from concurrent methods and prior world models like DreamZero and Cosmos-Policy, which encode actions using separate modules. The objective is to leverage pretrained video knowledge more fully by allowing the video backbone itself to function as a zero-shot policy, thereby enhancing generalization across viewpoints and robot embodiments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a recognized limitation in current robotics world models: the persistent gap between advancements in video generalization and the ability to generalize policies for robot control. A model might generate plausible future frames successfully but still lack the capacity to make appropriate decisions for acting in novel environments. The authors identify this disconnect as a central bottleneck.

The core motivation to address this bottleneck lies in the way robot actions are typically represented within existing world models. The paper critiques two prevalent approaches:
1. **Separate Action Modules or Policy Heads:** Many systems attach an additional network to decode control from learned video features. This design means the model's predictive understanding of the world is only indirectly linked to the act of controlling the robot. Consequently, the burden of generalization often falls on this specialized control module, which is precisely where transferability frequently degrades in new scenarios.
2. **Non-Spatially Grounded Action Representations:** Other methods adapt video models for action generation using representations that lack direct spatial grounding in the image space. This prevents the full utilization of the rich, pretrained knowledge embedded within powerful video models, limiting their potential for transfer across different viewpoints and operational environments.

In response to these limitations, the paper establishes several key objectives:
- **Formulate Policy Learning as Video Generation:** The central objective is to reconceptualize policy learning as a video generation task. This involves translating robot actions into a visual format that can be processed and understood by video models.
- **Develop a Unified Video-Space Representation:** To achieve the above, the research aims to create a unified representation where both robot observations (visual input) and robot actions share the same video-space modality. This allows a single video backbone to process and generate both.
- **Enable a Zero-Shot Policy:** A critical goal is to enable the video backbone itself to act as a zero-shot policy. This means the model should be capable of inferring and executing actions in unseen environments without requiring a separate, specialized policy head or action module.
- **Improve Generalizability:** By grounding actions in 2D pixels and explicitly tracking robot-arm motion across multiple views, the objective is to achieve a more generalizable policy model. This design aims to facilitate natural transfer across varying viewpoints, different motion patterns, and even different robot embodiments.
- **Leverage Multi-View Action Representation:** The choice of multi-view action videos is motivated by the need to bridge the gap between 2D images and 7-DoF robot actions in 3D space. A single view can offer an ambiguous projection of motion, making it challenging to infer the full action consistently from pixels alone. Using multiple views is intended to make the pixel-grounded action image more reconstructable and robust to partial occlusions.
- **Support Multiple Tasks within a Single Model:** Beyond just control, the research aims for the unified model to inherently support additional tasks such as video-action joint generation, action-conditioned video generation, and action labeling, all under a shared representation and training objective. This would demonstrate a comprehensive understanding of the observation-action dynamics within the environment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology, "Action Images," involves formulating robot policy learning as a multiview video generation problem. This is achieved by translating robot control into interpretable action images that are grounded in 2D pixels and explicitly track robot-arm motion.

#### 3.1. Action as Images

The method converts 7-DoF robot actions into a visual representation.
- **From 7-DoF Action to Semantic 3D Points:** A robot action at time $t$, denoted as $a_t = [p_t, \theta_t, g_t]$, consists of the end-effector position ($p_t \in \mathbb{R}^3$), orientation ($\theta_t \in \mathbb{R}^3$), and gripper openness ($g_t \in \mathbb{R}$). This 7-DoF action is converted into three semantic 3D points:
 - **Position Point ($q_{pos_t}$):** Directly corresponds to the end-effector position $p_t$.
 - **Up Point ($q_{up_t}$):** Calculated by extending $p_t$ along a canonical in-plane direction of the gripper using the rotation matrix $R(\theta_t)$ and a small length $\ell$.
 - **Normal Point ($q_{normal_t}$):** Calculated by extending $p_t$ along the direction normal to the robot gripper plane using $R(\theta_t)$ and $\ell$.
 These three points collectively capture the end-effector's pose in a format suitable for projection into image space.

- **Multi-view Action Image Rendering:** For each camera view $v$, the three semantic 3D points are projected into image space using camera intrinsics and extrinsics, yielding 2D pixel coordinates ($u_{pos,(v)_t}$, $u_{normal,(v)_t}$, $u_{up,(v)_t}$). These projected points are then rendered into an action image $A^{(v)}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ using 2D Gaussians:
 - The red channel encodes the position point.
 - The green channel encodes the normal point.
 - The blue channel encodes the up point.
 - Additionally, the binary gripper openness signal ($g_t$) is injected into low-response regions of the blue channel.
This process generates interpretable action images, which, when stacked over time, form action videos ($A^{(v)}$) for each view. These action videos share the same spatial and temporal structure as robot RGB observations, establishing a unified video-space representation.

#### 3.2. Action Images Decoding

To enable robot control, the generated multi-view action videos must be decoded back into 7-DoF continuous robot actions.
- **Decoding Gripper Openness:** Gripper openness ($\hat{g}_t$) is estimated by averaging the pixel values in the low-response regions of the blue channel across all views.
- **Reconstructing 3D Semantic Points from Multi-view Heatmaps:** For each semantic point (position, normal, up), a 2D point is selected from its corresponding heatmap in the main view using weighted averaging. A ray is then cast from the main-view camera center through this 2D point. A set of candidate 3D points are sampled along this ray. Each candidate is projected into a side view and scored against the side-view heatmap, with the 3D point yielding the best match being selected. This multi-view geometric procedure resolves depth ambiguity.
- **From Reconstructed Points Back to 7-DoF Action:** Once the 3D semantic points ($\hat{q}_{pos_t}$, $\hat{q}_{up_t}$, $\hat{q}_{normal_t}$) are reconstructed, the original action components are recovered:
 - The end-effector position $\hat{p}_t$ is set as $\hat{q}_{pos_t}$.
 - Directional vectors ($\hat{e}_{x_t}$, $\hat{e}_{z_t}$, $\hat{e}_{y_t}$) are derived from the relationships between $\hat{q}_{pos_t}$, $\hat{q}_{up_t}$, and $\hat{q}_{normal_t}$.
 - The end-effector orientation $\hat{\theta}_t$ is determined from these vectors.
 - The final decoded action $\hat{a}_t$ is then formed by combining $\hat{p}_t$, $\hat{\theta}_t$, and $\hat{g}_t$. The method states that information loss during this encoding/decoding is minor and predictable, primarily limited by sampling intervals and image resolution.

#### 3.3. Training Unified World Action Model

The unified world action model is built by fine-tuning a large, pretrained video generator (Wan 2.2).
- **Multi-view Video-Action Tokenization and Packing:** For each camera view, RGB observation clips ($V^{(v)}_{1:T}$) and aligned action-frame clips ($A^{(v)}_{1:T}$) are encoded into the backbone's latent space using a 3D-VAE. These latent representations are then concatenated temporally to form a single input sequence ($X_v = [V^{(v)}_{1:T}, A^{(v)}_{1:T}]$) for each view, allowing the model to process a unified timeline of observation and action. Weights are shared across views to ensure consistent cross-view learning.
- **Unified Training via Multiple Mask Strategies:** The model is trained to support multiple tasks through diverse masking strategies applied to the latent token space, operating within a diffusion-style denoising framework:
 1. **Action & video joint generation:** Most tokens are masked, except for the first observation frame, compelling the model to generate both future video and action conditioned on text and camera inputs.
 2. **Action-conditioned video generation:** Action tokens are kept visible while video tokens are masked, training the model to synthesize future visual observations consistent with given actions.
 3. **Video-to-action labeling:** Video tokens are visible while action tokens are masked, instructing the model to infer action images from the input video.
 4. **Video-only generation:** For datasets lacking action annotations or camera alignment (e.g., BridgeV2), the model is trained with only video tokens using the denoising objective.
- **Camera Conditioning:** Camera-controlled generation is integrated by injecting Plücker embeddings of camera parameters into the backbone, aiding multi-view consistency.
- **Optimization Objective:** The pretrained backbone is fine-tuned using a flow matching objective, minimizing an $L_2$ loss between predicted and target velocities over the masked latent tokens.
- **Training Datasets:** The model is trained on a mixture of RLBench (simulation data with accurate actions/cameras), DROID (real-robot data, filtered for quality), and BridgeV2 (real-world videos, with estimated camera annotations and used for video-only generation where action data is unavailable). This diverse dataset mixture provides complementary supervision for robust learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings that support the efficacy of the Action Images framework across various robotic tasks and evaluation metrics.

#### 4.1. Text-Controlled Action & Video Joint Generation

This is established as the primary evaluation setting, where the model jointly generates future robot videos and corresponding action videos from an initial observation and a language instruction. Executable controls are derived by decoding the predicted action images.
- **Zero-shot Policy Results:** On both RLBench (simulation) and real-world tasks, the proposed method achieved the highest zero-shot success rates. This was particularly evident under strong distribution shifts, such as tasks involving unseen objects, environments, and robot arms in real-world scenarios. The model outperformed several baselines, including MV-Policy, $\pi_{0.5}$, MolmoAct, TesserAct, and Cosmos-Policy, indicating that the pixel-grounded action representation contributes to enhanced generalization. For instance, on RLBench tasks like "pick cup" and "reach target," success rates were 30% and 60% respectively for "Ours," while most baselines reported 0-20%. In real-world "Place Cup," "Ours" achieved 40% success, compared to 5-10% for other methods.
- **RLBench In-domain Results:** When evaluated on in-domain RLBench tasks, the method remained competitive. Notably, adding an optional learned action head (a lightweight MLP) on top of the unified backbone significantly improved performance, boosting the average success rate from 20.6% to 36.7%. This suggests that while the pixel-grounded representation enables zero-shot capabilities, it can also support more precise action decoding when additional supervision is available.
- **Joint Generation Quality:** In terms of accurately predicting future robot videos and actions, the proposed method surpassed prior world-model baselines (Cosmos-Predict, Cosmos-Policy, TesserAct) across all video quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, FVD, LPIPS). It achieved comparable or superior action accuracy, measured by 2D and 3D trajectory error. For example, "Ours" reported a PSNR of 23.48 and SSIM of 78.62%, significantly higher than TesserAct (20.83 PSNR, 59.20% SSIM), while maintaining competitive 2D (1.61) and 3D (12.2 x $10^3$) trajectory errors.

#### 4.2. Additional Unified-Model Capabilities

The unified video-space representation enables several other functionalities.
- **Action-conditioned Video Generation:** The model's ability to generate future robot videos when provided with an action sequence was evaluated. It outperformed Tora, a 2D trajectory-conditioned video generation baseline, on all video quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, FVD, LPIPS). This indicates that the unified representation can effectively utilize action inputs for future video prediction.
- **Video-to-Action Labeling:** The model's capacity to infer action-related motion directly from input videos was assessed against point-tracking baselines like TAPIR and CoTracker. The proposed method demonstrated a clear advantage, achieving lower trajectory error and higher Jaccard scores, indicating its effectiveness in action labeling from video.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Qualitative evaluations further supported the quantitative findings.
- **Real-world Zero-shot Rollouts:** On an xArm platform with unseen objects and environments, the model successfully generated future observations and multi-view action images. The decoded 3D trajectories were visualized and replayed on the real robot, demonstrating that the predicted action images translate into plausible and executable movements consistent with the generated visual outcomes.
- **Zero-shot Generalization on FR3M Rooms:** The model generated coherent action images and videos with accurate target localization for unseen tasks (e.g., "pick up the mouse") in unseen environments, even when action supervision was not available for certain training datasets (like BridgeV2). This suggests that the learned action-generation capability transfers across datasets and domains.
- **Action Labeling, Camera Control, and Action-Conditioned Generation:** Additional qualitative examples demonstrated the model's ability to infer actions from human and robot videos, generate videos with controlled viewpoint changes, and produce consistent future videos based on specified actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work "Action Images: End-to-End Policy Learning via Multiview Video Generation" contributes to the field of robotics and artificial intelligence through several significant aspects:

- **Addressing the Policy Generalization Bottleneck:** The paper directly tackles the critical challenge of translating strong video generation capabilities into generalizable robot policies. By reformulating policy learning as a video generation problem, it proposes a unified approach that aims to overcome the limitations of separate control modules or non-pixel-grounded action representations prevalent in existing world action models.
- **Novel Action Representation for Robotics:** The introduction of "interpretable action images" is a key innovation. This pixel-grounded, multi-view visual representation of 7-DoF robot control allows robot actions to be processed within the same modality as visual observations. This design enables video backbones to directly learn and reason about actions, potentially fostering a deeper and more consistent understanding of robot-environment interactions.
- **Enabling Zero-Shot Policy Learning:** A major impact of this research is the demonstration of a zero-shot policy directly from a unified video backbone, without the need for a separate policy head or action module. This simplifies the policy learning architecture and suggests a pathway towards more autonomous and adaptable robot systems that can generalize to novel tasks and environments without task-specific retraining.
- **Enhanced Generalizability:** The pixel-grounded and multi-view nature of the action representation is posited to lead to improved policy generalization across diverse viewpoints, varied motion patterns, and even different robot embodiments. This enhanced transferability is crucial for deploying robots in complex and unstructured real-world settings.
- **Unified Model Capabilities:** Beyond policy learning, the same unified model inherently supports a range of functionalities, including video-action joint generation, action-conditioned video generation, and video-to-action labeling. This versatility indicates a more comprehensive understanding of the dynamics between observations and actions, making the model a robust tool for various aspects of robot learning and perception.
- **Future Directions for Real-world Deployment:** While the current system primarily demonstrates strong open-loop results, the authors identify a clear path toward closed-loop control through future work involving model distillation for faster inference. This suggests a potential for the framework to be integrated into real-time, interactive robotic systems, moving beyond predictive modeling to active control.
- **Broader Implications for Embodied AI:** The work implies that grounding abstract control signals in the visual domain can be a promising route for developing more generalizable embodied AI. By making action "native" to the visual space, it potentially unlocks the vast representational power of large pretrained video models for robotic control, bridging the gap between perception and action in a more cohesive manner.

In summary, this research presents a method for end-to-end policy learning through multiview video generation, offering a novel action representation and a unified modeling framework that could significantly advance zero-shot generalization and versatility in robotic systems.
