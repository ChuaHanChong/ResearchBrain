# Sampling-Based Coordination-Informed Multi-Objective Multi-Robot Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The CIMORL framework introduces a multi-objective multi-robot reinforcement learning approach that uses a distributed dynamic weight prediction mechanism and sampling-based policy search to achieve robust coordination and Pareto-optimal solutions. It demonstrated improved performance and real-world collision avoidance in complex cooperative and adversarial drone tasks.

## Problem

- Multi-robot systems need to optimize multiple, often competing, objectives (e.g., task completion, energy, collision avoidance) while maintaining coordinated behavior.
- Existing multi-objective multi-agent reinforcement learning (MOMARL) methods struggle with adaptability, distributed execution, dynamic objective weighting, and discovering the full Pareto frontier.
- Many approaches rely on fixed scalarization, centralized coordination, or lack a unified preference-conditioned solution, leading to instability and poor scalability in dynamic environments.

## Method

- The **CIMORL** (Coordination-Informed Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning) framework uses ==augmented weighted Tchebycheff scalarization== to discover the entire ==Pareto frontier==, including non-convex regions.
- It implements a ==distributed weight prediction== mechanism based on ==opinion dynamics== (a ==Graph Neural ODE== inspired by the Liquid Graph Time-Constant framework) to achieve ==intra-coalition weight synchronization== and inter-coalition diversity among agents.
- A ==privileged expert training== strategy, employing ==Multi-Objective Neural Tree Search (MONTS)== or ==Multi-Objective Model Predictive Path Integral (MOMPPI)==, provides high-quality supervisory signals for policy learning.

## Results

- CIMORL-MPPI and CIMORL-TS achieved up to **21.2%** hypervolume improvement over baselines like MOMAPPO-TS in multi-resource assignment, indicating superior Pareto front coverage and policy quality.
- Real-world Crazyflie drone experiments showed CIMORL-MPPI consistently avoided collisions and completed tasks in both cooperative and adversarial scenarios (with the lowest disconnection rate of **0.2%**), outperforming baselines that often led to catastrophic failures.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the dynamic synchronization and clustering mechanisms are essential for the full CIMORL framework's performance, contributing to better expected utility and Pareto diversity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving team-wide Pareto optimality in multi-robot systems necessitates either global weight synchronization or local synchronization within functional coalitions if objectives are disjoint.
> - Opinion dynamics can model adaptive, distributed weight prediction, allowing agents to form stable clusters with synchronized preferences while enabling diverse priorities across different coalitions.
> - Sampling-based planning techniques, used as privileged experts during training, effectively generate robust, high-quality multi-objective policies by exploring action spaces comprehensively and providing strong supervisory signals.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-robot systems frequently operate in complex environments requiring the simultaneous optimization of multiple, often competing, objectives while maintaining coordinated behavior. These applications, such as heterogeneous drone swarms or collaborative manipulation, necessitate balancing factors like task completion time, energy consumption, communication bandwidth, and collision avoidance. Multi-objective multi-agent systems (MOMAS) provide a framework to model these complexities.

Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) has emerged as a method for robotic team coordination. However, its extension to multi-objective settings (MOMARL) introduces significant challenges. Traditional single-objective Reinforcement Learning (RL) maximizes a scalar reward, which can mask critical trade-offs when multiple criteria are collapsed into a fixed linear scalarization. Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning (MORL) addresses this by using vector rewards to learn a set of Pareto-optimal policies. Existing single-agent MORL approaches generally follow two paradigms: outer-loop methods, which train separate policies for each preference weight, often requiring extensive retraining; and inner-loop methods, which condition a single policy on the weight, amortizing learning across different weightings. This work positions itself within the inner-loop category.

Despite advances in MORL, much research has focused on isolated agents, neglecting issues such as non-stationarity, partial observability, and credit assignment inherent in multi-robot interactions. Early MOMARL approaches often simplified the problem by scalarizing vector rewards with fixed weights, leading to instability and producing only a single policy per preference. More recent works, such as MOMIX, MO-AIM, and MOAVOA-MADDPG, explore multi-policy learning in multi-agent settings but frequently rely on outer-loop paradigms or lack a unified preference-conditioned solution. Furthermore, contemporary continuous-domain approaches like MOMA-AC and MOMAPPO may not properly synchronize preference weights among agents or use static sampling strategies that fail to adapt to dynamic environmental conditions.

This research addresses these limitations by proposing a framework that supports continuous state and action spaces, enables decentralized execution of preference-conditioned policies, and incorporates centralized coordination mechanisms that adaptively reshape weights based on evolving tasks. The work introduces a dynamic weight prediction methodology, a training paradigm enhanced by sampling-based planning for high-quality supervisory signals, and a Tchebycheff problem formulation to capture non-convex Pareto fronts while preserving decentralized execution. It aims to bridge the gap between theoretical optimality and practical deployment constraints in multi-objective multi-robot systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a principled, scalable framework for deploying adaptive, multi-objective robotic teams in complex environments. This overall goal is motivated by several identified limitations in existing multi-agent reinforcement learning approaches when applied to multi-objective scenarios:

1. **Lack of Adaptability and Distributed Constraints:** Existing methods often rely on fixed trade-off schemes or centralized coordination, which limits their adaptability to dynamic environmental conditions and violates the distributed execution requirements essential for real-world multi-robot systems. These centralized approaches also suffer from poor scalability as the number of agents or environmental complexity increases.
2. **Challenge of Objective Weighting:** A fundamental challenge in multi-objective multi-agent systems is dynamically determining appropriate objective weightings. These weights must guide robot teams toward globally optimal solutions while simultaneously respecting constraints imposed by distributed decision-making, limited communication bandwidth, and heterogeneous sensor capabilities.
3. **Theoretical and Practical Complexities of MOMARL:** Extending single-agent MORL to multi-agent settings introduces significant theoretical and practical complexities, including non-stationarity, partial observability, and credit assignment issues. Existing methods often fail to adequately address these, resulting in unstable or brittle behaviors.
4. **Necessity for Pareto-Optimal Solutions:** To ensure comprehensive and robust solutions, the learning framework must be capable of discovering the entire Pareto frontier, including non-convex regions, which simple linear scalarization methods cannot achieve. Furthermore, theoretical guarantees for Pareto-optimal solutions are needed to ensure the quality of learned policies.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out three specific objectives for the proposed Coordination-Informed Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning (CIMORL) framework:

1. **Distributed Weight Prediction:** To learn a distributed weight prediction model, denoted as Wψ: Z → ∆, that maps agent observations to weight distributions over objectives. This model aims to ensure intra-coalition weight synchronization (i.e., agents within the same functional group agree on objective priorities) while allowing inter-coalition weight diversity (i.e., different functional groups can have different priorities). This directly aligns with the theoretical conditions derived for team-wide Pareto optimality.
2. **Sampling-Based Policy Search:** To develop a reinforcement learning framework that approximates the NP-hard multi-objective optimization problem (formulated using augmented weighted Tchebycheff scalarization) through sampling-based policy search. This approach is designed to enable uniform exploration of the Pareto front via the dynamic weight distribution W, moving beyond the limitations of static weight sampling.
3. **Coalition Constraint Satisfaction:** To ensure that the learned policies satisfy specific coalition constraints derived from theoretical analysis. These constraints mandate that agents within each coalition maintain synchronized weights, while distinct coalitions are permitted to adopt different weight vectors, thereby facilitating efficient task specialization and coordination.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed **Coordination-Informed Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning (CIMORL)** framework integrates three primary innovations: a fully distributed weight prediction mechanism, a privileged expert training strategy, and theoretical guarantees for Pareto-optimal solutions.

**Problem Formulation:**
The multi-objective problem is formulated within a Markov game framework for a fully cooperative multi-agent team (Ns agents) operating in a convex environment with partial observations. Each agent receives an individual K-dimensional reward vector. The objective is to find optimal decentralized policies that maximize collective team performance.
The problem is scalarized using **augmented weighted Tchebycheff scalarization** (Equation 3). This formulation is chosen because, unlike simple linear scalarization, it can discover the entire Pareto frontier, including non-convex regions, by evaluating solutions based on their worst-performing weighted objective. The augmentation parameter ρ guarantees strict Pareto optimality.
The paper introduces two theoretical lemmas regarding weight synchronization:
- **Lemma 1:** For Pareto-optimal solutions for the team to be obtained when agents can achieve any combination of K objectives, the agents’ weight vectors must be synchronized across the entire team.
- **Lemma 2:** When the team is partitioned into coalitions, where each coalition influences a disjoint subset of objectives, Pareto-optimal solutions can be achieved if agents synchronize their weight vectors *locally within their respective coalitions*. This allows for clustered weight vectors among different coalitions.

**Multi-Objective Weight Prediction:**
A distributed dynamical system predicts agent weight distributions. This system is designed to promote local consensus among neighboring agents with similar observations (forming coalitions) and allow divergence between distinct groups (covering all objectives).
1. **Observation Processing:** Each agent processes its local observation (z) through a shared nonlinear mapping (g) to produce an observation-dependent preference embedding (Ξ).
2. **Opinion Dynamics:** The weight evolution is modeled as an opinion formation process. A continuous similarity matrix (σ_Ξ) is defined, approaching unity for similar preference embeddings and zero otherwise, facilitating coalition formation. This is constrained by the communication graph (S) via a Hadamard product to form So_Ξ.
3. **Two-State System:** The weight dynamics are characterized by a two-state system: a primary weight state (x_2) and an auxiliary coordination state (x_1). The auxiliary state acts as an integrator to accumulate disagreement and ensure x_2 converges to a stable consensus. The final weight distribution for each agent follows a Dirichlet distribution, whose concentration parameters are determined by the softmax transformation of x_2.
4. **Graph Neural ODE Framework:** The coupled opinion dynamics are defined as in Equation 7, inspired by the Liquid Graph Time-Constant (LGTC) framework. Learnable weight matrices (W_B, W_A) act as coupling gains. An attention mechanism (So_x) ensures subgroup constant row sum, crucial for convergence.
5. **Theorem 1:** The distributed system in (7) clusters into different stable equilibria, as dictated by the communication graph topology and observed environment, assuming a connected communication graph (Assumption 1).
**Weight Distribution Training:**
The concentration parameters (p_w) define a Dirichlet distribution from which agent weights are sampled. A regret-based loss function is developed to train this distribution (Equation on page 6). This loss encourages sampling high-quality weights by minimizing the regret incurred by using a sampled weight versus the optimal weight for a given Pareto-optimal value function, while also maximizing the policy's advantage. The optimal (utopian) value (v*\_i) for each objective is continuously estimated online.

**Policy Training:**
The framework trains policy (π_θ) and value function (V_ϕ) networks. For advanced variants (CIMORL-TS and CIMORL-MPPI), a **privileged expert framework** is employed during training to generate high-quality supervisory signals. The expert leverages full system dynamics and global environmental state.
1. **Multi-Objective Neural Tree Search (MONTS):** Extends Neural Tree Expansion (NTE) search. For a given node in the search tree, it scalarizes the multi-objective value function using predicted weights and the Tchebycheff formulation (Equation 9). This allows branches to evaluate gains across all objectives simultaneously. MONTS prioritizes deep expansion of promising paths and returns an optimal Gaussian action distribution.
2. **Multi-Objective Model Predictive Path Integral (MOMPPI):** Extends MPPI control. It applies the Tchebycheff utility function (Equation 10) to accumulated rewards across parallel rollout trajectories. It uses the pre-trained policy to directly yield action distributions, enabling broad, uniform exploration suitable for parallel computation.
3. **Policy and Value Function Loss:** The policy (π_θ) is trained using a supervised learning objective (Equation 12) to predict the expert-derived optimal policy distribution (π*), incorporating an auxiliary loss based on the scalarized advantage function and an entropy regularization term. The value function (V_ϕ) is trained by minimizing the squared L2 norm of the temporal difference error across all objectives.

**Closed-Loop Stability:**
The closed-loop stability of the system is analyzed using contraction theory for switching systems.
- **Assumptions:** Gaussian policy distribution (Assumption 2) and deterministic observation transition dynamics (Assumption 3).
- **Lemma 3:** The weight state (x_2) and auxiliary state (x_1) of the opinion dynamics system (7) remain bounded within an invariant set.
- **Theorem 2:** The overall closed-loop multi-agent system is contracting if a small-gain condition is satisfied (Equation 14). This condition relates the contraction rate of the weight dynamics and the observation dynamics to their respective Lipschitz constants (L_G for weight sensitivity to observations, L_w for observation sensitivity to weights). This ensures that the system globally converges to stable weight clusters.
- **Corollary 1:** The stability condition implies that each subsystem induced by a fixed weight selection exhibits contractive behavior, and the weight dynamics system also has its own contraction rate.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The CIMORL framework, including its sampling-based variants CIMORL-TS (Tree Search) and CIMORL-MPPI (Model Predictive Path Integral), was evaluated in two multi-agent domains: multi-resource assignment (cooperative) and multi-team attacker-defender (adversarial). Comparisons were made against MOMAPPO, MO-MIX, and an augmented MOMAPPO-TS baseline, using metrics such as Pareto Front hypervolume, sparsity, diversity, and Expected Scalarized Return (ESR). Ablation studies (CIMORL w/o Sync, CIMORL w/o Clust) were performed to validate the theoretical lemmas.

**Multi-Resource Assignment (Cooperative Scenario):**
- **Training Results:**
 - CIMORL-MPPI and CIMORL-TS achieved superior performance with statistically equivalent results, demonstrating hypervolume values of approximately 8.0 × 10^4 and expected utility of 12.0. This indicates strong Pareto front coverage and policy quality.
 - The base CIMORL method performed moderately lower (hypervolume ~6.4 × 10^4, utility ~11.0) but still outperformed baselines, highlighting the effectiveness of the weight prediction mechanism.
 - CIMORL-MPPI and CIMORL-TS showed a 21.2% hypervolume improvement over MOMAPPO-TS, emphasizing the impact of the proposed dynamic weight prediction model combined with expert guidance.
 - Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of synchronization and clustering. CIMORL w/o Sync yielded lower, stable hypervolume and reduced sparsity/diversity. CIMORL w/o Clust performed better than MOMAPPO but was subordinate to the full CIMORL, maintaining sparsity and diversity but producing strictly dominated solutions.
 - Scaling to 20 agents showed CIMORL-TS and CIMORL-MPPI maintaining returns above 0.9 across objectives, whereas base CIMORL dropped to approximately 0.8. This suggests broader coordination capabilities from sampling search.
- **Real-world Experiments (Crazyflie 2.1 drones):**
 - MOMAPPO agents experienced immediate collisions, preventing task completion.
 - CIMORL agents operated briefly before collisions, failing to complete resource allocation.
 - CIMORL-TS avoided collisions but failed complete resource allocation, attributed to computational starvation from reduced simulation budgets in real-time. It exhibited a higher disconnection rate (2.8%).
 - CIMORL-MPPI was the only method capable of both collision avoidance and successful resource allocation, demonstrating robust coordination with the lowest disconnection rate (0.2%). This highlights MOMPPI's effectiveness in generating robust, average-case optimal decisions under real-time constraints due to its uniform parallel sampling.

**Multi-team Attackers-Defenders (Adversarial Scenario):**
- **Training Results:**
 - CIMORL-MPPI achieved the strongest overall performance (hypervolume ~7.25 × 10^3, utility ~11.0) with low variance, indicating stable policy learning in adversarial settings.
 - CIMORL-TS showed comparable hypervolume but slightly lower utility and higher variance, suggesting excellent exploration but increased sensitivity to unpredictable adversarial shifts.
 - Base CIMORL performed strongly (hypervolume ~7.0 × 10^3, utility ~9.5), with a smaller performance gap to sampling-based variants in this adversarial context.
 - Baselines (MOMAPPO, MO-MIX, MOMAPPO-TS) exhibited lower performance and higher variance, particularly MO-MIX due to non-stationarity.
 - Ablation studies for both CIMORL w/o Sync and CIMORL w/o Clust converged to similar hypervolume values but confirmed performance gaps in expected utility and Pareto diversity compared to the full CIMORL framework.
 - Scaling to larger teams (up to 20 agents) demonstrated that both MPPI and Tree Search variants successfully sustained performance, while the baseline CIMORL policy struggled.
- **Real-world Experiments (Crazyflie 2.1 drones):**
 - Experiments with three competing teams, rotating policies, revealed significant behavioral differences.
 - MOMAPPO consistently led to catastrophic failures due to inter-agent collisions, attributed to its rigid fixed-weight architecture.
 - CIMORL demonstrated improved collision avoidance but exhibited oscillatory hesitation, with agents constantly shifting priority weights and delaying decisive actions. This behavior was consistent with a system that is provably contracting but bottlenecked by baseline training parameters.
 - CIMORL-MPPI demonstrated superior strategic coordination, including emergent behaviors like circular patrol patterns and enclosure strategies, effectively balancing protection and attack. It was the only method consistently capable of online adaptation to unfamiliar opponent behaviors not encountered during training.
 - These real-world results underscored the "deployment gap" between homogeneous self-play training and heterogeneous open-play reality, highlighting the crucial role of online adaptation provided by the sampling-based search in CIMORL-MPPI.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes contributions to the field of multi-robot systems by introducing the Coordination-Informed Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning (CIMORL) framework. The framework addresses fundamental challenges in managing competing objectives in multi-robot systems, particularly under conditions of partial observability and distributed execution constraints.

The **significance** of this research lies in several key areas:

1. **Bridging Theoretical Gaps:** The framework integrates a novel distributed weight prediction mechanism through opinion dynamics, provides theoretical guarantees for achieving Pareto-optimal solutions via coalition formation (Lemmas 1 and 2, Theorem 1), and employs the augmented Tchebycheff scalarization to handle non-convex Pareto fronts. This theoretical foundation offers a robust basis for distributed multi-objective coordination.
2. **Enhanced Adaptability and Scalability:** By dynamically adapting objective preferences based on local observations and neighbor communication, CIMORL overcomes the limitations of fixed or centralized coordination mechanisms. This adaptability is critical for multi-robot systems operating in dynamic and uncertain environments, promoting scalability by enabling decentralized decision-making.
3. **Superior Performance through Expert Guidance:** The integration of a privileged expert training strategy, leveraging multi-objective extensions of Monte Carlo Tree Search (CIMORL-TS) and Model Predictive Path Integral control (CIMORL-MPPI), demonstrably enhances policy optimality and accelerates convergence. The experimental results show significant improvements in hypervolume and expected utility compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
4. **Robustness in Real-World Scenarios:** The real-world experiments using Crazyflie drones provide empirical validation of the framework's robustness. CIMORL-MPPI, in particular, proved capable of both collision avoidance and successful task completion in complex cooperative and adversarial scenarios where baseline methods failed. This highlights the framework's practical applicability and its ability to handle deployment gaps between training and heterogeneous real-world conditions through online adaptation.

The **potential impact** of this research is broad, extending to various applications of multi-robot systems where complex, conflicting objectives must be optimized collaboratively. This includes:

- **Search and Rescue:** Deploying drone swarms to balance search coverage, energy consumption, and communication maintenance.
- **Logistics and Resource Management:** Optimizing delivery routes, resource allocation, and collision avoidance for robotic fleets.
- **Environmental Monitoring:** Coordinating sensor networks to maximize data collection while minimizing energy use and maintaining connectivity.
- **Autonomous Defense Systems:** Developing coordinated attack and defense strategies for unmanned aerial or ground vehicles.

Future research directions identified by the authors include improving the convergence speed of the weight model, investigating the hierarchical scheme within the weight-policy model, exploring methods to minimize policy variance, and conducting a formal analysis of the weight adaptation mechanism's robustness against communication delays and network noise. Additionally, extending the coordination framework to explicitly handle advanced path-planning in cluttered environments with static and dynamic obstacles is an avenue for future work. These planned extensions suggest continued efforts to refine the framework and broaden its applicability to even more complex real-world robotic challenges.
