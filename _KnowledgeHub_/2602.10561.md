# Morphogenetic Assembly and Adaptive Control for Heterogeneous Modular Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a closed-loop automation framework for heterogeneous modular robots, encompassing both their morphogenetic construction and adaptive control. It features a hierarchical planner for dynamic assembly of diverse module types and a GPU-accelerated, morphology-agnostic controller that enables real-time locomotion for newly formed configurations.

## Problem

- Efficiently constructing diverse, functional robot morphologies from heterogeneous modules faces challenges due to the high complexity and cost of self-reconfiguring systems and the lack of agency in externally assembled passive structures.
- Developing adaptive control policies for reconfigurable robots is difficult because the vast and variable morphological space requires methods that can generalize across radically different body plans without extensive, configuration-specific retraining.

## Method

- A hierarchical reconfiguration planner leverages a high-level bidirectional heuristic search, augmented with a type-penalization term for heterogeneous modules, to generate module relocation sequences, complemented by a low-level A* search for cost-optimal assembler robot trajectories.
- An online, morphology-agnostic motion controller utilizes a GPU-accelerated Annealed-Variance ==Model Predictive Path Integral== (MPPI) strategy, employing multi-stage variance annealing for robust exploration and local convergence, parallelized on a GPU for real-time operation.

## Results

- Heuristics augmented with the type-penalization term consistently achieved over **80%** success rates in reconfiguration tasks, significantly outperforming unaugmented heuristics which failed in complex heterogeneous scenarios.
- The Greedy heuristic consistently led to solutions with lower total physical execution costs for the assembler robot compared to the Hungarian heuristic, especially in highly heterogeneous module compositions.
- The GPU-accelerated Annealed-Variance MPPI controller achieved real-time control frequencies of 50 Hz and demonstrated significantly improved velocity tracking accuracy with minimal error, while standard MPPI was limited to approximately 3 Hz with persistent offsets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The inclusion of a type-penalization term within the high-level planning heuristics is essential for ensuring robustness and scalability when planning reconfigurations with heterogeneous module types.
> - A Greedy heuristic, while not globally optimal in abstract assignment, practically outperforms the Hungarian heuristic in generating more cost-efficient physical execution plans for the assembler robot.
> - Combining multi-stage variance annealing with GPU acceleration in the MPPI controller enables real-time (50 Hz) and highly accurate locomotion control for novel robot morphologies without specific retraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within a converging landscape of robotics, architecture, and materials science, where the long-term goal of automated construction extends beyond passive structures to functional, intelligent systems. A central theme in this broader vision is the concept of embodied intelligence that can self-create, self-evolve, and self-maintain, challenging the traditional assumption of a fixed robot body. This parallels the biological distinction between autopoietic (self-producing) and allopoietic (externally produced) systems.

Current approaches to physical assembly of modular robots generally fall into two categories:

- **Self-reconfiguration:** This paradigm involves embedding intelligence and electromechanical components within each module, allowing individual modules to reconfigure themselves. While successful for small-scale, multifunctional robots, this approach typically results in high unit complexity, cost, and weight, which can hinder scalability and conflict with the characteristics observed in living systems. Examples include homogeneous systems with self-soldering connectors or heterogeneous systems with active struts.
- **External assembly:** This approach utilizes simpler, often passive, modules that are manipulated and assembled by a dedicated external robot. While effective for constructing large, passive structures like building skeletons, the resulting structures often lack agency or intelligence.

Concurrently, the field of adaptive control for modular robots faces significant challenges due to the vast and diverse morphological space that arises from reconfigurability. This "manifold gap" makes it difficult to develop control policies that can generalize across radically different body plans. Existing research addresses this through various strategies:

- **Cross-embodiment locomotion:** Methods like Shared Modular Policies (SMP) attempt to control multiple heterogeneous morphologies with a single policy by reusing control modules and leveraging local message passing. Other approaches use unified representations based on morphology-task graphs and behavior distillation. However, these can struggle when the required action distributions for different embodiments diverge significantly.
- **Morphology-control co-design:** This involves simultaneously optimizing robot morphology and its control strategy. Robogrammar, for instance, uses Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) as an underlying controller to synthesize gaits for diverse terrains and robot topologies. End-to-end methods employing Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) and Transformers encode robot topology to generate control actions.
- **Evolutionary robotics:** This domain explores methods like the Triangle of Life theory, combining offline evolution and online learning to adapt control parameters (e.g., CPG weights) to body morphology during an "infant stage."

The presented work integrates elements from both external assembly and adaptive control. It proposes a novel modular robotic system that combines carrier robots with functional modules to enable dynamic assembly, aiming to overcome the limitations of high complexity in self-reconfiguration and the lack of agency in passive external assembly. Furthermore, it seeks to develop a morphology-agnostic control strategy to address the challenges of adaptive locomotion for newly formed and diverse configurations, building upon and optimizing existing MPPI-based control ideas.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish a closed-loop automation framework for heterogeneous modular robots. This framework is designed to manage the entire lifecycle from the physical construction of a robot's morphology to its adaptive control and subsequent operation. The motivation stems from a broader vision of automated construction that moves beyond static structures towards creating large-scale, functional robotic systems capable of sensing, responding, and acting autonomously.

The framework is motivated by two core challenges identified in the current state of modular robotics:

1. **Efficient Morphogenetic Construction**: The ability to dynamically assemble diverse robot configurations from a set of heterogeneous modules.
 - **Motivation**: Existing self-reconfiguring robots, while capable, often incur high complexity, cost, and weight per module, hindering scalability. Conversely, external assembly systems typically construct passive structures lacking intelligence and agency. The goal is to bridge this gap by enabling an external builder robot to manipulate simpler, specialized modules to form complex, functional robotic embodiments. This requires addressing the computational complexity of planning assembly sequences in a high-dimensional state space, particularly with multiple module types. The framework seeks to enable advanced tasks like merging separate robots into a unified system or splitting a composite robot into its constituent parts, which necessitates an efficient and robust planning mechanism.
 - **Problem Formulation**: This translates into the **Highly Efficient Reconfiguration Problem**, which seeks to find an optimal action sequence for the assembler robot to transform an initial module configuration into a desired goal configuration, minimizing the physical execution cost of these actions.

2. **Adaptive Control for Novel Morphologies**: The capacity to endow any newly assembled robot configuration with immediate and adaptive locomotion capabilities, without requiring extensive, configuration-specific retraining or manual design.
 - **Motivation**: The vast and variable morphological space inherent in reconfigurable robots presents a significant challenge for control. Traditional control methods are often designed for fixed-body robots and do not generalize well to systems with dynamically changing topologies. Current cross-embodiment learning methods can struggle when the required action distributions for different morphologies are too dissimilar. Therefore, there is a need for a control strategy that is "morphology-agnostic" and can provide real-time agency to an unknown, newly constructed robot body.
 - **Problem Formulation**: This leads to the **Adaptive Motion Problem**, which aims to find a morphology-agnostic control policy that can generate actions for a given high-level task command (e.g., target velocity) for any valid assembled robot, maximizing expected cumulative reward over a finite horizon. A critical constraint is that this method must operate without requiring architecture-specific retraining or manual design.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to realize "machine life" that exhibits morphological metabolism and adaptive self-maintenance, moving beyond fixed-body robotics to systems that can dynamically construct and control their own physical forms in response to task requirements or environmental changes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework addresses the stated objectives through a two-pronged approach: a hierarchical planning system for morphogenetic construction and a GPU-accelerated, morphology-agnostic controller for adaptive motion generation.

**System Description:**
The system comprises heterogeneous cubic modules and a single assembler robot. All modules share a 12 cm cubic form factor and utilize reversible self-soldering connectors for both mechanical robustness and electrical conductivity. Three types of modules are defined:
- **Base modules:** Provide structural support, forming the primary skeletal elements.
- **Joint modules:** Integrate motors to provide a single rotational degree of freedom (0-360 degrees).
- **Wheel modules:** Feature retractable wheels, deployed via a lead screw mechanism and secured by electromagnetic locks, enabling locomotion. When retracted, they maintain a cubic form for lattice compatibility.

The **assembler robot** features a dual-foot base that allows it to traverse the assembled structures by alternately engaging its feet with module surface recesses. It also has a multi-degree-of-freedom manipulator with a claw-like end-effector for module manipulation.

The framework supports complex reconfiguration tasks such as merging separate robots or splitting a combined robot. These tasks are decomposed into a fundamental reconfiguration problem: efficiently transforming one connected set of modules into another. The **robot combination process** involves an "approach phase" where a smaller robot reconfigures to dock with a larger one, followed by a "merge phase" where the combined entity is reconfigured to the target morphology. The **robot separation process** is the inverse, starting with reconfiguring the initial robot into a target sub-robot plus an auxiliary structure, which then detaches and reconfigures.

**Hierarchical Reconfiguration Planner:**
To manage the high-dimensional state space associated with discrete module placements and continuous robot motions, a hierarchical planner is employed. This planner decouples high-level task planning from low-level motion execution.

- **High-Level Planner:** Operates in the discrete configuration space of the modular structure. It uses a bidirectional heuristic search to generate a sequence of module relocations that transform an initial configuration (C_ini) into a goal configuration (C_goal).
 - **Heuristics:** Two types are investigated: a Hungarian algorithm-derived heuristic (admissible, optimal assignment) and a Greedy heuristic (faster, non-admissible).
 - **Type-Penalization Term:** Crucially, both heuristics are augmented with a type-penalization term. This term is designed to handle heterogeneous module types by breaking search symmetries and providing stronger directional guidance, which is essential for robustness in multi-type scenarios.
 - **Macro-actions:** Each step in the high-level plan is a "module relocation" (pick-and-place operation).
 - **Validity Checks:** The planner incorporates checks to ensure that: 1) any module picked up is a "leaf node" whose removal does not partition the existing structure's connectivity, and 2) the drop-off location is kinematically reachable by the assembler robot.

- **Low-Level Planner:** For each module relocation macro-action proposed by the high-level planner, this component computes a cost-optimal execution trajectory for the assembler robot.
 - **Algorithm:** A* search is used in the robot's state space, which is defined by its foot placements and any module it is holding.
 - **Primitive Actions:** These include locomotion (moving a foot to an adjacent cell), rotation (pivoting around a foot), and manipulation (pick and place operations).
 - **Cost Function:** The objective is to minimize the total execution cost of these primitive actions, which directly correlates to the physical effort and time required for the task.

**Dynamic Motion Generation (Adaptive Control):**
After assembly, the framework endows the new morphology with dynamic locomotion capabilities through a two-stage pipeline:
1. **Model Translation:** The final robot configuration graph (G_goal) is automatically converted into a simulatable model (M) for a GPU-accelerated physics engine. This allows for rapid evaluation of robot dynamics.
2. **Morphology-Agnostic Motion Controller:** An online control policy is generated using a sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) strategy.
 - **Controller Choice:** Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) is chosen due to its model-free nature (requiring no analytical dynamics) and inherent parallelizability.
 - **Enhancement: GPU-accelerated Annealing Variance MPPI:** To overcome the limitations of standard MPPI (fixed sampling variance, poor performance on complex, non-convex landscapes), an enhanced MPPI controller is introduced.
 - **Multi-stage Annealing:** Within each control cycle, the controller employs a multi-stage variance annealing strategy. It begins with a large sampling variance to facilitate global exploration of the action space and then progressively reduces the variance to achieve precise local convergence. This dynamic balancing of exploration and exploitation is critical for robust and high-performance locomotion.
 - **GPU Acceleration:** The entire process is massively parallelized on a GPU, enabling real-time control frequencies, a significant advancement over CPU-based MPPI methods often limited to offline policy optimization. The controller refines a nominal action sequence by sampling noise sequences, evaluating costs via parallel rollouts, and updating the nominal sequence based on weighted samples.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed framework was evaluated through extensive simulations conducted on a workstation equipped with an i9-13900KF CPU and an NVIDIA 4090D GPU. A suite of procedurally generated reconfiguration tasks was used, varying in problem scale (20 to 75 modules), configuration overlap (low 0.8, medium 0.5, high 0.2), and heterogeneous composition (module type ratios: TypeA [100% Base], TypeB [80% Base, 20% Joint], TypeC [60% Base, 20% Joint, 20% Wheel]). Each parameter combination was tested over 100 random instances with a 60-second timeout.

**A. Ablation Study on Heuristic Design (Type-Penalization)**
An ablation study was performed to assess the contribution of the type-penalization term within the high-level planner's heuristics.
- **Results**: Heuristics incorporating the type-penalization term consistently achieved high success rates (exceeding 80%) across nearly all tested problem scales and complexities. In contrast, heuristics without this term showed a significant degradation in performance. Their success rates sharply declined as problem scale and heterogeneity increased, approaching zero in the most challenging scenarios.
- **Conclusion**: This finding indicates that the type-penalization term is crucial for ensuring the planner's robustness and scalability in heterogeneous environments. It effectively addresses heuristic ties and prevents deadlocks that can arise in multi-type module reconfigurations.

**B. Impact of Heuristics on Execution Cost**
The research evaluated how the choice of high-level heuristic (Greedy vs. Hungarian) influenced the total physical execution cost of the generated plans, measured as the cumulative cost of all primitive assembler actions.
- **Results**: The Greedy heuristic consistently led to solutions with a lower total execution cost compared to the Hungarian heuristic. This advantage in cost-efficiency became more pronounced with increasing module heterogeneity, particularly in TypeC scenarios (high heterogeneity).
- **Conclusion**: Although the Hungarian algorithm provides an admissible, globally optimal assignment in an abstract sense, the Greedy approach demonstrated practical superiority in generating sequences of module relocations that were more efficient for the assembler robot to physically execute. This suggests that the Greedy heuristic leads to more economical and practical physical plans.

**C. Controller Performance Evaluation**
The dynamic motion generation pipeline was benchmarked on a velocity tracking task, where an assembled quadruped robot was required to track a target forward velocity of 0.8 m/s. Performance of a standard MPPI implementation was compared against the proposed GPU-accelerated Annealed-Variance MPPI.
- **Results on Tracking Accuracy**: The Annealed-Variance MPPI exhibited significantly improved tracking accuracy. It rapidly converged to the target velocity with minimal steady-state error. In contrast, the standard MPPI displayed a persistent velocity offset and notable oscillations around the target.
- **Results on Control Frequency**: The GPU-accelerated Annealed-Variance MPPI achieved a real-time control frequency of 50 Hz. The standard MPPI, on the same hardware and task, was limited to approximately 3 Hz.
- **Conclusion**: The integration of the Annealed-Variance MPPI controller substantially enhances the performance and stability of locomotion. Its GPU acceleration enables real-time operation, thereby effectively closing the loop from high-level assembly planning to adaptive dynamic control.

**Overall Validation:**
The framework successfully validated the entire closed-loop process, demonstrating module assembly, robot merging, robot splitting, and dynamic motion generation for the resulting diverse morphologies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that contributes to the field of robotics by addressing fundamental challenges in morphogenetic construction and adaptive control for heterogeneous modular robots. Its significance and potential impact can be considered across several dimensions:

- **Advancement in Autonomous Construction:** The framework moves beyond the construction of passive structures by enabling the automated assembly of functional robotic entities. This capability is relevant for future applications requiring uncrewed construction and maintenance in extreme or hazardous environments, such as space exploration, deep-sea operations, or post-disaster scenarios, where human intervention is limited.
- **Enabling Morphological Metabolism and Self-Maintenance:** By allowing robots to dynamically create, evolve, and maintain their own bodies, the work contributes to the concept of "robot life" and autopoietic systems. This challenges the traditional fixed-body assumption in embodied intelligence, paving the way for more biologically inspired robotic capabilities.
- **Enhanced Resource Efficiency and Adaptability:** The system treats modular components as a recyclable hardware resource pool. This allows for optimal resource allocation and reuse, improving the overall flexibility and hardware utilization efficiency of robotic systems. For instance, modules used to construct a quadruped for transport can be disassembled and reassembled into a mobile manipulator for subsequent assembly tasks, as illustrated in the paper. This adaptability enables rapid reconfiguration in response to changing mission requirements or environmental feedback.
- **Comprehensive Lifecycle Management for Modular Robots:** The framework offers a complete solution encompassing the entire operational lifecycle: from high-level planning and module selection to physical assembly, control deployment, task execution, and subsequent deconstruction and module recovery. This integrated approach provides a robust paradigm for managing complex reconfigurable robotic systems.
- **Robustness in Heterogeneous Reconfiguration:** The introduction of the type-penalization term within the hierarchical planner is a significant contribution to handling the complexities of heterogeneous modular systems. It ensures the planner's scalability and robustness in scenarios involving multiple module types, a common challenge in practical modular robotics.
- **Real-time Adaptive Control for Novel Morphologies:** The development of the GPU-accelerated Annealed-Variance MPPI controller is crucial for providing immediate agency to newly assembled, previously unknown robot configurations. Its ability to achieve high control frequencies (50 Hz) and superior tracking accuracy in real-time without configuration-specific retraining is a key step towards practical deployment of adaptive modular robots.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper sets a groundwork for future investigations, particularly emphasizing the critical challenge of Sim-to-Real transfer. Successfully bridging the gap between simulated validation and physical hardware implementation will be the next major step, validating the practical robustness and real-world applicability of these algorithms.

In summary, this research provides a comprehensive and efficient automation framework that moves reconfigurable modular robotics closer to realizing autonomous, adaptable, and resource-efficient robotic systems capable of dynamic self-assembly and real-time control across diverse morphologies.
