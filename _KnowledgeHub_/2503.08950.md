# FP3: A 3D Foundation Policy for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> FP3 (3D Foundation Policy) is the first large-scale 3D foundation policy model for robotic manipulation, which leverages point cloud data to achieve robust generalization across diverse tasks, objects, and environments. The model demonstrated a 100% success rate on household manipulation tasks like pouring water and folding towels, even with challenging variations such as novel objects or lighting changes.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic manipulation systems struggle with generalizing to novel scenarios, objects, and environments due to their reliance on limited 2D visual information.
- Current robot foundation models based on 2D images lack explicit 3D information, are sensitive to visual appearance changes, and have difficulty reasoning about occlusions and spatial constraints.
- Achieving precise and robust physical interaction in complex 3D environments requires a deeper understanding of object geometries and spatial relationships than 2D representations can provide.

## Method

- **FP3** (3D ==Foundation Policy==) is introduced as the first large-scale 3D foundation policy model for robotic manipulation, utilizing point clouds for explicit geometric information.
- The architecture employs a multimodal diffusion transformer, integrating point cloud features from third-person and wrist-view stereo cameras, natural language instructions via CLIP, and proprioceptive robot state.
- Training involves a pre-training phase on the DROID dataset (60,000 trajectories) followed by a parameter-efficient fine-tuning phase using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for new tasks.

## Results

- FP3 achieved a **100%** success rate across four challenging household manipulation tasks (folding towels, cleaning tables, standing up cups, pouring water) after fine-tuning with only 80 demonstrations.
- The model demonstrated superior generalization, maintaining high success rates in 'in-the-wild' scenarios such as different lighting conditions (**100%**), novel objects (**100%**), and background changes (**100%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that 3D representation is critical, with performance dropping from **100%** to **30%** average success rate when using 2D images instead of point clouds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - 3D point cloud representations explicitly encode geometric information, making them more robust to lighting and viewpoint changes compared to 2D image observations for robotic manipulation.
> - A two-stage training approach with large-scale pre-training on a diverse dataset and subsequent parameter-efficient fine-tuning with LoRA enables data-efficient adaptation to new tasks.
> - Integrating multimodal inputs (point clouds from multiple views, language instructions, and proprioception) within a diffusion transformer architecture is crucial for comprehensive understanding and precise action generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation systems have traditionally struggled with generalizing to new scenarios, objects, and environments. While recent advances in foundation models have revolutionized fields like natural language processing and computer vision, their application to robotics has been limited by the inherent challenges of physical interaction with the 3D world.

The recently introduced FP3 (3D Foundation Policy) represents a significant breakthrough in this domain. It is the first large-scale 3D foundation policy model specifically designed for robotic manipulation using point clouds. By leveraging 3D geometric information rather than relying solely on 2D images, FP3 achieves remarkable generalization capabilities across diverse manipulation tasks, environments, and objects.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. The Limitations of 2D Approaches

Current robot foundation models primarily rely on 2D image observations, which present several limitations:

1. Lack of explicit 3D information, making it difficult to accurately perceive spatial relationships and object geometries
2. Sensitivity to visual appearance changes, such as lighting conditions and viewpoint variations
3. Inability to effectively reason about occlusions and spatial constraints

These limitations are particularly problematic for robotic manipulation tasks that require precise understanding of the 3D environment. For instance, when a robot needs to grasp an object, pour liquid, or manipulate items in cluttered environments, 2D representations often fail to provide sufficient information for robust performance.

3D point clouds offer a more suitable representation for robotics as they:
- Explicitly encode geometric information
- Are more invariant to changes in lighting and appearance
- Better represent spatial relationships between objects
- Enable more accurate reasoning about physical interactions

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. FP3 Architecture

FP3 employs a sophisticated architecture that leverages multiple input modalities:

1. **Point Cloud Processing**: The model utilizes a pre-trained Uni3D ViT encoder to extract semantic and geometric features from point clouds captured by two stereo cameras - a third-person view and a wrist-mounted camera.

2. **Language Understanding**: A CLIP model processes natural language instructions, enabling the robot to understand user commands.

3. **Proprioceptive Awareness**: The robot's internal state (position, orientation, gripper state) is encoded through an MLP network.

4. **Diffusion Transformer**: At the core of FP3 is an encoder-decoder diffusion transformer that integrates these multimodal inputs to generate action sequences.

The diffusion transformer works by learning to denoise action trajectories, gradually refining noisy actions into precise control commands. This approach has proven particularly effective for complex sequential decision-making tasks.

The mathematical formulation of the diffusion process can be represented as:

$$q(\mathbf{a}_{1:T}^{1:K} | \mathbf{a}_{1:T}^{0}) = \prod_{t=1}^{T} \prod_{k=1}^{K} q(\mathbf{a}_t^k | \mathbf{a}_t^{k-1})$$

where $\mathbf{a}_t^k$ represents the action at timestep $t$ with noise level $k$, and the model learns to reverse this process.

#### 3.2. Training Methodology

FP3's training follows a two-stage approach similar to other foundation models:

#### 3.3. Pre-training Phase

- Trained on the DROID dataset containing 60,000 demonstration trajectories across 86 diverse manipulation tasks
- Point clouds are preprocessed through cropping and downsampling via farthest point sampling (FPS)
- Training utilizes the AdamW optimizer with a cosine learning rate schedule

#### 3.4. Fine-tuning Phase

- Employs parameter-efficient fine-tuning using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)
- Requires only 80 demonstrations (10 per environment) for each downstream task
- Fine-tuning is significantly more data-efficient compared to training from scratch

This approach enables FP3 to leverage knowledge gained during pre-training while efficiently adapting to new tasks with minimal additional data collection.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Experimental Results

FP3 was evaluated on a variety of household manipulation tasks using a Franka Panda 7DoF robot arm equipped with a Robotiq gripper and stereo cameras, as shown in Figure 5. The experimental setup included:

The model demonstrated impressive performance across four challenging tasks:
1. Folding towels
2. Cleaning tables
3. Standing up cups
4. Pouring water into cups

Quantitatively, FP3 achieved a remarkable 100% success rate on these tasks after fine-tuning, significantly outperforming baseline methods:

```
Task             | FP3   | DP    | DP3
----------------|-------|-------|-------
Fold Towel      | 100%  | 60%   | 40%
Clean Table     | 100%  | 0%    | 20%
Stand Up Cup    | 100%  | 40%   | 40%
Pour Water      | 100%  | 20%   | 20%
----------------|-------|-------|-------
Average         | 100%  | 30%   | 30%
```

What's particularly impressive is that these results were achieved with only 80 demonstrations for fine-tuning, highlighting the data efficiency of the approach.

#### 4.2. Generalization Capabilities

The most notable achievement of FP3 is its superior generalization ability. The model was tested in challenging "in-the-wild" scenarios with:

- Different lighting conditions
- Changed camera viewpoints
- Novel objects not seen during training
- Distracting objects in the environment
- Varied background surfaces

The results, shown in Figure 4, demonstrate that FP3 maintains high performance even under these challenging conditions:

- **Light variations**: 100% success rate (vs. 60% for 2D approaches)
- **Camera view changes**: 80% success rate (vs. 0-40% for 2D approaches)
- **Distractors**: 60% success rate (vs. 0-20% for 2D approaches)
- **Novel objects**: 100% success rate (vs. 20-40% for 2D approaches)
- **Background changes**: 100% success rate (vs. 0-40% for 2D approaches)

This robust generalization is attributed to the 3D point cloud representation, which provides explicit geometric information that is more invariant to visual appearance changes.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Comprehensive ablation studies were conducted to understand the contribution of different components of FP3:

1. **3D Representation**: Replacing point clouds with 2D images led to a significant drop in performance (from 100% to 30% average success rate), confirming the importance of 3D geometric information.

2. **Data Scaling**: Reducing the pre-training data from 60,000 to 10,000 demonstrations decreased performance by 15-20%, highlighting the importance of large-scale pre-training.

3. **Model Scaling**: Experiments with smaller model sizes showed that scaling the model is critical for achieving strong generalization.

4. **Input Modalities**: Removing either the third-person view or wrist-view point clouds reduced performance, demonstrating the value of multiple viewpoints.

These findings validate the design choices of FP3 and provide insights for future research in robot foundation models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Conclusion and Future Work

FP3 represents a significant advancement in robotic manipulation by introducing a 3D foundation policy that achieves remarkable generalization capabilities. By leveraging point cloud data, the model gains a more robust understanding of the 3D environment, enabling it to perform complex manipulation tasks even in previously unseen scenarios.

The key contributions of FP3 include:

1. **3D Representation**: The use of point clouds provides explicit geometric information critical for physical interaction.

2. **Foundation Model Approach**: Large-scale pre-training followed by efficient fine-tuning enables rapid adaptation to new tasks.

3. **Multimodal Architecture**: The integration of point clouds, language instructions, and proprioception creates a comprehensive understanding of the manipulation context.

4. **Robust Generalization**: Demonstrated ability to handle novel objects, environments, and conditions without retraining.

Future research directions include:
- Integration with larger 3D datasets for pre-training
- Combining 3D point cloud features with complementary 2D image features
- Exploring more complex and dynamic manipulation tasks
- Developing more sophisticated language understanding capabilities

As robotics continues to move toward more general-purpose systems, approaches like FP3 that combine 3D perception with foundation model techniques will be essential for creating robots that can operate effectively in the diverse and unpredictable environments of the real world.
