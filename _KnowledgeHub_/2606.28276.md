# SimFoundry: Modular and Automated Scene Generation for Policy Learning and Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> SimFoundry, a modular and automated system, transforms a single real-world video into diverse, interactive simulation environments for robot policy learning and evaluation. The system achieves a mean Pearson correlation of 0.911 between simulated and real-world policy performance and significantly improves sim-to-real transfer and generalization by automatically generating varied "digital cousins" for training.

## Problem

- High cost, labor, and time required for collecting large volumes of high-quality real-world robot manipulation data and systematically evaluating robot policies.
- Manual construction of simulation environments that accurately mirror real-world scenes in terms of visuals, geometry, and dynamics is complex and labor-intensive.
- Existing real-to-sim systems often lack a unified approach, focusing only on scene reconstruction without components for physical interaction, task specification, or diverse data generation for policy training and evaluation.

## Method

- **SimFoundry** employs a ==three-stage pipeline== (==Extraction==, ==Generation==, ==Augmentation==) to automatically convert a single RGB video into an interactive, 'sim-ready' ==digital twin== of a real-world scene.
- It integrates various ==vision-language models (VLMs)== and ==3D generative models== to automate object segmentation, 3D mesh generation, pose estimation, articulation modeling, and physical property annotation.
- The system expands reconstructed scenes into an 'unlimited number of ==digital cousins==' by automatically generating ==affordance-preserving variations== in object instances, scene layouts, and task specifications.

## Results

- SimFoundry achieved zero-shot F1 scores of **0.81-0.92** for scene reconstruction, surpassing state-of-the-art baselines, with minimal human tuning further increasing F1 scores to **0.93-0.99**.
- Simulation evaluations yielded a mean Pearson correlation coefficient of **0.911** with real-world policy performance across **7 tasks** and **5 policy architectures**, outperforming prior baselines by over **0.59**.
- Policies trained with SimFoundry's digital cousins demonstrated average task success rate improvements of **17%** (object), **21%** (scene), and **40%** (task) in sim-to-real transfer, leading to improved generalization to novel real-world conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A modular pipeline integrating state-of-the-art VLMs and 3D generative models can fully automate the complex process of creating physically grounded, interactive simulation environments from a single real-world video input.
> - Augmenting reconstructed scenes with 'digital cousins' (variations in objects, layouts, and tasks) is critical for training robust robot policies that exhibit enhanced generalization and improved zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
> - Simulation evaluations performed in environments reconstructed by SimFoundry demonstrate a strong correlation with real-world policy performance, establishing a reliable and cost-effective method for benchmarking robot policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robotic foundation models, trained on extensive manipulation datasets, has advanced autonomous robot capabilities. However, the acquisition of high-quality, large-volume robot manipulation data in the real world remains a labor-intensive and costly endeavor, often requiring prolonged teleoperation efforts. Furthermore, the systematic and scientific evaluation of these models in real-world scenarios demands substantial resources, frequently involving thousands of trials across various tasks to establish robust comparisons.

Simulation has emerged as a scalable alternative to address these bottlenecks in robot policy training and evaluation. Automated data generation tools can synthesize diverse, high-quality demonstrations, facilitating the training and improvement of real-world agents. Additionally, simulation-based evaluations have demonstrated strong correlations with real-world outcomes, offering a more efficient benchmarking method. Despite these advancements, the manual construction of simulation environments, particularly those requiring precise alignment with real-world scenes in terms of visuals, geometry, and dynamics, presents a significant challenge.

To mitigate the manual effort involved in simulation development, real-to-sim scene construction approaches have been explored. These methods leverage 3D reconstruction and generative models to create "sim-ready" environments that support physically grounded robotic interaction with reduced manual overhead. While this paradigm supports both sim-to-real transfer and predictive real-world benchmarking, existing systems often exhibit limitations. Many systems primarily focus on generating simulation-ready 3D scenes but lack the necessary components for physical interaction, task specification, or data generation to complete the sim-to-real policy-learning loop. Conversely, systems designed for simulation-based policy evaluation frequently rely on manually tuned scenes, target short-horizon atomic manipulation tasks, or do not automatically generate diverse objects, scenes, and tasks for policy training.

SimFoundry addresses these gaps by providing a unified and modular system that integrates scene reconstruction, environment augmentation, and policy learning/evaluation. Unlike many prior works that compartmentalize these capabilities, SimFoundry aims to automate the entire pipeline from a single real-world video input. This approach builds upon existing work in 3D asset generation, articulated object modeling, and real-to-sim transfer, but distinguishes itself by offering a more comprehensive and automated solution that supports a wider range of manipulation complexities and generates diverse "digital cousins" for enhanced policy generalization, as summarized in the comparison with prior systems (Table 1).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of SimFoundry are motivated by the significant challenges and costs associated with training and evaluating robot policies exclusively in real-world environments. The core motivations include:

- **Reducing Cost and Scaling Policy Development:** Real-world data collection and experimentation for robot policies are inherently expensive, time-consuming, and difficult to scale. SimFoundry aims to provide a more efficient alternative by enabling policy training and evaluation in simulation, thereby accelerating the development cycle and reducing the reliance on physical hardware.
- **Automating Simulation Environment Creation:** Manual construction of simulation environments that accurately mirror real-world scenes in terms of visual, geometric, and dynamic properties is a complex and labor-intensive process. A key objective is to automate this process, transforming a single real-world input video into an interactive, "sim-ready" digital twin of the scene with minimal human intervention.
- **Ensuring Realism and Physical Grounding:** The reconstructed simulation environments must be physically accurate to be useful for robotics. This involves correctly modeling rigid and articulated objects, their poses, collision geometries, and physical properties (e.g., mass, friction), ensuring that robotic interactions within the simulation are representative of real-world physics.
- **Generating Diverse Training Environments (Digital Cousins):** A significant limitation of merely reconstructing a single digital twin is its lack of diversity, which can hinder policy generalization. SimFoundry aims to automatically expand the reconstructed scene into an "unlimited number of digital cousins." These are affordance-preserving variations along three axes: object instance, scene layout, and task specification. This augmentation is crucial for training policies that are robust to variations in object morphology, spatial arrangements, and task conditions.
- **Supporting Real-to-Sim Policy Evaluation:** A critical objective is to enable reliable benchmarking of real-world policies in simulation. SimFoundry seeks to demonstrate that evaluations performed in its simulated environments can strongly correlate with actual real-world policy performance, offering a predictive and cost-effective method for assessing policy efficacy and comparing different architectures.
- **Facilitating Sim-to-Real Policy Training:** Beyond evaluation, SimFoundry aims to generate high-quality synthetic data that can be used to train robot policies in simulation, with these policies demonstrating effective zero-shot transfer to real-world tasks. The system is motivated to prove that simulation-trained policies, especially those benefiting from digital cousins, can generalize to novel real-world conditions.
- **Modular and Adaptable System Design:** To ensure longevity and continuous improvement, the system is designed with a modular architecture. This allows for the integration of advancements in underlying foundation models (e.g., for perception, asset generation, or physics annotation) as they become available, without requiring a complete redesign of the entire pipeline.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SimFoundry operates through a modular, three-stage pipeline: **Extraction**, **Generation**, and **Augmentation**, as illustrated in Figure 2. An additional process for **Background Reconstruction and Alignment** complements these stages.

#### 3.1. Extraction

The process begins with a raw RGB video input.
1. **Frame and Depth Estimation:** A representative RGB frame ($I_s$) is selected, and its corresponding depth map ($D_s$) is estimated using off-the-shelf depth estimation models ($V_{im2depth}$), such as DepthAnything3. Camera intrinsics are used to lift this RGB-D observation into a scene point cloud ($P_s$).
2. **Scene Alignment:** A ground plane is identified using a scene segmentation model ($V_{image_{seg}}$), and the reconstructed scene is aligned with the simulator's world frame based on this ground plane.
3. **Iterative Object Segmentation and Removal:** A scene-understanding VLM ($V_{scene}$) detects objects within the scene. For each detected object, $V_{image_{seg}}$ iteratively segments out its foreground mask ($m_i$) and corresponding RGB and depth pixels ($p_{rgb_i}$, $p_{depth_i}$). After each object extraction, it is removed from the RGB-D observation through image and depth inpainting, and the process repeats until no foreground objects remain. This iterative removal facilitates the detection of occluded objects. The output of this stage consists of per-object RGB-D crops and masks.

#### 3.2. Generation

This stage converts the extracted object information into sim-ready assets.
1. **Mesh Generation:** For each object crop ($p_{rgb_i}$), an image upsampling model ($V_{image}$) prepares the input for a 2D-to-3D mesh model ($V_{mesh}$), such as Hunyuan2.1 or TRELLIS.2, which generates a visual mesh ($M_i$).
2. **Pose Estimation and Refinement:** The object's 6-DoF pose ($p_i$) is estimated and refined by aligning the generated mesh to the reconstructed scene. This involves using the scene RGB-D observation, object mask, and point cloud geometry, with further refinement from a pose estimation model ($V_{pose}$), such as FoundationPose.
3. **Articulated Object Processing:** For articulated objects (e.g., cabinets, drawers), a dedicated module is invoked. This module detects movable parts, segments the mesh using a mesh segmentation model ($V_{mesh_{seg}}$), and generates joint parameters (e.g., joint axes, placements, limits) using $V_{articulation}$ (e.g., Gemini-Pro-3) and prior articulation-generation methods. A critic VLM evaluates the realism of the generated movements.
4. **Physical Property Annotation:** Collision geometry is generated using CoACD. Physical properties like mass and friction are assigned to each object by querying $V_{scene}$.
5. **Scene Composition and Stabilization:** All generated, aligned, and annotated objects are then composed within a physics simulator (e.g., PyBullet). Object penetrations are resolved to achieve a stable configuration, and the final sim-ready scene is exported to downstream robotics simulators (e.g., IsaacLab).

#### 3.3. Augmentation (Digital Cousins)

Once a digital twin is reconstructed, SimFoundry expands it into diverse "digital cousins" by varying object instances, scene layouts, and task specifications.
1. **Object Cousins:** New object instances are generated that retain the affordances of the original object but vary in geometry, topology, and appearance. A VLM decomposes the object into functional components, proposes variations along geometry, topology, and visual dimensions, and an image generation model synthesizes new object images. A VLM then verifies their realism and scene compatibility.
2. **Scene Cousins:** The spatial arrangement of objects in the scene is varied using semantic spatial predicates (e.g., "OnTop," "RightOf"). This generates alternative, meaningful layouts (e.g., moving an object into a receptacle). Controllable distractor objects from a library can also be added.
3. **Task Cousins:** The reconstructed scene is used to propose additional feasible manipulation tasks grounded in the available objects and affordances. A VLM proposes tasks, which are then converted into simulation-compatible goal specifications, enabling procedural demonstration collection for multi-task data generation.

#### 3.4. Background Reconstruction and Alignment

To achieve photorealistic backgrounds, SimFoundry can fuse reconstructed objects with a 3D Gaussian Splat background. Two pipelines are supported:
- **Automatic Pipeline:** This reuses the same input video from the Extraction stage. It removes foreground objects via prompted video segmentation and two-pass inpainting, recovers metric depth and camera poses, trains a depth-supervised splat, and aligns it rigidly to the simulator world frame.
- **Manual Pipeline:** This requires a second video capture of the scene with foreground objects physically removed. A splat is trained on this background-only video and then interactively aligned to the reconstructed scene using a scene editor.

This comprehensive approach leverages multiple vision-language models (VLMs) and 3D generative models, ensuring modularity and automation throughout the scene generation process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides quantitative and qualitative evidence across several key areas, demonstrating the capabilities of SimFoundry in scene reconstruction, policy evaluation, and policy training.

#### 4.1. Scene Reconstruction Fidelity

- Under fully automated operation, SimFoundry achieved zero-shot F1 scores ranging from 0.81 to 0.92 across 12 diverse reconstruction scenes. This performance surpassed that of the state-of-the-art baseline, SAM3D, which yielded F1 scores between 0.66 and 0.71. SimFoundry also demonstrated lower Chamfer Distance and position error, indicating more precise scene geometry recovery.
- The fidelity of reconstruction could be further improved with minimal human intervention. With approximately 3 minutes of per-object operator tuning, F1 scores consistently rose to a range of 0.93 to 0.99. This indicates a trade-off between automation and precision that can be managed by users.
- The system successfully reconstructs scenes involving complex manipulation characteristics, including rigid and articulated objects, cluttered environments, and bimanual setups (e.g., cabinets, drawers).

#### 4.2. Real-to-Sim Policy Evaluation

- SimFoundry environments produced simulation evaluations that strongly correlated with real-world policy performance. Across 7 manipulation tasks and 5 different policy architectures (including pre-trained generalist policies like $\pi_0$, $\pi_{0.5}$, GR00T N1.6, GR00T N1.7, and DreamZero, as well as fine-tuned policies), a mean Pearson correlation coefficient of 0.911 and a mean maximum ranking violation (MMRV) of 0.018 were observed. This correlation significantly outperformed the state-of-the-art baseline, PolaRiS, by over 0.59 on Pearson correlation, in zero-shot evaluation without simulator-specific finetuning.
- The correlation held consistently across various policy types, including vision-language-action (VLA) families and world-action models.
- A sub-task evaluation procedure, where policies were evaluated on partial tasks starting from intermediate states, further improved policy evaluation correlations from a mean Pearson score of 0.90 to 0.95, particularly for long-horizon multi-step tasks. This procedure also provided insights into specific sub-task failure modes.

#### 4.3. Sim-to-Real Policy Training

- **Zero-Shot Transfer:** Policies trained exclusively on SimFoundry-generated simulation data transferred effectively to real-world scenarios on both DROID and YAM robot embodiments. High success rates were achieved, such as 99% for "Pot on Stove" with YAM and 100% for "Stack Dishware" with DROID, demonstrating the utility of SimFoundry for direct sim-to-real deployment.
- **Impact of Object Cousins:** Policies trained with data augmented by object cousins (affordance-preserving variations of original objects) showed an average task success rate improvement of 17% during zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. Specifically, a 50-point real-world gain was observed on held-out "Pot on Stove" objects, and up to 20-point gains on "Throw Away Trash" tasks, improving robustness to unseen object instances.
- **Impact of Scene Cousins:** The inclusion of scene cousins (variations in object spatial arrangements) improved generalization to novel layouts. This resulted in an average task success rate improvement of 21% in simulation. For instance, simulation success increased by up to 28 points for "Throw Away Trash" in the twin scene, and policies achieved 16% success on novel "Store Marker" cousin scenes where policies trained without scene cousins failed (0% success).
- **Impact of Task Cousins:** Training with task cousins (additional feasible manipulation tasks derived from the scene) yielded the largest average improvement of 40% in task success. This approach improved generalist policies by up to 31% in simulation and 18% in the real world. Policies also achieved a 29% success rate on held-out real tasks without task-specific demonstrations, indicating enhanced few-shot downstream learning and multi-task generalization.
- **Sim-and-Real Co-training:** Combining SimFoundry-generated data with limited real-world data further boosted policy performance. For example, the real-world success rate of $\pi_{0.5}$ on "Store Marker" increased from 60% to 92% with co-training, suggesting that SimFoundry reconstructions can effectively complement real demonstrations.

#### 4.4. System Scalability

- The SimFoundry pipeline demonstrated efficient reconstruction, with an average rate of approximately 5 minutes per object across diverse real-world scenes. This throughput indicates its potential for large-scale environment generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SimFoundry represents a significant advancement in the field of robot learning and simulation, offering a comprehensive and automated approach to scene generation for policy development and evaluation. Its potential impact spans several critical areas:

- **Accelerated Robot Policy Development:** By automating the creation of interactive, sim-ready digital twins from single videos, SimFoundry significantly reduces the manual labor and time traditionally required to set up simulation environments. This automation, combined with its ability to generate diverse training data through "digital cousins," can substantially accelerate the iteration and refinement cycles for robot policies. Researchers and developers can quickly test new algorithms and policy architectures in a scalable virtual environment, leading to faster progress in robotics.
- **Enhanced Policy Generalization and Robustness:** The introduction of object, scene, and task cousins is a key innovation for improving policy generalization. By exposing policies to a wide range of variations in object appearance, geometry, scene layouts, and related tasks during simulation training, SimFoundry enables the development of more robust policies that can transfer effectively to unseen objects and novel real-world conditions. This is crucial for deploying robots in dynamic and unstructured environments.
- **Reliable and Cost-Effective Policy Evaluation:** The demonstrated strong correlation between SimFoundry's simulation evaluations and real-world policy performance provides a reliable and cost-effective alternative to extensive physical testing. This capability allows for rigorous benchmarking of policies at scale, offering actionable insights for model development without incurring the high costs and logistical complexities of thousands of real-world trials. The ability to perform sub-task evaluations further refines this by pinpointing specific areas for improvement.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** SimFoundry explicitly addresses the sim-to-real transfer challenge by enabling zero-shot deployment of simulation-trained policies to the real world and demonstrating the benefits of sim-and-real co-training. This contributes to making simulation a more practical and impactful tool for real-world robotics.
- **Modular and Future-Proof Architecture:** The modular design of SimFoundry allows for the seamless integration of new and improved foundation models for perception, 3D generation, and physics annotation as they emerge. This adaptability ensures that the system can evolve with the state-of-the-art, maintaining its relevance and performance over time.
- **Advancing Complex Robotic Tasks:** SimFoundry's capability to handle complex manipulation tasks, including multi-step actions, articulated object interactions, and bimanual coordination, pushes the boundaries of what is achievable with automated real-to-sim systems. This opens up new avenues for developing policies for sophisticated robotic applications in diverse domains, from industrial automation to household assistance.

In summary, SimFoundry offers a unified, automated, and scalable framework that can significantly de-bottleneck robot learning. By providing tools for both efficient policy evaluation and robust policy training, it has the potential to accelerate the development and deployment of intelligent robots capable of operating effectively in the real world.
