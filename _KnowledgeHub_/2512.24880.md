# mHC: Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at DeepSeek-AI developed Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections (mHC), an architectural modification that stabilizes and enhances the performance of large language models using expanded residual connections. This approach incorporates manifold constraints and infrastructure optimizations to ensure stable signal propagation and achieve higher efficiency, leading to improved downstream task performance with a 6.7% training overhead.

## Problem

- Advanced deep learning architectures like Hyper-Connections (HC) show performance potential but suffer from severe training instability due to unconstrained learnable mappings.
- Unconstrained residual mappings in HC compromise the identity mapping property, leading to exploding or vanishing signals and gradients that hinder large-scale model training.
- Expanded residual streams, while powerful, introduce significant memory access overhead and reduce hardware efficiency, posing a 'memory wall' challenge.

## Method

- ==Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections (mHC)== project learnable residual mappings onto the ==Birkhoff polytope==, ensuring they are doubly stochastic matrices, which guarantees stable signal propagation.
- Non-negativity constraints are applied to pre- and post-mappings using Sigmoid functions to prevent signal cancellation within the expanded residual stream.
- Extensive infrastructure optimizations, including ==kernel fusion==, recomputing (gradient checkpointing), and overlapping communication, are integrated to ensure high hardware efficiency and minimize training overhead.

## Results

- mHC achieves significantly enhanced training stability with smoother loss convergence and stable gradient norms, resolving the instability observed in unconstrained Hyper-Connections.
- The approach consistently outperforms baseline models and unconstrained HC on **8** diverse downstream benchmarks, showing gains of **2.1%** on BBH and **2.3%** on DROP tasks.
- mHC demonstrates robust scalability across varying compute and token budgets, introducing only a **6.7%** additional training time overhead for an expansion rate of 4, proving its practical efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Constraining residual mappings to be doubly stochastic matrices effectively restores signal stability by bounding spectral norms and ensuring compositional closure, mitigating gradient explosion/vanishing.
> - The Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm can be effectively used to project learnable matrices onto the manifold of doubly stochastic matrices, facilitating robust and stable feature mixing.
> - Careful co-design of architecture and system infrastructure is crucial for scaling complex network topologies, allowing advanced designs to be practical for large language models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work on Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections (mHC) is situated at the intersection of deep neural network architectural design and large-scale model training stability. Its roots lie in the ubiquitous residual connection paradigm introduced by ResNets, which has been fundamental to the success and scalability of deep learning over the past decade, especially in Transformer architectures for large language models (LLMs).

The success of residual connections (e.g., $x_{l+1} = x_l + F(x_l, W_l)$) is largely attributed to their identity mapping property, which ensures stable signal propagation and efficient training in very deep networks. This property allows signals from shallower layers to pass directly to deeper layers without modification, preventing vanishing or exploding gradients.

Recent advancements in macro-architecture design have sought to enhance the capabilities of deep networks by increasing the complexity and width of the residual stream. This trend is exemplified by works like Hyper-Connections (HC), Residual Matrix Transformer (RMT), and MUDDFormer. These approaches expand the feature dimension of the residual stream ($n \times C$ instead of $C$) and introduce learnable mappings to diversify connectivity patterns and facilitate richer information exchange among parallel streams. While these methods have shown promise in improving performance and increasing topological complexity without necessarily increasing FLOPs, they introduce a critical challenge: they fundamentally compromise the identity mapping property.

Specifically, the unconstrained learnable mappings (e.g., $H_{res,l}$) in architectures like HC lead to a composite mapping across multiple layers that can amplify or attenuate signals uncontrollably. This results in severe training instability, characterized by exploding or vanishing gradients, and restricts the practical scalability of such models. Furthermore, the widened residual streams inherently incur significant memory access overhead (the "memory wall" problem), which is often overlooked but crucial for real-world training efficiency.

mHC directly addresses these limitations. It builds upon the performance potential demonstrated by HC but tackles its inherent instability and inefficiency. By introducing manifold constraints and rigorous infrastructure optimizations, mHC seeks to restore the crucial identity mapping property while retaining the benefits of expanded connectivity. This positions mHC as a vital contribution that enables the safe and efficient scaling of advanced, topologically complex neural network architectures, paving the way for more powerful and stable foundational models. It represents a significant step forward in balancing architectural expressivity with training stability and computational efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The development of Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections (mHC) is driven by a clear set of motivations stemming from the challenges encountered with contemporary advanced deep learning architectures, particularly Hyper-Connections (HC). These motivations directly inform the core objectives of the proposed mHC framework.

**Motivation:**

1. **Performance Potential of Expanded Residual Streams:** Research, particularly exemplified by Hyper-Connections (HC), has empirically demonstrated that expanding the width of the residual stream and diversifying connectivity patterns can yield substantial performance gains in deep networks. This indicates that moving beyond the simple additive residual connection holds significant promise for enhancing model capabilities.
2. **Compromised Identity Mapping in HC:** Despite its performance benefits, the unconstrained nature of learnable mappings in HC (e.g., $H_{res,l}$) fundamentally compromises the identity mapping property. When these connections are extended across many layers, this leads to an inability to preserve the global mean of features, resulting in unbounded signal amplification or attenuation (vanishing/exploding signals and gradients). This numerical instability severely hinders the training process, especially at larger scales.
3. **Restricted Scalability of HC:** The instability caused by the loss of identity mapping directly restricts the scalability of HC-like architectures. Models become difficult, if not impossible, to train reliably as depth and size increase. This limits their applicability in the context of ever-growing large language models.
4. **System-Level Overhead in HC:** While HC preserves computational efficiency in terms of FLOPs, it introduces a notable memory access overhead due to the widened residual stream. This "memory wall" problem, coupled with increased memory footprints for intermediate activations and communication costs in distributed training, significantly degrades hardware efficiency and training throughput.

### 3. Key Objectives

Based on these motivations, the authors established the following objectives for mHC:

1. **Restore Identity Mapping Property:** The primary objective is to re-establish a form of the identity mapping property within the expanded residual connection space of HC. This aims to ensure stable signal propagation and gradient flow across layers, mitigating the risk of vanishing or exploding signals without reverting to a trivial, non-communicative identity function.
2. **Enhance Training Stability and Scalability:** By restoring stability, mHC aims to enable robust and efficient training of large-scale models that leverage expanded residual streams, thereby overcoming the scalability limitations of previous approaches like HC.
3. **Incorporate Rigorous Infrastructure Optimization:** To address the practical system-level challenges, mHC aims to integrate advanced infrastructure optimizations (e.g., kernel fusion, recomputing, communication overlapping) to ensure high hardware efficiency and minimize the additional training overhead, making the approach practical for real-world large-scale deployment.
4. **Preserve Performance Gains of HC:** Crucially, mHC seeks to achieve stability and efficiency while retaining, or even enhancing, the performance advantages demonstrated by HC, such as improved expressive power and topological complexity.
5. **Develop a General and Flexible Framework:** The goal is to propose a general framework that can be easily extended and adapted, contributing to a deeper understanding of topological architecture design and suggesting future directions for foundational models.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The mHC framework addresses the identified challenges through a two-pronged approach: a novel architectural design incorporating manifold constraints and comprehensive system-level infrastructure optimizations.

#### 4.1. Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections (mHC) Architectural Design

The core innovation of mHC lies in constraining the learnable residual mapping $H_{res,l}$ within the Hyper-Connections (HC) framework.
- **The Problem:** In HC, the residual mapping $H_{res,l} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ is unconstrained, leading to numerical instability across layers as the composite product $\prod H_{res,i}$ can cause signal explosion or vanishing.
- **The Solution: Doubly Stochastic Constraint:** mHC proposes projecting $H_{res,l}$ onto the **Birkhoff polytope**, which is the manifold of **doubly stochastic matrices**. A matrix $M$ is doubly stochastic if all its entries are non-negative ($M \geq 0$), and all its rows and columns sum to 1. This constraint, denoted as $\mathcal{P}_{M_{res}}(H_{res,l})$, is applied to $H_{res,l}$.
- **Benefits of Doubly Stochastic Matrices:**
 - **Norm Preservation:** The spectral norm of a doubly stochastic matrix is bounded by 1 ($\|H_{res,l}\|_2 \leq 1$), making the mapping non-expansive and effectively mitigating gradient explosion.
 - **Compositional Closure:** The set of doubly stochastic matrices is closed under matrix multiplication. This guarantees that the composite residual mapping across multiple layers remains doubly stochastic, ensuring stability throughout the entire model depth.
 - **Geometric Interpretation:** The Birkhoff polytope is the convex hull of permutation matrices. This means the residual mapping acts as a convex combination of permutations, monotonically increasing information mixing across streams, which functions as a robust feature fusion mechanism.
- **Non-Negativity for Pre/Post Mappings:** Additionally, mHC imposes non-negativity constraints on the input mappings $H_{pre,l}$ and output mappings $H_{post,l}$ using the Sigmoid function. This prevents signal cancellation due to mixed positive/negative coefficients.

#### 4.2. Parameterization and Manifold Projection

The calculation of the constrained mappings involves several steps:
1. **Flattening and Normalization:** The input hidden matrix $x_l \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times C}$ is first flattened into a vector $\tilde{x}_l = \text{vec}(x_l) \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times nC}$ to capture full context. This flattened vector is then RMSNormalized: $\tilde{x}'_l = \text{RMSNorm}(\tilde{x}_l)$.
2. **Dynamic and Static Mappings (HC-like):** Similar to the original HC, intermediate "tilde" mappings ($\tilde{H}_{pre,l}, \tilde{H}_{post,l}, \tilde{H}_{res,l}$) are computed using learnable gating factors ($\alpha$), linear projections ($\varphi$), and biases ($b$).
 - $\tilde{H}_{pre,l} = \alpha_{pre,l} \cdot (\tilde{x}'_l \varphi_{pre,l}) + b_{pre,l}$
 - $\tilde{H}_{post,l} = \alpha_{post,l} \cdot (\tilde{x}'_l \varphi_{post,l}) + b_{post,l}$
 - $\tilde{H}_{res,l} = \alpha_{res,l} \cdot \text{mat}(\tilde{x}'_l \varphi_{res,l}) + b_{res,l}$ (where $\text{mat}(\cdot)$ reshapes to $n \times n$).
3. **Manifold Projection:**
 - $H_{pre,l} = \sigma(\tilde{H}_{pre,l})$ (Sigmoid for non-negativity).
 - $H_{post,l} = 2\sigma(\tilde{H}_{post,l})$ (Sigmoid, scaled by 2, for non-negativity).
 - $H_{res,l} = \text{Sinkhorn-Knopp}(\tilde{H}_{res,l})$. The Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm takes $\text{exp}(\tilde{H}_{res,l})$ as input (to ensure positivity) and iteratively normalizes rows and columns to sum to 1 until convergence (20 iterations in experiments).

#### 4.3. Efficient Infrastructure Design

To ensure mHC's practicality and efficiency, especially for large-scale training, the authors implemented rigorous infrastructure optimizations:

1. **Kernel Fusion:**
 - **RMSNorm Reordering:** The RMSNorm operation on the high-dimensional $\tilde{x}_l$ is reordered to occur after matrix multiplication, maintaining mathematical equivalence while improving efficiency.
 - **Mixed-Precision & Unified Kernels:** Multiple operations with shared memory access are fused into unified compute kernels (e.g., two scans on $\tilde{x}_l$ for $\tilde{H}_{pre,l}, \tilde{H}_{post,l}, \tilde{H}_{res,l}$). The backward pass also combines two matrix multiplications into a single kernel.
 - **Lightweight Operations Fusion:** Small coefficient operations (Sigmoid, scaling) are fused to reduce kernel launch overhead.
 - **Sinkhorn-Knopp Kernel:** The entire iterative Sinkhorn-Knopp process, including its custom backward pass (recomputing intermediate results on-chip), is implemented within a single kernel.
 - **Application Kernels:** Two additional kernels are used to apply the mappings: one for $F_{pre} = H_{pre,l}x_l$ and another for $F_{post,res} = H_{res,l}x_l + H_{post,l}^T F(\cdot, \cdot)$, which fuses $H_{post,l}$ and $H_{res,l}$ application with residual merging to reduce I/O.
 - **TileLang:** Most kernels are implemented using TileLang, a framework for efficient kernel development on AI systems.

2. **Recomputing (Gradient Checkpointing):**
 - To mitigate the substantial memory overhead from $n$-stream residual design, intermediate activations of mHC kernels are discarded after the forward pass and recomputed on-the-fly during the backward pass.
 - Only the input $x_{l_0}$ to the first layer of a block of $L_r$ consecutive layers is stored persistently.
 - The optimal block size $L_r^*$ is determined to minimize total memory footprint ($\approx \sqrt{\frac{nL}{n+2}}$), and recomputation boundaries are synchronized with pipeline stages in distributed training.

3. **Overlapping Communication in DualPipe:**
 - The DualPipe schedule, used for pipeline parallelism in large-scale training, is extended to improve communication-computation overlap.
 - **High-Priority Stream:** $F_{post,res}$ kernels of MLP layers are executed on a dedicated high-priority compute stream to prevent blocking the communication stream.
 - **Preemption:** Attention layers avoid persistent kernels to allow for preemption and flexible scheduling.
 - **Decoupled Recomputation:** Recomputation is decoupled from pipeline communication dependencies as initial activations are cached locally.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The extensive empirical evaluation of mHC demonstrates its effectiveness in improving training stability, performance, and scalability for large language models.

1. **Enhanced Training Stability:**
 - **Loss Convergence:** Compared to HC, mHC effectively mitigates the training instability, characterized by unexpected loss surges, observed in HC. For a 27B model, mHC achieves a final loss reduction of 0.021 compared to the baseline and exhibits significantly smoother convergence than HC (Figure 5a).
 - **Gradient Norm Stability:** mHC maintains a stable gradient norm profile, comparable to the baseline, which is significantly better than the fluctuating and unstable gradient norms observed in HC (Figure 5b). This validates the restoration of stable signal propagation.

2. **Superior Downstream Performance:**
 - mHC consistently outperforms the baseline and generally surpasses HC across a diverse set of 8 downstream benchmarks, including tasks requiring reasoning (BBH, DROP, GSM8K, MATH) and general knowledge (MMLU, PIQA, TriviaQA).
 - Notably, mHC shows specific improvements in reasoning capabilities, delivering performance gains of 2.1% on BBH and 2.3% on DROP compared to HC (Table 4), suggesting that the stable feature mixing fosters richer representations.

3. **Robust Scalability:**
 - **Compute Scaling:** mHC's performance advantage over the baseline is robustly maintained across varying computational budgets, from 3B to 9B and up to 27B parameters. The compute scaling curve shows only marginal attenuation of this advantage at higher compute levels (Figure 6a).
 - **Token Scaling:** The token scaling curve for a 3B model trained on 1 trillion tokens further validates mHC's effectiveness over extended training durations, indicating that the benefits hold as models consume more data (Figure 6b).
 - These results collectively confirm mHC's exceptional stability and scalability in large-scale scenarios.

4. **Mechanistic Proof of Propagation Stability:**
 - **Amax Gain Magnitude Reduction:** A critical finding is the dramatic reduction in the "Amax Gain Magnitude" for both single-layer and composite residual mappings. While HC exhibited extreme values up to 3000, confirming exploding residual streams, mHC reduces this by three orders of magnitude, reaching a maximum of approximately 1.6 (Figure 7). This directly demonstrates that the doubly stochastic constraint effectively ensures stable forward signal and backward gradient flows.
 - **Visualization of Mappings:** Visualizations of learnable matrices show that mHC consistently yields stable matrices with row and column sums close to 1, indicative of controlled signal propagation, whereas HC produces highly volatile and unbounded matrices (Figure 8).

5. **High Efficiency:**
 - Despite the added architectural and algorithmic complexity, the rigorous infrastructure optimizations (kernel fusion, recomputing, overlapping communication) ensure that mHC (with expansion rate $n=4$) introduces only a **6.7% additional time overhead** during large-scale training. This makes the approach highly practical for real-world LLM development.

In summary, the main findings highlight that mHC successfully addresses the instability and scalability issues of expanded residual connections, not only restoring stability but also enhancing performance and doing so with minimal computational overhead.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections (mHC) carries significant implications for the future of deep learning architecture and the development of foundational models. Its significance can be understood in several key areas:

1. **Enabling Scalability for Advanced Architectures:** The most immediate and critical impact of mHC is its ability to make advanced, topologically complex residual connection paradigms (like Hyper-Connections) practical and scalable for large-scale training. By resolving the inherent numerical instability and mitigating system-level overheads, mHC unlocks the potential of wider residual streams, allowing researchers to explore richer information exchange patterns across layers without fear of training collapse. This directly contributes to the development of more powerful and robust large language models and other foundational models.

2. **Rejuvenating Macro-Architecture Design:** For over a decade, the residual connection has largely maintained its original form. mHC’s success in safely expanding and diversifying this fundamental component "rejuvenates community interest in macro-architecture design." It provides a concrete example that significant gains can still be achieved by rethinking the global topology of networks, beyond just optimizing individual computational blocks (micro-design). This could inspire a new wave of research into novel ways to connect layers and propagate information.

3. **Theoretical and Practical Framework for Stability:** The paper provides both a theoretical foundation (doubly stochastic matrices, Birkhoff polytope, norm preservation, compositional closure) and practical implementation details (Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm, infrastructure optimizations) for ensuring stability in complex deep networks. This dual approach offers a valuable blueprint for future architectural innovations that must balance expressivity with training robustness.

4. **Opening New Avenues for Manifold-Constrained Learning:** While mHC specifically uses doubly stochastic matrices, the authors explicitly state that the framework "accommodates the exploration of diverse manifold constraints tailored to specific learning objectives." This suggests a promising research direction where different geometric constraints could be applied to various architectural components to optimize trade-offs between plasticity (the ability to learn) and stability (the ability to train reliably). This could lead to specialized architectures for different tasks or data modalities.

5. **Deeper Understanding of Information Flow:** By systematically analyzing and demonstrating how unconstrained connections lead to "unbounded signal amplification or attenuation" and how manifold constraints restore "conservation of signal energy," mHC contributes to a deeper understanding of information flow within deep networks. This mechanistic insight is crucial for guiding future architectural designs that are not just empirically effective but also theoretically sound.

6. **Direct Impact on DeepSeek-AI Models and Beyond:** Given the authors' affiliation with DeepSeek-AI and the extensive use of their DeepSeek-V3 architecture as a basis for experiments, mHC is highly likely to be integrated into future generations of DeepSeek's LLMs. Its demonstrated efficiency (only 6.7% overhead) makes it a very attractive and practical upgrade. Beyond DeepSeek, the findings and techniques presented in mHC could be adopted by other organizations and researchers working on large-scale AI models, fostering broader improvements in the field.

In conclusion, mHC is a significant contribution that addresses a critical bottleneck in the evolution of deep learning architectures. By elegantly combining theoretical rigor with practical engineering, it enables the continued scaling of models that leverage expanded residual connections, thereby paving the way for more stable, performant, and potentially novel foundational AI systems.
