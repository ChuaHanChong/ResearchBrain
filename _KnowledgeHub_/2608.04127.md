# Teaching Foundation Models to Read mmWave: Pose-Guided Kinematic Representation for Human Behavior Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University, ETH Zurich, and Institut Polytechnique de Paris developed mmMind, a framework enabling large language models to interpret human behavior from privacy-preserving millimeter-wave (mmWave) radar data. mmMind generates kinematically grounded, continuous representations from sparse radar sequences, and, evaluated on a new large-scale real-world mmWave-language benchmark, it achieved substantial improvements across behavior captioning, spatio-temporal question answering, and generalization to unseen actions.

## Problem

- LLM agents struggle to perceive and understand human behavior in physical environments due to privacy concerns with cameras and compliance issues with wearables.
- Millimeter-wave (mmWave) radar data is sparse, noisy, and not directly interpretable by LLMs, often lacking explicit encoding of 3D body structure, global displacement, or local joint dynamics.
- Existing radar-language approaches often rely on synthetic data or simpler representations, failing to capture real-world complexities and limiting robust, fine-grained behavior understanding.

## Method

- Introduced **mmMind**, a ==three-stage training procedure== integrating a novel ==pose-guided kinematic tokenization== with a large language model (LLM).
- Developed a ==spatio-temporal radar encoder== pretrained with synchronized 3D human pose data to generate continuous, kinematically grounded tokens from radar sequences.
- Created **mmMind-Bench**, the first large-scale, real-world mmWave-language dataset with diverse environments, participants, and behaviors, annotated for captions, spatio-temporal QA, and multi-turn dialogues.

## Results

- mmMind achieved a METEOR score of **46.5** and BERTScore of **86.8** for radar captioning, improving over strongest baselines by **10.3** and **11.6** points, respectively.
- Demonstrated **89.2%** accuracy in single-turn question answering and **84.7%** in multi-turn dialogue, representing improvements of **14.6** and **23.3** points over baselines.
- Achieved **91.2%** accuracy on unseen-action recognition, outperforming the mmCLIP baseline by **11.5** points and indicating enhanced generalization through kinematically grounded tokens.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit pose-guided kinematic pretraining is crucial for organizing sparse mmWave radar observations into effective behavioral representations, significantly outperforming language supervision alone.
> - Continuous kinematic tokens from radar data enable more fine-grained behavior understanding and better generalization to unseen actions compared to discrete or textualized representations.
> - A large-scale, real-world mmWave-language dataset is essential for robustly aligning radar representations with natural language and enabling multitask instruction tuning for LLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the increasing need for large language model (LLM) agents to perceive and understand human behavior in physical environments. While LLMs are becoming adept at grounding responses in multimodal contexts such as text, images, audio, and video, continuous perception of human behavior presents specific challenges. Traditional sensing modalities, such as cameras, raise privacy concerns in sensitive indoor environments like bedrooms or bathrooms. Wearable devices, another common approach, depend on consistent user compliance, which may not always be practical for continuous monitoring.

Millimeter-wave (mmWave) radar technology has emerged as a privacy-friendly, contactless, and cost-effective alternative for sensing human activity. It operates by analyzing radio reflections, thereby avoiding the capture of recognizable visual imagery. mmWave radar has been utilized in applications like activity recognition, 3D pose estimation, and physiological monitoring.

However, mmWave radar data, typically in the form of sparse, noisy, and temporally irregular point clouds, is not directly interpretable by LLMs. Semantic understanding of behavior from such data relies on detecting subtle changes in body configuration and motion over time. Existing radar-language approaches have attempted to bridge this gap, for instance, by aligning 2D micro-Doppler spectra with language (e.g., mmExpert) or by quantizing temporal point clouds into discrete motion codes (e.g., RadarLLM). While these methods establish interfaces, they often do not explicitly encourage the latent space to preserve fine-grained human body structure, global displacement, or local joint dynamics. Additionally, a significant portion of research in this area has relied on synthetic data, which may not fully capture the complexities and noise inherent in real-world mmWave deployments. General point-cloud LLMs, designed for dense and static objects, are also not directly suited for the dynamic and sparse nature of human radar reflections.

This research aims to advance the integration of mmWave radar with LLMs by developing a method that generates kinematically grounded, continuous representations from sparse radar sequences. It also introduces a large-scale real-world dataset to facilitate robust radar-language alignment, thereby addressing limitations related to both representation learning and data realism in the existing landscape.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable large language model agents to "read" and understand human behavior from millimeter-wave (mmWave) radar data. This overarching goal is driven by two main challenges identified in the existing research landscape:

**Challenge 1: Preserving Human Kinematics in Radar Representations**
- **Motivation:** Existing mmWave-based behavior understanding methods often map an entire radar sequence to a general activity label, providing limited detail on how a behavior unfolds. Recent radar-language models use interfaces such as 2D micro-Doppler spectra or discrete motion codes. While useful, these representations may sacrifice explicit information about the 3D body structure, global movement, and local joint dynamics that are crucial for comprehensive behavior understanding. Generic point-cloud LLMs are primarily designed for dense, static objects, making them less suitable for the sparse, dynamic nature of human radar reflections. The paper posits that for an LLM to accurately interpret human behavior, its underlying radar representations must explicitly encode the nuances of body configuration and motion.
- **Objective:** To develop a radar tokenization method that learns kinematically grounded, continuous representations. This involves pretraining a spatio-temporal radar encoder using synchronized 3D human pose data as supervision. The intent is to shape the encoder's latent space to inherently preserve human body structure and its temporal evolution, even when pose information is not available during inference.

**Challenge 2: Real-World Radar-Language Alignment under Realistic Sensing Conditions**
- **Motivation:** Establishing a robust alignment between radar representations and natural language requires extensive paired data with fine-grained behavioral annotations. Prior research has frequently relied on synthetic data, which offers scalability but often fails to replicate the full spectrum of real-world sensing complexities, including sparsity, multipath interference, background clutter, and hardware-specific noise. Without large-scale, real-world annotated data, achieving robust and generalizable radar-language alignment and instruction tuning for LLMs remains a significant hurdle.
- **Objective:** To introduce a large-scale, real-world mmWave-language dataset and benchmark, named mmMind-Bench. This benchmark is designed to provide diverse, annotated data collected across various environments and participants. The goal is to enable the training and evaluation of radar-language models on tasks that require fine-grained behavior understanding, including behavior captioning, spatio-temporal question answering, and generalization to unseen actions, under conditions representative of actual deployments.

By addressing these two challenges, the research aims to enable LLMs to provide timely, behavior-aware assistance in physical environments, leveraging the privacy-preserving and contactless benefits of mmWave radar. The ultimate objective is to develop a mmWave-enabled foundation model capable of multi-turn reasoning and interaction for human behavior understanding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system, mmMind, employs a three-stage training procedure to enable foundation models to interpret mmWave data for human behavior understanding. This system integrates a novel pose-guided kinematic tokenization with a large language model (LLM) and is trained on a newly introduced real-world benchmark, mmMind-Bench.

#### 3.1. mmMind-Bench: Real-World mmWave-Language Dataset and Benchmark

mmMind-Bench is a large-scale real-world dataset comprising 17.9 hours of synchronized mmWave recordings, RGB-D videos, and 3D pose ground truth, collected from 23 participants across seven diverse indoor environments. It covers 27 daily-life, exercise, and fall-related behaviors.

- **Radar Point Cloud Generation:** Raw mmWave measurements are processed into 4D point clouds (x, y, z, v). To enhance quality, the methodology employs:
 - **Density Enhancement with ETCM-CFAR:** Extended Target Clutter Map CFAR is used to recover weaker human reflections, particularly from limbs, by dynamically estimating noise levels from each Range-Doppler bin's temporal history, rather than from spatially adjacent cells, preventing strong torso reflections from masking weaker ones.
 - **Multipath Suppression via AoA–AoD Consistency:** Multipath-induced ghost points, common in indoor environments, are suppressed by checking the consistency between the Angle of Arrival (AoA) at the receiver array and the Angle of Departure (AoD) from the virtual transmitter array. Direct reflections exhibit consistent angles, while multi-bounce paths do not.
- **Radar-Grounded Language Annotation:** Each continuous recording is segmented into 5-second clips (75 radar frames). A human-seeded, pose-aware VLM pipeline (using InternVL3 with synchronized RGB-D clips and rule-based JSON summaries of 3D poses) drafts captions, QA pairs, and dialogues. Annotations are restricted to radar-observable behavior, excluding visual-only information, and are manually reviewed for factual correctness, kinematic consistency, and quality. Each clip includes bilingual captions, 13 single-turn QA pairs, and four multi-turn dialogues covering various aspects of behavior.
- **Benchmark Tasks and Splits:** mmMind-Bench evaluates three capabilities: radar captioning, spatio-temporal question answering (single-turn and multi-turn), and unseen-action recognition. The dataset is divided into participant-, environment-, and session-disjoint train, validation, and test sets (approximately 80%/10%/10%). For unseen-action evaluation, specific behavior categories are entirely excluded from all training stages.

#### 3.2. mmMind Model Architecture and Training

mmMind uses Qwen3-8B as its language backbone. Its training proceeds in three stages:

- **Stage 1: Pose-Guided Kinematic Tokenization (Radar Encoder Pretraining)**
 - **Input Normalization and Augmentation:** Each radar frame is standardized to 150 points by subsampling or zero-padding. Coordinates are normalized, and radial velocity is clipped. Synchronized scaling, yaw rotation, horizontal translation, and coordinate jitter are applied to radar and pose sequences during training.
 - **Spatio-Temporal Radar Encoder:** Each 4D point (x, y, z, v) is projected into a 256-dimensional feature. Spatial positional embeddings are added. A spatial Transformer models intra-frame point relationships, aggregated into a single 256-dimensional representation (h_t) via learnable-query pooling. A temporal Transformer with rotary position embeddings then integrates information across frames, producing a sequence of continuous kinematic tokens (Y = {y1..., yT}).
 - **Pose-Guided Pretraining Objective:** A lightweight pose head maps each radar token (y_t) to the 3D coordinates of 17 body joints (b_S_t). The radar encoder is optimized with a kinematic loss (L_kin) comprising four terms: joint position (joint-weighted L1, emphasizing distal joints), global root motion velocity, local joint articulation velocity, and skeletal consistency (bone length preservation). After pretraining, the pose head is removed, and the encoder produces continuous kinematic tokens from radar data alone.

- **Stage 2: Continuous Radar-Language Alignment (Projector Training)**
 - **Cross-Modal Projector:** A two-layer linear projector (g_ϕ) maps the 256-dimensional continuous kinematic tokens from the radar encoder into the 4096-dimensional embedding space of the LLM. Each projected token replaces a frame-indexed placeholder in the input sequence, preserving temporal correspondence.
 - **Alignment Training:** With the radar encoder and LLM frozen, only the projector is optimized. Training uses caption-only supervision from mmMind-Bench, employing an autoregressive language-modeling objective. This stage establishes a stable correspondence between radar kinematics and basic behavioral language.

- **Stage 3: Multitask Instruction Tuning (Projector + LLM LoRA)**
 - **Multitask Optimization:** The model is instruction-tuned on behavior captioning, spatio-temporal question answering, and multi-turn dialogues. Kinematic rationales are provided for reasoning-oriented QA.
 - **LoRA Adapters:** The radar encoder remains frozen. The projector, initialized from Stage II, continues to be optimized, and Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) adapters are applied to the LLM (Qwen3-8B) to adapt it for language-based behavior understanding.
 - **Objective:** The masked autoregressive objective (L_SFT) is used, with the loss computed only over assistant-response tokens across all turns in multi-turn dialogues.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

All trainable baselines, including general-purpose LLM/VLMs (Qwen3-8B, mmWave-QA-style, Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct) and radar-specific models (mmExpert, RadarLLM, mmCLIP), are reimplemented and retrained on mmMind-Bench. Metrics include METEOR and BERTScore for captioning, accuracy for QA (single-turn, multi-turn, unseen-action), with canonical answer sets and normalization for QA. No RGB-D video or 3D pose is used during inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the mmMind-Bench benchmark demonstrated the performance of mmMind across various human behavior understanding tasks.

- **Overall Performance:** mmMind consistently demonstrated improved performance compared to existing radar-language baselines across all evaluated tasks, including captioning, spatio-temporal question answering, and unseen-action generalization. Text-only baselines and models relying on radar textualization or rendered point-cloud videos exhibited lower performance, indicating the value of direct radar data interpretation and kinematically grounded representations.

- **Radar Captioning:** mmMind achieved a METEOR score of 46.5 and a BERTScore of 86.8. This represents an improvement of 10.3 and 11.6 points, respectively, over the strongest baselines in these metrics. These results suggest that mmMind's continuous kinematic tokens preserve body configuration and motion evolution more effectively for language generation compared to textualized descriptions, rendered videos, micro-Doppler spectra, or discrete radar tokens.

- **Spatio-Temporal Question Answering:** In single-turn question answering, mmMind achieved 89.2% accuracy, which is an improvement of 14.6 points over the strongest baseline. For multi-turn dialogue, mmMind reached 84.7% accuracy, exceeding the Qwen3-VL-32B baseline by 23.3 points. This indicates that the learned radar tokens support both direct inquiries about observed motion and complex, context-dependent follow-up questions within a dialogue sequence.

- **Unseen-Action Recognition:** On action categories that were entirely excluded from both radar pretraining and instruction tuning, mmMind achieved 91.2% accuracy. This performance is an improvement of 11.5 points over the contrastive mmCLIP baseline. The consistent improvement suggests that kinematically grounded continuous tokens offer greater transferability to behaviors not explicitly observed during training.

- **Qualitative Results:** Qualitative examples illustrated that mmMind provided more detailed descriptions of body states and motion evolution compared to RadarLLM and mmExpert. For instance, mmMind could describe the nuanced transition from an upright stance to a seated posture, including gradual body lowering and knee flexion, and maintain consistent responses across dialogue turns. It also demonstrated the ability to distinguish subtle differences between actions, such as jumping jacks versus arm stretching, by grounding its explanations in specific, coordinated body movements.

- **Ablation Study:** The ablation study provided insights into the contributions of mmMind's key components:
 - **Removal of Pose-Guided Pretraining:** This caused the most significant degradation in performance, reducing captioning (BERTScore), single-turn QA, multi-turn QA, and unseen-action accuracy by 17.0, 20.6, 20.8, and 21.8 points, respectively. This outcome indicates that language supervision alone is insufficient for organizing sparse radar observations into effective behavioral representations without explicit kinematic guidance.
 - **Removal of Auxiliary Kinematic Losses:** Excluding global velocity, local joint velocity, and bone-length constraints resulted in smaller but consistent drops of 2.7–5.6 points across tasks. This suggests these auxiliary losses contribute to preserving motion dynamics and skeletal structure beyond just joint positions.
 - **Removal of Temporal Encoder:** This led to a 10.9-point decrease in multi-turn QA accuracy and an 18.1-point decrease in unseen-action accuracy, underscoring the importance of cross-frame modeling for temporal reasoning and generalization.
 - **Replacement with VQ Tokens:** Substituting continuous representations with discrete VQ tokens resulted in performance reductions of 9.3–11.2 points across tasks, supporting the advantage of avoiding early quantization for encoding fine-grained kinematic variations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant contributions and holds potential impact in the fields of human-computer interaction, pervasive sensing, and artificial intelligence.

- **Redefinition of Radar Tokenization:** The introduction of pose-guided kinematic representation learning redefines how mmWave radar observations are processed for language models. By using synchronized 3D pose as training-only supervision, mmMind enables the radar encoder to produce continuous kinematic tokens that explicitly preserve human body structure and motion dynamics. This approach addresses a fundamental challenge in translating raw, sparse radar data into semantically rich representations, moving beyond coarse classifications or limited 2D representations.

- **Introduction of mmMind-Bench:** The creation of mmMind-Bench provides the first large-scale, real-world mmWave-language dataset and benchmark. This resource fills a critical gap in the research landscape, as previous works often relied on synthetic data, which cannot fully capture the complexities of real-world radar sensing (e.g., sparsity, multipath, clutter, hardware noise). mmMind-Bench, with its diverse environments, participants, behaviors, and detailed bilingual language annotations (captions, spatio-temporal QA, multi-turn dialogues, synchronized 3D poses), offers a robust platform for developing, training, and evaluating mmWave-enabled behavior understanding models. Its release will facilitate future research and enable standardized comparisons.

- **Development of an mmWave-Enabled Foundation Model:** mmMind represents a step towards mmWave-enabled foundation models capable of multi-turn reasoning and interaction for human behavior understanding. By effectively aligning kinematically grounded radar tokens with an LLM, the system demonstrates the ability to generate fine-grained behavior descriptions, answer complex spatio-temporal questions, and generalize to unseen actions. This expands the perceptual capabilities of LLM agents into the physical environment using a privacy-preserving modality.

- **Enabling Privacy-Friendly Human-Centered Agents:** The work highlights the potential of mmWave sensing as a privacy-friendly and contactless perceptual interface. Unlike camera-based systems, mmWave radar does not capture identifiable imagery, making it suitable for deployment in privacy-sensitive spaces such as homes, healthcare facilities, or elder care environments. This capability can contribute to the development of human-centered AI agents that provide timely, behavior-aware assistance without compromising user privacy.

- **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The demonstrated performance on unseen actions indicates that the pose-guided kinematic representations foster better generalization. This is crucial for real-world deployments where models must adapt to novel behaviors or variations not explicitly encountered during training. The robustness achieved through real-world data and structured representation learning suggests a move towards more deployable and reliable systems.

- **Future Research Directions:** The paper also outlines limitations, such as challenges in estimating precise numerical quantities (speed, angle, repetition count) and handling multi-person scenarios. These limitations point to future research avenues, including the exploration of physics-aware reinforcement learning for quantitative accuracy and the development of end-to-end multi-person radar point-cloud understanding. These directions could further enhance the capabilities and applicability of mmWave-based human behavior understanding systems.
