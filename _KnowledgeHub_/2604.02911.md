# Learning Task-Invariant Properties via Dreamer: Enabling Efficient Policy Transfer for Quadruped Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shenzhen University developed DreamTIP, a framework for quadruped robots that learns task-invariant properties using Large Language Models (LLMs) and enables efficient sim-to-real policy transfer. The method achieved an average 28.1% performance improvement in simulation tasks and demonstrated 100% success rates in several challenging real-world locomotion scenarios.

## Problem

- Policies trained in simulation for quadruped robots often suffer performance degradation due to the sim-to-real gap when deployed in complex real-world environments.
- Existing sim-to-real transfer methods often yield policies overly dependent on specific simulated dynamic parameters, limiting their robustness to real-world dynamic variations.
- Manually designing effective intermediate features or "Task-Invariant Properties" for robust transfer is costly, expert-intensive, and prone to bias.

## Method

- The **DreamTIP** framework extends the ==Dreamer world model== by incorporating a predictor for ==Task-Invariant Properties== (TIPs) into its latent space learning.
- LLMs are utilized to automatically identify and extract these TIPs (e.g., base angle, contact stability, terrain clearance) from high-level task descriptions and ==privileged observations==.
- An efficient ==real-world adaptation strategy== employs a ==mixed replay buffer==, a frozen recurrent model, and ==cosine similarity loss regularization== to stabilize fine-tuning with minimal real-world data, preventing catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

- **DreamTIP** achieved an average performance improvement of **28.1%** across eight simulated transfer tasks compared to state-of-the-art baselines, exhibiting superior robustness as task difficulty increased.
- In real-world tests on a Unitree Go2 robot, **DreamTIP** demonstrated stronger transfer capabilities, achieving **100%** success rates in a 52 cm Climb task and 16 cm Stair traversal, significantly outperforming a WMP baseline's **10%** and **100%** respectively.
- The method proved robust to dynamic shifts (e.g., with a 2 kg counterweight) and required as few as **5** real-world trajectories for effective and stable adaptation, balancing performance and data efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly learning task-invariant properties within a world model architecture significantly enhances a policy's generalization and robustness against dynamic variations, reducing reliance on specific simulation parameters.
> - Large Language Models can serve as effective tools for automatically abstracting complex task descriptions and state information into fundamental, transferable physical and behavioral properties, bypassing manual feature engineering.
> - A stable adaptation strategy that combines limited real-world data with regularization and selective model freezing can effectively calibrate pre-trained world models to real dynamics without degrading learned representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a central challenge in embodied intelligence: enabling robots, specifically quadruped robots, to autonomously operate in complex real-world environments. A primary hurdle is the sim-to-real transfer gap, where policies trained in simulation often exhibit performance degradation when deployed in reality due to discrepancies in dynamics, sensor noise, and visual characteristics.

Existing sim-to-real transfer methods generally fall into three categories:
- **Domain Randomization:** Aims to improve robustness by training with varied simulation parameters. Its limitation lies in the difficulty of predefining distributions that fully encompass real-world complexity, which can restrict generalization.
- **Simulator Enhancement:** Focuses on creating high-fidelity simulations to reduce the reality gap. This approach requires accurate modeling, is costly, and struggles with complex, real-world dynamics.
- **Domain Adaptation:** Seeks to align feature distributions between simulation and reality, often facing issues such as training instability and high computational costs. Some recent domain adaptation methods leverage world models to extract dynamic representations or utilize privileged information as auxiliary supervision to enhance generalization. Examples include World Model Perception (WMP) and Denoised World Model (DWL), which aim to learn domain-invariant features.

Despite these efforts, policies developed with existing methods often remain overly dependent on specific dynamic parameters configured in the simulation. This dependency results in performance fragility when encountering variations in real-world dynamics. Additionally, defining effective intermediate features or properties that are truly invariant across tasks and robust to dynamic changes has been a challenge, often requiring manual, expert-intensive, and potentially biased design.

This paper positions itself within the domain adaptation and world model-based learning paradigms by proposing a framework, DreamTIP, that explicitly targets learning "Task-Invariant Properties" within a Dreamer-based world model. It innovates by utilizing Large Language Models (LLMs) to automatically identify and extract these properties, thereby addressing the limitations of manual design. Furthermore, it introduces an efficient adaptation strategy to fine-tune the world model with minimal real-world data, mitigating issues like representation collapse and catastrophic forgetting often encountered during fine-tuning with limited samples. By focusing on task-invariant properties and robust adaptation, the work aims to enhance policy generalization and bridge the sim-to-real gap more effectively than prior approaches, particularly for complex quadruped locomotion tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this research are:

1. **Reduce Policy Dependence on Specific Dynamic Parameters:** Current sim-to-real transfer policies often become fragile when faced with real-world dynamic variations because they implicitly learn to rely on specific dynamic configurations present during simulation training. The motivation is to develop a learning framework that enables policies to acquire representations less sensitive to underlying dynamic changes, thereby improving robustness and transferability.

2. **Enhance Generalization and Adaptation Capabilities in Diverse Environments:** Quadruped robots need to operate across various and dynamic terrains, which requires policies that can generalize to unseen environments and adapt efficiently to novel conditions. The research aims to improve the policy's capacity to maintain performance even when encountering environmental variations not explicitly covered during training.

3. **Facilitate Efficient Sim-to-Real Transfer with Minimal Real-World Data:** Real-world training for robots is characterized by high costs, safety risks, and low data efficiency. While simulation offers a scalable alternative, the reality gap necessitates fine-tuning with real data. A key objective is to enable effective policy transfer with only a small number of real-world samples for adaptation, reducing the resource demands of deployment.

4. **Automate the Identification of Task-Invariant Properties:** Manually designing task-specific intermediate features or "Task-Invariant Properties" is costly, requires deep domain expertise, and can introduce biases, limiting cross-task generalization. The motivation is to leverage advanced AI tools, specifically Large Language Models (LLMs), to automatically reason about task descriptions and state observations to identify fundamental physical and behavioral principles that are robust to dynamic changes and transferable across tasks. Examples of such properties include contact-related stability and terrain clearance for legged robots.

5. **Mitigate Representation Collapse and Catastrophic Forgetting During Adaptation:** When fine-tuning models with limited real-world data, there is a risk of disrupting previously learned representations or causing the model to forget knowledge acquired during extensive simulation training. The objective is to design a stable adaptation strategy that preserves the quality of latent representations and prevents issues like representation collapse and catastrophic forgetting, ensuring that adaptation effectively calibrates the model to real-world dynamics without detrimental side effects.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, DreamTIP (Learning Task-Invariant Properties via Dreamer), addresses the sim-to-real transfer challenge through a two-stage approach: Task-Invariant Properties driven Dreamer learning in simulation, followed by efficient real-world adaptation.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Base Architecture

The locomotion learning problem is framed as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). The core learning architecture builds upon the Dreamer framework, which utilizes a latent dynamics model to learn abstract representations of environmental dynamics. Dreamer's key component is the Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM), comprising a Recurrent model, Encoder, Dynamic predictor, and Decoder. The model is trained by minimizing a negative Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) loss. The policy (Actor-Critic network) subsequently uses Dreamer's hidden state and current proprioceptive observations as inputs to maximize cumulative returns, inspired by WMP.

#### 3.2. Stage I: Task-Invariant Properties Driven Dreamer Learning

1. **Task-Invariant Properties (TIP) Extraction:**
 - **LLM Guidance:** To overcome the limitations of manual feature design, Large Language Models (LLMs) are employed to create a TIP Extractor. The LLM receives a high-level task description ($I_{text}$) and the complete state observation space ($I_{priv}$) (containing privileged information) as input. It then reasons and outputs a transformation function, $TIP_{extrator}$, which extracts Task-Invariant Properties ($f_t$) from raw privileged observations ($s_t$). These properties are designed to be generalizable across tasks and insensitive to dynamic variations.
 - **Example TIPs:** For quadruped locomotion, LLMs identified properties such as base angle (pitch & roll from projected gravity), contact-related stability (e.g., mean and variance of contact flags/forces), and terrain clearance (estimated using root height and depth image).

2. **DreamTIP Architecture:**
 - DreamTIP extends the original Dreamer framework by incorporating an additional predictor. This predictor, implemented as a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), takes the concatenation of DreamTIP’s recurrent state ($h_t$) and stochastic state representation ($z_t$) at time step $t$ as input.
 - Its output, $\hat{f}_t$, is an estimate of the current Task-Invariant Properties.
 - The overall training loss for DreamTIP is modified to include an additional term, $L_{MLE}$, which is the negative log-likelihood of predicting the actual Task-Invariant Properties, $f_t$, given the latent states. This encourages the world model to learn representations that are robust to dynamic disturbances and generalizable across tasks.

#### 3.3. Stage II: Real-World Efficient Adaptation

1. **Mixed Replay Buffer:**
 - During simulation training of DreamTIP, a simulated experience replay buffer (Sim buffer) is constructed.
 - For real-world adaptation, the pre-trained DreamTIP model parameters are duplicated and frozen to create a reference model ($M_{sg}$). This $M_{sg}$ and a frozen policy $\pi$ are deployed on the robot.
 - Real trajectory data collected from limited robot interactions is incrementally merged into the original Sim buffer, forming a Mixed Replay Buffer (Mix buffer). This hybrid buffer helps balance data distributions, preventing over-optimization on limited real samples, and mitigating catastrophic forgetting or representation collapse.

2. **Regularization Constraints-based Stable Adaptation:**
 - **Frozen Recurrent Model:** To accelerate adaptation to real dynamics with minimal real-world data, the Recurrent Model module within DreamTIP is frozen during the adaptation update process. This focuses the adaptation on other parts of the world model.
 - **Reference Model Supervision:** The pre-trained and frozen DreamTIP model ($M_{sg}$) acts as a reference. At each timestep $t$, both the adaptable model ($M$) and the reference model ($M_{sg}$) encode observations ($o_t$) into stochastic state representations ($z_t$ and $z_{sg_t}$, respectively).
 - **Cosine Similarity Loss:** A negative cosine similarity loss ( $L_{COS}$ ) is minimized to align the directions of $z_t$ and $z_{sg_t}$. This regularization term enforces semantic consistency between the adapting model's latent space and the pre-trained model's latent space, making the adaptation process more stable and preserving the quality of learned representations. This approach is insensitive to variations in feature scale.
 - The complete adaptation loss function incorporates both the original Dreamer loss ($L_D$) and the cosine similarity regularization term ($L_{COS}$). During adaptation, the policy network $\pi$ remains frozen.

#### 3.4. Experiment Settings

- **Environments:** Experiments were conducted in both Isaac Gym simulation (Unitree Go2 robot) and real-world environments (Unitree Go2 robot with D435i depth camera).
- **Observation Space:** Includes proprioceptive data (base angular velocity, gravity projection, joint positions/velocities) and depth images.
- **Privileged Information:** Includes linear velocity, elevation maps, friction coefficients, center of mass (CoM) position, and foot contact forces.
- **Reward Function:** Similar to existing methods, encouraging commanded velocities and penalizing undesirable robot states (joint torques, accelerations, collisions, joint deviations).
- **Tasks:** Eight simulation transfer tasks (Stair, Gap, Climb, Crawl, Tilt with varying difficulties, CoM Transfer, Velocity Transfer, and a Compound Task combining challenges). Real-world evaluation included Stair (16cm), Climb (52cm), Tilt (33cm), and Crawl (25cm) with a counterweight to test robustness.
- **Baselines:** WMP, DreamTIP-DWL, WMP w/ Finetune, Ours w/o TIP, and Ours w/o Adapt were compared against the full DreamTIP method (Ours).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that DreamTIP enhances policy generalization and enables efficient sim-to-real transfer for quadruped robots.

#### 4.1. Simulation Evaluation

- **Overall Performance:** DreamTIP achieved an average performance improvement of 28.1% across eight distinct simulated transfer tasks compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
- **Robustness to Difficulty:** Performance differences between methods were minimal at low task difficulty. However, as task difficulty increased, DreamTIP consistently exhibited the least performance degradation, demonstrating greater adaptability and robustness.
- **Challenging Tasks:** In the Crawl task (gap widths varying from 26 cm to 23 cm), DreamTIP's average reward decreased from 36.58 to 25.35 (a 30.6% reduction) at the highest difficulty. In contrast, the WMP baseline's reward sharply declined from 33.51 to 5.66 (an 83.1% reduction), indicating near failure.
- **Impact of Regularization:** The proposed method without Task-Invariant Properties (Ours w/o TIP) consistently outperformed WMP w/ Finetune, especially in challenging Compound Transfer tasks. WMP w/ Finetune, lacking explicit regularization constraints, sometimes performed worse after fine-tuning. This suggests that the regularization constraints effectively mitigate representation drift and knowledge forgetting during adaptation.
- **Impact of Task-Invariant Properties (TIPs):** Comparisons between WMP (baseline) and DreamTIP without adaptation (Ours w/o Adapt) indicated that incorporating TIPs significantly improved the world model's task transfer capacity across almost all tasks. This confirms that TIPs help the model capture consistent structural and dynamic features, leading to more reliable prediction and decision-making in unseen scenarios.

#### 4.2. Real-World Evaluation

- **Success Rates:** Real-world tests on a Unitree Go2 robot across four terrains (Stair, Climb, Tilt, Crawl) showed that DreamTIP exhibited stronger transfer capability.
 - In the 52 cm Climb task, the baseline (WMP) achieved only a 10% success rate. DreamTIP without adaptation (Ours w/o Adapt) achieved 90%, and the full DreamTIP method (Ours) achieved a 100% success rate.
 - For Stair (16 cm) and Crawl (25 cm), DreamTIP (Ours) achieved 100% success, while WMP achieved 100% and 70% respectively.
 - For Tilt (33 cm), DreamTIP achieved 80% compared to WMP's 40%.
- **Robustness to CoM Shift:** The robot's robustness was tested with a 2 kg counterweight, demonstrating DreamTIP's capacity to handle dynamic environmental variations while maintaining strong cross-task adaptability.
- **Qualitative Observation:** In the Crawl task (25 cm obstacle), DreamTIP enabled the robot to traverse safely, whereas the baseline method resulted in collisions with the robot's head. This qualitative result reinforced the observed quantitative improvements in sim-to-real effectiveness.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **LLM-Driven TIPs:** Comparisons between DreamTIP-DWL (predicting privileged information directly) and LLM-driven DreamTIP variants (DreamTIP-GPT5, DreamTIP-DeepSeekV3) showed that LLM-based methods constructing Task-Invariant Properties as auxiliary targets significantly outperformed direct privileged information prediction in Climb and Tilt tasks. This indicates the effectiveness of LLMs in identifying and formulating features crucial for world model transfer performance.
- **Number of Adaptation Trajectories:** An ablation on the number of real-world trajectories ($n$) used for fine-tuning revealed that performance noticeably improved as $n$ increased from 3 to 5. Beyond 5 trajectories, diminishing returns were observed. This suggests that $n=5$ provides a balance between effectiveness and computational cost for adaptation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DreamTIP framework offers a notable contribution to the field of robotic locomotion and sim-to-real transfer, with several significant implications:

1. **Enhanced Robustness and Transferability:** By explicitly learning Task-Invariant Properties, DreamTIP reduces the policy's reliance on specific dynamic parameters of the simulation environment. This leads to more robust performance in diverse and dynamic real-world terrains, effectively bridging the simulation-to-reality gap. The significant performance improvements observed in challenging tasks and real-world deployments highlight its capacity for handling previously unseen variations.

2. **Efficient Adaptation with Minimal Real-World Data:** The proposed adaptation strategy, which combines a mixed replay buffer with regularization constraints and selective freezing of model components, enables rapid calibration to real-world dynamics using only a small number of real-world trajectories. This addresses a critical limitation of robot learning, reducing the high costs, time, and safety risks associated with extensive real-world data collection and fine-tuning.

3. **Novel Application of Large Language Models (LLMs) for Property Design:** The integration of LLMs to automatically identify and formulate Task-Invariant Properties is a novel approach. This method moves beyond laborious manual feature engineering, potentially accelerating research and development in robot control by leveraging LLMs' reasoning capabilities and vast knowledge base to abstract complex physical and behavioral principles. This could open avenues for more sophisticated, automated property extraction in other robotic tasks.

4. **Advancement in World Model-Based Reinforcement Learning:** DreamTIP extends the capabilities of world models like Dreamer by demonstrating how explicit learning of task-invariant features can further enhance their generalization and transfer characteristics. This could lead to more capable and adaptable embodied AI agents.

5. **Practical Implications for Quadruped Robotics:** The demonstrated success on a physical quadruped robot (Unitree Go2) across complex terrains validates the practical applicability of DreamTIP. It implies that future quadruped robots could be deployed and adapted more efficiently to diverse industrial, exploration, or service tasks with less human intervention and specialized fine-tuning.

Despite these advancements, the paper acknowledges a limitation: prolonged operation can lead to performance degradation due to compounding errors in the world model's long-term predictions. Future work aims to address this by leveraging richer data to improve prediction accuracy and robustness, thereby offering a more scalable framework for adaptive robot learning. Overall, DreamTIP represents a step towards more generalized, adaptable, and efficient robot locomotion in the real world.
