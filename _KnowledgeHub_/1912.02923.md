# Generating 3D People in Scenes without People

> [!summary] Summary
> PSI is an automatic system that populates empty 3D scenes with plausible human bodies. A conditional VAE, conditioned on scene depth and semantics, samples SMPL-X body shape, pose, and global placement; a scene geometry-aware optimization then refines each mesh to encourage contact and remove interpenetration. It generates more diverse and more physically plausible bodies than the prior stick-figure baseline, and doubles as a scene-dependent pose prior.

## Problem

- Existing 3D scene datasets and embodied simulators contain no people, and manually posing realistic humans in them demands prohibitive artist effort.
- Prior affordance methods output stick figures or coarse volumes, so contact between body surface and furniture cannot be reasoned about or validated visually.
- Earlier learned placement methods need large paired image-pose corpora with manual cleaning, limiting generalization to scenes rarely covered by sitcom or synthetic data.

## Method

- **PSI**: conditional ==VAE== over ==SMPL-X== parameters conditioned on a depth-plus-semantics scene image; a one-stage variant predicts all body features jointly, a two-stage variant factorizes global translation then local pose.
- Training loss adds ==VPoser== pose regularization plus human-scene terms: a ==signed-distance-field== collision loss and a Geman-McClure contact loss on annotated contact vertices, enabled after 75% of epochs.
- Scene geometry-aware fitting post-optimizes each sampled body against contact, collision, and VPoser terms; datasets **PROX-E** (virtual-camera, semantically annotated) and **MP3D-R** (Habitat snapshots) support training and testing.

## Results

- One-stage model cut reconstruction error from the baseline's **0.48** to **0.24**-**0.25** on held-out scenes, with negative ELBO dropping from **0.72** to **0.36**-**0.41**.
- Average diversity cluster size rose from **1.49**-**1.84** for the baseline to roughly **2.4**-**2.9**; fitting pushed non-collision to **0.97**-**0.98** and contact to **0.99** on PROX-E.
- Used as a scene-dependent pose prior, it lowered vertex-to-vertex error to **171.75mm** versus **173.97mm** for PROX; user study scored **3.49** versus **3.31** baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A surface-based differentiable body model makes physical constraints trainable end to end, enabling contact and penetration reasoning that skeletal pose representations structurally cannot express.
> - Diversity must be reported alongside plausibility metrics, since a posterior-collapsed generator scores well physically while producing one repeated body.
> - Rendering many noisy virtual camera views from a small captured dataset is a cheap route to generalization, but downstream quality still tracks scan and SDF fidelity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in 3D scanning and reconstruction technologies have led to the creation of high-quality 3D indoor scene datasets, such as Matterport3D, Replica, and Gibson, as well as virtual environments like the Habitat simulator. These resources serve as platforms for developing scene understanding methods, indoor robot navigation, and applications in AR/VR and computer games. A recognized limitation of these environments is their lack of integrated human representations, specifically realistic people interacting naturally with the scenes. Manually populating these environments with interacting humans is a labor-intensive process requiring significant artistic effort.

Prior research has explored various aspects of object and person placement in images and 3D scenes. Approaches to 2D image compositing and inpainting (e.g., Dvornik et al., Lin et al., Tan et al., Ouyang et al., Lee et al.) focus on contextual information to predict object locations and appearances. However, these methods are restricted to 2D representations and generally do not account for detailed physical interactions between humans and the environment.

Another line of research investigates affordances, which are opportunities for interaction with objects or scenes. Many works represent object affordance through human poses (e.g., Grabner et al., Kim et al., Zhu et al., Savva et al.). While some methods attempt to place human poses in 3D scenes (e.g., Gupta et al., Savva et al., Chen et al., Chao et al., Zanfir et al.), a common limitation is the use of simplified body models, such as 3D stick figures or coarse volumes (e.g., Wang et al., Li et al.). These simplified representations prevent detailed reasoning about contact surfaces, body shape variations, and physical feasibility (e.g., interpenetration), thus limiting the realism and utility of the generated interactions. Li et al. extended this by proposing a generative model for 3D stick figures in 3D scenes, but their method relied on a synthesized dataset and still used a simplified body model.

This work addresses the aforementioned limitations by introducing an automated system that generates semantically and physically plausible 3D human bodies as detailed meshes (using the SMPL-X model) within 3D environments. By employing a detailed body model and explicitly enforcing physical constraints, this research aims to provide a more advanced solution for populating virtual scenes with realistic, interactive human representations, thereby supporting applications that demand a high degree of fidelity in human-scene interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a fully automatic system capable of generating plausible 3D human bodies, naturally posed within a given 3D scene that initially contains no people. The authors articulate several specific requirements for the generated human bodies:

1. **Semantic Plausibility**: The generated human bodies must interact with the 3D environment in a semantically meaningful way. This implies that the poses and locations of the people should be consistent with the function and arrangement of objects in the scene. Examples include people sitting on a sofa or positioned near a stove for cooking.
2. **Physical Feasibility**: The interaction between the generated human bodies and the scene must be physically viable. This includes preventing interpenetration between the human body and scene geometry, while simultaneously ensuring that body-scene contact is established in a physically supportive manner, such as standing on the floor or resting on furniture, rather than floating.
3. **Diversity**: The system should generate a variety of human body shapes and poses, reflecting the range of possible interactions and individual differences.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several factors:
- **Addressing Data Scarcity**: Despite the availability of high-quality 3D scene datasets and simulators, these environments typically lack human occupants. This absence limits their utility for applications that require human-scene interaction data, such as training datasets for human pose estimation, embodied AI research, and simulation platforms.
- **Overcoming Manual Effort**: Manually generating realistic 3D humans and integrating them naturally into complex 3D scenes requires substantial artistic skill and time. An automated solution could alleviate this burden, making it feasible to populate large-scale virtual environments.
- **Enhancing Realism in Applications**: For applications like virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), and computer games, realistic and expressive human avatars that naturally interact with their surroundings are crucial for immersion and functionality. The proposed system aims to provide a foundation for such realistic integration.
- **Improving upon Prior Body Representations**: Existing methods for human synthesis or affordance inference often simplify the human body to stick figures or coarse volumetric models. These representations are insufficient for detailed reasoning about human-scene contact, surface interpenetration, and variations in body shape, thereby limiting the realism and utility of the generated interactions. The adoption of a detailed 3D human body model, such as SMPL-X, is motivated by the need for more accurate and physically-aware interactions.

In summary, the research aims to fill a critical gap in 3D scene generation by automatically synthesizing diverse, semantically plausible, and physically feasible 3D human bodies within unpopulated scenes, thereby enabling a range of advanced applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system operates in two sequential stages to generate plausible 3D human bodies in 3D scenes. This approach is conceptually inspired by how humans plan interactions, first considering semantic context and then applying physical rules.

### 4. Preliminaries

- **3D Scene Representation**: The scene is represented from the perspective of an embodied agent, as in the Habitat simulator. The primary scene input `x_s` consists of a stack of depth maps and semantic segmentation maps from a single camera view, resized to 128x128 pixels.
- **3D Human Body Representation**: The SMPL-X model is utilized to represent the human body as a 3D mesh with 10475 vertices and a fixed topology. The model is parameterized by:
 - Body shape (`β ∈ R¹⁰`)
 - Body pose (`θ_b ∈ R³²`, from the latent space of VPoser)
 - Hand pose (`θ_h ∈ R²⁴`)
 - Global rotation (`R`, 6D continuous representation for differentiability)
 - Global translation (`t`, 3D vector).
 The combined parameters are denoted as `x_h = (t, R, β, θ_b, θ_h)ᵀ ∈ R⁷⁵`. The differentiability of SMPL-X is leveraged for optimizing human-scene contact and collision.

### 5. Scene Context-aware Human Body Generator

This stage employs a conditional variational autoencoder (CVAE) framework to model the probability distribution `p(x_h | x_s)`.

- **Network Architectures**:
 - **One-stage (S1) Network**: Infers all body features `x_h` jointly.
 - **Two-stage (S2) Network**: Factorizes the probability into `p(x_l_h | x_g_h, x_s)p(x_g_h | x_s)`, inferring global features (`x_g_h`, global translation) first, and then local features (`x_l_h`, shape, pose, hand pose) conditioned on the global features and scene.
 - Both architectures use a scene encoder fine-tuned from the first 6 convolutional layers of ResNet18, pre-trained on ImageNet. Human features are lifted via a fully-connected layer and concatenated with encoded scene features.
- **Training Loss (`L`)**: The overall loss function combines multiple terms:
 - `L_rec`: Reconstruction loss, measuring the difference between ground truth and reconstructed global translation (and its projection), and other body features.
 - `L_KL`: Kullback-Leibler divergence to regularize the VAE's latent space towards a normal distribution.
 - `L_VPoser`: Encourages generated body poses to be natural by penalizing deviations from the VPoser latent space prior.
 - `L_collision`: Minimizes the absolute values of negative signed distance function (SDF) values at body mesh vertices, discouraging interpenetration with the scene.
 - `L_contact`: Encourages contact between specified body mesh vertices (based on) and the closest scene mesh vertices, using a Geman-McClure error function.

### 6. Scene Geometry-aware Fitting

After initial generation, a post-processing optimization step refines the body meshes to enhance physical plausibility. Given a generated human body configuration `x_0_h`, an optimization objective `L_f(x_h)` is minimized. This loss balances:
- Deviation from the initial generated pose (`|x_h - x_0_h|`).
- The `L_contact` and `L_collision` terms, promoting appropriate human-scene interaction.
- The `L_VPoser` term, encouraging natural poses.

### 7. Implementation Details

The system is implemented in PyTorch. Loss weights for `L_KL`, `L_VPoser`, `L_contact`, and `L_collision` are empirically determined, with `L_KL` using an annealing scheme and human-scene interaction losses (`L_HS`) enabled after 75% of training epochs to prevent early instability. Adam optimizer is used for training. For scene geometry-aware fitting, specific loss weights are set to ensure quality and reduce optimization iterations.

### 8. Datasets

- **PROX-E**: An extension of PROX-Qualitative. It includes 3D human body meshes (pseudo-ground truth) estimated from RGB-D data in indoor environments. Extensions involve:
 - Manually building virtual walls, floors, and ceilings to complete scene scans.
 - Manual semantic annotation of scene meshes using Matterport3D categories.
 - Generating about 70K frames by down-sampling original recordings and setting up multiple virtual cameras with varying poses and Gaussian noise, ensuring views of the human body without severe occlusion.
- **MP3D-R**: Consists of 7 rooms extracted from the Matterport3D dataset. A virtual agent in the Habitat simulator captures 32 snapshots per room, including RGB, depth, and semantic images, along with camera parameters. Scene SDFs are pre-computed. This dataset is primarily used for testing generalization to unseen environments.

### 9. Baseline for Comparison

For quantitative comparison, a modified version of Li et al.'s work is used as a baseline. Key modifications include:
- Changing scene representation to depth and semantics.
- Adapting the model to generate SMPL-X body meshes instead of stick figures.
- Training on PROX-E and combining with the geometry-aware fitting process.

### 10. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the proposed method (S1 and S2 networks, with and without human-scene interaction loss `L_HS`, and with and without geometry-aware fitting `L_f`) was conducted using various quantitative metrics and a user study, comparing against a modified baseline.

### 11. Evaluation of Representation Power

Using the PROX-E dataset, the ability of the networks to represent human-scene interaction was assessed through reconstruction error and negative evidence lower bound (ELBO).
- **Performance**: Both S1 and S2 models consistently outperformed the modified baseline, achieving lower reconstruction errors and negative ELBO values on both validation and test sets. For example, the S1 model achieved a reconstruction error of 0.25 and -logP(x) of 0.41 on the test set, compared to the baseline's 0.48 and 0.72, respectively.
- **Generalization**: The comparable metrics between validation and test sets indicated that the virtual camera approach used in data generation effectively prevented overfitting to seen environments.

### 12. Evaluation of 3D Body Mesh Generation

The generated 3D human bodies were evaluated based on diversity, physical plausibility, and semantic plausibility.

- **Diversity Metric**: This metric assesses the variety of generated human bodies by clustering SMPL-X parameters via K-means (20 clusters) and computing the entropy of cluster IDs and average cluster size.
 - **Results**: Our methods consistently demonstrated higher diversity than the baseline. For instance, on PROX-E, S1 without `L_HS` achieved a cluster ID entropy of 2.96 and an average cluster size of 2.51, compared to the baseline's 2.89 and 1.49, respectively. This indicated that the proposed models generated a broader range of distinct human body configurations.
 - **Impact of Fitting**: The scene geometry-aware fitting (`L_f`) generally maintained or slightly increased diversity for our models, while the baseline showed marginal improvement.

- **Physical Metric**: This metric quantifies collision and contact between the body mesh and the scene mesh.
 - **Non-collision Score**: The ratio of body mesh vertices with positive SDF values (i.e., in free space).
 - **Contact Ratio**: The ratio of generated body meshes with at least one body vertex having a non-positive SDF value (i.e., in contact with the scene).
 - **Results**: Our methods consistently achieved higher non-collision scores and contact ratios compared to the baseline, indicating better physical plausibility. For example, S1 with `L_f` achieved a non-collision score of 0.94 and a contact ratio of 0.99 on PROX-E.
 - **Impact of `L_HS` Loss**: The influence of the `L_HS` loss on 3D body generation was not as pronounced in this metric as its effect on interaction modeling.
 - **Impact of Fitting (`L_f`)**: The scene geometry-aware fitting consistently improved both the non-collision score and contact ratio across all models, confirming its role in refining physical plausibility.

- **User Study (Semantic Plausibility)**: A user study on Amazon Mechanical Turk (AMT) asked participants to rate the naturalness of rendered images of generated human bodies (1-5 scale).
 - **Results**: Ground-truth samples achieved the highest average score (4.04 ± 1.03 on PROX-E). Our S1 model with `L_f` achieved an average score of 3.49 ± 1.26 on PROX-E, comparable to the baseline with `L_f` at 3.32 ± 1.35.
 - **Combined Interpretation**: While average scores for our method and the baseline were similar in terms of semantic plausibility, our method demonstrated significantly higher diversity. This suggests that the proposed approach generates human bodies that are comparably natural but with greater variation in poses and shapes.
 - **Impact of Fitting**: Geometry-aware fitting consistently improved the user study scores for both the baseline and our model, likely due to enhanced physical realism contributing to perceived naturalness.

### 13. Scene-aware 3D Body Pose Estimation (Downstream Application)

The study demonstrated that the learned human-scene interaction prior can improve 3D human pose estimation from monocular RGB images.
- **Approach**: The S1 model with `L_HS` was used to generate a scene-dependent pose prior (`θ_s_b`) by averaging VPoser latent space features from multiple generated bodies. This prior was then used to initialize and regularize the optimization framework of SMPlify-X/PROX for 3D pose estimation.
- **Results**: The proposed method improved pose estimation performance on the PROX-Quantitative dataset. For instance, it achieved a mean per-joint error (p.PJE) of 71.73mm and a mean vertex-to-vertex error (p.V2V) of 62.64mm, compared to PROX's 73.20mm and 64.76mm, respectively. This indicates that the model learns contextual information about human-scene interactions that is beneficial for pose estimation.

### 14. Qualitative Results and Failure Cases

Qualitative examples illustrate the system's ability to generate varied and plausible interactions (e.g., sitting on beds, touching desks). However, failure cases were identified, primarily categorized into:
- **Wrong Generations**: The generative model sometimes produces implausible poses (e.g., floating, collision) in test scenes, particularly when sampled results do not align with scene geometry.
- **Wrong Fittings**: While fitting improves physical plausibility, the optimization process does not fully simulate real physics (e.g., gravity, elasticity), occasionally leading to abnormal body configurations, translations, rotations, or unnatural human-scene contact.
- **Data Quality Issues**: Noisy scans or complex scene structures in datasets like MP3D-R can lead to lower quality generated bodies and fitting failures.

### 15. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces an automated framework for populating 3D scenes with realistic and expressive 3D human bodies, addressing a recognized gap in the availability of human-populated virtual environments. The significance and potential impact of this work span several areas:

- **Advancement in 3D Human-Scene Generation**: The system provides a solution for generating semantically plausible and physically feasible 3D human bodies as detailed meshes, moving beyond simpler representations like stick figures used in prior work. This is achieved through a two-stage process combining a CVAE-based generative model for semantic context and a geometry-aware fitting step for physical realism, directly leveraging the differentiable SMPL-X body model.

- **Enhanced Synthetic Data Generation**: A primary impact is the ability to automatically create large-scale, high-quality synthetic datasets of humans interacting with diverse 3D environments. Such datasets are critical for training and evaluating machine learning models in various computer vision tasks, including:
 - **Human Pose Estimation**: Providing diverse poses and interactions in context can significantly improve the robustness of pose estimation algorithms. The demonstrated improvement in 3D pose estimation using the learned prior supports this potential.
 - **Action Recognition**: Generating humans performing actions within scenes can aid in developing models that better understand and predict human behavior.
 - **Embodied AI and Robotics**: Populated scenes offer richer training environments for AI agents to learn navigation, interaction, and scene understanding in human-centric contexts.

- **Applications in Interactive Technologies**:
 - **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR)**: The ability to automatically generate interactive 3D avatars in virtual or augmented environments can enhance immersion and realism, enabling more dynamic and engaging user experiences.
 - **Computer Games and Animation**: This work can significantly reduce the manual effort currently required by artists to populate game scenes with characters and animate their interactions, potentially accelerating content creation workflows and increasing scene diversity.

- **Contribution to Research Resources**: The development and extension of the PROX-E and MP3D-R datasets, along with the proposed evaluation metrics (diversity, physical plausibility, user study for semantic plausibility), aim to establish benchmarks for future research in scene-aware human body generation. The stated availability of data, code, and models fosters reproducibility and encourages further advancements in the field.

- **Improved Scene Understanding**: The explicit modeling of human-scene interaction, including contact and collision, contributes to a deeper understanding of scene affordances and human-environment relationships. The demonstration of using the learned prior to improve 3D pose estimation suggests that the model effectively captures meaningful contextual information, offering a potential avenue for more integrated human and scene perception systems.

Overall, this research represents a step towards fully automated, contextually aware digital human generation, with implications for a broad spectrum of research areas and commercial applications requiring sophisticated human-computer interaction and scene population.
