# Self-evolving Agents with reflective and memory-augmented abilities

> [!summary] Summary
> SAGE proposes a framework for self-evolving Large Language Model agents by integrating iterative feedback, reflection, and a novel memory optimization system inspired by the Ebbinghaus forgetting curve. This approach improves performance for both proprietary and open-source models, notably enabling Qwen-1.8B to achieve results comparable to GPT-3.5 and reducing memory consumption by nearly 50% in RAG tasks.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with continuous decision-making and adapting strategies in dynamic environments.
- LLM agents have limited context windows, hindering long-term memory retention, and accumulating interaction history in complex tasks causes information overload and increased resource demands.
- Existing self-improvement techniques are often task-specific, resource-intensive, or lack generalized self-evolution capabilities.

## Method

- A ==three-agent architecture== (User, Assistant, Checker) implements an ==iterative feedback loop== that lets the Assistant continuously refine its policy, theoretically shown to converge to a ==Nash equilibrium== for strategy stability and robustness.
- A ==dual-memory system== (Short-Term and Long-Term) is enhanced with a ==reflection mechanism== that converts sparse rewards into actionable insights for long-term storage.
- ==MemorySyntax==, a novel memory optimization method, integrates the ==Ebbinghaus forgetting curve== to intelligently prioritize, retain, or discard information based on its strength and retention rate.

## Results

- SAGE consistently improved performance across benchmarks: up to **2.26x** gain for GPT-3.5 on AgentBench-Database, **57.7%**-**100%** for Qwen-1.8B, **20.8%** accuracy gain for GPT-3.5 on HotpotQA, and **17.3%** for Mistral-7b on ALFWorld.
- SAGE enhanced RAG agent accuracy by **3.6%** to **4.7%** across various QA tasks while simultaneously cutting memory consumption by nearly **50%** without increasing latency.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the MemorySyntax optimization is critical for performance, leading to substantial gains (e.g., Qwen-1.8B's Knowledge Graph score jumped from **6.8** to **45.3**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Iterative feedback in a multi-agent setup provides a robust mechanism for policy refinement, converging towards a stable strategy as demonstrated by game theory principles.
> - Reflection transforms reward signals into actionable self-reflections enriching long-term memory; MemorySyntax, a biologically-inspired memory system, combats information overload by prioritizing high-utility info via perceived strength and decay rate.
> - The framework allows smaller open-source LLMs to achieve performance levels competitive with larger models, democratizing advanced agent capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) as autonomous agents represents a significant and rapidly evolving frontier in artificial intelligence. While LLMs have demonstrated remarkable abilities in natural language understanding and generation, their application as agents in dynamic, interactive environments surfaces several inherent limitations that the broader research community is actively trying to overcome.

**Existing Challenges and Limitations:**
1. **Continuous Decision-Making and Adaptability:** Traditional LLMs struggle with sustained, sequential decision-making in evolving environments. They often lack the ability to adapt their strategies based on continuous feedback and new information.
2. **Long-Term Memory and Context Window Constraints:** LLMs have notoriously limited context windows, hindering their capacity to remember information over long interactions. This necessitates external memory mechanisms, but many existing solutions are simplistic (e.g., FIFO queues) or task-specific.
3. **Information Overload in Multi-Agent Systems:** As LLM agents engage in complex tasks, the accumulation of interaction history can lead to communication overload, increased resource demands, and latency, making efficient memory management crucial.
4. **Lack of Generalizable Self-Improvement:** While techniques like meta-learning, multi-task learning, and prompt engineering exist for self-improvement, they often require extensive resources, are specific to certain tasks, or rely on simple one-off tweaks.

**SAGE's Contribution to the Landscape:**
SAGE (Self-evolving Agents with Reflective and Memory-augmented Abilities) is positioned as a novel framework that directly addresses these core challenges. It builds upon prior work in LLM agents (e.g., AutoGPT, BabyAGI, ReAct) and memory augmentation (e.g., MemGPT, MemoryBank) but differentiates itself by proposing a *general and systematically integrated framework* for self-improvement and memory management.

Instead of focusing on isolated improvements, SAGE combines three synergistic mechanisms:
- **Iterative Feedback:** Enhances continuous decision-making and adaptability by allowing agents to refine their policies through repeated interactions and feedback, moving towards a stable strategy.
- **Reflection:** Addresses the challenge of learning from past experiences by enabling agents to derive insights from their performance, transforming sparse rewards into actionable knowledge stored in long-term memory. This contrasts with simpler feedback loops or memory storage.
- **Memory Optimization (MemorySyntax based on Ebbinghaus Forgetting Curve):** This is a key differentiator, introducing a biologically inspired mechanism for intelligent information retention and discard. Unlike static memory management (e.g., FIFO) or simple time-based decay, MemorySyntax dynamically prioritizes information based on its perceived "strength," actively combating information overload and optimizing memory usage.

By integrating these elements, SAGE aims to provide a more robust, adaptable, and resource-efficient approach to building LLM agents. Its emphasis on self-evolution without additional training, particularly its strong performance with smaller open-source models, positions it as a significant step towards democratizing and scaling autonomous agent capabilities within the broader AI ecosystem. The theoretical justification for its iterative feedback mechanism, demonstrating convergence to a Nash equilibrium, also adds a layer of formal rigor not always present in agent frameworks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the SAGE framework is to overcome critical limitations faced by Large Language Models (LLMs) when deployed as autonomous agents in dynamic and complex environments. The authors are motivated by the inherent challenges that hinder LLM agents from achieving truly continuous decision-making, effective long-term memory, and efficient information processing.

The key objectives can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhance Continuous Decision-Making and Adaptability:** To enable LLM agents to continuously learn, adapt, and refine their strategies in response to changing environments and evolving task requirements. This addresses the challenge where agents might struggle to make consistent and optimal decisions over extended interactions.
2. **Overcome Long-Term Memory Deficiencies and Limited Context Windows:** To develop mechanisms that allow LLM agents to retain and effectively retrieve crucial information over long time spans, moving beyond the inherent limitations of their context windows. This is vital for tasks requiring sustained interaction or the integration of information from distant past experiences.
3. **Reduce Information Overload and Cognitive Load:** To design an intelligent memory management system that mitigates the problem of accumulating irrelevant information, especially in multi-agent systems where context maintenance can become resource-intensive and lead to increased latency. The goal is to optimize information storage and transmission, allowing agents to focus on key data.
4. **Provide a General, Self-Evolving Framework:** To propose an innovative framework that systematically improves LLM agents' capabilities without requiring additional training or being overly task-specific. The motivation is to create a robust and adaptable system that can be applied across a wide range of LLMs and tasks.
5. **Improve Performance, Especially for Smaller Models:** To demonstrate that the proposed mechanisms can significantly boost the performance of LLM agents, with a particular focus on showing substantial gains for smaller, open-source models, thereby potentially democratizing access to advanced agent capabilities.

The motivation stems from the observation that despite significant progress, existing LLM agent architectures often rely on simplistic memory solutions or lack sophisticated self-improvement loops. The authors aim to fill this gap by integrating cognitive science principles (Ebbinghaus forgetting curve) with multi-agent feedback and reflective learning, creating a more human-like and efficient AI agent system.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SAGE framework is built upon three foundational pillars: a multi-agent structure, an iterative feedback loop, and a dual-memory system augmented by reflection and a novel MemorySyntax mechanism.

#### 3.1. Multi-Agent Structure

SAGE employs a three-agent architecture designed to streamline task execution, evaluation, and refinement:
- **User (U):** Initiates the interaction by providing a task description ($d_U$) and an instance ($i_U$) that serves as a reference for the correct output.
- **Assistant (A):** The core LLM agent, responsible for generating outputs ($o_t$) and executing actions ($a_t$). Its policy ($\pi_\theta$) is conditioned on the current state, reward signals, and feedback from the Checker.
- **Checker (C):** Evaluates the Assistant's output against the expected outcome ($i_U$) and provides iterative feedback ($f_C$) to guide the Assistant's refinement process.

#### 3.2. Iterative Feedback Mechanism

This mechanism enables the Assistant to continuously refine its policy through repeated interactions and feedback from the Checker.
- **Initialization:** Roles are assigned, and the User defines the task.
- **Interaction Phase:** The Assistant generates outputs iteratively. At each step $t$, it selects an action $a_t$ based on its policy, reward ($R_t$), and Checker's feedback ($f^i_t$). The process continues until validation or an iteration limit is reached.
- **Theoretical Optimality:** The paper includes a theoretical justification, demonstrating that this iterative feedback mechanism leads to strategy stability in the sense of a Nash equilibrium. This is crucial for ensuring the convergence and robustness of the multi-agent system, providing a stronger basis for the three-agent setup compared to simpler alternatives.
- **Evolutionary Goals:** The Assistant formulates new evolutionary objectives ($G_{t+1}$) based on feedback, guiding the update of its policy ($\theta_{t+1}$) using a function $\psi$ that integrates current policy with these objectives, incorporating memory optimization and self-adjustments.

#### 3.3. Memory Management System

SAGE utilizes a dual-memory system to handle information effectively across different timescales.
- **Short-Term Memory (STM):** Designed for immediate, task-specific data. It rapidly updates with current observations ($O_t$) and actions ($a_t$), maintaining a recent trajectory history ($T_t$). This facilitates real-time decision-making.
- **Long-Term Memory (LTM):** Stores critical information and self-reflections ($r_t$) over extended periods. It accumulates knowledge from past interactions, enabling the agent to apply prior experiences to future tasks.

#### 3.4. Reflection Mechanism

To enhance the agent's learning capacity, SAGE incorporates a reflection mechanism:
- The Assistant processes sparse reward signals (e.g., success/failure), the current trajectory ($T_t$), and its Long-Term Memory ($M_L$).
- From these inputs, it derives self-reflections ($r_t$), which are deeper insights than simple scalar rewards.
- These reflections are then added to the Long-Term Memory ($M_L \leftarrow M_L \cup \{r_t\}$), continuously enriching the agent's accumulated experience and improving its decision-making.

#### 3.5. MemorySyntax (Ebbinghaus Forgetting Curve)

This is a novel memory optimization method that emulates human-like memory processes, integrating the Ebbinghaus forgetting curve with linguistic principles.
- **Retention Rate:** Information ($I_t$) is assigned a retention rate $R(I_t, \tau) = e^{-\frac{\tau}{S}}$, where $S$ is the information's strength (importance/complexity) and $\tau$ is the time interval.
- **Linguistic Optimization:** Information ($I_t$) is linguistically optimized into $I^*_t$ to increase its strength ($S^* > S$), thereby improving retention.
- **Dynamic Update Rule:** A threshold-based system governs memory updates:
 - If $R(I^*_t, \tau) \geq \theta_1$: $I^*_t$ is retained in STM ($M_S$).
 - If $\theta_2 \leq R(I^*_t, \tau) < \theta_1$: $I^*_t$ is transferred to LTM ($M_L$).
 - If $R(I^*_t, \tau) < \theta_2$: $I^*_t$ is discarded as insignificant.
- **Theoretical Background:** The paper provides an information-theoretic perspective, showing that this mechanism optimizes memory by maximizing information utility, specifically by preferentially retaining high-entropy (high-uncertainty) information, thereby mitigating information overload.

By combining these components, SAGE aims to create an autonomous agent that not only learns from feedback but also intelligently manages its memory, reflects on its performance, and adapts its strategies in a self-evolving manner.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation demonstrates that the SAGE framework significantly enhances the performance of Large Language Model (LLM) agents across a wide spectrum of tasks and benchmarks, with particularly notable effects on smaller models.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on AgentBench

- **Significant Performance Improvement:** SAGE consistently improves scores on the AgentBench benchmark, which includes diverse tasks like CODE (Knowledge Graph, OS, DB), GAME (ALFWorld), and WEB (WebShop, Mind2Web).
- **Proprietary Models:** GPT-3.5 and GPT-4, already strong baselines, show notable gains. For instance, GPT-3.5 achieves up to a 2.26x improvement in the Database task.
- **Open-Source Models:** The improvements are even more pronounced for open-source models, ranging from 57.7% to 100%. Llama2-7B and CodeLlama-7B show substantial enhancements. Qwen-1.8B, after applying SAGE, performs comparably to GPT-3.5, highlighting the framework's ability to boost weaker models.
- **Error Reduction:** SAGE notably reduces basic errors, such as "Context Limit Exceeded" and "invalid format" errors, and also decreases "invalid action" errors in OS and DB tasks. This indicates improved robustness and logical consistency.

#### 4.2. Complex Problem-Solving Tasks Evaluation

- **Outperformance Across Tasks:** SAGE consistently outperforms all baselines on tasks involving long-form Question Answering (HotpotQA), multi-turn dialog (MultiWOZ), and sequential task completion (ALFWorld).
- **Substantial Gains:** Examples include a 20.8% increase in answer accuracy for GPT-3.5 on HotpotQA and a 17.3% improvement in task completion for Mistral-7b on ALFWorld. These results underscore SAGE's effectiveness in enhancing reasoning, coherence, and task execution.

#### 4.3. Evaluation of Long-Context Tasks

- **Code Completion (LCC, RepoBench-P):** SAGE shows a slight improvement in F1 score (79.29 on LCC) compared to other self-refinement methods like Beam Search and Reflexion. While not revolutionary, its memory mechanisms contribute to refining code predictions.
- **Reasoning Tasks (HotPotQA, TriviaQA):** SAGE significantly outperforms Reflexion and Beam Search on complex reasoning tasks, achieving F1 scores of 22.06 and 22.76, respectively. This highlights SAGE's superior ability to integrate multi-document information and perform more accurate reasoning.

#### 4.4. Evaluation of RAG Agents

- **Accuracy Improvements:** SAGE (using ChatGPT-4) consistently achieves accuracy improvements ranging from 3.6% to 4.7% over various classical (BM25, DPR), commercial (OpenAI Retrieval), and state-of-the-art (TART, FiD) RAG methods on multi-document and open-domain QA tasks (HotpotQA, Natural Questions, TriviaQA).
- **Memory Efficiency:** Crucially, SAGE achieves these gains while cutting memory consumption by nearly 50% on some tasks, *without increasing latency*. This demonstrates remarkable efficiency, which is critical for practical deployments.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study (Memory Optimization)

- **Crucial Role of Memory Optimization:** The ablation study on Qwen-1.8B and CodeLlama-7B unequivocally demonstrates that the memory optimization mechanism is indispensable for SAGE's performance. Models without this component show significantly weaker results.
- **Quantifiable Impact:** For example, Qwen-1.8B's performance in the Knowledge Graph task jumps from 6.8 to 45.3 with memory optimization, and CodeLlama-7B's DB score increases from 2.7 to 41.3. This confirms the efficacy of the Ebbinghaus forgetting curve-based MemorySyntax.

In summary, the results firmly establish SAGE as a highly effective framework for developing more capable, adaptable, and efficient LLM agents, particularly excelling in complex multi-tasking, long-context handling, and resource optimization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SAGE framework represents a substantial advancement in the field of Large Language Model (LLM) agents, offering a generalizable and efficient solution to several persistent challenges. Its significance and potential impact span both theoretical contributions and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Addressing Core LLM Agent Limitations

SAGE directly tackles critical shortcomings of current LLM agents: the difficulty in continuous decision-making in dynamic environments, the inherent lack of long-term memory, and the limitations imposed by finite context windows. By integrating iterative feedback, reflective learning, and a sophisticated memory optimization mechanism, SAGE pushes the boundaries of agent autonomy and adaptability.

#### 5.2. A General and Robust Framework

Unlike many task-specific improvements or memory solutions, SAGE offers a *general framework* that can be applied to diverse LLMs (both proprietary and open-source) across a wide range of complex tasks (e.g., code generation, web navigation, multi-hop QA). This generality is a significant advantage, reducing the need for specialized engineering efforts for each new agent application. The theoretical grounding in game theory, demonstrating convergence to a Nash equilibrium for the multi-agent system, further enhances its robustness and reliability.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Performance and Efficiency

The experimental results showcase consistent and significant performance improvements across all evaluated benchmarks, including challenging AgentBench tasks, complex problem-solving scenarios, and long-context processing.
- **Quantitative Gains:** Improvements of up to 2.26x on closed-source models and 57.7% to 100% on open-source models are compelling.
- **Resource Optimization:** The ability to achieve substantial accuracy gains in RAG tasks while simultaneously cutting memory consumption by nearly 50% without increasing latency is a major breakthrough. This efficiency is paramount for deploying powerful LLM agents in real-world scenarios where computational resources and response times are critical constraints.

#### 5.4. Empowering Smaller Models

Perhaps one of the most impactful findings is the particularly strong performance enhancement observed in smaller open-source models (e.g., Llama2-7B, Qwen-1.8B). This suggests that SAGE can "punch above its weight," enabling more compact and cost-effective LLMs to achieve capabilities previously limited to much larger, more resource-intensive models. This has significant implications for democratizing access to advanced AI agent technology, making it feasible for researchers and developers with limited resources.

#### 5.5. Improved Reliability and Reduced Errors

The framework's ability to significantly reduce common LLM agent errors, such as "Context Limit Exceeded," "invalid format," and "invalid action," points towards more reliable and less error-prone autonomous systems. This increased stability is crucial for tasks requiring high precision and consistency.

#### 5.6. Biologically Inspired Innovation

The integration of the Ebbinghaus forgetting curve into MemorySyntax offers a novel, psychologically grounded approach to memory management. By intelligently prioritizing and pruning information, SAGE mimics aspects of human cognitive processes, which could inspire further research in designing more intuitive and efficient AI memory systems.

#### 5.7. Future Research Directions

SAGE opens several avenues for future research, including exploring more sophisticated reflection mechanisms, adaptive thresholds for memory optimization, and further theoretical analysis of multi-agent dynamics. It provides a strong foundation for developing truly self-improving and robust AI agents.

In conclusion, SAGE represents a crucial step towards building more intelligent, adaptable, and resource-efficient LLM agents. Its comprehensive approach to self-evolution and memory management, combined with its proven efficacy, positions it to have a profound impact on the development of autonomous AI systems, making advanced agent capabilities more accessible and practical across various domains.
