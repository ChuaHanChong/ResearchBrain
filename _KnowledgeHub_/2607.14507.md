# DRIFT: Drift and Aggregation for Motion Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> DRIFT (Drift and Aggregation for Motion Planning) presents an end-to-end planner for autonomous driving that generates multiple motion hypotheses through one-step conditional drifting in a compact PCA latent space and aggregates them into a single executable trajectory using a novel, label-free, scene-aware mechanism. It achieved 89.6 PDMS and 90.4 EPDMS on the NAVSIM navtest benchmark, representing state-of-the-art performance among non-scorer-based planners, while maintaining real-time inference speeds of 66.43 ms end-to-end.

## Problem

- End-to-end autonomous driving planners often struggle with inherent ambiguity in future driving scenarios, where multiple plausible maneuvers exist, necessitating multi-hypothesis generation.
- Efficiently generating diverse, long-horizon trajectory hypotheses without relying on computationally intensive iterative inference remains a challenge for real-time autonomous driving applications.
- Effectively aggregating multiple trajectory proposals into a single, stable, and executable plan typically requires complex, scenario-specific quality labels for supervision, which are difficult to define and acquire.
- Ensuring geometric compliance with drivable-area boundaries, beyond what imitation learning alone provides, is critical for safety and robust plan execution.

## Method

- Employs a three-stage end-to-end pipeline consisting of a ==V-JEPA== pretrained ==ViT== encoder for scene features, a **DRIFT Decoder** for conditional proposal generation, and a **Scene-Aware Aggregation Head** for final trajectory prediction.
- Generates multiple motion proposals by performing ==one-step conditional drift== in a compact 8-dimensional ==PCA latent space==, using a drift intensity factor (α) to balance exploration and imitation.
- Utilizes a **Scene-Aware Aggregation Head** that pools latent proposal features across different drift intensities and, combined with navigation commands and ego-state information, directly regresses a single physical-space trajectory without relying on trajectory-quality labels.
- Incorporates physical-space safety losses, including an ==arc-length-weighted L1 loss== for expert imitation and a map-derived ==road boundary loss==, to ensure geometric compliance and safety from drivable-area infringements.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on the NAVSIM navtest benchmark among non-scorer-based planners, with scores of **89.6 PDMS** on v1 and **90.4 EPDMS** on v2, surpassing previous baselines like MeanFuser and Drive-JEPA by **0.6-0.9 points**.
- Demonstrated leading performance in Drivable Area Compliance (DAC), No at-fault Collision (NC), and Traffic Light Compliance (TLC) across both NAVSIM protocols, indicating improved safety and rule adherence.
- Maintained real-time feasibility with a total end-to-end inference latency of **66.43 ms** on an NVIDIA RTX 4090, where the proposal generation and aggregation module contributed only **10.82 ms**.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the learned Aggregation Head, which improved PDMS by **5.5 points** compared to simple mean aggregation, and showed positive contributions from both the DRIFT Decoder and the road boundary loss.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - One-step conditional drifting in a compact PCA latent space is an effective and computationally efficient method for generating diverse and context-aware motion proposals, outperforming direct physical-space drifting.
> - A learned aggregation mechanism that directly decodes a final trajectory from pooled latent proposal features is crucial for integrating multi-hypothesis proposals, yielding significant performance gains over simple averaging and operating without explicit trajectory-quality labels.
> - Combining expert imitation with map-derived geometric regularization (road boundary loss) provides effective supervision for end-to-end planning, enhancing drivable-area compliance and safety without the need for complex, scenario-specific quality labels.
> - Leveraging pretrained visual backbones (like V-JEPA) allows downstream planning modules to focus specifically on trajectory proposal generation and scene-aware aggregation, simplifying the overall architecture.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous driving has been advancing through two primary paradigms: traditional modular pipelines and end-to-end (E2E) systems. End-to-end approaches, which directly map sensor observations and navigation context to ego motion, aim to reduce dependencies on hand-designed interfaces and optimize scene representations directly for planning objectives. However, a significant challenge in E2E planning is the inherent ambiguity of future driving behavior; multiple plausible maneuvers can exist for a given scene. An effective planner must accommodate these multi-hypothesis scenarios while adhering to real-time computational constraints.

Within multi-hypothesis planning, various strategies have emerged for generating candidate trajectories. Anchor-based methods and DETR-style set prediction offer computational efficiency but can restrict flexibility and long-horizon coverage. Generative planners, such as those based on diffusion models or autoregressive decoding, provide greater flexibility in candidate generation but typically require iterative inference, which can be computationally intensive. DRIFT positions itself within the one-step generative approach, similar to methods like MeanFuser, which also avoid iterative inference. However, DRIFT differentiates itself by performing generative drift in a compact PCA trajectory latent space rather than directly in physical trajectory space, and its decoder generates multiple proposal features in a single batched pass.

Beyond proposal generation, the subsequent step of converting a set of candidates into a single executable trajectory is another critical design problem. Existing solutions include learned trajectory scoring, where a model selects the highest-scoring candidate or refines proposals iteratively, or direct reconstruction, where proposals are integrated to predict a final trajectory. DRIFT aligns with the direct reconstruction approach, akin to MeanFuser, but employs a distinct proposal interface by pooling latent proposal features before physical decoding and using scene, navigation, and ego-state information to directly predict the final trajectory.

Supervision for the proposal-to-plan step also varies. Scorer-based methods often rely on trajectory-level quality targets derived from planning metrics. In contrast, DRIFT supervises its aggregation head with expert-trajectory imitation and a map-derived boundary regularization. This approach aims to circumvent the need for explicit trajectory-quality labels, which can be complex to obtain and define. Additionally, the geometric compliance of the final trajectory with drivable-area boundaries is a concern not fully addressed by imitation losses alone. DRIFT incorporates a map-derived boundary regularizer to address this, penalizing waypoints outside or too close to the drivable polygon.

The work also leverages advancements in pretrained visual representations. V-JEPA, which learns video features by predicting latent space representations, provides a powerful visual backbone. DRIFT adopts a V-JEPA-pretrained ViT encoder to extract scene features, allowing its trainable planning modules to focus specifically on trajectory proposal generation and scene-aware aggregation, rather than designing a new sensor-fusion backbone. In summary, DRIFT contributes to the E2E autonomous driving landscape by proposing an efficient, one-step, latent-space-based multi-hypothesis planner with a novel aggregation mechanism and a simplified, label-free supervision strategy, built upon a strong visual foundation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an end-to-end trajectory planner that can effectively represent multiple plausible driving behaviors and efficiently produce a single executable trajectory under real-time constraints. This objective is motivated by several identified challenges in current end-to-end autonomous driving systems:

1. **Handling Ambiguity in Driving Scenarios**: Many driving situations, such as intersections or lane changes, present multiple valid courses of action. A planner that predicts only a single trajectory might average over these distinct possibilities, leading to suboptimal or unsafe behavior. Therefore, the system needs to generate and maintain multiple motion hypotheses.

2. **Computational Efficiency for Multi-Hypothesis Planning**: While generating multiple hypotheses is crucial, it must not compromise the real-time performance required for autonomous driving. Existing methods often face trade-offs:
 - Anchor-based or set prediction methods can be efficient but may lack the flexibility to cover diverse, long-horizon behaviors.
 - Generative models like diffusion or autoregressive decoders offer flexibility but frequently involve iterative inference, which can be slow.
 The motivation is to achieve flexible, multi-hypothesis generation in a single, fixed-depth, computationally efficient step.

3. **Effective Aggregation of Proposals into a Single Plan**: Once multiple plausible trajectories (proposals) are generated, the system must consolidate them into a single, stable, and executable trajectory, integrating contextual information such as the current scene, route command, and ego-vehicle state. The challenge is to determine the most appropriate way to combine or select from these proposals. Current approaches often involve learned trajectory scoring, which can require complex, scenario-specific quality labels, or direct reconstruction. The authors aim for a direct reconstruction approach that is efficient and robust.

4. **Simplified Supervision for Aggregation**: Training the proposal-to-plan conversion traditionally relies on explicit trajectory-level quality labels or evaluator scores. Acquiring and defining these labels for diverse and complex scenarios can be resource-intensive and subjective. A key motivation for DRIFT is to develop an aggregation mechanism that can be trained effectively using simpler supervision, specifically expert-trajectory imitation and geometric regularization, thereby avoiding the need for detailed quality labels.

5. **Ensuring Geometric Compliance and Safety**: Imitation losses alone, while effective for learning driving policies, do not always explicitly enforce clearance from drivable-area boundaries. This can lead to predicted trajectories that occasionally infringe upon road limits. The motivation here is to integrate a mechanism that encourages geometric compliance and safety by explicitly regularizing waypoints in relation to the drivable area.

To address these motivations, DRIFT was developed with the following specific objectives:
- To adapt a one-step drifting mechanism to a compact trajectory latent space to generate a fixed-size set of scene-conditioned proposals without iterative denoising.
- To design a scene-aware Aggregation Head that pools latent proposal features across different drift intensities before physical decoding, and which can be trained without requiring trajectory-quality labels.
- To integrate this proposal generation and aggregation with a map-derived boundary regularizer to enhance drivable-area compliance.
- To demonstrate that this integrated planner achieves competitive or superior performance on established autonomous driving benchmarks while maintaining real-time inference speeds.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DRIFT is structured as a three-stage end-to-end planning pipeline designed to predict the ego vehicle's trajectory over a 4-second horizon from historical and current front-view camera frames, navigation commands, and ego-state vectors.

**A. Overall Architecture:**
The pipeline consists of:
1. **ViT Encoder**: A Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder, pretrained with V-JEPA, processes current and past front-view camera frames to extract shared scene features. This leverages robust visual representations learned from video data.
2. **DRIFT Decoder**: This module takes Gaussian noise and performs conditional drift in a latent feature space. It cross-attends to the scene features to generate multiple hidden proposal features, representing different driving intents. An MLP then maps these features to PCA coefficients for supervision and visualization.
3. **Aggregation Head**: This head pools the hidden proposal features, concatenates them with navigation commands and ego-state information, and refines them using cross-attention with the scene features. A final MLP head decodes the result into a single executable trajectory of T future waypoints, including position (x, y) and heading (θ).

**B. DRIFT Decoder (Conditional Trajectory Generation):**
The decoder acts as a conditional generator. It initializes with a Gaussian noise vector (trajectory token) and conditions its generation on the scene features and ego state. This trajectory token is combined with navigation command and ego-state tokens to form a decoder token sequence.
- **Decoder Blocks**: A cascade of blocks processes this sequence. Each block first performs cross-attention between the trajectory token and the ViT scene features. The full decoder token sequence then passes through an MLP-Mixer, facilitating information exchange among the trajectory, navigation, and ego-state tokens.
- **Drift Intensity Modulation**: A drift intensity factor, α ∈, is introduced via Adaptive Layer Normalization (adaLN) within the MLP-Mixer. This mechanism allows the decoder to generate proposals ranging from exploratory (low α, dominated by an unconditional prior) to imitation-biased (high α, pulled towards an expert demonstration).
- **Latent Space Representation**: The decoder produces a hidden proposal feature `h_k` for each noise sample. A learned MLP `f_ψ` then maps `h_k` to PCA coefficients `x_k`. A fixed inverse PCA transform reconstructs the position-only proposal `p_k` from `x_k`. This approach operates in a lower-dimensional PCA latent space to prevent fragmentation of the proposal distribution that was observed in preliminary experiments when drifting directly in high-dimensional physical space. Drift supervision is applied to `x_k`, while the Aggregation Head consumes `h_k`.

**C. Latent Feature Space Drift:**
The decoder is supervised using a drifting mechanism adapted from "Drifting Models." This mechanism drives generated samples towards real data (positive samples) and away from other generated samples (negative samples) via a drifting field.
- **Drift Vector Calculation**: For a given PCA-space representation `x_k`, a drift vector `V_τ(x_k)` is computed. This vector represents displacement towards affinity-weighted positive samples (expert trajectory) and away from negative samples (other generated proposals) using an RBF kernel at temperature τ.
- **Conditional and Unconditional Drift**: The total drift `V_total_k` is an interpolation between an unconditional drift `V_unc_k` (derived from trajectories respecting the current road topology in other scenes) and a conditional drift `V_cond_k` (derived from the expert trajectory in the current scene). The `α` factor controls this interpolation.
- **Drift Training Objective**: The training objective minimizes `||x_k - sg(x_k + V_total_k)||^2`, where `sg` denotes the stop-gradient operation. This allows the decoder to learn to generate `x_k` that, when drifted, align with the target `x_k + V_total_k`.
- **Unconditional Prior Construction**: For each training scene, ground-truth trajectories from other scenes that satisfy the current scene's road boundary constraints are filtered and 32 diverse trajectories are selected as the unconditional sample set. This provides a map-compatible geometric prior.

**D. Scene-Aware Aggregation:**
This module fuses the 48 hidden proposal features `h_k` (32 samples at α=0.5 and 16 samples at α=0.9, generated in one batched pass) into a single executable trajectory.
- **Proposal Pooling**: For each α value, a learnable query token cross-attends to the hidden features in its group, pooling them into a representative token. These pooled tokens form a compact proposal sequence.
- **Aggregation Blocks (AggBlocks)**: This sequence, concatenated with navigation and ego-state tokens, forms a query sequence. AggBlocks process this sequence, using gated cross-attention to attend to the ViT scene features, and MLP-Mixers for token and channel mixing.
- **Final Decoding**: An MLP compresses the final token sequence into a single query vector, which a LayerNorm and MLP head decode into `T` future waypoints `(x, y, θ)`.
- **Direct Regression**: Crucially, the Aggregation Head directly regresses `(x, y, θ)` from the hidden proposal features `h_k`, rather than selecting or averaging PCA coefficients `x_k` or their physical reconstructions `p_k`. This allows the final plan to transcend the fixed PCA subspace, avoiding truncation and reconstruction errors. The aggregation weights and output head are optimized directly by the imitation and road-boundary losses, without reliance on trajectory-quality labels.

**E. Physical-Space Safety Losses:**
To improve execution accuracy and ensure drivable-area compliance, the final aggregated trajectory is supervised in physical coordinates with two losses:
- **Arc-length-weighted L1 Loss (L_traj)**: This loss, adapted from Drive-JEPA, reweights samples in a batch based on the ground-truth trajectory length, giving higher weights to shorter (e.g., low-speed or congested) trajectories.
- **Map-derived Road Boundary Loss (L_road)**: This acts as a local geometric regularizer.
 - Waypoints outside the drivable polygon receive a constant penalty `C_out`.
 - Inside waypoints are penalized based on their differentiable minimum distance `d` to the nearest road boundary, using a piecewise function: `r(d) + q(d)` for `d < m_safe` (inverse distance + quadratic), `q(d)` for `m_safe ≤ d < m_soft` (quadratic), and zero beyond `m_soft`.

**F. Training Procedure:**
- **PCA Basis Fitting**: An 8-dimensional PCA basis is fitted offline on the 16-dimensional `(x, y)` position components of ground-truth trajectories from the `navtrain` dataset. This basis remains fixed.
- **Two-Stage Training**:
 1. **Stage 1 (Decoder Training)**: The V-JEPA-pretrained ViT encoder is frozen. The DRIFT Decoder (including `f_ψ`) is trained for 50 epochs using only the `L_drift` objective. 32 proposals are generated per scene from independent Gaussian noise and randomly sampled `α` ∈.
 2. **Stage 2 (Full Model Joint Optimization)**: The ViT encoder is unfrozen, and all learnable components (ViT, Decoder, `f_ψ`, Aggregation Head) are jointly optimized for another 50 epochs. The total objective `L_total = λ1 L_traj + λ2 L_drift + λ3 L_road` is used, with weights `λ1=1`, `λ2=0.1`, `λ3=0.2`.
- **Inference Configuration**: During inference, fixed `α` values (0.5 and 0.9) are used. The decoder samples 32 Gaussian noise vectors at `α=0.5` and 16 at `α=0.9`, generating 48 hidden proposal features in a single batched pass for the Aggregation Head.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DRIFT was conducted on the NAVSIM `navtest` benchmark, which provides a non-reactive pseudo-simulation environment using `nuPlan` sensor logs and map annotations. The benchmark uses two protocols: NAVSIM v1 (PDMS) and NAVSIM v2 (EPDMS), with the latter broadening evaluation to additional rule compliance and temporal behavior metrics.

**A. Quantitative Performance:**
- **Overall Scores**: DRIFT achieved 89.6 PDMS on NAVSIM v1 and 90.4 EPDMS on NAVSIM v2. These scores represent a new state-of-the-art among the non-scorer-based planners compared in the study.
- **Comparison to Alternatives**:
 - DRIFT surpassed MeanFuser and Drive-JEPA by 0.6 PDMS under v1 and 0.9 EPDMS under v2.
 - Under v1, DRIFT demonstrated leading performance in Drivable Area Compliance (DAC) and Ego Progress (EP), while maintaining near-optimal scores in No at-fault Collision (NC) and saturated Comfort (Comf.).
 - Under v2, DRIFT ranked first in NC, DAC, Traffic Light Compliance (TLC), and Time-to-Collision (TTC), in addition to EPDMS. It remained competitive in Driving Direction Compliance (DDC), Lane Keeping (LK), and Extended Comfort (EC). The consistency across both protocols suggests that the performance gains are robust to a broader set of evaluation metrics.
- **Performance Profile**: The strong performance in DAC across both protocols is attributed to the map-aware elements of DRIFT: the unconditional proposal set and the boundary loss regularizer. The competitive EP scores indicate that these geometric biases do not hinder effective route progression, suggesting a balanced approach rather than trading progress for compliance or vice versa.

**B. Efficiency:**
- **Latency**: On an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, the trajectory generation module (DRIFT Decoder, alpha pooling, and Aggregation Head) processed all 48 proposals in 10.82 ms. The full model inference, including the visual backbone, took 66.43 ms. This indicates that the proposal-to-plan computation accounts for approximately 16% of the total end-to-end latency, demonstrating its efficiency for real-time applications.

**C. Qualitative Results:**
- Visualizations across diverse urban maneuvers (turns, lane changes, straight driving on varying road widths) showed that the final trajectory effectively followed the demonstrated expert behavior. Simultaneously, the intermediate proposals exhibited locally plausible variations, with their spread correlating with both the drift intensity factor (α) and scene constraints (e.g., narrower regions leading to less spread). Lower α values generally resulted in greater proposal spread, consistent with an increased influence of the unconditional prior.

**D. Robustness Analysis:**
- The model's sensitivity to the stochasticity of Gaussian noise input was assessed by evaluating the trained model on `NAVSIM navtest` using 10 different random seeds. The PDMS showed a maximum absolute deviation of 0.08 points from the mean (89.56), and EPDMS showed a maximum absolute deviation of 0.04 points from the mean (90.36). These results indicate that the final aggregated trajectory scores are stable under stochastic proposal sampling, suggesting the robustness of the planning performance.

**E. Ablation Study:**
- **Learned Aggregation vs. Mean Aggregation**: Replacing the learned Aggregation Head with a simple arithmetic mean of the 48 PCA-decoded proposals resulted in a substantial decrease in PDMS from 89.6 to 85.1. This degradation was observed across multiple metrics (NC, DAC, EP, TTC), emphasizing that effective aggregation requires more than just generating plausible proposals; it needs a mechanism that can intelligently weigh and integrate scene-dependent relevance.
- **Contribution of DRIFT Decoder**: Removing the DRIFT Decoder led to a decrease of 0.3-0.4 PDMS points, indicating its positive contribution to the overall planning performance by enabling multi-hypothesis generation.
- **Contribution of Road Boundary Loss (L_road)**: Disabling the `L_road` resulted in a 0.1-0.2 PDMS point reduction. While smaller, this consistent improvement highlights the role of the boundary regularizer in enhancing geometric compliance.
- The ablation study demonstrated that both the DRIFT Decoder and the road boundary loss contribute positively to the integrated planner, with their effects being interacting rather than strictly additive.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DRIFT research presents several significant contributions to the field of end-to-end autonomous driving, with notable potential impacts:

1. **Advancement in End-to-End Planning Performance**: By achieving state-of-the-art results (89.6 PDMS and 90.4 EPDMS) on the NAVSIM `navtest` benchmark among non-scorer-based planners, DRIFT demonstrates an effective approach for improving overall planning quality. The consistent superior performance across both NAVSIM v1 and v2 protocols, which encompass a broad range of safety, compliance, and comfort metrics, indicates a robust and balanced planning capability.

2. **Efficient Multi-Hypothesis Trajectory Generation**: The adoption of one-step drifting in a compact PCA trajectory latent space, rather than iterative denoising or direct physical space generation, offers a computationally efficient method for generating diverse motion hypotheses. This design allows for the generation of 48 proposals in a single batched pass within the DRIFT Decoder, which is crucial for real-time autonomous driving applications.

3. **Novel and Efficient Proposal Aggregation**: The Scene-Aware Aggregation Head provides a new mechanism for fusing latent proposal features directly into a single executable trajectory. This approach avoids the limitations of PCA-subspace constraints for the final output and, importantly, achieves effective aggregation without requiring costly and complex trajectory-level quality labels for supervision. This reduction in annotation burden could accelerate the development and deployment of E2E planning systems.

4. **Enhanced Geometric Compliance and Safety**: The integration of a map-derived boundary regularizer (`L_road`) significantly improves drivable-area compliance (evidenced by leading DAC scores). This explicit geometric supervision complements imitation learning by preventing predicted trajectories from infringing upon road boundaries, contributing to safer driving behavior without sacrificing progress.

5. **Practical Real-time Feasibility**: With the trajectory generation and aggregation module running in just 10.82 ms on an NVIDIA RTX 4090, DRIFT showcases a design compatible with the strict real-time requirements of autonomous vehicles. This efficiency makes the proposed architecture a viable candidate for integration into production-level E2E driving systems.

6. **Foundation for Future Research**: The work validates the concept of evaluator-label-free aggregation as a practical alternative in efficient proposal-based planning. This opens new avenues for future research, particularly in evaluating such methods in more dynamic, interactive, and pseudo-closed-loop simulation environments, where the ego vehicle's actions influence other traffic participants. Further investigation into quantifying proposal diversity and analyzing aggregation behavior directly could also provide deeper insights.

In essence, DRIFT provides a compelling solution for the intricate problem of multi-hypothesis motion planning in end-to-end autonomous driving, balancing efficiency, accuracy, compliance, and reduced supervision complexity.
