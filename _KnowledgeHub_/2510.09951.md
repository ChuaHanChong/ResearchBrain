# Emergence of Spatial Representation in an Actor-Critic Agent with Hippocampus-Inspired Sequence Generator

> [!summary] Summary
> A study from the University of Freiburg presents a model where hippocampal sequences and spatial representations emerge from the intrinsic recurrent circuitry of the CA3 region, acting as a temporal memory buffer. When integrated into an actor-critic reinforcement learning agent for egocentric navigation, the model develops biologically plausible place fields and remapping, exhibiting enhanced navigation performance, especially under sparse sensory input, compared to standard recurrent networks.

## Problem

- Existing computational models often describe the function of hippocampal sequences but lack a direct, mechanistic explanation for their generation from intrinsic circuitry.
- Understanding how robust spatial representations are formed for navigation under sparse or noisy sensory input, given the extreme sparsity of Dentate Gyrus (DG) activity.
- Integrating neurobiologically inspired mechanisms into modern reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks to evaluate their functional utility in goal-directed tasks.

## Method

- An actor-critic RL agent was implemented for ==egocentric visual navigation== in a 3D maze environment.
- The Dentate Gyrus (DG) was modeled as a sparsification module, converting visual inputs into sparse representations consistent with biological DG activity.
- The CA3 region was implemented as a fixed, prewired linear recurrent neural network (shift register) designed to intrinsically generate fixed 'theta sequences' from sparse DG inputs, acting as a temporal memory buffer.

## Results

- The CA3 sequence-generating agent reliably solved continuous maze navigation tasks, outperforming LSTM and state-space model agents under sparse DG input conditions, and demonstrating robustness to increasing input noise.
- Through learning, units in the DG, CA3, and decoder layers developed spatially localized place fields, which exhibited remapping with changes in reward location and showed distance-dependent spatial tuning in CA3.
- Analysis confirmed the causal role of units with high spatial information for agent performance, and population-level spatial kernels in the decoder layer demonstrated the emergence of pronounced and smoothly varying spatial representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intrinsic recurrent circuitry, inspired by hippocampal CA3 dynamics, can mechanistically generate temporal sequences that are crucial for building robust spatial representations, even from sparse and transient inputs.
> - A synergistic relationship exists between sparse sensory encoding (DG-like activity) and sequence-generating dynamics (CA3-like), which leads to superior navigation performance under specific sparse input conditions.
> - Biologically plausible hippocampal features, such as localized place fields, distance-dependent spatial kernels, and remapping in response to goal changes, naturally emerge within a goal-directed learning agent leveraging this sequence-generating mechanism.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The mammalian hippocampus is recognized for its role in spatial navigation and memory, particularly through the activity of place cells, which fire when an animal is in a specific location, and the sequential firing of these cells, known as theta sequences. This research operates within the computational neuroscience and reinforcement learning domains, aiming to provide a mechanistic understanding of how these hippocampal spatial representations emerge.

Existing computational models of hippocampal function offer various interpretations:
- **Successor Representations (SRs):** These models propose that place cells encode predictive states, representing future occupancy from a given location (Stachenfeld et al., 2017; Mattar & Daw, 2018).
- **Reservoir Models:** Some models emphasize the role of pre-existing dynamics in shaping place cell sequences (Leibold, 2020).
- **Probabilistic Approaches:** These conceptualize place cells as latent states inferred from successive sensory inputs (Raju et al., 2024).
- **Self-supervised Methods:** These refine spatial tuning by leveraging the temporal smoothness of trajectories (Wang et al., 2024).

While these approaches can reproduce place-like activity and sequential patterns, the authors note that they often do not explicitly address the fundamental origin of hippocampal sequences. Bottom-up studies, conversely, suggest that structured representations arise from specific circuit dynamics and physiological constraints.

This work proposes a complementary perspective, focusing on the intrinsic recurrent circuitry of the hippocampal CA3 region as a primary generator of sequences. It positions these sequences as a temporal memory buffer crucial for navigation, especially under sparse sensory input. This approach connects to concepts in machine learning such as state-space models and structured linear RNNs, which aim to preserve long-range information by expanding inputs into a high-dimensional temporal feature space. The paper aims to bridge the gap between abstract computational models and neurobiological mechanisms by implementing a neurobiologically inspired recurrent structure within a modern reinforcement learning framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to propose and test a mechanistic and parsimonious interpretation for the emergence of hippocampal sequences and spatial representation. The authors hypothesize that these sequences arise from intrinsic recurrent circuitry within the CA3 region of the hippocampus. This circuitry, they propose, propagates transient input over extended temporal horizons, functioning as a temporal memory buffer. This mechanism is posited to be particularly advantageous when reliable sensory evidence is sparse, reflecting conditions often encountered in natural navigation.

Specific motivations underpinning this objective include:
- **Mechanistic Explanation for Hippocampal Sequences:** Current models often describe the function or correlational properties of hippocampal sequences, but less frequently provide a direct, bottom-up account of their generation. The authors aim to demonstrate how fixed, recurrent connections inspired by CA3 anatomy can intrinsically generate these sequences.
- **Addressing Sparse Sensory Input:** The ecological reality of navigation frequently involves relying on a limited number of salient landmarks amidst noisy sensory information. The extreme sparsity of activity in dentate gyrus (DG) granule cells, which project to CA3, further motivates the study of how robust spatial representations can be built from sparse inputs. The authors propose that sparse input, rather than being detrimental, synergizes with sequence generation to create high-capacity, low-interference codes.
- **Integration with Reinforcement Learning:** By embedding this hippocampus-inspired sequence generator into an actor-critic reinforcement learning agent for egocentric visual navigation, the authors seek to evaluate its functional utility in a goal-directed task. This allows for testing whether biologically plausible mechanisms can confer computational advantages in a modern machine learning context.
- **Reconciling Biological Observations with Computational Function:** The work is motivated by observations of place fields, distance-dependent spatial kernels, and task-dependent remapping in the hippocampus. The authors aim to show that these neurobiological phenomena can emerge naturally through learning within their proposed model, and that these emergent properties are causal to the agent's performance.
- **Comparison with Modern Recurrent Architectures:** A further motivation is to compare the performance and representational properties of the proposed CA3-inspired sequence generator with established recurrent neural networks like LSTMs and state-space models (SSMs). This comparison aims to delineate the specific conditions (e.g., input sparsity) under which the bio-inspired architecture offers an advantage.

In essence, the research is motivated by a desire to provide a unified mechanistic account for the origin of hippocampal sequences, their utility under sparse sensory conditions, and their role in facilitating robust spatial representation and navigation within a reinforcement learning framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors implemented an actor-critic agent tasked with egocentric visual navigation in a continuous virtual maze environment. The core of their approach lies in a hippocampus-inspired neural architecture integrated into the agent's perception-action loop.

#### 3.1. Virtual Environment

- **Platform:** DeepMind Lab (Beattie et al., 2016) was used to simulate a 3D continuous maze.
- **Layout:** The environment featured randomly placed walls on 15% of tiles within a 19x19 tile grid, ensuring complex spatial relationships not inferable solely from visual similarity. Wall layouts were fixed during training episodes, but varied for generalization tests (new maps, blocked paths, new reward locations).
- **Task:** The agent was initialized at a random location and had to navigate to an invisible reward zone. Episodes terminated upon reaching the goal or after a maximum of 7200 frames (900 action steps).
- **Action Space:** A reduced set of 5 actions (forward, strafe left/right, look left/right + forward) was used.
- **Depth Information:** The decoder module also received down-sampled average depth information (10 horizontal pixels) as a direct input, intended to aid collision avoidance and mimic non-hippocampal sensorimotor habits. This input bypassed the DG-CA3 pathway.

#### 3.2. Visual Processing

- **Encoder:** A pretrained ResNet (He et al., 2015) was used as a visual encoder to process the agent's first-person perspective input (96x72x3 pixels). This encoder was fixed during the hippocampus model training, mimicking general cortical visual preprocessing.

#### 3.3. Hippocampus Model

The hippocampus model comprised two main components:
- **Dentate Gyrus (DG) as a Sparsification Module:**
 - **Function:** Modeled as a linear projection of the visual encoder's features (256-dimensional output mapped to 16 features, F=16).
 - **Sparsification:** Batch normalization (running estimates, retention rate 0.95) followed by a high activation threshold (τ = 2.43) was applied to the projected features. This process aimed to achieve approximately 2.5% activity, consistent with the sparse firing rates of biological DG granule cells.
- **CA3 as a Sequence-Generating Shift Register:**
 - **Function:** Modeled as a linear Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) designed to propagate sparse DG inputs as fixed "theta sequences." The recurrent connections within CA3 were fixed (prewired) to isolate the effects of intrinsic sequence generation.
 - **Parameters:** Each of the F=16 DG features was assigned a dedicated prewired sequence of length ℓ. The total sequence length ℓ was defined as L + (R - 1), where L represents the number of theta cycles and R the number of active units per cycle. The default setting used was L=64 and R=8, resulting in ℓ=71 per feature.
 - **Single-feature Dynamics:** For a single feature, the CA3 state `x_t` (vector of length ℓ) updated as `x_(t+1) = S x_t + J u_t`, where `u_t` is the DG input. `S` is a shift operator matrix, and `J` injects the input into the first R slots of the sequence. This means a transient input activates R units, and this activity then shifts one position along the sequence at each timestep.
 - **Multiple-feature Dynamics:** For F features, the dynamics extended using Kronecker expansion: `X_(t+1) = A X_t + B u_t`, where `A = I_F ⊗ S` and `B = I_F ⊗ J`. `I_F` is an identity matrix for the F features. This implies that each DG feature triggers an independent sequence. The total CA3 state size was F * ℓ = 16 * 71 = 1136 units.

#### 3.4. Actor-Critic Network

- **Architecture:** The flattened activity of all 1136 CA3 units, combined with the 10 depth pixels, was fed into a decoder module. This module consisted of two fully connected (FC) layers with ReLU activation.
- **Readout:** Actions (5 discrete actions) and value estimates were computed as linear readouts from the second decoder layer.
- **Training Objective:** The agent was trained using a standard advantage actor-critic objective, specifically Asynchronous Proximal Policy Optimization (APPO) (Espeholt et al., 2018), which incorporates PPO's clipped surrogate objective (Schulman et al., 2017) with IMPALA's V-trace corrections (Petrenko et al., 2020) for stable distributed training.

#### 3.5. Evaluation and Analysis

- **Behavioral Metrics:** Success Rate (fraction of goal-reached episodes), Trajectory Length (frames to goal), and Stable Success (rate of 100 consecutive successful episodes).
- **Transfer Learning:** Tested by changing reward location, generating new maze maps, or blocking existing paths.
- **Comparative Analysis:** Performance was compared against agents using LSTM cores, randomly initialized fixed RNNs, and various State-Space Models (SSM_LegS, SSM_LegT, SSM_LagT) of comparable state size, under both sparse and dense input conditions (dense input involved removing batch normalization and high thresholding in the DG module).
- **Representational Analysis:**
 - **Occupancy Maps:** Visualization of time spent at different locations.
 - **Place Field Analysis:** Activity maps of individual units in DG, CA3, and decoder layers to visualize spatial tuning. Quantified by Spatial Information (SI) (Skaggs et al., 1992) and place field entropy. Orthogonalization of DG inputs was also measured.
 - **Population-Level Representations:** A novel method inspired by population vector (PV) correlation and representational dissimilarity analysis was used to measure the mean PV correlation as a function of spatial displacement, yielding spatial kernels.
 - **Causality Test:** Permuting weights of high-SI vs. low-SI decoder units to assess the causal role of spatial information for performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings concerning the behavioral performance and emergent neural representations within the hippocampus-inspired actor-critic agent.

#### 4.1. Behavioral Performance

- **Robust Navigation:** The agent with the CA3 sequence generator (L=64, R=8) reliably solved the continuous maze navigation task, achieving robust performance metrics (success rate, trajectory length, stable success) after approximately 350 million training frames (Figure 2A).
- **Dependence on Sequence Length:** Reducing the sequence length (L) in the CA3 module resulted in inferior and less stable performance. In the extreme case of L=1, R=1 (no sequence generation, effectively a feedforward architecture), the agent did not achieve robust navigation, indicating the functional importance of sequence generation.
- **Transfer Learning:** The fully trained agent demonstrated generalization capabilities by adapting to new reward locations (within 50 million frames), new maze maps (within 150 million frames), and blocked paths, suggesting the formation of a generalizable representation of the environment (Figure 2B).
- **Advantage under Sparse Input:** Under sparse DG input (batch normalized and thresholded activity), the CA3 agent learned faster and achieved higher success rates compared to agents using LSTM cores, randomly initialized RNNs, or State-Space Models (HiPPO-LegS, HiPPO-LegT, HiPPO-LagT) of comparable size (Figure 2C, Table 1).
- **Regime-Dependent Advantage:** This performance advantage was specific to sparse input conditions. Under dense input (without sparsification), LSTM agents performed better, consistent with prior reports, while the CA3 module performed worse than LSTMs, HiPPO-LegS, and random RNNs (Figure 2D, Table 1). This highlights a specific synergy between sparse representations and intrinsic sequence generation.
- **Noise Robustness:** The DG+CA3 agent exhibited greater robustness to increasing levels of Gaussian noise added to the input image pixels compared to the dense LSTM agent (Figure A5, Table A4), further supporting its synergy with sparse, reliable cues.

#### 4.2. Occupancy and Navigation Strategy

- **Strategic Trajectories:** During training, the agent developed stable navigation strategies, often converging to habituated paths and visiting salient locations before proceeding to the goal. For example, it learned to navigate around obstacles to reach the lower right corner before heading to the reward (Figure 3).
- **Comparison to LSTM Strategy:** In contrast, the LSTM agent under dense sensory input exhibited fewer converging trajectories, potentially relying more on direct visual search due to readily available goal information in the dense visual input (Figure 3, Figure A11).

#### 4.3. Place Field Analysis

- **Localized Place Fields:** Through learning, units in the DG and CA3 layers, as well as the first decoder layer, developed spatially localized firing fields (place fields), aligning with neurobiological observations (Figure 4A, B). LSTM hidden units, conversely, showed non-localized tuning (Figure 4C).
- **DG Orthogonalization:** The learning process led to increasing orthogonalization of the DG inputs to CA3, resulting in unique population activity representations for individual locations over time (Figure 5A). This was not observed in LSTM agents (Figure A14).
- **Distance-Dependent Spatial Tuning in CA3:** CA3 units activated later in a sequence (further from the DG input) showed broader spatial tuning fields (Figure 4A), quantified by higher entropy, consistent with experimental reports (Jung & McNaughton, 1993; Parra-Barrero et al., 2021) (Figure 5B).
- **Remapping:** Place fields of DG and CA3 units stabilized over training and exhibited remapping (shifts in place field center of mass) when the reward location was changed, a phenomenon observed in biological hippocampus (Figure A10).

#### 4.4. Spatial Information (SI) Analysis

- **SI Increase across Layers:** Spatial Information (bits per step) generally increased across hierarchical layers of the network, peaking in the first decoder layer, indicative of the development of a neural spatial representation (Figure A8).
- **Optimal Sequence Activation:** CA3 units activated approximately 16 steps after the DG input exhibited larger SI values (Figure 5C), which correlated with the reasonable performance of agents with L=16 (Figure 2A). Units activated later in a sequence showed decreasing SI.
- **Causal Role of SI:** Selective permutation of output weights confirmed the causal role of high-SI units for performance. Permuting the 32 units with the highest SI in Decoder layer 1 significantly impaired success rate and increased trajectory length, whereas permuting low-SI units had no significant impact.

#### 4.5. Population-Level Representations

- **Spatial Kernels:** Analysis of population vector (PV) correlations as a function of spatial displacement (spatial kernels) revealed that all layers (DG, CA3, Decoder) developed some dependence on distance. CA3 kernels were smoother than DG kernels (Figure 6A).
- **Pronounced Decoder Kernels:** Decoder layer 1 developed the most pronounced and smoothly varying spatial tuning kernels, exhibiting clear distance dependency (Figure 6A, C).
- **Remapping in Kernels:** Upon changing the reward location, kernels became less sharply defined, indicating disruption of representations despite behavioral adaptation (Figure 6A).
- **LSTM Kernel Differences:** In contrast, most layers in the dense LSTM agent did not show strong displacement-dependent spatial kernels, with only the LSTM output units developing a refined but non-isotropic kernel (Figure 6B, Figure A15).
- **Generalizable Knowledge:** Population vector correlations across training epochs suggested that the agent acquired generalizable knowledge about the arena's spatial layout, as similarity between fully trained and new reward networks was higher than between naive and trained networks (Figure A9).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to both computational neuroscience and artificial intelligence by proposing and validating a minimal, neurobiologically inspired model of hippocampal function.

**Biological Significance:**
- **Mechanistic Account of Theta Sequences:** The study offers a parsimonious mechanistic explanation for hippocampal theta sequences, positing their origin in intrinsic recurrent circuitry of CA3, which can maintain activity in the absence of continuous external input. This complements views that attribute sequences solely to sensory drive or planning.
- **Alignment with Neurobiological Phenomena:** The model reproduces key hallmark features of hippocampal activity, including the emergence of localized place fields, progressive orthogonalization of DG outputs, distance-dependent population kernels, and dynamic remapping in response to goal changes. These emergent properties align with experimental observations regarding sparse DG activity and CA3 sequences.
- **Synergy of Sparse Coding and Sequence Dynamics:** The work highlights a crucial synergy: sparse, informative inputs from DG, when expanded and buffered by CA3's sequence-generating dynamics, are more effective for robust navigation than dense inputs processed by conventional RNNs. This reflects the ecological reality of relying on few reliable landmarks.
- **Biological Predictions:** The model predicts that larger or sparser environments would necessitate longer sequences for successful navigation. It also suggests that hippocampal spatial representations might rely heavily on intrinsic sequence-generating circuits, with experience primarily tuning feedforward and readout connections, offering an explanation for place cell persistence after entorhinal cortex lesions.

**Computational and AI Impact:**
- **Inductive Bias for Reinforcement Learning:** The hippocampus-inspired CA3 shift register acts as a simple yet effective inductive bias for reinforcement learning agents in navigation tasks, particularly when dealing with sparse egocentric visual inputs. This fixed, sequence-based reservoir provides long-horizon history without the undifferentiated feature mixing found in general-purpose RNNs.
- **Regime-Dependent Architectural Utility:** The comparative analysis demonstrates that different recurrent architectures are suited to different sensory regimes: sparse coding synergizes with sequential expansion, while dense input may favor more general mixing-oriented recurrent dynamics (e.g., LSTMs). This insight can inform the design of more efficient and context-aware deep RL architectures.
- **Bridging Neuroscience and AI:** The framework exemplifies how modern reinforcement learning can serve as a testbed for computational neuroscience hypotheses. By embedding biologically inspired circuits within end-to-end learning agents, it allows for studying brain computation in the context of a full perception-action loop, offering a middle ground between unconstrained black-box models and heavily engineered, top-down representational schemes.
- **Alternative to Successor Representations:** The CA3 representations, while policy-dependent and exhibiting temporal ordering similar to SRs, arise purely from a fixed, physiology-inspired architecture rather than learned predictive maps or TD updates. This offers an alternative perspective on how anticipatory structures can emerge.
- **Implications for Structured Memory Models:** The CA3 shift register resonates with recent developments in machine learning, such as structured state-space models (SSMs) and linear attention, suggesting that hippocampal-like motifs might inform principles of efficient long-range computation in artificial neural networks.

**Future Directions:**
The authors propose several extensions, including incorporating local plasticity into the DG-CA3 pathway or CA3 readouts for greater biological realism, investigating interactions with path integration from medial entorhinal cortex, and exploring hierarchical coordination with theta sequences in other brain areas. On the ML side, combining sparse sequential reservoirs with learned SSMs could lead to hybrid architectures adaptable across input regimes. The simplicity and interpretability of the model also suggest approaches for algorithms that minimize internally measured trajectory length.
