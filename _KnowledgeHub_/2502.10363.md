# BeamDojo: Learning Agile Humanoid Locomotion on Sparse Footholds

> [!summary] Summary
> BeamDojo, a reinforcement learning framework developed by Shanghai AI Laboratory and collaborators, enables agile humanoid locomotion over sparse footholds by integrating a sampling-based foothold reward, a double critic architecture, and a two-stage training process. It achieves high success rates across diverse sparse terrains in simulation and demonstrates robust zero-shot transfer to a physical Unitree G1 robot, performing under heavy payloads and external disturbances.

## Problem

- Existing model-based and learning approaches struggle with the extreme precision and unique polygonal foot geometry required for humanoid locomotion on sparse, risky terrains.
- Reinforcement learning faces challenges with sparse reward signals and inefficient learning due to frequent early episode terminations from missteps on unstable terrain.
- Reliably acquiring accurate perceptual information (e.g., terrain height maps) from sensors in real-world environments is difficult due to noise and inaccuracies.

## Method

- Introduces a continuous, ==sampling-based foothold reward== designed for polygonal feet, penalizing improper foot placement based on sampled points.
- Employs a ==double critic architecture== to decouple the dense locomotion rewards from the sparse foothold reward, ensuring stable and efficient learning of both.
- Proposes a ==two-stage reinforcement learning== process: Stage 1 trains on flat terrain with task terrain perception for efficient exploration, followed by Stage 2 fine-tuning on actual challenging terrains with hard termination.

## Results

- Achieved a **91.67%** success rate on hard Stepping Stones and high success across various sparse terrains in simulation, along with significantly lower foothold errors (e.g., **7.79%**).
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a Unitree G1 robot, achieving success rates like **4/5** on Stepping Stones and **5/5** on Gaps in real-world tests.
- Exhibited high agility (maintaining average speeds up to **0.88 m/s** on sparse terrain) and robustness, successfully navigating with a **10 kg** payload, recovering from external pushes, and showing misstep recovery.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling sparse, task-specific rewards from dense locomotion rewards via a double critic significantly improves learning stability and efficiency for complex control tasks.
> - A two-stage training strategy, starting with "soft" terrain dynamics to enable extensive exploration and sample collection, is crucial for learning precise maneuvers that would otherwise be hampered by early episode terminations.
> - Systematic domain randomization, particularly for perceptual data like elevation maps, is essential for robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer in fine-grained locomotion tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Traversing risky terrains with sparse footholds—such as stepping stones or narrow beams—represents a formidable challenge for legged robots, demanding exceptional precision in perception, planning, and control. While significant progress has been made, particularly for quadrupedal robots, these advancements often fall short when applied to humanoid systems due to their unique complexities.

**Existing Approaches and Their Limitations:**

1. **Model-Based Methods:** Traditional approaches often rely on hierarchical controllers that decompose the task into distinct stages of perception, planning, and control. While effective in controlled environments, these methods are highly sensitive to deviations from model assumptions, which limits their robustness in dynamic, real-world scenarios. For humanoids, the polygonal shape of their feet (unlike the point-foot model often used for quadrupeds) introduces complex half-space constraints, significantly increasing computational burden for online planning.

2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Hybrid Methods:** Recent advancements have seen the emergence of end-to-end learning frameworks and hybrid approaches that combine RL with model-based controllers.
 - **For Quadrupeds:** These methods have demonstrated impressive capabilities, often relying on depth cameras for exteroceptive observations. However, depth cameras typically have a narrow field of view, sometimes restricting robots to backward movement, and require additional image processing modules to bridge the sim-to-real gap.
 - **For Humanoids:** Despite recent successes in general humanoid locomotion tasks like walking, stair climbing, and parkour, these learning-based methods still struggle with the extreme precision required for fine-grained foothold control on sparse terrains. The inherent instability and higher degrees of freedom of humanoids amplify these challenges.

**Specific Challenges for Learning-Based Humanoid Locomotion on Sparse Footholds:**

- **Sparse Reward Signals:** The reward for successful foot placement is typically sparse, only provided upon completion of a sub-process (e.g., landing a foot). This makes it difficult for RL agents to assign credit to specific actions and states, hindering efficient learning.
- **Inefficient Learning:** A single misstep on sparse terrain often leads to early episode termination during training. This drastically limits the robot's opportunities for trial-and-error exploration and hinders the acquisition of successful samples.
- **Perceptual Limitations:** Obtaining reliable and accurate perceptual information (e.g., terrain height maps) in real-world environments is challenging due to sensor noise, occlusions, and odometry inaccuracies.

**BeamDojo's Position:**

BeamDojo directly addresses these critical limitations, aiming to push the boundaries of humanoid locomotion on challenging terrains. It distinguishes itself by:
- **Tailoring for Humanoids:** Introducing a novel sampling-based foothold reward specifically designed for the polygonal foot geometry of humanoids.
- **Overcoming Sparse Rewards & Inefficient Learning:** Proposing a double critic architecture and a unique two-stage RL training process that tackles the issues of sparse rewards and premature episode termination, significantly enhancing learning efficiency.
- **Robust Perception:** Employing a LiDAR-based elevation map for enhanced, robot-centric perception, enabling stable forward and backward movement, unlike depth camera-based systems.
- **Claiming Novelty:** The authors state that BeamDojo is "the first learning-based method to achieve fine-grained foothold control on risky terrains with sparse footholds." This positions the work as a significant advancement in bridging the gap between general humanoid walking capabilities and agile, precise locomotion in highly constrained environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable **agile and robust humanoid locomotion across challenging sparse footholds**, such as stepping stones and narrow balancing beams. The authors aim to equip humanoid robots with the ability to precisely process perceptive information, execute accurate foot placements within safe areas, and maintain stable base locomotion throughout the process.

The motivation for this work stems from the significant limitations observed in existing legged locomotion strategies when applied to humanoid robots navigating fine-grained sparse terrains:

1. **Unique Challenges of Humanoid Morphology:** Unlike quadrupeds, humanoids possess polygonal feet and have higher degrees of freedom, combined with an inherently unstable bipedal morphology. This renders many existing model-based and point-foot-centric RL approaches inadequate. Model-based methods incur heavy computational burdens due to complex foot-ground contact constraints, while standard RL foothold rewards designed for point feet are unsuitable for polygonal feet.

2. **Inefficiencies in Learning-Based Approaches:** While RL has shown promise in general humanoid locomotion, its application to sparse footholds is hampered by:
 - **Sparse Reward Signals:** The "credit assignment problem" where the robot receives feedback only after completing a foot-landing sequence, making it hard to learn intermediate actions.
 - **High Sample Inefficiency:** The fragility of humanoid balance on sparse terrains means a single misstep can lead to early termination of an episode during training, severely limiting the robot's ability to explore and learn from its mistakes. This "disruption of trajectories" makes acquiring successful foothold samples extremely difficult.

3. **Perceptual Gaps:** Reliable perceptual information is crucial for precise foot placement. However, sensory limitations and environmental noise present significant challenges for perception systems in real-world deployments.

In summary, the key motivation is to overcome these fundamental limitations to allow humanoid robots to achieve human-like agility and precision when traversing complex, dangerous terrains, ultimately paving the way for their deployment in real-world applications such as disaster response, exploration, or construction in hazardous environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BEAMDOJO is a novel reinforcement learning (RL) framework designed to address the unique challenges of enabling agile humanoid locomotion on sparse footholds. It integrates several key innovations:

#### 3.1. Foothold Reward for Polygonal Feet

Traditional foothold rewards, often designed for point-shaped feet, are inadequate for humanoid robots with polygonal feet. BeamDojo introduces a **sampling-based foothold reward** (`r_foothold`) to accurately evaluate foot placement. This is achieved by:
- Sampling `n` points on the sole of the robot's foot.
- For each sample point, determining its height relative to the terrain (`d_ij`).
- A penalty is incurred if `d_ij` falls below a predefined depth tolerance (`ϵ`), indicating improper foot placement outside a safe area. The penalty is proportional to the number of unsafe sampled points, making it a *continuous* reward that encourages maximizing the overlap between the foot and the safe foothold area. This continuous nature is crucial for more granular learning.

#### 3.2. Double Critic for Sparse Reward Learning

To effectively manage the balance between dense locomotion rewards (crucial for gait regularization) and the sparse, task-specific foothold reward, BeamDojo employs a **double critic architecture** based on Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
- **Two Separate Critic Networks:** Two value networks (`V_ϕ1`, `V_ϕ2`) are trained independently.
 - `V_ϕ1` estimates the value function for a group of *dense locomotion rewards* (e.g., velocity tracking, base height, orientation, joint regularization).
 - `V_ϕ2` estimates the value function for the *sparse foothold reward*.
- **Independent Updates:** Each critic is updated independently using a temporal difference (TD) loss.
- **Synthesized Advantage:** Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) is used to calculate advantages (`Â_i,t`) for each reward group. These advantages are then individually normalized and synthesized into a single overall advantage, which is used to update the policy.
- **Benefit:** This decoupling prevents the sparse, potentially noisy, foothold reward from disrupting the learning of regular locomotion gaits, allowing for more stable and efficient optimization.

#### 3.3. Two-Stage RL for Terrain-Aware Locomotion

To mitigate the problem of early termination on complex terrains, which hinders exploration and sample efficiency, BeamDojo proposes a novel **two-stage RL training approach**:

- **Stage 1: Soft Terrain Dynamics Constraints Learning:**
 - **Concept:** The robot is trained on a *flat terrain* (`F`) but receives *perceptual information (elevation map)* corresponding to the *target task terrain* (`T`). This creates an "imagined" challenging environment while the robot physically walks on a safe, flat surface.
 - **Reward Decoupling:** Locomotion rewards are derived from the flat terrain `F`, while the foothold reward (`r_foothold`) is calculated based on the true task terrain `T`. Critically, missteps (points outside the safe foothold on `T`) incur a penalty but *do not terminate* the episode.
 - **Benefit:** This allows for extensive trial-and-error exploration, enabling the robot to accumulate numerous successful foothold placement samples that would be rare in an environment with hard termination. It primarily develops foundational terrain-awareness skills without disruptive restarts.

- **Stage 2: Hard Terrain Dynamics Constraints Learning:**
 - **Concept:** The policy learned in Stage 1 is fine-tuned directly on the *actual task terrain* (`T`) in simulation.
 - **Termination:** Missteps now *result in immediate termination*, forcing the robot to learn precise and safe locomotion strategies under realistic constraints.
 - **Benefit:** This stage refines the policy, making it robust and accurate for real-world deployment by enforcing strict adherence to true terrain dynamics.

#### 3.4. Training in Simulation

- **Observation Space:** Includes desired command velocities (linear x, y, angular yaw), proprioceptive feedback (base angular velocity, gravity direction, joint positions/velocities), an egocentric 15x15 LiDAR-based elevation map, and the previous action for temporal context.
- **Action Space:** 12 target joint positions for the lower-body, controlled by a PD controller.
- **Terrain and Curriculum Design:** Five types of sparse foothold terrains are designed, with Stones Everywhere used for Stage 1, and Stepping Stones/Balancing Beams for Stage 2 fine-tuning. Stepping Beams and Gaps are used for zero-shot generalization evaluation. A curriculum progressively increases terrain difficulty (smaller stones, narrower beams, larger gaps) as the robot achieves success.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer (Domain Randomization):** Extensive domain randomization is applied to observations, humanoid physical properties, and terrain dynamics. Crucially, **four types of elevation map measurement noise** are introduced to mimic real-world LiDAR data inaccuracies: vertical measurement noise, map rotation (simulating odometry errors in roll, pitch, yaw), foothold extension (simulating data smoothing), and map repeat (simulating update delays). This systematic randomization is vital for bridging the sim-to-real gap of perceptual data.

#### 3.5. Real-world Deployment

- **Hardware:** Unitree G1 humanoid robot equipped with a Jetson Orin NX for onboard computation and a Livox Mid-360 LiDAR.
- **Elevation Map Construction:** Raw LiDAR point clouds are processed using FAST-LIO for precise LiDAR-inertial odometry, followed by robot-centric elevation mapping to generate a robust, grid-based height map.
- **Control Loop:** Elevation map data is published at 10 Hz, the learned policy runs at 50 Hz, and the PD controller operates at 500 Hz for smooth actuation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of BEAMDOJO is rigorously demonstrated through extensive simulation and real-world experiments, benchmarking against various baselines and through detailed ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **Quantitative Performance (Table II):** BEAMDOJO consistently achieved the highest success rates (R_succ) and traverse rates (R_trav) across all four evaluated terrains (Stepping Stones, Balancing Beams, Stepping Beams, Gaps) and both medium and hard difficulty levels.
 - For instance, on hard Stepping Stones, BEAMDOJO achieved 91.67% R_succ, significantly outperforming PIM (46.67%), Naive (0.33%), Ours w/o Soft Dyn (42.00%), and Ours w/o Double Critic (55.67%).
 - The "Naive" implementation, lacking the proposed innovations, largely failed, demonstrating the necessity of the framework's components.
 - **Zero-Shot Generalization:** Remarkably, BEAMDOJO showed strong zero-shot generalization to "Stepping Beams" and "Gaps" terrains, which were not part of its explicit training set, indicating the robustness and adaptability of the learned policy.

- **Ablation Analysis:**
 - **Foot Placement Accuracy (Fig. 5):** BEAMDOJO achieved significantly lower foothold error (E_foot) (e.g., 7.79% on Stepping Stones) compared to ablations. This validates the effectiveness of the sampling-based foothold reward and the double critic in promoting precise foot placement.
 - **Learning Efficiency (Fig. 6):** BEAMDOJO converged much faster to higher terrain difficulty levels during training. Both the two-stage training (Stage 1 with soft dynamics) and the double critic contributed to this efficiency, with the two-stage approach being particularly impactful by enabling continuous exploration without early termination.
 - **Gait Regularization (Table III):** The double critic framework proved crucial for maintaining smooth and regular gaits. Designs without the double critic exhibited poorer smoothness and feet air time, as the sparse foothold reward would otherwise interfere with the learning of dense locomotion regularization rewards.
 - **Foot Placement Planning (Fig. 7):** Visualizations showed that BEAMDOJO, enabled by the double critic, allowed for smoother, more anticipatory foot placement planning. The robot adjusted its foot trajectory over a longer horizon, unlike reactive stepping seen in the ablation without the double critic.

#### 4.2. Real-world Experiments

- **Sim-to-Real Transfer (Fig. 8):** BEAMDOJO demonstrated high zero-shot sim-to-real transfer success rates (e.g., 4/5 on Stepping Stones, 5/5 on Gaps). Crucially, an ablation without height map domain randomization ("Ours w/o HR") showed significantly lower success rates, underscoring the critical role of the carefully designed perceptual randomization in bridging the sim-to-real gap.
- **Bidirectional Locomotion:** The use of a LiDAR-based elevation map enabled the robot to move smoothly and stably both forward and backward on risky terrains, a capability often limited in depth camera-based systems.
- **Agility Test (Table IV):** The humanoid robot demonstrated considerable agility, maintaining high average speeds (e.g., 0.88 m/s with a 1.0 m/s command) while traversing sparse footholds. Performance degraded beyond 1.25 m/s, indicating the practical limits on such challenging terrains.
- **Robustness Test (Fig. 9):** BEAMDOJO exhibited remarkable robustness:
 - **Heavy Payload:** Successfully maintained agile locomotion and precise foot placements while carrying a 10 kg payload (approx. 1.5 times torso weight).
 - **External Force:** Demonstrated recovery capabilities from external pushes, transitioning from two-leg to single-leg support and back to stable standing.
 - **Misstep Recovery:** Showed robust recovery even after initial missteps caused by occlusions or lack of prior terrain scanning.

#### 4.3. Extensive Studies and Analysis

- **Foothold Reward Design (Table V):** The continuous, sampling-based foothold reward in BEAMDOJO led to more accurate foot placements and higher success rates compared to binary or coarse reward designs.
- **Curriculum Design (Table VI):** The curriculum learning approach significantly improved performance and generalization ability across varying terrain difficulties, preventing the model from struggling when learning from scratch on hard terrains.
- **Command Design:** The decision to forego a heading command in Stage 2 (allowing the robot to learn to face forward solely from perceptive observation) improved real-world robustness compared to a design relying on continuous yaw corrections from noisy odometry.
- **Generalization to Non-Flat Terrains (Fig. 10):** With minor adaptations, BEAMDOJO also generalized well to stairs (8/10 success) and slopes (10/10 success), indicating broader applicability.
- **Failure Cases (Fig. 11) and Limitations:** Performance degrades sharply on extremely small stones (10 cm, half foot length) and very large steps (55 cm, roughly leg length). These extreme cases highlight challenges in maintaining balance on minimal contact areas and executing maximal strides, which the current reward structure might not fully optimize for. The main limitations include reliance on LiDAR perception accuracy (jitter, drift), the trade-off between noisy measurements and dynamic terrain changes, and currently limited handling of significant foothold height variations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

BeamDojo represents a substantial leap forward in the field of humanoid robot locomotion, particularly for navigating complex and hazardous environments. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Enabling Agile Humanoid Locomotion on Challenging Terrains:** BeamDojo is presented as the first learning-based method to achieve fine-grained foothold control for humanoid robots on risky, sparse terrains. This is a critical milestone, moving humanoids beyond basic flat-ground walking towards capabilities required for real-world applications in unstructured environments. The ability to precisely traverse stepping stones, balance beams, and generalize to gaps significantly expands their operational envelope.

2. **Addressing Core RL Challenges in Robotics:** The framework innovatively tackles two long-standing problems in applying reinforcement learning to complex robotic tasks:
 - **Sparse Rewards:** The sampling-based, continuous foothold reward tailored for polygonal feet provides a more informative signal, guiding the learning process effectively.
 - **Inefficient Learning due to Early Termination:** The novel two-stage RL approach, with its "soft terrain dynamics constraints" in Stage 1, fundamentally improves sample efficiency. By allowing continuous exploration and accumulating successful experiences, it accelerates the learning process for highly precise tasks.
 - **Reward Balancing:** The double critic architecture effectively decouples dense locomotion rewards from sparse task-specific rewards, preventing interference and promoting stable learning of both gait regularization and specific task objectives. This modular approach could inspire similar solutions for other multi-objective, mixed-reward robotic control problems.

3. **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer and Real-World Performance:** The meticulous design of domain randomization, particularly for the LiDAR-based elevation map, is a key contribution to successful sim-to-real transfer. This allows policies trained in simulation to perform reliably on physical hardware. The demonstrated robustness to heavy payloads, external disturbances, and misstep recovery further validates its practical utility, bringing humanoids closer to deployment in unpredictable, human-centric environments (e.g., disaster relief, search and rescue, logistics in constrained spaces).

4. **Advancing Perceptive Locomotion:** By leveraging LiDAR for a comprehensive, robot-centric elevation map, BeamDojo overcomes limitations of vision-only systems (like depth cameras) in terms of field of view and bidirectional movement. This showcases the power of multi-modal sensing and robust state estimation for complex navigation tasks.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** While acknowledging limitations (e.g., extreme terrain variations, perfect perception), BeamDojo provides a strong foundation. It opens avenues for future work on:
 - Developing more generalized controllers that seamlessly handle varying foothold heights and require explicit footstep planning.
 - Improving robust perception against more severe noise and dynamic changes.
 - Integrating higher-level reasoning and planning with low-level agile control for truly autonomous navigation in highly complex, unknown environments.

In conclusion, BeamDojo significantly advances the state-of-the-art in humanoid locomotion by offering a robust, efficient, and innovative learning framework. Its success in enabling agile and precise movement on sparse footholds in the real world is a testament to its practical utility and pushes humanoid robots one step closer to becoming versatile and reliable agents in challenging real-world scenarios.
