# Geometry without Position? When Positional Embeddings Help and Hurt Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at King Abdullah University of Science and Technology (KAUST) performed a token-level analysis revealing that positional embeddings (PEs) in Vision Transformers (ViTs) function as geometric priors, fundamentally shaping the spatial organization of representations. Their work demonstrates that misalignment of PE-induced spatial kernels across different views is a primary cause of geometric degradation, which can be reversed through consistent PE alignment.

## Problem

- Vision Transformers (ViTs) often exhibit limitations in multi-view geometric reasoning, especially with significant viewpoint changes, despite advances in visual representation.
- The self-attention mechanism in ViTs is permutation-invariant, requiring positional embeddings (PEs) to inject spatial information, but their exact causal role in facilitating or hindering spatial reasoning was underexplored.
- Prior research presented inconsistent findings on PEs' impact, sometimes associating them with improved performance and other times with degrading geometric alignment and correspondence accuracy.

## Method

- Developed a conceptual framework interpreting ==positional embeddings (PEs)== as inducing an ==implicit spatial kernel== that structures the representation space, governing token interactions based on position.
- Designed controlled token-level experiments, including ==overlapping image crops== and ==stereo correspondence probing==, to systematically decouple visual content from positional cues.
- Conducted extensive analyses across ==14 foundation ViT models==, encompassing various PE strategies (==absolute==, ==relative==, ==rotary==) and training paradigms, to generalize findings.

## Results

- Experiments with overlapping image crops showed a **pronounced degradation** in cross-view token similarity due to PE misalignment, even when visual content was identical.
- Removing PEs or shuffling them inconsistently across views **significantly collapsed** stereo correspondence (e.g., End-Point Error increased **beyond one patch size**), while pairwise shuffling of absolute PEs **largely preserved** it.
- A training-free ==token re-indexing procedure==, which aligns PE kernels across views, **consistently restored** multi-view token similarities across **all tested models**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Positional embeddings are not merely auxiliary indices but fundamentally function as geometric priors, inducing an implicit spatial kernel that determines how tokens interact spatially.
> - Geometric correspondence in ViTs critically depends on the consistency of PE-induced spatial kernels across different views, rather than solely on visual content.
> - The observed degradation in multi-view geometric consistency is often reversible through simple, training-free PE alignment, indicating that geometric information is present but inaccessible when PEs are inconsistent.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have significantly advanced visual representation learning, but their capacity for geometric reasoning remains an area of ongoing investigation. The self-attention mechanism, a core component of ViTs, is inherently permutation-invariant, meaning it lacks an explicit spatial understanding. Consequently, ViTs rely on positional embeddings (PEs) to inject spatial information, enabling the model to understand the location and arrangement of image patches.

Early analyses of ViTs presented mixed findings regarding their geometric capabilities. Raghu et al. (2021) suggested that standard ViTs could retain local spatial representations. However, subsequent studies, such as Probe3D (El Banani et al., 2024), indicated that large-scale foundation models often exhibit limitations in multi-view correspondence, particularly under significant viewpoint changes, suggesting a deficiency in geometric generalization. This contrast is further highlighted by the observation that single-view depth estimation methods (e.g., DepthAnything, Yang et al., 2024) can leverage pre-trained DINOv2 representations directly, whereas multi-view approaches (e.g., VGGT, Wang et al., 2025a; π3, Wang et al., 2025b) typically require retraining DINO-based backbones to achieve cross-view consistency. This discrepancy suggests that ViTs might encode geometry in a view-dependent manner, rather than maintaining a universally consistent spatial understanding tied solely to visual content.

A prominent hypothesis attributes this behavior to the design of PEs. Various PE formulations exist, including absolute coordinate embeddings (Carion et al., 2020; Chu et al., 2023; Touvron et al., 2021), relative positional biases (Li et al., 2021b), and rotary encodings (Su et al., 2024). While PEs are recognized for stabilizing training and enhancing recognition performance, they have also been associated with degrading geometric alignment and correspondence accuracy. For instance, DeiT (Touvron et al., 2021) demonstrated that inappropriate PE schemes can lead to overfitting to spatial layouts. LoFTR (Sun et al., 2021) observed that the persistent application of PEs across multiple layers could hinder feature matching. Li et al. (2021b) noted that the choice between absolute and relative PEs critically affects convergence and localization. More recently, PE-Fields (Bai et al., 2025) indicated that positional fields can influence volumetric reasoning in generative models, suggesting a more profound role for PEs in geometric understanding.

Despite these insights, the precise mechanisms through which PEs shape geometric structure within ViTs and their conditions for facilitating or hindering spatial reasoning have remained largely underexplored. This paper addresses this gap by providing a systematic, token-level analysis to clarify the causal role of PEs in governing the spatial structure of ViT representations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate the extent to which positional embeddings (PEs) determine the geometric structure of visual representations within Vision Transformers (ViTs), and to identify the conditions under which PEs either facilitate or hinder spatial reasoning.

This objective is motivated by several considerations:
1. **Reframing PEs as Geometric Priors**: The paper posits that PEs are not merely auxiliary token indices but fundamentally function as geometric priors that shape the spatial organization of the representation space. This perspective aims to move beyond viewing PEs as simple coordinates to understanding them as integral components that define how tokens interact based on their positions.
2. **Addressing Inconsistent Prior Findings**: Existing literature offers mixed insights into the role of PEs in geometric tasks. While PEs are crucial for spatial awareness, they have also been observed to degrade geometric alignment in certain scenarios. The research seeks to reconcile these seemingly contradictory observations by proposing a unified framework that clarifies the underlying causal mechanisms.
3. **Understanding View-Dependent Geometry**: The observation that ViTs, particularly large-scale foundation models, may encode geometry in a view-dependent manner (as suggested by the need for retraining backbones in multi-view systems) motivates an investigation into whether PEs contribute to this behavior. The hypothesis is that ViT representations define spatial relations relative to the model's own viewpoint, which is inherently tied to the PEs.
4. **Developing Token-Level Diagnostics**: Traditional methods often evaluate PEs indirectly through downstream task performance. This work aims to decouple visual content from positional structure explicitly at the token level. This requires developing novel token-level diagnostic tools to precisely measure how multi-view geometric consistency in ViT representations depends on the consistency of PEs.
5. **Systematic Analysis Across Models**: To ensure broad applicability and generalizability of findings, the research is motivated to conduct extensive experiments across a diverse set of 14 foundation ViT models, encompassing various PE strategies (absolute, relative, rotary) and training paradigms (supervised, self-supervised, vision-language modeling, masked image modeling).

By addressing these motivations, the study intends to provide a clearer empirical basis for understanding the role of PEs as a causal mechanism governing spatial structure in ViT representations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research comprises a conceptual framework that interprets PEs as spatial kernels, followed by a series of controlled token-level experiments designed to systematically probe the influence of PEs on geometric consistency in ViTs.

#### 3.1. Conceptual Framework: PEs as Spatial Kernels

The paper's core conceptual contribution is interpreting positional embeddings as inducing an implicit spatial kernel. Since self-attention is permutation-invariant, PEs are introduced to provide spatial order. The authors analyze three main PE variants:

- **Absolute Positional Encodings**: Each token $x_i$ is augmented with a unique embedding $p_i$ representing its absolute grid position ($q_i = W_Q(x_i + p_i)$, $k_j = W_K(x_j + p_j)$). This leads to position-dependent attention patterns.
- **Relative Positional Encodings**: Position is injected directly into the attention logits as a bias $b(\Delta_{ij})$ dependent on the relative displacement $\Delta_{ij} = j-i$ ($α_{ij} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} q_i^T k_j + b(\Delta_{ij})$). This aims to enhance translation invariance.
- **Rotary Positional Encodings (RoPE)**: Queries and keys are rotated in a complex-valued space based on position-dependent angles ($q_i \leftarrow R_{\theta_i}(q_i)$, $k_j \leftarrow R_{\theta_j}(k_j)$). The dot product then depends on the relative phase $\theta_j - \theta_i$, encoding angular or offset-based relationships multiplicatively.

The "kernel interpretation" expands the dot-product attention with additive PEs into four components: content-content, content-to-position, position-to-content, and position-position. Under the assumption of approximately zero-mean and normalized features in pre-trained ViTs, the expected attention logit $E[α_{ij}]$ is approximately proportional to $p_i^T M p_j + g(\Delta_{ij})$, where $M = W_Q^T W_K$. For RoPE, the expected dot product for a 2D feature pair is $λ_b \cos(\Deltaϕ_{b_{ij}})$, depending solely on the relative phase difference. This analysis suggests that PEs induce an implicit spatial interaction pattern, or "spatial kernel," that structures the representation space itself, governing how tokens at different spatial locations attend to one another.

#### 3.2. Experimental Design

The experimental methodology focuses on controlled interventions that decouple visual content from positional cues, using 14 foundation ViT models with diverse PE strategies (Table 1).

#### 3.2.1. Content vs. Position: Multi-View Geometry Degradation

- **Experiment 1: Overlapping Image Crops (Section 4.1)**
 - **Purpose**: To isolate the effect of PE misalignment when visual content is identical.
 - **Setup**: Two overlapping crops ($I_1$, $I_2$) are generated from an image, such that the overlapping region contains identical pixels but misaligned PEs due to different cropping origins.
 - **Measurement**: Cosine similarity of cross-view tokens within the overlapping region for increasing horizontal displacements $\Delta x \in \{1, 2, 3\}$.
 - **Dataset**: Imagenette (Howard, 2019) test split.

- **Experiment 2: Stereo Correspondence Probing (Section 4.2)**
 - **Purpose**: To measure fine positional anchoring at the sub-patch scale across views.
 - **Setup**: For rectified stereo pairs, a 4D correlation volume is computed between left ($F_L$) and right ($F_R$) tokens to measure pairwise feature similarity. A soft-argmax operation extracts disparity estimates.
 - **PE Settings**: Four conditions are tested:
 1. **Vanilla PE**: Original positional embeddings.
 2. **Zeroed Out PE**: Positional information is entirely removed.
 3. **Shuffled PE**: PEs are randomly permuted independently for each view.
 4. **Pairwise Shuffled PE**: PEs are randomly permuted, but the same permutation is applied consistently across both views.
 - **Metrics**: End-Point Error (EPE) for correspondence precision and Recall@n ($n \in \{1, 5\}$) for epipolar consistency.
 - **Dataset**: Spring dataset (Mehl et al., 2023) at 448x448 resolution.
 - **Visualization**: Epipolar peak responses and semantically meaningful feature visualizations using AnyUp (Wimmer et al., 2025).

#### 3.2.2. Consistent PEs for Multi-View Geometry

- **Experiment 3: Restoring PE Consistency via Token Re-Indexing (Section 5.1)**
 - **Purpose**: To determine if aligning PEs alone can restore spatial anchoring without retraining.
 - **Setup**: Using the overlapping crop setting from Experiment 1, a training-free token re-indexing procedure is applied. Tokens corresponding to the same scene-space location in the overlap are assigned identical positional indices.
 - **Measurement**: Token-wise cosine similarity before and after re-indexing.

- **Experiment 4: Implicit Positional Alignment in VGGT (Section 5.2)**
 - **Purpose**: To understand how a successful multi-view system (VGGT, Wang et al., 2025a) handles PE inconsistencies without modifying the backbone.
 - **Setup**: Layer-wise analysis of cross-view token similarity within VGGT's aggregator, using the overlapping-crop dataset.
 - **Measurements**:
 1. Cosine similarity between corresponding tokens across views as a function of aggregator depth (24 layers) for increasing horizontal displacements $\Delta x \in \{1, 2, 3\}$.
 2. Positional decoding accuracy (row and column indices) for individual token features using a lightweight MLP at each aggregation layer.

#### 3.3. Technical Details (Appendix)

The appendix provides additional technical details on:
- **Positional Weighting**: Modulating PE strength for different PE types.
- **4D Correlation Volume**: Construction and soft-argmax operation for dense affinity estimation.
- **Metrics**: Formal definitions of EPE and Epipolar Recall@n.
- **Additional Probing Experiments**: PE strength sweep, positional awareness probing, and spatial identity preservation analyses, which further characterize the nature of PE-induced spatial biases.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the role of positional embeddings (PEs) in Vision Transformers (ViTs):

#### 4.1. PEs as Spatial Kernels

The theoretical analysis, supported by visualizations (Figure 7 in supplementary material), demonstrated that PEs induce an implicit spatial kernel that governs the spatial interaction patterns between tokens. This kernel can be non-stationary (absolute PEs) or stationary/phase-stationary (relative/rotary PEs), influencing how tokens attend to each other based on their relative positions.

#### 4.2. ViT Representations Fail Under Identical Content with Mismatched PEs (Experiment 1)

- **Observation**: Experiments with overlapping image crops (Table 2) showed a pronounced degradation in cross-view token similarity as the horizontal offset between crops increased. This occurred despite the overlapping regions containing identical visual content.
- **Conclusion**: The degradation in spatial anchoring is attributed to mismatched PEs rather than inherent differences in visual representation, indicating that ViT representations are sensitive to their internal positional reference frames.

#### 4.3. Positional Mismatch in Stereo Correspondence (Experiment 2)

- **Observation (Vanilla PEs)**: With intact PEs, most models demonstrated sub-patch correspondence, characterized by low End-Point Error (EPE) and well-localized epipolar peaks (Table 3, Figure 1). This suggests that PEs provide crucial spatial anchoring.
- **Observation (Zeroed Out/Shuffled PEs)**: Removing PEs entirely ("Zeroed Out PE") or shuffling them inconsistently across views ("Shuffled PE") led to a significant collapse in correspondence. EPE typically increased beyond one patch size, Recall@n dropped substantially, and epipolar responses became diffuse or unstable.
- **Observation (Pairwise Shuffled PEs)**: For models with absolute PEs, applying the same permutation of PEs to both views ("Pairwise Shuffled PE") largely preserved correspondence, even though the resulting spatial organization was visually distorted (Table 3).
- **Conclusion**: Geometric correspondence in ViTs depends critically on the consistency of PEs across views. The success of pairwise shuffling for absolute PEs implies that geometric information is maintained relative to a consistent, albeit arbitrarily permuted, PE frame. BEiT was an outlier, showing poor geometric correspondence even with vanilla PEs.

#### 4.4. Restoring PE Consistency via Token Re-Indexing (Experiment 3)

- **Observation**: A training-free token re-indexing procedure, which aligns PE kernels across views by assigning identical positional indices to scene-space corresponding tokens, consistently restored token similarities across all tested models (Table 4).
- **Conclusion**: The observed degradation in multi-view geometric consistency is reversible through simple PE alignment. This suggests that the geometric information is present in the representations but becomes inaccessible or misaligned when PEs are inconsistent across views.

#### 4.5. Implicit Positional Alignment in VGGT (Experiment 4)

- **Observation (Token Similarity)**: In the VGGT aggregator, cross-view token similarity initially decreased in early layers (6-10) with increasing displacement, but consistently recovered and converged to near-perfect alignment in later layers (Figure 3, upper).
- **Observation (Positional Decodability)**: Positional indices remained highly decodable throughout the aggregator, with a significant surge in prediction accuracy around layer 13, approaching 100% (Figure 3, lower).
- **Conclusion**: VGGT achieves robust multi-view representation by implicitly inferring and aligning a canonical latent coordinate system that reconciles the initial view-dependent PEs, rather than explicitly modifying the backbone PE scheme.

#### 4.6. Additional Findings (Supplementary Material)

- **PE Strength Sweep**: Reducing PE strength weakened spatial similarity and shifted models towards globally homogeneous representations, except for BEiT, which lacked locality even with strong PEs (Figure 6).
- **Positional Awareness**: Most ViTs showed strong positional awareness, with absolute and relative PEs allowing almost perfect linear recovery of positions. Swin/SwinV2 and rotary embedding methods (DINOv3, MLCD) exhibited slightly weaker decodability for absolute 2D positions (Table 5).
- **Spatial Identity**: Without PEs, tokens at different positions became nearly interchangeable in feature space, indicating a collapse of positional identity. With PEs, reconstruction similarity decreased with offset, preserving spatial distinctiveness (Table 6).

In summary, the results indicate that PEs are critical for structuring spatial relations in ViT representations. Geometric consistency in multi-view tasks is highly dependent on the consistency of these PE-induced spatial kernels across views, and misalignment of these kernels is a primary cause of geometric degradation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a fundamental re-evaluation of the role of positional embeddings (PEs) in Vision Transformers (ViTs), identifying them as a causal mechanism that governs spatial structure and influences geometric reasoning. The findings offer several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Unified Interpretation of PE Failures**: The work offers a unified framework for understanding previously observed phenomena where PEs were reported to degrade geometric alignment or cross-view consistency. These failures are reinterpreted not as inherent flaws of PEs themselves, but as consequences of misaligned PE-induced spatial kernels across different views or crops. This shifts the focus from PE presence or absence to PE consistency.

2. **Causal Role of PEs**: By systematically decoupling visual content from positional structure through controlled experiments (e.g., overlapping crops, token re-indexing), the study establishes PEs as a direct causal mechanism for shaping geometric representations. It demonstrates that ViT representations are anchored to an internal positional reference frame defined by PEs, and misalignment of this frame is a key determinant of multi-view geometric degradation.

3. **Fundamental Insight for Multi-View Geometry**: The research highlights that geometric alignment in ViT representations does not solely derive from visual content but crucially depends on the consistency of PEs across views. The successful restoration of token similarity through simple, training-free token re-indexing underscores that geometric information is often preserved within the representation but becomes inaccessible or misaligned due to inconsistent positional frames.

4. **Implications for Model Design**: The findings suggest that future architectural advancements for multi-view tasks should prioritize addressing PE consistency and alignment, rather than solely focusing on improving visual feature extraction. Designing PE schemes that incorporate geometric constraints, such as explicit multi-view alignment or scene-aware positional systems, could lead to more robust and generalized geometric reasoning in ViTs. This could involve developing PEs that are intrinsically tied to scene-space coordinates rather than image-space coordinates.

5. **Diagnostic Tool Development**: The token-level diagnostics introduced in this paper provide valuable tools for researchers to analyze and diagnose spatial consistency and geometric properties within ViT representations. These tools can help in understanding the strengths and weaknesses of different PE strategies and architectural inductive biases.

6. **Understanding Foundation Models**: The analysis of 14 foundation ViT models, including DINOv2, SAM, and CLIP, provides insights into how these widely used models internally handle spatial and geometric information. The layered analysis of VGGT, for instance, reveals how some multi-view systems implicitly learn to reconcile PE inconsistencies through their aggregation mechanisms.

In conclusion, this research significantly advances the understanding of how positional embeddings fundamentally impact the spatial reasoning capabilities of Vision Transformers. By clarifying the causal relationship between PE consistency and geometric performance, it provides a basis for more principled analysis and design of positional mechanisms, potentially leading to the development of more geometrically aware and robust vision models for complex multi-view tasks.
