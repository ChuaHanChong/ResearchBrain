# From One Hand to Multiple Hands: Imitation Learning for Dexterous Manipulation from Single-Camera Teleoperation

> [!summary] Summary
> A single-camera iPad teleoperation system for dexterous manipulation: it estimates the operator's hand shape and builds a customized simulated robot hand matching that hand's kinematics, removing online retargeting. Collected trajectories are retargeted offline to Schunk, Adroit, and Allegro hands, then used for demo-augmented RL. Collection reaches ~60 successful demos per hour, and policies transfer robustly to a real Allegro hand.

## Problem

- VR headsets, wired gloves, and multi-camera rigs make dexterous teleoperation accurate but costly, device-heavy, and hard to scale across many operators.
- Online motion retargeting is unintuitive and slow, forcing operators to imagine future robot-hand configurations and adding highly variable per-frame latency during control.
- Demonstrations gathered on one specific robot hand cannot be reused for imitation learning on other hands with different geometry, degrees of freedom, or finger count.

## Method

- **Customized robot hand**: MediaPipe plus FrankMocap estimate SMPL-X shape and pose, and an articulated 45-DoF hand with the operator's own kinematics is built in the ==SAPIEN== simulator.
- ==Confidence-based PD control== uses deviation of per-frame shape parameters from the initialization shape as a Gaussian confidence score, lowering stiffness when perception is unreliable.
- ==Offline keypoint retargeting== optimization with a temporal-smoothness term maps customized-hand trajectories to Schunk, Adroit, and Allegro hands; inverse dynamics recovers actions for ==DAPG== training.

## Results

- User study with 17 operators: customized hand reached **78.9%** stage-1 success on Relocate versus **44.7%** for Allegro, and about **60** versus **10** successful demos per hour.
- Demo-augmented policy gradient beat pure RL on nearly all task-robot pairs, for example Relocate tomato soup can on Adroit at **95.0%** versus **41.7%**.
- Real Allegro plus XArm-6 transfer: **77.7%** versus **22.2%** on known objects, **66.6%** versus **18.5%** on novel objects, and **44.4%** versus **3.6%** on Flip.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Matching the interface to the operator's own morphology beats retargeting at capture time; deferring the mapping offline yields both smoother control and reusable multi-robot data.
> - Human-like demonstrations regularize policies away from simulator-exploiting contact hacks, so the imitation-versus-RL gap widens sharply after sim-to-real transfer.
> - Multi-finger force closure gives robustness to shape and friction variation, letting pose-only observations generalize to objects never seen during training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous manipulation with multi-finger robot hands represents a significant challenge in robotics due to complex contact patterns and high degrees of freedom (DoF). Traditional manual controller design for such tasks is difficult, and while Reinforcement Learning (RL) has shown promise, it often suffers from high sample complexity and can lead to unexpected or unsafe behaviors in high-DoF systems.

Imitation Learning (IL) from human demonstrations offers a viable alternative by providing external constraints and improving sample efficiency. Existing teleoperation systems for collecting human demonstrations commonly rely on Virtual Reality (VR) devices or wired gloves. While these methods can provide accurate data, they are often expensive, inflexible, and not easily scalable. Vision-based teleoperation offers a more scalable and cost-effective solution by removing the need for special wearable devices.

However, vision-based teleoperation introduces its own set of challenges, particularly motion retargeting—converting human hand motion to robot hand motion. If the retargeting is not intuitive (e.g., mapping a five-finger human hand to a four-finger robot hand), it can hinder human operator control and data collection efficiency. Furthermore, demonstrations collected for a specific robot hand are typically not transferable to other robot hand morphologies.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing a vision-based teleoperation system designed for scalability, intuitive control, and the generation of demonstrations applicable to multiple robot hands. It aims to bridge the gap between efficient human demonstration collection and the application of that data to train robust policies for diverse robotic platforms, ultimately improving the Sim2Real transfer of learned skills.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are:

1. **Develop a scalable and intuitive teleoperation system for collecting dexterous manipulation demonstrations:** The authors aim to overcome the limitations of existing VR/wired glove systems by creating a single-camera, vision-based teleoperation setup that is less intrusive and more accessible, requiring only an iPad and a computer. This system is motivated by the need for efficient and large-scale data collection.
2. **Facilitate intuitive human-robot interaction during teleoperation:** A key motivation is to avoid unstable and unintuitive online motion retargeting, which can impede human operators. The proposed solution is to generate a "customized robot hand" in simulation that mirrors the operator's actual hand kinematics and morphology, providing a direct and intuitive control interface.
3. **Enable the generation of demonstrations for multiple diverse robot hands from a single data collection effort:** The research seeks to overcome the problem of demonstrations being specific to one robot model. By collecting data with the customized robot hand, the authors aim to translate these demonstrations offline to various commercially available robot hands (e.g., Schunk, Adroit, Allegro), allowing a single human demonstration to be utilized for training policies across different robot platforms, even those with varying DoF or number of fingers.
4. **Improve the robustness and transferability of learned policies to real-world robots:** The authors hypothesize that incorporating human-like behaviors derived from demonstrations into RL training will result in policies that are less prone to "hacking" simulator physics and exhibit more natural, stable, and robust performance when deployed on physical robots, thus addressing the Sim2Real gap.

The motivation stems from the recognition that while imitation learning holds significant potential for dexterous manipulation, current data collection methods for complex tasks are either cumbersome (VR/wired gloves) or lack flexibility and transferability across different robot hardware (vision-based systems). This work intends to provide an end-to-end framework that integrates efficient, intuitive data collection with a flexible demonstration translation process and a robust policy learning pipeline, ultimately leading to more practical applications of dexterous robot manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates three main steps: (i) customized hand teleoperation, (ii) multi-robots demonstration translation, and (iii) demonstration-augmented policy learning.

### 4. Customized Hand Teleoperation

This step focuses on efficiently collecting 3D human demonstrations using a minimal hardware setup.

- **Hardware Setup:** The system uses an iPad's front camera to stream RGB-D video of the human operator's hand at 25 fps. A laptop with an RTX 2070 GPU processes the video, achieving a processing time of less than 30ms per frame.
- **Hand Detector:** RGB-D frames are fed into a hand detector built upon MediaPipe and FrankMocap. MediaPipe first detects the hand's bounding box. The cropped images are then processed by FrankMocap to estimate hand pose and shape parameters, represented by the SMPL-X model. The Perspective-n-Point (PnP) algorithm is used to determine the wrist's transformation to the camera. Outputs include wrist pose, hand pose parameters, and hand shape parameters.
- **Customized Robot Hand Construction:** A unique aspect is the dynamic construction of a customized robot hand in a physical simulator (SAPIEN). Based on the estimated hand shape parameters of the operator, an articulated robot model is built that matches the human hand's kinematics structure (e.g., joint skeleton, shape, and size). This customized hand has 45 DoF, aligning with the SMPL-X model. Primitive shapes (boxes for palm, capsules for fingers) are used for efficient collision detection.
- **Control Interface:** The customized robot hand is controlled using a PD controller. The estimated human hand pose parameters, passed through a low-pass filter, serve as position targets for the robot's joints. To mitigate perception errors inherent in visual teleoperation, a confidence score is incorporated into the PD control. This score is derived from the error between the current estimated hand shape parameters and a "ground-truth" shape established during initialization. When perception error is high, the controller's stiffness is reduced, preventing abrupt, undesired movements.

### 5. Multi-Robots Demonstration Translation

After demonstrations are collected with the customized hand, they are translated for various specified robot hands.

- **Hand Pose Retargeting:** Since customized hands have different DoF and morphology compared to manufactured robot hands (e.g., Schunk, Adroit, Allegro), motion retargeting is performed offline. This is formulated as an optimization problem. For each time step, it minimizes the squared difference between the keypoint positions (e.g., fingertip positions) of the customized hand and the target robot hand. A temporal consistency term penalizes large joint position changes, and joint limits are enforced. This optimization yields the joint position trajectory for the specified robot.
- **Action Computation:** To support demonstration-augmented policy learning, corresponding actions (joint torques or motor commands) are computed for each finger joint of the specified robot. This involves passing the retargeted joint pose trajectory through a first-order low-pass filter and then using the robot's inverse dynamics (manipulator equation) to calculate the required joint torques.

### 6. Demonstration-Augmented Policy Learning

The generated state-action trajectories are then used to train manipulation policies.

- **Imitation Learning Algorithm:** The authors employ Demo Augmented Policy Gradient (DAPG), an algorithm that augments the standard Reinforcement Learning (RL) objective with a behavior cloning term from collected demonstrations. This combines the exploration capabilities of RL with the guidance provided by expert trajectories. The policy and value functions are implemented as 2-layer Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs).
- **Task Setup:** Three types of dexterous manipulation tasks are designed in the SAPIEN simulator: Relocate (moving objects like cans/bottles to a target pose), Flip (rotating a mug 90 degrees), and Open Door (rotating a handle and pulling the door). These tasks are goal-conditioned with randomized initial and target poses.
- **State and Action Space:** The robot state space includes robot joint angles, hand palm velocity, object position, and orientation. For goal-conditioned tasks, target information (e.g., target position for Relocate, door joint angle for Open Door) is included. The action space controls both the hand palm (6 velocity controllers) and finger joints (PD position controllers).
- **Sim2Real Transfer:** For real-world experiments, policies are trained in a simulation environment that includes an XArm-6 robot arm with an Allegro hand, mirroring the physical setup. To facilitate transfer, additive Gaussian noise is applied to object pose observations, and dynamics parameters (friction, density, controller PD) are randomized during training. Object initial poses in real-world evaluation are estimated using ICP from a RealSense D435 camera.

### 7. Main Findings and Results

The research presented several findings across user studies, simulation learning, and real-world experiments.

### 8. Teleoperation User Study

A user study involving 17 operators compared the customized robot hand with standard robot hands (Schunk, Adroit, Allegro) for Relocate and Open Door tasks.
- **Efficiency and Success Rate:** The customized robot hand achieved the highest success rates and lowest completion times for both stages of Relocate (Stage 1: 78.9% success, 9.1s; Stage 2: 55.3% success, 23.0s) and Open Door (Stage 1: 95.3% success, 6.2s; Stage 2: 82.4% success, 15.3s). This significantly surpassed other robot hands (e.g., Allegro hand Relocate S1: 44.7% success, 18.7s).
- **Operator Experience:** Operators reported that the customized hand was more controllable. This was attributed to the absence of computationally intensive online motion retargeting, which introduced latency and variance (76 ± 65 milliseconds) in other systems, making real-time control difficult.
- **Data Collection Rate:** The customized hand enabled the collection of approximately 60 successful demonstrations per hour for the Relocate task, compared to about 10 per hour when directly operating an Allegro Hand in simulation.

### 9. Task Learning Comparison (Simulation)

Policies trained with DAPG (using demonstrations from the customized hand and translated to target robots) were compared against pure RL (TRPO) for Relocate (three objects), Flip Mug, and Open Door tasks across Schunk, Adroit, and Allegro robot hands.
- **Improved Performance:** DAPG consistently outperformed the pure RL baseline across most tasks and robot hands in terms of normalized return and final success rates. For example, on Relocate-Tomato Can, Schunk robot achieved 85.0% success with DAPG versus 45.3% with RL; Adroit achieved 95.0% vs. 41.7%.
- **Policy Naturalness:** Qualitative observations for the Open Door task revealed that DAPG policies exhibited more "human-like" and natural grasping behaviors, while pure RL policies sometimes resorted to less intuitive or forceful interactions (e.g., pushing the door purely by friction) to achieve the goal in simulation.
- **Task-Robot Specificity:** The study noted that different robot hands demonstrated varying performance across tasks (e.g., Adroit performed best on Relocate-Tomato Soup Can, Schunk on Relocate-Mustard Bottle), suggesting robot-specific skills due to geometry and DoF differences. Some tasks, like Relocate-Mustard Bottle with Adroit and Allegro, remained challenging for both RL and DAPG due to limitations in forming a tight shape closure.

### 10. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the Relocate task with a tomato soup can using the Schunk robot investigated the impact of various parameters.
- **Robustness to Dynamics Changes:** Learning curves indicated that the policy was robust to changes in object friction (x0.5, x1.0, x2.0) and object density (x0.5, x1.0, x2.0). Dexterous hands' ability to form force closure with multiple contact points made them less sensitive to friction changes compared to parallel grippers.
- **Controller Parameters:** Policies learned with higher PD controller stiffness achieved higher rewards faster, but policies with lower stiffness could still solve the task.
- **Number of Demonstrations:** Increasing the number of demonstrations (from 20 to 50) generally led to better and more stable performance, with less variance in learning curves.

### 11. Real-World Robot Experiments (Sim2Real)

Experiments were conducted using an Allegro hand attached to an XArm-6 robot, evaluating Relocate (known and novel objects) and Flip tasks.
- **Enhanced Sim2Real Robustness:** The gap between imitation learning (DAPG) and pure RL policies was significantly larger in real-world deployment compared to simulation. For Relocate-Known Objects, DAPG achieved 77.7% success compared to RL's 22.2%. For Relocate-Novel Objects, DAPG maintained 66.6% success versus RL's 18.5%. For Flip Mug, DAPG achieved 44.4% success, while RL managed only 3.6%.
- **Generalization to Novel Objects:** DAPG policies demonstrated an ability to generalize to novel objects not seen during training for the Relocate task, indicating a degree of robustness against shape changes when operating with human-like, multi-finger grasps.
- **Qualitative Observations:** The RL policy's tendency to utilize unstable two-finger grasps or swift pushing motions (e.g., for Relocate and Flip respectively) led to failures (object slips) in the real world. In contrast, DAPG policies exhibited stable, multi-finger grasps and more deliberate, human-like manipulations, resulting in greater real-world success. This suggests that "hacks" of simulator physics by pure RL policies hinder real-world transfer.

### 12. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of dexterous manipulation by offering a novel, comprehensive framework that addresses several critical challenges in imitation learning for robotics.

1. **Efficient and Accessible Data Collection:** The single-camera teleoperation system, utilizing a readily available device like an iPad, significantly lowers the barrier to entry for collecting high-quality human demonstrations. The "customized robot hand" concept provides an intuitive and effective interface, leading to faster data acquisition rates compared to existing methods. This increased accessibility can facilitate larger and more diverse datasets for training.
2. **Multi-Robot Applicability of Demonstrations:** The ability to translate a single set of demonstrations from the customized hand to multiple specified robot hands (even those with different morphologies or DoF) is a substantial step towards data efficiency. This reduces the need for re-collecting demonstrations for each new robot platform, potentially accelerating the development and deployment of dexterous manipulation skills across various hardware.
3. **Improved Sim2Real Transfer Robustness:** The findings strongly suggest that integrating human demonstrations into RL training produces policies that are not only more effective in simulation but also significantly more robust and transferable to real-world robots. By guiding the learning process towards human-like, natural behaviors, the approach mitigates the tendency of pure RL to exploit simulator inaccuracies, leading to more stable and reliable physical robot performance. This improved robustness is crucial for practical applications of robotics.
4. **Advancement in Dexterous Manipulation:** The work demonstrates that imitation learning, augmented with carefully collected human demonstrations, can enable robots to learn complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks that are challenging for pure RL. The ability to generalize to novel objects further highlights the potential for adaptable robot skills.

The potential impact of this work includes enabling wider adoption of dexterous robot manipulation in various industries by making training data collection more efficient and flexible. It could also lead to the development of more robust and reliable robotic systems capable of performing intricate tasks in unstructured environments, contributing to progress in fields like manufacturing, logistics, and assistive robotics.
