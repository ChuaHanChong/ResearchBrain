# $π^{*}_{0.6}$: a VLA That Learns From Experience

> [!summary] Summary
> A general-purpose reinforcement learning method, RECAP, improves the robustness and throughput of large-scale Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models like π*0.6 through real-world deployment experience. This approach doubles task throughput and halves failure rates on complex tasks such as espresso making and diverse laundry folding, achieving practically useful levels of robotic performance.

## Problem

- Imitation Learning-based VLA models suffer from compounding errors and are limited by demonstration data, preventing them from achieving true skill mastery.
- Scaling real-world reinforcement learning to large, high-capacity Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for complex, long-horizon tasks is challenging.
- Integrating diverse data sources, including initial demonstrations, autonomous rollouts, and human interventions, into a unified and scalable self-improvement process for robots is difficult.

## Method

- Introduces **RECAP** (RL with Experience and Corrections via Advantage-conditioned Policies), an ==iterative training method== for VLA models instantiated with π*0.6.
- Employs an iterative loop of data collection (autonomous rollouts and human interventions), ==value function re-training==, and ==advantage-conditioned policy re-training==.
- Utilizes a novel ==policy extraction method based on advantage conditioning== (similar to ==classifier-free guidance==) that integrates a binarized advantage indicator as a text input for ==flow-matching VLAs==.

## Results

- **RECAP** consistently doubled throughput and halved failure rates on complex real-world tasks, including diverse laundry folding and espresso making, with final success rates exceeding **90%** on most tasks.
- The π*0.6 model demonstrated practical robustness, performing tasks like espresso making for **13 hours** without human intervention and assembling boxes in a factory setting.
- The advantage-conditioned policy extraction method outperformed other RL approaches like Advantage-Weighted Regression (AWR) and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in terms of task throughput on a laundry task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning can substantially improve the real-world robustness and throughput of large-scale Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, moving beyond the inherent limitations of imitation learning.
> - Advantage-conditioned policy extraction offers a scalable and effective mechanism for integrating RL with expressive flow-matching VLA architectures, circumventing the challenges of traditional policy gradient methods.
> - An iterative training loop that seamlessly integrates heterogeneous data sources (demonstrations, autonomous experience, and human interventions) is critical for practical, data-efficient robot self-improvement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of efforts to bridge the gap between large-scale foundation models (like Vision-Language Models) and real-world robotic control. The broader research landscape for robotic learning has seen significant advancements in:

- **Imitation Learning (IL)**: Training robots from human demonstrations. While powerful, IL is known to suffer from compounding errors and is fundamentally limited by the optimality of the demonstration data.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: Learning from experience through trial and error with reward feedback. RL holds the promise of achieving superhuman performance, robustness, and adaptability. However, scaling RL to complex, high-dimensional robotic tasks, especially with large, pre-trained models, presents significant challenges.
- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models**: A recent paradigm that integrates visual perception, language understanding, and robotic actions into a single end-to-end model, enabling flexible task specification and generalization.

RECAP (RL with Experience and Corrections via Advantage-conditioned Policies) directly addresses the limitations of IL-centric VLA models by integrating RL into the training pipeline. It acknowledges that while VLAs can generalize to new tasks, mastery requires continuous learning from experience.

The paper carefully distinguishes its contribution from existing work:

- **Beyond Imitation Learning**: While prior work has used online interventions (e.g., DAgger) to improve robotic policies, RECAP uniquely combines both expert interventions and fully autonomous experience within an RL-based framework, allowing for comprehensive self-improvement.
- **Scaling Real-World RL to VLAs**: Much prior work on RL for robotic manipulation has focused on smaller-scale systems, specific skills, or virtual environments. RECAP extends real-world RL to *large VLA policies* for *long-horizon, fine-grained manipulation tasks*, a critical step toward general-purpose robots.
- **Novel Policy Extraction for Flow-Matching VLAs**: Unlike many VLA fine-tuning approaches that rely on PPO or simpler action distributions (discrete or Gaussian continuous), RECAP employs an *iterated offline RL* framework with a sophisticated advantage conditioning strategy. This is specifically designed to work with *expressive flow-matching VLAs*, which are harder to integrate with traditional policy gradient methods due to the intractability of log-likelihoods. This avoids the complexity and inefficiencies of on-policy methods like PPO/REINFORCE for large, high-capacity models.
- **Leveraging Heterogeneous Data**: The method’s ability to seamlessly integrate diverse data sources—demonstrations, autonomous rollouts, and human interventions—is crucial for data-efficient learning and robust improvement, a challenge often faced by offline RL methods.
- **Practical Task Complexity**: The evaluation on complex, dexterous, and temporally extended real-world tasks (folding diverse laundry, making espresso, assembling boxes) sets a high bar, demonstrating the method's efficacy in challenging scenarios often requiring robust handling of deformable objects and liquids.

In essence, RECAP offers a scalable and practical recipe for enabling VLA models to learn and adapt continually in real-world deployments, moving beyond static knowledge gleaned from demonstrations to dynamic mastery through experience. This positions it as a significant step towards developing truly autonomous and capable generalist robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to substantially improve their performance through real-world deployments using reinforcement learning (RL). The authors are motivated by the observation that, while current VLA models offer flexible task specification and generalization from demonstrations, true *mastery* of a skill, akin to human learning, necessitates "practice"—learning from repeated attempts and correcting mistakes made in deployment.

The key motivations and specific objectives can be summarized as follows:

- **Overcoming Imitation Learning Limitations**: Policies trained solely with imitation learning (IL) are prone to compounding errors and cannot surpass the performance level of their demonstration data. The goal is to move beyond this ceiling.
- **Leveraging Autonomous Experience**: To allow robots to correct their *actual* mistakes in deployment, improve speed and robustness beyond human teleoperation levels, and adapt to novel deployment conditions.
- **Developing a General and Scalable RL Method**: Instantiating RL principles in a general-purpose robotic learning system is challenging. The work aims to create a recipe for scalable and stable RL methods suitable for large VLA models.
- **Handling Heterogeneous Data**: Integrate various data sources—initial demonstrations, data from autonomous on-policy collection, and expert teleoperated interventions (corrections)—into a unified self-improvement process.
- **Addressing Real-World RL Challenges**: Tackle issues like ambiguous or stochastic reward signals and the practical setup of RL training in the real world.
- **Achieving Practically Useful Robustness**: Ultimately, the aim is to train VLAs to reach robustness and throughput levels that make them viable for real-world applications in homes, cafes, and factories.

The paper introduces RECAP as a method to achieve these goals, allowing VLA models to incorporate reward feedback at all stages of the training pipeline, from pre-training to online deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is RECAP (RL with Experience and Corrections via Advantage-conditioned Policies), an iterative method designed to train and improve Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The process involves repeating three main steps: data collection, value function training, and advantage-conditioned policy training.

**A. The π\*0.6 VLA Model**
RECAP is instantiated with a VLA named π\*0.6, an adaptation of the π0.6 model for RL. π0.6 itself is an evolution of π0.5, incorporating a larger VLM backbone (Gemma 3 4B model), a more diverse pre-training dataset, and an increased action expert size (860M parameters). π\*0.6 distinguishes itself by adding the ability to condition on a binarized advantage indicator (I_t), making it amenable to RL training with RECAP.

The model uses the Knowledge Insulation (KI) training procedure, which trains the VLA end-to-end on continuous actions (via flow matching) and discretized tokens, including textual sub-task predictions and tokenized actions. The full training objective combines log-likelihoods for discrete outputs (sub-tasks, tokenized actions) and a flow-matching loss for continuous actions. The advantage indicator is incorporated as an additional text input ("Advantage: positive" or "Advantage: negative") in the training sequence, influencing only the action log-likelihoods.

**B. Iterative Training Process (RECAP Algorithm)**
The RECAP process is structured as follows:

1. **Pre-training Phase**:
 - **Value Function Pre-training**: A multi-task distributional value function (V_pre) is trained on a massive, diverse multi-task and multi-robot demonstration dataset using Equation 1. This value function predicts the (negative) number of steps until successful completion, normalized per task. It shares a similar architecture with the VLA but with a smaller 670M parameter VLM backbone.
 - **VLA Pre-training**: The π\*0.6 policy (π_pre) is trained on the same demonstration dataset using Equation 3, leveraging the pre-trained value function to compute advantage estimates and the binarized advantage indicator (I_t). The task-dependent advantage threshold (ϵ_ℓ) is set such that approximately 30% of demonstration data has a positive advantage. This initial pre-training effectively serves as offline RL, creating a generalist VLA.

2. **Specialization/Fine-tuning Phase (Iterative On-Robot Data Collection and Improvement)**:
 - **Initial Fine-tuning (SFT)**: For a specific downstream task ℓ, the pre-trained π\*0.6 model (π_pre) is fine-tuned with demonstrations (D_ℓ) for that task. In this stage, the advantage indicator I_t is fixed to "True," essentially performing Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). This yields the initial task-specific policy π^0_ℓ.
 - **Iterative Improvement Loop (k=1 to K)**:
 - **Data Collection**: The current policy π^(k-1)_ℓ is deployed on the robot to collect new data. This includes fully autonomous rollouts and, optionally, human-teleoperated interventions to correct mistakes. The collected data (D_ℓ) is labeled for episode success. Human corrections are always treated as having I_t = True.
 - **Value Function Re-training**: The value function for task ℓ (V^k_ℓ) is fine-tuned from the *pre-trained V_pre checkpoint* on *all accumulated data* for task ℓ (D_ℓ). This helps avoid drift over iterations.
 - **Policy Re-training (Advantage-Conditioned)**: The policy π^k_ℓ is fine-tuned from the *pre-trained π_pre checkpoint* on all accumulated data (D_ℓ), using updated advantage indicators (I_t) derived from the newly trained V^k_ℓ. The advantage threshold ϵ_ℓ is typically adjusted so that about 40% of evaluation rollouts in each iteration have a positive advantage. Advantage conditioning dropout (30% of the time) is used during training to enable classifier-free guidance at inference time.

**C. Reward Function and Advantage Calculation**
A sparse, general-purpose reward function is used: `r_t = 0` for successful terminal steps, `-C_fail` for failed terminal steps, and `-1` otherwise. The value function is trained to predict the (negative of the) remaining steps to success. Advantage values A(o_t, a_t) are calculated as n-step estimates based on the value function, typically using N=50 lookahead for fine-tuning and N=T (full trajectory) for pre-training.

**D. Policy Extraction via Advantage Conditioning**
The method's core innovation lies in its policy extraction. It leverages a concept similar to classifier-free guidance (CFGRL) by training the VLA to represent both an unconditioned policy (π(a|o,ℓ)) and an advantage-conditioned policy (π(a|I,o,ℓ)). This allows for implicitly defining an improved policy that is proportional to `π_ref(a|o,ℓ) * (π_ref(a|I,o,ℓ) / π_ref(a|o,ℓ))^β`. This approach effectively utilizes all data (good and bad) and scales well to flow-matching VLA models, circumventing the challenges of policy gradient methods with complex action spaces. At inference, the parameter β can be used to further sharpen the policy, though the authors primarily rely on the training-time threshold ϵ_ℓ.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation demonstrates that RECAP, applied to the π\*0.6 VLA, yields substantial improvements in robotic performance across a suite of challenging, real-world tasks: laundry folding (simple and diverse items), making espresso drinks, and assembling cardboard boxes. The key findings are:

- **Significant Performance Gains**: RECAP consistently and significantly improves both the **throughput** (successful task executions per hour) and **success rate** compared to several baselines.
 - **Throughput**: More than doubles on some of the hardest tasks, such as diverse laundry folding and espresso making. For easier tasks like folding T-shirts and shorts, throughput still sees a significant increase even if success rate is already high.
 - **Success Rate**: Failure rates are reduced by approximately 2x or more. The final π\*0.6 model achieves 90%+ success rates on most tasks, making it suitable for practical deployment. For box assembly, RECAP leads to the most consistent and highest success rates across all subtasks (picking, building, labeling, placing).
- **Practically Useful Robustness**: The improved performance translates to real-world utility. The system was able to:
 - Make espresso drinks for 13 hours straight without human intervention.
 - Fold novel laundry items in a new home for over two hours.
 - Assemble boxes for real packaging in a factory setting.
- **Efficacy of Iterative Improvement**: Over multiple iterations of data collection and training, RECAP progressively improves policies. For the laundry task, a 50% improvement in throughput was observed over two iterations. For the long-horizon box assembly task, throughput doubled after the second iteration, and success rates continued to climb.
- **Superiority of Advantage-Conditioned Policy Extraction**: RECAP's advantage-conditioned policy extraction method significantly outperforms alternative literature methods like Advantage-Weighted Regression (AWR) and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in terms of throughput on the laundry task. AWR leads to slower policies, while PPO struggles to achieve comparable performance, likely due to the challenges of applying policy gradient methods to complex, off-policy, diffusion-based VLA models.
- **Targeted Failure Mode Removal**: RECAP proves effective at removing specific, undesirable behaviors with relatively little data. In a controlled laundry task with an adversarial initial condition and strict success criteria (collar facing up), RECAP improved the policy to a 97% success rate with high speed, demonstrating its ability to alter specific aspects of behavior.

In summary, the results highlight RECAP's ability to imbue generalist VLA models with a crucial capacity for self-improvement through real-world experience, leading to robust, fast, and reliable robotic performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "π\*0.6: a VLA That Learns From Experience" holds significant implications for the future of robotic learning and embodied AI, particularly in bridging the gap between powerful foundation models and practical real-world robotic applications.

**A. Novel Contribution and Methodological Advancement**
The paper's primary significance lies in demonstrating, for the first time, that a general-purpose reinforcement learning recipe, incorporating both human reward feedback and expert interventions, can *significantly* improve the robustness and throughput of large-scale Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models through real-world deployment experience. This is a crucial step beyond imitation learning, which, while foundational, inherently limits policy performance.

The RECAP methodology itself is a notable advancement:
- **Scalable RL for VLAs**: It successfully addresses the challenge of scaling real-world reinforcement learning to high-capacity, flow-matching VLA models, which have previously been difficult to integrate with traditional RL algorithms. The advantage-conditioning strategy provides an elegant and effective way to extract improved policies from diverse, off-policy data.
- **Holistic Data Integration**: RECAP's ability to seamlessly combine demonstrations, autonomous experiences, and human interventions into a unified learning loop is vital for practical robotics, where data is heterogeneous and precious.

**B. Practical Impact and Real-World Applications**
The quantitative results, particularly the doubling of throughput and halving of failure rates on complex tasks like espresso making and diverse laundry folding, are transformative. These improvements enable what the authors term "practically useful levels of robustness," making it feasible for robots to perform long-duration, multi-stage, fine-grained manipulation tasks in unstructured environments. The demonstrated applications in homes (laundry) and factories (box assembly) are compelling proof points, suggesting a path toward wider adoption of robots in daily life and industrial settings. The ability of a robot to consistently perform tasks for hours without interruption is a hallmark of true readiness for deployment.

**C. Foundation for Future Robotic Autonomy**
This research represents a meaningful step toward building genuinely autonomous and adaptable robots. By demonstrating that robots can learn from their own mistakes and experiences, RECAP lays the groundwork for:
- **Accelerated Skill Acquisition**: Robots can fine-tune and master skills more quickly in the field, reducing the need for extensive initial human programming or demonstration.
- **Continuous Improvement**: The iterative nature of RECAP supports a paradigm of continuous policy improvement, allowing robots to adapt to changing environments, new objects, or evolving task requirements over their operational lifetime.
- **Robustness to Unseen Scenarios**: By training on real-world deployments, policies are exposed to genuine variability and edge cases, leading to more robust behavior than what can be achieved purely in simulations or from limited demonstration datasets.

**D. Future Research Directions**
The authors acknowledge several promising avenues for future work, including:
- **Enhanced Autonomy**: Automating human aspects like reward labeling, interventions, and scene resets would further scale the approach and reduce human supervision. VLAs themselves might play a role in semantic reasoning for autonomous resets.
- **Sophisticated Exploration**: Integrating more advanced exploration methods could accelerate learning, especially in novel or sparse-reward environments.
- **Online RL Frameworks**: Moving from iterated offline updates to a fully concurrent online RL loop would allow for real-time policy improvement, increasing sample efficiency and reactivity.

In conclusion, "π\*0.6: a VLA That Learns From Experience" offers both a powerful methodological contribution and compelling empirical evidence that real-world reinforcement learning is becoming a viable and impactful strategy for developing highly capable robotic generalists. It pushes the boundaries of what is achievable with current VLA models, making significant strides toward the vision of intelligent robots that learn and adapt continuously in the physical world.
