# Recovering Physically Plausible Human-Object Interactions from Monocular Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> RePHO reconstructs physically plausible human-object interactions from monocular video by refining noisy kinematic estimates with a reinforcement-learned tracking policy inside a physics simulator. Adaptive sampling scores frames by rollout length to find reliable anchors, while forward and backward policies propagate successful simulated states back into the kinematic reference. Success rate rises from 17.1% to 51.4% on BEHAVE with large gains in contact, penetration, and floating metrics.

## Problem

- Kinematic monocular HOI reconstruction methods produce visually convincing motion but exhibit interpenetration, floating objects, and jitter because contact forces and gravity go unmodeled.
- Physics-aware losses only penalize contact violations without explicitly simulating collisions or forces, so reconstructions still violate real-world interaction dynamics.
- Existing reinforcement-learning HOI imitation frameworks assume clean motion-capture references, so they destabilize or fail outright on noisy, occlusion-corrupted monocular estimates.

## Method

- **RePHO**'s physics-based tracking policy formulates HOI as an ==MDP==: state combines proprioception, object pose, joint-to-surface distance vectors, hand contact markers, and future kinematic references; ==PD targets== over 51 joints.
- ==Traverse RSI== plus ==adaptive sampling== uniformly visits every frame early, records per-frame rollout length in a buffer, then upweights initialization at frames yielding longer survival.
- ==Dual propagation== with kinematics update trains simultaneous forward and backward policies whose successful rollout states overwrite noisy reference frames, serving as both initializations and new tracking targets.

## Results

- Versus the VisTracker kinematic baseline on BEHAVE, hand contact rate rises **0.52** to **0.89**, penetration falls **6.64** to **3.91** cm, object floating **0.30** to **0.10**, jerk **524.9** to **188.5**.
- Against the InterMimic physics baseline, binary success rate reaches **51.4%** versus **17.1%** finetuned and **0%** direct inference on BEHAVE; InterCap gives **52.6%** versus **21.1%**.
- Ablations on BEHAVE lift SR-B from **11.4%** naive to **14.3%** with adaptive sampling, **17.1%** adding kinematic updates, and **51.4%** with full dual propagation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Noisy perception outputs are not uniformly bad: rollout survival length is a cheap self-supervised proxy for which frames carry reliable contact signal.
> - Refined simulator states should replace the reference trajectory, not just the initialization; using them as tracking targets contributed the largest ablation gain.
> - Temporal reversal helps because some contact transitions are easier backward than forward, placing an object down is easier to learn than picking it up.
> - Physical plausibility trades against raw 3D accuracy: Chamfer distance degrades roughly 1.4 cm while every contact and physics metric improves.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The reconstruction of 3D human-object interactions (HOI) from visual data, particularly monocular videos, is a persistent challenge in 3D computer vision and computer graphics. This work addresses a recognized limitation within the current landscape of HOI reconstruction, which broadly consists of two main categories: kinematic-based methods and physics-based animation/imitation methods.

**Kinematic-based approaches** focus on inferring 3D human and object geometry and their spatial relationships directly from images or videos. Recent advancements in this area, often leveraging neural fields, motion priors, and contact-aware optimization, have yielded visually coherent 4D reconstructions from monocular RGB videos. Examples include methods that track human and object meshes across frames and handle occlusions. However, a significant drawback of these kinematic approaches is their tendency to produce physically implausible artifacts. These artifacts include interpenetration (human and object occupying the same space), floating contacts (objects appearing to hover without proper support), and jittering (unnatural, abrupt motions). While some kinematic methods attempt to mitigate these issues with physics-aware losses, they typically do not explicitly model underlying physical laws such as contact forces, gravity, and collisions.

**Physics-based animation and motion imitation** approaches, conversely, aim to synthesize human movements and interactions that adhere to physical laws by employing physics simulators and control policies, often learned through reinforcement learning (RL). These methods have demonstrated success in generating diverse and dynamic whole-body behaviors, including complex locomotion and manipulation tasks. However, existing physics-based HOI frameworks largely depend on access to clean, accurate motion capture (MoCap) data for training and reference. They are generally not designed to operate directly on the noisy, incomplete, and often physically inconsistent kinematic estimates derived from "in-the-wild" monocular videos. The presence of artifacts like incorrect contact patterns or unstable object poses in monocular reconstructions poses a substantial challenge for stable RL training and successful physics-based imitation.

This paper positions itself at the intersection of these two domains. It aims to bridge the gap by developing a framework that takes the output of a kinematic reconstruction method as input and refines it into a physically plausible HOI sequence using a physics simulator and reinforcement learning. By specifically addressing the problem of noise inherent in monocular video reconstructions, this work seeks to enable the application of robust physics-based control to real-world visual data, moving towards more realistic and interactive virtual representations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to reconstruct physically plausible human-object interactions from monocular videos. This involves transforming visually plausible but physically inconsistent kinematic estimates into sequences that respect fundamental physical laws such as gravity, collision, and contact forces.

The motivation for this work arises from several key observations and challenges in the field of 3D human-object interaction reconstruction:

1. **Physical Implausibility of Kinematic Reconstructions:** Existing state-of-the-art kinematic-based methods, while achieving impressive visual fidelity from monocular videos, often produce results that are physically unrealistic. Common issues include human and object interpenetration, objects floating in the air without proper support, and unstable contact points. These artifacts limit the utility of such reconstructions for applications requiring physical consistency, such as robotics simulation, virtual reality, or biomechanical analysis. The authors argue that merely adding physics-aware losses to kinematic optimization is insufficient, as it does not explicitly model the underlying physical dynamics.

2. **Difficulty of Applying Physics-Based Methods to Noisy Data:** Physics-based imitation learning techniques are highly effective at generating physically realistic motions when provided with clean motion capture (MoCap) data. However, direct application of these methods to the noisy outputs of monocular video reconstruction pipelines typically leads to unstable behaviors or complete failure of the physics simulator. The noise, particularly during occlusions or fast movements, can cause estimated object poses to drift, contacts to be missed, or the entire interaction to become unstable, rendering naive reinforcement learning (RL) training ineffective.

3. **Need for Robustness to Monocular Noise:** Despite their shortcomings, monocular reconstructions still contain valuable information. Frames with clear visibility, stable contacts, or slower motions often provide more accurate kinematic estimates. The motivation is to develop a method that can leverage these "reliable" signals within otherwise noisy sequences to guide a physics-based refinement process, rather than being overwhelmed by the noise.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out to achieve the following:
- Develop a physics-guided reconstruction framework that starts with a kinematic estimate and refines it in a physics simulator using reinforcement learning.
- Introduce a mechanism to handle the "extreme noise" prevalent in monocular video reconstructions, enabling stable RL training where conventional methods would fail.
- Propose an adaptive sampling strategy coupled with a dual self-updating mechanism to identify and exploit reliable kinematic cues and progressively propagate physical consistency throughout the interaction sequence.
- Demonstrate that this framework can significantly improve physical plausibility metrics compared to existing kinematic and physics-based baselines, while maintaining reasonable 3D accuracy.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to bring the physical realism of simulation-based methods to the accessibility of monocular video input, thereby enabling more robust and versatile applications of human-object interaction analysis.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, RePHO, is a two-stage framework designed to recover physically plausible human-object interactions (HOI) from monocular videos. It starts with a noisy kinematic reconstruction and refines it using reinforcement learning (RL) within a physics simulator.

#### 3.1. Two-Stage Pipeline Overview

1. **Kinematic Reconstruction:** An off-the-shelf kinematic reconstruction method, specifically VisTracker, is initially used to process the input monocular video. This stage produces a sequence of estimated human and object poses (kinematic estimates) in global coordinates, which serve as the initial reference. These estimates are acknowledged to be noisy and often physically implausible.
2. **Physics-Based Refinement:** The core of RePHO involves training a control policy using RL to imitate these reference kinematics within a physics simulator. The policy aims to reproduce the motion under physical laws, correcting implausibilities present in the initial kinematic sequence.

#### 3.2. HOI Tracking with Reinforcement Learning (MDP Formulation)

The problem of HOI tracking is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) with defined state space, action space, transition dynamics, and reward function.

- **State (`s_t`):** At each timestep `t`, the policy observes a state `s_t = {s_s_t, s_g_t}`.
 - **Physical State (`s_s_t`):** Encodes current proprioceptive (body-related) and object-related quantities. This includes joint rotations, positions, linear and angular velocities for both the human (modeled by SMPL-H) and the object (6DoF pose). Additionally, two geometric/haptic cues are included: vectors from human joints to the nearest object points (`d_t`), and binary contact markers for hands (`c_t`). Contact information for other body parts or ground contact is not explicitly provided, but acquired through RL and the adaptive sampling system.
 - **Goal State (`s_g_t`):** Aggregates a set of future kinematic reference states (`{ˆs_t,t+k}k∈K`). Each element `ˆs_t,t+k` encodes the reference at time `t+k` relative to the current physical state `s_t`. This includes relative rotations, positions, and differences in distance and contact markers between the current physical state and the future kinematic reference.
- **Action (`a_t`):** The policy outputs desired joint orientations for the human model's 51 actuated joints, represented in exponential-map form. These act as targets for a PD-controller in the simulator, which converts them into torques applied at each joint.
- **Reward (`r_t`):** A composite reward function (`r_t = r_h_t * r_o_t * r_c_t * r_d_t * r_e_t`) encourages physically accurate tracking.
 - `r_h_t`, `r_o_t`: Penalize pose, position, and velocity differences between the simulated human/object and their respective kinematic references.
 - `r_c_t`: Aligns simulated contact states (`c_t`) with reference contacts (`ˆc_t`) and penalizes distance between the object contact point and the paired hand joint.
 - `r_d_t`: Minimizes deviation in human-object proximity.
 - `r_e_t`: Penalizes abrupt motion and sudden contact forces to promote smoothness.
 Each term is formulated as `exp(-λE)` where `E` is a cost function and `λ` is a hyperparameter.

#### 3.3. Traverse RSI and Adaptive Sampling for Noise Handling

Directly training an RL policy on noisy monocular kinematic estimates often leads to instability and rollout failures. The method addresses this through an adaptive sampling strategy:

- **Traverse Reference State Initialization (RSI):** In the early stages of RL training, the simulator is initialized from a reference pose (`ˆq_t`) at randomly sampled timesteps (`t`) across the entire sequence. This "Traverse RSI" strategy ensures that all frames are visited equally often, allowing the system to observe the success rate of rollouts originating from different frames.
- **Reliable Frame Identification:** It is observed that rollouts initialized from more accurate frames (e.g., clear contact, slow motion) achieve longer durations before failure. The system maintains a buffer that records the rollout length associated with each initialization frame.
- **Adaptive Sampling:** Based on the rollout length statistics, the sampling probability for initialization frames is progressively adjusted. Frames that yield longer, more successful rollouts are sampled more frequently, thus directing the policy to learn from more reliable segments of the kinematic sequence.

#### 3.4. Dual Propagation with Kinematics Update

As training progresses with adaptive sampling, the policy learns to generate more physically plausible states from cleaner initialization frames. This leads to a phenomenon where the simulated rollout states are more physically consistent than the original noisy kinematic references. The method exploits this by:

- **State Buffering and Update:** A buffer stores successful rollout states. When simulated states from a rollout are more physically plausible (e.g., accurate contact configuration), they are used to update the corresponding noisy kinematic frames in the initial reference sequence.
- **Evolving Tracking Targets:** Subsequent rollouts then sample their kinematic references from this continually evolving buffer, which now contains refined states. This means the policy learns to track an increasingly cleaner and more physically consistent target sequence.
- **Dual Policies (Forward and Backward):** To accelerate and robustly propagate these physically plausible states across the entire sequence, RePHO trains two HOI tracking policies simultaneously:
 - **Forward Policy:** Tracks the sequence chronologically, using current physics states and *future* kinematic references.
 - **Backward Policy:** Tracks the sequence in reverse, using current physics states and *past* kinematic references.
 This dual propagation allows improvements to flow in both temporal directions. For example, a physically accurate "placing object down" action learned by the backward policy can refine earlier "picking up object" references for the forward policy. Both policies are initialized from a pre-trained InterMimic checkpoint and then finetuned.

This iterative process of adaptive sampling, physically guided rollouts, and dual-direction kinematics updates enables the policy to gradually overcome severe noise and reconstruct a physically consistent HOI sequence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of RePHO involved comparisons against both kinematic-based and physics-based state-of-the-art methods on two standard HOI benchmarks: BEHAVE and InterCap.

#### 4.1. Comparison with Kinematics-based Baselines (VisTracker)

- **Metrics:** Chamfer Distance (CD-H for human, CD-O for object), Contact Rate (ContRate-h), Contact Distance (ContDist-h, ContDist-w), Penetration (Pen), Object Floating (ObjFloat), and Object Jerk (ObjJerk).
- **Quantitative Results (Table 1):**
 - **3D Accuracy (CD):** RePHO showed a slight increase in Chamfer Distance (e.g., CD-H increased from 5.39 to 6.82 on BEHAVE, CD-O from 8.73 to 11.06). This indicates a minor degradation in raw 3D pose accuracy compared to VisTracker, likely a trade-off for physical realism.
 - **Physics-aware Metrics:** RePHO consistently and substantially improved all interaction-related and physics-aware metrics:
 - **Contact Rate (ContRate-h):** Significantly higher (e.g., 0.52 to 0.89 on BEHAVE), indicating more accurate detection of ground-truth contacts.
 - **Contact Distance (ContDist-h, ContDist-w):** Significantly lower (e.g., ContDist-h from 7.78 to 4.33 on BEHAVE), indicating closer alignment of reconstructed contacts.
 - **Penetration (Pen):** Substantially reduced (e.g., 6.64 to 3.91 on BEHAVE), demonstrating a decrease in human-object interpenetration.
 - **Object Floating (ObjFloat):** Markedly reduced (e.g., 0.30 to 0.10 on BEHAVE), indicating fewer instances of unsupported objects.
 - **Object Jerk (ObjJerk):** Dramatically lower (e.g., 524.9 to 188.5 on BEHAVE), reflecting smoother and more physically consistent object motion.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 4):** Visual comparisons illustrated that VisTracker suffered from contact floating and interpenetration, while RePHO produced physically grounded HOI sequences, correcting these artifacts despite starting from noisy VisTracker reconstructions.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Physics-Based Baselines (InterMimic)

- **Metrics:** Success Rate-Binary (SR-B), Success Rate-Frame (SR-F), and a subset of 3D and physics-aware metrics for successful clips.
- **Quantitative Results (Table 2):**
 - **Direct Inference (InterMimic):** When the pre-trained InterMimic policy was applied directly to noisy VisTracker reconstructions, it exhibited extremely low success rates (SR-B of 0% on both datasets, SR-F below 9%). This highlights its sensitivity to noise and inability to robustly handle monocular inputs. 3D metrics were not reported due to the low success rate.
 - **Finetuned InterMimic:** Finetuning InterMimic on single sequences improved performance but remained significantly lower than RePHO (e.g., SR-B of 17.1% vs. 51.4% on BEHAVE, SR-F of 26.7% vs. 60.0%).
 - **RePHO Performance:** RePHO achieved substantial improvements in both binary and frame-level success rates (e.g., SR-B of 51.4% and SR-F of 60.0% on BEHAVE), demonstrating superior robustness to noisy kinematic inputs. For successful clips, RePHO generally maintained or improved 3D accuracy and physics-aware metrics compared to finetuned InterMimic.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 5):** InterMimic was observed to struggle with recovering correct contact configurations, sometimes failing due to lack of contact in early phases or committing to unnatural contact poses that did not match the video. RePHO, in contrast, reconstructed full sequences with plausible interaction geometry.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (Table 3)

The ablation study on the BEHAVE dataset evaluated the contributions of key design choices:

- **Naive Training:** Achieved low success rates (SR-B: 11.4%, SR-F: 24.5%), confirming the difficulty of direct RL training on noisy inputs.
- **Adaptive Sampling:** Adding adaptive sampling alone significantly improved success rates (SR-B: 14.3%, SR-F: 40.4%).
- **Kinematic Updates (Initialization Only):** Updating kinematics for initialization, but not as tracking targets, resulted in SR-B of 40.0% and SR-F of 59.4%.
- **Kinematic Updates (Initialization and Tracking Targets):** Using kinematic updates as both initialization and tracking targets, but without dual propagation, further improved performance (SR-B: 17.1%, SR-F: 43.5%).
- **Dual Propagation (Single Policy):** Implementing dual propagation with a single policy instead of two led to SR-B of 48.5% and SR-F of 59.7%, indicating that two policies provide a slight but consistent benefit.
- **Full Method (Adaptive Sampling, Kinematic Updates as Tracking Targets, Dual Propagation with Two Policies):** Achieved the best results (SR-B: 51.4%, SR-F: 60.0%), validating the synergistic effect of all proposed components.

The ablation results indicate that adaptive sampling and kinematics updates, especially when serving as evolving tracking targets and combined with dual propagation, are crucial for robust learning from noisy monocular data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, RePHO, that addresses a notable gap between high-fidelity visual reconstruction of human-object interactions (HOI) and the imperative for physical plausibility. The significance and potential impact of this work span several areas:

1. **Bridging the Kinematic-Physics Divide:** The primary significance lies in demonstrating a viable approach to bridge the gap between kinematic reconstructions from "in-the-wild" monocular videos and physically consistent simulations. Previous kinematic methods often yielded visually convincing but physically impossible motions, while physics-based methods required clean, often expensive, motion capture data. RePHO enables the application of physics-based refinement to readily available monocular video data, making physically plausible HOI accessible from a wider range of sources.

2. **Robustness to Noisy Input:** The core contribution of RePHO's adaptive sampling and dual propagation with kinematics update mechanisms is its ability to handle the severe noise inherent in monocular reconstructions. This is critical because noise often prevents direct application of physics-based imitation learning techniques. By systematically identifying reliable frames, progressively updating kinematic targets with physically consistent states, and propagating these improvements temporally in both directions, the method offers a robust solution for learning in degraded data environments.

3. **Enhanced Realism and Utility of Reconstructions:** By enforcing physical laws, RePHO generates HOI sequences that are not only visually plausible but also dynamically consistent. This enhanced realism increases the utility of reconstructed motions for various applications:
 - **Robotics:** Physically plausible HOI sequences can serve as more realistic training data or reference demonstrations for robotic systems to learn manipulation and interaction skills, potentially leveraging vast amounts of human video data from the internet.
 - **Virtual Reality/Character Animation:** The output can be used to animate virtual avatars or characters in virtual environments with greater physical fidelity, leading to more believable and interactive experiences.
 - **Biomechanics and Ergonomics:** The physically consistent motions could provide more accurate inputs for biomechanical analysis or ergonomic studies, where precise contact and force dynamics are important.

4. **Advancement in Reinforcement Learning for Tracking:** The adaptive sampling and dual propagation strategy represents an advancement in how reinforcement learning can be effectively applied to noisy, real-world motion tracking problems. This methodology could potentially be generalized to other domains where RL is used for imitation learning from imperfect reference data.

**Limitations and Future Work:** The authors acknowledge limitations, primarily the two-stage pipeline's dependence on the initial kinematic reconstruction's quality. This suggests future work could focus on developing end-to-end video-to-dynamics systems that jointly infer geometry and physically grounded interactions, potentially reducing error propagation. Additionally, the current scope is limited to single-object interactions with less dynamic contacts, indicating future research directions in handling multi-object, multi-human interactions, and more complex contact scenarios or scene-aware HOI.

In summary, RePHO significantly contributes to the field by providing a robust and effective framework for recovering physically plausible human-object interactions from monocular videos. Its ability to navigate the challenges of noisy real-world data opens new avenues for leveraging visual information for applications requiring physical realism.
