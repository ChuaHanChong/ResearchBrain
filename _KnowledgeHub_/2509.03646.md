# Emergent Hierarchical Reasoning in LLMs through Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research reveals an emergent hierarchical reasoning structure in Large Language Models during reinforcement learning, where initial procedural consolidation is followed by strategic planning exploration. It introduces Hierarchy-Aware Credit Assignment (HICRA), an algorithm that enhances reasoning performance by focusing optimization efforts on high-level planning tokens.

## Problem

- Despite empirical success, the mechanistic understanding of how Reinforcement Learning (RL) improves complex reasoning in LLMs remained opaque.
- Puzzling phenomena during RL training, such as 'aha moments' and 'length-scaling' effects, lacked a unifying explanation.
- Existing RL algorithms often apply optimization pressure indiscriminately across all generated tokens, leading to inefficient learning.

## Method

- Functionally decomposed LLM-generated tokens into ==high-level planning tokens== (e.g., logical maneuvers) and ==low-level execution tokens== (e.g., calculations).
- Introduced =="Strategic Grams" (SGs)==, a data-driven method to empirically identify planning tokens, avoiding subjective manual annotation.
- Developed ==Hierarchy-Aware Credit Assignment (HICRA)==, an RL algorithm that amplifies the advantage signal for identified planning tokens to concentrate optimization.

## Results

- HICRA consistently outperformed baseline GRPO, achieving performance gains (e.g., **+5.4%** to **+6.1%** on AIME24 for Qwen3-4B models) across text-only and multimodal reasoning benchmarks.
- Error analysis revealed that RL primarily improves reasoning by rectifying 'Planning & Strategy' errors, showing a more pronounced reduction in high-level strategic faults.
- Semantic entropy accurately correlated with improved validation accuracy, demonstrating that targeted strategic exploration leads to better performance than indiscriminate token-level diversity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLM reasoning under RL exhibits a two-phase learning dynamic: initial procedural consolidation followed by an emergent phase of strategic planning exploration.
> - The learning bottleneck dynamically shifts from procedural correctness to high-level strategic planning, which becomes the primary driver for advanced reasoning.
> - "Semantic entropy" serves as a more reliable diagnostic metric for tracking genuine strategic exploration compared to traditional token-level entropy or Pass@K.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has witnessed remarkable advancements, particularly through the application of Reinforcement Learning (RL) techniques. RL has proven instrumental in fine-tuning LLMs to improve their ability to follow instructions, align with human preferences, and, crucially, enhance their complex reasoning capabilities across diverse domains. Seminal works like Ouyang et al. (2022) demonstrated the effectiveness of learning from human feedback, while more recent algorithms, such as Group Reward Policy Optimization (GRPO) by Guo et al. (2025) and its variants, have specifically targeted the incentivization of reasoning in LLMs and Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

However, despite this empirical success, a significant gap persists in the fundamental understanding of *how* RL actually facilitates these improvements. Researchers frequently observe puzzling phenomena during training, such as sudden "aha moments" where models appear to acquire new skills, "length-scaling" effects where reasoning performance correlates with the verbosity of outputs, and complex, often opaque, dynamics in token-level entropy. These phenomena are effective but poorly understood, leaving the underlying learning mechanisms largely unexamined.

This paper directly addresses this gap by proposing a novel, mechanistic understanding of RL's impact. It posits that RL does not simply train models from scratch but rather fine-tunes base models already imbued with hierarchical reasoning priors from vast pre-training corpora. Drawing a compelling parallel to human cognition's separation of high-level strategic planning from low-level procedural execution (Huntenburg et al., 2018), the authors hypothesize that RL unlocks advanced reasoning by rediscovering and operationalizing this inherent hierarchical structure.

Furthermore, the work critically evaluates prevailing RL algorithms like GRPO, arguing that their agnostic application of optimization pressure across all generated tokens is fundamentally inefficient. By diluting the learning signal uniformly, these methods fail to concentrate learning efforts on the most impactful aspects of reasoning. The proposed Hierarchy-Aware Credit Assignment (HICRA) algorithm directly challenges this by introducing a targeted approach, aiming to design more principled and efficient RL methodologies. In doing so, this research contributes not only to a deeper theoretical understanding of LLM learning but also offers practical algorithmic improvements that push the boundaries of advanced reasoning in AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to unravel the opaque learning dynamics that enable Reinforcement Learning to enhance complex reasoning abilities in Large Language Models. The authors are driven by a fundamental question: "What unlocks enhanced reasoning in LLMs during RL, and how should we leverage this understanding to design more principled and efficient RL algorithms?"

Specifically, the key motivations and objectives can be outlined as follows:

1. **Mechanistic Understanding:** To move beyond empirical observations of RL's success and establish a mechanistic understanding of the underlying learning dynamics. This involves investigating phenomena like "aha moments," "length-scaling," and token entropy dynamics, which are currently effective but poorly understood.
2. **Unifying Framework:** To provide a cohesive, unifying framework that explains these disparate puzzling phenomena during RL training. The core hypothesis is that an "emergent reasoning hierarchy," akin to human cognitive processes, is the key to this understanding.
3. **Identify Learning Bottlenecks:** To pinpoint the critical bottlenecks in the RL training process that, when overcome, lead to significant improvements in reasoning. The authors specifically hypothesize that the mastery of high-level strategic planning becomes the primary driver of advanced reasoning.
4. **Critique and Improve Existing Algorithms:** To expose the inefficiencies in current agnostic credit assignment methods (e.g., GRPO) that dilute the learning signal across all tokens. This critique motivates the development of a more targeted and efficient RL algorithm.
5. **Develop a Principled Algorithm:** To design and validate Hierarchy-Aware Credit Assignment (HICRA), a novel RL algorithm that concentrates optimization efforts on high-impact planning tokens, thereby accelerating the exploration and reinforcement of effective high-level reasoning.
6. **Validate the Hierarchical Reasoning Hypothesis:** To empirically confirm the existence and emergence of a two-phase learning dynamic in LLMs during RL training, where procedural correctness is followed by strategic exploration, across various LLM and VLM architectures.

Ultimately, the research aims to transform the opaque "black box" of RL-enhanced LLM reasoning into a more transparent, structured process, enabling the development of more effective and insight-driven AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employ a multi-faceted methodology that combines cognitive science insights, a novel token classification scheme, empirical analysis of training dynamics, and the development of a new RL algorithm.

#### 3.1. Functional Decomposition of Reasoning

Drawing inspiration from human cognitive architecture, the paper proposes a functional decomposition of LLM-generated tokens into two categories:
- **High-level Planning Tokens:** These tokens orchestrate the reasoning process, manifesting as logical maneuvers like deduction ("we can use the fact that"), branching ("let’s try a different approach"), and backtracing ("but the problem mentions that").
- **Low-level Execution Tokens:** These are the operational building blocks, including arithmetic calculations, variable substitutions, and direct application of formulas.

#### 3.2. Strategic Grams (SGs) as a Functional Proxy

To empirically distinguish between planning and execution tokens, the authors introduce "Strategic Grams" (SGs). SGs are defined as n-grams (specifically, 3-5 grams) that function as a single semantic unit to guide logical flow. This data-driven approach avoids subjective manual annotation and addresses linguistic diversity. The pipeline for identifying SGs involves three steps:
1. **Semantic Clustering:** Extract n-grams from a large corpus of successful reasoning solutions, embed them using `sentence transformers`, and cluster semantically equivalent phrases.
2. **Identification by Frequency:** Compute "Cluster Document Frequency" (Cluster DF) for each cluster, identifying those that appear frequently across diverse solutions (reusable scaffolding) but sparingly within individual solutions.
3. **SG Construction:** Select clusters with the top 20% Cluster DF. The union of n-grams within these clusters forms the final set of SGs. A sensitivity analysis confirmed the robustness of this SG set.

#### 3.3. Analysis of Emergent Hierarchical Reasoning

The authors empirically analyze the RL training process across eight different text-only and vision-language models (Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen3-4B, Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, MiMO-VL-7B). They track the learning dynamics through two phases:
- **Phase 1: Procedural Consolidation:** Monitored by tracking the relative perplexity (lower = higher confidence) and token-level entropy (lower = confident exploitation) of *execution tokens*.
- **Phase 2: Strategic Planning Exploration:** Monitored by tracking the "Semantic Entropy" of strategic grams (quantifying the diversity of high-level strategic plans) and the conditional entropy of subsequent procedural n-grams. Correlations between strategic diversity and performance metrics (accuracy, reasoning chain length) are observed.

#### 3.4. Hierarchy-Aware Credit Assignment (HICRA)

Based on the insight that strategic planning is the key learning bottleneck, the authors propose HICRA, an algorithm that modifies the GRPO framework. HICRA amplifies the advantage signal for planning tokens (identified by SGs) while dampening the penalty, using a hyperparameter `α` (set to 0.2). This creates an anisotropic policy update, focusing optimization pressure on strategic elements.

#### 3.5. Experimental Validation

- **Models and Datasets:** Experiments were conducted on open-source LLMs (Qwen, Llama) and VLMs (Qwen-VL, MiMO-VL), trained on DAPO, DeepScaleR, and ViRL39K datasets.
- **Benchmarks:** Evaluation used a suite of challenging text-only (AIME, Math500, AMC, Minerva, Olympiad) and multimodal (MathVista, MathVision, MathVerse, EMMA) benchmarks, employing Pass@1 accuracy with strict answer matching.
- **Baselines:** Performance was compared against the Base model, standard GRPO (with clip-higher), and an Entropy Regularization baseline (GRPO with uniform token-level entropy regularization).
- **Error Analysis:** GPT-4o was used to classify error types in failed rollouts into "Planning & Strategy" vs. "Others" to determine where RL applies the most leverage.
- **Exploration Metrics Comparison:** Semantic entropy was compared against token-level entropy and Pass@K to demonstrate its superior ability to track strategic exploration. The relationship between planning tokens and "high-entropy fork tokens" was also investigated.

This comprehensive approach allows the researchers to both theoretically frame and empirically validate their hypothesis regarding emergent hierarchical reasoning and its implications for RL algorithm design.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings that collectively provide a deeper understanding of RL's impact on LLM reasoning and demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed HICRA algorithm.

#### 4.1. Emergence of a Two-Phase Reasoning Hierarchy

The core finding is the consistent observation of a two-phase dynamic during RL training across diverse LLMs and VLMs.
- **Phase 1: Procedural Consolidation:** Initially, models rapidly consolidate low-level procedural skills. This is evidenced by a sharp decrease in the relative perplexity and token-level entropy of *execution tokens* (e.g., arithmetic, variable substitution). The model quickly gains confidence in basic operational steps. For stronger or instruction-tuned models, this phase can be brief or even appear absent, suggesting they already possess a solid procedural foundation.
- **Phase 2: Strategic Planning Exploration:** Once procedural reliability is achieved, the learning bottleneck shifts to high-level strategic planning. This phase is characterized by a distinct and sustained increase in the *semantic entropy of strategic grams*. This indicates that the model is actively exploring and expanding its repertoire of diverse high-level strategies (deduction, branching, backtracing). Crucially, this strategic diversification directly correlates with significant and sustained improvements in overall reasoning accuracy and the average length of reasoning chains ("length-scaling").

#### 4.2. HICRA's Superior Performance

HICRA consistently and significantly outperforms baseline models, including the widely adopted GRPO, across both text-only and multimodal mathematical reasoning benchmarks (Tables 1 & 2).
- **Text-only Models:** HICRA demonstrates substantial gains (e.g., +5.4% on AIME24 for Qwen3-4B-Instruct and +6.1% on AIME24 for Qwen3-4B-Base), validating that targeted optimization on strategic planning is more effective even for highly capable base models.
- **Multimodal Models:** The effectiveness of HICRA extends to Vision-Language Models (e.g., MiMO-VL-Instruct and Qwen2.5-VL-7B), showing improvements on benchmarks like MathVista and MathVision, thereby demonstrating the generality of the approach.

#### 4.3. RL Primarily Corrects Strategic Faults

An error analysis using GPT-4o to classify failed rollouts reveals that RL's primary leverage comes from rectifying "Planning & Strategy" errors. The reduction in these high-level strategic faults is significantly more pronounced than the reduction in low-level procedural errors. This finding strongly supports the hypothesis that the strategic bottleneck is indeed the key to unlocking advanced reasoning, as a perfectly executed incorrect plan still leads to failure.

#### 4.4. Targeted vs. Indiscriminate Exploration

The experiments clearly demonstrate the superiority of HICRA's targeted strategic exploration over indiscriminate token-level diversity:
- HICRA maintains a higher *semantic entropy*, which directly correlates with better and more stable validation accuracy.
- An entropy-regularized baseline, which uniformly promotes *token-level entropy*, successfully increases it but fails to translate this into performance gains, often leading to stagnation or uncontrolled verbosity. This highlights that "exploration" must be intelligently directed.

#### 4.5. Semantic Entropy as a Reliable Exploration Metric

The paper establishes *semantic entropy* as a more reliable diagnostic tool for tracking genuine strategic exploration compared to traditional metrics:
- *Token-level entropy* often "collapses" during training because low-level execution tokens become highly confident, misleadingly suggesting a cessation of exploration.
- *Pass@K* metrics quickly saturate, becoming ineffective for distinguishing ongoing learning dynamics.
- In contrast, semantic entropy remains high and accurately reflects the expansion of the model's strategic playbook, directly correlating with improved validation accuracy.

#### 4.6. Distinction Between Planning and High-Entropy Tokens

While a majority of functionally-defined planning tokens are indeed high-entropy (aligning with their role as decision points), the inverse is not true. Most high-entropy tokens do *not* serve a strategic planning function; they often correspond to non-strategic variations in phrasing or calculation. This highlights the limitations of using high-entropy alone as a proxy for strategic importance and justifies the functional definition used in this work.

#### 4.7. HICRA's Dependency on Procedural Foundation

An important caveat is that HICRA's effectiveness is predicated on the base model possessing a reasonable foundation for low-level procedural correctness. If this foundation is lacking (e.g., in some Llama-3.1-Instruct experiments), HICRA's enforced strategic exploration can be counterproductive, leading to unstable learning and reduced effectiveness.

These findings collectively provide strong empirical evidence for the emergent hierarchical reasoning in LLMs, offer a compelling explanation for previously puzzling phenomena, and validate HICRA as a more efficient and principled RL algorithm.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in understanding and enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models, with profound implications for both fundamental AI research and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Deepening Fundamental Understanding of LLMs

The most critical contribution is providing a mechanistic understanding of how RL improves LLM reasoning. By identifying an "emergent functional reasoning hierarchy" and a "two-phase learning dynamic" (procedural consolidation followed by strategic exploration), the paper moves beyond empirical observations to offer a theoretical framework. This framework unifies explanations for previously puzzling phenomena like "aha moments" and "length-scaling," attributing them to the discovery and mastery of high-level strategic reasoning. This fundamental insight transforms LLM learning from a black box process into a more interpretable and designable system.

#### 5.2. Advancing RL Algorithm Design

The revelation that the learning bottleneck dynamically shifts to strategic planning offers a "clear blueprint" for more efficient RL algorithm design. HICRA, as a demonstration of this principle, significantly outperforms agnostic credit assignment methods like GRPO. By concentrating optimization efforts on sparse, high-impact planning tokens, HICRA achieves accelerated development of advanced reasoning abilities. This sets a new standard for designing targeted RL algorithms, potentially reducing computational costs and improving performance across various complex tasks.

#### 5.3. Improved Diagnostic and Evaluation Tools

The introduction of "semantic entropy" as a robust metric for tracking strategic exploration is a crucial methodological advancement. Unlike token-level entropy (which often collapses) or Pass@K (which saturates), semantic entropy reliably reflects the expansion of a model's strategic playbook and correlates with sustained performance improvements. This provides researchers with a more accurate compass to guide and evaluate future RL training efforts.

#### 5.4. Guiding Future Research Directions

The work opens several promising avenues for future research:
- **Rethinking Action Space:** It challenges the prevailing token-level optimization paradigm, suggesting a paradigm shift towards treating "semantic, strategic units" as the primary action space for policy optimization. This could lead to novel architectural designs or credit assignment mechanisms.
- **Process-Oriented Evaluation:** The emphasis on strategic choice, even if the final answer is flawed, calls for developing new process-oriented evaluation and reward shaping methods. Such methods could provide more nuanced feedback, fostering robust reasoning pathways rather than solely focusing on final correctness.
- **Generalizability to Other Domains:** The universality of hierarchical reasoning in complex tasks (e.g., human cognition) suggests that these principles could be applied to other challenging AI domains like complex code generation, robotic control, and agentic tool-use, where strategic planning is paramount.
- **Cognitive Science Connection:** By drawing strong parallels between LLM learning and human cognitive processes, the research strengthens the interdisciplinary bridge between AI and cognitive neuroscience, potentially inspiring reciprocal insights.

In conclusion, this paper provides a groundbreaking perspective on how LLMs learn to reason under RL, not only offering a more profound understanding of current capabilities but also laying the groundwork for developing the next generation of more intelligent, efficient, and interpretable AI systems.
