# R+X: Retrieval and Execution from Everyday Human Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> R+X learns robot skills from long, unlabelled first-person human videos: a VLM retrieves relevant clips for a language command, then Keypoint Action Tokens performs few-shot in-context imitation over extracted 3D hand-joint trajectories, executing tasks with no training or finetuning, outperforming monolithic language-conditioned policies on 12 household tasks.

## Problem

- Prior learning-from-video methods need manually aligned human-robot videos, labelled demonstrations, MoCap hardware, or fixed third-person cameras, limiting in-the-wild use.
- Monolithic language-conditioned policies require costly finetuning of large models and suffer catastrophic forgetting when new tasks are added over time.
- Existing video-imitation approaches often replicate only object trajectories or single isolated skills, failing at diverse manipulations like pushing or pressing from unlabelled footage.

## Method

- Retrieval phase: prompts ==Gemini 1.5 Flash== VLM with the full unlabelled human video and a language command to extract timestamped clips depicting the requested behaviour, without manual annotation.
- Sparse 3D representation pipeline: ==DINO== descriptor clustering extracts common visual 3D keypoints per clip, while ==HaMeR== estimates 21-joint MANO hand trajectories, stabilised via ==CLIPSeg== segmentation and ==TAPIR== keypoint tracking.
- Execution via **Keypoint Action Tokens (KAT)**: conditions an off-the-shelf LLM on retrieved keypoint-trajectory pairs as ==few-shot in-context== learning to generate hand-joint trajectories for a live observation, mapped to gripper poses by grasp/press/push heuristics.

## Results

- Across 12 everyday tasks with 10 trials each, R+X averages **0.70** success versus **0.55** for R3M-DiffLang and **0.53** for Octo baselines.
- On hard language generalisation (out-of-distribution commands like 'give me something to call my mom'), R+X succeeds via VLM retrieval while baseline policies fail to interpret the commands.
- Sequential task learning over **6** tasks shows R3M-DiffLang and Octo degrade on old tasks from catastrophic forgetting, while R+X performance stays stable with no retraining needed; strong success reached with as few as **10** retrieved clips.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Splitting language-conditioned imitation into distinct retrieval and execution phases lets each fully exploit foundation-model strengths without any task-specific finetuning.
> - Representing scenes and actions as sparse, semantically grounded 3D keypoints rather than raw pixels yields stronger spatial and distractor generalisation than end-to-end policies.
> - Retrieval-based execution sidesteps catastrophic forgetting entirely, since new skills are added by growing the video pool rather than retraining a monolithic network.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning from human demonstrations and observation is a vibrant area, driven by the goal of making robots more autonomous and capable of performing a wide array of tasks in unstructured environments. Traditionally, this domain has faced significant challenges related to data collection, annotation, and generalization.

Many prior approaches to learning robot skills from human videos have relied on a set of strong, often costly, constraints:
- **Manual Data Alignment/Labeling:** Videos often need to be manually aligned with robot data, or painstakingly labeled with language descriptions.
- **Specialized Hardware:** Some methods require motion capture (MoCap) gloves or dedicated teleoperation hardware to collect precise action data.
- **Fixed Camera Views:** Much research assumes a fixed, third-person camera perspective, limiting "in-the-wild" applicability.
- **Single Task Focus:** Robots often learn single tasks in isolation, hindering broader applicability.
- **Computational Expense:** Language-conditioned policies, while powerful, typically require enormous computational effort for training and finetuning, making continuous adaptation difficult due to catastrophic forgetting.

R+X addresses these limitations directly by proposing a framework that operates under significantly relaxed assumptions. It tackles the problem of learning from "long, unlabelled, first-person videos of humans performing everyday tasks in everyday environments." This aligns with the emerging trend of leveraging abundant "in-the-wild" data, particularly from wearable devices like AR headsets and smart glasses, which are expected to generate vast quantities of passive, first-person visual data.

The work strategically leverages the capabilities of **Foundation Models**, a dominant paradigm in contemporary AI. By integrating Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for retrieval and in-context imitation learning models for execution, R+X positions itself at the forefront of combining powerful general-purpose AI with the specific challenges of robotics. Crucially, it demonstrates how these powerful models can be used *without finetuning* on robot-specific data, differentiating it from methods like RT-2 or PaLM-E that require substantial training on robotics datasets.

Furthermore, R+X distinguishes itself from other retrieval-based methods in robotics by retrieving directly from unlabelled human videos based on a language query, rather than retrieving pre-trained policies or requiring an additional demonstration as a query. It also offers a more comprehensive approach to skill learning than methods focused solely on object trajectory replication or limited linear movements, by tracking human hands directly to facilitate more articulated and dexterous tasks. In essence, R+X represents a significant step towards scalable, generalizable, and adaptable robot learning from ecologically valid human data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of R+X is to enable robots to learn and execute a wide range of everyday skills from **long, unlabelled, first-person videos of humans**, driven by natural language commands. This core objective is broken down into several specific aims and underpinned by clear motivations:

**Key Objectives:**
1. **Eliminate Manual Annotation:** A central goal is to remove the need for any manual labeling or alignment of human videos. The framework should interpret raw, uncurated footage.
2. **Immediate Skill Execution (Zero-shot/Few-shot):** Robots should be able to perform commanded skills immediately after retrieval, without requiring a separate, time-consuming training or fine-tuning period on the retrieved video clips. This emphasizes efficient and adaptive learning.
3. **Leverage Foundation Models Without Fine-tuning:** The work aims to harness the powerful visual understanding and reasoning capabilities of large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and in-context learning techniques without incurring the prohibitive computational cost and complexity of fine-tuning these massive models for robotics.
4. **Robust Generalization:** The system should exhibit strong generalization capabilities across various real-world challenges, including:
 - **Spatial Generalization:** Adapting to diverse object placements and environments.
 - **Object Generalization:** Handling previously unseen objects of the same category.
 - **Language Generalization:** Interpreting nuanced and complex language commands.
 - **Distractor Robustness:** Performing tasks effectively in the presence of irrelevant objects.
5. **Enable Continual Learning:** The framework should support sequential learning of new tasks over time without suffering from catastrophic forgetting, a common problem in deep learning where learning new information erases old knowledge.

**Motivation:**
- **Abundance of Future Data:** The authors foresee an unprecedented opportunity for scalability with the proliferation of wearable devices (e.g., AR headsets, smart glasses) that will passively record vast amounts of first-person human activity videos. R+X seeks to unlock the learning potential of this data.
- **Scalability and Cost Reduction:** Traditional methods of collecting robot data (teleoperation, expert demonstrations) are expensive, time-consuming, and require active human involvement. R+X proposes a more scalable paradigm where robots passively learn from human everyday life, reducing the need for dedicated teaching efforts.
- **Bridging Human and Robot Experience:** The goal is to allow robots to acquire skills in a natural, human-centric manner, by observing and imitating human behavior as depicted in real-world, unconstrained videos.
- **Foundation Model Advantages:** VLMs offer inherent abilities in understanding language and visual information. R+X is motivated to effectively transfer these rich capabilities to the robotic domain in a practical and efficient way, circumventing the challenges of retraining monolithic robot policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

R+X operates as a two-stage pipeline: **Retrieval** and **Execution**, designed to translate long, unlabelled human videos into actionable robot skills based on a language command. The framework leverages Foundation Models extensively to address the inherent challenges of cross-embodiment learning and unstructured data.

**Overall Pipeline Inputs and Output:**
- **Inputs:**
 1. A single, long, unlabelled first-person video (H) of a human performing various everyday tasks.
 2. A language command (L) specifying the task to be executed.
 3. A live RGB-D observation (O_live) from the robot's camera.
- **Output:** A trajectory of 6-Degrees of Freedom (6-DoF) gripper poses for the robot to execute.

**Challenges Addressed by Foundation Models:**
The paper explicitly highlights several non-trivial challenges that R+X tackles using Foundation Models:
- **Lack of Language Information:** The human video (H) is unlabelled.
- **Lack of Action Information:** No robot-specific joint or end-effector data is recorded, requiring inference of movements solely from video (cross-embodiment).
- **Environmental Diversity:** Videos contain distractors, varied illumination, and diverse camera viewpoints and motion.

**A. Retrieval Phase: Extracting Visual Examples from a Long, Unlabelled Video**
The first stage aims to identify and extract short, relevant video clips from the long human video (H) that depict the requested task (L).

1. **Vision Language Model (VLM) for Retrieval:**
 - **Tool:** Gemini Pro 1.5 Flash, a powerful multi-modal VLM capable of processing video and text.
 - **Process:** Gemini is prompted with the entire human video (H) and the language command (L). It then outputs the start and end times, identifying a list of Z shorter video clips [V_1..., V_Z] demonstrating the desired task. Each clip consists of T RGB-D frames.

2. **Preprocessing Videos into a Sparse 3D Representation:**
 To make the retrieved video clips suitable for robot control, they are converted from pixel-based RGB-D frames into a lower-dimensional, sparse 3D representation capturing key visual and action information.

 - **Visual 3D Keypoints (Λ):**
 - **Tool:** DINO, a Vision Foundation Model that generates powerful visual representations.
 - **Process:** From the first frame of each of the Z retrieved clips, DINO descriptors are computed. A clustering and matching algorithm then identifies a set of K common 3D visual keypoints (D ∈ R^(K×384)) that focus on the objects of interest, as their appearance is consistent across demonstrations while distractors vary. For each of the K descriptors, its 2D coordinates are found in all frames of the Z videos and then projected into 3D using depth information and camera intrinsics. This results in Λ = [K_V1..., K_VZ] ∈ R^(Z×K×3), a list of K 3D keypoints for each clip.
 - **Hand Joint Actions (M):**
 - **Tool:** HaMeR, a 3D hand pose estimation model based on DINO.
 - **Process:** HaMeR extracts the 3D pose of the human hand (represented by 21 3D MANO hand model joints) from each frame of every retrieved video clip.
 - **Stabilization Technique:** A custom technique is employed to account for camera movement in first-person videos. It uses CLIPSeg (an open-vocabulary segmentation model) to identify static parts of the scene (e.g., floor, wall, table) and remove dynamic elements (e.g., human arms, hands). Random 2D pixel keypoints are sampled in these static areas, tracked across frames using TAPIR, and projected to 3D. These 3D points are then used to compute relative SE(3) rigid transformations, expressing all hand joints relative to the first frame of each video clip. This yields M = [J_V1..., J_VZ], a list of Z sequences of 3D hand joint actions.

 The output of the retrieval phase is a collection of Z input-output pairs comprising visual 3D keypoints and corresponding hand joint action sequences ([Λ, M]), ready to serve as context for imitation learning.

**B. Execution Phase: Few-Shot, In-Context Imitation from Video Examples**
The second stage uses the retrieved demonstrations to generate robot actions for a new, live observation.

1. **In-Context Imitation Learning Model:**
 - **Tool:** Keypoint Action Tokens (KAT), a few-shot, in-context imitation learning model. KAT leverages pre-trained Large Language Models (LLMs) to extract numerical patterns from input-output pairs without explicit training.
 - **Process:**
 - From the robot's live RGB-D observation (O_live), its visual 3D keypoints (K_live) are extracted by computing DINO descriptors and finding K nearest neighbors to the previously derived descriptors (D).
 - KAT receives the retrieved examples ([Λ, M]) as context (Z examples of desired input-output mapping) and the live visual 3D keypoints (K_live) as the new input.
 - KAT then generates a new trajectory of desired 3D hand joint actions, J_live.

2. **Mapping Hand Joints to Gripper Poses:**
 A custom procedure maps the predicted 3D human hand joint trajectories (J_live) to 6-DoF gripper poses for the robot. This mapping uses different heuristics based on the type of interaction:
 - **Grasping:** Aligns gripper finger tips with human index and thumb tips, defining a rotation axis and position.
 - **Pressing:** Assumes a closed gripper and aligns three points on the gripper (tip, middle, bottom) with the human index finger joints (tip, pip, mcp, dip).
 - **Pushing:** Similar to pressing, but aligns gripper points with joints halfway between the human index and middle fingers.
 - **Heuristic Selection:** Gemini automatically determines which heuristic to use based on its understanding of the language command (L). Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) is used to match gripper pose with hand joints.

**Robot Setup:**
- The human video (H) was collected using an Intel RealSense 455 worn on the chest.
- The robot setup uses a Sawyer robot equipped with a RealSense 415 head-camera and a Robotiq 2F-85 two-fingered parallel gripper.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate R+X focused on its ability to learn and execute a diverse set of 12 everyday household tasks, comparing its performance against strong baselines and analyzing its generalization capabilities.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Human Video:** Collected with a chest-worn Intel RealSense 455, then filtered to include only frames with human hands.
- **Robot Tasks:** 12 everyday tasks (e.g., "Plate in Rack," "Grasp Beer," "Turn On Light") involving grasping, opening, inserting, pushing, pressing, and wiping actions with common household objects (Fig. 5).
- **Evaluation Protocol:** 10 evaluations per task, with randomized object pose, type/number of distractors, and object generalization (swapping objects for different instances of the same class).
- **Baselines:**
 - **R3M-DiffLang:** A language-conditioned policy combining a frozen ResNet-50 R3M visual encoder, Sentence-BERT for language encoding, and a Diffusion Policy head.
 - **Octo:** A fine-tuned version of the "octo-small-1.5" generalist robot policy.
 - Both baselines were trained on datasets generated by autonomously captioning snippets of the human video (H) using Gemini, creating (observation, actions, language) triplets.

**Key Findings and Results:**

1. **Overall Performance on Everyday Tasks (Table I):**
 - R+X achieved an average success rate of **0.7** across the 12 tasks, significantly outperforming R3M-DiffLang (0.55) and Octo (0.53).
 - This demonstrates R+X's ability to effectively translate unlabelled human videos into robust robot skills and validates the benefit of its two-stage retrieval and execution design, leveraging Foundation Models.

2. **Hard Spatial Generalization (Fig. 6, middle):**
 - R+X maintained strong performance (e.g., for "grasp a can" or "grasp a beer") even when objects were placed in challenging, elevated positions (e.g., on a box or microwave) not seen in the base training scenarios.
 - The baselines experienced a noticeable drop in performance under these hard spatial variations. This superiority is attributed to R+X's 3D keypoint representation and geometric data augmentation in KAT, which provides stronger spatial generalization.

3. **Hard Language Generalization (Fig. 6, left):**
 - R+X successfully interpreted and executed nuanced, out-of-distribution language commands such as "give me something to call my mom" (picking up a phone), "give me a non-alcoholic drink" (grasping a Fanta can), and "make the room less dark" (turning on a light).
 - The monolithic language-conditioned policies (baselines) struggled to interpret these complex commands, indicating R+X's superior ability to leverage Gemini's advanced language and video understanding for context-aware retrieval.

4. **Distractors Generalization (Fig. 6, right & Fig. 10):**
 - R+X exhibited greater robustness to the presence of random distractor objects in the scene.
 - This advantage stems from its keypoint extraction pipeline, which focuses on semantically and geometrically meaningful features common to the target objects, effectively filtering out irrelevant distractors that vary in the human video and during execution. Baselines, processing raw observations, had to learn to generalize to distractors during training.

5. **Sequential Learning and Catastrophic Forgetting (Fig. 7):**
 - R+X demonstrated the ability to continually learn new tasks without experiencing catastrophic forgetting of previously learned skills. Its performance on old tasks did not deteriorate when new tasks were added to its knowledge base.
 - In contrast, R3M-DiffLang and Octo showed clear performance degradation on old tasks when fine-tuned sequentially with new task data.
 - While retraining baselines on all accumulated data each time could prevent forgetting, this leads to substantially increasing training times, making R+X's "no training/finetuning" approach significantly more scalable for lifelong learning.

6. **Unseen Objects Generalization (Fig. 11 & Fig. 12):**
 - R+X proved robust to novel, unseen objects within the same category (e.g., different cans, kettles, pieces of clothing).
 - This is attributed to the keypoint extraction pipeline's capacity to identify generalizable, semantically and geometrically meaningful features that transfer across objects of similar types.

7. **Impact of Number of Retrieved Videos (Fig. 13):**
 - R+X could achieve strong performance even with a relatively small number of retrieved video clips (as few as 10) for a given task, indicating efficiency in leveraging contextual examples.

In summary, the experiments conclusively demonstrate that R+X provides a robust, generalizable, and scalable solution for teaching robots new skills from passive, unlabelled human observations, significantly outperforming monolithic language-conditioned policies by strategically integrating Foundation Models for retrieval and in-context imitation learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The R+X framework represents a significant advancement in robot learning from observation, with profound implications for the future of robotic autonomy and human-robot interaction.

**Key Significance Points:**

1. **Democratization of Robot Skill Acquisition:** By eliminating the need for costly, time-consuming, and specialized data collection (teleoperation, MoCap) or meticulous manual video annotation, R+X democratizes robot skill learning. It makes the vast, naturally occurring data of human activity directly accessible for robot education, significantly lowering the barrier to entry for developing new robot capabilities.
2. **Scalability and Lifelong Learning:** The ability to learn from long, unlabelled videos and to do so without requiring explicit training or fine-tuning of robot policies offers unprecedented scalability. This is crucial for robots that need to continuously acquire new skills throughout their operational lifetime without suffering from catastrophic forgetting. R+X's architecture natively supports continuous adaptation by retrieving from an ever-growing corpus of human experience.
3. **Harnessing Foundation Models Effectively:** R+X demonstrates an ingenious strategy for leveraging the immense knowledge and reasoning capabilities embedded within large Vision-Language Models. Instead of the computationally prohibitive approach of fine-tuning these models for specific robot embodiments and tasks, R+X uses them as powerful tools for intelligent retrieval and in-context learning. This "retrieval-augmented execution" paradigm is more efficient and adaptive, making the benefits of frontier AI models more practical for robotics.
4. **Robustness and Generalization in Real-World Settings:** The demonstrated capabilities in hard spatial, language, and distractor generalization are critical for deploying robots in complex, unstructured real-world environments. Robots will inevitably encounter variations in object placement, unexpected obstacles, and nuanced human commands. R+X's inherent robustness to these challenges, largely due to its 3D keypoint representation and VLM-driven interpretation, ensures more reliable and adaptable robot behavior.
5. **Paving the Way for Wearable Robotics Integration:** The framework is explicitly designed to learn from first-person, wearable camera data. This vision aligns perfectly with the anticipated proliferation of AR headsets and smart glasses, which will generate an abundance of personal visual data. R+X provides a blueprint for how future robots could learn directly from their human companions' daily lives, seamlessly integrating into human-centric environments.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Accelerated Robot Deployment:** R+X could significantly accelerate the development and deployment of robots in homes, offices, and service industries by providing a natural and efficient way for them to learn a wide repertoire of useful skills.
- **More Intuitive Human-Robot Interaction:** The ability to understand complex language commands and learn from natural human demonstrations makes human-robot interaction more intuitive and less reliant on specialized programming or interfaces.
- **Foundation for General-Purpose Robots:** By enabling continuous, adaptable learning from a broad spectrum of human activities, R+X pushes the field closer to realizing truly general-purpose robots capable of operating in diverse, dynamic settings.
- **New Research Avenues:** The work opens new research avenues in areas like efficient utilization of vast unlabelled video datasets, advanced VLM integration for robot perception and reasoning, and robust cross-embodiment mapping for diverse robot platforms.

While the authors acknowledge limitations such as precision in hand pose prediction, potential bottlenecks with many similar objects, and inference latency, the core contribution of R+X in overcoming fundamental challenges of data acquisition and generalization through an innovative retrieval-and-execution architecture with foundation models marks a substantial step forward for the field of robot learning.
