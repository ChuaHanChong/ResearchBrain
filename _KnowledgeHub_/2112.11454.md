# GOAL: Generating 4D Whole-Body Motion for Hand-Object Grasping

> [!summary] Summary
> GOAL generates whole-body avatar motion for grasping unseen 3D objects, combining SMPL-X body pose with head, arms, and hands. Two cVAE-based networks, GNet for goal grasp and MNet for auto-regressive in-between motion, jointly predict SMPL-X parameters and Euclidean vertex offsets, refined via optimization. Perceptual studies show realism approaching GRAB ground truth.

## Problem

- Prior human motion generation methods model only major limbs, ignoring hands and head, so avatars cannot realistically reach and orient toward objects.
- Hand-grasp generation methods focus on static grasps in isolation, without coordinating full-body walking, reaching, and gaze toward the target object.
- No existing automatic tool jointly generates coordinated whole-body, head, and hand motion across the much larger combined state space of body and hand dexterity.

## Method

- **GNet**, a ==conditional variational autoencoder== that predicts a goal whole-body SMPL-X grasp pose along with head orientation and hand-to-object vertex offsets given object shape and location.
- **MNet**, an ==auto-regressive network== that inpaints motion between a starting pose and GNet's goal pose, predicting SMPL-X parameter deltas and vertex offsets over 10 future frames per step.
- ==Interaction-aware attention== and ==offset-based optimization== post-processing that use predicted Euclidean vertex offsets as data-driven constraints to refine SMPL-X pose, contact, and foot-ground penetration.

## Results

- Perceptual study rated GNet+optimization hand-object grasp realism at **3.63** versus **2.70** without optimization, approaching ground truth's **3.98** on a 5-point scale.
- MNet-generated motion averaged **3.79** realism across body motion, contact, grasp, and head orientation versus **4.22** for ground truth, a modest gap.
- Ablation over MNet output frame count shows vertex-to-vertex hand error drops from **10.60** at 1 frame to **3.89** at 10 frames, confirming longer horizons improve accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Regressing Euclidean-space offset features alongside non-Euclidean pose parameters, then using them as optimization constraints, outperforms fitting pose parameters directly.
> - Predicting more future frames per auto-regressive step improves convergence and reduces drift, a useful design principle beyond this specific motion task.
> - Decomposing a complex embodied task into a goal-pose generator plus a motion-inpainting generator lets each subproblem stay tractable while preserving overall coordination.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of realistic digital human motion is an area of extensive research with applications across various fields, including film production, video games, augmented reality (AR), virtual reality (VR), and the development of virtual metaverses. Prior work in this domain has often focused on specific aspects of human animation in isolation.

Historically, human motion generation research has primarily concentrated on the major limbs of the body, frequently omitting the head and hands. Many methods treat bodies as isolated entities, without considering interaction with a scene or objects. Conversely, specialized research into hand motion generation has largely focused on synthesizing realistic static grasps of objects, rather than dynamic hand movements integrated with full-body motion. Work addressing body-scene interaction often still neglects the detailed articulation of hands during these interactions.

The existing body of literature includes several sub-categories:
- **Motion generation for bodies in isolation:** This category includes methods for synthesizing 3D skeletons or skinned models (like SMPL) without environmental context, often struggling with long-term coherence or specialized cyclic motions.
- **Motion generation for bodies in 3D scenes:** Approaches here range from extending MoCap databases with contact annotations and using optimization, to deep reinforcement learning for body-scene or hand-object interactions. Geometric methods also exist but can lack robustness to real-world noise. More relevant data-driven methods generate motion between a "start" and a "goal" pose, sometimes involving sub-goals, but typically focus on larger body movements like walking or sitting, not detailed grasping.
- **Motion generation for hands:** This area explores physically plausible hand poses for specific tasks or learns controllers for grasping, often relying on detailed MoCap data and physics simulations. Some work integrates missing hand motion into existing body/object motion, but usually as a separate problem.
- **Pose generation for bodies/hands in 3D scenes:** Methods in this category predict static human poses interacting with scenes or generate specific hand-object grasps, often using deep learning or physics-based reasoning.

This paper identifies a critical gap: the absence of methods capable of simultaneously generating coordinated full-body motion, including head and hands, for an avatar approaching and grasping an unknown 3D object. The problem is complex due to the high-dimensional state space, differing scales of body and hand dexterity, and the necessity for coordinated movement across all body parts. The proposed work, GOAL, distinguishes itself by addressing this integrated problem for the first time, aiming to bridge the gap between isolated body motion, hand grasp, and head orientation generation to produce a cohesive, realistic full-body grasping interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to generate realistic, dynamic, whole-body motion for digital avatars interacting with objects, specifically focusing on the complex action of approaching and grasping an unknown 3D object. The authors aim to synthesize motions that encompass the entire human form—body, head, and hands—in a coordinated and physically plausible manner.

The motivation for this work stems from the recognized limitations of existing motion generation techniques, which often address only fragmented aspects of human-object interaction. Current methods typically fall short in one or more of the following areas:
1. **Partial Body Coverage:** Many approaches generate motion for major limbs but omit the detailed articulation of hands and head, which are crucial for natural object interaction.
2. **Lack of Context:** Bodies are frequently animated "in isolation," without considering scene context or interaction with objects, leading to static or uncontextualized movements.
3. **Static Grasps:** While hand grasps have been studied, the focus has predominantly been on generating realistic *static* hand poses for objects, rather than the dynamic motion sequence leading to the grasp.
4. **Integration Challenge:** Combining solutions from separate sub-problems (body motion, head orientation, hand grasping) is not straightforward. The full action requires precise coordination across different scales of motion and dexterity, ensuring physical constraints and plausibility are met. For instance, a human approaching an object involves walking with foot-ground contact, orienting the head to look at the object, leaning the torso, extending arms, and finally dexterously posing the hands for fine contact and grasping. These steps must be gracefully executed and coordinated.

To address these challenges, the research seeks to develop an automatic tool capable of generating such coordinated full-body grasping motions. The specific objectives are:
- To accept a 3D object, its position, and an initial 3D body pose and shape as input.
- To output a sequence of whole-body poses that transitions smoothly and realistically from the starting pose to a final object grasp.
- To achieve this by developing a two-stage network architecture:
 - **GNet:** To generate a "goal" whole-body grasp that includes a realistic body, head, arm, and hand pose, along with accurate hand-object contact. This must account for physical plausibility and permit variation in grasping poses.
 - **MNet:** To generate the complete motion sequence between the initial pose and the "goal" grasp. This includes synthesizing walking motion with natural foot-ground contact, continuous head orientation towards the object, torso leaning, arm extension, and a smooth, realistic hand-object grasp.
- To ensure the generated motions generalize well to unseen objects and maintain a high level of realism, as evaluated both quantitatively and qualitatively.

By tackling the problem of full-body, head, and hand motion generation for grasping an unknown object simultaneously, this work aims to provide a more comprehensive and integrated solution for synthesizing virtual characters that can interact realistically with their digital environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GOAL (Generating Object-interActing whoLe-Body motions) framework addresses the problem of whole-body motion generation for hand-object grasping using a two-stage deep learning approach, followed by optimization-based post-processing. The method takes a 3D object, its position/orientation, and a starting 3D body pose/shape as input, and outputs a sequence of 3D SMPL-X body poses leading to a grasp.

#### 3.1. Human Model

The SMPL-X statistical 3D whole-body model is utilized, which represents the body, head, face, and hands. SMPL-X meshes (10,475 vertices, 20,908 triangles) are generated from shape (β), pose (θ), and expression (ψ) parameters. The pose parameters (Θ = {θ, t}) include joint rotations (55x6) and global translation (t). For interactions, 400 vertices are sampled from relevant body areas, guided by GRAB heatmaps.

#### 3.2. Interaction-Aware Attention

To focus the network's attention on body parts crucial for interaction (e.g., hands for grasping, feet for walking), an "interaction-aware" attention mechanism is introduced. This is formulated as `I_w(d) = exp(-w * d)`, where `d` is a distance vector from body vertices to object vertices, and `w` is a learnable parameter (set to 5). This exponentially emphasizes vertices closer to the object, providing relevant spatial awareness.

#### 3.3. "Goal" Network (GNet)

GNet is a Conditional Variational Auto-Encoder (cVAE) designed to generate a static "goal" whole-body grasp.
- **Input:** The encoder (E_G) receives SMPL-X parameters (Θ), sampled body vertices (v), body shape (β), head orientation (h), object translation (t_o), and Basis Point Set (BPS) representation of the 3D object shape (b_o). Critically, it also uses `d_b->o`, which are offset vectors from sampled body vertices to the closest object vertices, modulated by the interaction-aware attention.
- **Process:** The encoder maps inputs to a normal distribution (μ, σ) from which a latent grasp code (z_g ∈ R^16) is sampled (re-parameterization trick). During inference, z_g is sampled from a standard normal distribution.
- **Output:** The decoder takes z_g and object conditions (C = [b_o, t_o]) to predict SMPL-X parameters (Θ), a head direction vector (ĥ), and offset vectors (d̂_h) from 99 sampled right-hand vertices to object vertices.
- **Loss Function:** A composite loss `L` is used, including terms for L1 vertex distance (`L_v`, `L_hv`), L2 SMPL-X parameter distance (`L_p`, `L_h`), L1 hand-to-object offset distance (`L_dh`), and the Kullback-Leibler divergence (`L_KL`) for the cVAE.
- **GNet Optimization:** An optimization step refines GNet's initial SMPL-X predictions. It optimizes over SMPL-X pose (θ) and translation (t), initialized with GNet's output. Instead of traditional contact constraints, data-driven constraints from GNet's predictions are used:
 - `E_d`: L1 term between predicted hand-to-object offsets (`d̂_h`) and online-computed hand-to-object offsets.
 - `E_θ`, `E_t`: L2 and L1 coupling terms, respectively, to discourage large deviations from GNet's initial pose and translation.
 - `E_h`: L2 term for head orientation coupling to `ĥ`.
 - `E_f`: A term to enforce foot-ground contact by setting the lowest body vertex's y-coordinate to zero.
 The final energy function `E` is a weighted sum of these terms, minimized via gradient descent (Adam optimizer). This step addresses the empirical observation that networks predict Euclidean interaction features more precisely than non-Euclidean SMPL-X parameters.

#### 3.4. Motion Network (MNet)

MNet generates the motion sequence from the starting pose to the "goal" grasp predicted by GNet using an auto-regressive architecture.
- **Input:** In an auto-regressive fashion, MNet takes a concatenation of:
 - SMPL-X parameters of the last 5 frames (`Θ_t-5:t`).
 - Subject's shape (β).
 - Current frame's sampled body vertex locations (`v_t`) and velocities (`v̇_t`).
 - Hand vertex offsets from the current to the "goal" pose (`d_h_t`).
 - BPS representation of the hand in the "goal" grasping frame (`b_h_g`).
- **Output:** MNet predicts changes (`∆`) in SMPL-X pose (`∆θ_t+10`), translation (`∆t_t+10`), sampled vertex locations (`∆v_t+10`), and hand vertex offsets (`∆d_h_t+10`) for the next 10 motion frames relative to the current frame. These outputs are then fed back as inputs for subsequent iterations.
- **Loss Function:** Similar to GNet, MNet uses loss terms for hand-to-object offsets, body parameters, body vertices, and hand vertices. An additional loss term on foot vertices when close to the ground is included to mitigate "skating" (foot sliding).
- **MNet Optimization:** Post-processing with optimization refines MNet's generated motion to ensure better convergence to the "goal" grasp. This is applied when estimated hand vertices are within 10 cm of the "goal" hand vertex positions.
 - The optimization objective `L` includes terms for hand vertices (`L_hv`) and SMPL-X pose parameters (`L_p`).
 - It uses MNet's predictions as constraints, computing an average hand-vertex velocity (`v̇_h_t`) and linearly interpolating hand movement towards the "goal" grasp (`v_h_t+1 = v_h_t + ||v̇_h_t|| * l̂`). The rest of the body maintains MNet's predicted pose and velocity.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

Both networks are trained on the GRAB dataset, which contains whole-body SMPL-X humans grasping 3D objects.
- **GNet Data:** Stable right-hand grasp frames (160K training, 26K testing, 12.5K validation), with objects centered horizontally.
- **MNet Data:** Sub-sequences (40K training, 7K testing, 3K validation) from GRAB, starting from the approach until stable grasp. Each sub-sequence is 21 frames long (10 past, 1 current, 10 future), with all frames made relative to the current frame's body coordinate system.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both qualitative and quantitative evaluations of the GOAL framework, demonstrating its capacity to generate realistic and physically plausible whole-body motions for object grasping.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Experiments

- **GNet (Static Grasps):** Initial results from GNet show plausible body poses and head orientations for static grasps. However, the hand grasps exhibit room for improvement in realism. The subsequent optimization step is shown to effectively refine these hand grasps, making them appear more realistic and physically plausible. This suggests that the combination of deep network prediction and targeted optimization is beneficial for achieving high-fidelity poses.
- **MNet (Dynamic Motions):** MNet generates representative motion sequences for a variety of object shapes, locations, and avatar body shapes. The generated motions depict avatars walking towards an object, orienting their heads, reaching, and executing a grasp. Visual examples and a supplementary video illustrate the naturalness and coordination of the full-body movements.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Experiments

**Perceptual Study (Amazon Mechanical Turk - AMT):**
Human evaluators rated the realism of generated grasps and motions using a Likert scale (1-5, unrealistic to very realistic).
- **GNet Evaluation (Table 1):**
 - **GNet (without optimization):** Achieved an average realism rating of 3.60 ± 1.22.
 - **GNet + Optimization:** Showed improvement across all evaluated features, reaching an average rating of 3.93 ± 1.02.
 - Hand-Object Grasp realism significantly improved from 2.70 ± 1.37 to 3.63 ± 1.16, demonstrating the effectiveness of the optimization in refining contact.
 - Overall Grasping Pose (3.98 ± 0.94) and Head Orientation (4.01 ± 0.97) with optimization even slightly surpassed the ground truth (3.78 ± 1.06 and 3.84 ± 1.07, respectively). This is attributed to GNet consistently orienting the head towards the object, whereas some ground truth instances in the GRAB dataset show subjects looking away.
 - Foot-Ground Contact also improved from 3.98 ± 1.06 to 4.10 ± 0.93, surpassing ground truth (3.82 ± 1.11) due to the direct loss term in the optimization process.
 - **Conclusion for GNet:** The optimization post-processing is effective, particularly for hand grasps, bringing the quality of generated static grasps close to ground truth.

- **MNet Evaluation (Table 2):**
 - **GOAL (MNet generated motion):** Achieved an average realism rating of 3.79 ± 1.05 for dynamic grasping motions.
 - **Ground-truth [GRAB]:** Achieved an average rating of 4.22 ± 0.97.
 - **Feature Breakdown:** GOAL's ratings for Overall Body Motion (3.74 ± 0.97), Foot-Ground Contact (3.88 ± 1.14), Final Hand-Object Grasp (3.66 ± 1.05), and Head Orientation (3.86 ± 1.03) indicate a high level of realism, approaching that of ground truth.
 - **Conclusion for MNet:** MNet successfully generates realistic grasping motions that are perceived to be close to ground truth, despite the inherent difficulty of generating dynamic sequences compared to static poses.

**Foot-Sliding Metric:**
- To evaluate physical plausibility, a "foot-sliding" metric was used, identifying frames where the closest foot vertex to the ground moved more than 1cm.
- **Ground truth:** 6.7% of frames exhibited foot-sliding.
- **GOAL-generated sequences:** 13.7% of frames exhibited foot-sliding.
- **Conclusion:** While GOAL shows room for improvement in reducing foot-sliding, its performance is noted to be comparatively better than existing work (on other datasets).

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

**Number of Output Frames for MNet (Table 3):**
- The study investigated the impact of the number of future motion frames MNet generates in each auto-regressive step. Five networks were trained, outputting 1, 2, 3, 5, or 10 frames.
- **Results:** Generating more frames (e.g., 10 frames) consistently led to lower error metrics, including vertex-to-vertex distances for the body, hand, and feet, as well as SMPL-X pose and translation errors.
 - For hand V2V error, increasing from 1 to 10 output frames reduced the error from 10.60 to 3.89.
- **Qualitative Observation:** Networks generating fewer frames sometimes failed to converge to a final grasp, with hands gradually deviating from the object. Generating more frames resulted in smoother motion prediction and better convergence to the desired grasp.

**Generalization:**
- The system was evaluated on 5 withheld objects, demonstrating that GOAL generalizes well to unseen objects, producing natural motions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GOAL framework represents a significant advancement in the field of digital human animation and interaction, primarily by addressing the complex challenge of generating coordinated, whole-body motion for grasping previously unseen 3D objects.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
- **Integrated Full-Body Grasping:** This work is presented as the first method to generate full-body (including body, head, and hands) motion for an avatar approaching and grasping an unknown 3D object. This addresses a critical gap in existing research, which often focuses on isolated components of human motion.
- **Two-Stage Network Architecture:** The novel design of using two specialized networks, GNet for goal grasp generation and MNet for motion "inpainting," provides a structured approach to decompose and tackle the complexity of the problem. This modularity allows for focused learning of both static interaction poses and dynamic transitions.
- **Leveraging Euclidean Interaction Features:** The methodology's exploitation of Euclidean-space interaction features (e.g., 3D vertex offsets) in conjunction with non-Euclidean SMPL-X parameters, combined with optimization-based post-processing, enhances the accuracy and physical plausibility of the generated grasps and motions. This technical novelty improves upon methods that solely rely on SMPL-X parameter regression.
- **High Realism and Plausibility:** Both qualitative and quantitative evaluations, particularly the perceptual studies, indicate that GOAL generates natural and physically plausible grasping motions that approach the realism of ground truth data. This level of fidelity is crucial for compelling virtual character experiences.
- **Generalization to Unseen Objects:** The demonstrated ability of GOAL to generalize well to novel objects is a practical advantage, suggesting its applicability in dynamic virtual environments where object geometries may vary.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enriching Virtual Experiences:** The capacity to generate realistic full-body object interaction motions directly benefits industries such as film, gaming, augmented reality (AR), virtual reality (VR), and the emerging metaverse. It can lead to more immersive and believable virtual characters that interact with their digital worlds in a natural manner.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** GOAL establishes a robust baseline for integrated human-object interaction synthesis. It opens up avenues for future research to expand upon its capabilities, such as:
 - **Longer-Range Motions:** Extending the current framework to synthesize longer walking motions, enabling avatars to approach objects from greater distances.
 - **Scene-Aware Interactions:** Combining GOAL with human-scene interaction models to generate motions that are contextually aware of the broader environment, beyond just the target object.
 - **Multi-Object/Complex Interactions:** Potentially extending the framework to handle interactions with multiple objects or more complex manipulation tasks.
- **Robotics and Human-Robot Interaction:** While not directly addressed, the principles of learning coordinated, dexterous human motion for object interaction could inform research in robotics, particularly in areas requiring human-like manipulation or imitation learning for robot control.

**Social and Ethical Considerations:**
The authors acknowledge the potential for misuse, specifically mentioning the risk of full-body deepfakes given advancements in neural rendering. To mitigate this, they state that their models and code will be made available exclusively for research purposes. This proactive stance reflects an awareness of the broader societal implications of advanced motion synthesis technologies.
