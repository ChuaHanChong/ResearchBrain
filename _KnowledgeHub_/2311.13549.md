# ADriver-I: A General World Model for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> ADriver-I is a driving world model that couples a multimodal LLM with a video latent diffusion model through interleaved vision-action pairs, unifying visual tokens and text-encoded control signals. The MLLM autoregressively predicts current speed and steering angle; the diffusion model then generates future frames conditioned on that action, and the generated frame feeds back, enabling unbounded self-generated driving. It reaches 0.072 m/s speed error and 5.5 FID.

## Problem

- Modular driving stacks split perception, prediction, planning, and control into sequential components, introducing substantial redundancy unlike human drivers who act directly on vision.
- Prior generative driving world models act mainly as scenario generators, producing realistic future video while ignoring low-level control-signal prediction entirely.
- Existing action-plus-generation world models depend heavily on rich priors such as HD maps and 3D bounding boxes, limiting deployment where such annotations are unavailable.

## Method

- ==Interleaved vision-action pairs== unify ==CLIP-ViT== visual tokens with control signals converted to text digits, letting a ==Vicuna-7B== ==MLLM== reason over variable-length history in multi-round conversation. This forms **ADriver-I**.
- A video ==latent diffusion== model built on ==Stable Diffusion 2.1== with temporal modules and reference-video conditioning generates four future frames from history plus the predicted action.
- ==GPT-3.5== translates raw speed and steering numbers into natural motion descriptions, since the diffusion text encoder cannot interpret signed steering values as turn direction.

## Results

- On nuScenes, control prediction reaches **0.072 m/s** and **0.091 rad** L1 error, beating MLP, CNN, and ViT baselines whose speed errors are **0.122**, **0.106**, and **0.103**.
- Future generation attains **5.5 FID** and **97.0 FVD** with no box or map priors, versus **52.6 FID** and **452.0 FVD** for the prior map-conditioned world model.
- Ablations show absolute-number encoding beats English-word and relative-difference encodings, two decimals suffice, and multi-round supervision cuts speed error from **0.078** to **0.072**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Casting continuous control signals as raw absolute digits inside a language model outperforms binned tokens or verbal descriptions, so numeric fidelity matters more than linguistic framing.
> - Supervising intermediate autoregressive action steps, not just the current one, suppresses accumulated error, a cheap training-side fix for recurrent prediction drift.
> - Closing the loop between generator and controller yields unbounded rollouts but degrades when actions change fast, and separately trained modules forgo end-to-end optimization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving research has traditionally followed a **modular pipeline design**, breaking down the complex task into distinct, sequential components: perception (understanding the surroundings), prediction (forecasting object movements), planning (deciding the ego-vehicle's path), and control (executing low-level commands like steering and acceleration). While interpretable and debuggable, this modularity often leads to **redundancy** and **cascading errors**, where inaccuracies in one module propagate and amplify down the pipeline.

In contrast, an emerging paradigm seeks to emulate human drivers, who largely perceive and react in an **end-to-end manner**, directly inferring actions from visual input and possessing an innate ability to predict future scenarios. This desire for more holistic and efficient systems has spurred research into **end-to-end autonomous driving** and **world models**.

ADriver-I positions itself firmly within this evolving landscape, specifically at the intersection of **multimodal large language models (MLLMs)**, **diffusion models**, and **world models for autonomous driving**.
- **End-to-end Autonomous Driving:** The paper acknowledges existing end-to-end methods that directly predict planning results (e.g., waypoints) or use various scene representations. ADriver-I pushes this further by directly predicting low-level control signals, which is more akin to human reaction.
- **MLLMs and LLMs:** The recent explosion of MLLMs (like LLaVA, MiniGPT-4) and LLMs (GPT series, LLaMA) has demonstrated unprecedented capabilities in comprehension, logical reasoning, and generation across different modalities. ADriver-I leverages these advances by framing control signal prediction as a language generation task, converting actions into text-like expressions. This taps into the powerful reasoning abilities of LLMs.
- **Generative Models and Diffusion Models:** The use of diffusion models for high-quality image and video generation has also seen rapid progress. ADriver-I integrates a video diffusion model (VDM) to predict future driving scenes. This is a critical component for building a "world model" – a system that can simulate how the environment evolves based on its actions.
- **World Models for Autonomous Driving:** The paper directly relates to recent works like GAIA-1 and DriveDreamer, which also explore generative world models for AD. ADriver-I differentiates itself by:
 - **Unifying action prediction and future generation:** Unlike GAIA-1 which focuses more on scene generation, ADriver-I explicitly outputs control signals *and* generates future scenes.
 - **Reduced reliance on explicit priors:** Unlike DriveDreamer which uses extensive priors like HD maps and 3D bounding boxes, ADriver-I aims for future generation without such explicit high-level information.
 - **Concept of "infinite driving":** ADriver-I is presented as the first to introduce and demonstrate a system that can achieve "infinite driving" in a self-generated world, representing a significant step towards self-supervised learning and data augmentation in simulated environments.

By unifying control signal prediction and future scene generation within a framework powered by MLLMs and diffusion models, ADriver-I represents a cutting-edge approach that seeks to overcome the limitations of modular systems and move towards more human-like, intelligent autonomous agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind ADriver-I are rooted in addressing the inherent limitations of conventional autonomous driving systems and drawing inspiration from human driving behavior:

1. **Overcoming Redundancy in Modular Systems:** Traditional autonomous driving systems, with their sequential perception-prediction-planning-control pipeline, often introduce substantial redundancy. Each module processes information independently, potentially leading to inefficient data flow and accumulation of errors. The authors aim to replace this with a more unified and streamlined approach.

2. **Emulating Human-like End-to-End Driving:** Human drivers instinctively react to visual cues by directly adjusting steering and pedals, performing actions and anticipating future events simultaneously. This end-to-end, integrated process is a key motivation. ADriver-I seeks to build a system that directly outputs control signals based on visual information and concurrently predicts the near future, mimicking this human cognitive ability.

3. **Unifying Action Prediction and Future Scene Generation:** A central objective is to integrate two crucial capabilities: predicting the ego-vehicle's low-level control signals (e.g., speed, steering angle) and generating realistic future driving scenes. This unification is crucial for developing a comprehensive "world model" where the agent's actions directly influence and are conditioned by the simulated future environment.

4. **Enabling "Infinite Driving" and Self-Learning:** By creating a system that can predict its own actions and the subsequent environmental changes, ADriver-I aims to establish a closed-loop feedback mechanism. This allows the model to "drive in the world created by itself" for extended periods, theoretically enabling infinite driving within a simulated environment. This capability has significant implications for self-supervised learning, data generation, and robust policy learning without constant reliance on real-world data or human-annotated labels.

5. **Leveraging Advances in MLLMs and Diffusion Models:** The paper is motivated by the superior comprehension and generation abilities of recent MLLMs and diffusion techniques. The authors propose to harness these powerful foundation models to address the complex tasks of autonomous driving, particularly by converting traditionally numerical control signals into a format (text-like tokens) that MLLMs can process for reasoning.

In essence, ADriver-I strives to build a more intelligent, adaptable, and efficient autonomous driving system by consolidating traditionally separate functionalities into a cohesive "world model" that learns and operates within its self-generated reality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ADriver-I is conceptualized as a general world model for autonomous driving, fundamentally integrating a **Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM)** and a **Video Diffusion Model (VDM)**. The core innovation lies in the introduction of **interleaved vision-action pairs** which unify visual features and control signals into a common format.

#### 3.1. Overall Pipeline

The system operates in a recurrent, autoregressive manner:
1. **Input:** The MLLM receives historical interleaved vision-action pairs ({Image, Action} for t-1 to t-3) and the current video frame (Image at t).
2. **Control Signal Prediction (MLLM):** The MLLM processes these inputs to predict the low-level control signal (A't, e.g., speed, steer angle) for the current frame.
3. **Future Scene Generation (VDM):** The predicted action (A't) from the MLLM, along with historical frames, conditions the VDM to generate the next four future frames (I't+1 to I't+4).
4. **Infinite Driving Loop:** The crucial step for "infinite driving" involves taking the *predicted* next frame (I't+1) and feeding it back into the MLLM as the "current frame" for the subsequent timestamp. This allows the process to repeat indefinitely, with ADriver-I driving in a world it continuously creates.

#### 3.2. Multi-modal Large Language Model (MLLM)

- **Components:** The MLLM consists of three key modules:
 - **Large Language Model (LLM):** Vicuna-7B-1.5, fine-tuned on LLaMA2, serves as the central reasoning engine.
 - **Visual Encoder:** CLIP-ViT-Large, pre-trained on extensive image-text pairs, extracts visual features from input images.
 - **Visual Adapter:** Two Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) layers, pre-trained by LLaVA-7B-1.5, align the visual features from the encoder with the language feature space of the LLM.
- **Prompt Construction for MLLM:**
 - **Vision-Action Pairs:** Low-level control signals (speed, steering angle) are converted into text-like expressions (e.g., "{The speed is <num_start>3.63<num_end>, The steer_angle is <num_start>-4.555<num_end>}"). These action tokens are then paired with visual tokens extracted from video frames.
 - **System Prompt:** A descriptive system prompt guides the MLLM's reasoning process, setting the context ("You, as the 'ego' car...").
 - **Conversation Structure:** The input follows a Human-Agent conversation format, where the Human prompt provides historical vision-action pairs and the current image, and the Agent responds with the predicted action for the current frame. This multi-round conversation structure allows for flexible input lengths and better context utilization.

#### 3.3. Video Diffusion Model (VDM)

- **Architecture:** The VDM is built upon a latent diffusion model, specifically extending Stable Diffusion 2.1 with temporal-awareness modules (similar to video latent diffusion models).
- **Conditioning:** It is enriched with "history-conditioned functionality" by concatenating given historical frames with the diffusion input. Text condition modules are retained to enable "action-guided future generation," meaning the predicted action from the MLLM (converted to natural language) guides the video generation process.
- **Prompt Construction for VDM:**
 - **Motion Description:** Since the VDM's text encoder lacks the complex reasoning capacity of the MLLM, low-level control signals from consecutive frames are fed to GPT3.5. GPT3.5 then converts these numerical signals into natural language motion descriptions (e.g., "gradually decreases its speed, a gradual right shift"). This higher-level, human-readable prompt conditions the VDM for more accurate and coherent future scene generation.

#### 3.4. Model Training

The MLLM and VDM are trained **separately** and integrated during inference.
- **MLLM Training:**
 - **Pretraining:** Conducted on a large private dataset (1.4M highway vision-action pairs). During pretraining, the LLM is frozen, while the vision encoder and adapter parameters are updated.
 - **Supervised Finetuning (SFT):** Performed on both nuScenes and the private datasets. Only the vision encoder is frozen, and the rest of the MLLM parameters are trained. Cross-entropy loss is used for supervision, as the output is text.
- **VDM Training:**
 - **Pretraining:** Inherits weights from Stable Diffusion and is pre-trained on the private dataset (1.4M samples).
 - **Finetuning:** Further finetuned on the nuScenes dataset (23K video samples).

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- **MLLM:** Trained for 2 epochs for pretraining and SFT, batch size 16. Input image size 336x336. Control signals are scaled (multiplied by 1000) and rounded to 3 decimal places to aid LLM convergence.
- **VDM:** Pretrained for 40k steps (batch size 128), finetuned for 40k steps (batch size 32). Spatial resolution 256x512, video length 8 frames. DDIM sampler with 50 steps used during inference.

This comprehensive methodology allows ADriver-I to process complex multimodal information, reason about actions, generate future scenarios, and crucially, learn and operate within a self-created world.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ADriver-I demonstrates impressive performance in both control signal prediction and future scene generation, highlighting its potential as a general world model for autonomous driving.

#### 4.1. Control Signal Prediction

- **Quantitative Results:**
 - **Cross-Model Comparison (nuScenes):** ADriver-I significantly outperforms three competitive baselines (MLP-Only, CNN-Based, ViT-Based) in predicting speed and steer angle.
 - For speed prediction, ADriver-I achieves an L1 error of **0.072 m/s**, notably better than the baselines (e.g., ViT-Based at 0.103 m/s). It also shows higher accuracy at various thresholds (e.g., A$_{0.01}$ of 0.237 compared to ViT-Based at 0.200).
 - For steer angle prediction, ADriver-I achieves an L1 error of **0.091 rad**, outperforming baselines (e.g., ViT-Based at 0.092 rad). It shows a strong advantage in high-precision accuracy (A$_{0.01}$ of 0.411 compared to ViT-Based at 0.299), suggesting better handling of subtle steering changes.
 - **Cross-Dataset Comparison (Private Dataset):** On the large-scale private dataset (highway scenario), ADriver-I achieves even more impressive L1 errors of **0.035 m/s** for speed and **0.015 rad** for steer angle. This substantial improvement is attributed to the dataset's focus on highway driving (smaller variance in actions) and its larger scale (1.4M vs. 23K samples).

- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations in Figure 5 illustrate that ADriver-I's predicted control signals (speed and steer angle) closely match the ground-truth values across different driving scenarios (e.g., turns, straight driving), demonstrating its ability to reason about appropriate actions from visual context.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Encoding Methods for Control Signal:** Directly encoding control signals as "Absolute Number" (e.g., "The speed is 3.63") yields the best performance (L1 speed 0.072 m/s, L1 steer 0.091 rad) compared to "Num2English" (translating numbers to English words), "Special Token" (binning), or "Relative Diff" (predicting change from previous frame). This suggests the MLLM can effectively process numerical tokens directly.
 - **Number of Decimal Places:** Using two or three decimal places for numerical control signals achieves nearly identical and superior performance (L1 speed 0.073/0.072, L1 steer 0.091/0.091) compared to fewer decimal places (zero or one), indicating that a certain level of precision is necessary for accurate control.
 - **Effectiveness of Multi-Round Conversations:** The multi-round conversation approach, which adds supervision to intermediate action predictions during training, significantly improves speed prediction (L1 speed 0.072 m/s vs. 0.078 m/s for single-round). This suggests that providing continuous contextual history and training with intermediate targets helps reduce cumulative error in the final action prediction.

#### 4.2. Future Scene Generation

- **Quantitative Results:** ADriver-I achieves strong performance in generating future frames:
 - FID (Frechet Inception Distance): **5.5**
 - FVD (Frechet Video Distance): **97.0**
 These metrics indicate high fidelity and perceptual quality of the generated videos. Crucially, ADriver-I achieves this without relying on high-level priors like 3D bounding boxes or HD maps, which were used by other methods like DriveDreamer (52.6 FID, 452.0 FVD).

- **Qualitative Results:** Figure 6 showcases generated future scenes for various driving maneuvers (e.g., gradual left shift, gradual right shift). The generated frames appear realistic and consistent with the driving context and the predicted control signals, demonstrating the VDM's ability to create plausible future environments conditioned on historical visual data and action guidance.

#### 4.3. Joint Control & Generation (Infinite Driving)

The most compelling finding is the demonstration of "infinite driving" in a self-generated world (Figure 7).
- ADriver-I is initialized with only three historical vision-action pairs.
- It then iteratively predicts the current control signal using MLLM.
- Subsequently, it generates future frames using VDM, conditioned on the predicted action.
- The generated next frame becomes the "current frame" for the subsequent cycle, allowing the system to continue driving indefinitely within its own simulated reality.
- The qualitative visualization shows continuous, coherent driving behavior (changes in speed and steer angle) and corresponding scene evolution over a long sequence, entirely self-sustained by the model. This is a novel demonstration of a world model's closed-loop capabilities in autonomous driving.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ADriver-I introduces a significant step forward in the field of autonomous driving and embodied intelligence, offering several profound implications:

1. **Towards Truly End-to-End Autonomous Driving:** By moving beyond traditional modular pipelines to directly predict low-level control signals and generate future scenes, ADriver-I paves the way for more efficient, less redundant, and potentially more robust autonomous systems. This end-to-end approach, inspired by human driving, could reduce latency and simplify system architecture.

2. **Advancement in World Models for Embodied AI:** The concept of a "world model" that can learn the dynamics of its environment and predict future states is central to advanced AI, especially in robotics and embodied intelligence. ADriver-I's ability to unify action prediction and future generation within a closed loop establishes a powerful world model for the complex domain of autonomous driving. This contributes to the broader goal of creating agents that can reason, plan, and act within dynamic environments.

3. **Potential for Self-Supervised Learning and Data Augmentation:** The "infinite driving" capability is perhaps the most impactful aspect. By continuously generating its own driving scenarios and corresponding actions, ADriver-I can theoretically generate an unlimited amount of diverse training data. This could alleviate the immense burden and cost of collecting and annotating real-world driving data, enabling self-improvement and robust policy learning in a self-simulated environment. It opens avenues for training robust perception, prediction, and planning systems without relying solely on real-world datasets, especially for rare or dangerous scenarios.

4. **Leveraging Foundation Models (MLLMs and Diffusion) for Robotics/Control:** The successful integration of MLLMs and diffusion models for a complex real-world task like autonomous driving highlights the generalizability and power of these foundation models beyond their initial applications (e.g., dialogue, image generation). It demonstrates how their emergent reasoning and generative capabilities can be effectively harnessed for embodied control and environmental simulation.

5. **New Insights for Future Research:** ADriver-I provides a fresh perspective for future research by demonstrating a system that "lives in its own world." This could inspire investigations into:
 - **Unified Optimization:** The authors acknowledge the limitation of separate MLLM and VDM training. Future work could focus on end-to-end optimization of a unified comprehension-and-generation framework.
 - **Enhanced Generative Quality:** Improving the VDM's ability to generate high-quality frames, especially under rapid control changes, is critical for robust infinite driving.
 - **Integration of High-Level Reasoning:** Incorporating routing information and navigation maps would allow for long-distance autonomous driving, moving beyond purely reactive or short-term planning.
 - **Scaling Laws for Generative Driving:** Exploring how model performance scales with increasing data and model size, similar to the observed scaling laws in LLMs, could lead to more general and powerful AD world models.

While ADriver-I is still far from deployment-ready due to limitations like VDM quality and the lack of routing information, its conceptual framework and demonstration of infinite driving represent a significant conceptual leap towards truly intelligent, self-learning autonomous systems.
