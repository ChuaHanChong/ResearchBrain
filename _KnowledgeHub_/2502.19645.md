# Fine-Tuning Vision-Language-Action Models: Optimizing Speed and Success

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford University developed an Optimized Fine-Tuning (OFT) recipe for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, substantially increasing their inference speed and task success on new robotic setups, including complex bimanual manipulation, by integrating parallel decoding, action chunking, and L1 regression with continuous actions.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often have slow inference speeds (e.g., 3-5 Hz), making them unsuitable for high-frequency robotic control (25-50+ Hz) required for dexterous manipulation.
- Current VLA fine-tuning methods show unsatisfactory performance on complex tasks, particularly in bimanual manipulation scenarios.
- There is a lack of clear, empirically-validated guidance on how to effectively fine-tune VLAs for optimal speed and success on novel robot setups and tasks.

## Method

- A systematic empirical analysis was conducted on **OpenVLA**, evaluating key design choices for VLA fine-tuning: ==action generation strategy== (autoregressive vs. ==parallel decoding== with ==action chunking==), ==action representation== (discrete vs. continuous), and ==learning objective== (L1 regression vs. diffusion).
- An **OpenVLA-OFT** (Optimized Fine-Tuning) recipe was developed, integrating ==parallel decoding==, ==action chunking==, ==continuous action representation==, and a simple ==L1 regression== objective for efficient and high-performance VLA adaptation.
- Architectural modifications were introduced to support multiple camera views and low-dimensional robot state inputs, with ==Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM)== added to the OFT+ variant for enhanced language grounding.

## Results

- **OpenVLA-OFT** achieved a new state of the art on the LIBERO simulation benchmark with an average success rate of **97.1%** (up from **76.5%**) and a **2**6x** speedup** in action generation throughput (**109.7 Hz**).
- On a real ALOHA bimanual robot, OpenVLA-OFT+ successfully executed diverse dexterous tasks, consistently outperforming other fine-tuned VLAs and achieving up to **15%** higher average success rates, despite being pretrained only on single-arm data.
- The inclusion of Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) was essential for robust language grounding on the ALOHA robot, preventing language-dependent task success from plummeting to chance levels.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining parallel decoding with action chunking drastically improves both VLA inference efficiency and policy performance, increasing OpenVLA's success rate by 14% on LIBERO.
> - Using continuous action representations with a simple L1 regression objective yields comparable task performance to diffusion-based methods while offering significantly faster training and inference.
> - A well-designed fine-tuning strategy enables VLAs pretrained on single-arm data to successfully adapt and even outperform models with bimanual pretraining on complex bimanual manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper operates within the rapidly evolving landscape of **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** in robotics. VLAs represent a significant paradigm shift, building upon the success of large vision-language models (VLMs) by fine-tuning them on extensive robot datasets to enable low-level robotic control. Prior works, such as RT-2, OpenVLA, RDT-1B, and π0, have demonstrated the impressive capabilities of VLAs in executing complex tasks, following natural language instructions, and generalizing across various robots and semantic concepts.

Despite these advancements, a critical challenge remains: the effective **adaptation of VLAs to novel robot setups and tasks**. While pretraining on vast datasets provides a strong generalist foundation, fine-tuning is often indispensable for achieving satisfactory performance in new, unseen environments. The problem, as highlighted by the authors, is the lack of clear guidance on *how to fine-tune VLAs most effectively*, given the myriad possible design choices in action decoding, representation, and learning objectives.

The existing research landscape presents several limitations that this paper addresses:
- **Speed for High-Frequency Control**: Early VLAs, including the original OpenVLA, often rely on **autoregressive action generation**, which is inherently slow (e.g., 3-5 Hz). This speed is insufficient for high-frequency control tasks (25-50+ Hz) common in real-world dexterous manipulation, especially for bimanual robots. Previous attempts to improve efficiency through advanced action tokenization schemes offered some speedups (2-13x) but were still limited by the sequential nature of autoregressive modeling and introduced significant latencies between action chunks.
- **Performance on Complex Tasks**: Autoregressive VLAs, even with parameter-efficient fine-tuning like LoRA, have shown unsatisfactory performance in challenging scenarios such as bimanual manipulation.
- **Computational Cost of Generative Models**: More recent approaches leverage generative models like diffusion to predict multi-timestep action chunks simultaneously, thereby improving throughput. However, these methods introduce new computational trade-offs, including slower training times and multiple denoising/integration steps during inference, impacting real-time deployment. Furthermore, the diverse architectures and learning algorithms within these diffusion-based VLAs make it unclear which design elements are truly critical for performance.

This work critically addresses these gaps by providing a systematic empirical analysis of key fine-tuning design decisions. It seeks to develop a "recipe" that simultaneously optimizes for **both speed and quality**, a balance that remains largely unexplored. Unlike some prior works that might combine a slow VLA with a separate faster low-level controller or rely on online interaction for policy updates, this paper focuses on **end-to-end fine-tuning** of the base VLA through imitation learning, aiming for a self-contained, high-performing, and efficient policy. By doing so, it advances the practical deployment of generalist robot policies, making them more adaptable and responsive in diverse real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation of this research stems from the practical challenges encountered when deploying powerful Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in new robotic environments. While VLAs have demonstrated impressive capabilities in understanding language and performing complex tasks, their direct application to novel robot setups often falls short without adaptation. The core problem this paper aims to solve is the **lack of a clear, empirically-validated methodology for effectively fine-tuning VLAs** to new robots and tasks.

The authors observe that robotics practitioners often default to using the same training recipe as pretraining for fine-tuning, or a parameter-efficient variant thereof. However, it is not evident whether this yields optimal results, and comprehensive empirical analysis of alternative fine-tuning approaches is scarce. Specifically, existing fine-tuning methods for VLAs face two critical limitations:
1. **Slow Inference Speed**: The autoregressive nature of many current VLAs leads to slow action generation (e.g., 3-5 Hz), rendering them unsuitable for high-frequency control applications (25-50+ Hz) essential for dynamic and dexterous manipulation tasks.
2. **Unreliable Task Execution**: Especially on complex bimanual manipulators, current fine-tuning recipes for autoregressive VLAs often yield unsatisfactory performance.

To address these significant limitations, the paper sets forth several key objectives:
- **Systematic Empirical Analysis**: To conduct a thorough study of fundamental design choices for VLA adaptation, including different action decoding schemes (autoregressive vs. parallel generation), action representations (discrete vs. continuous), and learning objectives (next-token prediction vs. L1 regression vs. diffusion). This study uses OpenVLA as a representative base model.
- **Development of an Optimized Fine-Tuning (OFT) Recipe**: To synthesize the insights gained from the empirical analysis into a practical and effective fine-tuning methodology. This recipe should aim to enhance overall VLA performance while simultaneously improving inference efficiency and increasing flexibility in the model’s input-output specifications.
- **Achievement of State-of-the-Art Performance**: To demonstrate that the proposed OFT recipe, when applied to OpenVLA, can achieve new state-of-the-art results on challenging simulation benchmarks (LIBERO) and, critically, on complex, dexterous bimanual tasks in the real world (ALOHA robot).
- **Practicality and Simplicity**: To prioritize an algorithmic approach that maintains simplicity for easier implementation and faster training convergence, making it more amenable for real-world robotics deployment.
- **Open-Source Contribution**: To release the code and pretrained model checkpoints, fostering reproducibility and encouraging further research and adoption within the robotics community.

In essence, the motivation is to bridge the gap between powerful pre-trained VLA models and their practical, high-performance deployment on diverse robotic hardware, thereby enabling more robust and adaptable robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work revolves around a systematic investigation and optimization of fine-tuning strategies for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The authors adopt **OpenVLA**, a 7-billion parameter manipulation policy, as their representative base model. OpenVLA was originally created by fine-tuning the Prismatic VLM on the 1-million-episode Open X-Embodiment dataset. Its original formulation involves autoregressive prediction of 7 discrete robot action tokens per timestep, optimized via a next-token prediction objective using cross-entropy loss, akin to language models.

The authors propose an **Optimized Fine-Tuning (OFT) recipe** based on their empirical findings, implementing it via **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) fine-tuning**. LoRA is chosen for its parameter efficiency, which is crucial given the relatively smaller datasets typically used for fine-tuning (e.g., 500 demonstrations per task suite in simulation) compared to the vast pretraining datasets.

The methodology systematically evaluates three key design decisions for VLA fine-tuning:

1. **Action Generation Strategy**:
 - **Autoregressive Generation (Baseline)**: The original OpenVLA approach, which sequentially predicts each action token, leading to high latency.
 - **Parallel Decoding**: This novel approach modifies the OpenVLA decoder to receive "empty" action embeddings as input. Crucially, the causal attention mask is replaced with a **bidirectional attention mask**, enabling the decoder to predict all actions simultaneously in a single forward pass. This reduces the number of sequential decoder passes from *D* (action dimensionality) to just one.
 - **Action Chunking (AC)**: This technique is integrated with parallel decoding. Instead of predicting a single timestep's action, the model predicts a sequence of *K* future actions (an "action chunk") in one go. For a chunk size *K* and action dimensionality *D*, the model predicts *KD* actions in a single forward pass, dramatically increasing throughput with minimal additional latency. The study uses *K=8* for simulation and *K=25* for real-world experiments.

2. **Action Representation**:
 - **Discrete Actions (Baseline)**: The original OpenVLA discretizes normalized actions into 256 bins, treating them as tokens for next-token prediction.
 - **Continuous Actions**: The authors explore directly predicting continuous action values. This involves replacing the language model decoder's output layer with a separate MLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron) action head that directly maps the final hidden states to continuous action values.

3. **Learning Objective (for Continuous Actions)**:
 - **L1 Regression**: When using continuous action representations, the model is trained to minimize the mean L1 difference between predicted and ground-truth continuous actions. This is a simple and computationally efficient objective.
 - **Conditional Denoising Diffusion Modeling**: Inspired by recent generative approaches, the model learns to predict noise added to action samples, and during inference, it gradually denoises noisy samples via reverse diffusion. While potentially more expressive, this requires multiple forward passes during inference (e.g., 50 diffusion steps in their implementation).

**Additional Architectural Modifications for Flexibility**:
The authors extend the model's capabilities to handle richer input modalities. While the original OpenVLA processes a single camera view, the modified architecture can process:
- **Multiple Camera Views**: Using OpenVLA's dual vision encoder, it extracts patch embeddings from multiple viewpoints (e.g., third-person and wrist cameras). These are projected into the language embedding space.
- **Low-Dimensional Robot State**: Robot proprioceptive information (e.g., joint angles, gripper state) is processed by a separate projection network and mapped into the same embedding space.
All input embeddings (visual features, robot state, language tokens) are concatenated before being fed to the decoder, allowing the model to attend to all available information.

**Enhanced Language Grounding (OFT+)**:
For scenarios where robust language understanding is critical, particularly with multiple camera views introducing potential spurious correlations, the authors augment their recipe with **Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM)**. FiLM infuses language embeddings into the visual representations. Specifically, the average of language embeddings is used to generate scaling (γ) and shifting (β) vectors, which then modulate the visual features (F) through an affine transformation: `FiLM(F|γ,β) = (1 + γ) ⊙ F + β`. A key implementation detail is applying γ and β spatially-agnostically across all visual patch embeddings within each vision transformer block (SigLIP and DINOv2), rather than per patch, to ensure more effective language-vision integration. This augmented recipe is denoted as OFT+.

**Experimental Setup**:
- **Simulation (LIBERO Benchmark)**: Experiments are conducted on four task suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long) using a Franka Emika Panda arm. Policies are fine-tuned via LoRA on 500 expert demonstrations per suite. Evaluation metrics include success rate and inference efficiency (throughput, latency).
- **Real-World (ALOHA Robot)**: Evaluation on a bimanual ALOHA robot platform (two ViperX 300 S arms, 25 Hz control) performing four dexterous manipulation tasks. This setup differs significantly from OpenVLA's pretraining (single-arm, low-frequency, no robot state input). Metrics include task completion scores, language following ability, and inference efficiency.

By systematically isolating and testing these design choices across diverse simulation and real-world benchmarks, the authors build a strong empirical case for their proposed OFT recipe, demonstrating its ability to optimize both the speed and success of VLA adaptation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a comprehensive empirical analysis across simulation and real-world environments, leading to several key findings that inform and validate the proposed Optimized Fine-Tuning (OFT) recipe.

**Key Insights from Design Choice Analysis:**

1. **Impact of Parallel Decoding (PD) and Action Chunking (AC):** The study unequivocally shows that integrating parallel decoding with action chunking offers dual benefits. It not only drastically improves inference efficiency but also significantly boosts policy performance. On the LIBERO benchmark, this combination increased OpenVLA’s average success rate by an impressive 14% (absolute) over the original autoregressive OpenVLA policies. This improvement was particularly pronounced in long-horizon tasks (LIBERO-Long), suggesting that action chunking helps capture temporal dependencies and reduce compounding errors, leading to smoother and more reliable execution.

2. **Superiority of Continuous Action Representations:** Using continuous action representations further enhanced success rates by 5% (absolute) compared to discrete action variants on LIBERO. This is attributed to the higher precision afforded by continuous values, avoiding the information loss inherent in discretization.

3. **Effectiveness of L1 Regression Objective:** A crucial finding is that fine-tuning VLAs with a simple L1 regression objective yields comparable task performance to more complex, generative diffusion-based fine-tuning methods. Critically, L1 regression offers significant advantages in faster training convergence and vastly superior inference speed, making it highly practical for real-world deployment.

**Quantitative Results (LIBERO Simulation Benchmark):**

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** OpenVLA-OFT, an instantiation of the OFT recipe, achieved a new state of the art on the LIBERO simulation benchmark. Its average success rate across four task suites surged from 76.5% (original fine-tuned OpenVLA) to an impressive 97.1%. This performance also surpassed other strong baselines like π0 policies (94.2%).
- **Dramatic Inference Efficiency Boost:** OpenVLA-OFT demonstrated a 26× speedup in action generation throughput compared to the base OpenVLA, reaching 109.7 Hz (from 4.2 Hz) when using 8-step action chunks. This was achieved with a negligible increase in latency per chunk (0.0729 seconds). Even when processing additional inputs (wrist camera image, proprioceptive state), throughput remained high at 71.4 Hz, showcasing remarkable input-output flexibility.
- **L1 Regression vs. Diffusion Efficiency:** While diffusion-based variants could match L1 regression in performance (95.4% vs 95.3% on average LIBERO SR), they incurred significantly higher latency due to the need for multiple denoising steps (e.g., 1.9070 seconds latency for 50 steps at test time, although lower steps improved this trade-off with performance). L1 regression, in contrast, maintained high efficiency.

**Quantitative and Qualitative Results (Real-World ALOHA Robot):**

- **Dexterous Bimanual Task Execution:** OpenVLA-OFT+ (the OFT recipe augmented with FiLM for language grounding) successfully executed diverse and dexterous bimanual manipulation tasks on the real ALOHA robot, including complex tasks like folding clothes and precisely manipulating target food items based on language prompts.
- **Outperformance Against Strong Baselines:** OpenVLA-OFT+ consistently outperformed both other fine-tuned VLAs (π0 and RDT-1B) and prominent imitation learning policies trained from scratch (Diffusion Policy and ACT). It achieved up to 15% (absolute) higher average success rate. This is particularly noteworthy because OpenVLA was pretrained *only on single-arm robot data*, while RDT-1B and π0 had exposure to substantial bimanual pretraining data. This highlights the profound impact of the fine-tuning strategy itself.
- **Robust Language Following with FiLM:** An ablation study on the ALOHA robot revealed that FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation) was essential for strong language grounding. Without FiLM, OpenVLA-OFT's language following ability in language-dependent tasks plummeted to chance levels (33% success), indicating its crucial role in preventing the model from overfitting to spurious visual features and ensuring proper attention to linguistic instructions.
- **Real-time Control:** OpenVLA-OFT+ achieved a throughput of 77.9 Hz for the ALOHA robot with 25-timestep action chunks, demonstrating its capability for real-time robot control. While smaller architectures or JAX-optimized implementations (like ACT or π0) achieved even higher speeds, OpenVLA-OFT+ (a 7.5B parameter model) still approached the speed of RDT-1B (1.2B parameters) due to its single-pass action generation.

**Additional Findings:**

- **Value of OpenVLA Pretraining:** An ablation study confirmed that the base OpenVLA's robot-pretrained representations are indeed beneficial, as removing them (i.e., fine-tuning the Prismatic VLM from scratch with OFT) resulted in a 5.2% (absolute) drop in average success rate on LIBERO.
- **Scalability to Large Datasets:** OpenVLA-OFT proved scalable. A single policy trained on all four LIBERO task suites combined achieved comparable performance to suite-specific policies. Furthermore, OpenVLA-OFT successfully scaled to the much larger BridgeData V2 dataset (50,365 real WidowX demonstrations), surpassing the original OpenVLA checkpoint (69.2% vs 65.8% average success) on a subset of evaluation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Optimized Fine-Tuning (OFT)" recipe proposed in this paper holds significant implications for the field of robot learning and the practical deployment of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

1. **Enabling Practical VLA Deployment:** The most immediate impact is providing a highly effective and efficient recipe for adapting VLAs to novel robotic systems. This addresses a critical bottleneck in VLA research: moving beyond impressive benchmark results to reliable, high-performance real-world deployment. Robotics practitioners can now leverage pre-trained generalist VLAs and fine-tune them with the OFT recipe to achieve strong performance on specific tasks, even on robots not seen during pretraining, without requiring extensive, costly retraining from scratch.

2. **Redefining the Performance-Efficiency Trade-off:** The paper's empirical analysis demonstrates that a simple L1 regression objective, when combined with a high-capacity model and parallel decoding, can match or even exceed the task performance of more complex diffusion-based generative approaches, while offering substantial advantages in training convergence and, crucially, inference speed. This is a game-changer for real-time robotics, where low latency and high control frequency are paramount. It suggests that algorithmic simplicity can lead to superior practical outcomes when carefully designed within the context of large-scale foundation models.

3. **Boosting Generalization and Adaptation Capabilities:** The most striking finding is OpenVLA-OFT+'s ability to adapt a model pretrained *only on single-arm data* to perform complex *bimanual, high-frequency dexterous manipulation* tasks. This adaptation even allowed it to outperform VLAs that were explicitly pretrained on substantial bimanual datasets. This underscores that a well-designed fine-tuning strategy can be *more impactful* than extensive pretraining data coverage for downstream task performance, showcasing the profound adaptability of large vision-language models when appropriately configured. It highlights the importance of effective knowledge transfer mechanisms from general pretraining to specific robotic embodiments.

4. **Increasing Accessibility and Usability:** By simplifying the fine-tuning process and demonstrating the efficacy of straightforward techniques like L1 regression and parallel decoding, the OFT recipe makes VLA deployment more accessible to a broader range of researchers and engineers. This reduces the computational and algorithmic complexity often associated with state-of-the-art robot learning methods.

5. **Setting a New Standard for Benchmarking:** The paper's comprehensive evaluation on both the standardized LIBERO simulation benchmark (setting a new state of the art) and challenging real-world ALOHA tasks provides a robust validation of the OFT recipe. This sets a higher bar for future VLA research, emphasizing the need for practical efficiency alongside task success.

6. **Catalyzing Future Research:** The detailed analysis of design choices, along with the identified limitations (e.g., handling multimodal demonstrations, the exact source of language grounding inconsistencies), opens several promising avenues for future research. The release of code and pretrained models further facilitates reproducibility and enables the community to build upon these insights.

In summary, this work provides a crucial blueprint for unlocking the full potential of Vision-Language-Action models in real-world robotics. By optimizing the fine-tuning process for speed, performance, and flexibility, it significantly advances the state of the art in robot learning, pushing towards more general, adaptable, and deployable robotic agents.
