# SILO: Simulation-in-the-Loop Sim-to-Real Transfer for Multi-Stage Cable Routing

> [!summary] Summary
> SILO is a sim-to-real RL system for multi-stage cable routing. It approximates linear-deformable cables as GPU-parallelized articulated rigid capsules with elastic-plastic joint drives, trains localized PPO routing policies from motion-primitive-generated intermediate states, and deploys via simulation-in-the-loop, where the simulator steps policy actions and the real robot tracks resulting joint targets. It routes 3 harnesses 18/24 with ~2x lower cycle time.

## Problem

- Prior hierarchical imitation learning for cable routing needs thousands of task-specific demonstrations, limiting scalability across cable types, and relies on slow replanning and regrasping primitives.
- Soft-body simulators trade throughput for fidelity, so deformable RL rarely leaves simulation; existing sim-to-real deformable work handles only simple gripper-object interactions.
- Conventional sim-to-real deployment demands system identification and controller tuning; residual PD mismatch and solver residuals still leave roughly 0.5 degrees per-joint tracking error.

## Method

- ==GPU-parallelized cable model==: an articulated chain of rigid capsules joined by three orthogonal revolute joints, with PD drives to rest positions for elasticity and drifting rest targets for plasticity.
- ==Localized RL==: **PPO** trains only the single-harness routing subtask, resetting from 32768 cached intermediate states produced by GraspCable and MoveToHarness motion primitives, under a simple proximity-plus-grasp reward.
- **SILO** deployment plus state estimation: ==FoundationStereo== depth and ==SAM2== masks feed an SVD-binned equal-length polyline fit; policy actions step a ==digital twin== whose simulated joint angles command the real arm.

## Results

- Zero-shot real routing reaches **24/24**, **22/24**, **18/24** for one to three harnesses versus **19/24**, **14/24**, **12/24** for hierarchical imitation learning, at **87.5s** versus roughly **200s**.
- SILO deployment scores **15/20** without contact penalties, beating tuned-controller transfer at **0/20** without penalties and **10/20** with them; three PD settings transfer at **14-15/20**.
- Ablations: intermediate-state randomization from **0** to **0.40m** lifts success **5/20** to **15/20**; observing **4** cable points gives **15/20** versus **1/20** with one point; four cables score **14-18/24**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transferring simulated outcomes rather than actions sidesteps system identification: let the simulator be the controller reference, and the real robot merely tracks its joint targets.
> - Crude rigid-body approximations of deformables can suffice when RL is localized to the deformation-critical subtask and observations capture local curvature rather than global shape.
> - A digital twin at runtime enforces collision-freedom by construction, removing reward-shaping penalties, but demands quasi-static tasks, known fixture geometry, and start-stop synchronization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Industrial automation has historically focused on rigid body manipulation, where object dynamics are predictable, allowing for scripted trajectories or perception-driven motion planning. However, a significant portion of real-world assembly tasks, such as automotive wiring, hose routing, and textile handling, involves manipulating deformable objects like cables and ropes. These objects present a challenge due to their complex, high-dimensional, and often unpredictable deformations, which necessitate continuous, dexterous low-level control.

Existing approaches to deformable manipulation can be categorized into motion planning, imitation learning (IL), and reinforcement learning (RL). Traditional planning-based methods, while effective in specific, constrained settings, typically require extensive manual tuning and engineering, often assume full observability, and are limited in their ability to generalize to new scenarios, thereby impacting real-time performance.

Data-driven methods, particularly imitation learning, have demonstrated a higher degree of generalizability and reduced manual tuning requirements. However, IL approaches, including hierarchical IL (h-IL) which has shown promise in multi-stage cable routing, often depend on large datasets comprising thousands of high-quality demonstrations. Collecting such data for deformable object tasks can be prohibitively costly and impractical.

Reinforcement learning offers an alternative that bypasses the need for explicit demonstrations, training policies through interaction in simulation. While sim-to-real RL has achieved success in rigid-body manipulation and locomotion, its application to deformable objects has been significantly constrained. Prior sim-to-real work on deformables has been limited by slow simulation speeds, inaccurate dynamics models, and reduced generalizability, often restricted to simpler tasks involving only basic interactions between a gripper and a deformable object. The core difficulty lies in establishing both high-throughput simulation for efficient training and a sufficiently accurate simulator for seamless sim-to-real transfer.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing a sim-to-real RL framework specifically for multi-stage cable routing. It leverages advancements in GPU-parallelized simulation, applying rigid-body physics approximations to linear deformables to achieve the necessary training speed. Furthermore, it introduces a novel deployment strategy, Simulation In the LOop (SILO), aimed at bridging the dynamics gap between simulation and reality. By focusing on a complex, multi-stage deformable manipulation task, the paper contributes to extending the capabilities of sim-to-real RL beyond rigid objects, aiming for greater scalability, generalization, and efficiency in industrial automation involving linear deformables.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient sim-to-real reinforcement learning framework for multi-stage cable routing. This framework aims to enable reactive low-level policies that can handle the complex, subtle, and often unpredictable deformations inherent in cable manipulation tasks, facilitating successful transfer from simulation to real-world robotic systems without requiring human demonstrations.

The motivation behind this work stems from several challenges in robotic manipulation of deformable linear objects:

- **Complexity of Deformable Dynamics:** Linear deformable objects, such as cables and ropes, exhibit intricate deformation patterns that are difficult to predict solely from visual observations. These deformations can change abruptly due to contact, gravity, and accumulated slack, demanding reactive, low-level control policies. Traditional robotic systems struggle with this variability, often necessitating substantial task-specific engineering and frequent replanning, which hinders real-time, high-performance execution.
- **Limitations of Prior Data-Driven Methods:** While imitation learning approaches have shown potential, they typically require thousands of task-specific demonstrations, which are costly and impractical to acquire for deformable manipulation. Reinforcement learning, despite its promise for autonomous policy acquisition, has been largely restricted to rigid-body manipulation in sim-to-real contexts. Effective RL requires both fast, high-throughput simulation for training and an accurate simulator for transfer, conditions that have historically been difficult to meet for soft-body objects.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** A significant barrier to deploying RL policies trained in simulation to the real world is the "sim-to-real gap," encompassing discrepancies in dynamics, sensing, and control. For deformable objects, this gap is particularly pronounced due to the approximate nature of soft-body simulators. A robust mechanism to translate policies learned in simulation to reliable real-world performance is therefore critical.
- **Need for Scalability and Generalization:** Industrial applications require robotic systems that can adapt to diverse cable geometries, material properties (stiffness, elasticity), and initial configurations without extensive re-training or manual intervention. Current methods often lack this generalization capability, being tied to narrowly defined settings.
- **Efficiency and Cycle Time Reduction:** Many industrial tasks demand high throughput. Existing learning-based methods, particularly those relying on planning or frequent re-grasping, can incur high cycle times. The research aims to develop a system that not only achieves higher success rates but also significantly reduces execution time.

To address these motivations, the authors identify a key insight: linear deformables can be approximated using GPU-parallelized rigid-body physics. This approximation, coupled with a novel deployment framework, SILO, is hypothesized to enable the learning of reactive low-level policies capable of faster and more reliable cable routing.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology integrates GPU-parallelized simulation, reinforcement learning, a robust state estimation pipeline, and a novel sim-to-real deployment framework named SILO (Simulation In the LOop). The overall goal is to route a linear deformable cable through a sequence of harnesses.

#### 3.1. Simulation Design

The core design principle for simulation is to model linear-deformable objects using rigid-body APIs to prioritize high-throughput for fast RL training, trading off some physical fidelity.
- **Cable Modeling:** A linear-deformable object (cable) is represented as an articulated sequence of rigid capsules (links). Adjacent links are interconnected by three orthogonal revolute joints, allowing for 3D bending.
- **Deformation Behavior:**
 - **Elasticity:** Modeled by driving each joint towards a saved rest position using proportional-derivative (PD) control, parameterized by joint stiffness and damping.
 - **Plasticity:** Modeled by gradually updating each joint's rest position towards its current configuration after each physics timestep, using a "plasticity" coefficient ($\beta$).
- **Engine:** The system leverages the rigid-body APIs in ManiSkill3, which are built on GPU-parallelized PhysX. This allows for tuning parameters (PD gains, plasticity coefficients) to approximate a range of cable behaviors from elastic to plastic.
- **Trade-off:** This rigid-body approximation, while not replicating typical material parameters, provides sufficient state coverage for cable routing and supports sim-to-real transfer at a computationally inexpensive rate.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning Setup

- **Problem Localization:** The complex, long-horizon task of routing a cable through multiple harnesses is decomposed. RL is localized to the single subtask of routing a pre-grasped cable through one harness. This shortens episode horizons, simplifies credit assignment, and reduces simulation costs by allowing shorter cables.
- **Intermediate State Resets:** To generate a diverse and efficient training distribution, a dataset of "intermediate" simulation states is pre-generated. In these states, a set of motion primitives (GraspCable and MoveToHarness) are used to grasp the cable and position the robot's TCP (Tool-Center-Point) at a pose just behind a harness, ready for routing. During RL training, new episodes begin by uniformly sampling from this dataset.
- **Reward Function:** A simple reward function is used:
 - `r_t = exp(−c||l_1 −h_pos||^2) + exp(−c||l_2 −h_pos||^2) + 1_grasped + 1_success`
 - This includes proximity rewards for the two cable links closest to the gripper (`l_1`, `l_2`) to the harness center (`h_pos`), a bonus for maintaining grasp (`1_grasped`), and a bonus for successful routing (`1_success`).
- **RL Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is employed for policy training.

#### 3.3. Sim-to-Real Transfer

The transfer methodology focuses on state-based policies to isolate the dynamics gap from potential vision gaps.
- **Observations:** The policy receives an observation vector comprising the 3D positions of four cable points immediately behind the gripper, the robot's TCP pose, and its joint angles. Gaussian noise is added to cable points during training for robustness.
- **Robust State Estimation (Real World):** To provide sim-to-real compatible cable observations from real-world sensors:
 1. **Depth Image Prediction:** A depth image is generated from stereo RGB images using the Foundation Stereo model.
 2. **Cable Segmentation:** The Segment Anything Model 2 (SAM2) predicts a segmentation mask for the cable, which is then refined (shrunk) using a distance transform.
 3. **Segmented Cable Pointcloud:** The segmented depth is projected into a 3D point cloud in the world frame using calibrated camera parameters. Outlier removal is performed.
 4. **Polyline Fitting:** An ordered, equal-length polyline is fitted to the processed cable point cloud. This involves computing the dominant direction via SVD, binning projected points to get an initial ordered polyline, and then resampling to ensure equal spacing between points, matching the simulation's cable discretization. This reconstructs the required 4 cable points.
- **Motion Primitives:** Two motion-planning-based primitives handle structured segments of the task:
 - **GraspCable:** Robot approaches a cable endpoint and closes the gripper.
 - **MoveToHarness:** Robot's TCP moves to a predefined pose `p_i` behind harness `i`.
 These primitives are used both for generating intermediate training states and for managing the high-level task flow during real-world deployment, with the RL policy taking over for the critical routing maneuvers.

#### 3.4. SILO Deployment Paradigm

SILO (Simulation In the LOop) is a novel deployment framework designed to mitigate the sim-to-real dynamics gap without extensive system identification.
- **Principle:** Instead of transferring policy actions directly to the real robot, SILO executes actions within a digital twin (simulator) and then commands the real robot to track the *resulting simulated joint angles*.
- **Process (Algorithm 1):**
 1. Initialize simulator with real harness poses and the robot's state.
 2. Read real robot joint positions (`q_real`).
 3. Set simulated robot joints (`q_sim`) to `q_real`.
 4. Estimate cable points (`c_hat`) from real-world perception.
 5. Construct observation (`o`) using `q_sim` and `c_hat`.
 6. Compute action (`a`) from the RL policy (`π(o)`).
 7. Execute action (`a`) in the *simulator*.
 8. Command the *real robot* joints to move to the *simulated robot's next joint angles* (`q_sim_t+1`).
- **Advantages:**
 - **Controller Agnosticism and Flexibility:** Bypasses the need for precise alignment between simulated and real-world controllers, as the simulator generates the joint targets.
 - **Inherent Safety and Collision Avoidance:** Since actions are pre-executed in a digital twin that includes environmental constraints (like harnesses), the real robot receives collision-free joint targets, reducing reliance on explicit collision penalties in reward design.
 - **Reduced Engineering Overhead:** Reuses the same simulation codebase for training, observation generation, and deployment, minimizing duplicated logic, errors, and simplifying debugging.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the proposed SILO framework on multi-stage cable routing tasks, focusing on zero-shot sim-to-real performance, generalization across cable types, and the effectiveness of the SILO deployment method through ablations.

#### 4.1. Main Performance Results

- **Zero-shot Sim-to-Real Performance:**
 - The SILO approach demonstrated superior success rates compared to prior state-of-the-art learning methods (hierarchical imitation learning, h-IL) and scripted baselines. For routing through 1, 2, and 3 harnesses (H1, H2, H3), SILO achieved success rates of 24/24, 22/24, and 18/24, respectively. In contrast, h-IL reported 19/24 for H1, 14/24 for H2, and 12/24 for H3.
 - SILO also achieved a significant reduction in cycle times. Routing through three harnesses took 87.48 seconds with SILO, approximately a 2x reduction compared to h-IL, which had an estimated cycle time of around 200 seconds. This efficiency gain is attributed to the RL policy directly handling cable reshaping, eliminating frequent re-grasping and planning iterations seen in h-IL.
 - Failures with multiple harnesses were primarily associated with configurations involving large relative orientation changes between harnesses, leading to extreme cable entry angles.

- **Generalization:**
 - The learned policies exhibited robust zero-shot generalization across four distinct cable types (Nylon Rope, Ethernet Cable, Charger Cable, HDMI Cable), varying in materials and stiffness. Success rates for routing through three harnesses remained consistent, ranging from 14/24 to 18/24 across these cable types.
 - The thicker HDMI cable (8.0mm diameter) showed slightly lower success (14/24), suggesting that cable diameter can become a limiting factor in scenarios with small clearances between harnesses.

- **Learned Strategies:** Analysis of real-world rollouts revealed two emergent behaviors learned by the RL policy:
 - **Angling Behavior:** The gripper oriented itself to drive the cable closer to the harness opening, pushing the cable down to facilitate more reliable seating.
 - **Swinging Behavior:** The policy exhibited reactive lateral movements, swinging to one side of a harness depending on the cable’s curvature to aid successful routing. These behaviors were not explicitly programmed in the reward function.

#### 4.2. Ablations

Ablation studies were conducted to understand the factors contributing to the method's success, focusing on single-harness routing with controlled entry angles from -60° to 60°.

- **Intermediate State Sampling (Randomization):**
 - Increasing the randomization of initial cable states (specifically, translation along the y-axis) during training substantially improved real-world success rates. Policies trained with 0.0m randomization achieved 5/20 success, while those with 0.4m randomization achieved 15/20 success. This indicates that diverse training data is crucial for robustness.

- **Deployment Method (SILO vs. Tuned Controller):**
 - SILO deployment outperformed a baseline where the simulation PD gains were manually tuned to match the real robot controller. SILO achieved 15/20 success, whereas the tuned baseline without contact penalties achieved 0/20, and with contact penalties achieved 10/20.
 - SILO's advantage stems from its reduced sensitivity to controller discrepancies and its inherent collision avoidance mechanism, which simplifies reward design by negating the need for explicit contact penalties. The paper notes that even after tuning, a mean error of 0.5 degrees per joint remained between the real and simulated controllers in the baseline.

- **Controller Flexibility (SILO):**
 - To demonstrate SILO's controller agnosticism, policies were trained with different simulation controller PD values (low, medium, high). All policies demonstrated comparable success rates in real-world deployment (14/20 to 15/20), indicating that SILO allows zero-shot deployment regardless of specific simulation controller parameters, without additional manual tuning.

- **Observation Space (Cable Points):**
 - An ablation on the number of observed cable points demonstrated the importance of capturing cable curvature. Observing only 1 cable point resulted in 1/20 success. Increasing to 2 points significantly improved performance (11/20), enabling angling and swinging behaviors. Observing 4 points yielded the highest success rate (15/20). Exceeding 4 points led to diminishing returns and increased sensitivity to visual occlusions, hindering transfer. This indicates that sufficient information about cable curvature is critical for reactive routing.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of deformable object manipulation in robotics, particularly for multi-stage cable routing.

- **Pioneering Sim-to-Real Transfer:** To the authors' knowledge, this work represents the first successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of reinforcement learning policies for multi-stage cable routing. This demonstrates the feasibility of applying RL to complex deformable object tasks that previously relied on costly demonstrations or highly engineered planning systems.
- **Effective Deformable Simulation:** The approach validates the use of GPU-parallelized rigid-body physics to approximate linear-deformable dynamics. This method offers a computationally efficient alternative to high-fidelity soft-body simulators, enabling the large-scale parallel training necessary for modern RL algorithms. The ability to model elastic and plastic behaviors with this approximation is a significant contribution to scaling deformable manipulation research.
- **Novel Deployment Framework (SILO):** The introduction of SILO (Simulation In the LOop) as a deployment paradigm is a key innovation. By treating the simulator as the primary source of truth for robot motion during deployment, SILO effectively mitigates the sim-to-real dynamics gap, making policies robust to controller discrepancies and eliminating the need for extensive system identification or manual controller tuning. Its inherent collision avoidance mechanism also simplifies reward design and enhances safety in real-world operations.
- **Enhanced Performance and Efficiency:** The framework achieves higher success rates and a significant reduction in cycle times (approximately 2x faster) compared to prior state-of-the-art imitation learning methods. This demonstrates the potential for RL to drive efficiency in industrial automation tasks.
- **Generalization Capabilities:** The demonstrated zero-shot generalization across diverse cable types and harness configurations, without requiring specific demonstrations for each variant, highlights the robustness and practical applicability of the learned policies.
- **Modular Approach for Complex Tasks:** The strategy of localizing RL to deformation-critical subtasks while relying on classical motion primitives for grasping and free-space motion offers a scalable and effective decomposition strategy for long-horizon, complex manipulation problems. This hybrid approach leverages the strengths of both learning-based and classical robotics.
- **Broad Applicability:** While evaluated on cable routing, the underlying principles—approximated linear-deformable dynamics, localized RL, robust state estimation, and the SILO deployment—are broadly applicable to a wider range of linear-deformable manipulation tasks.

**Limitations:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations. Firstly, it assumes *a priori* knowledge of harness geometries and poses, which is common in industrial settings with CAD models but may not hold in unstructured environments. For such scenarios, combining high-level planning policies from prior work (e.g., hierarchical-IL) with the proposed RL policies could be a future direction. Secondly, the current SILO implementation requires strict synchronization between the real world and simulation, leading to sequential start-stop motions that constrain it to quasi-static tasks. Extending SILO to more dynamic settings would necessitate improvements in state synchronization and latency-aware control.

**Potential Future Impact:**
This research opens avenues for more widespread adoption of RL in industrial automation involving flexible objects, from assembly lines to maintenance tasks. Future work could focus on replacing explicit state estimation with vision-based observations leveraging large-scale visual domain randomization, scaling to more diverse and complex harness geometries, and developing cable-specific perception models for improved robustness under challenging sensing conditions. The insights from this work provide a foundation for developing highly autonomous and adaptable robotic systems capable of handling the inherent complexities of deformable materials.
