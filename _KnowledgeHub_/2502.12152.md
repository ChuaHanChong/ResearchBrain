# Learning Getting-Up Policies for Real-World Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> The HUMANUP framework from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign enables humanoid robots to autonomously recover from diverse fall configurations on varied real-world terrains, achieving faster and smoother recovery than hand-designed controllers while demonstrating efficient motor utilization.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots frequently fall in unstructured environments, requiring significant human intervention for recovery, which limits their autonomous deployment.
- Hand-designing robust fall recovery controllers is exceedingly difficult due to the unpredictability of post-fall robot configurations and the variety of terrains.
- Traditional learning-based methods for locomotion are not directly applicable to getting-up tasks due to their non-periodic nature, rich whole-body contacts, and sparse reward signals.

## Method

- The proposed **HUMANUP** framework uses a novel ==two-stage RL curriculum==: a ==Discovery Policy== (Stage I) for efficient trajectory generation and a ==Deployable Policy== (Stage II) for refinement.
- Stage I uses simplified simulation and weak regularization to discover a high-speed, effective trajectory, addressing reward sparsity and non-periodic behavior.
- Stage II refines this motion using the full robot ==URDF== model, extensive randomization of **20,000** initial lying postures and diverse terrains, and strong control regularization for ==Sim2Real== transfer.

## Results

- HUMANUP achieved real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid, recovering from falls on **6 diverse terrains** including concrete, muddy grass, and snow fields.
- Achieved a **78.3%** success rate for getting up from supine and **98.3%** for rolling over in real-world tests, significantly outperforming the manufacturer's controller which failed on most challenging terrains.
- Recovery time was **~6 seconds**, more than twice as fast as the manufacturer's **11 seconds**, with smoother motions and optimized motor usage producing significantly lower and safer arm motor temperatures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A two-stage curriculum separating motion discovery from deployable policy refinement is crucial for learning complex, contact-rich tasks for real-world robots; single-stage training failed to converge.
> - Accurate physical modeling (full URDF) and extensive domain randomization (initial postures, terrains, dynamics) are essential for successful simulation-to-real transfer in contact-rich scenarios.
> - Strong control regularization is indispensable for generating smooth, safe, and generalizable policies for real robot deployment, even if it slightly reduces raw simulation success rates.
