# HP-Edit: A Human-Preference Post-Training Framework for Image Editing

> [!summary] Summary
> Huawei Noah	's Ark Lab, Harbin Institute of Technology, and Nankai University researchers developed HP-Edit, a post-training framework to align image editing models with human preferences using a VLM-based scorer and an efficient dataset construction pipeline. This framework achieved an overall HP-Score of 4.667 on the new RealPref-Bench, outperforming the Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 baseline (4.472) and other state-of-the-art methods across various editing tasks.

## Problem

- Existing supervised fine-tuning (SFT) based image editing models often produce results that do not fully align with real-world human preferences due to diverse training data.
- Constructing large-scale, high-quality human preference datasets for image editing is resource-intensive and expensive, limiting the scalability of human feedback methods.
- The application of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) to diverse image-to-image (I2I) editing tasks is underexplored, lacking task-aware reward models and dedicated benchmarks.

## Method

- **HP-Edit**, a post-training framework, was developed, integrating a ==Visual Large Language Model== (VLM)-based HP-Scorer, an efficient hard-case-focused dataset construction pipeline, and task-aware RL post-training.
- Created RealPref-50K, a scalable dataset of over 50,000 real-world image editing cases, filtered by the HP-Scorer to focus on 'harder' examples, providing more informative gradients for RL.
- Employed the Flow-Group Relative Policy Optimization (Flow-GRPO) algorithm for online RL post-training, using the optimized VLM-based HP-Scorer as a reward model to guide the base diffusion editing model.

## Results

- HP-Edit achieved an overall HP-Score of 4.667 on the RealPref-Bench, showing an improvement over the Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 baseline (4.472) and surpassing other state-of-the-art models like Step1X-Edit (4.07).
- The framework consistently ranked first across nearly all eight editing sub-tasks on RealPref-Bench, with notable improvements in tasks such as color change (4.75) and background replacement (4.733).
- A user study validated the HP-Scorer's accuracy, demonstrating a strong Pearson correlation coefficient of **0.89** between human and HP-Scorer ratings on the GEdit-Bench-EN, confirming its reliability as an evaluator.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimized VLM-based scorers can effectively and scalably approximate human judgment for complex image editing tasks, significantly reducing the need for extensive manual annotation.
> - Filtering datasets to focus on 'harder' cases (where baseline models struggle) provides more effective gradients for preference learning, leading to more substantial performance improvements.
> - The combination of a carefully constructed, preference-focused dataset and a task-aware VLM-based reward model is crucial for achieving superior alignment with human preferences in diffusion-based image editing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Image-to-image (I2I) editing and text-to-image (T2I) generation have become fundamental applications in content creation, with generative diffusion models widely adopted as the leading paradigm due to their capacity for high-quality and controllable outputs. State-of-the-art I2I editing models typically build upon pre-trained T2I backbones through Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), utilizing extensive I2I datasets to acquire editing capabilities. However, a limitation of SFT-based approaches is the potential misalignment of generated content with real-world human preferences, often stemming from mixed data sources (e.g., synthetic images, cartoons). Furthermore, constructing large-scale datasets specifically aligned with human preferences for image editing is resource-intensive and expensive due to the requirement for manual annotation, limiting scalability.

In parallel, Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated effectiveness in enhancing the reasoning and alignment capabilities of large language models (LLMs). More recently, methods such as Diffusion-DPO and Flow-GRPO have extended RL from Human Feedback (RLHF) to improve the quality of T2I generation through post-training. Despite this progress, the application of RL-driven human-preference alignment to I2I editing remains underexplored. I2I editing presents a dual objective: it requires both task accuracy (e.g., precise object removal) and strong preference alignment, leading to natural and aesthetically pleasing results. This complexity necessitates specialized frameworks that can efficiently construct preference data without prohibitive annotation costs and employ task-aware reward models tailored to diverse editing sub-tasks. Additionally, the existing research landscape for image editing lacks comprehensive real-world, object-balanced benchmarks, which impedes accurate evaluation of preference-aligned editing models.

This work introduces HP-Edit as a framework designed to bridge these identified gaps. It focuses on efficiently applying RLHF to diffusion-based image editing by developing a scalable method for building preference datasets and an automatic evaluation mechanism, thereby enhancing alignment with human preferences in real-world editing scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and implement HP-Edit, a post-training framework that aligns image editing models with human preferences. This objective is driven by several motivations rooted in the limitations of existing image editing paradigms:

1. **Addressing Misalignment with Human Preferences:** Current SFT-based image editing models, while powerful, often produce results that do not fully align with human aesthetic judgments or expectations for realism in real-world scenarios. This misalignment stems from the diverse and sometimes non-realistic sources of pre-training data. The motivation is to enhance the visual quality and realism of edited images to better match human preferences.
2. **Overcoming High Annotation Costs for Preference Data:** Constructing large-scale, high-quality human preference datasets for image editing is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. This cost impedes the scalable application of RLHF to image editing. A key motivation is to devise an efficient and scalable method for acquiring or synthesizing preference-aligned data without extensive manual annotation.
3. **Developing a Task-Aware Reward Model for Diverse Editing:** Unlike open-ended T2I generation, I2I editing involves a variety of distinct sub-tasks (e.g., object removal, style transfer, relighting), each with specific requirements for accuracy and visual quality. Existing RLHF approaches for generative models are not directly transferable to this diverse set of tasks. The motivation is to create a reward mechanism that is sensitive to the nuances of different editing tasks and can accurately reflect human judgment across them.
4. **Establishing a Real-World, Object-Balanced Benchmark:** The absence of a standardized benchmark specifically designed for evaluating human-preference-aligned image editing in real-world contexts hinders rigorous comparison and advancement in the field. This motivates the creation of a new benchmark that addresses these criteria.
5. **Leveraging Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) for I2I Editing:** While RLHF has shown success in LLMs and T2I generation, its application to I2I editing, particularly for complex and diverse tasks, is largely unexplored. The motivation is to adapt and extend RLHF methodologies, specifically Flow-GRPO, to effectively post-train diffusion-based image editing models.

To address these motivations, the work outlines three specific contributions:
- **HP-Edit Framework:** To provide a comprehensive solution for human preference-aligned editing, integrating a Visual Large Language Model (VLM)-based scorer, an efficient hard-case-focused dataset construction pipeline, and task-aware RL post-training.
- **RealPref-50K Dataset:** To supply a scalable, real-world-oriented dataset (over 50,000 cases) covering eight common editing sub-tasks and balanced across common MS-COCO object categories, ensuring practical relevance and diversity.
- **RealPref-Bench Benchmark:** To establish a robust evaluation tool comprising real-world images and manually verified preference instructions, enabling rigorous and consistent assessment of model performance in preference-aligned editing.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HP-Edit framework consists of three main stages: the optimization of the Human Preference–Scorer (HP-Scorer), an efficient dataset construction pipeline for preference learning, and a task-aware RL post-training stage.

#### 3.1. HP-Scorer Optimization (Stage 1)

The first stage involves developing the HP-Scorer, an automatic evaluator designed to approximate human judgments.
- **Initial Data Collection:** A small set of editing cases (approximately 50–100 per editing sub-task), comprising an input image, an instruction, and an edited output from a pre-trained model, is collected. These triples are then manually rated by human annotators on a 0–5 scale, based on criteria covering correctness, realism, and adherence to instructions.
- **VLM-based Scorer Development:** A pre-trained Visual Large Language Model (VLM), such as Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct or GPT-4o, is employed as the foundation for the HP-Scorer.
- **Prompt Optimization:** An iterative refinement process is used to optimize task-specific scoring prompts. This starts with a basic prompt incorporating the general 0–5 scoring guidelines and progressively adds task-specific reasoning questions (e.g., for object swapping: "Is the original object completely replaced?"). This iterative process continues until the HP-Scorer’s ratings closely match the human annotations on the small validation set. The score output by the HP-Scorer is directly used as the HP-score for subsequent evaluation and reward.

#### 3.2. Human Preference Data Construction (Stage 2)

This stage focuses on creating RealPref-50K, a scalable and preference-focused dataset, by leveraging the HP-Scorer.
- **Raw Dataset Collection:** A large number of real-world image editing cases are collected from high-quality, open-source datasets (e.g., Pixabay, LSDIR, DIV2K). This raw dataset (D) aims for balanced coverage across various MS-COCO object categories.
- **Instruction Generation:** A VLM automatically generates textual editing instructions for the input images. CLIP scores are used to measure similarity between images and category embeddings, facilitating a balanced distribution of object categories.
- **Edited Image Generation:** Pre-trained editing models (e.g., Qwen-Image-Edit-2509) are used to generate edited images, forming triplets of (input image, edited image, instruction).
- **Hard-Case Filtering:** The generated triplets are then scored by the optimized HP-Scorer. A critical step is filtering this dataset. Recognizing that pre-trained models often perform well on "easy" cases (receiving a score of 5), the framework discards these high-score samples. The resulting dataset, denoted as D†, primarily comprises "harder" cases (scores less than 5) that offer more informative gradients for RL post-training. This strategy ensures the model focuses on improving areas where it currently struggles.

#### 3.3. Task-aware RL Post-Training (Stage 3)

In the final stage, the filtered dataset D† and the HP-Scorer are used for online reinforcement learning.
- **Reward Model:** The HP-Scorer functions as the reward model, providing a score (s) for each generated edited image based on the input image and instruction. This score is then normalized using a sigmoid function to produce a final reward (r) between 0 and 1: `r = 1 / (1 + exp(−α * s + β))`, where α and β are scaling and shift parameters set to 2 and 5, respectively.
- **Flow-GRPO Implementation:** The online Flow-Group Relative Policy Optimization (Flow-GRPO) algorithm is employed for post-training the pre-trained editing model. Flow-GRPO reformulates the iterative reverse-time sampling process of a flow matching model as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). It computes advantages by comparing rewards within a group of samples generated from the same prompt, normalizing each sample's reward relative to the group's statistics. The policy model parameters are updated to maximize an objective function that encourages actions leading to above-average rewards while maintaining stability via a KL divergence constraint. To enable stochastic sampling required by GRPO, the deterministic ODE of standard Flow Matching is converted into an equivalent Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE).
- **Training Details:** The open-source Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 is used as the base pre-trained model. Most of its parameters are frozen, and a lightweight LoRA (rank 32) is fine-tuned using the AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 3 × 10⁻⁴. During online RL training, Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct serves as the HP-Scorer due to its more stable latency compared to external APIs.

#### 3.4. RealPref-Bench Benchmark

To enable robust evaluation, RealPref-Bench is introduced. This benchmark comprises 1,638 cases, with approximately 200 instances per sub-task. It uses real-world images and manually verified editing instructions. Consistent with RealPref-50K, the benchmark ensures balanced coverage of common MS-COCO object categories. For evaluation purposes, HP-Scores are computed using GPT-4o to ensure an unbiased assessment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of HP-Edit in aligning image editing models with human preferences, both qualitatively and quantitatively.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Analysis

Qualitative comparisons (Figure 4) demonstrate that images processed with HP-Edit exhibit higher fidelity to editing instructions, increased realism, fewer visual artifacts, and better preservation of scene structure compared to baseline methods. Baseline models such as Step1X-Edit and UniWorld-V1 often introduce distortions during complex edits (e.g., large-area removal or background replacement). FLUX.1-Kontext-Dev occasionally produces results with an unrealistic, "painted" or "stylized" appearance. While the Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 baseline is strong, HP-Edit shows improvements in tasks requiring human aesthetic judgment, which is the primary focus of the framework.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Analysis

Table 1 presents quantitative results on the RealPref-Bench, utilizing the GPT-4o-based HP-Scorer for evaluation.
- **Overall Performance:** HP-Edit achieved an overall HP-Score of 4.667, demonstrating an improvement over the Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 baseline (4.472) and outperforming other state-of-the-art editing models, including Step1X-Edit (4.07), BAGEL (3.65), and X2Edit (3.396).
- **Task-Specific Performance:** HP-Edit consistently ranked first across almost all eight editing sub-tasks. Notable improvements were observed in tasks requiring fine-grained appearance consistency or strong realism priors, such as color change (4.75), bokeh (4.545), relighting (3.913), and background replacement (4.733). These are tasks where pre-trained models often face challenges, indicating that the preference-aligned reward effectively guides the model toward more human-desired outputs.
- **Generalizability:** Further evaluation on GEdit-bench (Table 3), an existing standard benchmark, also showed HP-Edit achieving state-of-the-art performance, outperforming Step1X-Edit and other comparative methods. This suggests that the human-preference alignment strategy developed is generalizable beyond the specific RealPref-Bench.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study (Table 2 and Figure 5) was conducted to evaluate the contributions of the RealPref-50K dataset filtering and the HP-Scorer.
- **BaseData + BaseScorer:** Using the unfiltered raw dataset (BaseData) with a simple primary scoring prompt (BaseScorer) resulted in a slight performance drop (HP-score of 4.391) compared to the pre-trained baseline (4.472). The reward curve for this setting showed minimal improvement, indicating that raw, unfiltered data with many easy cases led to saturated rewards and weak training signals.
- **RealPref-50K + BaseScorer:** Applying the filtered RealPref-50K dataset while retaining the BaseScorer yielded a noticeable improvement (HP-score of 4.577). The corresponding reward curve demonstrated a clear upward trend, confirming that filtering out trivial high-score samples and focusing on harder, low-score examples provides more informative gradients for preference learning.
- **RealPref-50K + HP-Scorer (HP-Edit):** The full HP-Edit framework, combining the filtered dataset and the task-specific HP-Scorer, achieved the highest score of 4.667. Its reward curve showed the most stable and consistent upward trajectory. This confirms that both the high-quality, preference-focused data and the task-aware preference scorer are essential for maximizing alignment with human preferences during RL post-training.
- **LoRA Rank:** An analysis of LoRA ranks (Table S1) indicated that performance improved up to rank 32, but remained stable or declined beyond that, suggesting rank 32 was optimal.
- **Comparison with DPO:** GRPO with HP-Scorer achieved better performance (4.590) than DPO (4.521) on the RealPref-Bench, highlighting the effectiveness of the online GRPO approach.

#### 4.4. User Study

A user study involving five annotators evaluating over 1,000 editing pairs on RealPref-Bench was conducted. The evaluation criteria for instruction adherence and image quality were consistent with the 0–5 scoring scale used for the HP-Scorer.
- **Alignment with HP-Scorer:** The average scores from the user study closely matched the results produced by the HP-Scorer (Figure 6). This provides external validation of the HP-Scorer’s accuracy and its alignment with human judgment.
- **Consistent Improvements:** The user study also showed consistent improvements for HP-Edit over the pre-trained model across all tasks, further validating the framework's effectiveness.
- **Correlation:** A correlation analysis (Figure S1) on GEdit-Bench-EN showed a strong Pearson correlation coefficient (PCC) of 0.89 between human and HP-Scorer ratings, indicating that the HP-Scorer is a reliable evaluator and provides valid reward signals for RL.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HP-Edit framework represents a significant contribution to the field of image editing, particularly in the domain of human-preference alignment.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Addresses Critical Gaps:** The work effectively addresses the identified limitations of current image editing models, specifically the misalignment with human preferences, the high cost of manual annotation for preference data, and the absence of task-aware reward mechanisms and dedicated benchmarks for preference-aligned editing.
- **Scalable RLHF for Image Editing:** HP-Edit provides a scalable and efficient framework for applying Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) to diverse image editing tasks. By leveraging a VLM-based scorer and a hard-case focused data construction pipeline, it makes RLHF practical for complex visual domains.
- **Novel Resources:** The introduction of RealPref-50K, a large-scale, real-world, object-balanced human-preference dataset, and RealPref-Bench, a rigorous evaluation benchmark, provides invaluable resources for future research and development in image editing. These datasets are curated to reflect real-world scenarios and human expectations, fostering more relevant model development and evaluation.
- **Validation of VLM-based Scorers:** The research validates the use of optimized VLM-based scorers (HP-Scorer) as effective and scalable proxies for human judgment in complex visual tasks, significantly reducing the dependency on expensive manual annotation.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Improved User Experience:** By training models to generate edits that align more closely with human preferences, HP-Edit has the potential to lead to more intuitive, user-friendly, and aesthetically pleasing image editing tools. This could benefit professionals and casual users in various industries, from graphic design and marketing to casual photo editing.
- **Accelerated Research:** The framework, dataset, and benchmark can accelerate research in RLHF for visual generation and editing. Researchers can leverage these resources to develop and evaluate new algorithms for preference alignment more efficiently.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** The ability to automatically construct preference datasets and evaluate models using the HP-Scorer can significantly reduce the cost and time associated with developing and fine-tuning advanced image editing models.
- **Broader Applications:** The principles and methodology introduced by HP-Edit could be extended to other generative visual tasks where human preference plays a critical role, such as video editing, 3D content generation, and multimodal content creation.

**Limitations:**
The paper acknowledges a limitation of HP-Edit, noting that it still struggles with code-switching or mixed Chinese-English text editing (e.g., 'Translate the English text into Chinese'). This limitation is largely inherited from the capabilities of the base models used in the framework. Addressing such complex linguistic and visual tasks remains a challenge for future research.
