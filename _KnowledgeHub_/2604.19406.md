# HP-Edit: A Human-Preference Post-Training Framework for Image Editing

> [!summary] Summary
> Huawei Noah	's Ark Lab, Harbin Institute of Technology, and Nankai University researchers developed HP-Edit, a post-training framework to align image editing models with human preferences using a VLM-based scorer and an efficient dataset construction pipeline. This framework achieved an overall HP-Score of 4.667 on the new RealPref-Bench, outperforming the Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 baseline (4.472) and other state-of-the-art methods across various editing tasks.

## Problem

- Existing supervised fine-tuning (SFT) based image editing models often produce results that do not fully align with real-world human preferences due to diverse training data.
- Constructing large-scale, high-quality human preference datasets for image editing is resource-intensive and expensive, limiting the scalability of human feedback methods.
- The application of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) to diverse image-to-image (I2I) editing tasks is underexplored, lacking task-aware reward models and dedicated benchmarks.

## Method

- **HP-Edit**, a post-training framework, was developed, integrating a ==Visual Large Language Model== (VLM)-based HP-Scorer, an efficient hard-case-focused dataset construction pipeline, and task-aware RL post-training.
- Created RealPref-50K, a scalable dataset of over 50,000 real-world image editing cases, filtered by the HP-Scorer to focus on 'harder' examples, providing more informative gradients for RL.
- Employed the Flow-Group Relative Policy Optimization (Flow-GRPO) algorithm for online RL post-training, using the optimized VLM-based HP-Scorer as a reward model to guide the base diffusion editing model.

## Results

- HP-Edit achieved an overall HP-Score of 4.667 on the RealPref-Bench, showing an improvement over the Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 baseline (4.472) and surpassing other state-of-the-art models like Step1X-Edit (4.07).
- The framework consistently ranked first across nearly all eight editing sub-tasks on RealPref-Bench, with notable improvements in tasks such as color change (4.75) and background replacement (4.733).
- A user study validated the HP-Scorer's accuracy, demonstrating a strong Pearson correlation coefficient of **0.89** between human and HP-Scorer ratings on the GEdit-Bench-EN, confirming its reliability as an evaluator.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimized VLM-based scorers can effectively and scalably approximate human judgment for complex image editing tasks, significantly reducing the need for extensive manual annotation.
> - Filtering datasets to focus on 'harder' cases (where baseline models struggle) provides more effective gradients for preference learning, leading to more substantial performance improvements.
> - The combination of a carefully constructed, preference-focused dataset and a task-aware VLM-based reward model is crucial for achieving superior alignment with human preferences in diffusion-based image editing.
