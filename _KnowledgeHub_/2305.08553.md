# Distilling Knowledge for Short-to-Long Term Trajectory Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> A short-to-long knowledge distillation framework, Di-Long, is developed to mitigate future uncertainty in long-term trajectory forecasting. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance, with an ADE of 14.89 and FDE of 19.85 on the inD dataset, and an FDE of 72.41 on the SDD dataset.

## Problem

- Long-term trajectory forecasting is challenged by increasing future uncertainty and complexity over extended horizons.
- Models for long-term prediction often struggle to maintain accuracy and robustness as the prediction length increases.
- Effectively leveraging multi-modal contextual information like scene semantics, social interactions, and long-term goals is crucial but complex for accurate human motion modeling.

## Method

- Di-Long, an autoregressive transformer-based architecture, integrates a goal estimation U-Net and a temporal transformer decoder.
- A short-to-long knowledge distillation framework guides a student network for long-term prediction using a teacher network that observes a longer past and predicts a shorter, less uncertain future segment.
- Knowledge distillation is applied to both the goal estimation and temporal trajectory prediction modules to transfer robust knowledge from the teacher to the student.

## Results

- Di-Long achieved state-of-the-art performance on the inD dataset for long-term forecasting, with an ADE of **14.89** and FDE of **19.85**.
- The framework reduced the FDE to **72.41** on the SDD dataset in the long-term setting, showing improved final position prediction.
- Ablation studies confirmed the distillation framework's positive impact on accuracy, demonstrating consistent performance and lower degradation for prediction horizons up to 60 seconds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Knowledge distillation from a teacher with a longer observation and shorter prediction horizon effectively reduces future uncertainty in long-term trajectory forecasting.
> - Optimizing the teacher's observation length, particularly aligning it with intermediate waypoint extraction, significantly enhances the student's long-term prediction accuracy.
> - Integrating multi-modal inputs, including scene semantic maps, goal/waypoint estimations, and social interactions, is essential for robust and comprehensive human motion modeling.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Trajectory forecasting, encompassing both pedestrian and vehicle movements, is a field of increasing relevance within computer vision, machine learning, and robotics. Its applications span autonomous driving, robotics, video surveillance, and embodied navigation, where accurate prediction of agent movements is crucial for safety and operational efficiency.

Prior research has extensively addressed short-term human trajectory forecasting, focusing on predicting movements over short time horizons (e.g., a few seconds). Various methodologies, including recurrent neural networks (RNNs), convolutional neural networks (CNNs), and graph neural networks (GNNs), have been proposed and refined for this task. However, long-term human trajectory forecasting, which involves predicting motion over significantly extended time horizons, remains a more challenging problem. This difficulty arises from the inherent increase in uncertainty and unpredictability as the future horizon expands, compounded by the complexity of human behavior and the variable nature of future interactions and intentions. The advent of transformer architectures has contributed to a renewed interest in long-range sequence processing, subsequently drawing more attention to transformer-based approaches for long-term trajectory prediction.

Beyond individual agent dynamics, the broader research landscape acknowledges the importance of contextual information in trajectory modeling. This includes environmental semantics, social interactions among agents, and the estimation of long-term goals. Methods have been developed to integrate social pooling mechanisms, generative adversarial networks (GANs) for socially compliant trajectories, and models conditioned on environmental semantic maps or learned long-term/intermediate goals using networks such as U-Nets.

Knowledge distillation (KD), a technique for transferring knowledge from a larger, more expert "teacher" model to a smaller "student" model, has been explored in trajectory forecasting for specific purposes. Previous applications include improving robustness to corrupted data in crowded scenes and transferring knowledge from models with additional modalities (e.g., HD Maps) to map-less student networks. This work positions itself by addressing a gap in the literature: the application of knowledge distillation to explicitly reduce future uncertainty in the context of *long-term trajectory forecasting*. The authors state that, to their knowledge, this particular application of KD has not been previously investigated in this domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the performance of long-term trajectory forecasting models by mitigating the inherent increase in uncertainty associated with longer prediction horizons. The authors identify this escalating uncertainty as a fundamental challenge in long-term prediction tasks.

The motivation for their proposed approach, named Di-Long, stems from the observation that as the time horizon for prediction extends, trajectories become progressively more uncertain and complex to model. To address this, the authors propose leveraging knowledge distillation, a paradigm typically used to transfer expertise from a powerful teacher model to a less powerful student model.

Specifically, the core motivation is to train a student network for long-term trajectory prediction by guiding it with the knowledge distilled from a teacher network. This teacher network is configured to perform a related, but inherently less uncertain, task. The teacher model observes a longer segment of the trajectory than the student and is tasked with predicting a comparatively shorter, remaining future segment. This setup means the teacher's prediction task is less ambiguous and more deterministic, allowing its accurate predictions to serve as a reliable source of guidance for the student.

The rationale is that by transferring this "less uncertain" knowledge from the teacher during the training process, the student network can learn to make more accurate and robust long-term predictions, thereby effectively reducing the future uncertainty that complicates such tasks. The research also aims to integrate multi-modal information—specifically goal prediction, social influence, and the spatial relationship of trajectory points to scene semantic maps—as these factors are recognized as crucial for accurate human motion modeling. The ultimate goal is to develop a method that effectively bridges the gap between short-term observation and long-term prediction, resulting in state-of-the-art performance on established trajectory forecasting datasets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Di-Long, employs a short-to-long knowledge distillation framework built upon an autoregressive transformer-based architecture. The architecture comprises two main components: a goal estimation module and a temporal module, instantiated for both a teacher and a student network.

**Trajectory Forecasting Problem Formulation:**
The task is defined as predicting an agent's future trajectory ($u_f$) over $t_f$ seconds, given its past observed trajectory ($u_p$) over $t_p$ seconds. For long-term settings, $t_p = 5$ sec and $t_f = 30$ sec are used, while for short-term, $t_p = 3.2$ sec and $t_f = 4.8$ sec are adopted. Trajectories are represented as 2D agent locations at discrete time steps, determined by a sampling rate $f_s$.

**Di-Long Model Architecture:**
Di-Long incorporates multi-modality modeling, addressing goal prediction, social influence, and spatial relations to scene semantics.

1. **Goal Estimation Module (G):** This module is a U-Net that predicts probability distributions for potential final destinations (goals) and intermediate waypoints.
 - **Input:** Observed trajectories ($u_p$) are spatially encoded into 2D Gaussian heatmaps ($U_p$). These are concatenated with 2D semantic maps ($M$) of the scene, which are extracted from the scene image ($I$) by a pre-trained U-Net ($Q$).
 - **Process:** The concatenated tensor, representing past trajectory and scene semantics, is fed into the U-Net ($G$).
 - **Output:** The module produces goal heatmaps ($z$), where specific channels correspond to predictions for different future time steps. The last channel ($z[K_f-1]$) represents the final destination, and an intermediate channel ($z[\bar{c}]$) represents a waypoint. To reduce future uncertainty, multiple goals and waypoints are sampled from these heatmaps and re-encoded into a tensor ($\hat{z}$) for use by the temporal module.

2. **Temporal Module (T):** This module is an autoregressive transformer decoder (similar to GPT).
 - **Input:** The module processes the past observed coordinates ($u_p$). It is conditioned by multi-modal inputs via cross-attention mechanisms. These inputs include the estimated sampled goal and waypoint maps ($\hat{z}$), the scene semantic maps ($M$), and social interaction information from other agents.
 - **Process:** The concatenated $\hat{z}$ and $M$ are patchified and linearly projected to generate tokens ($\hat{u}_G$). The transformer decoder uses agent-aware cross-attention between the past observed trajectory and these multi-modal tokens to learn intra- and inter-dependencies among agents.
 - **Output:** The module predicts the future trajectory ($v$).

**Short-to-Long Term Knowledge Distillation:**
The core of Di-Long lies in its knowledge distillation framework, which operates during training. Two instances of the model (teacher $T$ and student $S$) are used, differing in their observation and prediction spans.
- **Student (S):** Observes for $t_p$ seconds and predicts for $t_f$ seconds.
- **Teacher (T):** Observes for $t_p + t_a$ seconds and predicts for $t_f - t_a$ seconds, where $t_a$ is an "anchor time" ($0 \le t_a < t_f$). The teacher's task is less uncertain due to its longer observation and shorter prediction horizon. The teacher is only active during training.

1. **Goal Module Distillation:**
 - The student's goal module ($G_S$) computes predictions $z_S$ from its observation $x_S$.
 - The teacher's goal module ($G_T$) computes predictions $z_T$ from its longer observation $x_T$.
 - **Losses:** Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) losses $L^{goal}_S$ and $L^{goal}_T$ are applied to the student's and teacher's goal predictions, respectively, against the ground truth.
 - **Implicit Distillation:** A unique aspect is the introduction of an augmented teacher input ($x_{TS}$). This input replaces a segment of the teacher's observation (from $K_p$ to $K_p+K_a$) with the student's corresponding predictions ($z_S[0:K_a]$). This configuration enables the teacher to provide regularization guided by the student's current prediction state, and allows gradients to flow through the teacher's predictions to update the student. A distillation loss ($L^{distill}_{goal}$) is calculated as BCE between the teacher's predictions from this augmented input ($z_{TS}$) and the ground truth.
 - **Total Goal Loss:** $L_{goal} = L^{goal}_S + L^{goal}_T + L^{distill}_{goal}$.

2. **Temporal Module Distillation:**
 - The student's temporal module ($T_S$) predicts $v_S$, and the teacher's ($T_T$) predicts $v_T$.
 - **Losses:** L2 norm losses $L^{traj}_S$ and $L^{traj}_T$ are applied to the student's and teacher's trajectory predictions, respectively, against the ground truth.
 - **Explicit Distillation:** An explicit distillation loss ($L^{distill}_{traj}$) is computed as the L2 norm between a segment of the student's prediction ($v_S[K_a+k]$) and the teacher's prediction ($v_T[k]$). This directly aligns the student's forecast with the teacher's more accurate prediction for the common future segment.
 - **Total Temporal Loss:** $L_{traj} = L^{traj}_S + L^{traj}_T + L^{distill}_{traj}$.

**Overall Training:** The total loss for training is a weighted sum: $L = L_{goal} + \lambda L_{traj}$, where $\lambda$ is a hyper-parameter balancing the components.

**Experimental Setup:**
The model was implemented in PyTorch and trained on a GeForce RTX 2080 Ti GPU for 500 epochs. Hyperparameters included a batch size of 8 and an Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 1e-4. Scene images were resized to 256x256, and specific semantic categories were used for map generation. The segmentation network $Q$ was frozen and initialized with ImageNet-1k pre-trained weights. Goals were sampled using the Test-Time-Sampling-Trick (TTST).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study evaluated Di-Long on two public datasets, the Intersection Drone Dataset (inD) and the Stanford Drone Dataset (SDD), under both long-term and short-term forecasting settings. Evaluation metrics included Average Displacement Error (ADE), Final Displacement Error (FDE), Kernel Density Estimate Negative Log Likelihood (KDE-NLL), and Miss Rate (MR), typically reported as the best of 20 generated predictions.

**Long-Term Forecasting Results:**

- **Intersection Drone Dataset (inD):** Di-Long, with its knowledge distillation framework, achieved state-of-the-art performance across all evaluated metrics. It demonstrated improved accuracy compared to prior methods like Y-Net and Goal-SAR.
 - ADE: 14.89 (compared to Y-Net's 15.19)
 - FDE: 19.85 (compared to Y-Net's 23.17)
 - KDE-NLL: 9.52 (compared to Goal-SAR's 9.80)
 - Miss Rate: 0.24 (compared to Y-Net's 0.32)
 - The ablation study indicated that the distillation framework contributed to these improvements, as Di-Long without distillation showed higher ADE and comparable performance in other metrics to the second-best previous models.
- **Stanford Drone Dataset (SDD):** Di-Long also exhibited strong performance on SDD in the long-term setting.
 - FDE: 72.41 (compared to Y-Net's 77.45), indicating a substantial improvement in final position prediction.
 - KDE-NLL: 10.85 (compared to Y-Net's 11.23).
 - Ablation revealed a notable distillation gain of -3.9 FDE. While generally superior, Di-Long showed a marginal increase in ADE and Miss Rate compared to Y-Net (48.21 vs 46.00 ADE, 0.28 vs 0.26 MR).

**Short-Term Forecasting Results:**

- For both inD and SDD datasets in the short-term setting, Di-Long with distillation generally outperformed previous works across ADE, FDE, and KDE-NLL metrics. However, the performance gain attributed to knowledge distillation was observed to be less pronounced in the short-term context compared to the long-term, potentially due to the inherently lower future uncertainty in shorter prediction horizons.

**Ablation Studies:**

- **Di-Long Components:** An investigation into Di-Long's constituent parts confirmed the importance of multi-modal inputs, including semantic maps, goal and waypoint information, and social context. Critically, the knowledge distillation strategy, applied to both the goal and temporal modules, demonstrated a significant positive impact on ADE and FDE, allowing Di-Long to surpass previous benchmarks.
- **Increasing Long-Term Time Horizon:** Experiments extending prediction horizons from 10 to 60 seconds on the inD dataset showed Di-Long consistently outperforming Y-Net and Goal-SAR. Di-Long maintained a lower degradation in performance as the prediction length increased, without exhibiting sharp drops in accuracy beyond a specific time horizon.
- **Optimal Teacher Observation Length:** Analyzing the impact of the teacher's observation length ($t_p + t_a$) on the inD dataset revealed an optimal point. The best student performance (minimum ADE and FDE) was achieved when the teacher's observation extended to 20 seconds (i.e., $t_a = 15$ seconds, given $t_p=5$ seconds). This optimal anchor time coincided with the time step used for extracting the intermediate waypoint in the goal module.

**Qualitative Results:**
Qualitative examples illustrated the benefits of distillation. In one instance from inD, the Di-Long model with knowledge distillation predicted a final goal notably closer to the ground truth compared to the model without distillation, which tended to strictly follow road semantics but deviated from the true destination. Another example from SDD demonstrated the full Di-Long model accurately matching the ground truth trajectory.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Di-Long, a novel approach to long-term trajectory forecasting that addresses a fundamental challenge: the increasing uncertainty of predictions over extended time horizons. By leveraging a short-to-long term knowledge distillation framework, the work presents a method for a student network to benefit from the more deterministic predictions of a teacher network.

The significance of this work lies in several aspects:

1. **Addressing a Core Problem in Long-Term Forecasting:** The paper directly tackles the inherent difficulty of predicting far into the future due to accumulating uncertainty. The proposed knowledge distillation scheme provides a mechanism to reduce this uncertainty by guiding the student model with more reliable "short-term expert" knowledge from the teacher.
2. **Novel Application of Knowledge Distillation:** While knowledge distillation has been applied in various contexts, its specific application to transfer knowledge from a short-term-oriented teacher to a long-term-oriented student in trajectory forecasting is identified as a novel contribution, which has been demonstrated to be effective.
3. **Demonstrated State-of-the-Art Performance:** Di-Long achieves state-of-the-art results on widely recognized long-term trajectory prediction benchmarks, the inD and SDD datasets. This quantitative improvement across key metrics like ADE, FDE, KDE-NLL, and Miss Rate validates the effectiveness of the proposed architecture and distillation strategy.
4. **Robustness Across Longer Horizons:** The ablation study showing Di-Long's consistent performance and low degradation even when predicting up to 60 seconds into the future suggests improved robustness compared to previous methods. This capability is critical for practical applications requiring reliable predictions over extended periods.
5. **Multi-Modal Integration and Design:** The model's architecture, which integrates scene semantic maps, goal/waypoint estimations, and social interaction information, underscores the importance of multi-modal inputs for comprehensive trajectory understanding and prediction. The ablation studies confirm the individual contributions of these components, alongside the significant impact of the distillation framework.

The potential impact of this research is substantial, particularly for applications where accurate and reliable long-term trajectory forecasting is paramount. Improved prediction capabilities could enhance the safety and efficiency of:
- **Autonomous Driving:** Better anticipation of pedestrian and vehicle movements allows for more informed decision-making and safer navigation in complex urban environments.
- **Robotics:** Robots in human-centric environments (e.g., embodied navigation, human-robot interaction) can benefit from more accurate predictions of human intent and movement.
- **Video Surveillance:** Enhanced long-term tracking and behavior prediction could improve anomaly detection and crowd management systems.

By providing a method that not only achieves high accuracy but also maintains performance over longer prediction horizons and generalizes across different datasets, Di-Long contributes a valuable approach to advancing the field of trajectory forecasting.
