# DOGlove: Dexterous Manipulation with a Low-Cost Open-Source Haptic Force Feedback Glove

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and collaborating institutions introduce DOGlove, a breakthrough low-cost ($600) haptic feedback glove for robotic teleoperation that achieves professional-grade motion capture accuracy (±1° after calibration) while providing rich force feedback, dramatically reducing the barrier to entry for advanced manipulation research.

## Problem

- Existing haptic feedback gloves for teleoperation are prohibitively expensive ($5000+)
- Current solutions often lack multi-modal feedback needed for precise manipulation
- Limited accessibility hinders research progress in imitation learning and robotics

## Method

- **DOGlove**'s novel ==21-DoF motion capture== system with anthropomorphic joint design
- Integrated ==5-DoF cable-driven force feedback== with ==linear resonant actuators==
- Open-source hardware and software design for community adoption

## Results

- Achieved **±7.2°** raw accuracy (**±1°** after calibration) in motion capture
- Successfully completed complex manipulation tasks with and without visual feedback, demonstrating effective integration with imitation learning frameworks
- Total cost under **$600**, making it accessible to broader research community

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining precise motion tracking with multi-modal feedback enables complex manipulation
> - Cable-driven torque transmission provides compact, effective force feedback
> - Open-source approach can accelerate research progress through community contributions

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Research Landscape

- Addresses critical gap in dexterous manipulation teleoperation
- Builds on prior work in haptic feedback gloves but focuses on accessibility and cost-effectiveness
- Contributes to growing field of imitation learning for robotic manipulation

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. Primary Goals

1. Develop low-cost, open-source haptic feedback glove for dexterous manipulation
2. Enable precise teleoperation through combined motion capture and force feedback
3. Create system compatible with imitation learning frameworks

#### 2.2. Motivating Factors

- Current solutions are expensive ($5000+) and often lack multi-modal feedback
- Need for better demonstration collection methods for imitation learning
- Desire to make advanced teleoperation accessible to broader research community

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Technical Design

1. 21-DoF motion capture system using custom joint structure
2. 5-DoF cable-driven force feedback mechanism
3. Linear resonant actuators for fingertip haptic feedback
4. Total cost under $600

#### 3.2. Key Features

- Anthropomorphic design matching human hand kinematics
- Compact cable-driven torque transmission
- Integration of both force and haptic feedback
- Open-source hardware and software

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Performance Metrics

1. Precise motion capture with ±7.2° accuracy (±1° after calibration)
2. Successful completion of complex manipulation tasks
3. High success rates in both visual and non-visual feedback scenarios
4. Effective integration with imitation learning frameworks

#### 4.2. Key Results

- Outperformed baseline methods in complex manipulation tasks
- Enabled accurate object perception without visual feedback
- Successfully trained imitation learning policies for various tasks

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Research Contributions

1. First low-cost, open-source haptic force feedback glove
2. Novel combination of precise motion capture and multi-modal feedback
3. Demonstrated effectiveness for imitation learning data collection

#### 5.2. Broader Impact

- Makes advanced teleoperation accessible to more researchers
- Provides platform for developing better manipulation algorithms
- Open-source nature enables community improvements and modifications

#### 5.3. Concluding Remarks

The paper represents a significant advance in making high-quality teleoperation systems more accessible while maintaining performance comparable to much more expensive solutions. The open-source nature and low cost could accelerate research in robotic manipulation and imitation learning.
