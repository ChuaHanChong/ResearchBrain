# Scaling active inference

> [!summary] Summary
> A scalable implementation of active inference is presented, enabling its application to high-dimensional continuous control tasks with superior sample efficiency. The approach integrates amortized inference with neural networks, Cross-Entropy Method for policy optimization, and particle-based trajectory sampling to handle complex environments, demonstrating improved exploration and faster learning compared to model-free baselines.

## Problem

- Active inference, despite its theoretical power, was largely confined to simplified, low-dimensional problems due to computational constraints.
- Previous implementations struggled with representing complex belief distributions and accurately quantifying uncertainty in high-dimensional continuous spaces.
- Evaluating policies through expected free energy calculations became computationally intractable in continuous action and state domains.

## Method

- Neural networks are used for ==amortized inference==, parameterizing both the generative model (transition and observation) and the ==recognition model== to handle high-dimensional spaces.
- Policy optimization for continuous action spaces is performed using the ==Cross-Entropy Method (CEM)==, which samples and refines action sequences based on ==expected free energy== evaluation.
- Uncertainty is propagated through time using a ==particle-based trajectory sampling== approach for more accurate estimation of expected outcomes during policy evaluation.

## Results

- The active inference agent demonstrated enhanced exploration in the MountainCar environment, exploring the state space more thoroughly than reward-driven or ε-greedy agents.
- It achieved superior sample efficiency, reaching optimal performance on the Pendulum task in fewer than **100 epochs**, compared to over **1000 epochs** for DDPG.
- The agent showed robust performance across various continuous control benchmarks, including Pendulum and Hopper, converging to high-reward policies with an **order of magnitude fewer samples** than strong model-free baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Active inference, when scaled with neural networks and advanced planning methods, can effectively handle high-dimensional continuous control tasks.
> - The Expected Free Energy (EFE) framework naturally unifies exploration and exploitation under a single objective of free energy minimization.
> - Active inference provides a theoretically grounded approach to uncertainty-driven exploration by considering both pragmatic value (reward) and epistemic value (information gain).
