# Exploration from a Primal-Dual Lens: Value-Incentivized Actor-Critic Methods for Sample-Efficient Online RL

> [!summary] Summary
> The Value-Incentivized Actor-Critic (VAC) method, a model-free online reinforcement learning algorithm, achieves near-optimal sample efficiency by reframing optimistic exploration through a primal-dual lens. This approach leads to a single, optimizable objective function that avoids the complex bi-level optimization of prior frameworks like Maximize to Explore (MEX).

## Problem

- Online reinforcement learning with complex function approximators struggles with sample efficiency due to the inherent exploration-exploitation trade-off.
- Theoretically sound exploration strategies, often based on optimism in the face of uncertainty, frequently suffer from prohibitive computational complexity or sub-par empirical performance in practical, large-scale settings.
- The Maximize to Explore (MEX) framework, while offering theoretical advantages, presents a bi-level optimization problem that hinders direct application of first-order methods and complicates practical implementation.

## Method

- Reinterpreted optimistic regularization by deriving the MEX objective from a ==regularized Lagrangian== of a constrained value maximization problem, viewed through a primal-dual lens.
- Formulated a new optimization problem that jointly optimizes the Q-function (critic) and policy (actor), constrained by the Bellman consistency equation rather than optimality.
- Developed the Value-Incentivized Actor-Critic (VAC) method, which uses a single objective function amenable to first-order optimization, unlike prior bi-level approaches.

## Results

- The VAC method achieved a near-optimal cumulative regret bound of O(dH2Tlog⁡(log⁡(∣A∣)Tδ)log⁡(1+T3/2d))O(dH^2√(T)√(\log(\frac{\log(|A|)T){δ})\log(1 + \frac{T^{3/2}}{d})})O(dH2Tlog(δlog(∣A∣)T)log(1+dT3/2)) for episodic linear Markov Decision Processes (MDPs).
- For infinite-horizon discounted MDPs, VAC demonstrated a near-optimal sample complexity of O(d2(1−γ)5ϵ2)O((1−γ)5ϵ2d2) to reach an ϵ-optimal policy, up to logarithmic and polynomial factors.
- The theoretical framework extends to general function approximation, yielding regret bounds that match or slightly improve upon existing methods under similar assumptions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The principle of optimism in exploration can be formally understood and derived from a primal-dual perspective, linking it to regularized Lagrangian formulations.
> - Shifting the optimization constraint from the Bellman optimality equation to the Bellman consistency equation significantly simplifies the resulting objective to a single-level problem.
> - A unified Lagrangian objective can effectively integrate exploration and exploitation by promoting Q-function and policy estimates that are both consistent with observed data and yield higher value functions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a fundamental challenge within the field of online reinforcement learning (RL), particularly when employing complex function approximators such as deep neural networks and transformers. This area is significant in modern artificial intelligence, finding applications in domains including game playing, control systems, and the post-training and reasoning capabilities of large language models.

A core difficulty in online RL is managing the exploration-exploitation trade-off. This trade-off is particularly critical in real-world scenarios where data collection is costly, time-consuming, or risky (e.g., clinical trials, recommendation systems, autonomous driving). Existing naive exploration strategies, such as $\epsilon$-greedy methods, are recognized as sample-inefficient. While significant efforts have been made to develop statistically efficient approaches, often guided by the principle of "optimism in the face of uncertainty" (e.g., optimistic estimates with data-driven confidence sets, Bayesian methods like Thompson sampling), these approaches frequently encounter difficulties in practical implementation. Many theoretically robust methods exhibit prohibitive computational complexity or subpar empirical performance when scaled to complex problems with general function approximators.

A recent development in this area is the Maximize to Explore (MEX) framework introduced by Liu et al. (2024). MEX proposes optimizing a single objective function over the state-action value function (Q-function), aiming to unify estimation, planning, and exploration, and offers sub-linear regret guarantees under function approximation. However, the practical optimization of the MEX objective is complicated by its inherent bi-level structure, which involves the optimal value function as a regularizer. This structural complexity hinders direct application of first-order optimization techniques and necessitates non-trivial modifications in implementation, making it challenging to isolate the benefits of the MEX framework itself.

This paper positions itself by seeking to overcome the practical limitations of MEX while retaining its theoretical advantages, specifically by developing a sample-efficient, model-free online RL algorithm that utilizes a unifying objective without complex bi-level optimization. Furthermore, it introduces a novel application of primal-dual optimization in an online RL setting, an area largely unexplored formally in prior literature, which has seen use in offline RL for addressing "double-sampling" issues and for various policy optimization and evaluation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a sample-efficient, model-free online reinforcement learning algorithm that can effectively balance exploration and exploitation using a single, easily optimizable objective function, thereby avoiding the computational complexities associated with bi-level optimization problems. This objective directly addresses a recognized practical hurdle in current theoretically-grounded exploration strategies, specifically the bi-level structure of the recently proposed Maximize to Explore (MEX) framework.

The motivation for this work arises from several factors:

1. **Demand for Sample Efficiency in Online RL:** Modern AI applications increasingly rely on online RL with complex function approximations. However, acquiring interaction data in many real-world high-stakes environments (e.g., clinical trials, autonomous driving) is expensive, time-consuming, or carries inherent risks. This necessitates sample-efficient algorithms that can learn effective policies with minimal environmental interaction.
2. **Limitations of Existing Exploration Strategies:** While the principle of optimism in the face of uncertainty has provided a theoretical foundation for many statistically efficient RL algorithms, translating these into practical methods for general function approximators (like deep neural networks) remains challenging. Many such methods suffer from high computational complexity or do not perform as expected empirically.
3. **Tractability Issues with MEX:** The MEX framework, despite its theoretical appeal in unifying estimation, planning, and exploration, presents significant practical optimization difficulties. Its objective includes a regularizer derived from the optimal value function, leading to a bi-level optimization problem that is not directly amenable to standard first-order optimization tools. This practical barrier prevents a clear assessment of MEX's conceptual advantages.
4. **Novel Primal-Dual Perspective:** The authors are motivated to offer a new conceptual understanding of optimistic regularization, which is central to MEX, through the lens of primal-dual optimization. By reformulating the problem as a regularized Lagrangian, they aim to derive an objective function that maintains the core principles of optimistic exploration while being significantly more tractable for optimization. This approach seeks to provide deeper insight into the mechanism of exploration by regularization and facilitate the design of more practical algorithms.

In essence, the research aims to fill a gap between theoretical guarantees of sample-efficient exploration and the practical requirements of implementing such strategies with complex function approximation in online RL settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper involves a theoretical reformulation of the online RL problem through a primal-dual lens, leading to the development of a new algorithm.

#### 3.1. Reinterpreting Exploration from a Primal-Dual Perspective

The work begins by re-examining the Maximize to Explore (MEX) framework. The authors interpret the optimistic regularization mechanism within MEX as originating from a regularized Lagrangian formulation of a constrained value maximization problem. Specifically, they demonstrate that the MEX objective can be derived from maximizing the expected initial value of a Q-function, subject to the constraint that this Q-function satisfies the Bellman optimality equation. This is achieved by introducing dual variables and applying a reparameterization trick, which is noted for its ability to handle "double-sampling" issues. This conceptual step provides a foundational justification for the structure of MEX's objective.

#### 3.2. Developing the Value-Incentivized Actor-Critic (VAC) Method

Motivated by this primal-dual interpretation, the authors propose a new optimization problem. Instead of using the Bellman *optimality* equation as a constraint (as implied in the MEX derivation), they formulate a value maximization problem that jointly optimizes both the Q-function (critic) and the policy (actor), subject to the Bellman *consistency* equation. This shift in constraint is critical for achieving a more tractable optimization problem.

A regularized Lagrangian is then constructed for this new constrained problem. Applying the same reparameterization of dual variables, the Lagrangian is transformed into a single objective function. This objective function is designed to incentivize state-action and policy estimates that are both consistent with collected data transitions and result in higher value functions, effectively integrating exploration and exploitation.

The derived objective forms the basis of the **Value-Incentivized Actor-Critic (VAC) method** (Algorithm 1). The algorithm operates iteratively:
1. **Update Step:** In each iteration $t$, the algorithm updates the Q-function ($f_t$) and policy ($\pi_t$) by solving the sample-based version of the derived Lagrangian objective. This optimization integrates the maximization of the policy value with a loss term ($L_t(f, \pi)$) that measures the Bellman consistency of the Q-function with historical data, while encouraging higher value functions.
2. **Data Collection Step:** The newly updated policy $\pi_t$ is executed in the environment for one episode, and the resulting transition tuples are added to a cumulative dataset $D_t$.

The paper highlights that this reformulated objective, unlike MEX, is amenable to practical optimization using first-order methods. It outlines that the critic evaluation step can be viewed as a biased policy evaluation for a fixed policy, while policy updates can be performed via policy gradient techniques.

#### 3.3. Theoretical Analysis Framework

To substantiate the efficacy of VAC, rigorous theoretical analysis is conducted under specific assumptions:
- **Linear Markov Decision Processes (MDPs):** The primary setting for theoretical guarantees, characterized by rewards and transition probabilities that are linear functions of known feature maps (Assumption 1). This is a common model for analyzing RL algorithms.
- **Function Class Assumptions:**
 - **Linear Q-function class:** Q-functions are assumed to be linear in the feature space (Assumption 2).
 - **Log-linear policy class:** Policies are represented as log-linear functions, consistent with max-entropy policies (Assumption 3).
- **Regret Minimization:** The performance metric is cumulative regret over $T$ episodes, comparing the learned policies against an optimal policy.

The paper also indicates that these theoretical findings can be extended beyond linear MDPs to general function approximation settings. This extension relies on broader assumptions such as Bellman completeness and realizability of the Q-function class, boundedness, a log-linear policy class, a low Generalized Eluder Coefficient (GEC) for effective generalization of in-sample prediction errors, and finite $\epsilon$-nets for complexity bounds. Furthermore, the framework is extended to infinite-horizon discounted MDPs, utilizing a specific sampling procedure (Algorithm 3) to adapt the data collection to this setting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents several key findings and results:

#### 4.1. Primal-Dual Interpretation of Optimistic Exploration

The paper offers a novel interpretation of the principle of optimism in the face of uncertainty, a core tenet of exploration strategies like MEX. It demonstrates that the MEX objective, which combines value maximization with optimistic regularization, can be derived as a regularized Lagrangian from a primal-dual perspective. Specifically, it shows MEX's objective arises from a value maximization problem constrained by the Bellman optimality equation, subject to a specific reparameterization of dual variables. This reinterpretation provides a deeper theoretical understanding of exploration-by-regularization approaches.

#### 4.2. Introduction of Value-Incentivized Actor-Critic (VAC) Method

Based on the primal-dual insights, the authors develop the Value-Incentivized Actor-Critic (VAC) method (Algorithm 1). A central finding is that by formulating the problem based on the Bellman *consistency* equation rather than the Bellman *optimality* equation, and leveraging the Lagrangian reparameterization, VAC achieves a single, non-bi-level objective function. This objective jointly optimizes the Q-function and the policy. The resulting formulation is designed to be more amenable to standard first-order optimization techniques compared to the bi-level optimization required by MEX. VAC's objective integrates exploration and exploitation by promoting Q-function and policy estimates that are both consistent with observed data transitions and yield higher value functions.

#### 4.3. Theoretical Guarantees for Episodic Linear MDPs

For episodic linear Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), under Assumptions 1-3 (linear MDP, linear Q-function class, log-linear policy class), the VAC method achieves a near-optimal regret guarantee. Specifically, Theorem 1 states that with high probability, the cumulative regret over $T$ episodes is bounded by $O(dH^2\sqrt{T} \sqrt{\log(\frac{\log(|A|)T}{\delta})\log(1 + \frac{T^{3/2}}{d})})$. This result indicates a sub-linear regret scaling with $T$, making the algorithm sample-efficient. The bound is noted to be near-optimal, differing from the minimax lower bound by a factor of $\sqrt{H}$ and logarithmic factors.

#### 4.4. Extension to Infinite-Horizon Discounted MDPs

The VAC algorithm and its theoretical analysis are extended to the infinite-horizon discounted setting (Theorem 3). Using a specialized sampling procedure (Algorithm 3), the paper demonstrates that VAC can achieve an $\epsilon$-optimal policy with a sample complexity of $O(\frac{d^2}{(1-\gamma)^5\epsilon^2})$, up to logarithmic factors and polynomial factors of $\frac{1}{1-\gamma}$. This result also represents a near-optimal rate for this setting.

#### 4.5. Generalization to General Function Approximation

The theoretical framework is further extended to the setting of general function approximation (Theorem 2). Under broader assumptions of realizability, Bellman completeness, boundedness of the Q-function class, a log-linear policy class, a low Generalized Eluder Coefficient (GEC), and finite $\epsilon$-nets, VAC maintains strong regret guarantees. The derived regret bound in this general setting is reported to match or slightly exceed the tightness of existing bounds in prior art, such as that provided by Liu et al. (2024). This indicates the robustness and broad applicability of the VAC framework beyond specific linear models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of online reinforcement learning by addressing a critical gap between theoretically robust exploration principles and their practical implementation with complex function approximators.

#### 5.1. Enhanced Practicality and Implementability

The primary impact of this work lies in providing a model-free online RL algorithm, VAC, that offers provable sample-efficiency without resorting to the computationally intensive and complex bi-level optimization inherent in prior frameworks like MEX. By transforming the optimization problem into a single, easy-to-optimize objective function through a novel primal-dual interpretation, the paper proposes a method that is more amenable to existing first-order optimization toolkits. This enhanced practicality could accelerate the adoption of theoretically-grounded exploration strategies in real-world applications where complex neural networks are employed.

#### 5.2. Principled Integration of Exploration and Exploitation

The development of a unified Lagrangian-based objective offers a principled and coherent approach to integrating exploration and exploitation. This contrasts with heuristic-driven exploration methods, providing a robust theoretical foundation for how an agent gathers information while simultaneously maximizing rewards. The interpretation from a primal-dual lens deepens the understanding of optimistic regularization as a mechanism for incentivizing exploration.

#### 5.3. Broad Theoretical Applicability

The theoretical guarantees established for VAC, particularly its near-optimal regret bounds under linear Markov Decision Processes, provide strong confidence in its statistical efficiency. The further extension of these guarantees to general function approximation, under the Generalized Eluder Coefficient framework, suggests that VAC is applicable to a wide range of RL problems beyond simplified linear models. This broad theoretical applicability paves the way for future empirical validation across diverse and complex environments. Similarly, the extension to infinite-horizon discounted MDPs widens the scope of problems VAC can address.

#### 5.4. Foundation for Future Research

The work opens several avenues for future research and development. The authors explicitly mention follow-up efforts focused on empirical validation, which will be crucial for demonstrating VAC's performance in practice against existing state-of-the-art methods. Additionally, extending the algorithm design to multi-agent settings, where exploration becomes even more intricate, represents a promising direction for advancing the field. This research provides a foundational theoretical and algorithmic framework that can serve as a building block for addressing these complex challenges.
