# VideoSSR: Video Self-Supervised Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A new VideoSSR framework leverages intrinsic video information to self-generate verifiable training data, addressing the data bottleneck for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards in multimodal large language models. This approach consistently improves MLLM performance by over 5.1% on average across 17 diverse video understanding benchmarks, enhancing generalization and reducing reliance on costly and biased annotations.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) datasets for MLLMs are often too simple, prone to annotation biases from less capable models, or prohibitively expensive to scale.
- Current datasets lead to a 'zero variance issue' for advanced MLLMs, providing ineffective learning signals and limiting generalization across diverse video understanding tasks.
- The reliance on human or multi-agent annotations for verifiable answers creates unsustainable costs and introduces systemic biases that hinder robust MLLM training.

## Method

- Introduced a suite of self-supervised ==pretext tasks== (Anomaly Grounding, Object Counting, Temporal Jigsaw) to generate verifiable question-answer pairs directly from raw video data.
- Developed the **VideoSSR** framework for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), utilizing a 30,000-pair self-generated dataset (VideoSSR-30K) from these pretext tasks.
- Designed tailored smooth reward functions for each pretext task, providing dense and stable learning signals for efficient RLVR training using algorithms like GRPO.

## Results

- VideoSSR improved the Qwen3-VL-**8B**-Instruct model's performance by over **5.1%** on average across 17 benchmarks spanning General Video QA, Long Video QA, Temporal Grounding, and Complex Reasoning tasks.
- The VIUBench benchmark revealed that even leading closed-source models achieved only **58.7%** average scores on intrinsic video understanding, confirming the challenging nature of the self-supervised tasks.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that smooth reward functions are crucial for stable RLVR training and that task diversity within the self-supervised approach is more effective than merely scaling up single pretext tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art MLLMs, including closed-source models like GPT-5, demonstrate profound limitations in understanding intrinsic video properties (fine-grained, spatial, temporal perception) as shown by the VIUBench benchmark.
> - Leveraging diverse, self-supervised pretext tasks with parametrically controllable difficulty and smooth reward functions provides a scalable, cost-effective, and bias-free method for MLLM training.
> - Training on intrinsic video understanding tasks significantly enhances MLLM generalization, leading to consistent performance improvements across a wide array of downstream video understanding benchmarks.
