# VideoSSR: Video Self-Supervised Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A new VideoSSR framework leverages intrinsic video information to self-generate verifiable training data, addressing the data bottleneck for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards in multimodal large language models. This approach consistently improves MLLM performance by over 5.1% on average across 17 diverse video understanding benchmarks, enhancing generalization and reducing reliance on costly and biased annotations.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) datasets for MLLMs are often too simple, prone to annotation biases from less capable models, or prohibitively expensive to scale.
- Current datasets lead to a 'zero variance issue' for advanced MLLMs, providing ineffective learning signals and limiting generalization across diverse video understanding tasks.
- The reliance on human or multi-agent annotations for verifiable answers creates unsustainable costs and introduces systemic biases that hinder robust MLLM training.

## Method

- Introduced a suite of self-supervised ==pretext tasks== (Anomaly Grounding, Object Counting, Temporal Jigsaw) to generate verifiable question-answer pairs directly from raw video data.
- Developed the **VideoSSR** framework for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), utilizing a 30,000-pair self-generated dataset (VideoSSR-30K) from these pretext tasks.
- Designed tailored smooth reward functions for each pretext task, providing dense and stable learning signals for efficient RLVR training using algorithms like GRPO.

## Results

- VideoSSR improved the Qwen3-VL-**8B**-Instruct model's performance by over **5.1%** on average across 17 benchmarks spanning General Video QA, Long Video QA, Temporal Grounding, and Complex Reasoning tasks.
- The VIUBench benchmark revealed that even leading closed-source models achieved only **58.7%** average scores on intrinsic video understanding, confirming the challenging nature of the self-supervised tasks.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that smooth reward functions are crucial for stable RLVR training and that task diversity within the self-supervised approach is more effective than merely scaling up single pretext tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art MLLMs, including closed-source models like GPT-5, demonstrate profound limitations in understanding intrinsic video properties (fine-grained, spatial, temporal perception) as shown by the VIUBench benchmark.
> - Leveraging diverse, self-supervised pretext tasks with parametrically controllable difficulty and smooth reward functions provides a scalable, cost-effective, and bias-free method for MLLM training.
> - Training on intrinsic video understanding tasks significantly enhances MLLM generalization, leading to consistent performance improvements across a wide array of downstream video understanding benchmarks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have significantly propelled the field of video understanding forward. A critical recent development has been the integration of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), which further enhances MLLMs' capabilities by providing strong, verifiable feedback signals during training. This approach, exemplified by methods like GRPO, relies on datasets where the correctness of generated answers can be objectively validated, often through detailed annotations.

However, the paper identifies a growing bottleneck: the complexity of existing video datasets is rapidly being outpaced by the capabilities of advanced MLLMs. Current RLVR datasets, such as LongVideoReason and ReWatch, predominantly rely on multi-agent collaboration or human annotation to construct verifiable answers. This methodology introduces several significant limitations that "VideoSSR" aims to address:

1. **Insufficient Complexity:** For highly capable models like Qwen3-VL, many questions in existing datasets are no longer sufficiently challenging. The paper demonstrates a "zero variance issue" (Figure 1), where models either get all responses perfectly correct or all incorrect, leading to an ineffective learning signal for RLVR.
2. **Annotation Biases and Costs:** Multi-agent annotation processes, especially when annotator models are less capable than the target models, can introduce systemic biases and artifacts, leading to flawed reward signals. Manual annotation, on the other hand, is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming, making it unsustainable for generating the vast, high-quality data required to train increasingly powerful MLLMs.
3. **Lack of Generalization:** Many existing RLVR approaches specialize in enhancing a single capability, limiting their broader generalization across diverse video understanding tasks.

This work draws inspiration from the long-standing field of self-supervised learning (SSL) for video, which aims to learn effective spatio-temporal representations from unlabeled data by designing pretext tasks that exploit intrinsic video properties (e.g., video jigsaw puzzles, counting). While SSL has been used to train MLLMs with tasks like video jigsaw, its full potential for cultivating comprehensive video understanding has not been thoroughly explored.

"VideoSSR" positions itself as a crucial bridge between these two areas. It proposes a novel paradigm that leverages the intrinsic information within videos to *self-generate* high-quality, verifiable training data for RLVR. By doing so, it addresses the critical shortcomings of existing data sourcing methods, mitigating cost, bias, and the challenge of scalable complexity, thus enabling more robust and generalizable MLLM training for video understanding. It moves beyond single pretext tasks, investigating a richer suite to cultivate more comprehensive generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind "VideoSSR" stems from the urgent need for scalable, high-quality, and unbiased training data for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) in the context of rapidly advancing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). The authors observe that current datasets, whether human-annotated or MLLM-generated, are becoming a bottleneck. They are either too simple for state-of-the-art models, prone to introducing biases from less capable annotator models, or too expensive to scale.

Against this backdrop, the pivotal question guiding this research is: **"Can the rich, intrinsic information within videos be harnessed to self-generate high-quality, verifiable training data?"**

To answer this, the key objectives of the paper are:

1. **Design Self-Supervised Pretext Tasks:** To introduce a suite of novel self-supervised pretext tasks—Anomaly Grounding, Object Counting, and Temporal Jigsaw—that can parametrically scale in difficulty and generate verifiable question-answer pairs directly from raw video data, independent of human or external MLLM annotations.
2. **Construct a Diagnostic Benchmark (VIUBench):** To build the Video Intrinsic Understanding Benchmark (VIUBench) using these pretext tasks, validating their difficulty and revealing profound limitations of current state-of-the-art MLLMs (including closed-source models like GPT-5) in understanding intrinsic video properties.
3. **Develop a Self-Supervised RL Framework (VideoSSR):** To propose "VideoSSR," a novel video self-supervised reinforcement learning framework, and to construct the accompanying VideoSSR-30K dataset from the designed pretext tasks.
4. **Engineer Smooth Reward Functions:** To design tailored smooth reward functions for each pretext task to overcome the challenge of sparse reward signals inherent in difficult tasks, thereby ensuring efficient and stable RLVR training using algorithms like GRPO.
5. **Demonstrate Generalization and Impact:** To conduct extensive experiments across a wide array of 17 benchmarks spanning four major video domains (General Video QA, Long Video QA, Temporal Grounding, and Complex Reasoning) to demonstrate that VideoSSR consistently enhances MLLM performance and establishes itself as a potent foundational framework.

Essentially, the paper seeks to provide a cost-effective, scalable, and bias-free method for continuously challenging and improving MLLMs for video understanding by leveraging the inherent structure and information within video content itself.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of VideoSSR is centered on three core components: the design of self-supervised pretext tasks, the creation of a benchmark to validate their difficulty, and a reinforcement learning framework that leverages these tasks with specialized reward functions.

#### 3.1. Self-Supervised Pretext Tasks

The authors introduce three distinct pretext tasks, each designed to probe a specific aspect of intrinsic video understanding and to generate verifiable question-answer pairs without external annotations:

- **Anomaly Grounding:** This task assesses the model's ability to localize temporal segments where natural video dynamics are perturbed. A random temporal interval in a video is selected, and a perturbation function `P` (e.g., swapping color channels, rotating frames, shuffling intra-segment frames) is applied to this segment. The model is then tasked with identifying the start and end timestamps of this anomalous interval. The perturbations are categorized into Fine-grained Perception (e.g., channel swap), Spatial Perception (e.g., rotation), and Temporal Perception (e.g., shuffling frames).
- **Object Counting:** This task targets fine-grained perception and counting abilities. Procedurally generated geometric shapes (circles, rectangles, triangles) with randomized attributes (size, color, rotation, position) are overlaid onto a subset of video frames. The model must then count the total occurrences of each shape category across the modified frames. The difficulty can be controlled by adjusting the number of frames with objects and the number of instances per shape per frame.
- **Temporal Jigsaw:** Designed to evaluate temporal perception and understanding of coherence. A video is partitioned into `n` contiguous segments, which are then randomly shuffled. The model's objective is to restore the original temporal order by predicting the inverse permutation of the shuffled segments. Difficulty is controlled by the number of segments (`n`).

#### 3.2. Video Intrinsic Understanding Benchmark (VIUBench)

To validate the challenge posed by these pretext tasks, the authors construct VIUBench, comprising 2700 question-answer pairs generated from the three tasks. This benchmark systematically evaluates Fine-grained, Spatial, and Temporal Perception. Performance metrics include Mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) for Anomaly Grounding, average binary score for Object Counting (1 if correct count, 0 otherwise), and exact sequence match for Temporal Jigsaw. VIUBench is used to demonstrate that state-of-the-art MLLMs, even powerful closed-source models, struggle significantly with these intrinsic video understanding tasks and that the task difficulty is parametrically controllable.

#### 3.3. Video Self-Supervised Reinforcement Learning (VideoSSR) Framework

Building upon the insights from VIUBench, VideoSSR is introduced as a novel framework to enhance MLLM generalization.

- **VideoSSR-30K Dataset:** A dataset of 30,000 question-answer pairs is constructed using the aforementioned pretext tasks. Crucially, this dataset is entirely self-generated, independent of human or MLLM annotations, addressing the cost and bias issues.
- **RLVR Training:** The framework utilizes Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) based on the GRPO (Group Sequence Policy Optimization) algorithm. The base model for VideoSSR is Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, a strong open-source MLLM.
- **Smooth Reward Functions:** A key innovation for stable RLVR training is the design of smooth reward functions for each inherently difficult task, which provide denser and more informative learning signals than strict binary rewards:
 - **Anomaly Grounding:** The Mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) between the predicted and ground-truth temporal intervals serves directly as the smooth reward `R_ground`, ranging from 0 to 1.
 - **Object Counting:** The reward `R_count` is the average relative error across all shape categories. For each category, `R_count,k = max(0, 1 - |predicted_k - ground_truth_k| / (ground_truth_k + ε))`, providing a continuous score.
 - **Temporal Jigsaw:** The reward `R_jigsaw` is based on the normalized cumulative displacement error of elements from their correct positions in the predicted sequence. `R_jigsaw = 1 - (E_jigsaw / E_max)`.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

Training is performed for one epoch on VideoSSR-30K with a learning rate of 1e-6, a global batch size of 64, and 8 rollouts. MAX FRAMES is set to 48 and MAX PIXELS to 256x256 during training for efficiency. For evaluation, FPS is 2, MAX FRAMES is varied (32, 48, 64), and MAX PIXELS is 512x512. Greedy decoding is used, and Chain of Thought prompting is explicitly *not* used to focus on fundamental perceptual abilities and mitigate hallucination.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the effectiveness and generalization capabilities of VideoSSR across a diverse range of video understanding tasks.

- **VIUBench Validates Task Difficulty:** The VIUBench benchmark revealed that even leading closed-source models like GPT-5 achieved only a modest average score of 58.7, while open-source models like Qwen3-VL-8B scored a mere 19.5. This underscores that understanding intrinsic video properties (fine-grained details, spatial relationships, temporal coherence) remains a significant challenge for MLLMs. Crucially, the parametric control over task difficulty was confirmed; for example, increasing segment numbers in Temporal Jigsaw significantly reduced performance for all models.

- **Consistent Performance Improvements Across Benchmarks:** VideoSSR consistently enhanced model performance across 17 benchmarks spanning four major video domains: General Video QA, Long Video QA, Temporal Grounding, and Complex Reasoning. Under the 48-frame setting, VideoSSR achieved an average improvement of over 5.1% across all benchmarks compared to the baseline Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct model.

 - **General Video QA and Long Video QA:** VideoSSR showed consistent gains (e.g., +1.1% on MVBench, +10.6% on VinoGround at 64 frames), sometimes outperforming closed-source models (Table 2).
 - **Temporal Grounding:** Benefiting from the Anomaly Grounding task, VideoSSR achieved remarkable zero-shot improvements, notably +15.9% on QVHighlights and +5.6% on ActivityNet (Table 3), demonstrating enhanced temporal localization.
 - **Complex Reasoning:** The Temporal Jigsaw task significantly boosted performance on VCRBench, yielding a large improvement of +9.0% (Table 3). Consistent improvements were also observed on other reasoning benchmarks.

- **Ablation Studies Confirm Design Choices:**
 - **Effectiveness of Individual Pretext Tasks:** Each of the three pretext tasks (Anomaly Grounding, Object Counting, Temporal Jigsaw) individually contributed to improving general video understanding (e.g., on Video-MME) and specific related capabilities (e.g., Anomaly Grounding improved CharadesSTA, Temporal Jigsaw improved VCRBench) (Table 4).
 - **Importance of Smooth Reward Functions:** Models trained with strict (binary) reward functions performed closer to the baseline or even showed degradation (e.g., Anomaly Grounding on CharadesSTA). This validated that the smooth reward functions were crucial for providing dense, stable, and effective learning signals for RLVR.
 - **Benefit of Task Diversity:** Comparing single-task training with the mixed-task VideoSSR-30K dataset showed that task diversity is more effective than simply scaling up data for a single pretext task. Scaling single tasks led to diminishing returns or even performance degradation (Figure 5).
 - **Superiority Over MLLM-Annotated Datasets:** VideoSSR-30K training outperformed models fine-tuned on comparable-scale datasets annotated by MLLMs (LongVideoReason, ReWatch). The study also highlighted that fine-tuning powerful MLLMs on datasets annotated by less capable MLLMs could even lead to performance degradation, reinforcing the value of the self-supervised paradigm (Table 5).
 - **Anomaly Grounding Subtypes:** An ablation on 14 perturbation types for Anomaly Grounding (Figure 6) guided the selection of effective perturbations. Interestingly, perturbations targeting temporal speed (Slow/Fast) did not yield benefits and sometimes introduced negative side effects, possibly because the base model's reliance on textual timestamps conflicted with purely visual motion cues.

In essence, the findings firmly establish VideoSSR as a robust and effective framework for enhancing MLLMs' video understanding capabilities, largely due to its innovative self-supervised data generation and tailored reinforcement learning approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "VideoSSR" paper presents a highly significant contribution to the field of multimodal AI, particularly in advancing video understanding capabilities of MLLMs. Its potential impact spans several critical areas:

1. **Addressing the Data Bottleneck for RLVR:** The most profound impact of VideoSSR lies in its ability to generate high-quality, verifiable training data for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) in a self-supervised manner. This circumvents the prohibitive costs, time consumption, and inherent biases associated with manual or MLLM-based annotations. By providing a scalable and cost-effective method for data generation, VideoSSR removes a major bottleneck hindering the continuous improvement and scaling of MLLMs for video understanding. This paradigm shift can accelerate research and development in video-centric MLLMs.

2. **Enhancing Intrinsic Video Understanding:** The framework explicitly targets and improves MLLMs' understanding of intrinsic video properties, including fine-grained perceptual details, spatial relationships, and temporal coherence. The poor performance of even state-of-the-art MLLMs on VIUBench highlights a fundamental gap that VideoSSR effectively fills. By training models to grasp these intrinsic properties, VideoSSR lays a stronger foundation for more complex reasoning and generalization across diverse downstream tasks.

3. **Foundational Framework for Advanced MLLMs:** The consistent performance gains across 17 diverse benchmarks, spanning General Video QA, Long Video QA, Temporal Grounding, and Complex Reasoning, establish VideoSSR as a potent foundational framework. It offers a generalizable approach that can be integrated into future MLLM architectures and training pipelines to enhance their core video comprehension abilities, making them more robust and versatile.

4. **Parametrically Scalable Difficulty:** The ability to parametrically control the difficulty of the self-generated pretext tasks is a forward-looking feature. As MLLMs become even more capable, VIUBench and VideoSSR-30K can be continuously adapted to provide ever more challenging learning signals, ensuring the long-term relevance of the framework for benchmarking and training advanced models. This adaptive nature counters the problem of quickly "solving" static datasets.

5. **Robustness Against Annotation Biases:** By removing the reliance on external human or MLLM annotators for training data, VideoSSR inherently reduces the risk of models learning from flawed, biased, or spurious reward signals. This leads to more robust and reliable MLLMs that are less susceptible to the artifacts introduced by less capable annotator models, as demonstrated by the performance degradation seen when fine-tuning on some existing MLLM-annotated datasets.

6. **Open-Source and Reproducible Research:** The commitment to making the code available at `https://github.com/lcqysl/VideoSSR` ensures that this valuable framework can be easily adopted, extended, and built upon by the wider research community, fostering further innovation.

In summary, VideoSSR represents a significant step towards enabling MLLMs to learn directly from the rich, inherent structure of video, rather than being limited by external, static, and often costly annotations. It paves the way for a new generation of video-understanding MLLMs that are more capable, generalizable, and efficient to develop. The exploration of more diverse pretext tasks and scaling to longer video inputs remain promising avenues for future research building on this impactful work.
