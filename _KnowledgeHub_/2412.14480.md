# GraphEQA: Using 3D Semantic Scene Graphs for Real-time Embodied Question Answering

> [!summary] Summary
> GraphEQA enables robotic agents to answer natural language questions in unseen environments by constructing and enriching 3D metric-semantic scene graphs in real-time, coupled with a dynamically updated task-relevant visual memory. This approach achieved a 63.5% success rate on the HM-EQA benchmark, outperforming baselines, and demonstrated improved grounding for Vision-Language Models in real-world settings with a Hello Robot Stretch RE2.

## Problem

- Existing Embodied Question Answering (EQA) methods struggle to create and maintain semantic representations that are simultaneously rich, compact, and dynamically updatable for real-time online exploration.
- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often lack effective grounding in the agent's real-time physical environment, leading to a disconnect between commonsense reasoning and context-based decision-making.
- EQA agents frequently employ inefficient exploration strategies, resulting in excessive actions and long trajectories due to a lack of robust, dynamic semantic understanding of the environment.

## Method

- **GraphEQA** incrementally constructs and semantically enriches ==3D metric-semantic scene graphs (3DSGs)== by replacing generic room labels with semantic ones and integrating semantically contextualized frontier nodes.
- A multimodal memory is created, combining the global, sparse 3DSG with a concise, dynamically selected buffer of top-K task-relevant images from the agent's recent observations.
- A hierarchical ==Vision-Language Model (VLM)==-based planner uses this multimodal memory to orchestrate efficient, semantics-guided exploration and object-focused navigation within the environment.

## Results

- GraphEQA-GPT4o achieved a **63.5%** success rate on the HM-EQA dataset, surpassing the Explore-EQA baseline (**51.7%**) while requiring fewer planning steps and shorter trajectory lengths.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the scene graph (GraphEQA-SG: **13.6%** success) and the task-relevant visual memory (GraphEQA-Vis: **45.7%** success) are critical components for the system's performance.
- Real-world experiments on a Hello Robot Stretch RE2 demonstrated the system's practical applicability and improved VLM grounding, evidenced by a significant reduction in false positives (**6.36%** for GraphEQA-GPT4o versus **24.56%** for Explore-EQA-GPT4o).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining a global, semantically-sparse 3D scene graph with a local, task-relevant visual memory provides a more effective and robust multimodal memory for grounding VLM-based planners than relying on either modality alone.
> - Incrementally built and enriched 3DSGs, despite being constructed online, can enable more efficient VLM inference and superior performance compared to systems with full, static, offline scene graph access.
> - Semantically-guided hierarchical planning, leveraging enriched frontiers and object nodes within the 3DSG, leads to significantly fewer planning steps and reduced VLM overconfidence, improving exploration efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "GraphEQA" addresses the challenging field of **Embodied Question Answering (EQA)**, a critical benchmark for developing intelligent robots capable of understanding and interacting with complex, unseen environments. EQA requires an agent to not only comprehend natural language questions but also to physically explore an environment, build a semantic understanding of it, and then confidently answer the question based on its observations.

Historically, EQA has faced several significant hurdles:
- **Semantic Representation:** Creating useful, concise, and semantically rich representations of 3D environments that can be updated online. Many prior works either rely on extensive image sets, leading to scalability issues, or require expensive offline processing to build compact representations, hindering real-time deployment in novel settings.
- **Online Grounding for Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** While VLMs have demonstrated impressive commonsense reasoning, effectively grounding their decisions in the current environmental context for efficient planning and exploration remains a challenge. Existing approaches often disconnect commonsense reasoning from context-based decision-making. Some methods use semantic maps for retrieval or exploration but may struggle with incorporating dynamic or newly discovered information.
- **Efficient Exploration:** Developing exploration strategies that are semantically guided and task-relevant, rather than purely random or exhaustive, to reduce the number of actions and trajectory length required to answer a question.

GraphEQA positions itself by bridging these gaps. It leverages **3D metric-semantic scene graphs (3DSGs)** as a core representation, which are compact, semantically-rich, and capture hierarchical spatial and relational information. Critically, GraphEQA focuses on **real-time, online construction and enrichment** of these 3DSGs, distinguishing it from many prior works that rely on offline map building. By combining this global, semantically-sparse scene graph information with local, semantically-rich **task-relevant images** (selected dynamically), GraphEQA creates a novel multimodal memory for grounding VLM-based planners. This dual-modality allows the VLM to reason at both high (scene graph) and fine-grained (image) levels, facilitating more robust and efficient decision-making in unseen environments.

The work builds upon advancements in online 3DSG construction (e.g., Hydra) and the impressive reasoning capabilities of foundation models (LLMs and VLMs). It directly contrasts with methods that use only semantic maps for retrieval or exploration by integrating dynamic visual memory and a hierarchical planning strategy. Furthermore, by demonstrating superior performance compared to baselines that either rely heavily on explicit 2D semantic maps (Explore-EQA) or require full offline scene graphs (SayPlan), GraphEQA marks a significant step towards more adaptable and performant embodied AI systems capable of operating in the real world. Its focus on grounding VLMs in real-time 3D environments also contributes to the broader goal of making foundation models more useful for robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of GraphEQA is to enable robotic agents to effectively solve **Embodied Question Answering (EQA)** tasks in **unseen environments** by developing a novel approach that addresses the limitations of existing methods, particularly concerning semantic representations and VLM grounding.

The motivations driving this work stem from several key challenges in current EQA systems:
1. **Inefficient Semantic Representation:** Prior EQA methods often struggle to create and maintain semantic representations that are simultaneously rich enough for detailed reasoning, compact enough for scalability, and dynamically updatable in real-time. Many approaches either use large, memory-intensive visual memories (e.g., extensive image sets) or rely on offline processing to generate compact representations, precluding their use in real-time, online exploration scenarios. GraphEQA aims to create an **online, compact, and multimodal semantic memory**.
2. **Disconnected Reasoning and Grounding:** While Vision-Language Models (VLMs) excel at commonsense reasoning, integrating this capability with an agent's real-time perception and context-based decision-making in the physical environment remains a hurdle. Existing VLM-based planners often lack strong grounding in the current scene, leading to over-reliance on commonsense reasoning or unguided exploration. GraphEQA is motivated to **ground VLM-based planners** in a continuously updated, multimodal representation of the environment.
3. **Suboptimal Exploration Strategies:** Without a robust and dynamic semantic understanding of the environment, agents resort to less efficient exploration strategies. This can lead to excessive planning steps and longer trajectories, especially in long-horizon EQA tasks. The authors seek to develop an **efficient, semantics-guided hierarchical planning approach** that leverages the environment's structure for targeted exploration.
4. **Real-world Applicability:** Many EQA benchmarks primarily exist in simulation. A core motivation is to demonstrate the practical applicability and robustness of the proposed system in **real-world home and office environments**, using a mobile manipulation platform.

In summary, GraphEQA aims to enable confident and efficient EQA performance by integrating real-time 3D metric-semantic scene graphs with dynamically selected task-relevant images, all within a hierarchical VLM-based planning framework. This addresses the critical need for online, grounded, and semantically-guided exploration in complex, novel environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GraphEQA proposes a novel, real-time approach to Embodied Question Answering by integrating online 3D metric-semantic scene graphs with a task-relevant visual memory, both grounding a hierarchical Vision-Language Model (VLM) planner. The overall architecture is depicted in Figure 2 of the paper.

The methodology can be broken down into the following key components:

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The system relies on:
- **Hierarchical 3D Metric-semantic Scene Graphs (3DSGs):** Utilizing systems like Hydra, GraphEQA incrementally constructs a layered representation of the environment. These layers typically include a metric-semantic 3D mesh (Layer 1), objects (Layer 2), regions (Layer 3), rooms (Layer 4), and buildings (Layer 5). Edges denote either 'traversability' (intra-layer) or 'belonging' (inter-layer), such as an object belonging to a room.
- **2D Occupancy Mapping and Frontier Detection:** Alongside the 3DSG, a 2D occupancy map is maintained. This map distinguishes explored, occupied, and unexplored regions. Frontiers, which are boundaries between explored and unexplored areas, are identified, clustered, and used to guide further exploration.

#### 3.2. Scene Graph Construction and Enrichment

To enhance the utility of the 3DSG for VLM-based planning, GraphEQA performs two key enrichment steps:
- **Room Enrichment:** Generic room labels (e.g., R0, R1) assigned by the 3DSG construction system (Hydra) are replaced with semantic labels (e.g., "kitchen," "living room"). This is achieved by prompting a Large Language Model (LLM) with a list of objects present in each room (e.g., "Which room are these objects <object list> most likely located in?").
- **Frontier Enrichment:** To enable semantics-guided exploration, identified frontiers from the 2D occupancy map are integrated into the 3DSG as independent nodes. Edges are then added to connect each frontier node to its `top-j` nearest object nodes within a maximum distance `d` (e.g., j=3, d=2m). This provides semantic context to frontiers, allowing the VLM planner to reason about their relevance for exploration.

#### 3.3. Task-relevant Visual Memory

To provide local, semantically rich information to the VLM, GraphEQA maintains a concise visual memory:
- During action execution, images are collected and stored in a buffer at a specified sampling frequency.
- To avoid redundancy, only the top-K (K=2 in experiments) most task-relevant images are selected from this buffer. Relevance is determined by processing images and keywords from the question/task using a vision-language model (SigLIP) to obtain text-image relevancy scores.
- These selected images, along with the agent's current view, form the visual input to the VLM planner for the next planning step, ensuring the VLM has access to pertinent visual evidence.

#### 3.4. Hierarchical Vision-Language Planner

The core of GraphEQA is a VLM-based planner that orchestrates exploration and answers questions:
- **Inputs:** At each planning step, the VLM receives the multiple-choice question, the set of possible answers, the enriched 3DSG, the task-relevant visual memory, a structured history of past states and actions, and the agent's current state (including agent ID, position, room ID, and room name).
- **Outputs:** The planner outputs the current best answer, a boolean confidence (`is_confident`), a numeric confidence level (`confidence_level`), and the next high-level action (`u_t`). It also provides reasoning for its choices and a brief description of the scene graph and visual memory.
- **Action Types:**
 - `<Goto_Object_node>(object_id)`: Navigates the agent near a specific object. A hierarchical planning behavior is enforced: the VLM first reasons about which *room* to go to, then a *region* within that room, and finally the *object* itself. This reflects the 3DSG's structure and guides semantically relevant investigation.
 - `<Goto_Frontier_node>(frontier_id)`: Navigates to an unexplored frontier. The VLM is required to justify its choice by referencing object nodes connected to that frontier, promoting task-relevant information gathering.
- **Termination Condition:** An episode terminates if the VLM is confident in its answer (`is_confident=True` or `confidence_level > 0.9`) or if a maximum number of planning steps (`T_max`) is reached.
- **Prompting:** A detailed system prompt guides the VLM on interpreting the scene graph, choosing actions hierarchically (for objects) or based on semantic relevance (for frontiers), and always seeking visual confirmation before confident answering. The authors leverage structured output capabilities of the VLM (e.g., OpenAI's Python API) to enforce a desired response format.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

GraphEQA is evaluated in both simulation (Habitat-Sim on HM-EQA and OpenEQA datasets) and real-world environments using the Hello Robot Stretch RE2. Various foundation models (GPT4o, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Llama 4 Maverick) are used as VLM planners. Metrics include Success Rate, Average Number of Planning Steps, and Average Trajectory Length.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GraphEQA's performance, evaluated across two benchmark datasets in simulation (HM-EQA and OpenEQA) and validated in real-world settings, demonstrates significant advancements in Embodied Question Answering.

#### 4.1. Comparison to Baselines (Q1 & Q2)

- **Overall Superiority:** GraphEQA consistently outperforms all tested baselines across both HM-EQA and OpenEQA datasets. For example, GraphEQA-GPT4o achieves a 63.5% success rate on HM-EQA compared to 51.7% for the original Explore-EQA, while also requiring significantly fewer planning steps and shorter trajectory lengths. This directly answers Q1: hierarchical 3D scene graphs, when enriched and combined with visual memory, provide an effective metric-semantic memory for EQA.
- **Efficiency:** GraphEQA completes tasks with substantially fewer VLM planning steps and shorter trajectory lengths than Explore-EQA variants. This indicates more efficient navigation and decision-making due to the ability to plan across the entire explored space using the hierarchical scene graph, rather than relying solely on local image information.
- **Addressing VLM Overconfidence:** Error analysis (Table 6) reveals that GraphEQA-GPT4o has significantly fewer false positives (6.36%) compared to Explore-EQA-GPT4o (24.56%). This highlights GraphEQA's effectiveness in grounding the VLM's reasoning in the environment, preventing it from answering questions based purely on commonsense or guessing without visual evidence.
- **Real-time vs. Offline (Q2):** Surprisingly, GraphEQA, which incrementally builds its scene graph in real-time, outperforms SayPlanVision (a modified SayPlan with full, offline scene graph access). This suggests that providing the VLM with an overly large context (a complete scene graph of a large environment) can make inference harder for the VLM, leading to overconfidence in object-node actions and increased failure rates. GraphEQA's incremental memory construction and concise representation prove beneficial.
- **Performance Across Task Categories:** GraphEQA shows stronger performance across all HM-EQA task categories (identification, counting, existence, state, location), with a particularly notable improvement in challenging "Counting" and "State" tasks (Table 3).

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies (Q3)

- **Multimodal Memory is Crucial:** Ablations clearly demonstrate the benefits of GraphEQA's multimodal memory:
 - **GraphEQA-SG (Scene Graph only):** This variant, lacking visual memory, performs significantly worse (13.6% success rate on HM-EQA) than the full GraphEQA (63.5%). This underscores that a scene graph, even with enrichment, provides semantically sparse information that is insufficient without detailed visual context for complex EQA tasks.
 - **GraphEQA-Vis (Visuals only, random exploration):** This variant also performs poorly (45.7% success rate, but with only 1.0 average planning steps, indicating high overconfidence and premature termination). Without the scene graph to provide global semantic and spatial understanding, the agent relies solely on current visual input and performs random exploration, leading to ungrounded decisions and overconfidence.
- **Enrichment and Task-Relevant Views:** Further ablations (Table 4) show that **frontier enrichment** (GraphEQA-NoEnrich, 59.5%) and maintaining **task-relevant visual memory** (GraphEQA-CurrView, 53.1%) are both important contributors to GraphEQA's performance. The drop in performance when removing these features emphasizes their role in semantically informed exploration and providing crucial visual context.

#### 4.3. Real-world Experiments

- GraphEQA was successfully deployed on a Hello Robot Stretch RE2 platform in two distinct home and office environments.
- Qualitative examples (Figure 4, 16, 17 and Appendix D) illustrate the agent's ability to reason about the scene graph, select task-relevant frontiers and objects for exploration, and gather visual evidence to confidently answer questions like "How many white cushions are there on the grey couch?" or "Where is the backpack?". These demonstrations validate the practical applicability of the approach beyond simulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GraphEQA represents a significant step forward in the field of embodied AI, particularly for tasks requiring complex semantic understanding and interaction with novel environments. Its contributions have several key implications and potential impacts:

1. **Advancement in Embodied Question Answering (EQA):** By demonstrating superior performance across various metrics (success rate, planning efficiency, trajectory length) on established benchmarks and in real-world settings, GraphEQA pushes the state-of-the-art in EQA. It tackles the core challenges of semantic representation, VLM grounding, and efficient exploration in a unified framework.

2. **Effective Grounding for Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** The work provides a robust and real-time mechanism to ground powerful, but often unmoored, VLMs in the physical context of an environment. This is crucial for translating high-level language understanding and commonsense reasoning into actionable, verifiable decisions for robots. The reduced false positives observed in GraphEQA (compared to VLM-only baselines) directly showcases this improved grounding, leading to more reliable robotic behavior.

3. **Utility of Real-time, Multimodal Memory:** GraphEQA highlights the power of combining a real-time, semantically-sparse 3D scene graph with a compact, task-relevant visual memory. This multimodal approach offers a balanced solution, providing both global structural context and local visual detail, which is more effective than relying on either modality alone or on static, offline representations. The finding that incremental memory construction can outperform full, offline scene graph processing for VLM inference is particularly impactful, suggesting a paradigm shift for future embodied AI systems.

4. **Efficient and Explainable Exploration:** The hierarchical planning approach, guided by enriched scene graphs and semantically relevant frontiers, enables more targeted and efficient exploration. This reduces the number of costly robot movements and VLM inference calls, which is critical for real-world deployments where energy, time, and computational resources are limited. The planner's ability to provide reasoning for its actions also enhances transparency and trust in autonomous systems.

5. **Broader Applicability to Robotics:** While focused on EQA, the core principles of GraphEQA—real-time 3D semantic mapping, multimodal memory, and VLM-based hierarchical planning—are broadly applicable to a wider range of robotics tasks. These include object-goal navigation, mobile manipulation, and human-robot interaction in complex, dynamic environments. The paper itself notes that EQA presents a greater challenge than object-goal navigation, implying its solution offers benefits for simpler tasks too.

6. **Catalyst for Future Research:** The paper identifies several limitations that open avenues for future work, such as reducing reliance on off-the-shelf segmentation, developing calibrated VLM confidence models, enriching scene graphs with open-set semantics online, and extending to multi-floor environments. These directions are essential for pushing autonomous agents closer to human-like understanding and interaction.

In conclusion, GraphEQA contributes a practical and effective framework that significantly advances the capability of robots to understand and respond to complex questions in dynamic, unfamiliar physical spaces, paving the way for more intelligent and versatile robotic assistants.
