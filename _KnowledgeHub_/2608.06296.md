# On-Policy Self-Distillation without Any Supervision

> [!summary] Summary
> Unsupervised On-Policy Self-Distillation (U-OPSD) enables large language models to improve their reasoning capabilities without external supervision by generating multiple solutions, establishing a majority-vote pseudo-solution, and then distilling this consensus back into the model to correct divergent reasoning paths, leading to performance gains of 8.5-10.7 percentage points on mathematical reasoning benchmarks for Qwen3 models.

## Problem

- Existing on-policy distillation (OPD) and on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) methods are universally dependent on external supervision, such as ground-truth solutions or explicit guidance from stronger models.
- This reliance limits the scalability of these techniques to unlabeled problems and restricts their applicability in domains where acquiring high-quality supervision is costly, unreliable, or completely unavailable.
- Traditional self-distillation, even when sharing parameters between teacher and student, still requires externally provided "privileged" information to inform the teacher's superior performance.

## Method

- **U-OPSD** replaces external supervision with an ==internal consistency signal== derived from the model's own independent rollouts, constructing a problem-specific self-teacher.
- For each unlabeled problem, the method samples multiple rollouts, uses a ==plurality vote== to determine a pseudo-answer, calculates a ==self-consistency score==, and then distills this consensus.
- Training focuses on problems where the model shows partial competence but still generates conflicting trajectories, minimizing a ==token-level divergence== between the teacher's (consensus-guided) and student's (divergent) next-token distributions.

## Results

- U-OPSD improved Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B base models by **8.5** and **10.7 percentage points**, respectively, in non-thinking mode on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, outperforming all supervised and unsupervised baselines.
- In thinking mode, U-OPSD achieved average scores of **77.05%** for Qwen3-4B and **77.99%** for Qwen3-8B, performing comparably to or slightly better than supervised OPSD and consistently outperforming GRPO.
- Ablation studies confirmed the superiority of full-vocabulary logit distillation, found that a lower self-consistency threshold (**τ=0**) yielded the best average accuracy, and indicated that an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) teacher update strategy could provide further performance gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Internal consensus, derived from multiple self-generated solutions, can effectively serve as a supervisory signal for token-level distillation, eliminating the need for external ground-truth or a separate, stronger teacher model.
> - The process implicitly creates a self-curriculum by focusing distillation on
> - disagreeing rollouts
> - for problems where the model has achieved a certain level of internal agreement but still exhibits inconsistency.
> - Utilizing the full reasoning trace of the pseudo-solution for the teacher's reference is crucial, as distilling only the final boxed answer significantly degrades performance, emphasizing the importance of the reasoning process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Post-training methodologies are critical for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs). These methods commonly include supervised fine-tuning (SFT), knowledge distillation from more powerful teacher models, and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). On-policy distillation (OPD) represents a convergence point between SFT and RL, focusing on training LLMs using their own generated trajectories. This approach aims to mitigate issues such as train-inference mismatch and catastrophic forgetting often associated with teacher-forced SFT, while still providing dense, token-level supervision, in contrast to the sparse scalar rewards of traditional RL.

Previous work in OPD has explored various aspects, including objective functions (e.g., skewed KL divergence, asymmetric objectives), supervision strategies (e.g., restricting supervision to teacher-supported tokens, masking unreliable signals), and system optimizations (e.g., precomputing teacher scores). A significant advancement in this area is On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD), which removes the necessity of a separate, stronger teacher model by having a single LLM fulfill both teacher and student roles. In OPSD, the teacher component of the model is typically conditioned on a ground-truth solution or other privileged information, while the student operates solely on the problem statement. Subsequent research on OPSD has investigated issues such as information leakage and instability, redesigned privileged contexts, and extended the approach to agentic setups.

Despite these developments, a common characteristic of existing OPD and OPSD methods is their reliance on external supervision. This external information can manifest as ground-truth solutions, environmental feedback, guidance from larger models, or verified demonstrations. While the teacher and student might share parameters in "self-distillation," the crucial information driving the teacher's superior performance originates from outside the model itself. This dependence creates limitations, particularly for scaling to unlabeled problems and for applications in domains where acquiring supervision is expensive, unreliable, or entirely unavailable.

This paper addresses this specific limitation by proposing a method that removes the requirement for *any* external supervision in on-policy self-distillation. It builds upon the concept of self-rewarding reinforcement learning, which utilizes intrinsic signals such as self-certainty, predictive entropy, or agreement across sampled solutions (self-consistency) to generate reward signals from unlabeled data. Similarly, it draws on self-training and self-distillation techniques that derive supervision from a model's own generations or different instances/views of the same model. The proposed Unsupervised On-Policy Self-Distillation (U-OPSD) specifically connects self-consistency with on-policy self-distillation by using internal consensus to construct a problem-specific self-teacher, thereby performing "genuine self-distillation" where both the teacher reference and student trajectories are derived from the model’s own on-policy samples.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to ascertain whether on-policy self-distillation can be achieved without any external supervision. The authors investigate if an LLM can construct its own privileged context for teaching and perform genuine self-distillation, thereby overcoming the limitations inherent in existing methods.

The motivation for this objective stems from several identified bottlenecks in the current landscape of post-training LLMs:

1. **Reliance on External Supervision:** Existing OPD and OPSD approaches, despite their advancements, uniformly depend on external forms of supervision. This includes ground-truth solutions, environmental feedback mechanisms, or explicit guidance from more capable (often larger) models. Even in "self-distillation" where the teacher and student share parameters, the distinguishing information that makes the teacher "privileged" still originates externally.
2. **Limited Scalability to Unlabeled Data:** The need for labeled data, whether it be ground-truth solutions or detailed environmental feedback, inherently limits the scalability of these methods. Many real-world problems lack extensive, high-quality labeled datasets.
3. **Restrictions in Specific Domains:** In domains where supervision is inherently costly, difficult to obtain, unreliable, or entirely unavailable, existing OPD/OPSD methods are either impractical or impossible to apply. This restricts the potential deployment and utility of LLMs in such areas.

To address these challenges, the authors pose two fundamental questions:
- "Does the teacher in on-policy self-distillation need a ground-truth solution?"
- "Can a model construct its own privileged context and perform genuine self-distillation?"

By answering these questions affirmatively through the development of U-OPSD, the research aims to enable continuous model improvement without external data annotation, thereby broadening the applicability and scalability of advanced LLM reasoning capabilities. The core idea is that even if individual model generations are unreliable, the consensus across multiple independent generations can provide a robust, endogenous confidence signal suitable for self-supervision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Unsupervised On-Policy Self-Distillation (U-OPSD), replaces the external supervision typically required by OPSD with a self-generated, internal consistency signal derived from the model's own rollouts. The process for each unlabeled problem `x` involves three main steps: Sample, Vote, and Distill.

**Algorithm 1: U-OPSD (for one prompt)**

1. **Sample:** For a given prompt `x` from an unlabeled problem distribution `U`, the current policy `π_θ` (with gradients detached, denoted `¯π`) generates `G` independent rollouts (`y^(1), ..., y^(G)`). From each rollout, the final answer `a^(g)` is extracted and canonicalized. Rollouts failing to produce a parsable answer are marked as invalid (`∅`).
2. **Vote:**
 - Among the valid answers, a pseudo-answer `˜a(x)` is determined by a plurality vote (majority consensus). Ties are broken randomly.
 - Rollouts are then partitioned into two sets: `Y⁺_x` (agreeing with `˜a(x)`) and `Y⁻_x` (disagreeing with `˜a(x)`). Invalid rollouts are excluded.
 - A self-consistency score `c(x)` is calculated as the fraction of all sampled rollouts (including invalid ones) that agree with `˜a(x)`.
 - A critical step is the application of a self-consistency threshold `τ`. If `c(x) < τ` (default `τ = 0.5` for absolute majority) or if the set of disagreeing rollouts `Y⁻_x` is empty, the prompt is considered unreliable or having no inconsistency to correct, and no gradient is computed for this step. This effectively focuses training on the model's "competence frontier"—problems where it can identify a plausible solution consistently but still generates conflicting trajectories.
3. **Distill:**
 - If the vote is valid and there are disagreeing rollouts, a reference rollout `y⁺` is selected from `Y⁺_x` to serve as the "pseudo-solution" for the teacher. By default, the shortest agreeing rollout is chosen to potentially encourage concise reasoning.
 - The model's current policy `π_θ` acts as the student, conditioned only on the problem `x` and the prefix of its own rollout `y⁻_<t`.
 - The teacher policy `¯π` is conditioned on the problem `x`, the selected pseudo-solution `y⁺`, and the prefix of a disagreeing student rollout `y⁻_<t`.
 - The objective is to minimize a token-level divergence `D_β` (e.g., forward KL divergence) between the teacher's next-token distribution and the student's next-token distribution. This distillation occurs along the prefixes of the selected disagreeing rollouts from `Y⁻_x`. The loss function is defined as:
 `L_U-OPSD(θ) = E_x~U E_{y^(g)}~¯π(·|x) [1_{Y⁻_x ≠ ∅} * (1/|Y⁻_x|) * Σ_{y⁻∈Y⁻_x} (1/|y⁻|) * Σ_{n=1}^{|y⁻|} D_β (¯π(· | x, y⁺, y⁻_<n) || π_θ(· | x, y⁻_<n))]`
 - This formulation ensures that the model learns to align its behavior with its own consensus, specifically correcting its divergent reasoning paths.

**Experimental Setup:**

- **Models:** Four variants of the Qwen3 LLM were used: Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507, and Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507. Experiments were conducted in both "non-thinking" and "thinking" modes for Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B.
- **Dataset:** A 30k subset of the OpenThoughts dataset was used for training. U-OPSD only utilized the problem statements, without access to gold solutions.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Supervised:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with gold solutions, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with binary outcome rewards, and On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) with ground-truth solutions.
 - **Unsupervised RL (label-free):** TTRL, RENT, and Intuitor.
- **Evaluation:** Five competition-level mathematical reasoning benchmarks were used: AIME24, AIME25, HMMT25 (averaged over 12 samples), and MATH500, AMC23 (averaged over 4 samples).
- **Implementation Details:**
 - Forward KL divergence (β=0) with per-token pointwise clipping was used as the objective.
 - The teacher policy was fixed to the initial policy (base model with LoRA adapter disabled) for fair comparison with OPSD, though ablations later explored Exponential Moving Average (EMA) teacher updates.
 - LoRA (rank 64, α=128) was applied to all attention and MLP projections.
 - Learning rate: 5e-6; gradient-norm clipping: 0.1.
 - Sampling temperature: 1.1 with top-p 0.95 and top-k 20 for rollouts.
 - Training was conducted for 150 steps, with checkpoints every 25 steps.
 - Maximum completion length was increased from 1,024 (for OPSD) to 4,096 tokens for U-OPSD to ensure rollouts could reach a final boxed answer.
 - For thinking-mode experiments, the teacher remained in reasoning mode, distilling into a student in non-reasoning mode, with evaluation in reasoning mode. For non-reasoning experiments, both teacher and student were in non-reasoning mode during training and evaluation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrated that U-OPSD consistently improved performance across various settings and benchmarks without any external supervision.

**Non-Thinking Mode (Table 1):**
- U-OPSD achieved average scores of 49.49% on Qwen3-4B and 54.31% on Qwen3-8B. This represents an improvement of 8.5 percentage points for Qwen3-4B and 10.7 percentage points for Qwen3-8B over their respective base models.
- Despite operating without ground-truth solutions, U-OPSD outperformed all supervised baselines. It surpassed OPSD by 3.2 points on Qwen3-4B and 2.3 points on Qwen3-8B on average.
- Compared to other label-free RL baselines (TTRL, RENT, Intuitor), U-OPSD showed significantly larger average gains of 7.0–11.3 percentage points, indicating that consensus-derived solutions are more effective as privileged context for token-level distillation than as scalar rewards for policy optimization.
- U-OPSD achieved the best results on four out of five benchmarks for both 4B and 8B models in this mode.

**Thinking Mode (Table 2):**
- U-OPSD achieved average scores of 77.05% on Qwen3-4B and 77.99% on Qwen3-8B, improving over the base models by 2.2 and 1.9 percentage points, respectively.
- In this mode, U-OPSD performed comparably to or slightly better than supervised OPSD (ahead by 0.9 points at 4B and essentially tied at 8B), and consistently outperformed GRPO (by 0.7-1.1 points).
- The magnitude of improvement was smaller compared to the non-thinking mode. This suggests that U-OPSD is most impactful when the base model is sufficiently competent to generate informative consensus but still has substantial room for improvement, which is less the case for stronger thinking-mode models with higher baseline accuracy.

**Instruction-Tuned Models (Table 3):**
- U-OPSD was also evaluated on instruction-tuned models using a pass@1 metric. It improved Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 from 75.77% to 77.46% and Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 from 67.00% to 68.78%.
- It surpassed supervised OPSD by 1.1 and 1.7 percentage points on average for these models, demonstrating its transferability to a larger mixture-of-experts model without extensive hyperparameter tuning.

**Training Curves (Figure 3):**
- Training curves for Qwen3-4B showed that U-OPSD consistently improved performance from early checkpoints, particularly in non-thinking mode where it clearly separated from all other methods. In thinking mode, all methods including U-OPSD remained within a narrower performance band.

**Ablation and Analysis:**
- **Pseudo-label Quality:** A high percentage of rollouts (96.3%) yielded parsable answers. 94.0% of prompts cleared the self-consistency threshold, and 86.7% of these pseudo-labels matched the ground-truth answers.
- **Divergence Computation Strategy (Table 6):** Full-vocabulary logit distillation was found to be superior to sampled-token distillation or top-k truncation, leading to gains of 13.7 to 15.6 points compared to the sampled-token variant.
- **Self-Consistency Threshold (Figure 4, Table 10):** A lower threshold (τ=0, accepting all prompts) yielded the best average accuracy (59.21%), suggesting that the benefits of including more prompts outweigh the noise introduced by less confident pseudo-labels.
- **Rollouts Per Prompt (G) (Figure 4, Table 11):** Performance increased with G but showed saturating returns, with G=12 performing slightly better than G=8 (58.45% vs 53.93%).
- **Teacher Update Strategy (Figure 4, Table 12):** Using an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) for the teacher policy (e.g., decay 0.995) showed potential for improvement over a fixed initial teacher (up to 2.4 points at the best checkpoint).
- **Teacher Reference and Distillation Target (Table 4):** Conditioning the teacher on the longest agreeing rollout and distilling into the longest incorrect completion yielded the best results. Using only the boxed pseudo-label as the teacher's reference significantly degraded performance, highlighting the importance of the full reasoning trace.
- **Selection of Disagreeing Rollouts (Table 5):** Capping the number of distilled disagreeing rollouts (e.g., `disagree-1`, `disagree-3`) consistently outperformed the supervised baseline. `Disagree-1` (distilling one random disagreeing rollout) was generally the most consistent. Distilling all disagreeing rollouts (`disagree-all`) performed worse.
- **Divergence Choice (Table 7):** Forward KL divergence (teacher → student) was robust and effective, aligning with OPSD's findings. Reverse KL divergence led to training divergence (loss of termination), and Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) performed poorly.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a notable advancement in the field of LLM post-training by demonstrating the feasibility and effectiveness of genuinely unsupervised on-policy self-distillation. The significance and potential impact of U-OPSD can be outlined as follows:

1. **Enabling Truly Unsupervised LLM Improvement:** U-OPSD eliminates the dependency on external supervision (ground-truth labels, stronger teachers, environmental feedback) for on-policy self-distillation. This represents a foundational shift, allowing LLMs to iteratively improve their reasoning capabilities using solely their internal consistency and self-generated data.
2. **Increased Scalability and Applicability:** By removing the need for costly and often scarce labeled data, U-OPSD significantly enhances the scalability of advanced post-training techniques. It opens avenues for applying LLM fine-tuning and improvement in domains where external supervision is prohibitively expensive, unreliable, or completely unavailable, thus broadening the practical utility of LLMs.
3. **Competitive or Superior Performance:** The finding that U-OPSD can match or surpass supervised methods like OPSD (which relies on ground truth) in certain regimes (e.g., non-thinking mode, instruction-tuned models) demonstrates the potency of internal consistency as a supervisory signal. This suggests that the quality of self-generated supervision can be on par with, or even exceed, that of external, human-curated data for specific tasks.
4. **Implicit Curriculum Learning:** The mechanism of focusing distillation on "disagreeing rollouts" for problems where the model has achieved a certain level of internal consensus (`c(x) ≥ τ`) implicitly creates a self-curriculum. This allows the model to concentrate its learning efforts on areas where it exhibits inconsistency despite partial competence, potentially leading to more targeted and efficient learning.
5. **Insights into Self-Correction:** The methodology provides a concrete framework for how LLMs can detect and correct their own internal inconsistencies. By using the majority consensus as a "pseudo-ground truth" and training on divergent trajectories, the model learns to steer away from erroneous reasoning paths that it itself identified as inconsistent.
6. **Reduced Operational Costs:** For organizations and researchers, U-OPSD offers a pathway to continue improving LLMs without the continuous investment in data annotation or access to proprietary, larger teacher models, potentially democratizing access to advanced LLM training paradigms.

**Limitations Acknowledged by the Authors:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations:
- **Scope:** Experiments were confined to one model family (Qwen3) and a single domain (mathematical reasoning) requiring automatically checkable final answers. Extending to open-ended generation would require more flexible consensus mechanisms.
- **Regime-Dependent Gains:** The performance gains over supervised OPSD were more pronounced in non-thinking mode compared to thinking mode, where base models already demonstrate high accuracy and less "headroom" for improvement.
- **Base-Model Competence:** The effectiveness of majority-vote supervision is bounded by the base model's inherent accuracy, as it relies on the model generating a correct answer most often.
- **Variance:** While mitigated by extensive evaluation, seed-replicated error bars for every training setup were not provided in the preprint.

Despite these limitations, U-OPSD presents a significant step towards genuinely autonomous LLM improvement, offering a robust and label-free alternative to traditional supervised distillation methods.
