# VENOM: Versatile Embodied Network for Omni-bodied Motion tracking

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Osaka developed VENOM, a GPT-based model capable of full-body motion tracking across five different humanoid robots without decoupling body parts. The approach includes a novel 7705-hour multi-humanoid dataset and demonstrates comparable joint tracking accuracy to reinforcement learning experts while outperforming MLP baselines in motion stability.

## Problem

- Difficulty in achieving expressive full-body motion tracking across diverse humanoid robots from demonstration data.
- Existing cross-embodiment methods often decouple body control or are limited to simple locomotion tasks, restricting behavior range.
- Lack of large-scale, low-level control datasets for training generalist policies across varied humanoid platforms.

## Method

- Curated the **VENOM** dataset, a 7705-hour multi-humanoid dataset from five robots (Unitree G1, H1, H1 v2, Fourier N1, Booster T1), including noisy rollouts for enhanced robustness.
- Developed VENOM, a GPT-based transformer model with a ==shared central transformer block== for all humanoids, using distinct input embeddings and action heads for each embodiment.
- Trained the model using supervised learning to minimize Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss between predicted and expert actions derived from the curated dataset.

## Results

- VENOM-noisy demonstrated the best motion tracking among supervised models, outperforming MLP baselines, which showed degraded performance when trained on multi-robot data.
- Achieved comparable joint tracking accuracy (RMSE approximately **0.42** to **0.58** radians) to RL experts across various behaviors and humanoids, while also showing improved motion tracking stability.
- The 95th percentile absolute root error rate for Unitree G1 was **12.3560** cm/s for VENOM-noisy, indicating better stability compared to VENOM-clean (**14.0460** cm/s) and competitive with RL Experts (**10.0030** cm/s).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - GPT-based transformer architectures demonstrate superior performance in generalizing full-body motion tracking across multiple humanoid embodiments compared to MLP baselines, which can suffer from negative transfer.
> - Incorporating noisy demonstrations during training significantly improves the robustness and overall motion tracking capability of the policy by exposing it to underexplored observation regions.
> - Supervised learning, through distillation from expert data, can achieve joint tracking accuracy comparable to RL-trained experts, highlighting its potential for complex control tasks despite lacking direct reward feedback or privileged information.
