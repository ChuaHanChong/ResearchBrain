# DexterCap: An Affordable and Automated System for Capturing Dexterous Hand-Object Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University and Tencent Robotics X researchers developed DexterCap, an affordable and automated optical motion capture system designed for high-fidelity recording of complex in-hand object interactions using low-cost cameras and a deep learning pipeline. The system provides competitive motion smoothness (MSNR 9.31 ± 0.22) and physical plausibility (3.8mm ± 3.1mm hand-object penetration) compared to commercial systems, and is accompanied by the open-source DexterHand dataset for diverse manipulation research.

## Problem

- Accurately capturing fine-grained human hand and object poses during complex in-hand interactions remains challenging due to severe occlusions and the need for high precision.
- Existing marker-based optical systems are expensive, demand extensive manual setup and post-processing, and are prone to occlusion and marker misidentification.
- Markerless vision-based methods are convenient but often lack the robustness, accuracy, and physical plausibility necessary for subtle finger movements and complex manipulation tasks.

## Method

- **DexterCap** implements an affordable multi-camera optical motion capture setup using 13 synchronized industrial cameras (under $6,000 USD) and custom, high-contrast, character-coded checkerboard markers.
- A three-stage deep learning pipeline (CornerNet, EdgeNet, BlockNet) automates 2D marker detection, edge validation, and ID recognition, addressing scale variations, deformation, and noise for robust marker tracking.
- 3D hand motion is reconstructed using a subject-specific MANO model with anatomical priors, while object poses are estimated via the Kabsch algorithm for rigid objects and a PCA-based approach for articulated objects like a Rubik's Cube.

## Results

- The DexterCap system achieved a mean marker reconstruction error of **2.06** ± **1.09** mm during dynamic manipulation, with a reprojection error of **1.42** pixels for detected 3D markers.
- Motion quality metrics demonstrated competitive performance, with an MSNR of **9.31** ± **0.22** and a jerk value of **0.76** ± **0.18** m/s to rival or surpass commercial optical systems and significantly outperform vision-only methods.
- The open-source DexterHand dataset comprises detailed in-hand manipulations of various objects, including a Rubik's Cube, with sequences often exceeding 10 minutes, supporting further research in dexterous manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-precision hand-object interaction capture can be made economically accessible and largely automated by combining low-cost camera hardware with dense, coded marker patterns and a specialized deep learning-based image processing pipeline.
> - A cascaded neural network architecture effectively mitigates challenges such as limited camera depth of field, varying marker scales, and severe occlusions, leading to robust and accurate 2D marker localization and identification.
> - Integrating a parametric hand model with an initial shape calibration and biomechanical regularization, coupled with robust 3D reconstruction and specialized articulated object tracking, ensures physically plausible and detailed hand-object pose recovery during dynamic manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The precise capture and realistic modeling of fine-grained human hand skills, particularly during interactions with objects, remain an active area of research in computer graphics and robotics. Human hands execute a diverse array of dexterous actions critical for daily tasks and specialized activities. While human-object interaction (HOI) datasets and models are increasingly available, existing resources often concentrate on basic actions, such as grasping and moving, or interactions with simple articulated objects, leaving a gap in understanding and synthesizing nuanced in-hand manipulation.

Current motion capture technologies employ various approaches, each with distinct advantages and limitations:
- **Marker-based optical systems (e.g., Qualisys, Vicon)** are generally considered the "gold standard" for high precision, offering sub-millimeter accurate 3D reconstruction. However, they are typically expensive, involve complex camera setups, and are susceptible to occlusion, marker swapping, and mislabeling, necessitating extensive manual post-processing. Solutions involving T-pose initialization, learning-based correspondence, or unique markers exist, but challenges persist, particularly with multi-jointed objects where manual intervention is often still required.
- **Sensor-based methods (e.g., data gloves with IMUs or force sensors)** are less prone to visual occlusion and more cost-effective. Yet, they can suffer from inherent error accumulation, such as gyroscope drift and accelerometer bias, leading to progressive deviations in pose estimation and challenges in achieving fine-grained finger accuracy. Integrating objects into these systems often requires additional markers and alignment procedures.
- **Vision-based markerless methods** offer convenience, affordability, and scalability by estimating pose directly from images or videos without physical markers or specialized suits. These approaches leverage various imaging modalities, including RGB, depth, and infrared cameras, with multi-view systems enhancing 3D accuracy and mitigating occlusions. Despite advances, persistent challenges include severe occlusions, lighting variations, motion blur, maintaining temporal consistency, and ensuring the physical plausibility of estimated poses.

DexterCap aims to address these limitations by developing an affordable, automated optical capture system specifically designed for dexterous in-hand manipulation. It draws inspiration from vision-based approaches that combine deep learning with specially designed visual patterns to automate dense marker identification and localization, extending these concepts to the complex domain of hand-object interactions. The system seeks to offer the accuracy and robustness of marker-based systems without the prohibitive cost and manual effort, while overcoming the accuracy limitations of markerless vision-based methods in capturing subtle finger movements.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce DexterCap, a comprehensive framework for capturing, solving, and reconstructing hand and object motions during dexterous in-hand interactions. This includes developing both a novel motion capture system and an associated dataset. The core motivations and specific objectives are as follows:

- **Affordability and Accessibility**: A key motivation is to build a motion capture system that is economically accessible, utilizing low-cost hardware. Beyond hardware, the system aims to minimize operational costs by providing a largely automated pipeline. After an initial one-time setup for camera calibration and subject-specific hand shape estimation, and a small amount of initial manual labeling for model training, the subsequent data acquisition and processing are designed to be automatic, significantly reducing human effort and enabling scalable data collection.

- **Accurate Recovery of Fine-grained Motions**: The system is motivated by the recognized challenge of accurately capturing subtle finger movements during complex in-hand object manipulation. This includes ensuring robust tracking despite severe self-occlusion, which is common with closely spaced fingers. The goal is to achieve high fidelity in reconstructing both hand and object kinematics.

- **Robustness to Challenges in Hand Mocap**:
 - **Limited Camera Resolution and Depth of Field**: Hand motion capture often requires cameras to be placed close to the subject, leading to limited depth of field and variations in hand scale within images as the hand moves. This complicates robust marker detection across multiple scales. DexterCap aims to address this through a cascaded, three-stage marker detection and identification model.
 - **Occlusion and Marker Dropout**: Markers can be missing in varying patterns due to occlusion and noise. The system is motivated to develop robust calibration and solving procedures, leveraging a morphable hand model (MANO) and dense marker coverage, to accurately recover the full poses of the hand, fingers, and object even with partial visibility.

- **Creation of a Specialized Dataset (DexterHand)**: To demonstrate the capabilities of DexterCap and support future research, a critical objective is to introduce DexterHand. This open-source dataset is specifically designed to capture complex in-hand object manipulation, encompassing diverse behaviors and objects, from simple primitives to highly articulated objects like a Rubik’s Cube. The dataset aims to provide high-detail, precisely controlled, and long-duration motion sequences.

- **Open-Source Contribution**: The project aims to release the DexterHand dataset, along with all tools, models, and processing codebase. This open-source approach is intended to foster further research and development in the field of dexterous hand-object interaction, particularly in areas like robotic manipulation, virtual reality, and human-computer interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DexterCap is a multi-camera, vision-based motion capture and reconstruction framework designed for high-precision tracking of hand and object motions during in-hand interactions.

#### 3.1. Hardware Setup and Marker Design

- **Cameras**: The system employs 13 synchronized Hikvision MV-CS050-10GM industrial GigE PoE cameras. These cameras capture grayscale video at a resolution of 2048 × 2448 pixels at 20 frames per second (FPS). They are mounted on a 2 × 1 × 2 m cage, positioned to minimize occlusions. Standard OpenCV procedures are used for camera calibration. A short exposure time (1ms) is set to reduce motion blur, and the aperture is narrowed to increase depth of field. The total hardware cost for this camera setup is reported to be under 6,000 USD.

- **Markers**: The system utilizes a custom marker design inspired by previous work, featuring high-contrast checkerboard patterns with unique character-coded IDs. Each white square on a marker contains a two-character ID in black (from 26 uppercase letters and 10 digits, excluding visually similar characters, yielding 324 unique tags). An underscore below the left character indicates orientation. Unlike systems where patterns are printed on fabric (e.g., gloves), which can stretch or slide, these marker patches are transfer-printed onto medical adhesive tape. They are then attached directly to rigid regions of the hand, including finger knuckles, the dorsum, and the palm, with 19 patches per hand providing over 500 detectable corners. Markers are also applied to object surfaces, including articulated objects like a Rubik’s Cube, to facilitate robust pose estimation.

#### 3.2. Image Processing and Marker Extraction

The raw video data undergoes a three-stage image processing pipeline to detect and identify visual markers, addressing challenges like marker deformation common in hand manipulation:

- **Corner Detection (CornerNet)**: A U-Net based deep learning model, termed CornerNet, is used for precise localization of checkerboard pattern corners. It takes 64 × 64 grayscale image patches as input and generates a heatmap representing corner locations with Gaussian peaks. To handle scale variations, images are also downsampled and processed. The model is trained to minimize Mean Squared Error (MSE). During inference, a low confidence threshold (0.6) is applied to prioritize recall, and identified corners are the centers of connected components in the binarized heatmap.

- **Edge and Block Detection (EdgeNet)**: After corner detection, a ResNet-based neural network, EdgeNet, acts as a binary classifier to determine if a valid edge exists between any pair of candidate corners within a predefined Euclidean distance. Rectangular image patches centered on these segments are input to EdgeNet. Validated connections are then used in a graph search to assemble convex quadrilateral blocks with acceptable aspect ratios. This edge-first assembly strategy reduces the number of candidate blocks significantly compared to exhaustive quadrilateral validation. EdgeNet is trained with Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss using a balanced sampling strategy.

- **Block Identification (BlockNet)**: The final stage uses BlockNet, another ResNet-based architecture, to validate and identify the detected blocks. BlockNet has three output heads for predicting the two characters of the tag and the block's orientation, trained with standard cross-entropy loss. Invalid blocks and their associated corners are discarded.

- **Post-Processing**: A voting mechanism enhances robustness by leveraging the known marker pattern template. Each candidate block votes on the tags of other blocks within the same marker patch, and the most voted tag is selected as the final prediction for each block, correcting potential mislabeling.

#### 3.3. 3D Marker Reconstruction

- **Triangulation and Outlier Rejection**: Once 2D marker coordinates and IDs are extracted from multiple camera views, their 3D positions are reconstructed via triangulation. Only markers observed by at least three cameras are considered, and RANSAC is applied to reject outlier observations.
- **Outlier Removal Heuristics**: Additional outlier removal steps are performed. Within each marker patch, points are clustered based on 3D distances, retaining only the largest cluster. Furthermore, points whose z-scores within a sliding window exceed a predefined threshold are removed.
- **Missing Marker Filling**: Missing marker positions are linearly interpolated if the marker is observed in at least one of the two preceding frames and one of the two subsequent frames.

#### 3.4. Hand Motion Reconstruction

- **Hand Model Parameterization**: The parametric MANO model is used to represent the 3D hand mesh. Hand shape is controlled by β ∈ R^10, while pose is defined by joint rotations θ ∈ R^45. To ensure biomechanical plausibility, pose is reduced to an anatomically informed φ ∈ R^27, which is mapped to the full MANO pose space. Global hand translation t ∈ R^3 and orientation o (6D representation) are also estimated.
- **Shape Estimation**: For each new subject, the hand shape parameters β are estimated once by fitting the MANO model to a coarse 3D scan of the subject's hand (e.g., from a low-cost depth camera like Structure Sensor 1). This is done by minimizing the Chamfer Distance. The estimated β is then fixed for all subsequent motion capture sessions.
- **Calibration**: An initial calibration phase is performed where the subject holds an open-hand pose and slowly rotates it. During this sequence, the system jointly optimizes the global translation, orientation, hand pose, and marker association parameters (barycentric coordinates b and their triangle indices). The optimization minimizes the error between predicted marker positions on the MANO surface and the captured 3D marker positions. Anatomical priors are incorporated by restricting marker-to-vertex correspondences to predefined submeshes of the MANO model (e.g., specific finger segments). After calibration, these barycentric coordinates are fixed.
- **Solving Motion**: For new motion sequences, the hand parameters (t, o, φ) are optimized per-frame, initialized from the previous frame's solution, with the marker associations (b) fixed. The objective function includes the point error and a regularization term (E_reg) to penalize deviations from natural joint angle limits. The Adam optimizer is used. In cases of marker occlusion, if a marker and its kinematic chain are fully hidden, the corresponding joint degrees of freedom are maintained at their previous-frame values to enhance temporal stability.

#### 3.5. Object Pose Estimation

- **Rigid Objects**: For rigid objects, their 6-DoF pose (position and orientation) is estimated using the Kabsch algorithm. This algorithm aligns the observed object markers with their known canonical positions on the 3D mesh model, minimizing the squared differences.
- **Articulated Objects (Rubik’s Cube)**: Reconstruction of the Rubik's Cube is more complex, involving 384 markers. The system detects face rotations by analyzing the coplanarity scores of marker clusters using Principal Component Analysis. When a rotation is detected (e.g., two opposing face clusters remain coplanar while orthogonal ones become non-coplanar), the cube is decomposed into two 1×2×2 blocks along the rotation axis. Each block is independently registered to its reference configuration using the Kabsch algorithm. The relative rotation angle between blocks is then computed and snapped to discrete 90-degree increments to reflect quarter-turn rotations, updating the internal facelet index mapping. This robustly handles noise and occlusion for articulated object manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DexterCap covers system setup, image processing, 3D reconstruction accuracy, and motion quality, benchmarked against other systems and leading to the DexterHand dataset.

#### 4.1. System Setup

The prototype system, costing under 6,000 USD for hardware, utilizes 13 industrial GigE PoE cameras. To train the deep learning models for marker extraction, a limited dataset of approximately 180 manually labeled frames (2-3 frames per camera per session) was used. The models, trained with standard image augmentations and an AdamW optimizer, reportedly generalize well to new sessions in the same environment without retraining. The unoptimized offline processing pipeline operates at 20 FPS, requiring approximately 5 seconds per frame for marker recognition and 5-12 seconds per frame for hand-object reconstruction, which runs in a fully automated batch mode once models are trained.

#### 4.2. Image Processing and Marker Recognition

The marker detection and recognition pipeline was evaluated using a manually annotated dataset:
- **Corner Detection**: CornerNet, which is heatmap-based, achieved 94.7% precision, 81.6% recall, and an 87.7% F1 score (within a 5-pixel radius of ground truth). This was reported as an improvement over a coordinate-regression model, which yielded many false positives despite high accuracy (98.3% accuracy, 46.3% precision).
- **Edge Recognition**: EdgeNet demonstrated high robustness with 99.02% accuracy, 98.9% precision, 99.1% recall, and a 99.0% F1 score.
- **Block Identification and ID Recognition**: BlockNet achieved 98.39% orientation accuracy, 97.95% left-character accuracy, and 97.36% right-character accuracy on the test set. At the image level, block recognition had a precision of 94.5% and a recall of 41.3%. The lower recall is attributed to conservative strategies in corner and edge detection aimed at reducing false positives.
- **Efficiency**: The edge-first assembly strategy significantly improved efficiency, reducing the average number of candidate quadrilaterals for block identification from 5550 (as in a reimplemented prior method) to 83 blocks (derived from 707 edges).
- **Post-processing**: The voting mechanism corrected an average of 1.825% of mislabeled blocks, further improving recognition accuracy.

#### 4.3. Hand-Object Reconstruction

- **Reprojection Error**: The triangulation of detected 3D markers resulted in a reprojection error of 1.42 pixels. This is higher than the 0.4 pixels achieved during camera calibration (performed under ideal rigid conditions) but is considered satisfactory given the deformability of markers attached to finger segments.
- **Marker Reconstruction Error (MRE)**: The mean Euclidean distance between the predicted marker positions on the MANO model surface and the observed 3D marker positions was 0.77 ± 0.28 mm during calibration phases (high marker visibility) and 2.06 ± 1.09 mm during dynamic manipulation phases (more complex motions and occlusions).
- **Object Pose Estimation**: For object pose estimation, the distance between each reconstructed object marker and its corresponding target position on the optimized rigid object model was 1.512 mm.

#### 4.4. Motion Quality and Plausibility

The DexterHand dataset's motion quality and plausibility were evaluated using several metrics, with a 5 Hz Butterworth low-pass filter applied during post-processing.
- **Physical Plausibility**: The average penetration between the reconstructed hand model and the object mesh was 3.8mm ± 3.1mm. This is consistent with typical human hand deformation under grasping forces, suggesting good accuracy.
- **Benchmarking**: DexterCap and DexterHand were benchmarked against public datasets captured by commercial marker-based systems (GRAB, ARCTIC), data gloves (HUMOTO), and vision-only methods (HaMeR, GigaHands).
 - **Motion Signal-to-Noise Ratio (MSNR)**: DexterCap achieved an MSNR of 9.31 ± 0.22, which is competitive with or superior to commercial systems (GRAB: 7.29±1.51, ARCTIC: 7.82±0.40, HUMOTO: 7.51±3.83) and substantially outperformed vision-only methods (HaMeR: -0.05±0.07, GigaHands: 3.50±1.23). Higher MSNR indicates smoother motion.
 - **Jerk**: DexterCap demonstrated a jerk value of 0.76 ± 0.18 m/s³, indicating smoother kinematics compared to GRAB (3.68±1.74), HUMOTO (1.90±2.09), HaMeR (23.76±0.78), and GigaHands (2.62±1.42). It was slightly lower than ARCTIC (0.91±0.01), where lower values indicate smoother motion.
 - **Diversity and Coherence**: DexterCap exhibited high diversity (0.97) and coherence (0.68), comparable to other benchmarked datasets.
- **DexterHand Dataset Content**: The dataset includes in-hand manipulations of seven basic object shapes and a Rubik’s Cube. Sequences often exceed 10 minutes in duration, and the data is anonymized and publicly released.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents DexterCap, a system designed for capturing and reconstructing complex hand-object interactions, particularly focusing on dexterous in-hand manipulation. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

- **Advancement in Accessible Motion Capture**: DexterCap addresses a notable gap by offering a low-cost yet high-precision optical motion capture system. By leveraging affordable hardware (under $6,000 USD) and an automated reconstruction pipeline, it significantly lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners in fields requiring detailed hand-object interaction data. This affordability makes advanced motion capture more accessible compared to traditionally expensive commercial systems.

- **Enhanced Accuracy and Robustness for Fine-Grained Manipulation**: The system demonstrates competitive performance in terms of motion smoothness (MSNR and Jerk metrics) and physical plausibility (low hand-object penetration) when compared to commercial marker-based systems, while substantially outperforming markerless vision-only methods. Its dense, character-coded marker patches and robust deep learning-based image processing pipeline enable accurate reconstruction of subtle finger movements and reliable tracking even under severe self-occlusion. This is a crucial improvement for capturing the nuances of dexterous manipulation that existing systems often struggle with.

- **Contribution of a Specialized Dataset (DexterHand)**: The introduction of DexterHand, an open-source dataset, is a significant contribution. It provides a rich collection of fine-grained hand-object interactions, including diverse manipulation behaviors with both simple primitive shapes and complex articulated objects like a Rubik’s Cube. This dataset, characterized by high detail, precise control, and extended durations, serves as a valuable resource for training and validating models in areas such as robotic manipulation, human pose estimation, and virtual reality.

- **Support for Future Research**: The release of the DexterHand dataset and the associated codebase encourages and facilitates further research in understanding and synthesizing dexterous manipulation. It provides a standardized benchmark and toolset for developing new algorithms, learning manipulation priors, and evaluating different approaches to hand-object interaction.

- **Limitations and Future Directions**:
 - **Occlusion Sensitivity**: The system's primary limitation remains its vulnerability to severe and simultaneous marker occlusion across all camera views, which can degrade reconstruction quality. Future work is suggested to explore occlusion-aware estimation techniques, integrate learning-based pose priors, or incorporate additional sensing modalities like Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) to mitigate this.
 - **Dataset Expansion**: While valuable, the DexterHand dataset can be expanded in scale and diversity to include a wider range of subjects, objects (deformable, multi-articulated, and tools), and more complex manipulation tasks (e.g., bimanual interactions). Augmenting the dataset with finer-grained semantic annotations (e.g., grasp types, functional intent, contact regions, applied forces) would further enhance its utility for developing controllable and context-aware generative models.
 - **Integration with Physics Simulation**: A promising avenue for future research involves integrating the capture and generation pipeline with physics simulation environments. This could enable rigorous validation of motion plausibility and facilitate the training of robotic agents, with subsequent exploration of sim-to-real transfer techniques for real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

In conclusion, DexterCap represents a notable step forward in making high-quality hand-object interaction capture more accessible and automated, providing both a robust system and a valuable open-source dataset for advancing research in dexterous manipulation.
