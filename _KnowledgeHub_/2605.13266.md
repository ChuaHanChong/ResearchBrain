# Galilean State Estimation for Inertial Navigation Systems with Unknown Time Delay

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel Equivariant Filter, leveraging Galilean symmetry, enables consistent online joint estimation of navigation states and unknown time delays in Inertial Navigation Systems. It exhibited superior consistency and stability in simulations with delays up to 500 ms, where EKF-based alternatives diverged, and successfully tracked real-world UAV GNSS delays.

## Problem

- Unknown and variable time delays (50-300ms) in external sensor measurements, particularly from commercial GNSS receivers, degrade the accuracy and consistency of Inertial Navigation Systems (INS).
- Current Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) approaches for online time-delay estimation exhibit fundamental structural issues, leading to estimation bias and inconsistency that are difficult to mitigate.
- Reliance on offline calibration for delay compensation is impractical for dynamic scenarios where sensor latencies can change or are initially unknown.

## Method

- Introduction of a geometric framework based on the ==Galilean matrix Lie group Gal(3)== to inherently couple spatial navigation states with temporal offsets.
- Derivation of an **Equivariant Filter (EqF)** that jointly estimates navigation states (position, velocity, orientation) and unknown time delays, leveraging the Lie group structure for improved consistency.
- Adaptation of ==IMU preintegration== for time-delayed measurements, utilizing a ==sliding buffer== for efficient management and interpolation of IMU data.

## Results

- The Equivariant Filter (EqF) demonstrated consistent online tracking of unknown time delays in real-world UAV flights, achieving navigation state accuracy comparable to or exceeding offline calibration methods.
- Monte Carlo simulations revealed the EqF maintained stability, consistency (low **NEES**), and low estimation errors for delays up to **500 ms**, whereas EKF-based online delay estimation became inconsistent and diverged.
- All delay-compensating filters, including the EqF, significantly reduced navigation state errors compared to an EKF without delay compensation, underscoring the importance of addressing latency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Galilean matrix Lie group provides a natural geometric framework to model inertial motion with explicit temporal offsets, enabling a coupled representation of space and time for state estimation.
> - Equivariant Filters (EqFs) are more inherently consistent and stable for joint state and time-delay estimation compared to traditional EKFs, particularly when dealing with the non-linear coupling introduced by unknown delays.
> - Efficient IMU preintegration, adapted to account for estimated time delays through a sliding buffer, is crucial for consistently bridging measurement gaps and enabling robust online estimation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Inertial Navigation Systems (INS) are fundamental for estimating the pose (position, velocity, and orientation) of a vehicle, particularly in autonomous systems and robotics. These systems commonly fuse high-rate Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) data with measurements from other sensors, such as Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS). A persistent challenge in this field is the presence of measurement delays, which can arise from sensor processing, communication latency, or asynchronous sampling. Commercial-grade GNSS receivers, for instance, typically introduce unknown time lags ranging from 50 ms to 300 ms, with potential for jitter, due to factors like coherent integration time and system thread priority. Uncompensated delays can significantly degrade INS accuracy and consistency.

Existing approaches to address sensor delays include:
- **Offline Calibration:** Optimization-based techniques that estimate delays by minimizing measurement residuals over batch data. While effective for known delays, this requires re-calibration if delays change.
- **Online Compensation without Explicit Delay Estimation:** Methods like Extended Kalman Filters (EKF) that incorporate delayed measurements through extrapolation and interpolation, or robust filters designed to handle asynchronous data. These focus on processing the delayed data rather than determining the unknown delay itself.
- **Online Delay Estimation (EKF-based):** Several EKF-based methods have been proposed to jointly estimate navigation states and measurement time delays online. However, prior research has indicated that integrating online time-delay estimation into recursive filters can introduce fundamental structural issues, leading to bias and inconsistency that are difficult to mitigate through filter tuning.

The broader landscape has also seen the emergence of Equivariant Filters (EqF), which leverage the intrinsic geometry and symmetries of the state space. EqFs have demonstrated improved accuracy, faster convergence, and enhanced consistency compared to traditional EKFs in various navigation problems, including biased-INS and multi-GNSS navigation. Recent theoretical work has formalized the Galilean matrix Lie group and applied Galilean symmetry to model discrete-time INS and IMU preintegration, recognizing its potential for handling temporal uncertainty.

This paper positions itself within this context by addressing the known limitations of EKF-based online delay estimation. It aims to develop a novel, geometrically principled approach using Galilean symmetry within an EqF framework to achieve consistent online joint estimation of navigation states and unknown time delays, thereby extending the capabilities of INS in real-world applications where delays are prevalent and variable.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a robust and consistent online state estimation method for Inertial Navigation Systems (INS) that accurately accounts for and estimates unknown time delays in external sensor measurements, specifically Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) position data.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in practical INS implementations:
- **Performance Degradation from Unknown Delays:** Commercial-grade GNSS receivers introduce variable and unknown time delays, typically between 50 ms and 300 ms, which can extend up to 500 ms depending on sensor quality and operating conditions. These delays, if not explicitly compensated, can significantly compromise the accuracy and consistency of INS position, velocity, and orientation estimates.
- **Limitations of Existing Delay Compensation Methods:**
 - **Offline Methods:** While effective, these require a priori knowledge or laborious batch optimization for delay estimation, making them unsuitable for scenarios where delays are dynamic or unknown at runtime.
 - **Current Online Methods:** State-of-the-art EKF-based methods that attempt online time-delay estimation have been shown to suffer from fundamental structural issues, leading to biases and inconsistencies that cannot be fully resolved by filter tuning. This limits their reliability and applicability in critical navigation tasks.
- **Need for Consistent Online Estimation:** Many robotic and autonomous applications require continuous, accurate, and consistent navigation data in the presence of varying sensor latencies. An online method that can reliably estimate and compensate for these delays is crucial for maintaining performance and ensuring system integrity.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out the following key objectives:
1. **Develop a Geometric Framework:** Introduce a novel mathematical framework for modeling time-delayed INS. This framework leverages Galilean symmetry to provide a joint representation of space and time, aiming to naturally couple temporal offsets with position and velocity states for consistent estimation.
2. **Derive an Equivariant Filter (EqF):** Based on the proposed Galilean geometric framework, derive an EqF specifically designed for the coupled estimation of navigation states (position, velocity, orientation) and unknown time delays in a GNSS-IMU system. The EqF approach is selected due to its demonstrated advantages in preserving the intrinsic geometry of the state space, leading to improved consistency and stability compared to traditional EKFs.
3. **Validate Performance with Real-World Data:** Conduct experimental validation using data from fixed-wing Uncrewed Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) equipped with GNSS sensors exhibiting realistic time lags (e.g., 90 ms and 120 ms). This objective aims to demonstrate the practical applicability and performance of the proposed EqF in real-world scenarios.
4. **Provide Statistical Comparison through Simulations:** Perform Monte Carlo simulations to statistically compare the proposed EqF against a state-of-the-art EKF with online time-delay estimation across a range of measurement delays (up to 500 ms). This objective seeks to quantitatively assess the accuracy, consistency (e.g., using Normalized Estimation Error Squared - NEES), and stability of the EqF, particularly in scenarios with larger delays where EKF performance is known to degrade.
5. **Address Inconsistency Issues:** Demonstrate that the developed EqF can overcome the inherent inconsistencies and instabilities observed in existing EKF-based online time-delay estimation methods, thereby offering a more reliable solution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on developing a geometric framework for INS using Galilean symmetry and subsequently deriving an Equivariant Filter (EqF) for joint state and time-delay estimation.

#### 3.1. Galilean Spatio-Temporal State Estimation Framework

The work introduces a geometric framework based on the Galilean matrix Lie group Gal(3) to model inertial motion with temporal offsets.
- **Galilean Transformations:** The paper defines a Galilean frame or transformation as a 5x5 homogeneous matrix that expresses coordinates between two inertial frames, incorporating relative orientation ($R \in SO(3)$), inertial velocity ($v \in \mathbb{R}^3$), position ($p \in \mathbb{R}^3$), and time offset ($t \in \mathbb{R}$). This structure inherently couples spatial and temporal relationships.
- **Modeling INS with Delay:**
 - **Intra-body Transformations:** Describes the transformation for the *same* rigid body at different times (e.g., from time $t-\delta$ to $t$). This formulation directly encodes the time delay $\delta$ within the Galilean frame.
 - **Cross-body Transformations:** Extends this concept to model the spatio-temporal relationship between *two different* rigid bodies (e.g., body A at time $t-\delta$ and body B at time $t$). The key idea is to filter for this cross-body Galilean frame, allowing for the joint estimation of spatial navigation states and the time delay $\delta$.
- **Kinematics of Galilean Frames:** Continuous-time kinematics for these frames are derived based on IMU measurements (angular velocity $\omega$, linear acceleration $a$) and a constant term $N$ that models time evolution ($\dot{t}=1$). The kinematics of cross-body frames depend on inputs at two different times, $t-\delta$ and $t$.
- **Application to Spatio-Temporal State Estimation:** The system's state is defined as the cross-body Galilean frame $F(t) \in Gal(3)$. An observer $\hat{F}$ is designed with group-affine kinematics that preserve the Lie group structure.
 - **IMU Preintegration:** To handle delayed measurements and bridge the time gap, the concept of IMU preintegration matrices, $\Gamma(\delta)$ and $\Upsilon(\delta)$, is adapted. These matrices encapsulate the integrated IMU measurements over the delay interval $\delta$. They are treated as known functions of the time delay and are efficiently managed using a sliding buffer of preintegrated IMU matrices, updated recursively and interpolated as needed to avoid recomputing from scratch.
 - **Residual Formulation:** A residual is constructed by comparing a delayed measurement (e.g., position $T_m(t-\delta)$) with its prediction derived from the current state estimate $\hat{F}$ and the inverse of the preintegration matrix $\Upsilon(\hat{\delta})^{-1}$. This residual drives the innovation term in the filter.

#### 3.2. Equivariant Filter for Delayed GNSS-INS

A discrete-time EqF is derived for a biased INS with delayed GNSS measurements, built upon the geometric framework.
- **System Formalization:** The system state is $\xi = (F, b) \in \mathcal{M} = Gal(3) \times \mathbb{R}^{10}$, where $F$ is the core Galilean state (including $\delta$) and $b$ represents IMU biases and virtual biases. The system kinematics are defined, incorporating Earth's rotation and gravity.
- **Lifted System and Error Dynamics:**
 - The EqF is derived from a "lifted system" state $X = (X_F, X_b) \in \mathcal{G}$, where $\mathcal{G}$ is a symmetry group (left-trivialized tangent group of Galilean group).
 - An "equivariant error" $e = \phi(\hat{X}^{-1}, \xi)$ is defined, which is intrinsically on the manifold $\mathcal{M}$ and offers favorable linearization properties.
 - Local parametrization $\varepsilon = \vartheta(e)$ is used to derive linearized error dynamics ($\varepsilon_{i+1} \approx A \varepsilon_i + B \eta_i$) for filter propagation.
- **Delayed Position Measurement Update:**
 - Delayed GNSS position measurements $y_m \in \mathbb{R}^3$ are modeled.
 - A measurement function $h(\xi)$ is defined to predict the position, using the estimated state and bias-corrected IMU preintegration.
 - A measurement residual $r = y_m - h(\phi(\hat{X}, \mathring{\xi}))$ is formed.
 - This residual is linearized ($r \approx C \varepsilon + D \mu$) to compute the Kalman gain $K$, which then updates the state estimate $\hat{X}$ and its covariance $\hat{\Sigma}$ via standard EqF update procedures, including a reset step.

#### 3.3. Experimental Validation

- **Real-World UAV Flights:** Data from two fixed-wing UAV platforms (striver and hero) across three flights were used. Ground truth trajectories were obtained from an onboard calibrated EKF fusing multiple sensors. The EqF was compared against three EKF baselines: a standard EKF without delay compensation, an EKF with offline-estimated fixed delay, and a state-of-the-art EKF with online delay estimation (following similar design principles for extended pose and preintegration).
- **Simulation Experiments:** Monte Carlo simulations (50 realizations) were performed with an analytically generated, dynamically rich trajectory. Synthetic IMU (200 Hz) and GNSS (20 Hz) data were generated with varying fixed delays (100 ms, 200 ms, 300 ms, 500 ms). The EqF was compared against the EKF with online delay estimation.
- **Performance Metrics:** Accuracy was assessed using Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) for Absolute Rotation Error (ARE), Absolute Velocity Error (AVE), Absolute Position Error (APE), and Absolute Delay Error (ADE). Filter consistency was evaluated using the Normalized Estimation Error Squared (NEES). The trajectories were designed to ensure sufficient excitation for delay observability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from both real-world UAV flights and Monte Carlo simulations, comparing the proposed Equivariant Filter (EqF) against various Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) baselines for Inertial Navigation Systems (INS) with unknown time delays.

#### 4.1. Real-World UAV Flights

Experiments were conducted using three flight sequences from two fixed-wing Uncrewed Aerial Vehicles (UAVs), with GNSS time lags of 90 ms and 120 ms.
- **Delay Estimation Performance:** The EqF demonstrated successful online tracking of unknown time delays across all three flight sequences. For the "striver" dataset (120 ms delay), the EqF exhibited rapid convergence to the reference delay, while the EKF with online delay estimation showed a slower transient and a less accurate asymptotic estimate. In the "hero-1" sequence (90 ms delay), the EqF displayed a minor residual bias (approximately 8 ms) compared to the nominal 90 ms reference, but this corresponded with superior performance across other navigation metrics, suggesting the EqF's estimate might have been more accurate than the offline reference in this instance. For "hero-2" (90 ms delay), both EqF and EKF (online) showed slower transients, but the EqF ultimately converged accurately to the 90 ms reference.
- **Navigation State Accuracy:**
 - All delay-compensating filters (EKF with offline delay, EKF with online delay, and EqF) consistently achieved significantly lower state errors (rotation, velocity, position) compared to the standard EKF without delay compensation. This highlights the importance of addressing measurement delays.
 - The EqF generally matched or exceeded the accuracy of the EKF with offline-estimated delay compensation, while operating fully online.
 - Compared to the EKF with online delay estimation, the EqF showed improved transient performance and comparable or better overall RMSE values for navigation states. For instance, in the "hero-1" dataset, the EqF achieved an Absolute Velocity Error (AVE) RMSE of 0.27 m/s, which was lower than all other tested EKFs.
- **Online Operation:** The results indicate that the proposed EqF is capable of reliably operating fully online under unknown GNSS time delays, obviating the need for tedious offline calibration procedures.

#### 4.2. Simulation Experiments

Monte Carlo simulations (50 realizations) were performed for measurement delays of 100 ms, 200 ms, 300 ms, and 500 ms, comparing the EqF against the EKF with online time-delay estimation.
- **Consistency:** A key distinction emerged regarding filter consistency, measured by Normalized Estimation Error Squared (NEES). Even for a small delay of 100 ms, the EKF exhibited elevated NEES values, indicating inconsistencies (i.e., the estimated covariance did not accurately reflect the true error distribution). In contrast, the EqF maintained consistent performance across all delay scenarios, with NEES values generally within expected statistical bounds.
- **Accuracy and Stability with Increasing Delays:**
 - As the measurement delay increased (200 ms, 300 ms, 500 ms), the EKF with online delay estimation showed a clear degradation in performance. It exhibited growing oscillations in estimation errors, increasing inconsistency, and larger overall estimation errors for rotation, velocity, and position.
 - In the 500 ms delay scenario, the EKF ultimately diverged, rendering its estimates unreliable.
 - Conversely, the EqF maintained stable, low-error, and consistent performance across all tested delays, including the 500 ms case, demonstrating full convergence without divergence.
- **Alignment with Prior Research:** The observed behavior of the EKF in simulations, particularly its inconsistency and instability with increasing delays, aligns with previous findings indicating fundamental structural issues when incorporating online time-delay estimation into classical recursive filters.

In summary, the EqF demonstrated superior consistency and robustness, particularly for larger and increasing measurement delays, where the EKF with online delay estimation exhibited significant instability and eventual divergence. The real-world experiments further supported the EqF's ability to provide accurate and online delay estimation, comparable to or surpassing offline-calibrated methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of Inertial Navigation Systems (INS) by addressing the persistent challenge of unknown measurement delays from sensors like GNSS in a novel and robust manner. The significance and potential impact of this work can be outlined as follows:

- **Advancement in Online Delay Estimation:** The paper introduces a geometrically principled approach for *consistent* online joint estimation of navigation states and unknown time delays. This is a notable advancement over previous EKF-based online delay estimation methods, which have been shown to suffer from inherent inconsistencies and instabilities, particularly with larger delays. By demonstrating stable and consistent performance across a range of delays, the proposed Equivariant Filter (EqF) provides a more reliable solution.
- **Leveraging Galilean Symmetry:** The work effectively leverages Galilean symmetry to construct a geometric framework that naturally couples spatial and temporal states. This intrinsic coupling within the Galilean group Gal(3) allows for a coherent representation of INS dynamics with time offsets, which forms the mathematical foundation for the EqF's improved performance. This demonstrates the practical utility of advanced Lie group theory in complex robotics and navigation problems.
- **Enhanced INS Accuracy and Consistency:** By accurately estimating and compensating for unknown delays online, the EqF significantly improves the overall accuracy of INS position, velocity, and orientation estimates compared to systems that neglect delays or rely on fixed, offline-calibrated delays. The consistent estimation, verified through NEES analysis in simulations, ensures that the filter's uncertainty estimates are reliable, which is critical for safety-critical applications.
- **Reduced Operational Complexity:** The ability of the EqF to estimate delays online removes the requirement for tedious and time-consuming offline calibration procedures or manual tuning to account for sensor latencies. This simplifies system deployment and maintenance, particularly in dynamic environments where sensor characteristics or communication latencies may vary over time.
- **Broader Applicability for Autonomous Systems:** Unknown and variable delays are common in commercial-grade GNSS receivers and other sensors used in robotics, Uncrewed Aerial Vehicles (UAVs), automotive, and marine applications. This work provides a practical and robust method for these systems to operate with greater reliability and precision, even when utilizing lower-cost or less perfectly synchronized sensors. This could expand the domains where high-performance navigation is feasible.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The developed geometric framework and EqF can serve as a foundation for further research in spatio-temporal state estimation. This could include extending the framework to handle multiple delayed sensors, dynamically varying delays, or exploring its application in other areas of robotics where temporal synchronization is critical. The robust handling of IMU preintegration within the Galilean framework also offers avenues for further improvements in inertial measurement processing.

In essence, this research offers a theoretically sound and empirically validated method to mitigate a critical performance bottleneck in INS, enabling more accurate, consistent, and practical navigation for a wide array of autonomous technologies.
