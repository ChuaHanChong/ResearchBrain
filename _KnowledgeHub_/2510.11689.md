# Phys2Real: Fusing VLM Priors with Interactive Online Adaptation for Uncertainty-Aware Sim-to-Real Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Phys2Real, developed by researchers at Stanford and Princeton, presents a framework for robotic manipulation that fuses vision-language model (VLM) inferred physical priors with online interactive adaptation through an uncertainty-aware mechanism. This approach demonstrates improved success rates and efficiency in challenging sim-to-real tasks requiring adaptation to varying object physical properties.

## Problem

- The "sim-to-real gap" presents a fundamental challenge in transferring robotic policies learned in simulation to real-world environments, particularly for tasks requiring precise dynamics.
- Existing methods like Domain Randomization (DR) often lead to averaged behaviors that struggle with out-of-distribution physical properties, while online adaptation methods can fail in manipulation tasks with intermittent contacts due to uninformative interaction histories.
- Robots need to adapt to diverse and uncertain physical properties of objects (e.g., friction, mass distribution) for successful manipulation, a capability often lacking in current systems.

## Method

- The framework utilizes a three-stage ==real-to-sim-to-real pipeline== that includes high-fidelity 3D geometric reconstruction, physics-conditioned policy learning, and uncertainty-aware sim-to-real transfer.
- Deep reinforcement learning policies are trained in simulation, explicitly conditioned on interpretable physical parameters (e.g., center of mass, friction) and optionally fine-tuned with noisy parameters for enhanced robustness.
- During real-world deployment, physical parameter estimates from VLMs and an ensemble of online adaptation models are combined using an ==inverse-variance weighting== mechanism, which dynamically prioritizes estimates with lower uncertainty.

## Results

- In the T-block pushing task with weight at the bottom, Phys2Real achieved a **100%** success rate, outperforming Domain Randomization (**79.17%**) and RMA-only (**79.17%**) baselines.
- For the more challenging T-block pushing task with weight at the top, Phys2Real achieved a **57.14%** success rate, significantly higher than DR (**23.81%**), VLM-only (**4.76%**), and RMA-only (**14.29%**).
- In the hammer pushing task, Phys2Real completed the task **14.2%** faster on average (77.79 ± 44.08 s) compared to the Domain Randomization baseline (90.65 ± 42.03 s), indicating more efficient manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The uncertainty-aware fusion of VLM-inferred physical priors with interaction-based online adaptation is crucial for robust sim-to-real transfer in dynamic manipulation tasks, effectively mitigating limitations of each source alone.
> - Conditioning RL policies on interpretable physical parameters rather than abstract latents enables direct integration of VLM knowledge and improves the policy's adaptability to varying object properties.
> - The proposed method can achieve performance comparable to policies with privileged ground-truth physical information by dynamically combining broad initial visual estimates with precise, context-specific interaction feedback.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation faces a fundamental challenge in transferring policies learned in simulation to real-world environments, often referred to as the "sim-to-real gap." This gap is particularly pronounced in tasks requiring precise dynamics, where factors like friction, mass distribution, and compliance of objects are difficult to model accurately.

Current approaches to address the sim-to-real gap include:
- **Domain Randomization (DR)**: This method involves training policies across a randomized range of simulation parameters to encourage robustness to real-world variations. While effective in some scenarios, DR policies may generalize poorly to out-of-distribution object properties and often default to averaged behaviors, sacrificing performance for broad robustness.
- **System Identification**: Explicitly calibrating simulation parameters to match real-world observations. These methods often require manual tuning and result in static models that lack adaptability to changing deployment conditions.
- **Online Policy Adaptation**: Techniques like Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA) train policies conditioned on environment parameters and then use an adaptation model to infer these properties from interaction history at runtime. While successful in contact-rich tasks like locomotion, RMA can struggle in general manipulation settings due to intermittent contacts leading to uninformative histories.
- **Digital Twin Simulations and Photorealistic Rendering**: Using methods like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (GSplat) to create high-fidelity 3D scene reconstructions for simulation. However, these often focus on visual fidelity and typically rely on conventional physics engines with default or manually specified parameters that may not accurately reflect real-world physics. Some recent efforts use vision-language models (VLMs) to initialize simulation parameters but often do not update them online during closed-loop control.
- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for Physical Reasoning**: VLMs have demonstrated capabilities in physical reasoning from images, such as estimating material properties, weight, and fragility. However, this has primarily been applied to high-level planning rather than directly integrated into low-level, real-time control policies for precise manipulation.

Phys2Real positions itself at the intersection of these areas. It aims to overcome the limitations of existing methods by combining high-fidelity 3D reconstruction with VLM-based physical parameter estimation and real-time interactive adaptation. Specifically, it extends online policy adaptation methods by conditioning policies on interpretable physical parameters rather than learned latents, and by fusing VLM priors with interaction-based estimates through an uncertainty-aware mechanism. This integration addresses the problem of uninformative interaction histories and aims to achieve both robustness to broad parameter ranges and adaptation to specific object properties for enhanced manipulation performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve robotic manipulation performance in real-world environments by bridging the sim-to-real gap, particularly for tasks requiring precise dynamics that are sensitive to object physical properties. The central question motivating this work is: "Can combining visual physical reasoning with interactive learning improve robot manipulation performance in real-world environments after training in simulation?"

This objective is driven by several motivations:
1. **Cost and Time of Real-World Learning**: Training robotic manipulation policies directly in the real world is typically expensive and time-consuming. Simulation offers a scalable alternative, but effective transfer remains a challenge.
2. **Limitations of Domain Randomization (DR)**: While DR makes policies robust, it often leads to averaged behaviors that may not be optimal for specific objects or conditions, especially for out-of-distribution physical properties. The goal is to achieve both robustness and performance adaptation.
3. **Need for Adaptation to Varying Object Properties**: Robots frequently encounter objects with diverse and uncertain physical properties (e.g., friction, mass distribution, compliance). Policies need to adapt to these variations for successful manipulation.
4. **Inspiration from Human Cognition**: Humans combine visual perception with interactive exploration to form initial judgments about an object's physical properties and then refine these estimates through interaction. This integration allows for adaptation to novel objects without extensive prior experience. The research seeks to provide robots with a similar capability.
5. **Addressing Challenges in Online Adaptation (RMA)**: Existing online adaptation methods like RMA can struggle in manipulation tasks characterized by intermittent contact, as these scenarios often yield uninformative interaction histories, leading to poor parameter estimates. Integrating visual priors is intended to mitigate this issue.
6. **Enhancing Digital Twins with Physical Information**: While digital twin reconstructions from techniques like NeRF and GSplat offer high visual fidelity, they often lack accurate physical properties. The motivation is to create "physically-informed digital twins" that incorporate both geometric and physical information to improve sim-to-real transfer.
7. **Leveraging VLMs for Low-Level Control**: VLMs have shown promise in physical reasoning, but their application has largely been confined to high-level planning. A key motivation is to demonstrate their utility in providing interpretable, uncertainty-calibrated physical estimates for real-time, low-level closed-loop control.
8. **Enabling Uncertainty-Aware Fusion**: There is a need for methods that can effectively combine information from different sources (vision and interaction) while accounting for their respective uncertainties. This motivates the development of an ensemble-based uncertainty quantification and an inverse-variance weighting fusion mechanism.

In summary, the key objective is to develop a framework, Phys2Real, that combines VLM-inferred physical priors with interaction-based online adaptation through uncertainty-aware fusion to create adaptive, high-performing robotic manipulation policies that can generalize to novel objects and challenging dynamics in the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Phys2Real employs a three-stage real-to-sim-to-real pipeline for robotic manipulation, integrating geometric reconstruction, physics-conditioned policy learning, and uncertainty-aware sim-to-real transfer.

**A. Real-to-Sim Scene Reconstruction**
For objects without pre-existing simulation-ready meshes, the pipeline reconstructs high-fidelity geometric assets from real-world observations:
1. **Image Capture and Segmentation**: A video of the target object is captured, and individual frames are extracted. The target object in these frames is then segmented using SAM-2 (Segment Anything Model 2).
2. **3D Gaussian Splatting (GSplat) Training**: A 3D Gaussian Splatting model is trained on the segmented object foreground images to create a dense 3D representation.
3. **Mesh Extraction**: Surface-Aligned Gaussian Splatting (SuGaR) is used to extract an object-centric mesh from the trained GSplat.
4. **Watertight Mesh Generation**: For objects with approximate symmetry, the mesh is mirrored across its primary axis of symmetry. The Marching Cubes algorithm is then applied to generate a clean, watertight mesh suitable for simulation environments.

**B. Physics-Conditioned Policy Learning**
Deep reinforcement learning (RL) policies are trained in simulation. Unlike standard domain randomization, these policies are explicitly conditioned on interpretable physical parameter values (e.g., center of mass, friction) rather than learned latent representations. The training procedure consists of three phases, inspired by Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA):

1. **Phase 1: Policy Training with Ground Truth Parameters**: The RL policy is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in IsaacLab. The actor network is conditioned on object pose, robot end-effector position, and ground-truth physical properties available in simulation. This phase enables the policy to learn optimal behaviors across different physical configurations. The critic receives privileged observations including object velocity and pose.
2. **Phase 1.5: Policy Fine-tuning with Noisy Parameters (Optional)**: To enhance robustness against potentially inaccurate parameter estimates during real-world deployment, the policy is fine-tuned by adding random Gaussian noise to each physical parameter during training episodes. This prepares the policy to handle the noisy estimates from the downstream VLM and adaptation module.
3. **Phase 2: Adaptation Model Training with Ensemble-based Uncertainty Quantification**: After freezing the policy weights, an ensemble of M=10 neural network adaptation models is trained. Each model learns to predict physical parameters from a history of observations and actions over a sliding window of H=10. This phase enables online adaptation during deployment.
 - **Epistemic Uncertainty**: The variance of the predictions across the M ensemble members (σ_epistemic²) quantifies the model's uncertainty due to limited training data or model disagreement.
 - **Aleatoric Uncertainty**: Each ensemble member is trained to output both a mean (μ_i) and variance (σ_i²) for its prediction, using a Gaussian negative log-likelihood loss. The mean of these variances across the ensemble (σ_aleatoric²) represents irreducible noise in the observations or environment.
 - **Total RMA Uncertainty**: The total uncertainty (σ_rma²) is the sum of epistemic and aleatoric uncertainties.

**C. Sim-to-Real Transfer with Physical Parameter Estimation**
During real-world deployment, Phys2Real combines physical parameter estimates from two sources: Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and the online adaptation models, using an uncertainty-aware fusion mechanism.

1. **Obtaining VLM Estimates (θ_vlm and σ_vlm)**:
 - A VLM (GPT-5) is queried to estimate task-relevant physical parameters (e.g., CoM along a vertical axis) from images of the object.
 - Multiple images (V) from different viewpoints are captured, and for each image, the VLM is queried multiple times (Q) using a specific prompt that requests a numerical estimate and an uncertainty (sigma).
 - The VLM estimate (θ_vlm) is the aggregate mean of all VLM predictions across images and queries.
 - The VLM uncertainty (σ_vlm) is calculated as the mean of the VLM's self-reported uncertainty estimates, as empirical tests suggested this was more reliable than the standard deviation of the estimates themselves.

2. **Obtaining RMA Estimates (θ_rma and σ_rma)**:
 - At each timestep, a sequence of recent observations and actions from the real-world interaction history is fed into the ensemble of M adaptation models.
 - The ensemble mean of the individual predictions (θ_rma) provides the current physical parameter estimate.
 - The total RMA uncertainty (σ_rma) is calculated as described in Phase 2, combining both epistemic and aleatoric components.

3. **Fusing Estimates with Inverse-Variance Weighting**:
 - The VLM estimate (θ_vlm, σ_vlm) and the RMA estimate (θ_rma, σ_rma) are fused using inverse-variance weighting. This mechanism assigns greater weight to the estimate with lower uncertainty.
 - The fused estimate (ˆθ) is computed as: ˆθ = (θ_vlm/σ_vlm² + θ_rma/σ_rma²) / (1/σ_vlm² + 1/σ_rma²).
 - This fused estimate (ˆθ) is then used to condition the RL policy in real-time. This approach is theoretically justified as the Best Linear Unbiased Estimator (BLUE) under assumptions of unbiased estimates with independent noise and known variances.

The methodology is designed to provide robust, online adaptation by leveraging both the broad reasoning capabilities of VLMs as initial priors and the precise, context-specific refinement offered by real-world interaction, all while explicitly accounting for uncertainty in both sources.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates Phys2Real on two non-prehensile manipulation tasks: T-block pushing with varying center of mass (CoM) and hammer pushing with an off-center mass distribution. Performance is measured by success rate, final position error, final orientation error, and task completion time.

**A. T-block Pushing Task**
This task evaluates adaptation to varying CoMs by placing a metal weight at either the top or bottom of a 3D-printed T-block. The T-block mesh is known, allowing for isolation of physical parameter estimation effects.

1. **Weight at the Bottom Configuration (Easier)**:
 - **Success Rate**: Phys2Real achieved a 100% success rate, outperforming all baselines including Domain Randomization (DR) (79.17%), RMA-only (79.17%), and a policy conditioned only on VLM estimates (91.67%). It also matched the performance of the physics-conditioned policy with privileged ground-truth CoM information (95.83%).
 - **Position and Orientation Error**: Phys2Real demonstrated the lowest position error (1.76 ± 0.54 cm) and comparable orientation error, indicating precise manipulation. The cumulative distribution function (CDF) of position errors showed Phys2Real’s curve rising sharply and staying to the left, indicating consistently low position error across nearly all trials.
 - **Conclusion**: Phys2Real can match the performance of a privileged oracle by fusing vision-based priors and interactive adaptation, demonstrating its ability to accurately estimate and adapt to CoM in a less challenging scenario.

2. **Weight at the Top Configuration (More Challenging)**:
 - **Success Rate**: In this more difficult scenario, Phys2Real achieved a 57.14% success rate, significantly outperforming DR (23.81%), VLM-only (4.76%), RMA-only (14.29%), and Diffusion Policy (20.83%). The privileged ground-truth policy achieved the highest success at 90.48%.
 - **Position and Orientation Error**: Phys2Real also showed lower position (2.60 ± 0.90 cm) and orientation (2.62 ± 1.73 deg) errors compared to most baselines.
 - **Ablation Studies**: The substantial performance drop for VLM-only and RMA-only policies (4.76% and 14.29% success, respectively) highlighted that neither visual estimates nor interactive adaptation alone were sufficient for success in this challenging task. The combined uncertainty-aware fusion mechanism of Phys2Real was critical.
 - **Dynamic Parameter Estimation**: A representative rollout for the "weight at top" case illustrated how the initial uncertain VLM CoM estimate (4.0 cm, ground truth 6.1 cm) was refined. As the robot interacted, the uncertainty of the adaptation models decreased, and the fused estimate converged toward the ground truth. Uncertainty rose again when contact ended, demonstrating the interplay of the two information sources.
 - **Robustness to Inaccurate VLM Priors (Appendix I)**: Ablations showed Phys2Real maintained consistent success rates (e.g., 8-10 successes out of 12 trials) even when VLM CoM estimates deviated significantly from ground truth (from +6.0 cm to -2.0 cm). In contrast, a Phase 1.5 policy trained only with noisy parameters suffered substantial degradation with less accurate VLM priors.

**B. Hammer Pushing Task**
This task involved an object with an off-center mass distribution, using a hammer whose mesh was generated via the real-to-sim reconstruction pipeline.

1. **Success Rate**: Both Phys2Real and the DR baseline achieved a 100% success rate across 15 trials. This indicated that the task was solvable by both methods given sufficient training.
2. **Task Completion Time**: Phys2Real completed the task 14.2% faster on average (77.79 ± 44.08 s) compared to DR (90.65 ± 42.03 s). This suggests that Phys2Real produced more efficient manipulation trajectories, despite similar final position and orientation errors.
3. **Conclusion**: Phys2Real's real-to-sim pipeline successfully generated a usable asset, and the overall framework enabled more efficient manipulation of a real-world object compared to domain randomization.

In summary, the main findings indicate that Phys2Real consistently outperforms domain randomization and ablation baselines, particularly in challenging manipulation scenarios requiring adaptation to object physical properties. The uncertainty-aware fusion of VLM-inferred priors and interaction-based estimates is demonstrated to be essential for achieving high success rates and efficient task completion. Phys2Real can achieve performance comparable to a policy with privileged ground-truth physical information without requiring such information at test time.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in Phys2Real contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by integrating several advanced techniques to address the long-standing sim-to-real gap and the challenge of adapting to novel object properties.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

1. **Uncertainty-Aware Fusion for Robust Adaptation**: Phys2Real introduces a method for fusing vision-language model (VLM) priors with online, interaction-based physical parameter estimates through an uncertainty-aware mechanism. This is significant because it provides a principled way to combine diverse information sources, leveraging the strengths of each (VLM for initial estimates, interaction for refinement) while accounting for their respective reliability. This approach helps overcome limitations of prior adaptation methods that struggle with intermittent contact or lack external priors.
2. **VLMs for Low-Level Control**: The research demonstrates a novel application of VLMs beyond high-level planning. By extracting interpretable, uncertainty-calibrated physical parameters (like Center of Mass) from VLMs and directly incorporating them into low-level closed-loop control policies, Phys2Real extends the utility of foundation models for precise robotic manipulation tasks.
3. **Physically-Informed Digital Twins**: The pipeline combines high-fidelity geometric reconstruction using 3D Gaussian Splatting with online physical property estimation. This enables the creation of "physically-informed digital twins" that go beyond visual fidelity to include accurate dynamic characteristics, which is crucial for robust sim-to-real transfer.
4. **Improved Performance in Challenging Tasks**: The experimental results show consistent performance gains over established baselines like Domain Randomization and Diffusion Policy, especially in tasks with complex dynamics (e.g., pushing a T-block with a high center of mass). Phys2Real often approaches the performance of policies with privileged ground-truth physical information, demonstrating the practical efficacy of its approach.
5. **Interpretability of Policy Conditioning**: By conditioning policies on physically interpretable parameters rather than abstract learned latents, the system's behavior becomes more understandable and potentially easier to diagnose or improve. This also facilitates the direct fusion with VLM estimates.

**Potential Impact:**

- **More General and Adaptive Robotic Systems**: Phys2Real offers a pathway towards robotic systems that can adapt to a wider variety of novel objects and environments without extensive manual tuning or object-specific training. This could accelerate the deployment of robots in unstructured environments like logistics, domestic assistance, or hazardous operations.
- **Reduced Sim-to-Real Gap**: By systematically addressing both geometric and physical discrepancies between simulation and reality, the framework contributes to narrowing the sim-to-real gap, making RL policies trained in simulation more reliably transferable to real-world robots.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction**: Robots that can infer and adapt to physical properties of objects could interact more safely and effectively with unknown items, potentially leading to more intuitive and capable human-robot collaboration.
- **Advancement in Foundation Models for Robotics**: This work highlights the potential of integrating large foundation models with physical interaction, suggesting future directions where semantic knowledge from VLMs can be grounded and refined through real-world experience, moving towards Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.
- **Future Research Directions**: The paper opens avenues for future research in multi-parameter uncertainty-aware estimation (e.g., friction, mass, stiffness), improving VLM uncertainty calibration, and generalizing to a broader range of object geometries (asymmetric, deformable) and contact-rich or impulsive tasks.

Overall, Phys2Real represents an advancement in adaptive control for robotic manipulation by effectively leveraging and fusing perception-based priors from foundation models with data-driven interactive refinement, providing a robust framework for handling physical uncertainties in real-world scenarios.
