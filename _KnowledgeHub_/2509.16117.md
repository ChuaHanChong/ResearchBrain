# DiffusionNFT: Online Diffusion Reinforcement with Forward Process

> [!summary] Summary
> DiffusionNFT introduces an online reinforcement learning framework for diffusion models, optimizing the forward (noising) process via a flow matching objective and negative-aware fine-tuning. This method achieves 3x to 25x greater efficiency and superior performance compared to existing approaches, notably generating high-quality images without Classifier-Free Guidance and outperforming larger, CFG-based models in multi-reward settings.

## Problem

- Applying online reinforcement learning to diffusion models is challenging due to intractable likelihoods required by conventional policy gradient algorithms.
- Existing methods that discretize the reverse (denoising) process for RL lead to 'forward inconsistency,' solver restrictions, and complex integration of Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG).
- Computational inefficiency and memory-intensive data collection limit the scalability and practical application of current diffusion RL techniques.

## Method

- **DiffusionNFT** performs online reinforcement learning directly on the ==forward diffusion process==, optimizing the model's velocity predictor through a flow matching objective.
- It transforms raw rewards into 'optimality probabilities' to implicitly categorize data into positive and negative samples, guiding policy improvement through a 'Negative-aware FineTuning' (NFT) loss.
- The approach decouples sampling and training, allowing the use of arbitrary black-box solvers for data collection, requires only clean images for optimization, and inherently operates without Classifier-Free Guidance.

## Results

- DiffusionNFT demonstrates **3x** to **25x** greater efficiency and superior performance over FlowGRPO, achieving a GenEval score of **0.98** within 1,000 training steps (approx. 100 GPU hours) while being CFG-free.
- When jointly optimized on multiple reward models, the CFG-free DiffusionNFT model outperforms larger, CFG-based models like SD3.5-L (**8B** parameters) and FLUX.1-Dev (**12B** parameters) on various benchmarks (e.g., GenEval, PickScore, HPSv2.1).
- Ablation studies confirm the necessity of the negative loss component to prevent online training collapse, the benefits of ODE samplers for data quality, and the importance of adaptive loss weighting and soft policy updates for stable and fast convergence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing the forward diffusion process, rather than the reverse, offers a principled way to apply RL to diffusion models, bypassing issues of likelihood intractability and maintaining consistency.
> - Reinforcement signals can be effectively integrated into a supervised learning objective by implicitly defining and contrasting 'positive' and 'negative' velocity predictors based on reward-transformed data.
> - Decoupling the sampling and training policies with soft updates and utilizing advanced ODE samplers for data collection significantly enhances stability and efficiency in online diffusion RL.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "DiffusionNFT" is situated at the intersection of online reinforcement learning (RL) and generative models, specifically diffusion models, aiming to address critical challenges in aligning these powerful models with human preferences or specific objectives.

**The Rise of RL in Generative AI:**
Online Reinforcement Learning has proven transformative in the post-training of large language models (LLMs), leading to significant advancements in their alignment with human values, reasoning abilities, and overall utility (e.g., GPT-4, DeepSeek-R1). This success has naturally spurred interest in extending similar paradigms to other modalities, particularly visual generation with diffusion models.

**Challenges in Applying RL to Diffusion Models:**
Despite the success in LLMs, directly applying online RL to diffusion models presents unique challenges:
1. **Intractable Likelihoods:** Diffusion models, unlike autoregressive LLMs, often have intractable likelihoods, making it difficult to apply conventional policy gradient algorithms (e.g., PPO, GRPO) that rely on computing exact model likelihoods.
2. **Reverse Process Focus:** Existing works often discretize the reverse (denoising) sampling process to formulate diffusion generation as a multi-step Markov Decision Process (MDP). While this enables GRPO-style training, it introduces several limitations:
 - **Forward Inconsistency:** Focusing solely on the reverse process can break adherence to the forward diffusion process, potentially leading to model degradation.
 - **Solver Restrictions:** These methods typically rely on first-order SDE samplers, precluding the use of more efficient ODE or high-order solvers common in flow models.
 - **Complicated CFG Integration:** Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), crucial for high-quality conditional generation, requires training both conditional and unconditional models, making RL integration complex and inefficient.
3. **Data Collection and Storage:** Many approaches require storing entire sampling trajectories for policy optimization, which is memory-intensive.

**Existing Diffusion RL Approaches and Their Limitations:**
The paper categorizes existing diffusion RL methods into:
- **Likelihood-free methods:** These include Reward Backpropagation (limited to differentiable rewards, memory-intensive), Reward-Weighted Regression (offline, lacks negative objective), Policy Guidance (combines multiple models, complicates online optimization), and Score-based RL (direct score optimization, still developing).
- **Likelihood-based methods:** Diffusion-DPO (adapts DPO for paired preference data, requires likelihood and loss approximations), and Policy Gradient methods like PPO/GRPO (discretize reverse process, but face forward inconsistency, solver coupling, and efficiency bottlenecks). MixGRPO improves efficiency but issues remain.

**DiffusionNFT's Position in the Landscape:**
DiffusionNFT directly addresses these fundamental limitations by proposing a radical departure: performing RL on the **forward diffusion process** via **flow matching**. This novel approach offers several benefits:
- **Likelihood-Free Formulation:** Bypasses the intractable likelihood problem entirely.
- **Solver Flexibility:** Decouples training from sampling, allowing the use of any black-box solver.
- **Forward Consistency:** Maintains adherence to the underlying forward diffusion process.
- **CFG-Free Operation:** Integrates reinforcement signals directly, negating the need for separate conditional/unconditional models.
- **Efficiency:** Achieves significant computational efficiency gains compared to GRPO-style methods.

By redefining the online RL paradigm for diffusion models, DiffusionNFT positions itself as a robust, efficient, and theoretically principled alternative that could unify supervised learning and reinforcement learning within the diffusion framework, offering a generalized recipe for off-policy RL across modalities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate a novel online reinforcement learning paradigm for diffusion models, specifically designed to overcome the inherent limitations and inefficiencies of current approaches. The authors aim to enable robust and efficient post-training of diffusion models to align with various reward signals and human preferences.

The key motivations driving this work stem from the shortcomings of existing diffusion reinforcement learning methods, particularly those based on the conventional policy gradient framework:

1. **Addressing Intractable Likelihoods:** Traditional policy gradient algorithms fundamentally rely on computing model likelihoods, which are notoriously difficult and costly to approximate for diffusion models. This barrier limits the direct application of successful RL techniques from autoregressive models (like LLMs) to the continuous domain of diffusion models.

2. **Overcoming "Forward Inconsistency" in Reverse-Process RL:** Existing GRPO-style methods discretize the *reverse* sampling process, reformulating diffusion generation as a multi-step MDP. While this enables policy gradient application, it risks breaking the fundamental consistency with the *forward* diffusion process, potentially leading to degenerate models. The authors seek an RL formulation that intrinsically respects the forward noising process.

3. **Removing Solver Restrictions:** Reverse-process RL methods often couple policy training with first-order SDE samplers, restricting the utilization of more advanced, efficient, and higher-order ODE solvers. A key motivation is to decouple data collection and policy optimization, allowing for arbitrary "black-box" solvers during sampling.

4. **Simplifying Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) Integration:** CFG is a standard technique in diffusion models for enhancing generation quality but complicates post-training due to the need for separate conditional and unconditional models. The authors aim to develop an RL approach that naturally integrates reinforcement signals without this overhead, ideally leading to a "CFG-free" yet high-performing model.

5. **Improving Efficiency and Scalability:** The computational cost of existing diffusion RL methods, especially those requiring storage of full sampling trajectories or extensive likelihood approximations, is a significant bottleneck. A core motivation is to develop a substantially more efficient algorithm that requires only clean images for policy optimization.

6. **Developing a Unified Off-Policy RL Recipe:** Beyond diffusion models, the authors are motivated to explore a general, unified, and native off-policy RL recipe grounded in supervised learning principles, which could be applicable across various modalities. DiffusionNFT is presented as a step towards this broader vision, leveraging the "Negative-aware FineTuning" (NFT) paradigm previously explored in language models.

In essence, DiffusionNFT seeks to disentangle data collection, remove solver restrictions, maintain consistency with supervised pretraining, and enhance the efficiency and performance of online reinforcement learning for diffusion models by shifting the focus from the complex reverse process to the more amenable forward process through a novel "negative-aware" supervised learning objective.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DiffusionNFT introduces a novel online Reinforcement Learning (RL) paradigm that fundamentally shifts the focus from optimizing the reverse (denoising) process, as in traditional policy gradient methods, to optimizing the **forward (noising) process** of diffusion models. This is achieved through a "Negative-aware FineTuning" (NFT) approach using a flow matching objective.

**Core Principle: Forward-Process RL via Flow Matching**
Instead of formulating diffusion generation as a multi-step MDP on the reverse process, DiffusionNFT directly optimizes the diffusion model's velocity predictor, $v_\theta(x_t, t)$, which defines the tangent of the data trajectory in the forward process. This optimization is framed as a supervised learning problem using a flow matching objective, maintaining consistency with how diffusion models are typically pretrained.

**Problem Setup and Optimality Reward:**
1. **Online RL:** The process starts with a pretrained diffusion policy ($v_{old}$) and a dataset of prompts ($c$).
2. **Data Collection (Rollout Step):** In each iteration, $K$ images ($x_{0}$) are sampled for a given prompt $c$ using the current sampling policy ($v_{old}$).
3. **Reward Evaluation:** Each generated image $x_{0}$ is evaluated by a scalar reward function $r_{raw} \in \mathbb{R}$.
4. **Optimality Probability Transformation:** Raw rewards $r_{raw}$ are transformed into an "optimality probability" $r \in$ using a group-wise normalization and clipping mechanism: $r(x_0, c) := 0.5 + 0.5 \cdot \text{clip}\{(r_{raw} - \mathbb{E}_{\pi_{old}(\cdot|c)}[r_{raw}]) / Z_c, -1, 1\}$. This creates an implicit binary partition, splitting collected data into "positive" ($D^+$) and "negative" ($D^-$) samples based on their reward.

**Reinforcement Guidance and Policy Improvement:**
The central idea is to define an implicit policy improvement direction by contrasting positive and negative generations.
1. **Implicit Policies:** The method defines three conceptual policies: $\pi_{old}$ (the current sampling policy), $\pi_+$ (a "positive" policy biased towards high-reward samples), and $\pi_-$ (a "negative" policy biased towards low-reward samples).
2. **Improvement Direction ($\Delta$):** Theorem 3.1 establishes that the directional differences between the velocity predictors ($v_{old}$, $v_+$, $v_-$) corresponding to these policies are proportional. Specifically, an "reinforcement guidance" vector $\Delta$ is defined such that it points from the negative policy towards the positive policy, relative to the old policy. This $\Delta$ represents the desired direction for policy improvement.
 $\Delta := [1 - \alpha(x_t)][v_{old}(x_t, c, t) - v_-(x_t, c, t)] = \alpha(x_t)[v_+(x_t, c, t) - v_{old}(x_t, c, t)]$
 where $\alpha(x_t)$ is a scalar coefficient.

**Negative-aware Policy Optimization (Theorem 3.2):**
Instead of explicitly learning $\Delta$ or $v_+$ and $v_-$, DiffusionNFT uses an implicit parameterization technique within a single supervised learning objective:
1. **Implicit Policies for Optimization:** The training policy $v_\theta$ is used to implicitly define a "positive" velocity $v_\theta^+$ and a "negative" velocity $v_\theta^-$:
 - $v_\theta^+(x_t, c, t) := (1-\beta)v_{old}(x_t, c, t) + \beta v_\theta(x_t, c, t)$
 - $v_\theta^-(x_t, c, t) := (1+\beta)v_{old}(x_t, c, t) - \beta v_\theta(x_t, c, t)$
 where $\beta$ is a guidance strength hyperparameter.
2. **Loss Function:** The model $v_\theta$ is optimized by minimizing a combined supervised learning objective:
 $L(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{c, \pi_{old}(x_0|c), t} [r \|v_\theta^+(x_t, c, t) - v\|^2_2 + (1-r) \|v_\theta^-(x_t, c, t) - v\|^2_2]$
 where $v$ is the ground-truth target velocity derived from the forward process ($v = \dot{\alpha}_t x_0 + \dot{\sigma}_t \epsilon$).
3. **Optimal Solution:** Theorem 3.2 proves that, with unlimited data and model capacity, the optimal solution for $v_\theta^*$ in this objective is $v_\theta^*(x_t, c, t) = v_{old}(x_t, c, t) + \frac{2}{\beta}\Delta(x_t, c, t)$. This means the training policy $v_\theta$ is directly pushed towards an improved policy guided by the $\Delta$ direction, leveraging both positive and negative signals.

**Practical Implementation Details (Algorithm 1):**
- **Decoupled Policies:** The sampling policy ($v_{old}$) and the training policy ($v_\theta$) are decoupled. $v_{old}$ is initialized from a reference model and then softly updated using an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) from $v_\theta$ (i.e., $\theta_{old} \leftarrow \eta_i \theta_{old} + (1-\eta_i)\theta$). This off-policy nature improves stability and allows for continuous online learning.
- **Solver Flexibility:** Data collection uses arbitrary black-box solvers (e.g., 2nd-order ODE samplers) on the sampling policy $v_{old}$. Only clean images $x_0$ and their rewards are needed for training, not full trajectories.
- **Adaptive Loss Weighting:** Instead of a fixed time-dependent weighting $w(t)$ in the flow matching loss, an adaptive self-normalized $x_0$-regression loss is used, which dynamically adjusts weights based on prediction error.
- **CFG-Free Optimization:** The model is trained without Classifier-Free Guidance. The initial policy is conditional, and the reinforcement learning process inherently learns the preference alignment, effectively substituting CFG's role.

This methodology provides a robust, efficient, and theoretically grounded way to perform online RL for diffusion models by leveraging the simplicity and consistency of the forward diffusion process and flow matching, while intelligently incorporating positive and negative feedback.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DiffusionNFT demonstrates significant improvements in efficiency, performance, and flexibility for online reinforcement learning of diffusion models, particularly in comparison to existing GRPO-style methods and CFG baselines.

**1. Exceptional Efficiency and Performance vs. FlowGRPO:**
- **Head-to-head Comparison:** DiffusionNFT consistently outperforms FlowGRPO in terms of training efficiency and final performance across various single-reward tasks (GenEval, OCR, PickScore, HPSv2.1).
- **Quantified Efficiency:** DiffusionNFT is **3x to 25x more efficient** than FlowGRPO. For example, it improves the GenEval score from a CFG-free baseline of 0.24 to **0.98 within 1,000 training steps (around 100 GPU hours)**. In contrast, FlowGRPO achieves 0.95 (a slightly lower score) but requires over 5,000 steps and the employment of CFG during inference.
- **CFG-Free Advantage:** A key finding is that DiffusionNFT achieves superior results while being entirely **CFG-free**, demonstrating its ability to learn strong conditional generation without the need for separate unconditional models or guidance at inference time.

**2. Superior Multi-Reward Joint Training:**
- **Comprehensive Enhancement:** When jointly optimized on multiple reward models (GenEval, OCR, PickScore, ClipScore, HPSv2.1), DiffusionNFT significantly boosts the performance of the base SD3.5-Medium model across all tested in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks.
- **Outperforming Strong Baselines:** The final CFG-free DiffusionNFT model not only surpasses the performance of the CFG-enabled SD3.5-Medium baseline but also **outperforms larger, CFG-based models** like SD3.5-L (8B parameters) and FLUX.1-Dev (12B parameters) on metrics such as GenEval, PickScore, HPSv2.1, Aesthetic, ImageReward, and UnifiedReward. This highlights its effectiveness in learning complex preferences and generalizing them.
- **Qualitative Improvements:** Qualitative comparisons (Figures 5, 11-13) visually confirm the superior image quality and alignment with prompts generated by DiffusionNFT compared to FlowGRPO and base models.

**3. Ablation Studies Confirming Design Choices:**
The ablation studies provide crucial insights into the importance of DiffusionNFT's core components:
- **Necessity of Negative Loss:** The "negative-aware" component is critical. Training solely on positive data (akin to Rejection FineTuning, RFT) leads to catastrophic collapse during online training. This contrasts with LLM findings where RFT can be a strong baseline, emphasizing the unique role of negative feedback in diffusion RL.
- **Benefits of ODE Samplers:** Online data quality is vital. ODE samplers (especially 2nd-order) consistently outperform SDE samplers for data collection, particularly on noise-sensitive rewards like PickScore.
- **Effectiveness of Adaptive Weighting:** The adaptive loss weighting scheme leads to faster and more stable training compared to fixed heuristic choices (e.g., $w(t)=1/t$, $w(t)=1$, $w(t)=t$).
- **Importance of Soft Update:** The soft EMA update for the sampling policy ($v_{old}$) is essential. A fully on-policy approach ($\eta=0$) leads to instability and collapse, while an overly off-policy approach ($\eta \to 1$) slows convergence. A dynamic schedule (e.g., gradually increasing $\eta$) strikes a balance between speed and stability.
- **Guidance Strength Parameter ($\beta$):** The hyperparameter $\beta$ controls the trade-off between stability and convergence speed. Values near 1 typically offer stable performance.

In summary, the main findings demonstrate that DiffusionNFT provides a highly efficient, high-performing, and robust online RL framework for diffusion models. Its CFG-free nature, combined with its ability to generalize across multiple rewards and outperform larger models, marks a significant advancement in aligning generative visual models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DiffusionNFT represents a significant advancement in the field of generative AI, particularly in bridging the gap between reinforcement learning and diffusion models. Its potential impact spans theoretical understanding, practical application, and future research directions.

**1. Novel Paradigm for Diffusion RL:**
The most profound significance of DiffusionNFT lies in its introduction of a novel online RL paradigm that operates on the **forward diffusion process** via **flow matching**. This is a fundamental shift from previous, often problematic, attempts to apply RL to the reverse (denoising) process. By focusing on the forward process, DiffusionNFT intrinsically adheres to the model's underlying probabilistic structure, offering a theoretically principled foundation for diffusion RL.

**2. Overcoming Core Limitations of Existing Approaches:**
DiffusionNFT effectively addresses several long-standing challenges in diffusion RL:
- **Likelihood Intractability:** It completely bypasses the need for likelihood estimation, a major bottleneck for policy gradient methods. This removes a source of estimation bias and computational overhead.
- **Solver Flexibility:** By decoupling data collection from policy optimization, it allows the use of arbitrary, potentially more efficient, black-box ODE or high-order solvers during sampling. This broadens the applicability and efficiency of diffusion RL.
- **CFG-Free High Performance:** Demonstrating that high-quality, aligned generation can be achieved without Classifier-Free Guidance is a substantial breakthrough. This simplifies model architectures, streamlines inference, and makes post-training more efficient by eliminating the need for separate conditional and unconditional models.

**3. Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability:**
The demonstrated 3x to 25x efficiency gain over FlowGRPO, coupled with superior performance, highlights DiffusionNFT's practical utility. This efficiency is critical for the iterative nature of online RL, making it feasible to fine-tune large diffusion models more rapidly and economically. The reduced reliance on storing full sampling trajectories also contributes to scalability.

**4. Advancing Model Alignment and Control:**
By enabling robust and efficient optimization across multiple reward models, DiffusionNFT offers a powerful tool for aligning generative models with complex human preferences, specific aesthetic criteria, or task-specific objectives. This leads to more controllable, safer, and higher-quality image generation, addressing a critical need in the deployment of AI-powered creative tools. The ability to generalize across diverse in-domain and out-of-domain rewards, even outperforming larger, CFG-based models, underscores its strong alignment capabilities.

**5. Step Towards Unifying SL and RL:**
The authors position DiffusionNFT as a concrete step toward a "general, unified, and native off-policy RL recipe across various modalities," leveraging a supervised learning foundation. This suggests a broader conceptual impact, potentially inspiring similar "negative-aware fine-tuning" paradigms in other domains where RL currently faces challenges, thus fostering a more cohesive understanding between supervised and reinforcement learning.

**6. Future Research Directions:**
DiffusionNFT opens new avenues for research into:
- Exploring the theoretical underpinnings of forward-process RL in continuous generative models.
- Developing more sophisticated negative-aware learning mechanisms.
- Applying this paradigm to other generative model architectures or modalities (e.g., video, audio) where similar challenges with likelihoods and computational expense exist.
- Investigating the interplay between different reward models and multi-objective optimization strategies within this framework.

In conclusion, DiffusionNFT significantly advances the state-of-the-art in online reinforcement learning for diffusion models. Its innovative methodology promises to make aligning and fine-tuning these powerful generative models more efficient, effective, and accessible, ultimately leading to more sophisticated and user-aligned AI-generated content.
