# Persistent Object Gaussian Splat (POGS) for Tracking Human and Robot Manipulation of Irregularly Shaped Objects

> [!summary] Summary
> The Persistent Object Gaussian Splat (POGS) system enables robots to track and manipulate previously unseen, irregularly shaped objects in dynamic environments by creating persistent 3D representations using 3D Gaussian Splatting and multi-modal feature embeddings. This approach allows robots to maintain object identity through human and robot manipulation, achieving an average placement error of 2.92 cm in object reset tasks and recovering from 80% of in-grasp tool perturbations.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic systems require pre-existing CAD models for object tracking and manipulation, which is impractical for diverse, previously unseen objects in dynamic real-world environments.
- Existing 3D reconstruction and tracking methods (e.g., point clouds, NeRFs) struggle with consistency across viewpoints, reconstructing occluded regions, computational cost, or maintaining object identity during movement and occlusions.
- A lack of integrated visual understanding, geometric information, and language features in current object representations limits sophisticated robotic interaction and persistence in dynamic scenes.

## Method

- The **Persistent Object Gaussian Splat** (POGS) system leverages 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== to create explicit, dynamic 3D representations of objects without prior CAD models.
- It embeds grouping features (object masks), language features (CLIP embeddings), and visual features (DINO self-supervised features) directly into the 3D Gaussians for comprehensive object understanding.
- An online tracking algorithm continuously extracts features from live camera observations, computes a loss against rendered features, and optimizes object poses to maintain persistent identity and accurate tracking in dynamic environments.

## Results

- POGS achieved an average placement error of **2.92** cm and up to 12 consecutive successful object resets in tasks involving human manipulation.
- The system recovered from **80%** of in-grasp tool perturbations (up to 30 degrees) in tool servoing experiments and successfully tracked 6DOF motion of targets and tools.
- POGS enabled fluid human-robot collaboration by maintaining object identity and tracking during object handovers and occlusions, allowing the robot to resume manipulation effectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - 3D Gaussian Splatting offers an explicit and efficient 3D representation, allowing for faster training, rendering, and easier editing compared to implicit methods like NeRFs for dynamic scene modeling.
> - Embedding multiple feature types (grouping, language, visual) directly into the 3D Gaussians enables a holistic understanding of objects, supporting robust tracking, semantic identification, and distinction between individual objects.
> - An online, feature-based tracking algorithm continuously updates object poses by minimizing a loss between rendered and observed features, ensuring persistent object identity and accurate pose estimation during complex human and robot manipulations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Robots working in dynamic environments face a significant challenge: tracking and manipulating irregularly shaped objects that are constantly being moved and reoriented. Traditional robotic systems rely heavily on pre-existing CAD models of objects, which is impractical for real-world settings where robots encounter diverse, previously unseen objects.

The Persistent Object Gaussian Splat (POGS) system represents a breakthrough in addressing this challenge. Developed by researchers from UC Berkeley's AUTOLab and Toyota Research Institute, POGS enables robots to track and manipulate objects without requiring prior models. It creates persistent 3D representations of objects that maintain their identity even as they're moved by humans or robots.

POGS leverages the recently developed 3D Gaussian Splatting technique, which offers significant advantages over previous approaches by providing an explicit 3D representation that can be efficiently updated in real-time. By integrating visual features, geometric information, and language understanding, POGS creates a holistic system that can track objects across multiple viewpoints and time steps, enabling sophisticated robotic manipulation.

### 2. Current Approaches and Limitations

Traditional approaches to object tracking and manipulation in robotics have faced several limitations:

1. **CAD Model Dependence**: Most systems require precise CAD models for each object, which is impractical for environments with diverse, constantly changing items.

2. **Point Cloud Limitations**: RGBD and point cloud-based methods struggle to maintain consistency across multiple viewpoints and have difficulty reconstructing occluded regions of objects.

3. **NeRF Computational Cost**: Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) provide high-quality 3D reconstructions but are computationally expensive and difficult to update efficiently in dynamic scenes.

4. **Lack of Persistence**: Many tracking systems lose object identity when significant occlusions occur or when objects are moved between observations.

5. **Limited Feature Integration**: Previous approaches often fail to integrate language features, visual understanding, and geometric information in a unified representation.

POGS addresses these limitations through its novel approach, combining the efficiency of Gaussian Splatting with rich feature embeddings and online tracking capabilities.

### 3. POGS System Overview

The POGS system comprises three main phases that work together to create a robust object tracking and manipulation framework:

#### 3.1. Scene Capture

The first phase involves capturing the environment through multiple viewpoints using an RGBD stereo camera mounted on a robot arm. This creates a comprehensive view of the scene that serves as the foundation for building the 3D object representations.

#### 3.2. Training and Feature Embedding

During this phase, the system:
- Initializes a 3D Gaussian Splatting model from the captured images and depth information
- Embeds three types of features into the 3D Gaussians:
 - **Grouping features**: Object masks that distinguish individual objects
 - **Language features**: CLIP embeddings that enable natural language understanding
 - **Visual features**: DINO self-supervised features for robust tracking

#### 3.3. Persistent Tracking

The system continuously tracks objects as they move through the environment by:
- Extracting features from live camera observations
- Computing a loss between rendered and observed features
- Optimizing object poses to minimize this loss
- Maintaining object identity across movement and occlusions

The key innovation of POGS is its ability to maintain a persistent representation of objects even as they're manipulated, relocated, or temporarily occluded. This persistence enables sophisticated robotic manipulation tasks that would be challenging or impossible with previous approaches.

### 4. Technical Implementation

The technical implementation of POGS builds on several cutting-edge technologies while introducing novel approaches for tracking and manipulation:

#### 4.1. Gaussian Splatting Foundation

POGS utilizes 3D Gaussian Splatting as its core representation. Unlike implicit neural representations like NeRFs, Gaussian Splatting provides:

- An explicit 3D representation using a set of Gaussian primitives
- Faster training and rendering times
- Easier editing capabilities for dynamic scenes

The system initializes the Gaussian Splatting model with a point cloud derived from multiple RGBD views, then optimizes the Gaussians' positions, scales, rotations, and colors to match the observed scene.

#### 4.2. Feature Embedding

A key innovation in POGS is the embedding of multiple feature types into the 3D Gaussians:

```python
# Simplified pseudocode for feature embedding
def embed_features(gaussians, rgb_images, depth_images):
    # Extract object masks using Detic
    object_masks = detic_model.segment(rgb_images)

    # Extract DINO features for tracking
    dino_features = dino_model.extract_features(rgb_images)

    # Extract CLIP features for language understanding
    clip_features = clip_model.encode_images(rgb_images)

    # Embed all features into Gaussians
    for gaussian in gaussians:
        gaussian.grouping_features = interpolate(object_masks, gaussian.position)
        gaussian.tracking_features = interpolate(dino_features, gaussian.position)
        gaussian.language_features = interpolate(clip_features, gaussian.position)

    return gaussians
```

This multi-feature approach allows POGS to understand what objects are (language features), which points belong to which objects (grouping features), and how to track objects over time (visual features).

#### 4.3. Online Tracking Algorithm

POGS implements an online tracking algorithm that continuously updates object poses based on new observations:

1. For each new frame, DINO features and a depth map are extracted
2. A rendering of the current Gaussian model from the camera viewpoint is generated
3. A loss function is computed between rendered and observed features
4. Object poses are optimized to minimize this loss
5. The result is an updated scene representation that reflects current object states

This process runs at approximately 1-2 Hz, allowing the system to maintain accurate object tracking during manipulation tasks.

### 5. Applications and Experiments

The POGS system was evaluated on several practical robotic manipulation tasks:

#### 5.1. Object Reset Task

The object reset task involves a human moving objects from their original positions, and the robot returning them to their initial locations. This demonstrates POGS' ability to:

- Track objects through human manipulation
- Maintain persistent object identity
- Understand spatial relationships described in natural language

The experiments included various household objects like an iron, jigsaw, shoe, and shelf. The robot would receive commands like "Place the iron onto the shelf" and successfully execute them based on its persistent object tracking.

#### 5.2. Tool Servoing

The tool servoing task tests the system's ability to maintain accurate tracking even when the robot is manipulating tools. In this experiment:

- The robot grasps a drill
- A human perturbs the drill's position in the gripper
- The system detects this perturbation through feature tracking
- The robot automatically adjusts to realign the drill with a target

This capability is essential for robust manipulation in dynamic environments where tools may slip or be adjusted during use.

The system also demonstrated 6DOF target following, where the robot can track and follow a moving target while maintaining proper tool orientation.

#### 5.3. Human-Robot Collaboration

POGS enables fluid collaboration between humans and robots in shared workspaces. In experiments, the system successfully:

- Tracked objects as they were handed between humans and robots
- Maintained object identity during occlusions
- Enabled the robot to resume manipulation after human interaction

This collaborative capability is particularly important for industrial settings where humans and robots work side-by-side on assembly or maintenance tasks.

### 6. Results and Performance

The POGS system demonstrated impressive performance across several metrics:

#### 6.1. Object Reset Performance

In the object reset task, POGS achieved:
- An average of up to 12 consecutive successful resets before losing track of objects
- Average placement error of 2.92 cm
- Successful tracking through significant viewpoint changes and occlusions

#### 6.2. Tool Servoing Robustness

For tool servoing, the system:
- Recovered from 80% of in-grasp tool perturbations up to 30 degrees
- Maintained accurate drill alignment with a target
- Tracked 6DOF motion of targets and tools simultaneously

#### 6.3. System Specifications

The current implementation of POGS runs on:
- A standard workstation with an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU
- Tracking frequency of 1-2 Hz
- Initial scene capture and training taking approximately 15-20 minutes
- Tracking and manipulation using a UR5 robot arm with a wrist-mounted stereo camera

The computational requirements and processing times represent a significant improvement over previous approaches like NeRFs, while providing superior tracking capabilities compared to point cloud-based methods.

### 7. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its impressive capabilities, POGS has several limitations that point to directions for future research:

#### 7.1. Current Limitations

1. **Tracking Frequency**: The 1-2 Hz tracking rate limits applications requiring high-speed manipulation or tracking of rapidly moving objects.

2. **Occlusion Sensitivity**: While more robust than previous approaches, POGS can still lose track of objects during extended or severe occlusions.

3. **Initial Training Time**: The 15-20 minute initial scene capture and training may be prohibitive for some applications requiring immediate operation.

4. **Lighting Sensitivity**: The visual feature-based tracking can be affected by significant changes in lighting conditions.

#### 7.2. Future Directions

Potential improvements and extensions to the POGS system include:

1. **Real-time Tracking**: Optimizing the implementation to achieve higher tracking frequencies (10+ Hz).

2. **Dynamic Scene Updates**: Enabling continuous updating of the Gaussian representation to incorporate new objects without full retraining.

3. **Multi-robot Collaboration**: Extending POGS to enable multiple robots to share and update a common scene representation.

4. **Integration with Motion Planning**: Tighter integration with robot motion planning to enable more complex manipulation sequences.

5. **Mobile Robot Applications**: Adapting POGS for use on mobile manipulation platforms to track objects across larger environments.

### 8. Conclusion

The Persistent Object Gaussian Splat (POGS) system represents a significant advance in robotic perception and manipulation. By combining the efficiency of Gaussian Splatting with rich feature embeddings and online tracking capabilities, POGS enables robots to maintain persistent representations of objects in dynamic environments without requiring prior models.

The system's ability to track objects through human and robot manipulation, maintain object identity across viewpoints and time, and enable natural language-based interaction creates new possibilities for collaborative robotics. The successful demonstrations of object reset, tool servoing, and human-robot collaboration highlight POGS' practical utility in real-world applications.

As robots increasingly move from controlled industrial settings to unstructured human environments, systems like POGS will be essential for enabling the flexibility, adaptability, and persistence required for effective operation. The approach taken by POGS—focusing on creating adaptable, feature-rich 3D representations rather than relying on pre-existing models—points the way toward more general-purpose robotic systems capable of working with the diversity of objects found in the real world.
