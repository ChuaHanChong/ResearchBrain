# Visual Point Cloud Forecasting enables Scalable Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> ViDAR pre-trains visual autonomous-driving encoders with a new pretext task, visual point cloud forecasting: predicting future LiDAR point clouds from historical multi-view images. A BEV History Encoder feeds a multi-group Latent Rendering operator that builds geometric latent features, and an auto-regressive, ego-motion-conditioned Future Decoder rolls out future BEV occupancy. It lifts detection, tracking, motion forecasting, occupancy, and planning across UniAD.

## Problem

- Visual driving models depend on costly 3D annotations such as boxes, semantic occupancy, and trajectories, which are hard to collect at production scale.
- Depth-estimation pre-training recovers geometry from a single image only, so it never learns the multi-view 3D structure surround-view BEV encoders require.
- Scene-rendering pre-training reconstructs multi-view images but omits temporal modeling, leaving prediction and planning without scene flow or object motion cues.

## Method

- ==Visual point cloud forecasting== pretext task: a **BEV History Encoder** embeds multi-frame multi-view images, jointly supervising semantics, multi-view geometry, and temporal dynamics from raw Image-LiDAR sequences.
- **Multi-group Latent Rendering** applies conditional-probability and feature-expectation functions per ray across 16 parallel channel groups, turning ray-shaped BEV features into discriminative 3D geometric latents.
- Auto-regressive **Future Decoder**: six ==deformable self-attention== and ==temporal cross-attention== layers conditioned on ego-motion embeddings roll BEV features forward, projected to occupancy and supervised by ==ray-wise cross-entropy==.

## Results

- Camera-only forecasting beats LiDAR-based 4D-Occ: roughly **33%** Chamfer Distance reduction for 1s future from 1s history, and about **18%** at 3s.
- On UniAD, ViDAR raises NDS from **49.36** to **52.57**, AMOTA from **38.3** to **42.0**, cuts minADE **0.75** to **0.67** and collision **0.27%** to **0.23%**.
- Ablation: dropping Latent Rendering falls to **40.20** NDS, below the **44.11** baseline; 16 groups reach **47.58**, and half the labels beat full supervision.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Forecasting a future sensor modality from past images forces semantics, geometry, and temporal dynamics into one representation, a stronger pretext than reconstruction alone.
> - Naive differentiable ray-casting supervision can hurt transfer: ray-shaped features suffice for rendering yet lack the discriminability downstream perception needs.
> - Pre-training gains grow as labels shrink, so annotation-free pretext tasks pay off most exactly where supervised data collection is expensive.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous driving is rapidly evolving, with significant progress made in perception, prediction, and planning. Traditionally, these systems heavily relied on LiDAR sensors for precise 3D geometry information, complemented by cameras for semantic understanding. However, there's a growing trend towards **visual (camera-only) autonomous driving** due to the cost-effectiveness, widespread availability, and richness of semantic information provided by cameras. Existing camera-only approaches have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) feature extraction and performance across various tasks.

Despite these advancements, a major bottleneck remains: the **heavy reliance on precise 3D annotations**. Tasks like 3D object detection, semantic occupancy prediction, and trajectory forecasting require labor-intensive and expensive labeling, which is difficult to scale for real-world deployment. This issue mirrors challenges in general computer vision before the advent of large-scale pre-training.

**Pre-training** has emerged as a crucial paradigm in general computer vision to mitigate the need for vast amounts of labeled data. Self-supervised learning (SSL) methods, such as contrastive learning and masked signal modeling (e.g., BERT, MAE), have enabled models to learn robust representations from unlabeled data, significantly boosting downstream performance. However, applying these general pre-training techniques to autonomous driving is challenging. Autonomous driving demands features that simultaneously encompass **semantics, 3D geometry, and temporal dynamics** for integrated perception, prediction, and planning. Existing pre-training approaches in AD often fall short:
- **Supervised pre-training** (e.g., on 3D detection or occupancy) still requires annotations and is not scalable.
- Methods using **Image-LiDAR pairs** for pre-training (e.g., depth estimation, scene rendering) struggle to model multi-view 3D geometry and, crucially, temporal dynamics. Temporal modeling is vital for predicting future states and enabling robust planning.

This paper addresses this critical gap by proposing a novel self-supervised pre-training task specifically tailored for visual autonomous driving. By focusing on **visual point cloud forecasting**, it aims to learn comprehensive features (semantics, 3D, temporal) in a scalable, annotation-free manner. It positions itself as a foundational step towards large-scale, general-purpose vision models for autonomous driving, similar to how large language models (LLMs) or masked autoencoders (MAEs) serve as foundation models in their respective domains. The work stands out by bridging modalities (visual input to 3D point cloud output) and explicitly incorporating temporal modeling, which are weaknesses of prior AD pre-training efforts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enable **scalable visual autonomous driving** by developing an effective pre-training framework that reduces reliance on expensive 3D annotations.

The key motivations are:
- **Addressing Annotation Scarcity:** Current state-of-the-art visual autonomous driving models heavily depend on meticulously labeled 3D data (e.g., 3D bounding boxes, semantic occupancy maps, trajectories). Collecting and annotating such data at scale is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming, hindering the widespread deployment and continuous improvement of autonomous systems.
- **Developing Comprehensive Feature Learning:** Autonomous driving tasks (perception, prediction, planning) require a holistic understanding of the environment. This necessitates features that simultaneously capture:
 - **Semantics:** What objects are present?
 - **3D Geometry:** Where are objects in 3D space? How is the scene structured?
 - **Temporal Dynamics:** How are objects moving? How will the scene evolve over time?
 Existing pre-training methods for autonomous driving often fail to learn all these aspects effectively and simultaneously from readily available data.
- **Bridging Modalities for Richer Representation:** While cameras are abundant and cheap, LiDAR provides direct 3D structural information. The motivation is to leverage abundant visual data to learn 3D understanding, effectively allowing cameras to "see" in 3D and understand motion, bridging the gap between perception and prediction/planning.
- **Enabling End-to-End Systems:** The ultimate goal of autonomous driving is end-to-end operation. Features learned through pre-training should be beneficial across all stages—from perceiving the environment to predicting future states and finally making optimal planning decisions. Prior pre-training often optimizes for individual perception tasks, leading to sub-optimal performance for prediction and planning.

To achieve these, the paper proposes a novel pre-text task: **visual point cloud forecasting**, which involves predicting future point clouds solely from historical visual inputs. This task is hypothesized to implicitly supervise the learning of semantics, 3D structures, and temporal dynamics concurrently, making the learned representations highly valuable for a wide range of downstream applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is **ViDAR (Visual Autonomous Driving Pre-training Model)**, a general framework designed to pre-train visual encoders for autonomous driving. ViDAR tackles the visual point cloud forecasting task, predicting future 3D point clouds from historical multi-view camera images.

ViDAR comprises three main components:

1. **History Encoder:** This is the primary component targeted for pre-training. It can be any visual BEV (Bird's-Eye-View) encoder (e.g., BEVFormer encoder, which uses a ResNet backbone and FPN neck). Its role is to extract rich, multi-view camera features from historical image sequences and transform them into a BEV embedding (F_bev). This BEV representation encapsulates the semantic and spatial information from the visual stream.

2. **Latent Rendering Operator:** This is a crucial and novel component designed to address a critical limitation observed when attempting to predict 3D from 2D. A straightforward approach using differentiable ray-casting often leads to "ray-shaped features" where BEV grid features along the same ray (corresponding to a single pixel in an image) become too similar, losing discriminative 3D geometric information.
 The Latent Rendering operator solves this by:
 - **Transforming Ray-shaped Features to Geometric Features:** Instead of directly rendering depths from an occupancy volume, it first computes "ray-wise features" through a feature expectation function. This averages features along a ray, weighted by conditional probabilities derived from a learnable probability map.
 - **Enhancing Discriminativeness:** It then customizes features of each BEV grid by weighting the ray-wise feature with its associated conditional probability. This process promotes feature responses that are discriminative and representative of true 3D geometry rather than merely reflecting ray continuity.
 - **Multi-group Latent Rendering:** To further enhance the diversity and richness of learned geometric features, the operator is designed to run in parallel across different feature channels, allowing diverse information to be captured.
 The output of Latent Rendering is a refined geometric embedding (F̂_bev), bridging the encoder's BEV features with the future decoder's 3D prediction capabilities.

3. **Future Decoder:** This component is an auto-regressive transformer designed to predict future BEV features (F̂_t) iteratively for arbitrary timestamps (t+1, t+2, etc.).
 - **Architecture:** It takes the current BEV latent space (F̂_t-1, where F̂_0 is F̂_bev from Latent Rendering) and an ego-motion condition (e_t, describing the ego-vehicle's expected future pose) as input. It utilizes transformer layers with Deformable Self-Attention and a novel Temporal Cross-Attention. The Temporal Cross-Attention is designed to align coordinate systems between frames, accounting for ego-vehicle movement.
 - **Output:** The predicted BEV features are then projected into a 3D occupancy volume (P_t), from which future point clouds can be derived (similar to previous point cloud forecasting methods like 4D-Occ, by casting rays and finding maximum occupancy responses).

**Pre-training Task and Loss:**
The pre-training task is **visual point cloud forecasting**: predicting future point clouds from historical visual inputs. The loss function is a **ray-wise cross-entropy loss**. Instead of supervising rendered depths with L1 loss, it directly maximizes the response of ground-truth point positions along their corresponding rays in the predicted occupancy volume and minimizes responses for other waypoints on the same ray. This effectively compels the model to accurately pinpoint the 3D locations of objects and structures in the future.

By integrating these components, ViDAR aims to learn representations that inherently capture semantics (from visual inputs), 3D geometry (via Latent Rendering and 3D occupancy prediction), and temporal dynamics (through the auto-regressive Future Decoder and prediction of future states), all without relying on explicit 3D annotations for pre-training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling experimental results demonstrating ViDAR's effectiveness across a wide spectrum of autonomous driving tasks on the nuScenes dataset.

#### 4.1. Point Cloud Forecasting Performance

- ViDAR, using **visual inputs only**, consistently outperforms 4D-Occ (a state-of-the-art method that uses LiDAR inputs for point cloud forecasting) in terms of Chamfer Distance.
- For 1s history input, ViDAR achieves a ~33% reduction in forecasting errors for 1s future predictions compared to 4D-Occ.
- With 3s history input, ViDAR shows an ~18% error reduction for 3s forecasting.
- Its auto-regressive design enables effective prediction of arbitrary future timestamps, even with limited input horizons. This validates the core capability of visual point cloud forecasting.

#### 4.2. Downstream Perception Tasks (3D Detection, Semantic Occupancy, Map Segmentation, Multi-Object Tracking)

- **3D Object Detection:** ViDAR pre-training consistently boosts performance across various backbones and initialization settings (ImageNet-cls, COCO-2D-det, nuScenes-3D-det). Notably, ViDAR, pre-trained *solely on Image-LiDAR sequences*, *outperforms models supervised by 3D detection pre-training* (e.g., 42.6% mAP vs. 41.5% mAP for RN101 backbone). When combined with 3D detection pre-training, it further boosts performance significantly (e.g., 45.8% mAP vs. 41.5% mAP).
- **Semantic Occupancy Prediction:** Similar to 3D detection, ViDAR consistently improves OccNet's performance across different backbones and initializations, highlighting its ability to learn robust 3D geometric features beneficial for dense scene understanding.
- **Map Segmentation & Multi-Object Tracking:** ViDAR also shows significant improvements in map segmentation (e.g., 33.2% IoU for lanes vs. 31.3% for UniAD baseline) and multi-object tracking (e.g., 45.1% AMOTA for UniAD with ViDAR vs. 39.0% without), demonstrating its positive impact on other perception-related tasks.

#### 4.3. Downstream Prediction Tasks (Motion Forecasting, Future Occupancy Prediction)

- **Motion Forecasting:** ViDAR substantially improves UniAD's motion forecasting performance, with a ~10% error reduction in minADE and a 3.5% improvement in EPA. This indicates enhanced temporal modeling.
- **Future Occupancy Prediction:** ViDAR improves UniAD for future occupancy prediction in both near and far areas, even allowing UniAD to surpass previous state-of-the-art BEVerse in all areas. This further confirms its superior temporal understanding.

#### 4.4. Downstream Planning Tasks

- ViDAR significantly reduces UniAD's average collision rate by ~15% and decreases the average planning displacement error by 0.21m. This showcases its direct positive impact on critical planning capabilities, even outperforming VAD, a state-of-the-art method.

#### 4.5. Joint Perception-Prediction-Planning (on UniAD)

- A summary table (Table 10) conclusively shows that ViDAR brings substantial and consistent improvements across *all* sub-modules of UniAD (detection, tracking, mapping, motion forecasting, future occupancy prediction, and planning). This validates ViDAR's effectiveness as a unified pre-training solution for end-to-end autonomous driving.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

- **Efficiency of Supervised Pre-training:** ViDAR dramatically reduces the reliance on labeled 3D annotations. BEVFormer pre-trained by ViDAR achieves better performance (39.4% mAP) using only *half* the supervised data compared to the baseline trained on the *full* dataset (37.7% mAP). The performance gains *increase* as the amount of available supervision decreases, highlighting its scalability benefit.
- **Effect of Latent Rendering Operator:** This component is critical. Without Latent Rendering, performance drops significantly (from 44.11% NDS to 40.20% NDS). With it, performance improves notably (to 47.58% NDS). The study also shows that increasing the number of parallel groups in Latent Rendering consistently improves downstream performance.
- **Impact of Future Supervision:** More future supervision frames during pre-training lead to consistent improvements in multi-object tracking performance, emphasizing the value of learning temporal dynamics.
- **Pre-train Structure:** ViDAR primarily benefits the "view-transformation" part of visual BEV encoders, which is responsible for correlating 2D features with 3D geometries, indicating its effectiveness in learning 3D understanding from images.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ViDAR introduces a highly significant paradigm shift for scalable autonomous driving, moving beyond the limitations of expensive 3D annotations and partial feature learning. Its potential impact is multi-faceted:

- **Enabling Scalable Autonomous Driving:** By leveraging readily available Image-LiDAR sequences to predict future point clouds, ViDAR offers a cost-effective and scalable pre-training solution. This significantly reduces the bottleneck of 3D data annotation, accelerating the development and deployment of autonomous systems in diverse environments.
- **Foundation Models for Autonomous Driving:** The approach lays groundwork for developing "foundation models" for autonomous driving. Just as large language models (LLMs) or masked autoencoders (MAEs) have revolutionized their respective fields by learning generalizable representations from vast unlabeled data, ViDAR aims to do the same for visual autonomous driving. A highly capable, pre-trained visual encoder could serve as a backbone for various downstream tasks, from perception to planning, leading to more robust and adaptable systems.
- **Holistic Feature Learning:** ViDAR's key innovation lies in its ability to simultaneously learn semantics, 3D geometry, and temporal dynamics from visual data. This holistic understanding is critical for complex real-world autonomous driving scenarios, where an integrated perception-prediction-planning loop is essential for safe and efficient operation.
- **Improved End-to-End Systems:** The consistent and substantial improvements across all modules of UniAD (a state-of-the-art end-to-end system) demonstrate ViDAR's practical utility. Better perception, more accurate predictions, and safer planning contribute directly to enhanced autonomous driving performance and reliability.
- **Advancement in Modality Bridging:** Predicting 3D point clouds from 2D images is a challenging task. The Latent Rendering operator's success in mitigating the "ray-shaped features" problem and effectively learning 3D geometric features from visual inputs is a notable technical advancement in multimodal representation learning.
- **Future Directions in Simulation and Reinforcement Learning:** The ability of ViDAR to act as a "world model" by simulating various future point clouds based on different ego-vehicle motions opens new avenues for model-based reinforcement learning in autonomous driving. This could enable autonomous agents to train in simulated environments that are highly realistic in terms of 3D geometry and temporal dynamics, without requiring human supervision.

In conclusion, ViDAR represents a crucial step towards truly scalable, robust, and end-to-end visual autonomous driving systems by offering an effective self-supervised pre-training paradigm. Its novel approach to learning comprehensive environmental representations from easily accessible data holds immense promise for the future of autonomous technology.
