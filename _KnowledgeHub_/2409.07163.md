# Mamba Policy: Towards Efficient 3D Diffusion Policy with Hybrid Selective State Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Mamba Policy proposes an efficient 3D diffusion policy for robotic manipulation, utilizing a hybrid Mamba-Attention UNet architecture. This design significantly reduces computational demands, cutting model parameters by 81.2% and FLOPs by 90.0% compared to prior 3D diffusion policies, while enhancing task success rates across benchmark tasks and demonstrating effectiveness in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing 3D diffusion policies require extensive computational resources due to large backbone architectures, such as UNets with hundreds of millions of parameters.
- These large models pose challenges for efficient long-horizon prediction and are impractical for deployment on resource-constrained hardware.
- High computational demands limit the widespread application of advanced visuomotor policies in real-world robotic systems.

## Method

- **Mamba Policy** introduces a novel X-==Mamba UNet== architecture that integrates a hybrid Mamba (selective state space model) and Attention mechanism.
- The core XMamba block combines FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation) for perception-action fusion with MambaAttn refinement for efficient sequence processing.
- The policy uses a conditional diffusion model with a DDIM solver to iteratively denoise a randomly initialized action sequence.

## Results

- Achieved generally higher average success rates across Adroit, MetaWorld, and DexArt datasets compared to existing 3D diffusion policies like DP3.
- Demonstrated an **81.2%** reduction in model parameters and a **90.0%** reduction in FLOPs, alongside an **86.2%** decrease in GPU memory usage, compared to DP3.
- Successfully enabled a Franka arm to perform a real-world grasping and placement task, confirming practical applicability in a physical environment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating Mamba models within a diffusion policy framework significantly reduces model parameters (81.2%) and FLOPs (90.0%) while maintaining or improving performance.
> - A hybrid architecture combining Mamba's efficient long-range dependency capturing with Attention's refinement capabilities is effective for 3D robotic manipulation.
> - The Mamba Policy demonstrates improved robustness and efficiency in handling longer action horizons, which is crucial for complex robotic tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work operates within the field of visuomotor policies for robotic manipulation, specifically focusing on imitation learning using diffusion models. Visuomotor policies, which integrate visual perception with motor control, are recognized as essential for robots to execute complex tasks based on visual input. Imitation learning is a common approach in this domain, allowing robots to acquire skills by observing human demonstrations.

Diffusion models have recently gained attention in robotic manipulation due to their capability to learn and generate action distributions, which facilitates the prediction of diverse and adaptable behaviors in complex environments. These models have been applied across various robotic tasks, including dexterous hand control, grasping, and navigation. Existing diffusion policies in robotics typically fall into two categories: those relying on 2D visual inputs (e.g., Diffusion Policy) and those leveraging 3D visual information such as point clouds (e.g., 3D Diffusion Policy or DP3). The current study is based on 3D visual perception.

A challenge associated with many diffusion policies is their reliance on large backbone architectures, often UNet models, which can possess hundreds of millions of parameters. Such large models demand significant computational resources, limiting their deployment on resource-constrained devices or edge systems. Additionally, efficient long-horizon prediction is an ongoing requirement in robot learning for accurate decision-making and planning over extended periods.

Mamba models, based on selective state space models (SSMs), have emerged as a potential solution for efficient sequence modeling due to their low computational complexity and capacity for capturing long-horizon dependencies. These models have seen increasing adoption in various sequence-based tasks, including trajectory modeling in robotics. This paper integrates Mamba models into 3D diffusion policies to address the aforementioned challenges of computational overhead and long-term prediction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and robust policy for 3D robotic manipulation that addresses the computational limitations of existing diffusion models, while maintaining or improving performance.

The motivation for this work stems from several observed challenges within the current landscape of diffusion policies for robotics:
- **High Computational Demands:** Current state-of-the-art 3D diffusion policies, such as DP3, frequently employ large UNet-based policy networks. These networks can contain over 200 million parameters, leading to substantial computational requirements in terms of FLOPs, GPU memory, and processing time. This makes their deployment impractical on hardware with limited resources, such as embedded systems or smaller robots, thereby restricting the widespread application of these advanced policies.
- **Limited Efficiency for Long-Horizon Tasks:** While diffusion policies are effective, the efficiency and stability of long-horizon prediction remain areas for improvement. Complex robotic tasks, such as navigation or intricate manipulation, often require accurate decision-making and planning over extended periods. Large and computationally intensive models may struggle to scale efficiently for these prolonged sequences, impacting real-time performance and system responsiveness.
- **Potential of Mamba Models:** The recent development of Mamba models, characterized by their selective state space model (SSM) architecture, offers a promising avenue for efficient sequence modeling. Mamba models demonstrate low computational complexity and strong performance in capturing long-range dependencies, features that are highly relevant to robotic trajectory modeling and control. The researchers are motivated to explore whether integrating Mamba's efficiencies can mitigate the resource demands of diffusion policies without sacrificing performance.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the "Mamba Policy" are:
1. **Reduce Model Parameter Count and Computational Overhead:** Develop a policy network that significantly reduces the number of parameters and computational demands (FLOPs, GPU memory) compared to existing large-scale diffusion policy backbones.
2. **Maintain or Enhance Performance:** Ensure that the reduction in model size and computational cost does not compromise task success rates. The goal is to achieve comparable or superior performance in various 3D manipulation tasks.
3. **Improve Robustness in Long-Horizon Scenarios:** Design a policy that exhibits enhanced stability and performance when dealing with longer sequences of actions, which is crucial for complex robotic operations.
4. **Validate Effectiveness Across Diverse Datasets and Real-World Scenarios:** Conduct comprehensive experiments on established robotic manipulation datasets (Adroit, MetaWorld, DexArt) and a real-world robotic setup to empirically demonstrate the proposed policy's effectiveness and practical applicability.
5. **Analyze the Impact of Mamba Variants and Key Components:** Systematically investigate the contributions of different Mamba variants and the proposed hybrid architectural components to understand their respective impacts on overall policy performance and efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Mamba Policy integrates a hybrid state space model with attention mechanisms into a diffusion policy framework to enhance efficiency and performance in 3D robotic manipulation. The overall approach is structured into perception extraction and decision prediction, primarily relying on a novel X-Mamba UNet architecture.

**A. Overview of Mamba Policy**
The model takes a noised action and a condition as inputs. The condition is composed of three elements: a point cloud perception embedding, a robot state embedding, and a time embedding. Each of these components is processed through a respective encoder ($\Phi_{\text{type}}$). The core of the policy is the X-Mamba UNet, which processes these inputs to predict the noise component. This prediction is used in a DDIM solver to reconstruct actions.

**B. Perception Extraction**
For perception extraction, the model utilizes a simple Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) encoder, similar to DP3, to process single-view point clouds. This encoder extracts relevant features from the point cloud data. These extracted features are then combined with the robot's self-state features and time embeddings to form the comprehensive conditional input for the decision prediction phase.

**C. X-Mamba UNet and XMamba Block**
The denoise network, referred to as X-Mamba UNet, is built upon a series of XMamba blocks. An XMamba block processes an input perception feature `c` (from the DP3 encoder, combining point cloud and self-state information) and a noised action input `a`. The block consists of two main stages: FiLM Fusion and MambaAttn Refinement.

1. **FiLM Fusion:** This stage integrates the perception features `c` with the noised action input `a`.
 - The noised action `a` is first processed through a 1D Convolutional layer (`Conv1D`), Group Normalization (`GN`), and a Mish activation function ($\sigma$) to produce `x_in`.
 - The perception feature `c` undergoes Group Normalization and Mish activation, then passes through a function `f` that splits it into two parts: `x_s` (scale) and `x_b` (bias).
 - The fusion is performed using Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM), where `o_1 = x_s * x_in + x_b`. This mechanism allows the conditional features to modulate the input transformation adaptively.

2. **MambaAttn Refinement:** Following FiLM fusion, the fused feature `o_1` is further refined using a hybrid Mamba and Attention module.
 - A generic mixer module `F(x; Mixer)` is defined, which applies a given `Mixer` (either Mamba or Attention) followed by a DropPath mechanism and trainable scaling parameters ($\gamma_1$). It then adds an MLP layer with another DropPath and scaling ($\gamma_2$).
 - The feature `o_1` first passes through a Mamba mixer: `o_2 = F(o_1; Mamba)`. This leverages Mamba's selective state space model for efficient sequence modeling and capturing long-range dependencies.
 - Subsequently, `o_2` passes through an Attention mixer: `o_3 = F(o_2; Attention)`. This incorporates standard attention mechanisms to refine features by focusing on relevant parts of the sequence.
 - After passing through N such XMamba blocks, a simple decoder, consisting of Conv1D, GN, Mish, and another Conv1D, maps the refined feature `o_3` to the predicted noise `a_out`.

**D. Decision Making**
The decision module is based on conditional diffusion models, specifically utilizing the Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM) solver.

1. **Training Process:**
 - Action samples `a_0` are randomly drawn from the expert dataset.
 - Gaussian noise `ϵ` is added to `a_0` to create a noised action `a_t` at a random time step `t`.
 - The X-Mamba UNet, parameterized by `θ` (denoted as `ϵ_θ`), predicts the noise `ϵ_θ(a_t, c, t)` given the noised action `a_t`, the perception condition `c`, and the time step `t`.
 - The model is trained by minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss between the true noise `ϵ` and the predicted noise `ϵ_θ`, as defined by the DDIM objective function: `L_ddim = ||ϵ - ϵ_θ(√(α_bar_t)a_0 + √(1 - α_bar_t)ϵ, c, t)||^2`.

2. **Inference Process:**
 - After training, the denoising network `ϵ_θ` is used to iteratively denoise a randomly initialized noisy action `a_K` (where K is the total diffusion steps) back to the predicted action `a_0`.
 - Each iterative step `t` to `t-1` is performed non-stochastically using the DDIM formula: `a_t-1 = √(α_bar_t-1) * ((a_t - √(1 - α_bar_t) * ϵ_θ(a_t, c, t)) / √(α_bar_t)) + √(1 - α_bar_t-1 - σ_t^2) * ϵ_θ(a_t, c, t) + σ_t * ϵ_t`. Here, `α_bar_t` and `σ_t` are related to the noise scheduler settings.
 - The resulting `a_0` represents the predicted action sequence, which is then used to interact with the environment.

**E. Experiment Settings**
- **Datasets:** Experiments were conducted on Adroit, MetaWorld (hard levels), and DexArt datasets. Expert data consisted of successful trajectories, following the DP3 collection method.
- **Baselines:** Comparisons were made against 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3), 2D Diffusion Policy (DP), BCRNN, and IBC. DP3 results were reproduced by the authors for fair comparison.
- **Parameters:** Prediction horizon T=4, observation length T_o=2, action prediction length T_a=3. X-Mamba UNet dimensions:. DDIM noise scheduler with 100 total timesteps and 10 inference timesteps. AdamW optimizer with an initial learning rate of 1e-4 and cosine learning rate scheduler. Training for 3000 epochs with a batch size of 128.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Average of the highest 1, 3, and 5 success rates (SR1, SR3, SR5) across 3 random seeds (0, 1, 2) for each domain, reporting mean and standard deviation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments and analyses presented in the paper indicate the Mamba Policy's performance and efficiency characteristics in comparison to established methods.

**A. Comparisons with State-of-the-Art Baselines**
The Mamba Policy was evaluated against Diffusion Policy (DP), BCRNN, IBC, and 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) across a range of manipulation datasets, including Adroit, MetaWorld, and DexArt.

- **Quantitative Performance (Table I):** Mamba Policy generally achieved higher success rates (SR5) compared to the baselines. For example, in the Adroit Door domain, Mamba Policy attained 68.3% compared to DP3's 64.5%. Across all evaluated domains, Mamba Policy exhibited a higher overall average performance.
- **Detailed Success Rate Metrics (Figure 4):** Visualizations of SR1, SR3, and SR5 metrics show that Mamba Policy performed comparably or better across these different performance levels, indicating its robustness. The training curves also suggest a stable and reliable training process for the model.

**B. Efficiency Analysis**
A key objective was to reduce computational demands. The analysis focused on difficult MetaWorld environments.

- **Parameter and FLOPs Reduction (Table II):** Mamba Policy achieved a significant reduction in model parameters (by 81.2%, from 255.1M to 47.9M) and Floating Point Operations (FLOPs) (by 90.0%, from 0.30G to 0.03G) compared to DP3.
- **GPU Memory Usage (Table II):** The PyTorch allocated GPU memory per loss backward during training decreased by 86.2%. Total GPU usage with Mamba Policy was 127.7% lower than that of DP3.
- **Performance Despite Efficiency (Table II):** Despite these substantial reductions in computational resources, Mamba Policy exhibited comparable or improved success rates in challenging MetaWorld tasks. For instance, SR1 increased by 5.1% in Stick-Pull, 1.7% in Disassemble, and 3.3% in Pick-Out-Of-Hole, demonstrating a favorable balance of efficiency and performance.

**C. Ablation Study**

1. **Ablations on SSM Variants (Table III):**
 - Different Structured State Space Model (SSM) variants were integrated into the Mamba Policy framework.
 - Mamba-V1 showed improvements, particularly in SR3 and SR1 metrics, relative to DP3.
 - Mamba-V2 yielded a marginal improvement over DP3.
 - Bidirectional SSM (Bi-SSM) resulted in a negative impact on performance.
 - Hydra policy delivered high success rates, securing top SR5 and SR1 outcomes, but was noted to be computationally slow during training.
 - The overall assessment suggested Mamba-V1 offered a good balance of performance and training efficiency.

2. **Ablations on Horizon Lengths (Figure 5):**
 - The model's performance was tested across various sequence lengths (4, 8, 16, 32).
 - Mamba Policy consistently performed comparably or better than the baseline at each length. For a horizon length of 4, Mamba Policy achieved 53.3% success rate compared to the baseline's 45.0%.
 - This indicates the architecture's capacity for handling longer temporal dependencies, contributing to enhanced robustness in long-horizon scenarios, while also demonstrating reduced GPU usage for longer horizons.

3. **Ablations on Key Components (Table IV):**
 - An ablation study was conducted on the core components of the XMamba block, starting from a baseline with only the FiLM fusion module.
 - Adding the Mamba module alone resulted in a substantial improvement in success rates, suggesting its contribution to model robustness.
 - Using Attention alone, or MambaAttn without the MLP, led to decreased accuracy compared to Mamba alone.
 - The full model, incorporating all proposed components (Mamba, Attention, and MLP), achieved the highest success rates across SR5, SR3, and SR1, indicating the importance of each element in achieving the reported performance.

**D. Real-World Experiment**
- **Setup:** A Franka arm equipped with a gripper and a Realsense D455 camera was used for real-world validation. Inference was run on a 4090 GPU. 40 human teleoperation demonstrations were collected for training.
- **Task:** The robot was tasked with grasping a cup and placing it on a cup holder.
- **Results (Figure 6):** The Mamba Policy enabled the robot to successfully perform the grasping and placement task with precision. This validation indicated the practical applicability and effectiveness of the proposed method in a physical environment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Mamba Policy research presents a contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing the computational constraints of existing 3D diffusion policies. The primary significance lies in demonstrating that a substantial reduction in model complexity and resource consumption can be achieved without compromising, and in some cases enhancing, performance in complex robotic tasks.

The potential impacts of this work include:

- **Enabling Deployment on Resource-Constrained Devices:** By reducing the parameter count by over 80% and significantly lowering GPU memory usage and FLOPs, the Mamba Policy makes advanced 3D diffusion policies more feasible for deployment on edge devices, embedded systems, and robots with limited computational power. This could broaden the applicability of sophisticated robotic control methods in real-world scenarios where large-scale computing infrastructure is not available.
- **Improved Efficiency for Robotics Applications:** The demonstrated computational efficiency could facilitate faster training, inference, and real-time execution of robotic tasks. This is particularly relevant for applications requiring quick decision-making and rapid adaptation, such as in dynamic manufacturing environments or human-robot collaboration.
- **Enhanced Robustness in Long-Horizon Tasks:** The Mamba Policy's performance across varying horizon lengths suggests an improved capability to handle long-term dependencies. This robustness could lead to more reliable and extended task completion for complex robotic operations like assembly, long-range navigation, or multi-stage manipulation, where planning over a longer sequence of actions is critical.
- **Advancement in Hybrid Model Architectures:** The introduction of the XMamba Block, which integrates both Mamba (selective state space model) and Attention mechanisms, provides a model for designing efficient and effective sequence processing architectures in robotics. This hybrid approach leverages the strengths of both paradigms—Mamba's efficiency in capturing long-range dependencies and Attention's ability to focus on salient features—offering a template for future model development.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a baseline for exploring the integration of state space models with diffusion policies in robotics. Future research could build upon this foundation to investigate further optimizations, explore different SSM variants, or apply similar principles to other domains within robot learning that benefit from efficient sequence modeling.
- **Practical Validation:** The successful execution of a real-world grasping and placement task with a Franka arm further validates the practical utility and transferability of the Mamba Policy from simulation to physical robot systems, indicating its readiness for applied scenarios.
