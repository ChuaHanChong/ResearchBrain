# HumDex: Humanoid Dexterous Manipulation Made Easy

> [!summary] Summary
> A portable IMU-based teleoperation system, HumDex, combined with a learning-based hand retargeting method and a two-stage imitation learning framework, enables efficient data collection and robust control for humanoid whole-body dexterous manipulation. The system achieved a 90% success rate on complex tasks, reduced data collection time by 26%, and significantly improved policy generalization to unseen scenarios by nearly doubling success rates.

## Problem

- Collecting high-quality, diverse demonstration data for humanoid whole-body dexterous manipulation is challenging due to limitations of current teleoperation systems (e.g., fixed infrastructure, occlusion, precision issues).
- Existing optimization-based methods for dexterous hand retargeting can result in imprecise, non-smooth motions and require extensive manual tuning.
- Bridging the human-robot embodiment gap to effectively leverage diverse human motion data for improving robot policy generalization is difficult for precise manipulation tasks.

## Method

- Developed **HumDex**, a portable, IMU-based teleoperation system for whole-body and dexterous hand control, addressing occlusion and infrastructure limitations in data collection.
- Introduced a learning-based ==Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP)== for dexterous hand retargeting, providing smooth and generalized robot hand control from human fingertip positions.
- Proposed a two-stage imitation learning framework: pre-training on diverse human data for general priors, followed by fine-tuning on robot data to adapt to the robot's specific embodiment.

## Results

- HumDex achieved a **90%** teleoperation success rate for the complex Scan&Pack task, which was not achievable by a vision-based baseline, and reduced data collection time by **26%** across common tasks.
- Policies trained on HumDex data demonstrated a higher average execution success rate of **80.0%**, compared to **57.5%** for policies trained on baseline data.
- The two-stage imitation learning approach significantly improved policy generalization, nearly doubling success rates on unseen variations (e.g., 20/30 vs 10/30 for unseen objects) compared to robot-only training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Utilizing IMU-based motion capture for teleoperation significantly improves robustness against occlusion and enhances data collection efficiency for complex humanoid dexterous tasks.
> - A lightweight learning-based model can effectively retarget human dexterous hand poses to robot hands, yielding smoother and more reliable movements than traditional optimization methods.
> - Pre-training policies on diverse human demonstration data, followed by fine-tuning on robot-specific data, is an effective strategy to leverage human priors and improve robot policy generalization for complex manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics is advancing towards enabling robots to perform complex, long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks in diverse real-world environments. A common approach to acquiring such skills is imitation learning, which necessitates high-quality demonstration data. However, for humanoid robots equipped with dexterous hands, collecting this data presents a notable challenge due to the complexity of their morphology and the limitations of existing teleoperation systems.

Current teleoperation systems generally fall into several categories, each with its own trade-offs:
- **Motion-capture (mocap) or exoskeleton-based systems:** These offer high accuracy but typically require fixed, often dedicated, infrastructure or are heavy and constrain operator movement, limiting portability and the environments in which data can be collected.
- **Vision-based or Virtual Reality (VR)-based systems:** These provide greater portability but often suffer from reduced tracking accuracy and occlusion issues. Operators may need to keep their hands within the sensor's field of view, restricting the range of motions and tasks that can be demonstrated.

Specifically for dexterous hand control within these systems, many approaches rely on optimization-based retargeting. While functional, these methods can lead to reduced accuracy and limited generalization capabilities across varied tasks and robot embodiments.

Furthermore, leveraging human demonstration data for robot learning is a recognized area of research. For tabletop dexterous manipulation, prior work has explored strategies such as hardware design mimicking human embodiment, human-in-the-loop correction, or aligning action spaces to mitigate the human-robot embodiment gap. For humanoid manipulation, human motion data has been used for general whole-body movements and low-level control, focusing on approximate motion following. However, using human data to achieve the precision required for complex dexterous manipulation tasks with humanoid robots, especially without explicit alignment strategies, remains an area requiring further development. Many manipulation tasks with humanoids still predominantly rely on robot teleoperation data.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing a portable teleoperation system designed for humanoid whole-body *dexterous* manipulation, aiming to overcome the portability-precision trade-off and occlusion issues. It also proposes a method to effectively leverage diverse human motion data to improve policy generalization, specifically for complex manipulation tasks, by addressing the embodiment gap more efficiently.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to facilitate efficient collection of high-quality demonstration data for humanoid whole-body dexterous manipulation and to leverage this data, particularly human motion data, to enhance the generalization capabilities of robot policies.

The motivation for this work is multifaceted:
- **Addressing Data Collection Bottlenecks:** Current teleoperation systems for humanoid robots with dexterous hands are considered less mature than those for tabletop robots. The limitations of existing systems—such as restricted portability, susceptibility to occlusion, or insufficient precision—create a significant bottleneck for acquiring the high-quality, diverse demonstration data necessary for imitation learning, especially for complex, long-horizon, and whole-body tasks involving fine-grained dexterous manipulation. The authors aim to develop a system that overcomes these limitations.
- **Improving Dexterous Hand Control:** Many existing approaches for mapping human hand movements to robot hands rely on optimization-based retargeting. This often necessitates manual parameter tuning and can result in motions that are not consistently smooth, natural, or generalizable across different real-world deployment scenarios. A key motivation is to develop a more robust and adaptable method for dexterous hand control that can generate smooth and natural motions without extensive manual intervention.
- **Leveraging Human Data for Generalization:** Human motion data offers a valuable source of diverse movement priors and can be collected more efficiently than robot teleoperation data. However, directly transferring human motions to humanoid robots for precise manipulation tasks is challenging due to inherent morphological differences and embodiment gaps. The research is motivated by the potential to effectively utilize the diversity and generalizability of human data to pre-train robot policies, thereby reducing the reliance on costly robot-specific data and improving the policy's ability to generalize to unseen task variations (e.g., new positions, objects, backgrounds). This requires a framework that can bridge the human-robot embodiment gap without complex data alignment or correction strategies.

In summary, the authors seek to make humanoid dexterous manipulation easier and more scalable by developing a superior teleoperation system and an effective learning framework that leverages human data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HumDex system addresses the challenges of humanoid whole-body dexterous manipulation through a multi-component approach: a portable teleoperation system for efficient data collection, a learning-based method for dexterous hand retargeting, and a two-stage imitation learning framework leveraging human data.

#### 3.1. Whole-Body Dexterous Teleoperation System

The teleoperation system is designed for real-time, whole-body dexterous control. It employs a hierarchical control architecture, decoupling the system into a low-level controller for stable body balance and a high-level command generator.

- **Low-Level Hierarchical Control Interface:** The system utilizes a pre-trained motion tracking policy (e.g., from TWIST2 or SONIC) as the foundation for the robot's base and body balancing. This policy generates actuation torques based on a whole-body reference command vector `p_cmd`. Crucially, this work decomposes the joint targets `q_ref` into `q_body` (fed to the low-level policy) and `q_hand` (directly executed by joint position controllers for precise dexterous motions).
- **High-Level Teleoperation via IMU-based Retargeting:**
 - **Hardware Setup:** The system uses a tether-free inertial motion capture interface for portability and robustness against occlusion.
 - **Body Tracking:** A 15-node IMU system (e.g., Vdmocap or a custom <$200 SlimeVR-based system with ICM-45686 IMUs and nRF52840 SoCs) is worn on the operator's body.
 - **Dexterous Hand Tracking:** High-fidelity commercial inertial gloves (Vdhand or Manus) capture fine-grained hand motions, addressing self-occlusion issues common in vision-based systems.
 - **Retargeting Algorithm:** The General Motion Retargeting (GMR) framework is employed to map the IMU-derived human skeleton (`S_H`) to robot commands. To mitigate global position drift inherent in IMU systems, a pelvis-centric optimization formulation is used. This minimizes the orientation error of all links and the relative position error of key end-effectors with respect to the operator's pelvis frame, generating the robot configuration `q*`.
- **Data Collection Process:** The system enables collection of both human and robot data. Human data is collected with the operator wearing the suit and gloves, with an egocentric camera on a neckband, and raw motion data retargeted to the robot's 35-DoF body and 40-DoF hands. Robot data is collected by controlling the physical Unitree G1 robot with the same teleoperation system, recording egocentric RGB images, full robot state, and applied action commands. All data is managed via a Redis-based distributed framework for low-latency synchronization.

#### 3.2. Learning-Based Dexterous Hand Retargeting

To control the 20-DoF dexterous hands, the system introduces a learning-based retargeting method.
- **Input/Output:** The input is a compact 15-dimensional representation of the 3D positions of the operator's five fingertips (thumb, index, middle, ring, little) in the glove wrist frame. The output is the 20-DoF joint angle vector for the robot's Wuji hand.
- **Model:** A lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) regressor, parameterized as $f_{\theta}$, is trained to map fingertip positions to robot joint angles: $\min_{\theta} E_{(p,q) \sim D} ||f_{\theta}(p) - q||^2_2$. The MLP uses a finger-wise modular design with FC layers, LeakyReLU activations, and Batch Normalization, culminating in a Tanh output.
- **Training Data:** A paired dataset `D` of human hand poses and corresponding robot hand joint configurations is generated by an offline optimization-based inverse kinematics (IK) solver across diverse hand poses. This process takes approximately 20 minutes to collect ~20k frames.
- **Advantage:** This learning-based approach aims to provide smooth, continuous joint trajectories and generalize across various hand sizes without the computational cost and manual tuning associated with per-frame optimization-based methods.

#### 3.3. Two-Stage Imitation Learning with Human Data

To effectively leverage human data and address the embodiment gap between humans and the G1 robot, a two-stage imitation learning framework is proposed:
- **Policy Backbone:** The Action Chunking Transformer (ACT) is used as the policy backbone, combined with a ResNet-18 pre-trained visual encoder.
- **Observation/Action Space:** The observation space includes egocentric RGB images (640x480) and proprioceptive states (joint positions of body and hands). The action space consists of 20 dimensions per hand and 35 dimensions for the humanoid body, representing low-level motion tracker targets.
- **Stage 1: Pre-training on Human Data:**
 - The policy is initially trained solely on human demonstration data collected with HumDex.
 - A key challenge is the absence of robot proprioceptive states in human data. This is addressed by approximating these states using the previous timestep's action ($a_{t-1}$).
 - The objective of this stage is to enable the policy to learn generalizable motion and perception priors from the diverse settings in which human data can be collected.
- **Stage 2: Fine-tuning on Robot Data:**
 - After pre-training, the policy is fine-tuned exclusively on robot teleoperation data, also collected with HumDex.
 - This stage focuses on refining the learned movements to precisely match the robot's specific kinematics and embodiment, bridging the gap between human and robot morphologies.
- **Rationale:** This sequential approach avoids directly mixing human and robot data during training, which can lead to conflicting gradient signals and training instability due to the substantial embodiment differences. Instead, it aims to combine the diversity and efficiency of human data collection with the precision required for robot execution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated HumDex's performance in data collection, hand retargeting, and policy generalization, across a suite of challenging tasks.

#### 4.1. Whole-Body Dexterous Teleoperation

- **Tasks:** Five tasks were used for evaluation: Scan&Pack (dexterous, bimanual, long-horizon), HangTowel (bimanual coordination), OpenDoor (articulated object, loco-manipulation), PlaceBasketOnShelf (whole-body movement), and PickBread (dexterous grasping).
- **Comparison:** HumDex was compared against a baseline vision-based teleoperation system (PICO headset with vision-based hand tracking, adapted from TWIST2).
- **Results (Table I):**
 - **Task Coverage and Reliability:** The baseline system was unable to perform the Scan&Pack task (0/60 success rate) due to severe occlusion of the hands by the scanner, demonstrating a limitation of vision-based systems for tool use. HumDex achieved a 90% teleoperation success rate (54/60) on this task.
 - **Collection Efficiency:** For the four tasks feasible for both systems, HumDex reduced the average data collection time for 60 episodes from 59.8 minutes (Baseline) to 44.3 minutes (Ours), representing a 26% improvement. This efficiency was attributed to the robustness of IMU-based tracking against occlusion, allowing operators to focus on task execution rather than maintaining sensor visibility.
 - **Collection Success Rate:** HumDex achieved an average teleoperation success rate of 91.7% on common tasks, compared to 74.6% for the baseline.
 - **Policy Success Rate:** Policies trained on data collected with HumDex achieved an average success rate of 80.0% in execution, outperforming policies trained on baseline data (57.5%). This indicated improved data quality for downstream learning.

#### 4.2. Dexterous Hand Retargeting

- **Comparison:** The learning-based hand retargeting method was compared to a classical optimization-based retargeting baseline for Wuji dexterous hands.
- **Qualitative Evaluation (Figure 4):** On canonical dexterous poses, the learning-based retargeter generally provided more reliable pose reproduction and exhibited less unwanted finger coupling compared to the optimization-based baseline.
- **Teleoperation Task Evaluation (Table II):** Performance was assessed on three dexterity-critical sub-tasks:
 - **Scanner Triggering:** Our learning-based method achieved 20/30 successes, compared to 14/30 for the optimization-based method, for actuating the scanner trigger five consecutive times with 3-second holds.
 - **Hanger Stabilization:** Both learning-based (20/30) and optimization-based (21/30) methods showed comparable performance in maintaining a stable grasp on a hanger during wrist rotations.
 - **Doll Grasping (with a 3x larger doll):** Our learning-based method achieved 29/30 successes, outperforming the optimization-based method (23/30).
- **Efficiency:** The learning-based model is a lightweight MLP, trained with approximately 20k frames (collected in ~20 minutes), and provides constant-time inference.

#### 4.3. Human Data for Policy Generalization

- **Task:** The PickBread task was used to evaluate generalization.
- **Data:** A base robot dataset (50 episodes, fixed setup) and diverse human datasets (100 episodes for Position Variation, 300 for Object Variation, 300 for Background Variation) were collected.
- **Baselines:** "RobotOnly" (trained on robot base data), "Ours" (two-stage human pre-training, robot fine-tuning), and "Mix" (naive mixing of human and robot data, which failed to converge).
- **Generalization Settings:** Policies were evaluated on "Seen" (matching robot training data), "UnseenPos" (randomized object positions), "UnseenObj" (novel object instances), and "UnseenBg" (new background textures).
- **Results (Table III):**
 - **Seen Setting:** Both RobotOnly (29/30) and Ours (30/30) performed well.
 - **UnseenPos:** RobotOnly achieved 12/30, while Ours achieved 21/30 successes.
 - **UnseenObj:** RobotOnly achieved 10/30, while Ours achieved 20/30 successes.
 - **UnseenBg:** RobotOnly achieved 9/30, while Ours achieved 25/30 successes.
- **Conclusion:** The results indicated that the two-stage approach leveraging human data significantly improved policy generalization across all out-of-distribution settings, nearly doubling success rates in some cases compared to training on robot data alone. The failure of the "Mix" baseline highlighted the importance of the sequential training strategy in managing the human-robot embodiment gap. This suggests that pre-training on diverse human data helps policies learn more robust visual features and high-level motion priors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HumDex system and the associated methodologies contribute to the field of humanoid robotics by addressing several critical challenges in dexterous manipulation and imitation learning.

- **Enhanced Data Collection for Complex Tasks:** HumDex introduces a portable and precise teleoperation system that significantly improves the efficiency and success rate of data collection for humanoid whole-body dexterous manipulation. By leveraging IMU-based tracking, it overcomes limitations such as occlusion and infrastructure dependency, which have hindered the collection of high-quality demonstrations for tasks involving fine-grained dexterity, bimanual coordination, and whole-body movement. This enables the acquisition of data for tasks previously difficult or infeasible with existing systems, such as tool manipulation causing self-occlusion.
- **Improved Dexterous Hand Control:** The development of a learning-based hand retargeting method provides smoother, more natural, and more reliable control of complex robot hands. This approach reduces the need for manual tuning and computational overhead associated with traditional optimization-based methods, making dexterous manipulation more accessible and robust in real-world deployments.
- **Effective Utilization of Human Data:** The proposed two-stage imitation learning framework demonstrates a practical strategy for leveraging diverse human motion data to improve policy generalization for humanoid loco-manipulation. By pre-training on human data to learn broad motion and perception priors and then fine-tuning on limited robot data to adapt to specific robot embodiment, the framework effectively bridges the human-robot gap. This approach substantially reduces the need for extensive, costly robot-specific data collection for every new task variation, offering a pathway toward more scalable robot learning.
- **Advancement Towards General-Purpose Humanoid Manipulation:** The combined contributions of a robust teleoperation system and a human-data-leveraging learning framework represent a step forward in enabling humanoid robots to perform a wider array of complex, real-world tasks. The ability to generalize to unseen object positions, categories, and backgrounds with minimal robot-specific data is crucial for deploying robots in dynamic and unstructured environments.
- **Open-Sourced Contribution:** The commitment to open-sourcing the entire system is significant, as it promotes reproducibility, encourages further research, and facilitates community-driven development in humanoid dexterous manipulation.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as the scale of training data and hardware payload, the advancements presented offer a foundation for future research in general-purpose humanoid manipulation.
