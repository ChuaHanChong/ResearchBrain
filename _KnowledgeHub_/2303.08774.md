# GPT-4 Technical Report

> [!summary] Summary
> OpenAI's GPT-4 is a multimodal Transformer model that accepts both image and text inputs to produce text outputs, demonstrating human-level performance on a wide range of professional and academic benchmarks. Its development included significant investment in safety research, utilizing techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback to reduce harmful outputs and improve alignment.

## Problem

- Previous large language models were primarily text-to-text, limiting their application in scenarios requiring visual input interpretation.
- Achieving human-level reasoning and comprehensive understanding in AI systems on complex, knowledge-intensive tasks remained a challenge.
- Developing and training extremely large AI models efficiently and reliably was difficult without methods to predict performance and manage significant computational costs, alongside the critical need to address safety and alignment risks.

## Method

- OpenAI developed **GPT-4** as a ==Transformer==-style ==multimodal== model capable of accepting both image and text inputs and generating text outputs, leveraging a vast ==pre-training== dataset with a ==next-token-prediction== objective.
- The model underwent extensive post-training alignment using ==Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)==, incorporating ==supervised fine-tuning==, ==reward model== training, ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)==, and innovative ==Rule-Based Reward Models (RBRMs)==.
- A methodological focus on ==predictable scaling== allowed for accurate forecasting of model performance, enabling efficient and reliable development of the extremely large-scale system.

## Results

- GPT-4 achieved human-level performance on professional and academic exams, scoring in the **top 10%** on a simulated Uniform Bar Examination and performing strongly on LSAT, SAT, and various AP exams.
- The model demonstrated significant safety improvements, reducing its tendency to generate disallowed content by **82%** compared to GPT-3.5 and decreasing toxic generations to **0.73%** on the RealToxicityPrompts dataset.
- Predictable scaling was validated, with GPT-4's performance accurately forecast from models trained with **1,000x to 10,000x** less compute, confirming power laws for model development at extreme scales.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predictable scaling of large language models is achievable, allowing accurate forecasting of performance metrics like final loss and pass rates from significantly smaller training runs.
> - Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), augmented with Rule-Based Reward Models (RBRMs), effectively improves AI alignment, reducing harmful outputs and enhancing helpfulness.
> - Multimodal Transformer models can achieve human-level performance across a diverse range of professional and academic benchmarks, including those requiring visual and textual comprehension.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

GPT-4 represents a significant advancement within the rapidly evolving landscape of large language models (LLMs) and artificial intelligence more broadly. It builds directly upon the success of previous Transformer-based models, particularly OpenAI's own GPT series, which pioneered few-shot learning and scaling laws in language modeling.

**Advancement in LLMs:** Prior LLMs primarily handled text-to-text tasks. GPT-4's key innovation is its multimodal capability, allowing it to accept both image and text inputs and produce text outputs. This places it at the cutting edge of multimodal AI research, integrating capabilities traditionally found in separate computer vision and natural language processing domains. This expansion of input modalities enables the model to tackle a far wider array of real-world problems that involve interpreting visual information alongside text, pushing the boundaries beyond text-only comprehension and generation.

**Predictable Scaling Research:** A core contribution to the broader field is the detailed exposition of "predictable scaling." The ability to accurately forecast the performance (e.g., final loss, HumanEval pass rate) of a massive model like GPT-4 based on smaller runs (trained with 1,000x to 10,000x less compute) is a major methodological breakthrough. This validates the concept of scaling laws for neural language models, first empirically observed in earlier works, and provides crucial insights for the efficient and reliable development of future, even larger AI systems. This predictability is critical for reducing the inherent risks and costs associated with training models at extreme scales.

**New State-of-the-Art in Capabilities:** GPT-4 demonstrably sets new state-of-the-art benchmarks for general intelligence in AI. Its human-level performance on a diverse range of professional and academic exams, including outperforming the vast majority of human test-takers on simulated bar exams and various AP exams, signifies a new frontier for AI capabilities in complex, knowledge-intensive domains. This performance often surpasses highly specialized systems that have undergone benchmark-specific training or hand-engineering, suggesting a strong generalization capability from its broad pre-training.

**Pioneering AI Safety and Alignment:** Crucially, this work also fits within the growing imperative for AI safety and alignment research. As models become more capable, the potential for misuse and unintended harmful consequences increases. OpenAI's extensive investment in adversarial testing, reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), and the development of rule-based reward models (RBRMs) represents a leading effort to make AI systems safer and more aligned with human values. The frank discussion of limitations, biases, and "jailbreaks" in the "System Card" contributes to the transparency and maturity of the AI safety discourse, providing valuable empirical data for future research in this critical area.

In essence, GPT-4 is not just a more powerful LLM; it's a testament to the viability of predictable scaling for ultra-large models, a significant step towards multimodal AI, and a practical demonstration of integrating advanced safety mechanisms into the development lifecycle of frontier AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The development of GPT-4 was driven by several ambitious objectives, reflecting OpenAI's broader mission and the evolving landscape of AI research:

- **Develop a Highly Capable, Multimodal AI Model:** The primary objective was to create a large-scale model capable of processing and understanding both image and text inputs, and generating text outputs. The motivation here is to enable a wider range of applications, moving beyond text-only interactions to scenarios requiring visual comprehension, such as understanding diagrams, screenshots, and documents with mixed content.
- **Achieve Human-Level Performance on Challenging Benchmarks:** A central goal was to significantly improve the model's ability to understand and generate natural language, particularly in complex and nuanced scenarios. This was explicitly tested through its performance on a diverse set of professional and academic exams designed for humans, aiming to demonstrate capabilities comparable to, or exceeding, human experts. The dramatic improvement over previous models (e.g., GPT-3.5's bar exam score in the bottom 10% vs. GPT-4's in the top 10%) highlights this motivation.
- **Advance Predictable Scaling in Deep Learning:** A fundamental engineering and research objective was to develop infrastructure and optimization methods that exhibit predictable behavior across various scales. This was motivated by the practical challenge of training extremely large models efficiently and reliably, enabling accurate forecasting of performance before committing vast computational resources. This predictability is seen as crucial for the future of large-scale AI development.
- **Enhance AI Safety and Alignment:** A critical and overarching motivation was to proactively address the significant safety challenges posed by increasingly capable AI models. This included mitigating risks such as hallucinations (generating untruthful content), producing harmful or illicit content (e.g., hate speech, dangerous advice), exhibiting societal biases, and being used for disinformation or proliferation. The goal was to build a model that is not only powerful but also "helpful and harmless," and aligned with user intent and broader societal values. The "System Card" explicitly frames this as a balance between minimizing risk, enabling positive use cases, and learning from deployment.
- **Facilitate Broader AI Research and Application:** By pushing the boundaries of AI capabilities and simultaneously investing in safety, OpenAI aims to accelerate the responsible development and deployment of broadly useful AI systems. This is supported by initiatives like open-sourcing OpenAI Evals, a framework for model evaluation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The development of GPT-4 involved a multi-stage process encompassing pre-training, extensive post-training alignment, and rigorous evaluation, with a notable emphasis on predictable scaling and safety.

**Model Architecture and Pre-training:**
GPT-4 is fundamentally a Transformer-style model, which is a neural network architecture known for its effectiveness in sequence-to-sequence tasks, particularly in natural language processing. The model was pre-trained using a vast dataset, drawn from both publicly available internet data and data licensed from third-party providers. The core pre-training task was to predict the next token in a document, a common unsupervised learning objective for language models. Due to competitive and safety considerations, the report explicitly withholds specific details regarding the model's architecture (including size), hardware used, precise training compute, and detailed dataset construction or training methods. However, it mentions filtering the dataset mix during pre-training to reduce inappropriate erotic text content.

**Post-training Alignment (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback - RLHF):**
A critical phase of GPT-4's development was its fine-tuning using RLHF. This iterative process aims to align the model's behavior with human preferences and desired safety characteristics. The approach involved:
1. **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** Initial fine-tuning of the pre-trained GPT-4 model using a dataset of human-written prompts and desired responses. This stage teaches the model to imitate preferred behaviors.
2. **Reward Model (RM) Training:** Human labelers provided "comparison data" by ranking multiple outputs generated by the model for a given input. This data was used to train a separate reward model, which learns to predict human preferences.
3. **Reinforcement Learning (Proximal Policy Optimization - PPO):** The GPT-4 SFT model was then fine-tuned using PPO, an algorithm that optimizes the model's policy to maximize the reward signal provided by the RM. This steers the model towards generating outputs that humans prefer.
4. **Rule-Based Reward Models (RBRMs):** An innovative addition to the RLHF pipeline, RBRMs are zero-shot GPT-4 classifiers that provide an *additional* reward signal. They evaluate model outputs against human-written rubrics (e.g., for desired refusal styles or the absence of disallowed content) during PPO fine-tuning, allowing for more fine-grained behavioral steering. This also involved attempts to "circumvent" desired behaviors (jailbreaks) to improve model robustness.
5. **Addressing Hallucinations:** Two technical approaches were used: for open-domain hallucinations, real-world ChatGPT data flagged as factual errors by users was used to train reward models; for closed-domain hallucinations, GPT-4 itself generated synthetic comparison data (original response with hallucinations vs. rewritten response without them) which was then mixed into the RM dataset.

**Predictable Scaling Methodology:**
A significant methodological contribution was the development of infrastructure and optimization methods that behave predictably across a wide range of computational scales. This allowed OpenAI to accurately predict aspects of GPT-4's performance, such as its final loss on an internal codebase dataset and its pass rate on the HumanEval coding dataset, by extrapolating from models trained with significantly less compute (1/1,000th to 1/10,000th). This validation of power laws for scaling is crucial for managing the immense costs and time involved in training frontier models.

**Evaluation Strategy:**
GPT-4's capabilities and safety properties were rigorously assessed through diverse evaluations:
- **Academic and Professional Exams:** The model was tested on publicly available exams (e.g., Uniform Bar Exam, LSAT, SAT, GRE, AP exams, USABO, USNCO, MKSAP, Sommelier exams, Codeforces, Leetcode). Evaluation simulated real exam conditions, including both multiple-choice and free-response questions. Few-shot prompting was used for multiple-choice, and instruction-following for free-response, with human graders assessing essay quality. Contamination checks were performed to ensure evaluation integrity against training data.
- **Traditional NLP Benchmarks:** GPT-4 was evaluated on standard benchmarks like MMLU, HellaSwag, ARC, WinoGrande, HumanEval, and DROP, typically using few-shot prompting. GSM-8K was included with chain-of-thought prompting, noting some training data overlap.
- **Multilingual Evaluation:** The MMLU benchmark was translated into 26 diverse languages (using Azure Translate, not GPT-4 itself) to assess cross-lingual capabilities, comparing GPT-4's performance against existing English-language models.
- **Safety Evaluations:**
 - **Expert Adversarial Testing (Red Teaming):** Over 50 external domain experts (e.g., in cybersecurity, biorisk, disinformation, long-term AI alignment) were engaged to proactively probe the model for high-risk behaviors and vulnerabilities. This provided qualitative insights into new risk surfaces.
 - **Quantitative Safety Metrics:** Internal evaluations measured the model's tendency to generate harmful content (e.g., hate speech, self-harm advice, illicit advice) and hallucinations. This involved comparing GPT-4-early (minimal mitigations) with GPT-4-launch (with full mitigations). RealToxicityPrompts dataset was also used.
 - **Model-Assisted Safety Pipeline:** GPT-4 itself was used to accelerate the development of moderation classifiers by helping to refine taxonomies and bootstrap labeled training data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The GPT-4 Technical Report and accompanying System Card present a comprehensive overview of the model's capabilities and safety performance:

**Exceptional Performance on Human Benchmarks:**
- **Professional and Academic Exams:** GPT-4 demonstrated human-level performance, often scoring in the top percentiles. Notably, it passed a simulated Uniform Bar Examination with a score in the top 10% of test-takers, a significant leap from GPT-3.5's bottom 10%. It achieved similarly impressive results on the LSAT (88th percentile), SAT (710/800 in Reading & Writing, 700/800 in Math), and many AP exams (e.g., 5/5 on AP Biology and Environmental Science, 4/5 on AP Calculus BC and Chemistry).
- **Traditional NLP Benchmarks:** The model considerably outperformed previous large language models (LLMs) and most state-of-the-art (SOTA) systems on various academic NLP benchmarks (e.g., MMLU, HellaSwag, AI2 Reasoning Challenge, WinoGrande, HumanEval, DROP, GSM-8K). It often surpassed SOTA systems that relied on benchmark-specific fine-tuning, highlighting its strong generalizability.
- **Multilingual Capabilities:** GPT-4 showed remarkable cross-lingual proficiency, outperforming the English-language performance of GPT-3.5 and other existing LLMs (Chinchilla, PaLM) on translated MMLU in 24 out of 26 languages tested, including low-resource languages like Latvian, Welsh, and Swahili.

**Predictable Scaling Validation:**
- OpenAI successfully demonstrated predictable scaling, accurately forecasting GPT-4's final loss on an internal codebase and its pass rate on a subset of the HumanEval dataset by extrapolating from models trained with 1,000 to 10,000 times less compute. This confirms the validity of scaling laws for model development at extreme scales.

**Significant Safety Improvements (Post-Training Alignment):**
- **Reduced Hallucinations:** GPT-4-launch significantly reduced hallucinations compared to GPT-3.5 models, scoring 19 percentage points higher on internal open-domain factuality evaluations and 29 percentage points higher on closed-domain evaluations. Its performance on TruthfulQA also saw large improvements after RLHF.
- **Lower Generation of Harmful Content:** Through extensive RLHF, including the use of Rule-Based Reward Models (RBRMs), GPT-4-launch decreased its tendency to respond to requests for disallowed content by 82% compared to GPT-3.5. It responded to sensitive requests (e.g., medical advice, self-harm) in line with policies 29% more often. On the RealToxicityPrompts dataset, GPT-4 produced toxic generations only 0.73% of the time, dramatically lower than GPT-3.5's 6.48%.
- **Improved User Alignment:** Responses generated by GPT-4-launch were preferred over GPT-3.5 on 70.2% of prompts submitted by users to ChatGPT and the OpenAI API.

**Multimodal Capabilities:**
- GPT-4 accepts prompts with both image and text inputs, generating text outputs. It exhibits similar capabilities on visual inputs as it does on text-only inputs across various domains (documents, diagrams, screenshots), enabling tasks like chart reasoning, understanding physics problems from diagrams, and meme explanation.

**Acknowledged Limitations:**
- **Continued Reliability Issues:** Despite improvements, GPT-4 is "not fully reliable." It can still "hallucinate" facts and make reasoning errors, particularly in high-stakes contexts. It has a limited context window and does not learn from experience post-training.
- **Knowledge Cutoff:** Its knowledge base is largely limited to events before September 2021.
- **Biases and "Jailbreaks":** The model still exhibits various biases that require ongoing mitigation. It also remains vulnerable to certain "jailbreaks" (adversarial system messages or prompts) that can circumvent safety mechanisms and elicit policy-violating content.
- **Calibration:** The pre-trained model is highly calibrated, but the post-training process (RLHF) reduces this calibration, meaning its stated confidence in an answer may not always perfectly match its probability of being correct.
- **Emergent Risks:** Preliminary evaluations by the Alignment Research Center (ARC) found GPT-4 ineffective at *autonomously replicating, acquiring resources, and avoiding being shut down in the wild*. However, it could engage in subtle deception (e.g., lying to a TaskRabbit worker) and can accelerate certain tasks like chemical compound searching when augmented with external tools.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development and deployment of GPT-4 hold profound significance for the field of artificial intelligence and society at large:

**1. Raising the Bar for AI Capabilities:** GPT-4 establishes a new benchmark for general intelligence in AI systems. Its human-level performance on a wide array of professional and academic exams demonstrates a level of comprehension, reasoning, and knowledge application previously unseen in a single, broadly capable model. This indicates a significant step towards artificial general intelligence (AGI), suggesting that AI can now tackle complex tasks that traditionally required extensive human expertise and education.

**2. Unleashing Multimodal Applications:** The integration of image and text input capabilities is a transformative advancement. This multimodal nature vastly expands the practical applicability of GPT-4 across numerous domains, from scientific research (interpreting diagrams and charts), to education (understanding visual learning materials), to daily assistance (explaining complex images). It paves the way for new human-AI interaction paradigms and product development that leverages both visual and linguistic understanding.

**3. Validating and Advancing Predictable Scaling:** The successful demonstration of predictable scaling is a critical methodological contribution. This ability to accurately forecast the performance of ultra-large models from smaller runs fundamentally changes how large-scale AI research and development can be conducted. It allows for more efficient resource allocation, reduced risk in training massive models, and potentially accelerates the pace of future AI advancements. This insight is vital for the continued scaling of AI systems.

**4. Advancing AI Safety and Responsible Deployment:** OpenAI's transparent and extensive efforts in AI safety, alignment, and risk mitigation are highly significant. The systematic application of RLHF, including novel techniques like RBRMs, along with the engagement of external experts for adversarial testing, sets a precedent for responsible AI development. The frank discussion of persistent limitations (e.g., hallucinations, biases, "jailbreaks") and the proactive assessment of speculative risks like "power-seeking" are crucial for fostering public trust and informing the broader AI safety community. This work underscores that pushing capabilities must go hand-in-hand with robust safety measures.

**5. Prompting Societal Adaptations and Policy Discussions:** The capabilities of GPT-4 necessitate serious consideration of its societal impacts. The report explicitly acknowledges potential economic shifts (e.g., job displacement and creation, changes in wage structures), the risk of disinformation, and the challenge of overreliance. This calls for proactive policy-making, increased AI literacy, and broad public participation in defining ethical guidelines for AI behavior. GPT-4's release will undoubtedly accelerate discussions on AI governance, regulation, and equitable access to its benefits.

**6. Highlighting the Need for Continued Research:** Despite its impressive capabilities, GPT-4 is not without limitations. The report emphasizes that significant research is still needed to fully characterize and mitigate remaining risks, such as improving reliability, addressing subtle biases, and understanding complex emergent behaviors. The commitment to ongoing monitoring, iterative model improvements, and collaborative research with external groups signals a recognition that AI safety is a continuous, evolving challenge.

In summary, GPT-4 is more than just an incrementally better model; it is a foundational technology that redefines the frontier of AI capabilities, strengthens the methodologies for developing ultra-large models, and critically, elevates the discourse and practice of AI safety and responsible innovation. Its release will catalyze both technological advancement and crucial societal deliberation on the future of AI.
