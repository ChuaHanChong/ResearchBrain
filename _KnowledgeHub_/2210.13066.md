# DaXBench: Benchmarking Deformable Object Manipulation with Differentiable Physics

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the National University of Singapore and Sea AI Lab developed DaXBench, a differentiable simulation framework using JAX for deformable object manipulation across liquid, rope, and cloth. This benchmark evaluates planning, imitation learning, and reinforcement learning algorithms, showing differentiable physics benefits long-horizon, low-level control tasks and enables sim-to-real transfer with a Kinova Gen 3 robot.

## Problem

- Deformable object manipulation (DOM) presents significant challenges in robotics due to high-dimensional state/action spaces and complex, non-linear dynamics.
- Existing DOM simulators and benchmarks often lack differentiability, specialize in a single type of deformable object, or offer insufficient support for diverse manipulation tasks and algorithms.
- While differentiable physics shows promise for DOM control policies, current differentiable simulators are typically limited to modeling only one type of deformable material.

## Method

- Introduced DaXBench, a general-purpose differentiable simulation framework built on JAX, which supports diverse deformable objects including liquid, rope, cloth, and elasto-plastic materials.
- Integrated memory and efficiency optimizations, such as Lazy Dynamic Update and Checkpointing, into the custom DaX simulator to manage the computational demands of long-horizon differentiable physics tasks.
- Provided a comprehensive set of DOM tasks wrapped with the OpenAI Gym API, including novel long-horizon and low-level control tasks, for benchmarking various algorithmic paradigms.

## Results

- Differentiable Reinforcement Learning (APG) outperformed PPO on long-horizon, low-level control tasks (e.g., "Whip-Rope"), while PPO showed better performance on macro-action tasks.
- Imitation Learning via Differentiable physics (ILD) achieved superior performance with substantially fewer expert demonstrations compared to non-differentiable Transporter for macro-action tasks.
- Differentiable planning (diff-CEM-MPC) consistently matched or exceeded CEM-MPC, but its success was highly reliant on good initialization, with randomly initialized diff-MPC largely failing.
- Real-world experiments validated DaX's simulated dynamics, enabling a Kinova Gen 3 robot to successfully plan and execute a "Push-Rope" task, with observed trajectories aligning with simulation predictions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Differentiable physics significantly enhances DOM methods, particularly for tasks requiring long-horizon, low-level controls, and improves imitation learning with fewer expert demonstrations.
> - The effectiveness of differentiable methods is context-dependent: they can struggle with exploration in macro-action tasks but improve planning when provided with good initialization.
> - DaXBench demonstrated successful sim-to-real transfer for deformable object manipulation, evidenced by a Kinova Gen 3 robot executing a rope pushing task with dynamics fidelity similar to simulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deformable object manipulation (DOM) presents a significant challenge in robotics due to the high-dimensional state and action spaces involved, as well as the complex, often non-linear, dynamics of deformable materials. This field has broad applications, ranging from domestic tasks like laundry folding to industrial processes. Existing research in DOM has led to the development of various simulators and benchmarks, such as SoftGym and ThreeDWorld. However, these tools often possess limitations, including a lack of differentiability, specialization in a single type of deformable object, or insufficient support for diverse manipulation tasks and algorithms.

A promising direction in developing control policies for deformable objects is differentiable physics, which represents physical laws as differentiable computational graphs. This approach allows for the optimization of control policies using analytical gradients, potentially improving sample efficiency in learning algorithms. While several differentiable DOM simulators have emerged (e.g., ChainQueen, Diff-PDE, PlasticineLab, DiSECt, DiffSim), each typically focuses on modeling only a single type of deformable object, thereby limiting their scope for comprehensive benchmarking across various material types.

This work, DaXBench, is positioned to address these gaps. It aims to provide a general-purpose, differentiable simulation framework that supports a wide range of deformable objects (fluid, rope, cloth, elasto-plastic materials) and a diverse set of manipulation tasks. By integrating recent advances in deformable object simulation with high-performance computational frameworks like JAX, DaXBench seeks to offer a standardized platform for evaluating different DOM methods, including planning, imitation learning (IL), and reinforcement learning (RL), particularly those leveraging differentiable physics. The authors emphasize the need for such a unified platform to enable fair comparisons and foster advancements in DOM techniques.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce DaXBench, a differentiable simulation framework designed for deformable object manipulation (DOM), serving as a comprehensive and standardized benchmark for the research community.

The motivation for developing DaXBench stems from several limitations observed in the existing DOM research landscape:

- **Lack of General-Purpose Benchmarks:** Current DOM benchmarks often focus on a specific type of deformable object, such as liquid, fabric, or elastoplastic materials. This specialization restricts the ability to evaluate and compare DOM methods across a broad spectrum of material behaviors within a single framework. DaXBench aims to overcome this by supporting diverse object types, including liquid, rope, cloth, and elasto-plastic materials.
- **Need for Differentiable Simulation:** Differentiable physics is recognized as a promising avenue for developing control policies for deformable objects due to its potential for improved sample efficiency through analytical gradients. While some differentiable simulators exist, they are typically limited to a single object type. DaXBench is motivated by the need for a general-purpose differentiable simulation platform that can accommodate and facilitate the development of these gradient-based DOM methods.
- **Comprehensive Algorithm Evaluation:** Existing benchmarks may not be designed to thoroughly evaluate the wide array of DOM algorithms, which span planning, imitation learning, and reinforcement learning paradigms. DaXBench seeks to provide a versatile environment suitable for benchmarking methods from all these categories, including both traditional and differentiable physics-based approaches.
- **Addressing Task Complexity and Horizon:** Many existing DOM benchmark tasks are characterized by relatively short horizons or rely on high-level macro-actions, which may not fully capture the challenges of real-world manipulation. DaXBench is motivated to introduce more challenging tasks, including novel long-horizon tasks and those requiring finer-grained, low-level controls, to better evaluate the scalability and dexterity of DOM algorithms. Examples include "Whip-Rope" and "Pour-Soup," which require continuous adjustment and precise control.
- **Leveraging High-Performance Computing:** The complexity of DOM tasks, coupled with the computational demands of differentiable physics, necessitates efficient simulation. DaXBench is motivated to integrate recent advances in deformable object simulation algorithms with high-performance computational frameworks like JAX, enabling efficient automatic differentiation and parallelization across multiple accelerators (e.g., GPUs). This aims to accelerate algorithm development and experimentation.
- **Standardization and Integration:** By wrapping all DOM tasks with the OpenAI Gym API, the project aims to provide a standardized interface that allows for easy integration with existing and new DOM algorithms, thereby streamlining the development and evaluation process for researchers.

Overall, DaXBench's motivation is to provide a comprehensive, flexible, efficient, and standardized simulation environment that can drive forward the development and rigorous evaluation of advanced deformable object manipulation techniques.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DaXBench framework is designed as a general-purpose differentiable simulation platform for deformable object manipulation (DOM), integrating a custom differentiable simulator named DaX with a comprehensive suite of benchmark tasks.

#### 3.1. DaX Simulator Design

DaX is implemented using JAX, a computational framework known for its capabilities in automatic differentiation and high-performance parallelization, particularly on hardware accelerators like GPUs. This integration allows for efficient end-to-end gradient computation.

- **State Representations and Dynamic Models:** DaX employs distinct modeling approaches tailored to the specific deformation characteristics of different deformable object types:
 - **Liquid and Rope:** For objects undergoing significant deformation, such as liquid and rope, DaX uses a particle-based representation. Their dynamics are modeled using the Moving Least Square Material Point Method (MLS-MPM), which is capable of simulating severe deformations by tracking individual particles and their interactions.
 - **Cloth:** For objects with more limited deformation, such as cloth, DaX uses a mesh-based representation. Its dynamics are modeled with a mass-spring system, which is less computationally intensive while still accurately capturing the essential deformation behavior of fabric.
- **Customization Capabilities:** The simulator is designed to be highly customizable, allowing users to easily add objects of arbitrary shapes and varying rigidness. Primitive actions can also be customized, providing flexibility for diverse research needs.
- **Memory and Efficiency Optimizations:** To manage the computational and memory demands of differentiable physics, particularly for long-horizon tasks and high-dimensional deformable objects, DaX incorporates two primary optimization techniques:
 - **Lazy Dynamic Update:** This technique optimizes MPM's time and memory consumption by only updating the dynamics within a small "active region" that is directly affected by the manipulation at a given timestep. The system detects this region, shifts it to the origin for computation, and then shifts the updated region back to its original coordinates, significantly reducing unnecessary computations across the entire environment.
 - **Checkpointing Method:** To mitigate the large memory consumption associated with storing all intermediate values for back-propagation in long-horizon tasks, DaX employs a checkpointing strategy. It stores only a few selected states (checkpoints) during the forward pass. During the backward pass, segments of the forward values are recomputed in reverse order from these checkpoints, trading a modest increase in computation time for substantial memory savings.
- **Gradient Continuity:** The effectiveness of differentiable simulators relies on accurate gradients. DaX addresses potential issues of discontinuous gradients, which can arise when a gripper is not in contact with the object, by implementing local adjustments. These adjustments ensure that the gripper consistently maintains contact with the deformable objects, thereby ensuring end-to-end gradient continuity and improving the accuracy of gradient estimation.

#### 3.2. Benchmark Task Design

DaXBench provides a comprehensive set of DOM tasks wrapped with the OpenAI Gym API, facilitating ease of integration for algorithms.

- **Diverse Task Coverage:** The benchmark includes tasks for various deformable materials:
 - **Liquid Manipulation:** "Pour-Water" and "Pour-Soup." The agent controls the linear and angular velocity of a gripper holding a bowl. "Pour-Soup" is more challenging due to interactions with solid ingredients.
 - **Rope Manipulation:** "Push-Rope" (pushing to a target configuration with macro-actions) and "Whip-Rope" (whipping to a target configuration with low-level velocity controls).
 - **Cloth Manipulation:** "Fold-Cloth" (1 or 3 folds), "Fold-T-shirt," and "Unfold-Cloth" (1 or 3 folds). These tasks primarily use pick-and-place macro-actions.
- **Long-Horizon and Low-Level Control Tasks:** To evaluate the scalability and precision of DOM methods, DaXBench introduces tasks with longer horizons and those that require fine-grained, low-level controls. Examples include "Pour-Water" (100 steps) and "Whip-Rope" (70 steps), which are explicitly designed to be unsuitable for abstract macro-actions due to the need for continuous momentum adjustments.
- **Reward Function Definition:** For each task, the goal is defined by the desired final positions of the object's particles. The primary reward function, $r_{gt}$, is based on the exponential of the negative distance between the current object state and the goal state. An auxiliary reward, $r_{aux}$, is added during training to encourage the robot to contact the object, based on the L2 distance between the end-effector and the object. Only $r_{gt}$ is used during evaluation.

#### 3.3. Benchmarked Algorithms

The study benchmarks eight representative DOM methods across three algorithmic paradigms, comparing their performance with and without the use of analytical gradients from differentiable physics:

- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) (non-differentiable), Short-Horizon Actor-Critic (SHAC), and Analytic Policy Gradients (APG) (both utilizing analytical gradients).
- **Imitation Learning (IL):** Transporter (non-differentiable, typically using pick-and-place macro-actions) and Imitation Learning via Differentiable physics (ILD) (utilizing differentiable dynamics).
- **Planning:** Cross Entropy Method with Model Predictive Control (CEM-MPC) (gradient-free), differentiable Model Predictive Control (diff-MPC) (randomly initialized), and diff-CEM-MPC (differentiable MPC initialized with CEM-MPC).

Experiments were conducted over multiple seeds and rollouts to provide statistical robustness, with specific attention to how differentiability impacts performance across different task types and control levels.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted using DaXBench provided insights into the capabilities and limitations of various DOM methods, particularly highlighting the role of differentiable physics.

#### 4.1. Reinforcement Learning (RL) Methods

- **PPO vs. Differentiable RL:** The study benchmarked PPO (non-differentiable), SHAC, and APG (differentiable).
 - **Macro-Action-Based Tasks (Short-Horizon):** For most short-horizon tasks relying on high-level macro-actions (e.g., cloth folding/unfolding), PPO consistently demonstrated better performance than the differentiable RL methods (SHAC and APG).
 - **Low-Level Control Tasks (Long-Horizon):** In contrast, for long-horizon tasks requiring fine-grained, low-level controls (e.g., "Whip-Rope"), differentiable RL methods, especially APG, significantly outperformed PPO.
- **Challenges for Differentiable RL:**
 - **Exploration:** The authors suggest that the primary limiting factor for differentiable RL in macro-action tasks is a lack of effective exploration. The high-dimensional state space and complex, often non-smooth or non-convex optimization landscapes of DOM tasks make it challenging for gradient-based methods to escape local optima without robust exploration strategies. Differentiable RL methods, which largely rely on simulation gradients without strong entropy regularization, can collapse to local optima.
 - **Optimization Landscape Sensitivity:** Differentiable-physics-based RL methods are sensitive to the smoothness and convexity of the optimization landscape. They perform well on tasks where the optimal control sequence involves smooth motions within a well-behaved gradient space (e.g., "Whip-Rope").

#### 4.2. Imitation Learning (IL) Methods

- **Transporter vs. ILD:** The study compared Transporter (non-differentiable, particle-based) and ILD (differentiable).
 - **Performance Gain from Differentiability:** For high-level macro-action-based tasks, ILD significantly outperformed Transporter across all evaluated tasks. This improvement was achieved with a substantially smaller number of expert demonstrations.
 - **Explanation:** The simulation gradients in ILD provide additional information, enabling the model to optimize trajectories closer to expert demonstrations. The gradients, derived from globally optimal expert trajectories at the current and subsequent steps, offer more direct guidance than the current-step rewards used in some RL settings, helping to mitigate issues like exploding/vanishing gradients and exploration challenges.
- **Limitations of ILD:** ILD exhibited poor performance on tasks with unbalanced state representations, such as "Pour-Water" and "Pour-Soup," where a large number of particle features (e.g., 1000x3) could overwhelm the signals from a few gripper states (e.g., 6). This suggests a need for more balanced state representations or weighting strategies.
- **Transporter with RGB Input:** An observation was made that explicitly rendering particles into RGB images significantly improved Transporter's performance compared to using particle-based height maps alone.

#### 4.3. Planning Methods

- **CEM-MPC vs. Differentiable MPC:** The study benchmarked CEM-MPC (gradient-free), diff-MPC (differentiable, random initialization), and diff-CEM-MPC (differentiable, initialized by CEM-MPC).
 - **Benefit of Differentiability with Good Initialization:** diff-CEM-MPC consistently performed better than or on par with CEM-MPC. This indicates that simulation gradients can provide effective local optimization signals, reducing sample complexity when starting from a near-optimal control sequence.
 - **Importance of Initialization:** In contrast, diff-MPC, which used a random action sequence for initialization, largely failed across most tasks. This highlights that differentiable planning methods are highly sensitive to the initial control sequence and the characteristics of the optimization landscape (e.g., non-convexity, non-smoothness). A good initialization, potentially provided by a sampling-based method like CEM-MPC, is crucial for differentiable planning to avoid getting stuck in poor local optima.
 - **Simulation Gradients as Additional Signals:** The findings suggest that simulation gradients serve as valuable additional optimization signals, but their efficacy is critically dependent on a well-behaved local optimization landscape and a sufficiently close initial solution.

#### 4.4. Sim-to-Real Gap

- A real-world experiment was conducted using a Kinova Gen 3 robot for the "Push-Rope" task. CEM-MPC, leveraging DaX as its predictive model, successfully planned and executed actions. The resulting state trajectories in reality were observed to be similar to those predicted by DaX. This finding supports the fidelity and correctness of DaX's simulated dynamics and suggests the potential for sim-to-real transfer of policies developed within DaXBench.
- The authors also note that the differentiability of DaX facilitates system identification, a process that can further reduce the sim-to-real gap by enabling gradient-based parameter tuning through comparison of simulated and real-world observations.

In summary, the experiments reveal that differentiable physics can significantly enhance DOM methods, particularly for tasks requiring long-horizon, low-level controls, or when provided with good initialization or expert demonstrations. However, these methods can struggle with exploration and sensitivity to non-convex optimization landscapes, especially in tasks reliant on high-level macro-actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DaXBench represents a notable contribution to the field of deformable object manipulation (DOM) research, offering several significant aspects and potential impacts:

- **Unified and Comprehensive Benchmark:** DaXBench provides the first general-purpose differentiable simulation framework that simultaneously supports a diverse range of deformable materials, including liquid, rope, cloth, and elasto-plastic objects. This unified platform addresses a critical gap in existing DOM benchmarks, which often specialize in a single material type. It enables researchers to evaluate and compare DOM methods across a broader spectrum of challenges, fostering more holistic algorithmic development.
- **Advancement of Differentiable Physics in Robotics:** By being fully differentiable and highly parallelizable through its JAX implementation, DaXBench directly facilitates the development and rigorous evaluation of DOM methods that leverage differentiable physics. The detailed empirical study within the paper provides crucial insights into the benefits and limitations of gradient-based approaches across planning, reinforcement learning, and imitation learning paradigms, guiding future research in this promising area.
- **Enhanced Algorithmic Development and Evaluation:** The framework's integration with the OpenAI Gym API streamlines the process of developing and benchmarking new DOM algorithms. Its support for both high-level macro-actions and low-level controls, combined with the inclusion of challenging long-horizon tasks, allows for a more comprehensive assessment of algorithmic scalability, precision, and dexterity. This can accelerate the iteration cycle for new method development.
- **Efficiency and Scalability:** The adoption of JAX, coupled with memory optimization techniques like lazy dynamic updates and checkpointing, allows DaXBench to perform highly parallelized simulations with efficient automatic differentiation. This computational efficiency is crucial for tackling the high-dimensional and complex dynamics inherent in DOM tasks, thereby improving research productivity.
- **Bridge to Real-World Applications:** The demonstrated sim-to-real transfer capabilities, as evidenced by the successful deployment of a DaX-trained planning method on a real robot for rope manipulation, highlight the practical applicability of the simulation platform. The fidelity of DaX's dynamics suggests that policies developed within DaXBench could potentially be transferred to real-world robotic systems, an important step towards deployment in household and industrial settings. Furthermore, its differentiability opens avenues for system identification, which can further reduce the sim-to-real gap.
- **Resource for the Research Community:** The public availability of DaXBench's code and supplementary materials positions it as a valuable open-source tool. It provides a standardized environment, diverse tasks, and a robust simulation engine that can serve as a foundation for a wide array of future research in DOM, fostering collaboration and cumulative progress within the community.

In conclusion, DaXBench is a significant contribution that provides a robust, versatile, and efficient infrastructure for advancing the state of the art in deformable object manipulation, particularly for methods leveraging differentiable physics.
