# Humanoid Agent via Embodied Chain-of-Action Reasoning with Multimodal Foundation Models for Zero-Shot Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Humanoid-COA framework enables humanoid robots to execute complex loco-manipulation tasks from natural language instructions in a zero-shot manner, leveraging multimodal foundation models. It consistently surpassed baseline methods across manipulation, locomotion, and combined tasks, demonstrating robust performance in real-world, unstructured environments.

## Problem

- Integrating whole-body locomotion with dexterous manipulation for humanoid robots remains a challenge in real-world, high-dimensional scenarios.
- Translating abstract natural language instructions into physically feasible, low-level control for complex humanoid platforms often leads to brittleness or limited scalability.
- Existing robotic planning methods typically treat locomotion and manipulation in isolation, struggle with zero-shot generalization, or underutilize advanced reasoning capabilities of large language models.

## Method

- A novel **Humanoid-COA** framework integrates multimodal foundation models (GPT-4V for perception, GPT-4 for reasoning) within a perception-reasoning-action paradigm.
- Its core is an ==Embodied Chain-of-Action== (CoA) Reasoning mechanism, which iteratively decomposes natural language instructions into executable action sequences.
- CoA Reasoning explicitly incorporates object affordance analysis, region spatial reasoning, and whole-body movement inference to ensure physical feasibility, spatial awareness, and kinematic consistency for generated actions.

## Results

- Humanoid-COA consistently outperformed three baseline methods across simple and complex manipulation, locomotion, and combined loco-manipulation tasks on real-world Unitree H1-2 and G1 humanoid robots.
- The framework achieved success rates up to **96.6%** for simple tasks and maintained over **60%** success for highly complex, long-horizon, and occlusion-aware loco-manipulation scenarios, significantly exceeding baseline performances which often failed (e.g., **0%** for some baselines).
- An ablation study confirmed that all three components of the Embodied Chain-of-Action Reasoning (object affordance, spatial reasoning, whole-body movement inference) incrementally contribute to the framework's superior executability and success rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating object affordance, spatial reasoning, and whole-body movement inference within a Chain-of-Action reasoning mechanism is crucial for robust, physically grounded humanoid loco-manipulation.
> - Multimodal foundation models, when appropriately grounded through embodied reasoning, can enable zero-shot execution of complex, long-horizon tasks in unstructured real-world environments.
> - Iterative reasoning and plan adaptation based on new observations significantly improve humanoid reliability and adaptability in dynamic and partially observable scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics, particularly humanoid robotics, has long grappled with the challenge of "loco-manipulation," which involves seamlessly integrating whole-body locomotion with dexterous manipulation. This paper addresses a critical gap in this landscape, moving beyond isolated capabilities to enable coherent, generalizable, and zero-shot performance in complex, unstructured environments.

Historically, the challenge of interpreting human instructions for robots began with **semantic mapping** and **language-conditioned policies**. Early approaches translated natural language into symbolic task representations or directly grounded instructions into robot actions via imitation or reinforcement learning. While effective in constrained settings, these methods suffered from reliance on manual annotation, predefined task structures, and limited scalability.

The recent emergence of **foundation models** (e.g., large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs)) has revolutionized AI, offering transferable multimodal representations and powerful reasoning capabilities. In robotics, pioneers like **SayCan** and **PaLM-E** demonstrated the potential of these models to ground natural language instructions in robotic affordances, bridging high-level reasoning with low-level control. Subsequent works extended this to specific tasks such as zero-shot object-goal navigation, social navigation, and object grasping.

However, the authors identify significant limitations in these prior foundation model applications:
1. **Isolation of Tasks:** Most efforts remain confined to either locomotion *or* manipulation, but rarely both in a tightly integrated manner.
2. **Limited Embodiment:** A large portion of evaluations are conducted in simulation or on non-humanoid platforms (e.g., wheeled robots, quadruped robots), which do not fully capture the high-dimensional and tightly coupled challenges of humanoid loco-manipulation (e.g., maintaining dynamic balance while manipulating).
3. **Underutilization of Reasoning:** While LLMs are used for planning, some approaches, like Wang et al.'s LLM-based behavior planning framework, still rely on relatively direct mappings between instructions and a predefined behavior library, potentially underutilizing the deep, iterative reasoning capabilities of LLMs.

The presented work directly addresses these limitations. By proposing **Humanoid-COA**, the authors introduce the "first humanoid agent framework that integrates foundation model reasoning for zero-shot loco-manipulation under natural language instructions." This explicitly extends the power of foundation models to the complex, high-dimensional domain of humanoids, moving beyond isolated tasks to achieve true loco-manipulation. The core innovation, **Embodied Chain-of-Action (CoA) Reasoning**, represents a significant step forward from direct mapping or predefined templates, enabling a more nuanced and physically grounded decomposition of high-level human intent. This positions Humanoid-COA as a crucial development towards building more autonomous, versatile, and adaptable humanoid robots that can operate in dynamic, unstructured real-world environments with minimal prior training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a humanoid agent capable of **zero-shot loco-manipulation** based on natural language instructions in complex, unstructured environments. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Bridging High-Level Instructions with Low-Level Control:** A fundamental challenge in humanoid robotics is translating abstract human commands into a coherent sequence of physically realizable whole-body actions. The authors aim to bridge this cognitive gap, moving beyond semantic understanding to ensure physical feasibility.
2. **Integrating Locomotion and Manipulation:** Existing research often treats locomotion and manipulation in isolation, or evaluates them on less complex platforms than humanoids. The paper aims to develop a framework that truly integrates these two capabilities, coordinating high-dimensional degrees of freedom while maintaining dynamic balance, which is crucial for humanoid versatility.
3. **Leveraging Foundation Models for Embodied AI:** While foundation models have shown promise in robotics, their application to complex, high-dimensional humanoid loco-manipulation tasks in a zero-shot manner remains underexplored. The motivation is to harness the transferable multimodal representations and reasoning capabilities of these models to enable robust generalization without task-specific training.
4. **Addressing Limitations of Prior Approaches:** Previous methods for robotic planning, whether geometric search or semantic understanding, often suffer from brittleness, reliance on predefined task structures, or underutilization of LLM reasoning capacity. The authors are motivated to overcome these limitations by introducing a more cognitive, iterative, and physically grounded reasoning process.
5. **Enabling Robustness in Unstructured and Partially Observable Environments:** Real-world environments are often cluttered, dynamic, and partially observable. A key motivation is to enable humanoids to infer hidden objects, adapt to changes, and execute long-horizon tasks reliably, going beyond structured laboratory settings.

To achieve these objectives, the paper proposes **Humanoid-COA**, a framework centered around an **Embodied Chain-of-Action (CoA) Reasoning mechanism**. This mechanism is designed to incrementally decompose high-level human instructions into structured sequences of locomotion and manipulation primitives through a multi-faceted reasoning process involving affordance analysis, spatial inference, and whole-body action reasoning. The ultimate goal is to enable humanoid robots to understand, plan, and execute complex tasks safely and effectively in diverse, real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Humanoid-COA framework adheres to the classical **perception–reasoning–action paradigm**, with its core innovation residing in the "Reasoning and Planning" stage.

**A. Problem Definition**
The problem is formalized as generating an executable action sequence $A = \{a_1, \dots, a_T\}$ from a predefined action library $L$, given a natural language instruction $I$ and ego-centric observations $O$. The goal is to learn a mapping function $f: (I, O, L) \rightarrow A$ that accounts for physical and dynamical robot constraints.

**B. Humanoid Agent Framework**

1. **Perception and Understanding Stage:**
 - **Input Conversion:** Ego-centric RGB observations ($O$) are converted into a natural-language scene description ($S$) using a pre-trained **vision-language foundation model (VLM)**, specifically GPT-4V. This allows for open-vocabulary and context-aware environmental understanding.
 - **Instruction Input:** A natural language instruction ($I$) provided by the user specifies the high-level task.
 - **Tokenization:** Both the scene description ($S$) and the instruction ($I$) are tokenized into discrete sequences ($T_S$, $T_I$) for input into the reasoning module.

2. **Reasoning and Planning Stage (Core Innovation: Embodied Chain-of-Action (CoA) Reasoning):**
 This stage is the "cognitive substrate" that bridges high-level human intent and low-level embodied execution. It operates by incrementally producing intermediate reasoning states ($R$) before generating the executable action chain ($A$). The process is conditioned on the tokenized instruction, scene description, and the action library. CoA Reasoning integrates three complementary processes:

 - **a) Object Affordance Analysis:**
 - **Purpose:** Ensures that generated actions are physically realizable by assessing object properties. Pure semantic reasoning is insufficient for this.
 - **Mechanism:** For each perceived object ($e_i$), an affordance vector ($a_i$) is computed, quantifying properties like size, weight, rigidity, and movability. This vector is integrated into the reasoning state ($R_{aff}$).
 - **Impact:** Action generation is then conditioned on this affordance-refined state, ensuring compatibility between candidate actions and object properties, thus pruning the search space to physically feasible actions (e.g., a small object might be "GRASP" while a heavy one might require "LIFT" or "RAISE" with bi-manual operation).

 - **b) Region Spatial Reasoning:**
 - **Purpose:** Addresses partial observability and cluttered environments by inferring plausible locations for occluded or unseen objects.
 - **Mechanism:** A set of candidate regions ($R$) is defined, and each region ($r_k$) is assigned a weight ($w_k$) based on CLIP-based visual-semantic similarity between the cropped region and the target description. This creates a probabilistic spatial prior.
 - **Impact:** The reasoning state ($R_{spatial}$) is updated, fusing observed entities and spatial priors. This biases action generation towards more plausible hidden targets, guiding exploration and action selection (e.g., "target location may be occluded; robot should prioritize exploration behind the green screen"). This process is sequential, depending on the history of prior actions.

 - **c) Whole-Body Movement Inference:**
 - **Purpose:** Guarantees that planned actions are consistent with the robot’s kinematic and dynamic capabilities, preventing physically infeasible actions (e.g., reaching outside the workspace or exceeding joint torque limits).
 - **Mechanism:** The robot's controllable joints ($J$) are characterized by feasibility descriptors ($b_m$), including kinematic range of motion, dynamic limits, and functional roles (e.g., reaching, grasping, locomotion).
 - **Impact:** The body-level reasoning state ($R_{body}$) is updated, integrating joint feasibility with prior reasoning. Action generation is constrained to align with the kinematic and dynamic capabilities of the humanoid body, ensuring safe and executable behavior.

 - **Implementation:** GPT-4 is employed as the LLM to construct these reasoning chains, iteratively combining these three analyses. The system updates the action list after each execution step by integrating new observations, enabling real-time plan adaptation.

3. **Execution and Control Stage:**
 - **Action Library:** A structured action library ($L$, summarized in Table I) provides low-level primitives categorized into perception-guided (e.g., FIND), locomotion (e.g., MOVE, ROTATE, STOP, HEIGHT adjustments), and manipulation (e.g., HOLD, RELEASE, GRASP, LIFT, RAISE, REARRANGE, PUT).
 - **Motor Commands:** The symbolic action chain ($A$) generated by the reasoning stage is grounded into concrete behaviors. A policy maps these actions into low-level motor commands (e.g., joint torques or control signals), which are executed by trained locomotion and manipulation controllers. For stability during loco-manipulation, a trained policy is integrated.
 - **Communication:** Data Distribution Service (DDS) middleware coordinates communication across all system components.

This hierarchical and iterative approach, particularly the sophisticated CoA reasoning, allows Humanoid-COA to decompose complex instructions into executable steps that are physically grounded, spatially aware, and kinematically feasible for a humanoid robot, enabling zero-shot performance in dynamic environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were rigorously conducted on two real-world humanoid robots, Unitree H1-2 and Unitree G1, across two distinct environments: an open test area and a standardized apartment. The framework was evaluated against three baselines: Loco-Manipulation Planning, Translated GPT3, and LLM Behavior Planner. Performance was assessed using two key metrics: **Executable** (syntactically valid and executable action sequences) and **Success** (accomplishment of intended task goals). Tasks were categorized into manipulation, locomotion, and loco-manipulation, each with simple and complex variations.

**A. Manipulation Tasks (Table II)**
- **Simple Tasks (Object Grasping, Object Relocation):** Humanoid-COA achieved near-perfect executability and success rates (e.g., 100% Executable, 96.6% Success for Object Grasping), substantially outperforming all baselines.
- **Complex Tasks (Spatial Placement, Sequential Manipulation, Rearrangement):** The advantage of Humanoid-COA was even more pronounced. For "Rearrangement," the most challenging, Humanoid-COA achieved 80% Executable and 73.3% Success, significantly higher than the best baseline (LLM Behavior Planner at 63.3% Executable, 40% Success). This demonstrates the robustness of CoA reasoning in long-horizon, multi-step manipulation.

**B. Locomotion Tasks (Table III)**
- **Simple Tasks (Target Approach, Pose Adjustment):** Humanoid-COA showed perfect executability and high success rates (100% Executable, 96.6% Success for Target Approach; 100% Executable, 90% Success for Pose Adjustment), clearly outperforming baselines.
- **Complex Tasks (Sequential Navigation, Navigation under Occlusion, Long-horizon Relocation):** The framework maintained strong performance, achieving 86.6% success for "Sequential Navigation" compared to 56.6% for the best baseline. Crucially, for "Navigation under Occlusion," Humanoid-COA's success rate was 63.3%, more than double that of competing approaches (LLM Behavior Planner at 26.6%), highlighting the effectiveness of explicit spatial reasoning.

**C. Loco-Manipulation Tasks (Table IV)**
- **Simple Tasks (Mobile Pick, Mobile Place):** Humanoid-COA again achieved superior performance, exceeding 90% success (e.g., 100% Executable, 96.6% Success for Mobile Place).
- **Complex Tasks (Sequential Loco-Manipulation, Occlusion-Aware Loco-Manipulation, Long-Horizon Loco-Manipulation):** The benefits of embodied CoA were most evident here. For "Sequential Loco-Manipulation," Humanoid-COA reached 83.3% success, significantly higher than the strongest baseline (63.3%). In "Occlusion-Aware Loco-Manipulation," Humanoid-COA maintained 66.6% success, while other methods largely failed (0% success for Translated GPT3). Even in "Long-Horizon Loco-Manipulation," Humanoid-COA preserved over 60% success, more than doubling baseline performance.

**D. Action Library Evaluation (Table V)**
The evaluation of individual primitive actions in the action library showed that most skills (e.g., FIND, MOVE, GRASP, LIFT, PUT) achieved high executability and success rates. Simpler actions like RELEASE were fast, while complex ones like REARRANGE took longer and were more prone to errors, validating the need for the CoA reasoning to compose these primitives dynamically and robustly.

**E. Ablation Study of Chain-of-Action Reasoning (Table VI)**
This study confirmed the incremental contribution of each CoA component:
- **No Modules:** Baseline performance was low (50% Executable, 45% Success).
- **+ Object Affordance Analysis:** Substantially improved executability (75%) and success (65%), demonstrating its role in ensuring physical feasibility.
- **+ Region Spatial Reasoning:** Further boosted performance (85% Executable, 70% Success), proving its effectiveness in guiding exploration under partial observability.
- **+ Whole-Body Movement Inference (Full Humanoid-COA):** Achieved the best performance (90% Executable, 75% Success), underscoring the importance of reasoning over kinematic and dynamic feasibility for reliable execution.

In summary, Humanoid-COA consistently and substantially outperformed prior methods across all task categories and complexities, demonstrating robust zero-shot generalization to long-horizon and unstructured scenarios, largely attributed to its Embodied Chain-of-Action Reasoning mechanism.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Humanoid-COA framework represents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robotics and embodied artificial intelligence, addressing long-standing challenges in intelligent loco-manipulation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Pioneering Humanoid Agent Framework:** The paper introduces, to the best of the authors' knowledge, the "first humanoid agent framework that integrates foundation model reasoning for zero-shot loco-manipulation under natural language instructions." This is a crucial step towards creating truly autonomous and versatile humanoids that can interpret high-level human commands without extensive task-specific training.
2. **Robust Zero-Shot Generalization:** The ability to perform complex, long-horizon tasks in unstructured environments in a zero-shot manner is a major breakthrough. This eliminates the need for vast datasets for every new task or environment, making robot deployment more flexible and scalable. The experimental results clearly demonstrate superior generalization compared to baselines.
3. **Enhanced Cognitive Reasoning for Robotics:** The Embodied Chain-of-Action (CoA) Reasoning mechanism is a key innovation. By explicitly integrating object affordance analysis, region spatial reasoning, and whole-body movement inference, the framework imbues the humanoid with a more sophisticated cognitive capability to decompose tasks, handle partial observability, and ensure physical feasibility. This moves beyond simple semantic mapping or direct action-instruction pairings to a deeper, more robust form of embodied intelligence.
4. **Improved Performance in Complex Scenarios:** Humanoid-COA shows particularly pronounced improvements in complex, long-horizon, and occlusion-aware tasks across manipulation, locomotion, and integrated loco-manipulation. This indicates its potential to tackle real-world challenges where robots must operate in cluttered, dynamic, and unpredictable settings.
5. **Bridging the Gap Between AI and Embodiment:** The work successfully bridges the gap between powerful, abstract reasoning capabilities of foundation models (GPT-4V, GPT-4) and the concrete physical demands of a high-dimensional robotic platform like a humanoid. This demonstrates a path forward for grounding large-scale AI models in real-world physical interaction, overcoming the limitations of purely simulated or less embodied experiments.
6. **Catalyst for Future Humanoid Applications:** The framework has broad implications for future humanoid applications. Robots equipped with such capabilities could be deployed in diverse fields, including:
 - **Logistics and Warehousing:** Performing complex pick-and-place tasks, shelf rearrangement, and mobile manipulation in dynamic environments.
 - **Assisted Living and Healthcare:** Assisting humans with daily tasks, retrieving objects, and navigating complex indoor spaces.
 - **Hazardous Environments:** Executing inspection, repair, or disaster response tasks that require both mobility and dexterous interaction.
 - **Research and Development:** Providing a robust platform for further research into human-robot interaction, multi-modal learning, and general-purpose AI for robotics.

By pushing the boundaries of what humanoid robots can achieve through intelligent reasoning and multimodal integration, Humanoid-COA paves the way for a new generation of more autonomous, adaptable, and useful humanoid agents in our daily lives and industries.
