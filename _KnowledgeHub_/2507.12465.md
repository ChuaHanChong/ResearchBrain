# PhysX-3D: Physical-Grounded 3D Asset Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysX-3D introduces PhysXNet, the first comprehensive dataset for physics-grounded 3D assets, and PhysXGen, an end-to-end framework capable of generating 3D objects with fine-grained, part-level physical properties from single image prompts. The approach successfully integrates physical knowledge into 3D generation, achieving improved geometric and aesthetic quality while significantly enhancing physical property prediction over baselines.

## Problem

- Current 3D generative models predominantly focus on geometric structures and visual appearances, neglecting crucial physical properties essential for real-world interaction.
- Existing 3D datasets lack comprehensive, part-level annotations for physical attributes like absolute scale, material composition, functional affordances, and precise kinematic parameters.
- This omission creates a critical bottleneck for deploying generated 3D assets in physical domains such as robotics, embodied AI, and high-fidelity simulations.

## Method

- Developed PhysXNet, a pioneering dataset with over 26,000 3D objects, providing fine-grained, part-level annotations for absolute scale, material, affordance, kinematics, and function descriptions.
- Created a scalable human-in-the-loop annotation pipeline, leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and expert refinement, to efficiently convert existing geometry-focused assets into physics-enabled ones, complemented by PhysXNet-XL (6M procedurally generated objects).
- Proposed PhysXGen, a feed-forward, dual-branch generative framework based on diffusion models, designed to generate 3D assets from image prompts while simultaneously predicting plausible physical properties by integrating physical knowledge into pre-trained 3D structural latent spaces.

## Results

- PhysXGen consistently outperformed baseline methods, achieving better geometry/appearance (e.g., PSNR 24.53 vs. 24.31) and significantly improved physical property generation across absolute scale (error 7.24 vs. 13.21), material (score 13.01 vs. 8.63), affordance (score 11.30 vs. 7.23), and kinematics.
- The PhysXNet dataset, with 26,000 objects, established itself as the first comprehensive physics-grounded 3D dataset, while PhysXNet-XL expanded its scale to over 6 million procedurally generated and annotated 3D objects.
- Qualitative evaluations confirmed that PhysXGen generates plausible and consistent physical-grounded 3D assets from single image prompts, demonstrating superior stability and accuracy in distinguishing part-level properties compared to fragmented approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating explicit physical properties into 3D generation can enhance both the structural and aesthetic quality of generated assets, demonstrating that physical grounding is not solely an add-on.
> - A dual-branch architecture, which fuses structural guidance with the primary physical generation module, effectively leverages the inherent correlations between geometric and physical attributes for more consistent outputs.
> - Scalable human-in-the-loop annotation, supported by VLMs and procedural generation techniques, provides an efficient strategy to overcome the data scarcity challenges for complex, multi-modal 3D physical properties.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of diverse and high-quality 3D assets has seen rapid advancements, primarily driven by applications in gaming, virtual reality, robotics, and embodied simulators. However, the existing research landscape in 3D generative modeling predominantly emphasizes geometric structures and visual appearances (textures, colors). Datasets such as ShapeNet, Objaverse, and OmniObject3D provide vast collections of 3D models, but their annotations are largely confined to structural and aesthetic attributes. While some datasets, like PartNet and PartNet-Mobility, introduce finer-grained part annotations and kinematic constraints for articulated objects, they still lack a comprehensive suite of physical properties essential for realistic interaction and simulation. ABO, for instance, includes some material metadata and keywords but only at an object level, limiting its utility for part-aware applications.

This oversight creates a critical bottleneck for deploying generated 3D assets in physical domains, where attributes like absolute scale, material composition, functional affordances, and precise kinematic parameters are crucial for understanding object behavior. Without these physical properties, synthesized 3D objects remain largely confined to virtual visualization rather than serving as actionable entities in embodied AI or physically accurate simulations.

"PhysX-3D" directly addresses this fundamental gap by pioneering an end-to-end paradigm for physical-grounded 3D asset generation. It distinguishes itself by moving beyond mere visual fidelity to integrate a rich array of physical and semantic characteristics directly into the 3D generation process. By focusing on fine-grained, part-level physical properties, this work positions itself as a foundational step towards closing the representational gap between purely structural 3D models and their real-world counterparts. It seeks to bridge both the data scarcity problem (with PhysXNet) and the modeling challenge (with PhysXGen) in a unified framework, offering a more holistic approach compared to fragmented efforts that address only specific physical attributes or types of articulated objects. This integrated approach aims to unlock new possibilities for physics-aware 3D vision models and their critical applications in robotics, embodied AI, and advanced simulations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation behind PhysX-3D stems from the recognized limitation of current 3D generative models: their primary focus on geometry and texture fidelity leads to a neglect of crucial physical properties. This omission severely hampers the real-world applicability of synthesized 3D assets in domains requiring physical understanding and interaction, such as robotic manipulation, embodied AI, and high-fidelity simulations. The authors explicitly state that "3D modeling is moving from virtual to physical," underscoring the necessity for physically-grounded modeling.

To address this challenge, the paper sets forth two key objectives:

1. **To bridge the critical gap in physics-annotated 3D datasets:**
 - This involves creating the **PhysXNet** dataset, which is designed to be the first comprehensive physics-grounded 3D dataset. It systematically annotates 3D assets across five foundational dimensions: absolute scale, material, affordance, kinematics, and function descriptions, with a particular emphasis on *part-level* annotations.
 - A significant part of this objective is the development of a **scalable human-in-the-loop annotation pipeline** that leverages vision-language models (VLMs) to efficiently convert existing geometry-focused 3D assets into physics-first assets.
 - An extended version, **PhysXNet-XL**, aims to further alleviate data scarcity by providing over 6 million procedurally generated and annotated 3D objects.

2. **To develop an end-to-end paradigm for physical-grounded 3D asset generation:**
 - This involves proposing **PhysXGen**, a feed-forward framework capable of generating 3D assets directly from image prompts, while simultaneously predicting plausible physical properties.
 - A core goal of PhysXGen is to inject physical knowledge into pre-trained 3D structural latent spaces, ensuring that the generated assets not only possess rich physical attributes but also maintain high native geometry and appearance quality.
 - The framework is designed to explicitly model the latent correlations between 3D structures and physical properties, leading to consistent and plausible physical predictions.

In essence, the motivation is to evolve 3D asset generation from purely visual representations to functional, physically interactive entities, thereby unlocking their full potential for advanced AI applications in the physical world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "PhysX-3D" paradigm comprises two main components: a novel physics-grounded 3D dataset called PhysXNet, and an end-to-end generative framework named PhysXGen.

#### 3.1. PhysXNet Dataset Creation

1. **Definition of Physical Properties:** The authors systematically categorize object properties into three stages for annotation:
 - **Identification:** Absolute scale (physical dimensions) and material (name, Young’s modulus, Poisson’s ratio, density).
 - **Function:** Functional affordance analysis (priority rank of being touched, 1-10) and comprehensive function descriptions (basic, functional, and kinematic descriptions).
 - **Operation:** Kinematic parameter quantification, covering five types of joints (No constraints, Prismatic, Revolute, Hinge, Rigid) and a combined type (Revolute & Prismatic). For movable parts, detailed parameters like motion range, motion direction, child parts, and parent parts are annotated. The assumption of homogeneous internal composition for each part simplifies material property assignment.

2. **Human-in-the-loop Annotation Pipeline:** To build PhysXNet (26K objects) upon existing datasets like PartNet, an efficient semi-automated pipeline is introduced:
 - **Preliminary Data Acquisition:** Utilizes large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like GPT-4o for initial property annotation, especially for basic information and descriptions. This VLM output is then subjected to human expert verification and refinement to ensure accuracy and consistency.
 - **Kinematic Parameter Determination:** This phase is structured into four subtasks:
 - **(2.a) Calculate Contact Region:** Point cloud data from child-parent mesh pairs are extracted, and Euclidean distances are computed to identify regions of contact, filtering out irrelevant points.
 - **(2.b) Plane Fitting:** A plane-fitting algorithm is applied to the identified contact regions.
 - **(2.c) Candidate Generation and Selection:** Human experts generate and select candidate kinematic axes/locations based on the fitted planes and contact regions. For revolute joints, k-means clustering helps identify candidate rotation axis locations.
 - **(2.d) Determine Parameters:** Human reviewers finalize the detailed kinematic parameters (e.g., rotation axis, range, location, parent/child parts).
 - The pipeline employs a "part-based annotation" strategy, rendering each component individually to avoid occlusion issues inherent in segmentation-based methods, ensuring robust annotation even for fine details.

3. **PhysXNet-XL:** To expand the dataset, PhysXNet-XL is created with over 6 million procedurally generated and annotated 3D objects. This involves defining intra-category and cross-category procedural generation rules (e.g., combining different types of cabinets or integrating modular components like drawers and doors) by identifying connected regions and adaptively scaling new components.

#### 3.2. PhysXGen Framework

PhysXGen is a feed-forward, dual-branch framework designed for physical 3D generation, leveraging pre-trained 3D generative priors (specifically, TRELLIS) due to the inherent correlation between geometry and physical properties.

1. **Physical 3D VAE Encoding and Decoding:**
 - A **physical VAE (E_phy, D_phy)** encodes physical properties ($P_{phy}$ – absolute scale, affordance, density, kinematic parameters) and semantic function descriptions ($P_{sem}$ from CLIP embeddings) into a unified physical latent space ($P_{plat}$).
 - A separate **aesthetic VAE (E_aes, D_aes)**, derived from the pre-trained model, encodes structural and appearance features ($P_{aes}$ from DINOv2) into a structured latent space ($P_{slat}$).
 - A residual connection from the physical decoder ($D_{phy}$) to the aesthetic decoder ($D_{aes}$) is introduced to allow the study of physical properties' influence on geometry/appearance.
 - The VAE is trained with a composite loss function ($L_{vae}$) including color, geometry (mask, normal, depth), physical property, semantic, KL divergence, and regularization losses.

2. **Physical Latent Generation (Diffusion Model):**
 - A transformer-architecture diffusion model is employed to jointly generate physical and structural attributes.
 - It features a **dual-branch architecture** where the structural guidance from the aesthetic module is fused with the primary physical generation module via learnable skip-connection layers. This design effectively leverages the inherent correlations between physical properties and structural features.
 - The model uses Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) as its optimization objective, with the total loss ($L_{diff}$) being a sum of geometric ($L_{aes}$) and physical ($L_{phy}$) branch losses.

3. **Implementation Details:** PhysXGen is fine-tuned on the PhysXNet dataset, repurposing the structural space of TRELLIS. Training is conducted on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs, using AdamW optimizer.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Metrics

- **Physical properties evaluation:** Euclidean distance (absolute scale), PSNR (density, affordance maps), instantiation distance (kinematics), and PSNR on cosine similarity score maps (function descriptions).
- **Geometry evaluation:** Mean PSNR (appearance from 30 random views), Chamfer Distance (CD), and F-score (FS) for geometry quality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the PhysXNet dataset validate the superior performance and generalization capabilities of the proposed PhysX-3D framework.

#### 4.1. Dataset (PhysXNet and PhysXNet-XL)

- **First-of-its-kind:** PhysXNet successfully establishes itself as the "first comprehensive physical 3D dataset" with over 26K richly annotated 3D objects, uniquely providing fine-grained, part-level annotations for absolute scale, material, affordance, kinematics, and function descriptions.
- **Scalable Annotation:** The human-in-the-loop pipeline, integrating VLMs and expert refinement, proved robust and efficient in converting existing geometry-focused datasets into physics-enabled ones.
- **Expanded Data:** PhysXNet-XL, with over 6 million procedurally generated and annotated 3D objects, significantly addresses the scarcity of physics-grounded data, providing a vast resource for generative models.
- **Detailed Statistics:** The dataset exhibits a long-tailed distribution for part numbers (avg. ~5 parts/object) and significant variation in physical dimensions. Detailed breakdowns of kinematic types, material properties (density, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio), and object tags are provided.

#### 4.2. PhysXGen (Quantitative Results)

- **Overall Superiority:** PhysXGen consistently outperforms the baseline (TRELLIS + separate physical property predictor, "PhysPre") across all evaluated metrics.
 - **Geometry/Appearance:** Achieves better PSNR (24.53 vs. 24.31), lower Chamfer Distance (12.7 vs. 13.2), and higher F-Score (77.3 vs. 76.9), indicating that integrating physical knowledge *improves* structural and aesthetic quality.
 - **Physical Properties:** Demonstrates significant improvements in physical property generation, with lower errors for absolute scale (7.24 vs. 13.21), higher scores for material (13.01 vs. 8.63), affordance (11.30 vs. 7.23), and function descriptions (10.11 vs. 6.55). Kinematic parameters also show improved metrics (e.g., COV 0.33 vs. 0.24, MMD 0.08 vs. 0.12).
- **Ablation Studies:** The studies confirm the effectiveness of the dual-branch architecture and the integration of geometric and physical information:
 - Introducing dependencies in the diffusion model ("Dep-Diff") improved physical generation over independent models.
 - Adding dependencies in the VAE ("Dep-VAE") further enhanced geometry quality.
 - The full PhysXGen framework (with both Dep-VAE and Dep-Diff) achieved the best performance, validating the design rationale of jointly modeling cross-property consistency.
- **Comparison with GPT-based Baseline:** PhysXGen showed strong performance against a GPT-based baseline (Trellis + PartField + GPT-4o), achieving relative improvements of 24% (absolute scale), 64% (material), 28% (kinematics), and 72% (affordance). While GPT-4o showed higher PSNR for function descriptions (14.31 vs. 10.11), this was attributed to PhysXGen's training on a relatively smaller dataset for these complex semantic properties.

#### 4.3. PhysXGen (Qualitative Results)

- **Plausible Physical Assets:** PhysXGen successfully generates physical-grounded 3D assets from single image prompts, demonstrating its ability to infer and embed complex physical attributes.
- **Improved Consistency:** Qualitative comparisons against baselines show that PhysXGen produces more stable and accurate results for physical properties, particularly in distinguishing properties of different parts and achieving better consistency in material, kinematics, and affordance for neighboring structures. It exhibits fewer artifacts compared to fragmented approaches or models with fewer integrated dependencies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "PhysX-3D" project represents a significant stride forward in the field of 3D content generation, addressing a critical and long-standing limitation in existing models. Its contributions hold substantial significance and promise for a wide range of applications:

1. **Pioneering a New Frontier:** By introducing the first end-to-end paradigm for physical-grounded 3D asset generation, PhysX-3D moves the research frontier beyond purely aesthetic 3D modeling towards functionally rich and physically interactive digital assets. This work sets a new standard for what constitutes a "high-quality" 3D model in the context of intelligent systems.

2. **Bridging the Reality Gap:** The lack of rich physical properties in generated 3D assets has been a major hurdle for their deployment in real-world physical domains. PhysX-3D directly addresses this by providing comprehensive, part-level physical annotations and a generative model capable of predicting them. This makes the synthesized assets suitable for applications like:
 - **Robotics and Embodied AI:** Robots need to understand not just an object's shape but also its material properties, how it moves, and what actions it affords to interact effectively with their environment. PhysX-3D provides the foundational data and models for training such intelligent agents.
 - **Realistic Simulations:** Game engines, architectural visualization tools, and scientific simulations demand physically accurate models. The integration of absolute scale, material properties, and kinematics allows for far more realistic and predictive simulations.
 - **Virtual Content Creation:** Content creators can generate sophisticated 3D models with inherent physical behaviors, streamlining workflows for animation, VR/AR experiences, and interactive digital twins.

3. **Facilitating Future Research:**
 - **PhysXNet and PhysXNet-XL:** The release of these first-of-their-kind datasets is a monumental contribution. They will serve as invaluable benchmarks and training resources for the broader research community, accelerating progress in generative physical AI, 3D vision, and embodied AI. The efficient human-in-the-loop annotation pipeline also provides a blueprint for future dataset creation efforts.
 - **PhysXGen Framework:** The dual-branch, physics-aware generative framework offers a robust and effective approach to integrate physical knowledge into existing powerful 3D structural generative models. Its success demonstrates the feasibility and benefits of joint optimization for geometric and physical attributes.

4. **Revealing Fundamental Challenges:** The paper's analysis of limitations and error distributions for different physical properties (e.g., challenges with long-tailed scale distributions, spatial inconsistency in material/affordance, complexity in kinematic representation, and disentanglement issues in semantic descriptions) provides crucial insights into the remaining hurdles in physical 3D generation. These identified challenges will guide future research directions and inspire innovative solutions from the community.

In conclusion, PhysX-3D is a foundational work that significantly advances the state-of-the-art in 3D content generation. By marrying visual fidelity with physical plausibility, it promises to unlock a new era of intelligent systems capable of perceiving, reasoning about, and interacting with the physical world with unprecedented realism and effectiveness. The release of the dataset, code, and models will undoubtedly catalyze a wave of innovation across multiple disciplines.
