# SAM 3: Segment Anything with Concepts

> [!summary] Summary
> Meta Superintelligence Labs developed SAM 3, a model that formalizes and solves the "Promptable Concept Segmentation" (PCS) task, enabling it to detect, segment, and track all instances of a concept in images and videos based on natural language or image exemplars. The model achieved a cgF1 score of 54.1 on the new SA-Co/Gold benchmark, more than doubling previous baselines, and demonstrated over 80% of human pHOTA performance on video concept segmentation.

## Problem

- Prior Segment Anything Models (SAM) focused on "Promptable Visual Segmentation" (PVS), which segments only a single object instance based on geometric prompts.
- There was no generalized method for identifying and segmenting all instances of a specified visual concept (e.g., "all cars") across images and videos using natural language or image exemplars.
- Existing open-vocabulary methods typically provided bounding boxes or coarse semantic segmentation, lacking the high-quality instance masks and identity preservation across video frames that a general concept segmentation model would require.

## Method

- Introduces and formalizes the ==Promptable Concept Segmentation (PCS)== task and develops **SAM 3**, a unified ==dual encoder-decoder transformer== architecture with a shared ==Perception Encoder== backbone for vision-language inputs.
- Employs a novel, scalable human- and model-in-the-loop ==data engine== leveraging Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) as "AI annotators" and "AI verifiers" to construct a vast, high-quality training dataset (SA-Co).
- Integrates a detector for image-level concept segmentation and a ==memory-based video tracker== with ==temporal disambiguation== strategies to maintain object identities across video frames and address ambiguities.

## Results

- SAM 3 achieved a cgF1 score of **54.1** on the SA-Co/Gold benchmark, more than doubling the strongest baseline (OWLv2* at **24.6 cgF1**) and reaching **74%** of estimated human performance for image PCS.
- For video PCS, the model attained over **80%** of human pHOTA on the SA-Co/VEval benchmark and established new state-of-the-art results on several public video instance segmentation benchmarks, including LVVIS and OVIS.
- The model demonstrated strong few-shot adaptation capabilities, improved upon SAM 2's interactive segmentation and VOS performance, and, when integrated as an MLLM tool (SAM 3 Agent), surpassed previous zero-shot methods on reasoning segmentation benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The inclusion of a "presence head" that decouples concept recognition from object localization significantly improves precision, particularly when handling challenging negative concept phrases.
> - A scalable data engine, where MLLMs propose concepts and verify annotations, can double data throughput and enable efficient, high-quality dataset creation for open-vocabulary tasks, including automatic domain adaptation.
> - Effective temporal disambiguation strategies like track confirmation delay and periodic re-prompting are critical for robust multi-instance concept tracking and maintaining identity consistency in video sequences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research builds upon the foundational work of the "Segment Anything Model" (SAM) series, specifically SAM (Kirillov et al., 2023) and SAM 2 (Ravi et al., 2024). Previous iterations focused on "Promptable Visual Segmentation" (PVS), enabling the segmentation of a *single object instance* per prompt using geometric cues such as points, bounding boxes, or masks. While effective for individual object segmentation, these models did not address the more general requirement of identifying and segmenting *all instances of a specified concept* within an image or video.

SAM 3 introduces and formalizes the "Promptable Concept Segmentation" (PCS) task, which expands the capabilities of promptable segmentation to include arbitrary visual concepts specified by natural language noun phrases or image exemplars. This positions SAM 3 at the intersection of several computer vision research areas:
- **Open-Vocabulary Object Detection and Segmentation (OVD/OVS):** This field aims to detect and segment objects belonging to categories not seen during training, typically by leveraging vision-language models like CLIP. SAM 3 contributes by extending this to a broader range of concepts and providing high-quality instance masks rather than just bounding boxes or coarse semantic segmentation.
- **Visual Grounding:** This involves localizing a language expression to a specific region or object in an image. SAM 3 performs a more complex form of grounding, identifying all instances of a concept and preserving their identities across video frames.
- **Multi-Object Tracking and Segmentation (MOTS):** This domain focuses on tracking multiple objects over time in videos while providing their segmentation masks. SAM 3 integrates tracking capabilities to maintain object identities for concept instances across video sequences.
- **Foundation Models:** The work aligns with the trend of developing large, general-purpose models that can perform a wide array of tasks and generalize to new data distributions. SAM 3's unified architecture and extensive training on diverse data support its role as a foundational model for visual understanding.

By tackling the PCS task, SAM 3 aims to bridge the gap between single-instance visual prompting and comprehensive, concept-driven visual understanding across dynamic scenes, advancing the capabilities of multimodal AI for various applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **introduce and advance the Promptable Concept Segmentation (PCS) task**. This involves developing a unified model capable of detecting, segmenting, and tracking all instances of a visual concept in images and videos, where the concept is specified through concept prompts. Concept prompts are defined as either short noun phrases (e.g., "yellow school bus"), image exemplars, or a combination of both.

The detailed objectives and motivations include:

- **Addressing Limitations of Previous SAM Models:** Prior SAM versions (SAM 1 and SAM 2) focused on Promptable Visual Segmentation (PVS), which segments a *single object* based on geometric prompts. The key motivation for SAM 3 is to overcome this limitation and enable the segmentation of *all instances of a concept* (e.g., "all cats in a video") which is a more general and widely applicable task.
- **Formalizing the PCS Task:** To provide a clear framework for this new capability, the authors formalize the PCS task, outlining input prompt types (noun phrases, image exemplars) and expected outputs (segmentation masks and unique identities for all matching instances, preserved across video frames). The scope of text prompts is constrained to simple noun phrases, acknowledging that complex language queries might require integration with Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).
- **Developing a Unified Architecture:** A core objective is to design a single, generalized model that not only performs the new PCS task but also improves upon the existing PVS capabilities of SAM 2. This unified approach is motivated by the desire for a versatile system that can handle both concept-level and instance-level segmentation.
- **Building a Scalable Data Engine:** Recognizing that training a model for open-vocabulary concept segmentation requires vast amounts of high-quality, diverse labeled data, a significant objective was to construct an efficient and scalable data annotation engine. This engine is motivated by the need to curate media from diverse domains, increase label diversity and difficulty, generate hard negatives, and significantly boost annotation throughput by leveraging AI models in the verification process.
- **Creating a Comprehensive Benchmark:** To facilitate rigorous evaluation and future research in PCS, the development of a new, large-scale benchmark dataset, Segment Anything with Concepts (SA-Co), was a key objective. This benchmark is motivated by the inadequacy of existing datasets for evaluating open-vocabulary, multi-instance concept segmentation in images and videos, especially with respect to concept diversity and the inclusion of hard negatives.
- **Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance:** The ultimate motivation is to achieve a substantial improvement in accuracy and capabilities over existing systems for both image and video PCS, and to enhance PVS performance relative to SAM 2.
- **Open-Sourcing Resources:** The project aims to open-source SAM 3 along with the SA-Co benchmark and inference code to foster community engagement and accelerate research and application development in multimodal AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in SAM 3 combines architectural innovations, a novel data engine, and comprehensive evaluation protocols to address the Promptable Concept Segmentation (PCS) task.

#### 3.1. Task Formalization

The PCS task is defined as, given an image or short video, detecting, segmenting, and tracking all instances of a visual concept specified by a short text phrase, image exemplars, or a combination. Text prompts are limited to simple noun phrases, and image exemplars can be positive or negative bounding boxes for iterative refinement. The model outputs instance and semantic masks for matching objects, preserving identities across video frames. Ambiguity in open-vocabulary concepts is addressed by collecting multiple expert annotations for evaluation and including an ambiguity module in the model.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

SAM 3 generalizes SAM 2, featuring a dual encoder-decoder transformer architecture. It consists of an image-level detector and a memory-based video tracker, both sharing a Perception Encoder (PE) backbone for vision-language inputs.

- **Perception Encoder (PE) Backbone:** A shared vision and text encoder (from Bolya et al., 2025) provides aligned image and text embeddings. This encoder is pre-trained on 5.4 billion image-text pairs, providing broad concept coverage and robustness. The vision encoder uses windowed attention and RoPE, and is run once per frame.
- **Detector Architecture (Image-level):**
 - **Components:** A DETR-based model conditioned on encoded text, geometry (points, boxes for PVS, or exemplars), and image embeddings from the PE. It comprises a fusion encoder and a DETR-like decoder.
 - **Fusion Encoder:** Conditions the unconditioned image embeddings from the PE on prompt tokens (text and/or exemplar tokens) using a stack of transformer blocks.
 - **Decoder:** Uses learned object queries that cross-attend to prompt tokens and conditioned image embeddings. It incorporates iterative box refinement, look-forward-twice, hybrid matching, and DAC-DETR techniques.
 - **Presence Head:** A key innovation decoupling recognition from localization. A global presence token predicts if the target concept exists in the image. Object queries then focus solely on localization *given* concept presence. The final score is the product of the object score and the presence score. This is particularly effective with challenging negative phrases.
 - **Image Exemplars and Interactivity:** Image exemplars (bounding box + binary label) are encoded separately and concatenated with text prompts. This enables detecting *all* matching instances and allows interactive refinement.
 - **Ambiguity Handling:** An ambiguity head, implemented as a mixture of experts (K=2 performs best), specializes in different interpretations of a phrase. During inference, a classification head predicts the most probable correct expert. Intersection-over-Minimum (IoM) helps detect overlapping instances.
 - **Segmentation Head:** Adapted from MaskFormer, it outputs semantic and instance segmentation masks using conditioned features from the fusion encoder and object queries.
- **Tracker and Video Architecture:**
 - **Detection and Propagation:** On each video frame, the detector finds new objects, and the tracker propagates masklets (spatio-temporal masks) from previous frames using a SAM 2-style propagation mechanism. A matching function associates propagated masklets with new detections.
 - **Tracker Module:** Shares the PE backbone with the detector. After detector training, the PE is frozen, and the tracker is trained with a prompt encoder, mask decoder, memory encoder, and memory bank to encode object appearance over time.
 - **Temporal Disambiguation Strategies:**
 - **Track Confirmation Delay:** Delays masklet output to validate candidates, removing unconfirmed (low Masklet Detection Score) or duplicate masklets.
 - **Masklet Suppression:** Suppresses masklets if their detection score falls below a threshold over their lifetime, addressing ambiguous detections.
 - **Periodic Re-Prompting:** Re-prompts the tracker with high-confidence detection masks at regular intervals to recover from tracking failures due to occlusions or distractors.
 - **Detection-Guided Re-Prompting:** Reconditions the tracker using detector output if the tracker's bounding box IoU with a detection is low, addressing mask drift.
- **Training Stages:** A four-stage training process:
 1. **Perception Encoder Pre-training:** PE image and text encoders are pre-trained on 5.4 billion image-text pairs.
 2. **Detector Pre-training:** Trains the image-level detector and the PE with large-scale image segmentation data (including video frames as images), focusing on broad concept coverage for PCS and basic PVS.
 3. **Detector Fine-tuning:** Further trains on high-quality human-annotated image data, expands PVS interactivity to 7 steps, introduces PCS interactivity (positive/negative exemplars), and adds the presence token for improved precision.
 4. **Tracker Training:** Trains the tracker on video data with a frozen backbone, focusing on spatio-temporal tracking.

#### 3.3. Data Engine

A human- and model-in-the-loop data engine is crucial for generating a large and diverse training dataset.

- **Media Curation:** Curates diverse media domains (15 distinct datasets) using retrieval models and taxonomy-guided mining strategies. Filters for crowded scenes, small objects, and challenging tracking scenarios in videos.
- **Label Curation:** Leverages a 22.4M-node SA-Co ontology (derived from Wikidata) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) as "AI annotators."
 - **Noun Phrase Proposal:** MLLMs (Llama 3.2, Llama 4) generate image-level captions, parse noun phrases, filter non-groundable phrases, and balance NP diversity.
 - **Hard Negative Proposal:** MLLMs and ontology navigation generate adversarial negative noun phrases for improved robustness.
- **Label Verification:** MLLMs (Llama 3.2 fine-tuned on human data) act as "AI verifiers" for:
 - **Mask Verification (MV):** Accepts or rejects individual mask quality.
 - **Exhaustivity Verification (EV):** Checks if all instances of a concept are masked.
 This doubles data engine throughput by directing human annotators to correct challenging errors (e.g., adding missing masks, editing poor-quality ones).
- **Phased Development:** Four phases (1-3 for images, 4 for video) progressively increase AI involvement and expand domain coverage.

#### 3.4. SA-Co Dataset and Metrics

- **Training Data:** SA-Co/HQ (high-quality, human/AI verified), SA-Co/SYN (synthetic, AI-only), SA-Co/EXT (external, enriched with hard negatives), and SA-Co/VIDEO (video, human-verified).
- **Benchmark:** SA-Co benchmark includes 207K unique concepts across 121K images and videos. Splits: SA-Co/Gold (multi-annotator for ambiguity), SA-Co/Silver, SA-Co/Bronze, SA-Co/Bio, and SA-Co/VEval (video).
- **Metrics:**
 - **Localization (on positive pairs):** `pmF1` (positive micro F1) averaged over IoU thresholds [0.5, 0.95].
 - **Classification (image/video-level concept presence):** `IL_MCC` (Image-Level Matthews Correlation Coefficient) for binary presence prediction.
 - **Main Metric:** `cgF1` (classification-gated F1) = 100 * `pmF1` * `IL_MCC`.
 - **Video-specific:** `pHOTA` (phrase-based HOTA), `pDetA`, `pAssA` for tracking.
 - Evaluations use a fixed confidence threshold of 0.5. Ambiguity in SA-Co/Gold is handled by an oracle protocol selecting the best score against multiple ground truths.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SAM 3 demonstrates substantial improvements across promptable segmentation tasks in both images and videos.

#### 4.1. Image PCS with Text Prompts

- **SA-Co Benchmark:** SAM 3 achieved a `cgF1` score of 54.1 on the SA-Co/Gold benchmark, more than doubling the performance of the strongest baseline, OWLv2* (24.6 `cgF1`). This score represents 74% of the estimated human performance. Improvements were also observed across other SA-Co splits.
- **Closed-Vocabulary Datasets:** SAM 3 established a new state-of-the-art on several closed-vocabulary datasets for box detection and instance segmentation, including COCO, COCO-O, and LVIS.
- **Semantic Segmentation:** SAM 3 outperformed specialist baselines like APE on open-vocabulary semantic segmentation benchmarks such as ADE-847, PascalConcept-59, and Cityscapes.

#### 4.2. PCS with Exemplars

- **Single Exemplar:** When prompted with a single image exemplar, SAM 3 surpassed prior state-of-the-art T-Rex2 on COCO (+18.3 `AP+`), LVIS (+10.3 `AP+`), and ODinW (+20.5 `AP+`).
- **Interactive K Exemplars:** In an interactive setting with iterative positive/negative exemplar prompts, SAM 3's PCS performance (`cgF1`) improved more rapidly than text-only or PVS refinement. After 3 clicks, interactive PCS yielded a +21.6 `cgF1` point gain over text-only and +2.0 over PVS refinement.

#### 4.3. Few-Shot Adaptation

- SAM 3 demonstrated state-of-the-art 10-shot performance on the ODinW13 and RF100-VL benchmarks for object detection, surpassing MLLM-based in-context prompting and specialized object detection models like GroundingDino.

#### 4.4. Object Counting

- SAM 3 achieved high accuracy in object counting benchmarks (e.g., MAE of 0.12 and Accuracy of 93.8% on CountBench) while simultaneously providing object segmentations, a capability not typically offered by MLLMs.

#### 4.5. Video PCS with Text Prompts

- **SA-Co/VEval Benchmark:** SAM 3 significantly outperformed baselines such as GLEE, LLMDet + SAM 3 Tracker, and SAM 3 Detector + T-by-D, especially on subsets with a large number of noun phrases. It achieved over 80% of human `pHOTA` on SA-Co/VEval.
- **Public Benchmarks:** SAM 3 showed strong performance and new state-of-the-art results on several public video instance segmentation benchmarks, including LVVIS and OVIS.
- **Temporal Disambiguation:** Ablation studies confirmed that the introduced temporal disambiguation strategies (track confirmation delay, masklet suppression, periodic re-prompting) positively impacted video PCS performance, particularly for the `pHOTA` metric.

#### 4.6. PVS (Visual Prompting Capabilities)

- **Video Object Segmentation (VOS):** SAM 3 achieved significant improvements over SAM 2 on most VOS benchmarks, notably a 6.5-point gain on the challenging MOSEv2 dataset.
- **Interactive Image Segmentation:** SAM 3 outperformed SAM 2 on the 37-dataset benchmark for interactive image segmentation, yielding more accurate masks with 1 or 5 clicks.

#### 4.7. SAM 3 Agent (MLLM Integration)

- When used as a tool by a Multimodal Large Language Model, SAM 3 Agent surpassed previous zero-shot methods on reasoning segmentation benchmarks like ReasonSeg and OmniLabel. It also improved upon prior zero-shot results for referring expression segmentation on RefCOCO+ and RefCOCOg, indicating its effectiveness in handling complex language queries requiring multi-step reasoning.

#### 4.8. Ablation Studies

- **Presence Head:** The presence head improved `cgF1` by +1.5, primarily by boosting image-level recognition (`IL_MCC`).
- **Hard Negatives:** Incorporating hard negative noun phrases in training data significantly improved the model's performance, especially the `IL_MCC` score (from 0.44 to 0.68 with 30 hard negatives/image).
- **Training Data Scaling:** Both synthetic (SA-Co/SYN) and high-quality (SA-Co/HQ) training data contributed substantially to performance gains, with HQ data offering a +14.6 `cgF1` increase over a baseline. Synthetic data, without human annotation cost, showed scaling behavior similar to human-annotated data.
- **AI Verifiers:** AI verifiers for mask and exhaustivity verification improved SAM 3's `cgF1` by +7.2 and +1.1 points respectively, effectively closing half the gap between SAM 3 and human performance.
- **Domain Adaptation:** Synthetic data generated by SAM 3 and AI verifiers for a new domain ("Food&drink") demonstrated similar scaling behavior to human-annotated data for adapting to new data distributions, without requiring human annotation.

#### 4.9. Inference Efficiency

- SAM 3 runs in 30 ms for a single image with over 100 detected objects on an H200 GPU. For video, it sustains near real-time performance for approximately 5 concurrent objects. SAM 3 Multiplex, a shared-memory approach, further improves efficiency for multi-object tracking, showing a 5.2x speedup at 128 objects on an H100 GPU over SAM 3.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of SAM 3 and the introduction of the Promptable Concept Segmentation (PCS) task represent a notable advancement in the field of visual AI, with several potential impacts:

- **Expanded Capabilities in Visual Understanding:** SAM 3 moves beyond single-instance segmentation to enable the identification and segmentation of all instances of a concept using natural language or visual exemplars across images and videos. This capability is foundational for more advanced visual reasoning and scene understanding in AI systems.
- **Foundation for Multimodal AI Applications:** The ability to "segment anything with concepts" directly impacts various multimodal AI applications. In robotics, it could facilitate more intuitive object manipulation and scene interaction. For content creation and augmented reality, it offers tools for precise content editing and virtual object integration. In data annotation, it provides a powerful tool for accelerating the creation of large-scale, high-quality datasets.
- **Advancement in Data Annotation Methodologies:** The innovative human- and model-in-the-loop data engine, particularly the strategic use of MLLMs as "AI annotators" and "AI verifiers," demonstrates a scalable and efficient paradigm for dataset creation. This approach significantly boosts annotation throughput and ensures high data quality by focusing human effort on challenging cases, potentially reducing the cost and time associated with building large datasets for complex visual tasks. The demonstration of automatic domain adaptation using synthetic data further highlights the potential for scalable improvements without extensive human intervention in new domains.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** SAM 3 exhibits strong zero-shot and few-shot generalization capabilities across diverse visual domains and a wide range of open-vocabulary concepts. This robustness is critical for real-world deployment, where models need to perform reliably on unseen categories and in varied environments.
- **Facilitating Complex AI Agent Development:** The ability of SAM 3 to be integrated as a "vision tool" within Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to form an "SAM 3 Agent" demonstrates a path towards more sophisticated AI agents. These agents can interpret complex, reasoning-heavy language queries and leverage SAM 3's precise segmentation capabilities to produce accurate visual outputs, enhancing the interactivity and utility of MLLMs in visual tasks.
- **Catalyst for Future Research:** By open-sourcing SAM 3, its code, and the new SA-Co benchmark, the researchers provide valuable resources for the academic and industrial communities. This is intended to stimulate further research into open-vocabulary segmentation, multi-modal prompting, efficient tracking architectures, and the development of more general and adaptable vision models.
