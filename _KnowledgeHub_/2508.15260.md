# Deep Think with Confidence

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UCSD and Meta AI developed DeepConf, a method that enhances Large Language Model reasoning efficiency and performance by leveraging local confidence signals to dynamically prune low-quality reasoning traces and weight final answer aggregation. DeepConf achieved up to 84.7% reduction in generated tokens and improved accuracy, reaching 99.9% on AIME 2025 with GPT-OSS-120B, without requiring additional model training.

## Problem

- Existing LLM reasoning methods, particularly those using parallel thinking like self-consistency, incur high computational overhead due to generating numerous traces.
- Standard majority voting suffers from diminishing returns and suboptimal performance because it treats all reasoning traces equally, irrespective of quality.
- Prior global confidence measures fail to detect localized reasoning breakdowns and cannot enable early stopping, thus missing crucial efficiency gains.

## Method

- ==DeepConf== introduces a suite of confidence metrics, emphasizing *local* signals like ==Group Confidence==, Bottom 10% Group Confidence, and Lowest Group Confidence.
- In an offline setting, DeepConf improves answer aggregation by ==confidence-weighted majority voting== and filtering a predefined pool of traces based on their quality.
- In an online setting, DeepConf dynamically terminates low-confidence traces during generation using an ==adaptively set threshold==, enabling real-time early stopping to save computational resources.

## Results

- Offline DeepConf achieved significant accuracy gains, for instance, boosting GPT-OSS-120B to **99.9%** accuracy on AIME 2025, an improvement over **97.0%** for standard self-consistency.
- Online DeepConf-low reduced generated tokens by **43%** to **84.7%** across various benchmarks while maintaining or improving accuracy compared to full parallel thinking.
- The method demonstrates robust benefits across a range of models (from 8B to 120B parameters) and challenging mathematical and STEM reasoning tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Local confidence measures are more effective than global averages at identifying critical reasoning breakdowns and differentiating between correct and incorrect reasoning paths.
> - Dynamically pruning low-confidence reasoning paths during generation (online filtering) substantially reduces computational cost while preserving or improving accuracy.
> - Weighting votes based on trace confidence and selectively filtering out low-quality traces leads to more robust and accurate final answers than unweighted majority voting.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has seen remarkable progress in reasoning capabilities, particularly through test-time scaling methods. A prominent technique is self-consistency (Wang et al., 2023), which involves sampling multiple reasoning paths (often called "parallel thinking") and aggregating the final answers through majority voting. While this approach significantly boosts reasoning accuracy, it comes with substantial drawbacks that DeepConf aims to address.

**Limitations of Existing Methods:**
1. **High Computational Overhead:** Generating numerous reasoning traces per query scales inference cost linearly, severely limiting practical deployment. The paper cites an example where improving pass@1 accuracy from 68% to 82% on AIME 2025 required 511 additional traces, consuming 100 million additional tokens for Qwen3-8B.
2. **Diminishing Returns:** Performance often saturates or degrades as the number of traces increases (Chen et al., 2024a). This is partly because standard majority voting treats all reasoning traces equally, irrespective of their quality (Pal et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025). Low-quality or erroneous traces can dilute the voting process, leading to suboptimal outcomes.

**Recent Advances in Confidence Estimation:**
Recognizing the need to differentiate trace quality, recent work has explored leveraging next-token distribution statistics, such as entropy and confidence scores, to assess the reliability of reasoning paths (Geng et al., 2024; Fadeeva et al., 2024; Kang et al., 2025). These methods compute a global confidence measure across an entire trace to filter out low-quality ones, thereby enhancing majority voting.

**DeepConf's Contribution and Position:**
DeepConf positions itself by critically analyzing the limitations of these global confidence measures:
1. **Lack of Local Detail:** Global measures may obscure critical confidence fluctuations at local reasoning steps, masking specific breakdowns even if the overall trace seems plausible.
2. **Post-Hoc Application:** Global confidence measures require generating complete reasoning traces before quality assessment, preventing early stopping of unpromising generations and thus failing to improve computational efficiency.

DeepConf directly tackles these issues by introducing a method that leverages *local* confidence measurements for dynamic filtering and early stopping. It builds upon the established idea of confidence-aware selection (Kang et al., 2025) but significantly advances it by enabling *real-time* quality assessment and computational savings *during* token generation. This places DeepConf at the forefront of research aimed at making LLM reasoning both more accurate and significantly more efficient, a critical step towards broader and more sustainable deployment of advanced LLM capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of "Deep Think with Confidence" is to develop a simple, yet powerful, test-time method that simultaneously enhances both the reasoning efficiency and performance of Large Language Models.

The core motivations driving this research stem from the inherent challenges of existing LLM reasoning techniques:

1. **Addressing Computational Overhead:** Current state-of-the-art reasoning methods, particularly those relying on parallel thinking like self-consistency, demand substantial computational resources. Generating hundreds or thousands of reasoning traces for a single query incurs high inference costs, making these techniques impractical for many real-world applications where latency and throughput are critical. The authors are motivated to reduce this overhead significantly.
2. **Mitigating Diminishing Returns and Quality Disparity:** Standard majority voting, while effective, often exhibits diminishing returns; beyond a certain point, adding more traces does not proportionally increase accuracy and can even degrade performance. This is largely because all traces are treated equally, failing to account for variations in reasoning quality. Many generated traces may be erroneous or low-quality, yet they contribute to the final vote, potentially leading to incorrect answers. The motivation is to introduce a mechanism that intelligently prioritizes or filters out unreliable reasoning paths.
3. **Overcoming Limitations of Global Confidence Measures:** While recent work has shown the utility of internal confidence signals for assessing trace quality, existing methods typically apply these globally (e.g., averaging confidence over an entire trace). This approach has two key limitations:
 - It can mask localized reasoning failures, where a few high-confidence tokens might obscure critical errors in intermediate steps.
 - It necessitates the generation of *complete* traces before assessment, thereby preventing the crucial efficiency gain of early stopping. The authors are motivated to develop more fine-grained, local confidence signals that can detect errors mid-generation.
4. **Ease of Integration and Practicality:** A significant motivation is to propose a method that requires no additional model training or hyperparameter tuning. This design choice makes DeepConf highly practical, allowing for seamless integration into existing LLM serving frameworks and widespread adoption without complex retraining pipelines.

In essence, DeepConf seeks to enable LLMs to "think deeper" by focusing compute on high-quality reasoning, while "thinking with confidence" by leveraging intrinsic model signals to intelligently prune low-quality or redundant thought processes, thereby achieving superior accuracy at a fraction of the traditional computational cost.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DeepConf's methodology centers on leveraging model-internal confidence signals to dynamically filter or terminate low-quality reasoning traces, operating in both offline and online modes.

#### 3.1. Confidence Measurements

The paper introduces several confidence metrics, moving beyond simple average trace confidence to capture more localized and critical signals of reasoning quality:

- **Token Entropy (Eq. 1):** Measures the uncertainty of a model's prediction for a single token. Lower entropy implies higher certainty.
- **Token Confidence (Eq. 2):** Defined as the negative average log-probability of the top-k tokens. Higher confidence indicates a more peaked distribution and greater certainty.
- **Average Trace Confidence (Eq. 3):** The mean of token confidences across an entire reasoning trace. While useful, the paper highlights its limitation in obscuring local failures.
- **Group Confidence (Eq. 4):** A more localized and smoother signal, calculated by averaging token confidences over a sliding window (group) of `n` previous tokens. This addresses the masking issue of global averages.
- **Bottom 10% Group Confidence (Eq. 5):** A critical metric that focuses on the mean of the *lowest 10%* of group confidences within a trace. This effectively identifies traces with significant "low-confidence" segments, which often correlate with reasoning breakdowns.
- **Lowest Group Confidence (Eq. 6):** Represents the confidence of the single least confident group in a trace (a special case of Bottom 10% Group Confidence). This metric is particularly vital for the online early stopping mechanism.
- **Tail Confidence (Eq. 7):** Measures the average confidence over the final portion (e.g., last 2048 tokens) of a reasoning trace. This is motivated by the observation that errors often occur or become evident towards the end of long reasoning chains.

Figure 2 illustrates that Bottom 10% and Tail Confidence metrics show better separation between correct and incorrect trace distributions compared to mean confidence, suggesting their superior effectiveness for quality estimation.

#### 3.2. Offline Thinking with Confidence (Algorithm 1)

In the offline setting, DeepConf aims to improve answer aggregation from a pre-generated pool of complete reasoning traces.

- **Standard Majority Voting:** The baseline approach where each trace's answer contributes equally.
- **Confidence-Weighted Majority Voting:** DeepConf enhances this by weighting each trace's vote by its associated trace-level confidence (C_t), chosen from the metrics above (e.g., Bottom 10% Group Confidence). This prioritizes answers from higher-confidence traces.
- **Confidence Filtering:** To further refine the process, DeepConf can filter traces, retaining only the top-η percent of traces based on their confidence scores before applying weighted majority voting.
 - **η = 10% (Aggressive):** Focuses on the highest-confidence traces, suitable when a few very reliable paths are expected.
 - **η = 90% (Conservative):** A more balanced approach that maintains diversity and reduces bias by including a broader range of plausible traces.

#### 3.3. Online Thinking with Confidence (Algorithm 2)

The online mode allows DeepConf to estimate trace quality in real-time *during* generation, enabling dynamic termination of unpromising traces to save computational resources. This utilizes the **Lowest Group Confidence** metric.

- **DeepConf-low and DeepConf-high:** Two online algorithms based on Lowest Group Confidence, designed to adaptively stop generation.
- **Offline Warmup:** Before full online generation, an initial set of `N_init` traces (e.g., 16) are generated to establish a stopping threshold `s`. This `s` is defined as the (100-η)th percentile of the Lowest Group Confidences from the warmup traces, where η=10% for DeepConf-low and η=90% for DeepConf-high. This threshold ensures that traces terminated online would likely be filtered out by the offline policy.
- **Adaptive Sampling:**
 1. During token generation for a new trace, the group confidence of the current token sequence is continuously monitored.
 2. If the current group confidence falls below the pre-calibrated threshold `s`, the trace generation is immediately halted (early stopping).
 3. Completed (or early-stopped but sufficiently confident) traces are aggregated using confidence-weighted majority voting.
 4. Generation for the current problem continues until a predefined consensus threshold (`β ≥ τ`) among the collected answers is met, or a maximum trace budget (`B`) is reached. This dynamically adjusts the number of traces generated based on problem difficulty.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

DeepConf was evaluated extensively across:
- **Models:** DeepSeek-8B, Qwen3-8B/32B, GPT-OSS-20B/120B.
- **Benchmarks:** AIME24, AIME25, BRUMO25, HMMT25 (mathematical reasoning), and GPQA-Diamond (graduate-level STEM reasoning).
- **Baselines:** Pass@1 (single-trace accuracy) and Cons@K (unweighted majority voting with K traces).
- **Evaluation Protocol:** A pool of 4,096 complete reasoning traces was pre-generated for each problem. Experiments involved resampling working sets of size K (e.g., 512) and averaging results over 64 independent runs to ensure robustness. Metrics included accuracy and total generated tokens, with early-terminated traces contributing only tokens produced before stopping.

The modular design and rigorous evaluation across diverse models and benchmarks underscore the robustness and generalizability of DeepConf's approach.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on DeepConf demonstrate significant improvements in both reasoning accuracy and computational efficiency across various LLMs and challenging reasoning benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Offline Evaluations

In the offline setting, where complete reasoning traces are available, DeepConf significantly outperforms standard majority voting (Cons@512) by intelligently weighting and filtering traces based on confidence.

- **Accuracy Gains:** Confidence-aware weighting with filtering consistently yielded higher accuracies.
 - **η=10% Filtering:** This aggressive filtering strategy, focusing on the top 10% most confident traces, showed the largest gains. For example:
 - DeepSeek-8B on AIME25 improved from 82.3% (Cons@512) to 87.4% (DeepConf@512 with Bottom-10% Conf).
 - Qwen3-32B on AIME24 increased from 85.3% to 90.8%.
 - Notably, GPT-OSS-120B achieved an impressive 99.9% accuracy on AIME 2025, saturating the benchmark, compared to 97.0% for standard self-consistency and 91.8% for Pass@1.
 - **η=90% Filtering:** The more conservative approach typically matched or slightly exceeded standard majority voting, providing a safer option when aggressive filtering might risk "overconfidence on incorrect problems."
- **Effectiveness of Local Confidence Measures:** Both local (Tail Conf and Bottom-10% Group Conf) and global (Average Trace Conf) measures proved effective in identifying confident traces, with local signals often providing equal or better accuracy. Lowest Group Confidence, in particular, consistently showed strong performance gains, highlighting its utility in pinpointing traces with localized reasoning breakdowns.
- **Improvements over Pass@1:** All DeepConf methods showed substantial improvements over single-trace accuracy (Pass@1), confirming the value of ensemble approaches when combined with confidence signals.

#### 4.2. Online Evaluations

The online mode of DeepConf, which dynamically terminates low-confidence traces during generation, yielded remarkable token savings while largely preserving or improving accuracy.

- **Token Reduction and Efficiency:**
 - **DeepConf-low (η=10%):** This variant achieved substantial computational savings, reducing generated tokens by 43% to 84.7% compared to full parallel thinking (Cons@512). For instance, on AIME 2025 using GPT-OSS-120B, DeepConf-low saved 84.7% of tokens while improving accuracy from 97.1% to 97.9%.
 - **DeepConf-high (η=90%):** This more conservative variant still saved 18% to 59% of tokens across benchmarks, while maintaining nearly identical accuracy to the baseline or incurring only minimal degradation.
- **Accuracy-Cost Trade-offs:** DeepConf methods consistently demonstrate superior efficiency-accuracy Pareto frontiers. DeepConf-low, for example, achieved a mean token saving of 62.88% (DeepSeek-8B) at comparable accuracy levels to standard majority voting.
- **Adaptive Sampling:** The adaptive stopping mechanism based on consensus (`β ≥ τ`) further contributed to token savings by halting generation once sufficient confidence in a modal answer was achieved. Ablation studies showed that a consensus threshold of τ=0.95 offered the optimal balance between token savings and accuracy preservation.
- **Robustness Across Models and Tasks:** The benefits of DeepConf were observed across a wide range of models (from 8B to 120B parameters) and challenging reasoning tasks, demonstrating the method's robustness and generalizability.

In summary, DeepConf successfully addresses the two major limitations of conventional LLM reasoning methods: it significantly reduces computational overhead by dynamically pruning low-quality traces, and it boosts reasoning accuracy by ensuring that the final answer aggregation is heavily weighted towards the most reliable reasoning paths.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

"Deep Think with Confidence" presents a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Model reasoning, with far-reaching implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Efficiency for Real-world Deployment:** The most immediate and impactful contribution is the substantial reduction in computational overhead for LLM reasoning tasks. By achieving token savings of up to 84.7% while maintaining or improving accuracy, DeepConf makes advanced reasoning techniques like self-consistency much more practical and cost-effective for real-world deployment. This efficiency is critical for applications requiring high throughput, low latency, or operation within resource-constrained environments.
2. **Improved Reasoning Performance:** Beyond efficiency, DeepConf consistently boosts reasoning accuracy, especially on highly challenging mathematical and STEM benchmarks. The method's ability to intelligently filter out low-quality reasoning traces, rather than treating all traces equally, allows LLMs to arrive at more reliable conclusions. Achieving 99.9% accuracy on AIME 2025 with GPT-OSS-120B is a testament to its effectiveness in saturating challenging benchmarks.
3. **No Additional Training Overhead:** A key strength of DeepConf is that it requires no additional model training or hyperparameter tuning. It operates purely at test-time by leveraging existing model-internal confidence signals. This "plug-and-play" nature makes it exceptionally easy to integrate into current LLM serving frameworks, accelerating its adoption and impact across diverse applications without requiring costly and time-consuming retraining.
4. **Broader Applicability and Generalizability:** The extensive evaluation across a range of open-source models (DeepSeek, Qwen3, GPT-OSS series) and challenging datasets (AIME, BRUMO, HMMT, GPQA-Diamond) demonstrates the method's robustness and wide applicability. This suggests DeepConf can generalize well across different LLM architectures and reasoning domains.
5. **New Paradigm for Test-Time Optimization:** DeepConf highlights the immense potential of sophisticated test-time compression and selection strategies as a scalable solution for efficient LLM reasoning. It suggests that significant performance and efficiency gains can still be extracted from existing LLMs by smarter inference-time algorithms, rather than solely relying on larger models or more extensive pre-training.

**Future Work:**
The authors identify several promising future directions:
- **Reinforcement Learning Integration:** Extending DeepConf to RL settings could leverage confidence-based early stopping to guide policy exploration, potentially improving sample efficiency during training.
- **Robust Confidence Calibration:** Addressing instances where models exhibit high confidence on incorrect reasoning paths ("confidently wrong" predictions) is crucial. Future work can explore more robust confidence calibration techniques and uncertainty quantification methods to better identify and mitigate such erroneous predictions, further refining the filtering process.

In conclusion, DeepConf represents a practical, scalable, and impactful innovation for LLM reasoning. By intelligently harnessing internal confidence signals for dynamic pruning and refined aggregation, it paves the way for more efficient and performant AI systems, democratizing access to powerful LLM capabilities and pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with current model architectures.
