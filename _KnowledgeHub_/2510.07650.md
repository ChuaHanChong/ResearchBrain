# Value Flows

> [!summary] Summary
> Value Flows leverages flow-based generative models to estimate full, continuous future return distributions, providing a more granular understanding of uncertainty than existing discrete or quantile-based methods. This approach achieved competitive or superior performance in offline and offline-to-online reinforcement learning benchmarks, demonstrating a 3x lower 1-Wasserstein distance to ground-truth distributions and up to a 40% mean improvement on certain tasks.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning primarily uses scalar Q-values, which average out crucial information about the spread and shape of future returns.
- Existing distributional RL methods use coarse approximations like discrete bins or a finite set of quantiles, limiting expressiveness and potentially overlooking fine-grained structural details or true return uncertainty.
- The inability to accurately quantify aleatoric uncertainty hinders robust learning and application in areas like exploration and safe RL.

## Method

- Developed **Value Flows**, a framework using ==flow-based generative== models to estimate the full, continuous probability density function of future returns.
- Formulated a novel distributional flow-matching objective that ensures the generated probability density paths satisfy the distributional Bellman equation.
- Introduced a distributional conditional flow matching (DCFM) loss for practical estimation and a bootstrapped conditional flow matching (BCFM) loss for regularization to stabilize learning.
- Quantified aleatoric uncertainty (return variance) directly from the learned flow models using an efficient flow derivative ODE and employed confidence weighting to prioritize learning at uncertain transitions.

## Results

- Value Flows achieved a 1-Wasserstein distance **3x** lower than the best-performing baseline, demonstrating a more accurate alignment with ground-truth return distributions.
- Outperformed or matched all baselines on 9 out of 11 OGBench and D4RL domains in offline RL, including a **40%** mean improvement on easier state-based tasks and **1.24x** better performance on image-based tasks.
- Showcased strong fine-tuning performance in offline-to-online RL, achieving **15%** higher performance on tasks like puzzle-4x4-play and demonstrating improved sample efficiency compared to baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that BCFM regularization boosted performance by **2.6x** and confidence weighting, which prioritizes uncertain transitions, increased the average success rate by **60%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Flow-matching models can effectively represent complex, continuous return distributions, capturing fine-grained structural details and multi-modality more accurately than discrete or quantile-based methods.
> - The distributional Bellman equation can be satisfied by learning a time-dependent vector field within a flow-matching framework, ensuring theoretical convergence guarantees.
> - Efficiently computable aleatoric uncertainty, derived from the flow derivative ODE, provides a powerful signal to adaptively reweight learning objectives, leading to more robust and accurate return estimation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) commonly involves estimating future returns as a single scalar value, primarily through Q-functions. However, a subfield known as Distributional RL posits that modeling the entire future return distribution offers richer information, providing stronger learning signals and enabling applications in areas such as exploration and safe RL. Prior work in Distributional RL has primarily parameterized return distributions as categorical distributions over discrete bins (e.g., C51) or by estimating a finite number of quantiles (e.g., IQN, CODAC). While these methods have demonstrated convergence guarantees and performance on various benchmarks, they face limitations regarding the expressiveness of the estimated distributions, potentially overlooking fine-grained structural details or the ability to accurately identify states with high return uncertainty.

Concurrently, modern generative models, including flow-matching and diffusion models, have achieved proficiency in representing complex, multi-modal distributions in continuous spaces. These models have seen applications within RL for tasks such as modeling trajectories, transitions, skills, and policies.

This paper positions itself at the intersection of Distributional RL and generative modeling. It aims to address the limitations of existing distributional RL methods by leveraging the flexibility and expressiveness of flow-based generative models. By adopting flow-matching, the work seeks to estimate the full, continuous return distribution, which can provide a more granular understanding of return uncertainty compared to discrete or quantile-based approximations. The research specifically targets offline RL and offline-to-online RL problems, contexts where learning from fixed datasets or fine-tuning with limited online interaction is critical. The work also builds upon prior approaches that use confidence weights derived from Q-estimates to reweight learning objectives, extending this concept to incorporate aleatoric uncertainty (return variance) derived from the expressive flow models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method, termed Value Flows, that uses flexible flow-based models to estimate the full future return distributions in reinforcement learning environments. This approach is motivated by the recognized limitations of existing RL paradigms and aims to fulfill specific desiderata:

1. **Estimating the full distribution without coarse-graining:** Traditional RL often relies on scalar Q-value estimation, which averages away potentially critical information about the spread and shape of future returns. Even distributional RL methods that exist often discretize the return space into bins or approximate it with a finite set of quantiles. This coarse-graining can obscure fine-grained details of the return distribution, which might be crucial for nuanced decision-making. Value Flows seeks to model the continuous probability density function of returns directly, thereby avoiding such information loss.

2. **Learning probability densities satisfying the distributional Bellman backup while preserving convergence guarantees:** A core principle in RL is the Bellman equation, which describes the relationship between the value of a state and the values of successor states. In distributional RL, this translates to the distributional Bellman equation, where the distribution of returns for a state-action pair is related to the distribution of returns for the next state-action pair. A key objective is to ensure that the learned flow-based models, which represent these distributions, adhere to this fundamental relationship. Furthermore, the approach must maintain the theoretical convergence guarantees associated with the Bellman operator to ensure stable and reliable learning. The paper aims to formulate a flow-matching objective that inherently satisfies this distributional Bellman equation.

3. **Prioritizing learning a more accurate return estimation at transitions with high variance:** The ability to represent the full return distribution inherently allows for the computation of its statistics, such as variance. This variance represents aleatoric uncertainty—the inherent stochasticity in the environment's transitions and rewards. A significant motivation is to leverage this uncertainty information to improve the learning process itself. Specifically, the researchers aim to use areas of high return variance as indicators of transitions where the model's return estimation requires greater precision. By adaptively reweighting the learning objective to prioritize these uncertain transitions, the method intends to focus computational resources where they can yield the most benefit in terms of accuracy and robustness.

In summary, the motivation stems from the belief that a more expressive, continuous representation of return distributions can yield a richer learning signal, enable better uncertainty quantification, and ultimately lead to more robust and higher-performing RL agents, particularly in complex offline and offline-to-online settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Value Flows framework introduces a novel approach to modeling future return distributions in RL using flow-matching models.

**Core Idea and Distributional Bellman Equation:**
The central methodology involves formulating a *distributional flow-matching objective* that ensures the generated probability density paths satisfy the distributional Bellman equation. This equation, defined as $p^{\star}(z_t | t, s, a) = \frac{1}{\gamma} E_{p(s'|s,a),\pi(a'|s')} [p^{\star}(\frac{z_t - r(s,a)}{\gamma} | t, s', a')]$, governs the evolution of the return distribution. The method aims to learn a time-dependent vector field $v(z_t | t, s, a)$ that, when integrated, produces a flow $\phi$ whose probability density path $p(z_t | t, s, a)$ converges to the true discounted return distribution $p_{Z^{\pi}}(z | s, a)$ at flow time $t=1$.

**Vector Field Update Rule and Loss Functions:**
The paper constructs an iterative update rule for the vector field $v_{k+1}$ and the probability density path $p_{k+1}$ based on the distributional Bellman operator. Proposition 1 formally demonstrates that if $v_k$ generates $p_k$, then $v_{k+1}$ (as defined by the Bellman operator on $p_k$) generates $p_{k+1}$. This theoretical link allows the problem of estimating the return distribution to be reframed as learning a vector field within the flow-matching framework.

Initially, a *distributional flow matching (DFM)* loss ($L_{DFM}$) is proposed, minimizing the mean squared error between the current vector field and the target $v_{k+1}$. However, this loss is impractical due to unknown transition probabilities and intractable expectations. To address this, the *distributional conditional flow matching (DCFM)* loss ($L_{DCFM}$) is introduced. This loss allows estimation using samples from the dataset $D$ and is proven to have the same gradient as $L_{DFM}$ (Proposition 2), ensuring it guides the learning towards the correct minimizer.

To stabilize learning and prevent vector field degeneration, Value Flows incorporates a *bootstrapped conditional flow matching (BCFM)* loss ($L_{BCFM}$) as a regularization term. This loss utilizes a bootstrapped target return, similar to TD targets in Q-learning, and employs a target vector field $\bar{v}$ (an exponential moving average of historical vector fields) to prevent model collapse. The final critic objective is a weighted sum: $L_{ValueFlows}(v) = L_{wDCFM}(v) + \lambda L_{wBCFM}(v)$, where $\lambda$ is a regularization coefficient and a confidence weight $w(s,a,\epsilon)$ is applied.

**Harnessing Uncertainty in Return Estimation:**
A key advantage of the flow-based model is its ability to quantify uncertainty.
1. **Return Expectation (Q-value):** The expected return $E[Z^{\pi}(s,a)]$ (the Q-value) is estimated by taking the expectation of the initial vector field $v(\epsilon | 0, s, a)$ over Gaussian noise $\epsilon$ (Proposition 3, building on prior work).
2. **Return Variance (Aleatoric Uncertainty):** The return variance $Var(Z^{\pi}(s,a))$ is estimated using a first-order Taylor approximation of the diffeomorphic flow $\phi(\epsilon_0 | 1, s, a)$ around $\epsilon$ (Proposition 3). Crucially, computing the flow derivative $\partial\phi/\partial\epsilon$ (required for variance) is made efficient by observing that it satisfies a *flow derivative ODE* (Proposition 4), which can be solved numerically alongside the main flow ODE using vector-Jacobian products, avoiding costly backpropagation through ODE solvers.
3. **Confidence Weighting:** The estimated variance is then used to construct a confidence weight $w(s,a,\epsilon) = \sigma((\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\epsilon}(\epsilon | 1, s, a))^{-\tau} + 0.5)$. This weight, an increasing function of return variance, prioritizes learning at state-action pairs exhibiting higher aleatoric uncertainty, aiming to improve the accuracy of return distribution estimation where stochasticity is high.

**Policy Extraction Strategies:**
Value Flows supports two policy extraction strategies:
1. **Offline RL:** A behavioral cloning (BC) flow policy $\pi_{\beta}$ is trained. Actions are selected via rejection sampling, where a set of candidate actions from $\pi_{\beta}$ are proposed, and the one maximizing the Q-estimate (derived from the flow model) is chosen. This implicitly imposes a KL constraint towards the BC policy.
2. **Offline-to-Online RL:** A one-step flow policy $\pi$ is learned, optimized to maximize the Q-estimates while being regularized towards the fixed BC flow policy, using a DDPG-style loss with behavioral regularization. This provides flexibility for online fine-tuning.

The entire algorithm (Algorithm 1) involves training the return vector field, the BC flow policy, and optionally the one-step flow policy using stochastic gradient descent, with Polyak averaging for the target vector field.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to evaluate Value Flows across various continuous control tasks in both offline and offline-to-online settings, addressing four key questions.

**Q1: Alignment with Underlying Return Distribution (Section 5.1)**
Value Flows demonstrated a notable ability to predict return distributions that align with the underlying ground-truth distributions. Visualizations showed that Value Flows produced smooth return histograms, closely resembling the ground truth. In contrast, existing distributional RL baselines like C51 generated noisy multi-modal distributions, while CODAC often collapsed to a single return mode. Quantitatively, Value Flows achieved a 1-Wasserstein distance 3x lower than the best-performing baseline on a representative scene-play OGBench task, indicating a more accurate and faithful representation of the return distribution.

**Q2: Offline RL Performance (Section 5.2, Table 1)**
In offline RL, Value Flows exhibited competitive or superior performance across a wide array of benchmarks. It matched or surpassed all baselines on 9 out of 11 domains from OGBench and D4RL.
- On easier state-based OGBench tasks (e.g., cube-double-play, scene-play), Value Flows achieved a 40% mean improvement over baselines.
- For more challenging state-based tasks, Value Flows achieved success rates 1.6x higher than the best-performing baseline.
- On image-based visual tasks, Value Flows outperformed the best baseline by 1.24x, demonstrating its capability to handle high-dimensional observations.

**Q3: Offline-to-Online Sample Efficiency (Section 5.2, Figure 3, Table 4)**
Value Flows showed strong fine-tuning performance and high sample efficiency during the online fine-tuning phase of offline-to-online RL. For example, on the puzzle-4x4-play task, Value Flows achieved a 15% higher performance compared to prior offline-to-online RL algorithms. The method consistently outperformed baselines across a selection of 6 OGBench tasks when leveraging online interactions.

**Q4: Key Components of Value Flows (Section 5.3, Figures 4, 5)**
Ablation studies provided insights into the contributions of key components:
- **BCFM Regularization Loss ($\lambda$):** The bootstrapped conditional flow matching (BCFM) regularization loss was identified as important for learning progress. Removing this regularization ($\lambda=0$) resulted in poor performance. Appropriate tuning of the regularization coefficient $\lambda$ boosted Value Flows' performance by 2.6x and improved sample efficiency by 2x.
- **Confidence Weight ($\tau$):** The confidence weight, which prioritizes learning at uncertain transitions, proved beneficial. Employing an appropriate temperature $\tau$ for the confidence weight led to a 60% increase in average success rate. A constant weight (equivalent to $\tau=0$) resulted in slower convergence or slightly worse performance.
- **Number of Flow Steps:** Value Flows demonstrated robustness to the number of flow steps used in the Euler method for the return vector field, particularly after a threshold of 10 steps. However, using a very small number of steps (e.g., 1 or 2) could drastically reduce success rates on more complex tasks.
- **Rejection Sampling Candidates:** The number of candidates used in rejection sampling for action selection had a minor impact on Value Flows' success rates, with 16 candidates generally found to be sufficient.

**Additional Findings:**
- **Computational Efficiency:** Analysis indicated that Value Flows required intermediate computational time and GPU memory compared to baselines, typically comparable to FQL and more efficient than IQN in terms of wall time and GPU memory.
- **Risk-Sensitive Control:** In a Machine Replacement MDP requiring exploration and risk-sensitive control, Value Flows converged to the optimal CVaR with a similar number of gradient steps as IQN, outperforming C51 and traditional Q-learning which struggled with risk sensitivity.
- **Policy Type for D4RL Adroit Tasks:** On D4RL Adroit tasks, using Gaussian BC policies instead of flow BC policies for rejection sampling improved Value Flows' performance by 10%, matching state-of-the-art results, suggesting potential challenges for expressive flow policies with narrow dataset distributions.
- **Role of $L_{BCFM}$:** An ablation showed that the $L_{BCFM}$ loss primarily serves as regularization, with the $L_{DCFM}$ loss providing the main learning signal for Value Flows.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Value Flows framework represents an advancement in the field of distributional reinforcement learning, offering a flexible and expressive approach to modeling the complete distribution of future returns.

**Methodological Advancements:**
The core innovation lies in the formulation of a novel distributional flow-matching objective. This objective effectively transforms the problem of estimating complex, continuous return distributions into learning a time-dependent vector field that adheres to the distributional Bellman equation. This method moves beyond the limitations of discrete categorical or quantile-based representations, allowing for a more accurate capture of the fine-grained structure, continuity, and multi-modality of return distributions. The introduction of a flow derivative ODE for efficient computation of return variance directly from the learned flow models is a significant technical contribution, avoiding computationally expensive gradient backpropagation through ODE solvers.

**Enhanced Performance and Robustness:**
The empirical results demonstrate that Value Flows consistently achieves competitive or superior performance compared to state-of-the-art offline RL and offline-to-online RL methods across a diverse set of challenging continuous control tasks, including both state-based and image-based environments. The observed improvements in success rates, sometimes by substantial margins, indicate that the more accurate and flexible return distribution estimation translates into better policy learning. Furthermore, its strong performance in online fine-tuning suggests improved adaptability and sample efficiency when interacting with the environment.

**Quantification and Utilization of Uncertainty:**
The ability to efficiently estimate the aleatoric uncertainty (return variance) directly from the learned flow model is a key aspect of Value Flows. Leveraging this information through a confidence weighting mechanism to prioritize learning on uncertain transitions contributes to more robust and focused training. This explicit quantification of environmental stochasticity provides a richer signal for decision-making compared to methods that only estimate expected values.

**Potential for Broader Applications:**
The framework has implications for various advanced RL applications:
- **Risk-Sensitive Control:** As demonstrated in the risk-sensitive MDP experiment, the full return distribution enables the derivation of risk measures (e.g., CVaR), allowing policies to be optimized not just for expected return, but also for risk profiles, which is crucial in safety-critical domains.
- **Exploration:** A detailed understanding of uncertainty could guide more informed exploration strategies in environments with high stochasticity.
- **Safe RL:** By explicitly modeling the distribution of outcomes, Value Flows could contribute to the development of safer RL agents that can better anticipate and mitigate undesirable, high-risk outcomes.

The work also highlights the flexibility of the flow-matching paradigm in RL, showing its compatibility with different policy extraction mechanisms (rejection sampling, DDPG-style policy distillation) and its applicability to diverse task types. This research provides a foundational step towards more comprehensive and reliable return modeling in reinforcement learning.
