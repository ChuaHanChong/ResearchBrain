# ConRFT: A Reinforced Fine-tuning Method for VLA Models via Consistency Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> ConRFT, a reinforced fine-tuning method, adapts pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for real-world robotic manipulation by combining offline and human-in-the-loop online fine-tuning with a consistency policy. The method achieved a 96.3% average success rate across eight diverse tasks, representing a 144% improvement over supervised fine-tuning and 1.9x shorter episode lengths compared to offline baselines.

## Problem

- Pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lack the precision and robustness needed for complex, contact-rich real-world robotic tasks.
- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for VLA models is constrained by the limited quantity and consistency of human-collected demonstration data, leading to suboptimal performance.
- Direct application of reinforcement learning (RL) to real-world VLA robotics is impractical due to severe safety risks, high data collection costs, and poor sample efficiency.

## Method

- Introduces **ConRFT**, a two-stage reinforced fine-tuning pipeline: Cal-**ConRFT** for offline pre-training and HIL-**ConRFT** for online adaptation.
- Employs a computationally lightweight consistency policy as the VLA model's action head, which is robust to inconsistent demonstrations.
- Cal-**ConRFT** combines ==Behavior Cloning== (BC) with Calibrated Q-Learning (==Cal-QL==) to initialize a robust policy from small, potentially inconsistent, offline datasets, while HIL-**ConRFT** uses adaptive-weighted online reinforcement learning with human-in-the-loop (HIL) interventions to ensure safety and accelerate learning.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **96.3%** across eight diverse real-world robotic manipulation tasks after 45-90 minutes of online fine-tuning.
- Demonstrated a **144%** improvement in average success rate and **1.9x** shorter episode lengths (30.7 steps) compared to initial supervised fine-tuning baselines.
- Outperformed training from scratch approaches (e.g., HIL-SERL at **31.9%** success rate) and other RL-based or supervised methods like HG-DAgger (**65%**) and PA-RL (**71.3%**) within the same training duration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining Behavior Cloning (BC) with a Q-learning objective allows for robust policy and value function learning from small, inconsistent datasets, providing a stable foundation for online fine-tuning.
> - Human-in-the-loop (HIL) interventions are crucial for ensuring safety during real-world robot exploration and significantly accelerate policy convergence by guiding learning.
> - Beginning with a pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model drastically improves sample efficiency and final performance compared to training a policy from scratch, minimizing risky online exploration.
