# ConRFT: A Reinforced Fine-tuning Method for VLA Models via Consistency Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> ConRFT, a reinforced fine-tuning method, adapts pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for real-world robotic manipulation by combining offline and human-in-the-loop online fine-tuning with a consistency policy. The method achieved a 96.3% average success rate across eight diverse tasks, representing a 144% improvement over supervised fine-tuning and 1.9x shorter episode lengths compared to offline baselines.

## Problem

- Pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lack the precision and robustness needed for complex, contact-rich real-world robotic tasks.
- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for VLA models is constrained by the limited quantity and consistency of human-collected demonstration data, leading to suboptimal performance.
- Direct application of reinforcement learning (RL) to real-world VLA robotics is impractical due to severe safety risks, high data collection costs, and poor sample efficiency.

## Method

- Introduces **ConRFT**, a two-stage reinforced fine-tuning pipeline: Cal-**ConRFT** for offline pre-training and HIL-**ConRFT** for online adaptation.
- Employs a computationally lightweight consistency policy as the VLA model's action head, which is robust to inconsistent demonstrations.
- Cal-**ConRFT** combines ==Behavior Cloning== (BC) with Calibrated Q-Learning (==Cal-QL==) to initialize a robust policy from small, potentially inconsistent, offline datasets, while HIL-**ConRFT** uses adaptive-weighted online reinforcement learning with human-in-the-loop (HIL) interventions to ensure safety and accelerate learning.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **96.3%** across eight diverse real-world robotic manipulation tasks after 45-90 minutes of online fine-tuning.
- Demonstrated a **144%** improvement in average success rate and **1.9x** shorter episode lengths (30.7 steps) compared to initial supervised fine-tuning baselines.
- Outperformed training from scratch approaches (e.g., HIL-SERL at **31.9%** success rate) and other RL-based or supervised methods like HG-DAgger (**65%**) and PA-RL (**71.3%**) within the same training duration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining Behavior Cloning (BC) with a Q-learning objective allows for robust policy and value function learning from small, inconsistent datasets, providing a stable foundation for online fine-tuning.
> - Human-in-the-loop (HIL) interventions are crucial for ensuring safety during real-world robot exploration and significantly accelerate policy convergence by guiding learning.
> - Beginning with a pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model drastically improves sample efficiency and final performance compared to training a policy from scratch, minimizing risky online exploration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper positions itself at the intersection of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, reinforcement learning (RL), and real-world robotic manipulation. The broader research landscape is characterized by:

- **Emergence of Generalist Robotic Policies (VLA Models):** Recent years have seen significant progress in training large-scale, generalist robotic policies, often through large-scale imitation learning and grounding with robot actions (e.g., Open X-Embodiment, RT-2). These VLA models possess powerful representations and can understand and execute various manipulation tasks based on visual and language inputs.
- **Challenges in Real-World Deployment:** Despite their impressive capabilities, pre-trained VLA models frequently fall short when deployed in complex, real-world scenarios. They often lack the fine-grained precision or robustness needed for contact-rich tasks or novel configurations not seen in their vast pre-training datasets. This necessitates fine-tuning for domain-specific tasks.
- **Limitations of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) using human teleoperation data is a common adaptation approach for VLA models. However, this method is heavily reliant on the quality and quantity of task-specific data. Human-collected datasets inherently suffer from issues like suboptimality, inconsistency, and limited state coverage, leading to performance plateaus and fragility in real-world contact-rich environments.
- **Reinforcement Learning's Role in Large Models:** RL has proven highly effective in fine-tuning Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) (e.g., Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback - RLHF), bridging the gap between model capabilities and human preferences or improving reasoning. This success has sparked interest in applying RL to VLA models.
- **Unique Challenges for RL in VLA Robotics:** Direct application of RL to VLA models in real-world robotics is uniquely challenging. Unlike LLMs that operate in synthetic environments, VLA models require direct physical interaction. This introduces severe safety and cost constraints, demanding exceptionally high sample efficiency and risk-aware exploration to prevent damage to the robot or environment.
- **Existing RL Approaches for Robotics:** While some real-world RL systems exist, many require prolonged training sessions or large amounts of interaction data, which is impractical for contact-rich tasks. Previous attempts to leverage RL for VLA models primarily focused on data augmentation or quality improvement (e.g., using RL to collect expert demonstrations for SFT), rather than directly optimizing the VLA model through RL objectives to explore beyond the demonstration data.
- **Offline-to-Online RL Paradigms:** Offline-to-online RL is a relevant field that aims to bootstrap policies from pre-collected datasets and then fine-tune them online. However, these methods often assume the availability of large, diverse offline datasets, a condition rarely met in real-world robotic deployments where data collection is expensive.

This paper, ConRFT, directly addresses these gaps. It proposes a novel reinforced fine-tuning pipeline that intelligently combines offline pre-training (Cal-ConRFT) with online fine-tuning (HIL-ConRFT) specifically for VLA models. By starting with a pre-trained VLA model, it mitigates the "training from scratch" problem common in RL, which demands excessive exploration. Furthermore, by integrating a consistency policy (a computationally lightweight diffusion-based action head) and human-in-the-loop (HIL) learning, it tackles the crucial issues of sample efficiency and real-world safety, enabling effective fine-tuning even with small initial datasets. It departs from prior RL-for-VLA works by directly optimizing the VLA model via RL objectives in conjunction with behavior cloning, allowing for exploration of new states and behaviors that go beyond initial demonstrations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind ConRFT stems from the acknowledged limitations of existing fine-tuning methods for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in real-world robotic manipulation. While pre-trained VLA models demonstrate considerable promise, their direct application in complex, contact-rich environments often falls short due to several critical challenges:

1. **Limited and Inconsistent Demonstrations:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), the predominant method for adapting VLA models, heavily relies on human-collected demonstrations. These datasets are frequently small, contain suboptimal trajectories, and exhibit inconsistencies in actions due to human variability. This inherent "data quality" problem prevents SFT from achieving robust and generalizable performance, especially when precise control is required.
2. **Lack of Exploration in SFT:** SFT inherently restricts the policy to the behaviors observed in the demonstration data. It struggles to generalize to out-of-distribution (OOD) states or explore more optimal ways of completing a task, limiting the policy's ability to recover from errors or improve beyond the demonstration quality.
3. **Challenges of Direct Reinforcement Learning (RL) in Real-World Robotics:** While RL offers the benefit of learning from interaction and optimizing for task-specific rewards, its direct application to real-world VLA models is problematic. Robotic tasks, particularly contact-rich ones, pose significant safety risks (potential damage to the robot or environment) and incur high costs due to extensive data collection requirements. Traditional RL often demands vast amounts of interaction data, leading to low sample efficiency and potentially unsafe exploratory behaviors in early training phases.
4. **Ineffectiveness of Prior RL-based Approaches for VLA Models:** Existing works that attempt to incorporate RL for VLA models often focus on indirect applications, such as using RL for data augmentation or quality improvement, rather than directly optimizing the VLA model's policy through RL objectives. This limits the policy's ability to genuinely explore and learn from novel experiences in real-world settings.

Against this backdrop, the **key objectives** of the ConRFT project are:

1. **Develop a Reinforced Fine-Tuning (RFT) Approach for VLA Models:** Propose a novel pipeline, ConRFT, that leverages the strengths of reinforcement learning to efficiently fine-tune pre-trained VLA models for real-world robotic manipulation.
2. **Integrate Offline and Online Fine-tuning with a Unified Objective:** Design a two-stage process – an offline fine-tuning stage (Cal-ConRFT) and an online fine-tuning stage (HIL-ConRFT) – that share a consistent, unified training objective based on a "consistency policy." This aims to provide a stable initialization and seamless transition between stages.
3. **Address Data Scarcity and Inconsistency in the Offline Stage:** Enable effective policy extraction and stable value estimation from a *small* set of initial demonstrations by combining behavior cloning (BC) with calibrated Q-learning (Cal-QL). This prepares the VLA model to handle out-of-distribution (OOD) states before real-world interaction.
4. **Ensure Safety and Sample Efficiency in the Online Stage:** Facilitate rapid policy adaptation in the real world by utilizing task-specific rewards and integrating human interventions (Human-in-the-Loop, HIL). HIL learning is crucial for guiding safe exploration and accelerating learning, particularly in high-stakes robotic environments.
5. **Achieve Superior Performance and Efficiency:** Demonstrate that the proposed ConRFT framework can significantly outperform existing supervised fine-tuning methods and other RL-based baselines in terms of success rate and sample efficiency (measured by episode length and training time) across diverse real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

In essence, the motivation is to bridge the gap between powerful pre-trained VLA models and their robust, safe, and efficient deployment in real-world robotic applications by intelligently injecting reinforcement learning capabilities while mitigating its inherent risks and data demands.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ConRFT (Consistency-based Reinforced Fine-Tuning) proposes a two-stage pipeline for fine-tuning pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for real-world robotic manipulation tasks: an **offline fine-tuning stage (Cal-ConRFT)** and an **online fine-tuning stage (HIL-ConRFT)**. A unified, consistency-based training objective is employed across both stages. The base VLA model chosen for experiments is Octo-small, known for its balance of performance and inference efficiency, and it is augmented with a consistency policy as its action head.

**A. VLA Model and Consistency Policy Action Head:**
The approach assumes access to a pre-trained VLA model, $\pi_{\phi_{pre}}$, which encodes high-level representations from visual inputs and language instructions. For fine-tuning, this pre-trained VLA model ($E_\phi(s)$) is coupled with a **consistency policy** ($f_\psi(a_k, k | E_\phi(s))$) that serves as the action head.
- **Consistency Policy**: This is a diffusion-model-based policy that learns to map random actions sampled from a unit Gaussian distribution to expert-like actions conditioned on the current state. Its advantages are computational lightweightness for efficient inference and robustness to inconsistent/sub-optimal demonstrations compared to standard diffusion models or simple BC action heads. The diffusion process is discretized over a horizon, ensuring efficient action generation.
- The overall VLA policy is $\pi_\psi(a|s) = f_\psi(a_k, k | E_\phi(s))$.

**B. Stage I: Offline Fine-tuning with Cal-ConRFT**
The goal of this stage is to initialize a robust policy and value function from a small, pre-collected offline dataset (typically 20-30 demonstrations), before any real-world online interaction. This reduces the exploration burden in the subsequent online stage.
- **Challenge**: Standard offline RL methods like Calibrated Q-Learning (Cal-QL) struggle with very small datasets due to limited state coverage, leading to poor value estimates and generalization issues.
- **Proposed Solution**: Cal-ConRFT addresses this by augmenting Cal-QL with a Behavior Cloning (BC) loss.
 - **Cal-QL for Critic**: The Q-function ($Q_\theta$) is trained using Cal-QL's objective ($L_{offline}^Q(\theta)$). This objective minimizes the Temporal Difference (TD) error while also regularizing Q-values for out-of-distribution (OOD) actions, penalizing them if they exceed the value of a reference policy and compensating for actions within the dataset. This helps in robust value estimation.
 - **Unified Policy Objective**: The consistency policy is fine-tuned using a combined objective:
 $L_{offline}^\pi(\psi) = \beta L_{BC}^\pi + \eta L_Q^\pi$
 - **Behavior Cloning (BC) Loss** ($L_{BC}^\pi$): Directly minimizes the difference between policy-generated actions and demonstrated actions. This provides a strong supervisory signal, especially critical when demonstrations are few, ensuring the model imitates known good behaviors.
 - **Q-Loss** ($L_Q^\pi$): Encourages the policy to select actions that maximize the Q-value, steering it towards high-reward behaviors and providing guidance for state-action values.
- **Benefits**: This combination allows for efficient policy learning and stable value estimation even with limited demonstrations, providing a reliable initialization for safe and effective exploration in the online stage.

**C. Stage II: Online Fine-tuning with HIL-ConRFT**
This stage further fine-tunes the VLA model through real-world interaction, addressing limitations of offline data scope and quality.
- **Data Management**: A replay buffer ($R$) is introduced to store newly collected online interaction data, alongside the existing demo buffer ($D$) from the offline stage. Training batches are formed by symmetrically sampling from both $D$ and $R$. This continuously updates the policy with its own generated data, mitigating distribution shift issues.
- **Critic Update**: The Q-function is updated using a standard Q-loss ($L_{online}^Q(\theta)$) on the combined $(D \cup R)$ buffer.
- **Unified Policy Objective (Adaptive Weights)**: The policy objective remains the same in structure as offline:
 $L_{online}^\pi(\psi) = \beta L_{BC}^\pi + \eta L_Q^\pi$
 - **Adaptive Weights**: Crucially, the weights $\beta$ (for BC loss) and $\eta$ (for Q-loss) are adjusted: $\beta$ typically decreases, and $\eta$ increases during online training.
 - **Importance of Retaining BC Loss**: The BC loss is retained during online fine-tuning for two reasons:
 1. **Preventing Policy Collapse**: It ensures the policy remains aligned with demonstration data, preventing drastic deviations (catastrophic forgetting) that could lead to unsafe or inefficient behaviors in contact-rich tasks.
 2. **Stabilizing Exploration**: In high-dimensional state-action spaces, RL exploration can be unstable. BC loss provides a stabilizing effect, preventing the policy from straying too far from a reasonable baseline, thereby reducing risk.
- **Human-in-the-Loop (HIL) Learning**: Human operators can intervene during online exploration.
 - **Mechanism**: If the robot engages in destructive, unrecoverable, or undesirable behaviors, a human operator can take over control via teleoperation tools (e.g., SpaceMouse).
 - **Data Integration**: These human corrective actions and the resulting transitions are added to the demo buffer ($D$).
 - **Benefits**: HIL ensures safety by preventing hazardous situations, guides exploration towards safer and more efficient directions, and accelerates policy convergence by overcoming local optima or correcting critical errors that would otherwise take significant autonomous steps to resolve.

The overall algorithm alternates between policy learning (updating $\psi$ and $\theta$) and real-world interaction (collecting data into $R$ or $D$ with HIL), forming a closed-loop system for continuous improvement. The VLA model's visual encoders and transformer backbone are kept frozen during fine-tuning for real-time performance, with only the action head (consistency policy) being updated.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of ConRFT is thoroughly evaluated through extensive real-world experiments on eight diverse robotic manipulation tasks, designed to represent a spectrum of challenges from object placement to precise, contact-rich assembly and soft object handling. The evaluation uses a 7-DoF Franka Emika robot arm, with the Octo-small model as the VLA backbone and a consistency policy as the action head. Performance is measured by success rate and episode length, with comparisons against various supervised and reinforcement learning baselines.

**A. ConRFT Outperforms Supervised Methods:**
- **Overall Superiority:** ConRFT achieves an impressive average success rate of **96.3%** across all tasks after just 45-90 minutes of online fine-tuning. This represents a remarkable **144% improvement** over the initial Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) baseline.
- **Comparison to SOTA Baselines:**
 - **HG-DAgger (Supervised with Human Correction):** Achieved an average success rate of 65%. It struggled with contact-rich tasks (e.g., Insert Wheel, Hang Chinese Knot) due to the inherent suboptimality and inconsistency of human corrections, which can introduce noise and conflicting information, hindering the policy's ability to learn precise behaviors.
 - **PA-RL (Policy-Agnostic RL):** Achieved an average success rate of 71.3%. While it showed more stable improvement in simpler tasks by optimizing actions based on reward signals, it failed to improve policy performance in complex, precise manipulation tasks (e.g., Insert Wheel). This limitation is attributed to the policy-agnostic Q-function's inability to generalize effectively to variations in state transitions critical for precise insertion, given limited state coverage.
- **Efficiency in Episode Length:** HIL-ConRFT demonstrated superior task completion efficiency with an average episode length of **30.7 steps**, which is **1.9x shorter** than the offline baselines. In contrast, HG-DAgger (56.3 steps) and PA-RL (51.1 steps) showed less significant improvements in episode length, indicating that supervised methods and conservative RL approaches struggle to find more efficient paths to task completion.

**B. Fine-tuning VLA Outperforms Training From Scratch:**
- **HIL-SERL (RL from Scratch):** An approach that trains policies through RL from scratch with human interventions, HIL-SERL, achieved an average success rate of only **31.9%** within the same training duration.
- **Benefits of Pre-trained VLA:** ConRFT, by starting from a pre-trained VLA model and undergoing offline fine-tuning, significantly accelerates policy convergence and enhances final performance. This highlights that leveraging powerful pre-trained representations as a strong initialization drastically reduces online training time and the need for extensive, risky exploration compared to learning from scratch. HIL-SERL typically requires over two hours of online training per task with higher intervention rates to reach comparable performance.

**C. Detailed Analysis and Further Comparisons:**
- **Why Cal-ConRFT for Initialization?** While Cal-ConRFT's offline success rate was similar to SFT, initiating online fine-tuning from Cal-ConRFT proved more stable. Learning curves showed that starting from SFT led to higher intervention rates in early online training, indicating severe "policy forgetting." Cal-ConRFT, by incorporating Q-loss in the offline stage, provides a more stable foundation for online adaptation, enabling more effective policy improvement with small demonstration datasets. Cal-QL alone performed poorly (0% success rate) with limited demos.
- **Impact of More Demonstrations for SFT:** Even when provided with a larger quantity of human-collected demonstrations (150 demos, comparable to total data used by ConRFT) for Diffusion Policy (DP) and standard SFT, their success rates (41.7% and 58.3% respectively) failed to match ConRFT's performance, especially in contact-rich tasks. This underscores that simply increasing the volume of inconsistent human data does not guarantee significant performance gains. In contrast, RLDG (fine-tuning with 150 demonstrations collected by an RL policy) achieved 83.3% success, suggesting the consistency and optimality of RL-generated data are crucial, which ConRFT directly optimizes for.
- **Practicality Across Various VLA Models:** ConRFT demonstrated versatility by effectively fine-tuning RoboVLM with two different VLM backbones (Kosmos-2 and PaliGemma). By fine-tuning only the action head while keeping visual encoders and transformer backbones frozen, ConRFT consistently improved success rates to 100% (from 53.3% and 51.7% respectively). This confirms its broad applicability across various VLA architectures.

In summary, the experimental results convincingly demonstrate that ConRFT significantly advances the state-of-the-art in fine-tuning VLA models for real-world robotic manipulation. Its robust performance, high sample efficiency, and ability to handle complex tasks with limited initial demonstrations highlight the effectiveness of integrating consistency policies with a reinforced fine-tuning framework and human-in-the-loop guidance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ConRFT framework presents a significant advancement in the field of robotic learning, particularly for deploying Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in real-world environments. Its significance and potential impact can be articulated as follows:

**A. Significance:**

1. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real and Data-Efficiency Gaps:** ConRFT provides a practical and effective method for adapting powerful pre-trained VLA models to real-world complexities. It effectively addresses the inherent challenges of real-world robotic learning, namely the scarcity of high-quality, consistent demonstration data and the safety/cost implications of extensive online exploration. By leveraging a strong pre-trained VLA model and employing human-in-the-loop (HIL) learning, it drastically improves sample efficiency and safety compared to training policies from scratch.
2. **Robustness to Imperfect Data:** A key contribution is the ability to achieve high performance even when relying on a small set of initial, potentially inconsistent, and suboptimal human demonstrations. The integration of Behavior Cloning (BC) loss with a calibrated Q-learning objective in the offline stage, combined with the consistency policy, effectively extracts learnable signals from imperfect data and provides a stable initialization for subsequent online learning.
3. **Enhanced Policy Adaptation and Exploration:** Unlike purely supervised fine-tuning (SFT) methods that are limited by the scope of demonstration data, ConRFT's reinforcement learning component enables the policy to actively explore and optimize its behavior based on task-specific rewards. This leads to more efficient task completion (shorter episode lengths) and improved generalization to out-of-distribution states, allowing the robot to recover from unexpected situations or discover more optimal execution paths.
4. **Practicality and Generalizability:** The use of a computationally lightweight consistency policy as the action head ensures efficient inference and adaptability. Furthermore, the demonstrated success of ConRFT across different VLM backbones (Kosmos-2, PaliGemma within RoboVLM) highlights its versatility and potential applicability to a wide range of VLA architectures, making it a broadly useful tool for robot policy fine-tuning.
5. **Safe and Guided Real-World Learning:** The Human-in-the-Loop (HIL) component is crucial for ensuring safety during online exploration, especially in contact-rich tasks. Human interventions not only prevent destructive behaviors but also accelerate learning by providing high-level, corrective guidance, transforming potentially lengthy and risky autonomous exploration into a more focused and efficient learning process.

**B. Potential Impact:**

1. **Accelerated Deployment of VLA Models in Robotics:** By making the fine-tuning process significantly more sample-efficient and robust, ConRFT can dramatically reduce the time and resources required to deploy VLA models for new manipulation tasks. This could accelerate the adoption of generalist robot policies in various industrial, service, and research applications.
2. **Democratization of Robot Learning:** Reducing the reliance on massive, meticulously curated datasets of expert demonstrations lowers the barrier to entry for developing and deploying robotic skills. This could enable smaller labs or even individual users to fine-tune advanced robotic capabilities for specific needs, fostering innovation.
3. **Safer and More Reliable Robotic Systems:** The explicit incorporation of human supervision for safety during online training, combined with a policy that learns to be more robust and precise, contributes directly to the development of safer and more reliable robotic systems, particularly for tasks involving human interaction or delicate objects.
4. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens up avenues for future research in several directions:
 - **Advanced Reward Engineering:** The identified limitation regarding sensitivity to sparse reward engineering suggests a need for more robust, perhaps learned or human-in-the-loop, dense reward functions.
 - **Perception-Action Co-adaptation:** While the current work freezes the VLA model's visual encoders and transformer backbone, future research could explore parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods (e.g., LoRA) to allow for co-adaptation of perception and action, potentially leading to even higher performance and adaptability in novel scenarios.
 - **Scalability to More Complex Tasks:** The demonstrated success on diverse tasks suggests ConRFT could be a stepping stone towards fine-tuning for even more intricate, long-horizon, or multi-stage robotic operations.

In conclusion, ConRFT provides a compelling framework that effectively leverages the strengths of both pre-trained foundation models and reinforcement learning, making robotic skill acquisition more efficient, safer, and practical for real-world applications. It represents a significant step towards realizing truly capable and adaptable generalist robots.
