# CSWAM: Better Causal Semantic Representations for Out-of-Distribution Generalization in World Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> CSWAM augments FastWAM, an action-only world action model, with a causal semantic expert built on frozen V-JEPA 2.1. The expert encodes a sparse observation history, predicts future semantic features via flow matching, and shares history-derived context with video and action streams through masked Mixture-of-Transformers attention. It keeps action-only inference and lifts RoboTwin 2.0 Clean-to-Randomized success from 10.16% to 45.18% and real-robot OOD success from 27.5% to 70.0%.

## Problem

- FastWAM-style action-only world action models act on reconstruction-oriented VAE latents that preserve appearance details, so background or object changes collapse success under visual distribution shift.
- FastWAM's action expert sees only the current observation, lacking history that reveals motion, state transitions, and task progress, so policies latch onto training-scene-specific correlations.
- Semantic encoders like DINOv3 used in prior WAMs are framewise and do not model scene evolution, leaving unclear how temporally predictive features fit action-only inference.

## Method

- **Causal semantic expert**: frozen ==V-JEPA 2.1== encodes a strided sparse history of observations into ==tubelet tokens==, and a DiT predicts future V-JEPA features by ==flow matching==.
- ==Three-expert Mixture-of-Transformers== with ==structured attention mask==: video and action experts attend to observed semantic history, future video and semantic tokens interact, action tokens never see future targets.
- Joint ==flow-matching loss== over video, semantic, and action streams with shared video-semantic noise level; at inference future streams are dropped, observed context is cached once, and only the action expert denoises iteratively.

## Results

- RoboTwin 2.0 Clean-to-Randomized with embodied pretraining: Randomized success rises from **10.16%** to **45.18%** over FastWAM (**+35.02** points); without pretraining, only **1.82%** to **9.36%**.
- Real dual-arm robot, Sort and Fold across three OOD levels: average success rises from FastWAM's **27.5%** to **70.0%**; Hard and Extreme settings climb from **10%** to **55%**.
- RoboTwin2.0-Full mixed training: **94.5%** average versus FastWAM **91.9%**, best among listed baselines; ablations: removing video expert costs **8.1** Randomized points, DINOv3 swap **1.5**, no history **1.8**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Semantic prediction complements rather than replaces generative video prediction: dropping the video expert costs far more than dropping history, so augment reconstruction latents instead of swapping them out.
> - Mixed-training benchmarks hide OOD fragility: FastWAM scores near-equal Clean and Randomized when both are trained on, yet collapses once Randomized scenes are held out; evaluate held-out shifts.
> - Embodied pretraining and temporally predictive semantics are complementary: the same pretrained initialization yields about four times more OOD gain when paired with history-conditioned V-JEPA features.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A robot arm may learn to sort toys or fold a towel in familiar scenes. When the task stays the same but backgrounds, lighting, or object appearances change, the policy may fail. Collecting demonstrations for every possible scene is infeasible.

CSWAM keeps the present view that a conventional action-only model sees and adds a short, semantically encoded memory of what just happened. At deployment, it still predicts actions rather than generating a future video. In physical-robot tests, CSWAM had higher success than FastWAM in both tasks across the three tested shift levels. The hardest Fold setting remained difficult.

Right: real-robot success rate (%) after training in the collection environment. Sort: FastWAM 65%, 25%, 10%; CSWAM 100%, 95%, 75% (Normal, Hard, Extreme). Fold: FastWAM 60%, 5%, 0%; CSWAM 100%, 40%, 10%.

### 2. A causal memory, not a future video

The authors propose two reasons for the generalization gap. First, reconstruction-oriented visual features can preserve distracting appearance changes. Second, a policy given only the current frame lacks direct evidence of motion and task progress. These are proposed reasons, and the experiments do not uniquely establish them as causes.

A world action model (WAM) is a robot policy trained jointly to predict visual dynamics and actions. FastWAM's deployment design already avoids generating future video, but it conditions action denoising on current-observation context. CSWAM keeps that video-and-action backbone and adds a sparse history pathway.

The history pathway works in three steps:

- A frozen V-JEPA 2.1 encoder jointly encodes the history into temporally grounded semantic features.
- A trainable semantic expert learns how those features evolve in the future.
- Masked Mixture-of-Transformers attention shares the observed semantic history with the video and action experts. The mask prevents the action stream from reading future prediction targets.

Future semantic and video prediction therefore provide training objectives. They are not inputs to deployed actions.

At inference, the training-only groups are removed. The current video state and observed history are computed once and reused, and only action denoising is evaluated iteratively.

Future video and semantic tokens participate in training-time prediction, while the inference mask retains only observed context and action tokens.

### 3. Transfer to unseen appearances

The decisive simulation test is RoboTwin 2.0 Clean-to-Randomized. Policies are fine-tuned on 2,500 Clean demonstrations and then evaluated without adaptation on Clean (in-distribution) and Randomized (OOD) scenes. "Embodied PT" is a separate 5,000-hour robotics-data pretraining stage for the video/action initialization. CSWAM's semantic expert is initialized separately and is not pretrained on that corpus.

Success rates are averaged across 50 tasks, with 100 evaluation rollouts per task. With embodied pretraining, CSWAM reaches 45.18% success on the unseen Randomized setting, compared with FastWAM's 10.16%. That is a gain of 35.02 percentage points.

Success rates (%) after fine-tuning only on Clean demonstrations. With embodied PT, Randomized success rises from 10.16% (FastWAM) to 45.18% (CSWAM). "Average" averages the Clean and Randomized rates and is not the Randomized result.

The physical-robot tests provide a second, distinct kind of evidence. Each combination of task, difficulty, and model was run for 20 trials. All three difficulty levels use an unseen deployment environment:

- **Normal** changes the environment.
- **Hard** also changes the lighting and tabletop.
- **Extreme** additionally changes the task objects and containers.

Across toy sorting and towel folding at all three levels, CSWAM succeeds in 70.0% of 120 trials, compared with 27.5% for FastWAM. The hardest Fold setting remains difficult: CSWAM succeeds in 10% of trials, compared with 0% for FastWAM.

### 4. A better use of context

Under visual shift, the policy does not need examples of every appearance or a generated future video at deployment. CSWAM gives action prediction a temporally grounded account of recent observations while keeping action-only inference. The measured transfer gains support this design in the tested settings. The physical-robot results concern two tasks and the specified shifts, not every robot or unseen condition. The paper does not establish that CSWAM reduces elapsed time or total computation relative to FastWAM.
