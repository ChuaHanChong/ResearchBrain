# HaWMPO: Hallucination-Aware World Model-based Policy Optimization for Generalist Robot Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> HaWMPO is a closed-loop reinforcement-learning pipeline that post-trains VLA policies inside an action-conditioned video world model. A Hallucination-Aware Model scores each imagined eight-frame chunk for reliability, and a Reward-Soft mechanism downweights rewards of hallucinated chunks before GRPO advantage computation. Average LIBERO success rises 15.0 points over OpenVLA-OFT and 2.8 over the strongest baseline; real G1 success reaches 80.0%.

## Problem

- Online RL on physical robots for VLA policies demands costly interaction, is sample-inefficient, and risks hardware damage and safety incidents during trial-and-error exploration.
- Generative world models are not physical simulators, so autoregressive long-horizon rollouts accumulate prediction errors and hallucinations that bias state transitions and mislead policy updates.
- Prior world-model RL such as WMPO treats every imagined rollout as equally trustworthy, letting policies exploit generator biases instead of learning real-world-effective actions.

## Method

- **HaWMPO**'s ==Hallucination-Aware Model (HAM)==: frozen DINOv3 encoder, context and action projectors, four-layer fusion Transformer with action-to-visual cross-attention, learnable query and sigmoid head emitting chunk-level hallucination scores.
- ==Composite proxy supervision== built from DINOv3 similarity, depth consistency, optical-flow trajectory consistency (NDTW) and MUSIQ quality, min-max normalized then averaged into a continuous MSE regression target.
- ==Reward-Soft== shaping multiplies each chunk's raw reward by (1 - alpha*H) before group-relative advantage normalization, feeding hallucination-discounted rewards into ==GRPO== across eight parallel imagined environments.

## Results

- LIBERO average success reaches **63.7%** versus **48.7%** for base OpenVLA-OFT, **56.8%** for WMPO and **60.9%** for reproduced WoVR*, with Spatial highest at **77.2%**.
- HAM detects human-annotated hallucinations at **0.9375** AUROC and **0.8952** AP, beating reference-based DINO-only (**0.9062**) without observed future frames; MUSIQ-only collapses to **0.3056**.
- Ablating HAM with Reward-Soft costs **8.0-11.6 points** on Spatial; alpha=**0.3** suits Object and Spatial while Goal prefers alpha=**1**, reaching **66.2%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Image-quality scores barely correlate with hallucination; motion and semantic consistency dominate, so sharpness-based filters are poor reliability proxies for generated rollouts.
> - Rollout reliability can be learned reference-free, letting a critic score imagined trajectories during deployment when ground-truth future observations are unavailable.
> - Penalty strength is task-dependent: too strong discards useful imperfect experience, too weak lets generator bias through, so hallucination discounting needs tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist robot policies capable of strong generalization across diverse manipulation tasks represents a significant area of research in robotics. These policies, often based on Visual-Language-Action (VLA) models, aim to overcome the limitations of task-specific robots. However, a primary challenge in improving these policies lies in the high cost and sample inefficiency of real-world interactions required for online reinforcement learning (RL). Direct physical trials can also pose risks of hardware damage and safety concerns, hindering the scalability and widespread adoption of such methods.

World models have emerged as a promising paradigm to address these limitations by enabling policy optimization within an "imagination space." These models learn to simulate environment dynamics and generate synthetic experiences, thereby reducing the reliance on real-world interactions. Despite their potential, generative world models are not exact physical simulators. They are susceptible to accumulating prediction errors and generating "hallucinations" during long-horizon rollouts. These hallucinations, or biased state transitions, can mislead policy learning, causing the robot to exploit model inaccuracies rather than learn behaviors effective in real environments. Prior work, such as WoVR, has identified that hallucinated imagined rollouts can produce false learning signals, destabilizing reinforcement learning and degrading policy performance.

This work positions itself within this landscape by seeking to enhance the reliability of world model-based reinforcement learning for VLA policies. It specifically aims to mitigate the adverse effects of hallucinations, thereby enabling more robust and effective policy optimization in simulated environments, with subsequent transfer to physical robots. By addressing the challenge of unreliable synthetic experiences, this research contributes to making world models a more viable and dependable tool for developing advanced generalist robot policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and effective closed-loop reinforcement learning pipeline that can improve generalist robot policies using world models, specifically by addressing the issue of prediction hallucinations in imagined rollouts.

The key motivations driving this work are:

- **Reducing Real-World Interaction Costs:** Current methods for improving VLA policies often rely on extensive online reinforcement learning on physical robots. This process is inherently sample-inefficient, time-consuming, and expensive due to the need for numerous physical interactions. The authors aim to minimize this dependency by enabling policy optimization within a synthetic imagination space constructed by a world model.
- **Mitigating Risks in Physical Environments:** Direct online reinforcement learning on real robots carries inherent risks of hardware damage and safety issues, especially during exploratory phases of learning. Utilizing a world model as a virtual simulator can provide a safer environment for policy training, allowing for extensive trial and error without physical consequences.
- **Addressing World Model Hallucinations:** While world models offer a promising alternative to real-world interaction, their generative nature makes them prone to accumulating prediction errors and generating visual hallucinations during long-horizon rollouts. These hallucinations can lead to biased state transitions and misleading reward signals, causing the policy to learn from an inaccurate representation of the environment. The authors observe that existing world model-based RL methods can suffer from this "exploitation of world-model biases" rather than learning robust real-world actions.
- **Improving Policy Learning Stability and Performance:** By explicitly accounting for the reliability of world-model-generated experiences, the research aims to provide a more stable and effective learning signal for VLA policies. The goal is to prevent the policy from being derailed by unrealistic or inaccurate imagined trajectories, thereby improving its overall performance and generalization capabilities in real-world scenarios.

In summary, the motivation is to unlock the full potential of world models for generalist robot policy training by introducing a mechanism to discern and mitigate the impact of unreliable, hallucinated synthetic experiences, thereby providing a more faithful and effective learning environment for reinforcement learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Hallucination-aware World Model-based Policy Optimization (HaWMPO), is a closed-loop reinforcement learning pipeline designed for post-training VLA policies using world models. It addresses the issue of hallucinated imagined rollouts by explicitly estimating their reliability and modulating their influence on policy updates.

The HaWMPO pipeline consists of three core components integrated into a Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework: an action-conditioned world model, a hallucination-aware model (HAM), and a reward model, combined with a novel Reward-Soft mechanism.

#### 3.1. Action-Conditioned World Model

The world model serves as an interactive virtual simulator. It is an adaptation of Wan2.2-TI2V-5B, comprising a video variational autoencoder (VAE) and a latent-space diffusion Transformer (DiT).
- **Inputs:** At each step, it receives a five-frame visual context (a fixed reference frame and four recent frames), an associated action context, and an eight-step action chunk predicted by the VLA policy.
- **Output:** It generates eight subsequent future RGB frames (256x256 resolution), which constitute the imagined trajectory chunk. Robot actions (7-dimensional: 3 translational, 3 rotational, 1 gripper) are incorporated via an MLP projecting individual actions for cross-attention and grouped actions for diffusion-timestep embedding modulation.
- **Role in RL:** This world model is trained prior to policy optimization and remains frozen during the reinforcement learning phase. It provides the visual transitions for the VLA agent's online RL within the imaginative space.

#### 3.2. Hallucination-Aware Model (HAM)

The HAM is introduced to quantify the reliability of the video chunks generated by the world model. It operates independently of the world model to assess the quality of its predictions.
- **Inputs:** A predicted 8-frame video chunk (ˆV<sub>c</sub>), the conditioning actions (a<sub>c</sub>), the current observation (I<sub>c</sub>), and an initial anchor image (I<sub>0</sub>) of the rollout.
- **Supervision Construction:** To train HAM, the authors generate video chunks from real recorded actions in successful and failed trajectories and compare them with the corresponding observed sequences. Four quality-oriented metrics are used:
 1. **DINOv3 similarity:** Measures visual appearance similarity.
 2. **Depth consistency:** Captures geometric agreement.
 3. **Optical-flow-based trajectory consistency:** Assesses motion coherence.
 4. **MUSIQ image quality:** Evaluates general visual quality.

 Each metric is min-max normalized (s<sup>(m)</sup><sub>c</sub>), and a composite hallucination target (h<sup>⋆</sup><sub>c</sub>) is derived as 1 minus the average of these normalized scores. A higher h<sup>⋆</sup><sub>c</sub> indicates more severe hallucination.
- **Architecture (Fig. 2):**
 - A frozen DINOv3 encoder extracts visual features from predicted frames, current observation, and anchor image.
 - Context and Action Projectors map contextual images and actions into a shared feature space.
 - A four-layer Fusion Transformer combines these inputs using action-to-visual cross-attention, self-attention, and feed-forward layers.
 - A learnable query token aggregates the fused representations, followed by an MLP and sigmoid activation to output a continuous chunk-level hallucination score (ˆh<sub>c</sub>) between 0 and 1.
- **Training:** HAM is trained using a mean squared error (MSE) loss between its predicted score (ˆh<sub>c</sub>) and the composite target (h<sup>⋆</sup><sub>c</sub>) on a labeled dataset. During imagined rollouts, HAM predicts ˆh<sub>c</sub> without requiring observed future frames.

#### 3.3. Reward Model

The reward model provides a raw reward (R) for each action chunk based on the synthesized visual observations generated by the world model. This reward indicates task progress or completion. (Its specific architecture and training are not detailed in the paper but are assumed to be a standard component in such a pipeline).

#### 3.4. Reward-Soft Mechanism

This mechanism integrates the hallucination scores from HAM into the reward signal for reinforcement learning.
- **Function:** It modulates the raw reward (R<sub>c</sub>) from the reward model based on the average hallucination score (H<sub>c</sub>) of the corresponding action chunk.
- **Formula:** ˆR<sub>c</sub> = (1 − α · H<sub>c</sub>) · R<sub>c</sub>, where ˆR<sub>c</sub> is the modulated reward, and α is a tunable hyperparameter controlling the penalty strength. A higher H<sub>c</sub> (more hallucination) leads to a lower modulated reward, effectively down-weighting unreliable experiences.

#### 3.5. Hallucination-Aware World Model-Based Policy Optimization (HaWMPO Algorithm)

The overall training pipeline (Algorithm 1) leverages the GRPO framework:
1. **Initialization:** The pretrained VLA policy (π<sub>θ</sub>), world model (M<sub>ϕ</sub>), hallucination-aware model (H), and reward model (R) are utilized. Synthetic imaginative environments are initialized from M<sub>ϕ</sub>.
2. **Parallel Synthetic Rollouts:** The VLA policy (π<sub>θ</sub>) performs rollouts concurrently in G=8 parallel imaginative environments provided by M<sub>ϕ</sub>. This generates action-chunk-level trajectories.
3. **Hallucination Score Estimation:** For each generated action chunk, HAM computes its average hallucination score (H<sub>c</sub>).
4. **Reward-Soft Modulated Reward Calculation:** The reward model provides a raw reward (R<sub>c</sub>) for each chunk. This raw reward is then modulated by the Reward-Soft mechanism using H<sub>c</sub> to produce the hallucination-aware reward (ˆR<sub>c</sub>).
5. **GRPO-based Policy Post-training:** Group-wise normalized advantages are calculated from the modulated rewards (ˆR<sub>c</sub>). The VLA policy (π<sub>θ</sub>) is then updated by minimizing the GRPO loss, which incorporates these hallucination-aware rewards. This ensures that policy updates are guided by more reliable synthetic experiences.

The world model and HAM are trained separately and kept frozen during the policy optimization phase. The VLA policy is initialized from a supervised fine-tuned (SFT) base model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of HaWMPO was evaluated through simulation experiments on the LIBERO benchmark and real-world experiments on a G1 robot.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments on LIBERO Benchmark

- **Setup:** Experiments were conducted on three LIBERO task suites (Spatial, Goal, and Object). The base model was OpenVLA-OFT, initially supervised fine-tuned with one expert trajectory per task. The world model served as a fixed simulator for VLA policy optimization. Policy training involved 200 reinforcement learning steps.
- **Baselines:** OpenVLA-OFT-base, WMPO, and WoVR*. WoVR* was a reproduced baseline that did not include the original PACE strategy for fair comparison under the fixed world model protocol.
- **Overall Performance (Table 1):** HaWMPO achieved the highest average success rate of 63.7%. This represented a 15.0 percentage point improvement over the vanilla OpenVLA-OFT policy, a 6.9 percentage point improvement over WMPO, and a 2.8 percentage point improvement over the strongest baseline, WoVR*.
- **Task-Specific Performance:** HaWMPO obtained the best results on the Spatial (77.2%) and Object (52.2%) task suites, surpassing WoVR* by 8.0 and 2.6 percentage points, respectively. Its performance on the Goal suite was comparable to WoVR*.
- **Conclusion:** These results indicate that world model-based reinforcement learning can enhance VLA policy capabilities, and that explicitly modeling rollout hallucinations and integrating them through the Reward-Soft mechanism leads to more robust policy optimization and improved performance in the LIBERO simulation.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments on a G1 Robot

- **Setup:** HaWMPO was tested on a physical G1 robot across two manipulation tasks: "Tissue-to-Box" and "Headphone-on-Stand." Each method underwent 20 trials per task.
- **Baselines:** SFT-trained OpenVLA-OFT base policy and WoVR.
- **Results (Table 2):**
 - **Tissue-to-Box:** HaWMPO achieved an 85% success rate (17/20), compared to 75% (15/20) for the base VLA and 80% (16/20) for WoVR.
 - **Headphone-on-Stand:** HaWMPO achieved a 75% success rate (15/20), compared to 60% (12/20) for the base VLA and 65% (13/20) for WoVR.
 - **Average Success Rate:** HaWMPO achieved an average success rate of 80.0% across the two tasks, which was 12.5 percentage points higher than the SFT baseline (67.5%) and 7.5 percentage points higher than WoVR (72.5%).
- **Conclusion:** These preliminary real-world results suggest that HaWMPO can transfer its benefits to physical manipulation tasks, indicating its potential for practical application.

#### 4.3. Analysis of the Reward-Soft Mechanism

- **Hyperparameter α:** An ablation study investigated the impact of the α hyperparameter, which controls the strength of the hallucination penalty in the Reward-Soft mechanism.
- **Findings (Fig. 4):** The optimal α value was task-dependent. A moderate penalty (α = 0.3) was found to be effective for Object and Spatial tasks, leading to the best and most stable results. For the Goal task, which experienced more severe hallucination accumulation, a stronger penalty (α = 1) yielded better performance. Training with a low penalty (α = 0.1) consistently resulted in slower improvement across all tasks, underscoring the importance of hallucination-aware reward modulation.

#### 4.4. Analysis of the Hallucination-Aware Model (HAM)

- **Evaluation:** HAM's ability to detect hallucinations was assessed against human annotations on 50 LIBERO video chunks (18 hallucinated, 32 non-hallucinated).
- **Performance (Table 3):** HAM achieved the highest AUROC (0.9375) and average precision (AP) (0.8952) among the evaluated methods. Reference-based metrics (DINO-only, Trajectory-only, Depth-only) also provided informative signals but required observed future frames, unlike HAM. The MUSIQ-only metric performed substantially worse (AUROC 0.3056, AP 0.3750), suggesting image quality alone is insufficient for hallucination detection.
- **Regression Metrics:** HAM achieved an MAE of 0.0174, an RMSE of 0.0250, and a Spearman correlation of 0.9310 against the composite proxy targets, indicating strong agreement with its constructed supervision.
- **Qualitative Examples (Fig. 5):** Qualitative analysis confirmed that higher HAM scores corresponded to more evident discrepancies from observed frames, while lower scores indicated closer matches.
- **Conclusion:** HAM provides a reliable, reference-free signal for detecting world-model hallucinations, supporting its use in the Reward-Soft mechanism.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study of Hallucination-Aware Model and Reward-Soft

- **Setup:** This study assessed the combined impact of removing both HAM and the Reward-Soft mechanism.
- **Findings (Table 4):**
 - **Spatial and Object:** Removing HAM resulted in a clear performance degradation. On Spatial, the full model consistently improved success rates by 8.0 to 11.6 percentage points at various training steps. On Object, the full model also showed higher success rates at most steps.
 - **Goal:** Under a fixed α=0.3 setting, the full model slightly lagged the ablated variant, though the difference was minor (within 2.4 points). However, when α was tuned to 1 for Goal (as identified in the Reward-Soft analysis), the full HaWMPO model (66.2%) surpassed the ablated variant (64.0%).
- **Conclusion:** The ablation study confirmed that explicitly modeling world-model hallucinations and incorporating them into policy optimization via the Reward-Soft mechanism significantly improves the reliability of virtual rollouts and reduces the negative impact of low-quality generated trajectories on policy updates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents HaWMPO, a hallucination-aware world model-based reinforcement learning pipeline, which offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of robot learning.

#### 5.1. Significance of Contributions

- **Enhanced Reliability of World Model-Based RL:** The core contribution is addressing the critical problem of prediction hallucinations in world models, which traditionally limit their utility for policy optimization. By introducing the Hallucination-Aware Model (HAM) and the Reward-Soft mechanism, HaWMPO explicitly quantifies and mitigates the impact of unreliable synthetic experiences. This enhances the fidelity of the "imagination space," making world models a more trustworthy simulator for reinforcement learning.
- **Closed-Loop VLA Reinforcement Learning without Extensive Real-World Interaction:** The pipeline provides a robust framework for online-style policy optimization for VLA models that significantly reduces the dependency on costly and risky physical robot interactions. This is crucial for scaling up robot learning, making it more accessible and efficient.
- **Action-Conditioned Hallucination Quantification:** The design of HAM, which takes action inputs into account to predict the reliability of generated video sequences, represents a step toward more nuanced and context-aware assessment of world model predictions. Providing a continuous hallucination score that can be directly used in the RL loop is a practical advancement.
- **Mitigation of World Model Bias:** The Reward-Soft mechanism strategically penalizes hallucinated action chunks, preventing the VLA policy from exploiting inaccuracies or biases within the world model. This leads to more stable and effective policy updates, guiding the agent to learn behaviors that are robust in real-world dynamics.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Accelerated Robot Skill Acquisition:** By enabling more efficient and reliable training in simulation, HaWMPO could significantly speed up the development and refinement of complex robot manipulation skills for generalist policies.
- **Reduced Development Costs and Risks:** The ability to train policies without constant real-world interaction can lower the financial burden and mitigate the risks associated with hardware damage and safety incidents, making advanced robotics more deployable.
- **Improved Generalization:** By learning from more reliable synthetic data, generalist robot policies trained with HaWMPO may exhibit stronger generalization capabilities to novel scenarios and long-horizon tasks in the real world, as demonstrated by the initial real-world experiments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a framework for integrating reliability awareness into world model-based learning. It opens avenues for future research into more sophisticated hallucination detection mechanisms, adaptive penalty schemes, and joint optimization of world models and policies.
