# Keyframe-Guided Structured Rewards for Reinforcement Learning in Long-Horizon Laboratory Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Science and Technology of China developed a framework that generates structured, dense rewards for reinforcement learning in long-horizon laboratory robotics by automatically extracting kinematics-aware keyframes from demonstrations. The approach achieved an 82% average success rate across four complex laboratory tasks, notably reaching 100% on "Petri Dish De-lidding" and "Precision Liquid Transfer," significantly outperforming existing baselines.

## Problem

- Long-horizon, multi-stage laboratory tasks suffer from reward sparsity, leading to inefficient exploration and unstable convergence in high-dimensional action spaces.
- Standard reinforcement learning methods struggle to leverage the strict physical and logical constraints inherent in laboratory protocols.
- Existing approaches often rely on high-quality expert demonstrations or labor-intensive manual reward engineering, limiting scalability and robustness.

## Method

- A Latent Dynamics-Based Keyframe Extraction module automatically identifies critical operational states (keyframes) from potentially non-expert demonstrations using multi-view latent encoding and kinematics-aware analysis.
- A Generative Diffusion-Based Online Reward System constructs dense, stage-wise rewards by measuring the multi-view latent similarity between the robot's current state and a sequence of keyframe-anchored target states.
- The framework integrates human-in-the-loop policy optimization with a pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model (Octo) backbone, utilizing a mixed-buffer strategy for efficient learning.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved an average success rate of **82%** across four real-world laboratory tasks (e.g., Petri Dish De-lidding, Precision Liquid Transfer), significantly outperforming baselines like HG-DAgger (**42%**) and Hil-ConRFT (**47%**).
- Learning curves demonstrated substantially faster policy convergence and a rapid decrease in human intervention rates, approaching near zero, indicating high sample efficiency.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the kinematics-aware keyframe extraction mechanism significantly improved step-wise coverage (**0.933** vs. **0.742**) and core milestone recall (**0.938** vs. **0.525**), and removing keyframe-guided rewards led to severe performance drops and erroneous behaviors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Automatically identifying kinematics-aware keyframes from demonstration data is crucial for capturing the intrinsic procedural logic of complex tasks and generating effective intermediate guidance.
> - Providing dense, stage-wise rewards derived from these structural keyframes effectively mitigates reward sparsity and significantly accelerates policy convergence in high-precision, long-horizon manipulation.
> - Combining multi-view perception for robust state representation, keyframe-guided rewards, and human-in-the-loop fine-tuning on a VLA backbone enables efficient and robust learning for delicate laboratory operations.
