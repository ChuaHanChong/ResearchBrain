# World Action Planner: Generalizable Decision-Making with Action-Conditioned World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Harvard University developed the World Action Planner (WAP), a system that combines Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with an action-conditioned world model, enabling robots to execute diverse tasks in novel and compositional environments. WAP achieved high success rates (e.g., 72% on compositional tasks, 88-90% on new layouts) by systematically proposing, imagining, and refining action plans, outperforming existing end-to-end imitation learning methods.

## Problem

- End-to-end (E2E) imitation learning policies struggle to generalize to novel scenes, compositional tasks, or out-of-distribution scenarios, often overfitting to specific demonstration data.
- These E2E methods frequently lack the ability to infer physical causality or synthesize novel action sequences, limiting their adaptability beyond direct training examples.
- Vision-Language Models (VLMs), while proficient in high-level planning and reasoning, inherently lack the physical grounding required to generate executable, physically plausible robot actions.

## Method

- Developed the **World Action Planner (WAP)**, which integrates a VLM agent with a novel ==pose-image conditioned world model== to enable generalizable robotic decision-making.
- Utilized a pose-image conditioned multi-view world model that represents abstract actions as future robot joint positions rendered into visual ==pose skeleton images==, serving as a generalizable, embodiment-agnostic interface.
- Implemented a systematic action planning pipeline where the VLM proposes initial actions, the world model simulates their outcomes, and the VLM provides iterative semantic feedback for ==global optimization== and ==local search== to refine the action sequence.

## Results

- The pose-image conditioned world model demonstrated an **11.4%** average improvement in prediction accuracy (PSNR/LPIPS) on in-distribution data and a **16.8%** average improvement in zero-shot generalization to novel actions/scenes compared to baselines.
- WAP achieved significantly higher success rates on compositional tasks (e.g., **72%** for LIBERO-Long vs. **0-4%** for baselines) and tasks with new object layouts (e.g., **88-90%** for LIBERO-Object tasks vs. near-zero for baselines).
- In zero-shot Robosuite tasks, WAP achieved high success rates (**80%** for PickPlaceCan, **76%** for StackCube) without expert demonstrations, surpassing baseline vision-language planners, with ablation studies confirming the contribution of each pipeline component.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing robot actions via pose-images provides a scalable and embodiment-agnostic conditioning mechanism for world models, enhancing their generalization across diverse robot hardware and action spaces.
> - Model-based planning, which combines VLM reasoning with iterative world model imagination, offers superior multi-task generalization compared to imitation learning, supported by theoretical analysis in tabular and linear MDP settings.
> - The iterative feedback loop, where a VLM evaluates imagined world model rollouts, effectively addresses physical safety concerns (e.g., collisions) and allows for fine-grained action refinement to achieve precise manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Developing generalizable robotic agents that can perform diverse tasks in varied environments remains a central challenge in artificial intelligence and robotics. Current approaches frequently rely on end-to-end (E2E) imitation learning policies, which utilize pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) or video generation models as foundational components. Examples include Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and World Action Models (WAMs). While these E2E methods demonstrate proficiency in their specific training domains, they often exhibit limitations when confronted with novel scenes, compositional tasks, or scenarios requiring systematic reasoning beyond the scope of their demonstration data. These limitations include overfitting to spurious motion patterns, an inability to infer physical causality, and difficulties in synthesizing novel action sequences for out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks.

In contrast, classical robotics often employs programmatic abstractions for environment and action representation, facilitating modular composition and generalization. However, modern foundation models, while capable of high-level planning, typically lack inherent physical grounding required for directly generating executable actions.

This work positions itself at the intersection of these paradigms. It moves beyond the E2E imitation learning approach by proposing a principled planning system. This system integrates the reasoning capabilities of VLMs with the physical grounding provided by an action-conditioned world model. The research aims to bridge the gap between high-level semantic understanding from VLMs and the precise physical interactions required for robot manipulation, thereby enabling more robust generalization capabilities than current state-of-the-art E2E policies. It draws on advancements in robot world models (e.g., diffusion-based video backbones, pose-conditioned models), planning with VLMs and world models (e.g., sampling-based optimization, video planners), and VLMs for high-level robot decision-making. The paper also incorporates theoretical insights that suggest model-based planning offers advantages in multi-task generalization compared to imitation learning.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop generalizable robotic agents capable of mastering diverse tasks across heterogeneous and novel environments. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations:

- **Addressing Limitations of End-to-End Imitation Learning:** A significant motivation stems from observed shortcomings in state-of-the-art end-to-end (E2E) imitation learning policies. These policies, while effective in training environments, frequently fail to generalize to novel scenes, layouts, or compositional tasks. This failure is attributed to their fundamental dependence on demonstration data, leading to overfitting of specific motion patterns and an inability to adapt to scenarios requiring reasoning beyond the exact trajectories observed during training. For instance, policies trained on individual pick-and-place tasks may struggle with transitions between sub-tasks in longer compositional sequences or with handling shifted object positions in new layouts.
- **Bridging High-Level Reasoning and Physical Grounding:** The authors aim to integrate the advanced reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with a robust understanding of physical causality and dynamics. While VLMs can generate high-level action plans and instructions, they inherently lack direct comprehension of real-world physics, making it challenging for them to produce executable, physically plausible actions. This work is motivated by the need to bridge this gap, ensuring that VLM-generated plans are physically viable and safe.
- **Enabling Systematic Model-Based Planning:** The research is motivated by the potential of model-based planning to offer superior multi-task generalization. By training a generalizable world model on diverse trajectories, the system can synthesize new action sequences for unseen tasks and configurations through imagination-based planning. This approach allows for a systematic process of proposing, simulating, and iteratively refining action plans, which is a departure from reactive E2E policies tied to expert demonstrations.
- **Facilitating Iterative Refinement and Error Correction:** A key motivation is to enable agents to iteratively refine proposed actions based on imagined future states. VLMs, while powerful, can propose actions that lead to physical consequences like collisions. The system is motivated to incorporate mechanisms for detecting such issues via world model rollouts and to allow the VLM agent to provide corrective feedback, thereby optimizing action sequences for safe and effective execution.
- **Improving Fine-Grained Manipulation:** For tasks requiring high precision, such as grasping a specific part of an object, VLMs may struggle with metric grounding. The motivation here is to develop mechanisms, such as local search combined with VLM-based ranking of imagined outcomes, to address these subtle positioning inaccuracies and achieve fine-grained manipulation.
- **Providing Theoretical Justification:** The work is also motivated by the desire to provide theoretical justifications for the benefits of model-based planning over imitation learning, particularly in multi-task generalization settings, in both tabular and linear function approximation contexts.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The World Action Planner (WAP) system integrates a VLM agent with an action-conditioned world model to achieve generalizable robotic decision-making. The methodology consists of two main components: a novel pose-image conditioned world model and a systematic action planning pipeline.

#### 3.1. Pose-Image Conditioning for Action-Controlled Robot World Modeling

The core of the WAP's physical grounding is a generalizable, pose-image conditioned multi-view world model. This component addresses limitations of prior robot action-conditioned video diffusion models that typically rely on low-dimensional action vectors, which often struggle to generalize to novel actions.

- **Action Representation:** Instead of low-dimensional action vectors, the system pre-computes future robot joint positions by executing forward dynamics based on the abstract action commands. These positions are then rendered as a "pose skeleton image" from corresponding camera viewpoints. This pose-image acts as a semantically rich visual condition for the world model.
- **Decoupling Physical Simulation:** The forward dynamics provide a mapping between abstract actions and visual prompts (pose-images). While these forward-simulated poses may not perfectly capture real-world physics (e.g., object interaction forces), they serve as a generalizable, embodiment-agnostic interface for action representation.
- **Multi-view Prediction:** The model processes multi-view camera feeds (e.g., wrist-view, third-person view) concatenated into a grid. This multi-view input enables the model to infer the relative 3D positions of robot joints and objects, enhancing 3D understanding.
- **World Model Architecture and Training:** The system utilizes a diffusion-based video generation backbone (Wan-T2V-1.3B). It employs diffusion-forcing with a flow-matching objective for training. Independent random noise is added to history video tokens, and uniform noise to future video tokens, while pose-image tokens remain noise-free for conditioning.
- **Data Augmentation for Generalization:** To improve generalization to exploratory actions, demonstration trajectories are perturbed with Gaussian noise prior to training the world model, simulating conditions found in Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control.

#### 3.2. World Action Planner Pipeline

The WAP orchestrates the VLM agent and the action-conditioned world model through a systematic action planning pipeline (Algorithm 1):

- **Agent Action Proposal:**
 - A VLM agent (e.g., Gemini 3.0 Flash) is used to interpret high-level task descriptions and propose a sequence of primitive actions (e.g., MOVE, ROTATE, GRASP, RELEASE).
 - For `MOVE` actions, the VLM identifies target gripper positions across multi-view images. These 2D pixel coordinates are then triangulated into 3D space.
 - A low-level robot controller (implemented as a Diffusion Policy) processes the current and VLM-proposed target end-effector poses to generate "action chunks" for execution.
- **Global Optimization Guided by Agent Feedback:**
 - After the VLM proposes initial actions, the action-conditioned world model "imagines" the resulting trajectory by rolling out future frames.
 - The VLM is then prompted to evaluate this imagined rollout for physical safety (e.g., collisions) and alignment with the task goal.
 - If the trajectory is suboptimal, the VLM provides high-level semantic feedback (e.g., "increase height for clearance" or "adjust forward").
 - This feedback is used to refine the action sequence via the low-level controller, allowing for global adjustments to the plan.
- **Local Search with Agent Ranking:**
 - For fine-grained manipulation or when subtle positioning inaccuracies persist, a grid search is performed to sample a set of candidate actions around the current proposal.
 - The world model generates imagined rollouts for each candidate action.
 - The VLM agent then ranks these imagined outcomes based on their plausibility and effectiveness for achieving the task goal. This leverages the VLM's evaluative capabilities to compensate for its lack of absolute metric grounding.
 - For tasks like grasping, a diffusion policy is used for fine-grained completion once the gripper is proximal to the target. This policy's rollout is also imagined within the world model to identify the optimal state for execution.
- **Policies as Tools:** The WAP framework conceptualizes existing imitation learning policies (e.g., Diffusion Policy, VLAs, WAMs) as modular tools. This allows for their integration for in-distribution tasks where demonstrations are readily available, while the full model-based planning system handles out-of-distribution scenarios.
- **Execution:** After optimization and search, the selected actions are executed in the environment.

#### 3.3. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides theoretical justifications for the benefits of model-based planning over imitation learning in multi-task generalization.

- **Tabular Setting:** In a tabular MDP setting with known rewards but unknown transitions, model-based exploration can achieve an `˜O(1/√K)` suboptimality gap, independent of the number of tasks. In contrast, imitation learning suffers a suboptimality gap scaling with `Ω(|C|/K)`, where `|C|` is the number of tasks, due to experts being distinct across tasks.
- **Linear Function Approximation:** For linear MDPs where rewards and transitions are linear functions of feature maps, a model-based approach can learn dynamics and multi-task rewards with an `˜O(1/κ√K)` suboptimality gap. Imitation learning, however, struggles with the complex, non-linear dependency of optimal policies on task features in multi-task settings, even when they have simple linear structures in single-task scenarios.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research presents findings across two primary areas: the performance of the action-conditioned world model and the generalization capabilities of the World Action Planner.

#### 4.1. Action-Conditioned World Modeling

The proposed pose-image conditioned world model demonstrated performance improvements and enhanced generalization compared to existing methods.

- **In-Distribution Prediction Accuracy (Q1):** When evaluating on in-distribution data from diverse robots (7-DoF Franka-Panda in LIBERO-90 and bi-manual dexterous robot in DexMimicGen), the model achieved an average of 11.4% improvement in PSNR (higher is better) and LPIPS (lower is better) metrics compared to baselines (WPE, IRA-Sim, Ctrl-World). This indicates more accurate generation of multi-view future images conditioned on robot pose-images.
- **Generalization to Novel Actions/Scenes (Q2):** In zero-shot evaluation settings (LIBERO-10, LIBERO-Spatial) and few-shot finetuning on distinct hardware (Sawyer, IIWA, UR5e), the pose-image conditioning yielded a 16.8% average improvement over prior methods. This confirms its ability to generalize effectively to novel trajectories and physical configurations. Qualitatively, the model successfully imagined physical outcomes of out-of-distribution actions, such as knocking over a mug, despite not having such collision events in its training data.
- **Cross-Embodiment Modeling (Q3):** When trained on a heterogeneous mixture of datasets encompassing various robot embodiments and action spaces (fixed arm, mobile manipulator, bi-manual grippers, dexterous hands), the method consistently outperformed prior cross-embodiment baselines (unified action spaces, embodiment-aware encoders, soft prompting). This suggests that representing actions via robot pose images provides a scalable and embodiment-agnostic interface.

#### 4.2. World Action Planner

The World Action Planner (WAP) demonstrated superior generalization across compositional tasks, new layouts, and zero-shot scenarios.

- **Compositional Task Generalization:** On LIBERO-Long tasks, which combine two sub-tasks, WAP achieved significantly higher success rates (e.g., 72% for "PnP alphabet soup & tomato sauce" vs. 4% for π0.5 and 0% for cosmos-policy). End-to-end VLA models frequently completed the first sub-task but then stagnated due to their inability to generate bridging actions or transition to the next sub-task. WAP overcame this by leveraging the VLM agent to propose and refine intervening actions and imagine policy rollouts to ensure successful continuation.
- **New Layout Generalization:** For tasks in LIBERO-Object with modified object layouts, WAP achieved high success rates (e.g., 88% on "PnP alphabet soup", 90% on "PnP salad dressing"). In contrast, generalist VLAs and WAMs exhibited near-zero success rates, often defaulting to motion priors from the training layout or attempting to grasp objects at their original coordinates. WAP successfully decoupled high-level object identification and coarse navigation (via VLM) from low-level fine-grained manipulation (via policy), using only 5 expert demonstrations per task, significantly fewer than baselines.
- **Zero-shot Generalization:** In Robosuite tasks (PickPlaceCan, StackCube) without any expert demonstrations or specialized policies, WAP achieved high success rates (80% and 76% respectively). This performance was notably superior to a baseline vision-language planner, particularly in precision-intensive tasks like stacking cubes. This highlights the necessity of refining VLM action proposals through world model imagination for successful execution in novel, unstructured scenarios.
- **Ablation Experiments:** Ablations confirmed the individual contributions of each component within the WAP pipeline. Global optimization, local search, and policy rollout imagination each contributed to maximizing success as task complexity increased. For instance, global optimization was critical for object clearance, and local search effectively identified optimal gripper rotations that VLMs struggled to describe analytically. Imagining consecutive policy rollouts was essential for identifying suitable states for policy execution in fine-grained tasks. The optimization and search pipeline proved more efficient and robust than naive Best-of-N VLM sampling, which often repeated physical oversights across multiple samples.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The World Action Planner (WAP) represents a significant advancement in robotic decision-making by addressing the fundamental challenge of generalization to novel tasks and environments. Its primary significance lies in moving beyond the limitations of end-to-end imitation learning paradigms, which often struggle with out-of-distribution scenarios.

**Key areas of significance and potential impact include:**

- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The WAP framework enables robots to generalize effectively across compositional tasks, novel layouts, and in zero-shot settings. This capability is critical for deploying robots in unstructured, dynamic real-world environments where precise pre-training data for every conceivable scenario is impractical.
- **Principled Model-Based Planning:** By systematically integrating VLM reasoning with action-conditioned world model imagination, the research establishes a principled approach to robotic planning. This contrasts with purely reactive or data-driven methods, offering a more robust and adaptable framework for complex problem-solving.
- **Effective VLM-World Model Synergy:** The work demonstrates a functional synergy between large language models and physical world models. VLMs provide high-level semantic understanding, task decomposition, and iterative feedback, while the world model grounds these abstract plans in physical reality, simulating consequences and enabling precise refinement. This integration allows each component to compensate for the other's weaknesses.
- **Modularity and Extensibility:** Treating existing imitation learning policies as "tools" within the WAP framework promotes modularity. This allows for flexible integration of specialized skills developed via various training paradigms, leveraging their strengths for in-distribution tasks while relying on the full WAP for novel situations. This could accelerate the development of generalist robots by combining diverse expert modules.
- **Theoretical Foundation for Generalization:** The theoretical analysis provides formal justification for the advantages of model-based planning in multi-task generalization. This contributes to a deeper understanding of why certain approaches generalize better, guiding future research in algorithmic design for generalist agents.
- **Potential for Sample Efficiency:** By decoupling high-level reasoning from low-level control and enabling systematic exploration of action spaces through imagination, the WAP has the potential to reduce the reliance on vast quantities of expert demonstration data, particularly for adapting to new tasks or layouts.

**Potential impact on future research and applications:**

- **Real-world Robotics:** While currently evaluated in simulation, the principles of WAP could pave the way for more capable and robust real-world robotic systems, particularly in domains requiring complex manipulation and adaptability, such as manufacturing, logistics, domestic assistance, and hazardous environment exploration.
- **Advanced AI Agents:** The framework contributes to the broader development of intelligent agents that can reason, plan, and act in complex environments, integrating symbolic reasoning with continuous control.
- **Improved Human-Robot Interaction:** The VLM-driven feedback mechanism could facilitate more intuitive human interaction with robots, allowing humans to provide high-level guidance that the robot can then translate into precise, physically grounded actions.

**Limitations and Future Work:**

The paper acknowledges that its evaluations are confined to simulation, leaving real-robot experiments as future work. The computational cost of world model imagination and VLM reasoning is also noted, suggesting future work on inference acceleration and few-step sampling techniques to improve planning speed and real-time efficiency. This research lays a foundation for addressing these practical challenges.
