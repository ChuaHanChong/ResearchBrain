# WarmPrior: Straightening Flow-Matching Policies with Temporal Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces WarmPrior, a method that replaces the standard uninformative Gaussian source distribution in flow-matching generative policies with one anchored on recent action history for visuomotor robotic control. The approach consistently improves success rates on diverse manipulation tasks in simulation and real-world environments, reduces the curvature of learned probability paths, and enhances prior-space reinforcement learning efficiency.

## Problem

- Generative policies for robotic control often rely on an uninformative isotropic Gaussian source distribution, which is stateless.
- The stateless prior forces policies to reconstruct each action chunk from scratch, leading to inefficient learning and less direct probability paths.
- Continuous robotic motion is temporally correlated, a property not leveraged by existing context-free source distributions.

## Method

- **WarmPrior** modifies the ==source distribution (p0)== of flow-matching policies to anchor its mean on ==recent action history==, providing a temporally grounded starting point.
- Two variants are proposed: ==WarmPrior-Past==, using the previously executed action chunk, and ==WarmPrior-Preview==, utilizing the model's own previous forecast for the prior mean.
- This modification preserves the existing neural network architecture, interpolant functions, and training objective, focusing solely on optimizing the `p0` distribution.

## Results

- WarmPrior consistently improved success rates on Robomimic and MimicGen simulation benchmarks, with WP-Preview showing gains of up to **+11.2%** on Transport-MH at NFE=1.
- Real-world experiments on a Franka Research 3 robot validated the findings, consistently improving success rates across four tabletop manipulation tasks, with larger gains in precision-demanding scenarios.
- Quantitative analysis showed WarmPrior reduced pathwise curvature and 'branching cost' of flow trajectories, directly correlating with improved success rates and indicating straighter, less ambiguous learned paths.
- Integration into prior-space reinforcement learning frameworks led to faster learning, more stable convergence, and higher asymptotic success rates on robotic tasks, improving sample efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modifying the source distribution to include temporal context significantly straightens the learned probability flow paths, implicitly functioning as an amortized approximation to optimal transport couplings.
> - A temporally grounded prior offers tunable implicit temporal consistency between consecutive action chunks, mitigating issues like 'mode switching' without explicit history conditioning.
> - The source distribution is an underexplored design axis in generative robotic control, and its optimization yields substantial performance gains across various task settings.
