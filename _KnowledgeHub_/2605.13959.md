# WarmPrior: Straightening Flow-Matching Policies with Temporal Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces WarmPrior, a method that replaces the standard uninformative Gaussian source distribution in flow-matching generative policies with one anchored on recent action history for visuomotor robotic control. The approach consistently improves success rates on diverse manipulation tasks in simulation and real-world environments, reduces the curvature of learned probability paths, and enhances prior-space reinforcement learning efficiency.

## Problem

- Generative policies for robotic control often rely on an uninformative isotropic Gaussian source distribution, which is stateless.
- The stateless prior forces policies to reconstruct each action chunk from scratch, leading to inefficient learning and less direct probability paths.
- Continuous robotic motion is temporally correlated, a property not leveraged by existing context-free source distributions.

## Method

- **WarmPrior** modifies the ==source distribution (p0)== of flow-matching policies to anchor its mean on ==recent action history==, providing a temporally grounded starting point.
- Two variants are proposed: ==WarmPrior-Past==, using the previously executed action chunk, and ==WarmPrior-Preview==, utilizing the model's own previous forecast for the prior mean.
- This modification preserves the existing neural network architecture, interpolant functions, and training objective, focusing solely on optimizing the `p0` distribution.

## Results

- WarmPrior consistently improved success rates on Robomimic and MimicGen simulation benchmarks (WP-Preview up to **+11.2%** on Transport-MH at NFE=1) and across four real-world Franka Research 3 tabletop manipulation tasks, with larger gains in precision-demanding scenarios.
- Quantitative analysis showed WarmPrior reduced pathwise curvature and 'branching cost' of flow trajectories, directly correlating with improved success rates and indicating straighter, less ambiguous learned paths.
- Integration into prior-space reinforcement learning frameworks led to faster learning, more stable convergence, and higher asymptotic success rates on robotic tasks, improving sample efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modifying the source distribution to include temporal context significantly straightens the learned probability flow paths, implicitly functioning as an amortized approximation to optimal transport couplings.
> - A temporally grounded prior offers tunable implicit temporal consistency between consecutive action chunks, mitigating issues like 'mode switching' without explicit history conditioning.
> - The source distribution is an underexplored design axis in generative robotic control, and its optimization yields substantial performance gains across various task settings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Generative policies, specifically those based on diffusion and flow matching, have emerged as a dominant paradigm for learning visuomotor robotic control. These frameworks typically transport samples from a fixed source distribution to the target data manifold of action chunks. Historically, the isotropic Gaussian distribution N(0, I) has been almost universally adopted as this source distribution, a convention largely inherited from its role in diffusion models for denoising. Prior research efforts in this domain have primarily focused on advancements in neural network architectures, interpolant functions, and integration schemes, while the design of the source distribution itself has remained largely unexamined.

However, as denoising schedules in these generative models become shorter, the initial starting point of the generation process assumes a more significant role. The use of a stateless, uninformative Gaussian source distribution presents a challenge in robotic control, as continuous robotic motion is inherently temporally correlated. Such a context-free source necessitates that the policy reconstruct each action chunk from scratch, potentially leading to inefficient learning and less direct probability paths.

This work revisits the established choice of the N(0, I) source distribution. It aligns with an emerging direction that explores modifying the source of generative policies. Other related efforts include BRIDGER, which replaces the Gaussian source with a data-aware, non-Gaussian source policy; STEP, which trains an auxiliary action predictor to inject a warm start at an intermediate denoising step; and A2A, which anchors the prior on past actions but through a deterministic latent encoding. WarmPrior distinguishes itself by preserving the stochastic flow-matching formulation end-to-end and specifically focusing on constructing the prior space (p0) itself by incorporating temporal information directly into its mean. Furthermore, while methods like Rectified Flow and Multisample Flow Matching reshape optimal-transport couplings to straighten flows, WarmPrior is complementary by reshaping the source distribution to initiate the flow closer to the data manifold, thereby implicitly straightening paths without requiring explicit optimal transport solvers or retraining stages.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that while generative policies have advanced significantly in robotic control, the source distribution used for generating actions remains a largely inherited and unoptimized component. The standard isotropic Gaussian prior, N(0, I), is stateless and uninformative regarding the continuous and temporally correlated nature of robotic motion. This forces the policy to reconstruct every action chunk from a random starting point, which can lead to inefficient learning and sub-optimal control. As the computational budget for generative models (e.g., number of function evaluations, NFE) decreases, the initial starting point becomes more critical, highlighting the limitations of an uninformative prior.

The key objectives of this work are:

1. **Introduce a Temporally Grounded Prior:** To design and implement WarmPrior, a simple modification that replaces the standard Gaussian source distribution with one whose mean is anchored on readily available recent action history. This aims to provide the generative process with a more informed starting point.
2. **Evaluate Performance Improvement:** To empirically demonstrate whether WarmPrior consistently improves success rates on a range of robotic manipulation tasks, comparing it against the standard N(0, I) baseline across various simulation environments and real-world robot setups, and under different inference budgets.
3. **Analyze Geometric Properties of Flow Paths:** To investigate and quantify the effect of WarmPrior on the geometry of the learned probability paths. Specifically, to determine if starting closer to the target manifold leads to shorter and straighter transport paths, analogous to the effects observed in optimal transport couplings.
4. **Investigate Temporal Consistency:** To understand how WarmPrior influences temporal consistency between consecutive action chunks, particularly in scenarios where explicit action chunking is minimal or absent, thereby providing an implicit mechanism for maintaining coherent action sequences.
5. **Explore Impact on Reinforcement Learning:** To extend the WarmPrior concept to the reinforcement learning (RL) domain, specifically within prior-space RL frameworks, to evaluate its potential in reshaping the exploration distribution, improving sample efficiency, and enhancing final asymptotic performance.
6. **Highlight Source Distribution as a Design Axis:** To establish the source distribution as an important and previously underexplored design axis in generative robotic control, encouraging further research into its optimization beyond the conventional N(0, I) default.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WarmPrior operates by modifying only the source distribution (p0) of a flow-matching policy, preserving the existing neural network architecture, interpolant, and training objective. The core formulation of WarmPrior samples a starting point `a0` such that warm positions (`τ ∈ W`) are centered around a mean `μ` with residual Gaussian noise `σ ετ`, while cold positions (`τ ∈ C`) remain pure Gaussian noise (`ετ`). The scalar `σ > 0` controls the magnitude of residual noise on warm positions.

The paper introduces two minimal variants:

1. **WarmPrior-Past (WP-Past):**
 - **Prediction:** Predicts a single chunk of H actions.
 - **Prior Mean (μ):** Anchored on the previously executed action chunk.
 - **Training:** For a sample at in-buffer index `i`, the `H` preceding actions (`adata[i-H:i]`) are retrieved from the replay buffer and used as `μPastτ`. If the window crosses an episode boundary, it defaults to a standard Gaussian (W=∅).
 - **Inference:** The previously executed action chunk (`ˆaprev`) is used as `μPastτ`. For the first chunk of an episode, it defaults to a standard Gaussian.
 - **Noise Scale (σ):** Fixed at 0.5.

2. **WarmPrior-Preview (WP-Preview):**
 - **Prediction:** Trains the policy to predict 2H actions at each inference step, but only the first H actions are executed. The second H actions serve as a "preview" for the next chunk.
 - **Prior Mean (μ):** For the first H steps of the current prediction, the prior mean is anchored on the model’s own previous forecast of the current chunk (i.e., the second H steps from the previous prediction). The second H steps of the current prediction start from pure Gaussian noise.
 - **Training:** To address the chicken-and-egg problem (model's own preview being unavailable before training), the ground-truth target `a1` itself is used as a proxy for a perfectly calibrated preview, setting `μPreviewτ = a1[τ]` for `τ ∈ {0. . . , H − 1}`.
 - **Inference:** The second half (`H` to `2H`) of the previous 2H-step prediction (`ˆaprev`) is used as `μPreviewτ` for the first H steps of the current prediction. For the first chunk, it defaults to a standard Gaussian.
 - **Noise Scale (σ):** Fixed at 1.0.

**Experimental Setup:**

- **Simulation Environments:** Experiments were conducted on Robomimic (state and image observations across SQUARE, TRANSPORT, and TOOL-HANG tasks in PH and harder MH splits) and MimicGen (image observations on STACK, COFFEE, and THREADING tasks).
- **Policy Backbone:** The Diffusion Policy (ChiTransformer) was used, trained with flow matching.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** Success rates were computed over 200 episodes and 3 random seeds at three inference budgets (NFE ∈ {9, 3, 1}). An action chunk length H=8 was used. To mitigate variance, the performance of the top-3 checkpoints per seed was averaged.
- **Real-Robot Experiments:** The method was deployed on a Franka Research 3 robot for four tabletop manipulation tasks (Food Waste Disposal, Cup Stacking, Block Stacking, Cable Insertion).
- **Real-Robot Policy Backbone:** The GR00T N1.5 VLA model, which employs a flow-matching action head, was used.
- **Real-Robot Data:** 30 human teleoperation demonstrations per task were collected using the DROID platform.
- **Real-Robot Evaluation:** 3 independent training seeds, 50 evaluation trials per seed, with NFE fixed to 4.

**Geometric Analysis:**
The paper quantitatively measures pathwise curvature `κ(o)` using the velocity-variance surrogate `∫₀¹ ||ȧ_t - ȧ_bar||² dt`, evaluated by finite differences along the Euler sampler. An analysis of the "branching cost" `B(o)` (the irreducible error of the flow-matching regression problem) is provided to explain how WarmPrior reduces endpoint ambiguity.

**Temporal Consistency Analysis:**
A 1D navigation toy example illustrates how standard flow matching can lead to "mode switching." To isolate WarmPrior's implicit consistency bias, experiments were conducted with an action chunk length `H=1`, where the policy performs a fresh inference at every timestep, making WarmPrior the sole source of inter-step consistency.

**Prior-Space Reinforcement Learning (RL) Extension:**
WarmPrior was integrated into the DSRL framework (which fine-tunes pretrained diffusion policies by having an RL agent output prior samples `a0`). The observation space was augmented (`õ = [o, μ]`), and the RL agent was trained to learn a bounded residual `Δ` around the WarmPrior mean: `a0 = μ + Δ`, with `Δ = πRL(õ) ∈ [−δ, δ]`. This aimed to center the RL exploration around a temporally grounded prior.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings across simulation, real-robot experiments, and theoretical analyses.

#### 4.1. Simulation Performance Improvements

Table 1 demonstrates that WarmPrior consistently improves success rates on both Robomimic and MimicGen benchmarks.
- **Consistent Gains:** Across the majority of evaluated tasks and inference budgets, WarmPrior methods achieved performance gains over the N(0, I) baseline, with many instances showing non-overlapping one-standard-deviation intervals, indicating statistical significance.
- **WP-Preview's Effectiveness:** WP-Preview generally exhibited greater improvements than WP-Past. For example, on Robomimic Transport-MH (state observation) at NFE=1, WP-Preview improved success rate by +11.2% (34.5% vs 23.3%), compared to WP-Past's +6.9% (30.2%).
- **Low Inference Budget Benefit:** The magnitude of performance improvements was most pronounced at the lowest inference budget (NFE=1), suggesting that WarmPrior's informed starting point is particularly beneficial when the policy has fewer steps to refine its action.

#### 4.2. Real-Robot Experiment Validation

Figure 3 reports that WarmPrior consistently improved the overall success rate across all four real-world Franka Research 3 tabletop manipulation tasks.
- **Generalizability:** The observed gains in simulation transferred to the real world, using the GR00T N1.5 VLA model backbone.
- **Precision Task Benefits:** The largest gains were observed on precision-demanding tasks such as Cable Insertion and Block Stacking, where accurate initial actions are critical.

#### 4.3. Straightening of Flow Trajectories

- **Visual Evidence:** Figure 4 illustrates that the integration paths generated by WarmPrior are visibly straighter and more parallel compared to the baseline flow policy, which exhibits noticeable curvature.
- **Quantitative Curvature Reduction:** Table 2 provides quantitative measurements of pathwise curvature `κ(o)`. All tasks showed a reduction in mean curvature with WarmPrior. Tasks with the largest curvature reductions (e.g., SQUARE-MH, TRANSPORT-MH) corresponded to those with the largest success rate gains, supporting the hypothesis that straightened paths contribute to improved performance.
- **Branching Cost Reduction:** The analysis in Section 5.1 and Appendix B explains that WarmPrior reduces the "branching cost," which is the irreducible error caused by endpoint ambiguity where the velocity network must average over multiple possible targets for a given intermediate point. By providing a prior mean `μ` closer to the target `A1` and controlling residual noise `σ`, WarmPrior directly reduces this ambiguity, leading to straighter flows (Equation 7). The ordering of variants (Preview outperforming Past) is explained by Preview's `μ` being closer to the target, resulting in a smaller mean mismatch term in the branching cost bound. An ablation study in Appendix D showed an optimal `σ` exists, balancing straightness and robustness to prior-mean diversity.

#### 4.4. Tunable Temporal Consistency

- **Addressing Mode Switching:** The paper identifies "mode switching" as a pathology in generative control where the policy oscillates rapidly between distinct modes across consecutive inferences due to the objective treating chunks independently.
- **Implicit Consistency:** WarmPrior provides implicit temporal consistency. A small `σ` keeps the new chunk within the basin of the previous mode, preventing mode switching, while a larger `σ` allows for broader multimodal exploration. This offers a continuous trade-off between temporal commitment and multimodal expressiveness, without the computational cost or fragility of explicit history conditioning.
- **H=1 Experiment (Figure 6):** When action chunking was disabled (H=1), the N(0, I) baseline performance degraded sharply (e.g., TRANSPORT-MH dropped from 23.3% to 1.3%). WarmPrior, however, recovered most of the lost performance, with gains up to +14.8% on MimicGen COFFEE. This indicates that WarmPrior's prior is a primary source of inter-step consistency when explicit chunking is absent.

#### 4.5. Improved Prior-Space RL Efficiency

- **Enhanced Exploration:** Figure 7 demonstrates that WarmPrior significantly improved prior-space reinforcement learning (RL) performance. By centering the RL agent's exploration around a temporally grounded WarmPrior mean (using a conditioned-residual approach), the agent refines a local correction rather than searching an uninformative random noise space from scratch.
- **Faster Learning and Higher Asymptote:** Both WP-Past and WP-Preview variants, when integrated into DSRL-NA, exhibited faster learning, more stable convergence, and achieved higher asymptotic success rates compared to vanilla DSRL-SAC and DSRL-NA baselines. On Robomimic SQUARE, they consistently exceeded 0.99, and on TRANSPORT, they reached approximately 0.97, surpassing previous benchmarks.

#### 4.6. Comparison with Real-Time Chunking (RTC)

- Appendix E presents a comparison with Real-Time Chunking (RTC), an inference-time procedure that clamps early chunk positions. On dynamic real-robot tasks (Block Throwing, Towel Folding), both RTC and WarmPrior individually improved over the baseline. Crucially, the combination of RTC+WarmPrior yielded additional improvements, suggesting that their benefits arise from distinct mechanisms: RTC enforces explicit commitment at inference time, while WarmPrior reshapes the training-time coupling for straighter learned flows.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work identifies the source distribution as a significant and underexplored design axis in generative robotic control. By demonstrating that a simple, temporally grounded prior, WarmPrior, consistently improves success rates across diverse robotic manipulation tasks in both simulation and real-world environments, the paper challenges the conventional and often implicit reliance on the isotropic Gaussian as a default.

The significance of WarmPrior lies in its multi-faceted benefits:

1. **Enhanced Performance and Efficiency:** WarmPrior consistently boosts success rates, particularly at low inference budgets where efficiency is critical. This is crucial for real-time robotic applications where computational resources are often constrained.
2. **Geometric Optimization of Flows:** The geometric analysis reveals that WarmPrior implicitly acts as an amortized approximation to optimal transport couplings, leading to markedly straighter probability paths. This reduced curvature means the generative policy spends less capacity on realigning samples and more on refining actions, which directly translates to more effective control.
3. **Tunable Temporal Consistency:** WarmPrior offers an implicit mechanism for maintaining temporal consistency between consecutive actions. This is particularly valuable as it mitigates issues like "mode switching" without requiring explicit action chunking or computationally expensive history conditioning. The ability to tune the `σ` parameter provides a continuous knob for balancing within-rollout commitment and multimodal expressiveness, offering flexibility that explicit chunking cannot provide, especially at very short chunk lengths (H=1).
4. **Improved Reinforcement Learning:** The successful integration of WarmPrior into prior-space reinforcement learning frameworks demonstrates its utility beyond behavior cloning. By providing a semantically meaningful and temporally grounded exploration space, WarmPrior improves sample efficiency and leads to higher asymptotic performance, facilitating the fine-tuning of pretrained policies.
5. **Minimal Intervention, Broad Impact:** WarmPrior's appeal lies in its minimal intervention: it modifies only the source distribution while leaving the network architecture, interpolant, and loss function untouched. This ease of integration makes it broadly applicable to existing generative policy frameworks.

The findings suggest that reconsidering and optimizing the source distribution can yield substantial gains. This opens a new avenue for research in generative policy design, potentially leading to more robust, efficient, and versatile robotic control systems. Future work could explore dynamic adaptation of the `σ` parameter, more sophisticated constructions of `μ`, or the interaction of WarmPrior with other policy stack components for even greater performance.
