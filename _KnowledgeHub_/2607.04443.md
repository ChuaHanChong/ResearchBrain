# Wan-Streamer v0.2: Higher Resolution, Same Latency

> [!summary] Summary
> Wan-Streamer v0.2, developed by Alibaba Group's Wan Team, upgrades a real-time audio-visual interaction model to deliver 640x368 pixel output at 25 FPS, an increase from 192x336, while preserving an approximate 200 ms model-side latency. This enhancement supports more detailed and scene-grounded interactions with digital agents.

## Problem

- Wan-Streamer v0.1 was limited to a low 192x336 pixel output resolution, restricting visual interactions to close-up framings.
- The low resolution made body posture, nearby objects, and broader scene context unintelligible, hindering scene-grounded interaction capabilities.
- The challenge was to significantly enhance visual fidelity and interaction scope without compromising the crucial real-time latency performance of the system.

## Method

- The core ==native-streaming formulation== of **Wan-Streamer v0.1** was retained, but a novel ==Thinker-Performer serving topology== was introduced.
- The **Thinker**, operating on a single GPU, handles low-latency tasks like audio/video encoding, ==Transformer== execution, ==KV cache== construction, and latent decoding.
- The **Performer**, a multi-GPU group using ==Ulysses-style context parallelism==, is dedicated to the computationally intensive high-resolution ==flow-matching latent generation==, with operations overlapped with the Thinker's work.

## Results

- Wan-Streamer v0.2 successfully upgraded the interactive output stream from **192x336** to **640x368** pixels at **25 FPS** while maintaining an approximate **200 ms** model-side signal-to-signal latency.
- Total remote interaction latency, including a **350 ms** network budget, remained consistent at approximately **550 ms**, matching v0.1 performance despite increased visual complexity.
- The higher output resolution enabled expanded visual interaction scope, supporting higher-fidelity close-ups and legible scene-grounded mid-shot agents displaying body posture, gaze, and hand movements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Splitting the model into a low-latency 'Thinker' and a high-throughput 'Performer' effectively isolates the computational cost of higher-resolution video generation, preserving real-time responsiveness.
> - Leveraging multi-GPU context parallelism with pre-sharded KV caches and sequence parallelism for video denoising enables significant resolution upgrades within strict latency budgets.
> - The underlying native-streaming formulation for multimodal data is robust enough to support substantial visual enhancements through system-level optimization without needing a fundamental model redesign.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the rapidly evolving domain of real-time audio-visual interaction, integrating advancements across several interconnected fields. It draws upon and contributes to research in full-duplex spoken dialogue systems, which emphasize continuous, natural conversation rather than alternating turn-based interactions. The paper also builds on progress in omni-modal models, extending perception capabilities to include image, video, and audio inputs. Furthermore, it leverages state-of-the-art techniques in streaming video generation and causal rollout methods, which are foundational for synthesizing interactive visual outputs. The development of real-time avatars and digital human systems, encompassing audio-driven faces, streaming visual agents, and end-to-end embodied interaction, forms another critical context for this research.

Wan-Streamer v0.1, the predecessor to this work, established a "native-streaming formulation." In this framework, user and agent text, audio, and video data are represented on a single causal timeline and modeled by a unified Transformer. This approach differs from cascaded visual-agent systems by integrating perception, response timing, speech, visible listening behavior, and synchronized video response within one causal interaction state, thereby closing the audio-visual interaction loop. However, the v0.1 iteration was limited by its preliminary 192×336 pixel output resolution. This resolution restricted visual interactions primarily to close-up video-call framings, which effectively conveyed facial responses and speaking behavior but rendered body posture, nearby objects, and broader scene context too compressed for scene-grounded interaction. Wan-Streamer v0.2 directly addresses this limitation, aiming to enhance the visual fidelity and scope of interaction while preserving the established real-time performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Wan-Streamer v0.2 is to deliver a latency-preserving upgrade to the native-streaming, end-to-end audio-visual interaction model, specifically by increasing the output resolution without compromising real-time responsiveness.

The key objectives can be detailed as follows:
- **Resolution Enhancement**: To raise the interactive output stream from 192×336 to 640×368 pixels. This constitutes a significant increase in visual detail, moving beyond the capabilities of the v0.1 model.
- **Latency Preservation**: To maintain approximately 200 ms model-side signal-to-signal latency at 25 frames per second (FPS). This objective is critical to ensure that the user experience of real-time conversation is not degraded by the increased computational demands of higher resolution.
- **Expanded Visual Composition**: To support scene-grounded mid-shot agents whose posture, gaze, hands, nearby objects, and local scene layout remain legible during real-time conversation. This aims to expand the visual format from portrait-like video calls to situated conversations where the agent is visibly grounded in its surroundings.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the limitations identified in Wan-Streamer v0.1. While v0.1 successfully established a robust native-streaming formulation for synchronized multimodal interaction, its low output resolution of 192p restricted the practical application and visual expressiveness of the generated agents. This low resolution meant that wider compositions, which might include an agent's body, hands, or surrounding environment, resulted in details being too compressed to be intelligible or useful for interaction. Consequently, the range of possible interactions was constrained to close-up, face-focused scenarios.

The developers aimed to overcome this constraint by enabling a higher-resolution visual stream, which would allow for richer, more nuanced interactions. For instance, a mid-shot agent provides context regarding body language, hand gestures, and the immediate physical environment, facilitating more natural and immersive dialogues where visual cues are as important as verbal ones. The central challenge and motivation was to achieve this substantial visual upgrade while adhering to stringent real-time latency budgets (approximately 200 ms model-side and 550 ms total remote interaction latency, including network delay), ensuring that the system remained suitable for interactive applications. This required rethinking the deployment and computational allocation strategy without altering the fundamental native-streaming modeling formulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Wan-Streamer v0.2 retains the core "native-streaming formulation" established in v0.1, where user and agent text, audio, and video inputs and outputs are processed on a unified causal timeline through block-causal attention. The model is trained end-to-end, with generated audio-video latents committed back into history after each unit to allow subsequent responses to depend on both user and agent behavior. The primary methodological change in v0.2 lies in its serving topology, specifically designed to accommodate the increased computational load of higher-resolution video generation while preserving real-time latency.

The deployed model is split into two specialized roles:

1. **Thinker**:
 - This component operates on a single Graphics Processing Unit (GPU).
 - Its responsibilities include low-latency tasks such as processing causal audio and video encoders, executing the token-causal Transformer path for language understanding and state update, constructing the Key-Value (KV) cache, and finally, utilizing causal decoders to convert returned latents into output audio and video.
 - The language/state computation within the Thinker generates the KV cache, which subsequently conditions the generation process.

2. **Performer**:
 - This component comprises a multi-GPU group, configured in a "Ulysses-style context-parallel" arrangement. Ulysses context parallelism is an optimization technique designed to handle very long sequences by sharding the KV cache and distributing attention computations across multiple devices.
 - The Performer is dedicated to the computationally intensive "flow-matching latent generation path," which is responsible for generating the high-resolution 640×368 video latents.
 - **Communication and Data Handling**:
 - The Thinker broadcasts compact, performer-compatible KV slices to the Performer group.
 - Each rank within the Performer group receives these slices and writes them into its pre-sharded local cache, which holds parts of the full interaction history.
 - To handle the long latent video sequence, denoising is split across the Performer ranks, implementing sequence parallelism.
 - Communication among Performer ranks for attention operations leverages Ulysses all-to-all/gather collectives.
 - Audio latents, being significantly shorter, are generated without sequence sharding to avoid introducing unnecessary overhead.
 - Crucially, the language and state computation from the Thinker reaches the Performer solely as KV conditioning. This design choice eliminates the need to communicate a separate language sequence within the Performer group, thus maintaining a compact boundary between the Thinker and Performer.

The operational schedule is designed to overlap the Thinker's work with the Performer's. At each streaming unit `k`, the Thinker processes current user observations, generates a new performer-compatible KV slice, receives the previous unit's generated latents, decodes them, and emits the response. Concurrently, the Performer ranks receive the current KV slice, update their local cache shards, and execute Ulysses context-parallel denoising for the next unit's latents. This architecture isolates the increased visual generation cost within the context-parallel Performer, ensuring that the Thinker remains a compact, low-latency interaction path. This separation allows the real-time throughput of the Performer group to fit within a 160 ms unit, maintaining the overall 200 ms model-side response latency.

While the serving topology changed, the underlying training approach for the end-to-end causal stream remained stable. The data used for training was expanded to emphasize wider, scene-grounded conversational settings to facilitate the new mid-shot compositions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The development and deployment of Wan-Streamer v0.2 yielded several key findings and results related to its performance and capabilities:

- **Resolution Upgrade with Latency Preservation**: The primary objective of increasing output resolution while maintaining latency was achieved. Wan-Streamer v0.2 successfully upgraded the interactive output stream from a 192×336 resolution to 640×368 at 25 FPS. Simultaneously, it maintained an approximate model-side signal-to-signal latency of 200 ms. This demonstrates that the architectural and serving changes effectively absorbed the increased computational demand without elongating the critical interactive loop.

- **Consistent Total Interaction Latency**: Including a 350 ms bidirectional network budget, consistent with the v0.1 evaluation, the total remote interaction latency remained approximately 550 ms. This indicates that the proposed serving topology effectively manages the computational overhead, ensuring the user-visible delay for remote interactions is not increased despite the enhanced visual fidelity.

- **Efficacy of the Thinker-Performer Serving Topology**: The introduction of the v0.2 serving topology, comprising a single-GPU Thinker and a multi-GPU Ulysses-style context-parallel Performer, proved effective. This architecture successfully concentrated the additional computational cost of high-resolution visual latent generation within the Performer group. The use of pre-sharded KV caches and sequence parallelism for video denoising within the Performer allowed the higher resolution stream to be generated without impacting the latency-critical operations handled by the Thinker.

- **Expanded Visual Interaction Scope**: The higher output resolution enabled a significant expansion of the system's visual interaction capabilities. Specifically, the v0.2 output now supports:
 - **Higher-fidelity close-up interactions**: Improved clarity and detail in traditional video-call framings.
 - **Scene-grounded mid-shot agents**: The ability to render agents that are visibly situated within a scene. This allows for the legibility of an agent's body posture, gaze direction, hand movements, nearby objects, and the local scene layout during real-time conversation.

- **Qualitative Visual Observations**: Qualitative assessments of generated 640×368 conversations revealed improvements in visual stability and legibility. Details such as facial expressions, gaze, mouth movements, hand gestures, body posture, and scene context remained clear and discernible throughout both listening and speaking intervals. These observations confirm that the upgraded output format effectively supports the intended visual compositions, moving beyond simple facial interactions to include a broader range of visual cues in real-time.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Wan-Streamer v0.2 represents a notable advancement in the field of real-time, multimodal human-AI interaction, with several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Realism and Naturalness of Interaction**: By significantly increasing the output resolution while preserving ultra-low latency, v0.2 enables a leap in the visual realism and naturalness of digital agents. The ability to render scene-grounded mid-shot agents with legible body posture, gaze, hands, and surrounding objects moves interactions beyond isolated talking heads. This allows for a more comprehensive representation of an agent's non-verbal communication, fostering a richer and more intuitive user experience akin to in-person conversations.

- **Expanded Application Domains**: The improved visual fidelity and contextual awareness unlock new application possibilities. The system could be deployed in scenarios requiring agents to interact with their environment, demonstrate actions, or engage in dialogues where shared visual context is crucial. Potential areas include advanced virtual assistants capable of visual explanations, interactive educational tools, sophisticated customer service avatars, virtual reality companions, and dynamic character generation in entertainment.

- **Architectural Blueprint for Real-time Generative AI**: The novel "Thinker-Performer" serving topology offers a significant engineering contribution. It demonstrates a practical and effective method for deploying computationally intensive generative AI models under strict real-time constraints. The strategy of offloading expensive generation tasks to a context-parallel multi-GPU group (Performer) while maintaining a low-latency control path (Thinker) provides a scalable paradigm. This approach, leveraging techniques like pre-sharded KV caches and sequence parallelism, could serve as a blueprint for other complex AI systems that need to balance high throughput with minimal interaction latency.

- **Validation of Native-Streaming Formulation**: The success of v0.2 in scaling the visual complexity within the established native-streaming formulation of v0.1 validates the robustness of this underlying modeling approach. It demonstrates that an end-to-end causal timeline for multimodal data can support substantial upgrades through system-level optimizations without requiring a fundamental redesign of the core AI model's training methodology.

- **Contribution to Embodied AI Research**: The work contributes directly to the ongoing development of embodied AI, where agents interact with and respond to their environment and users in a physically coherent manner. The enhanced ability to convey an agent's presence within a scene through legible visual cues is a foundational step towards creating more sophisticated and believable embodied intelligent systems.
