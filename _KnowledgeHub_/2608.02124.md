# HAFI-VLM: A Frequency Perspective for Diagnosing and Enhancing Visual Perception in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research identifies "spectral response rigidity" in vision encoders, where they maintain invariant frequency profiles irrespective of query demands, hindering fine-grained perception in Vision-Language Models. It proposes HAFI-VLM, a method that dynamically injects task-conditioned spectral evidence into the vision encoder, leading to consistent performance gains on VQA and text-rich tasks and improved hallucination robustness.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often exhibit unreliability in tasks requiring fine-grained visual evidence, such as small text, subtle object attributes, or precise boundaries.
- Pretrained vision encoders show "spectral response rigidity," maintaining largely invariant layerwise frequency profiles across different tasks and images, failing to dynamically adapt to query-dependent visual evidence needs.
- This rigidity leads to insufficient task-relevant visual input for the language model, forcing it to over-rely on linguistic priors, resulting in perception failures and increased hallucinations.

## Method

- **HAFI-VLM** introduces a parallel frequency branch that tokenizes images into low-, mid-, and high-frequency bands using ==Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT)==.
- ==Hierarchical Adaptive Frequency Injection (HAFI)== modules are placed at multiple depths within the vision encoder to dynamically retrieve and inject text-conditioned, spatially-aligned frequency tokens.
- A ==Visual Enrichment Layer Adapter (VEL-Adapter)== applies ==LoRA== to shallow LLM layers, enabling the language backbone to effectively process the frequency-enriched visual tokens without fully unfreezing the LLM.

## Results

- HAFI-VLM consistently improved performance across general VQA (e.g., VQAv2), text-rich understanding (e.g., TextVQA), and hallucination robustness (POPE) benchmarks for both LLaVA-1.5-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbones.
- For the LLaVA-1.5-7B base, HAFI-VLM achieved an **82.8** score on VQAv2 (up from **78.5**), **68.5** on TextVQA (up from **58.2**), and **89.0** on POPE (up from **86.6**), outperforming other representation-level and high-resolution methods.
- Mechanistic analyses demonstrated HAFI-VLM induces task-adaptive and layer-dependent shifts in frequency band allocation, increasing emphasis on mid- and high-frequency components for fine-grained tasks while preserving semantic grounding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - "Spectral response rigidity" is a fundamental limitation of pretrained vision encoders, which provide static visual representations regardless of the query, impeding fine-grained perception in VLMs.
> - Dynamic, task-conditioned frequency injection can rebalance spectral evidence, selectively amplifying query-relevant details without disrupting the vision encoder's core semantic-spectral prior.
> - Targeted adaptation of the language backbone, such as via the VEL-Adapter, is crucial for effectively utilizing frequency-enriched visual tokens and recalibrating the visual-language interface.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language models (VLMs) have demonstrated significant advancements in integrating visual perception with language reasoning. However, a notable limitation persists: these models often exhibit unreliability when task performance hinges on fine-grained visual evidence, such as discerning small text, subtle object attributes, precise boundaries, or local textures. Current approaches to address this issue generally fall into categories such as increasing input image resolution, aggregating features from multiple layers of the vision encoder, or adapting the language backbone. These methods implicitly assume that the pretrained vision encoder possesses the inherent flexibility to expose the specific visual evidence required by diverse downstream tasks.

This work departs from these conventional assumptions by introducing a frequency-domain perspective to analyze how pretrained vision encoders process and allocate spectral information across different layers, images, and tasks. By identifying a phenomenon termed "spectral response rigidity," the authors propose that existing vision encoders maintain a largely invariant spectral profile, irrespective of the dynamic evidence requirements of a given query. This rigidity represents a structural mismatch between the static visual encoding and the dynamic, query-dependent nature of VLM tasks.

The proposed HAFI-VLM (Hierarchical Adaptive Frequency Injection in Vision-Language Models) positions itself as a complementary solution. Unlike input-level methods that modify image resolution or cropping, or readout-level methods that selectively fuse existing features, HAFI-VLM introduces a novel *task-conditioned frequency pathway* directly into the vision encoder. This allows for dynamic, query-aware spectral evidence retrieval and injection without requiring additional high-resolution encoding or repeated passes through the vision encoder. This approach addresses a previously underexplored avenue for enhancing VLM perception by enabling flexible spectral adaptation, thereby augmenting the pretrained semantic representation with fine-grained, task-relevant details.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed unreliability of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in tasks that necessitate fine-grained visual understanding. Such tasks include optical character recognition (OCR), precise attribute recognition, localizing subtle features, and robustly identifying hallucinated content. The authors hypothesized that a fundamental limitation, rather than merely a lack of model capacity or resolution, might underlie these challenges.

The key objective of this work is to diagnose and overcome a newly identified issue termed "spectral response rigidity" in pretrained vision encoders. This rigidity is characterized by the following observations:
1. **Persistent Spectral Profiles**: Pretrained vision encoders consistently produce nearly invariant layerwise frequency trajectories. These trajectories remain largely consistent across various datasets and individual images, despite substantial spectral variation in the input data.
2. **Encoder-Specific Allocation**: Different vision encoder architectures exhibit distinct spectral profiles. This indicates that the allocation of spectral information (low-, mid-, and high-frequency components) is primarily determined by the encoder's architecture and its pretraining objectives, rather than dynamically adapting to the specifics of the current visual input.
3. **Resistance to Fine-tuning**: Even under extensive downstream fine-tuning, the pretrained spectral profiles change only marginally. This demonstrates that conventional adaptation strategies do not readily reorganize the inherent spectral prior established during pretraining.

The consequence of this spectral response rigidity is particularly restrictive for VLMs. A standard vision encoder is conditioned solely on the image, meaning it generates identical visual tokens for the same image regardless of the accompanying language query. For instance, whether a query asks about global scene structure, minute text details, or object boundaries, the visual representation provided to the language backbone remains the same. When task-relevant visual evidence resides in frequency bands that have been attenuated or compressed by the rigid pretrained spectral profile, the language model receives an insufficient visual input. This deficiency can compel the language model to rely more heavily on linguistic priors, leading to perception failures, reduced accuracy in fine-grained tasks, and increased susceptibility to hallucinations.

Therefore, the central objective of this research is to develop a mechanism that enables task-conditioned spectral extraction. This mechanism aims to allow VLMs to dynamically adapt their spectral evidence retrieval based on the specifics of a given query, thereby augmenting the pretrained semantic pathway with relevant fine-grained information without destructively modifying the core semantic capabilities of the vision encoder.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed HAFI-VLM addresses spectral response rigidity by introducing a query-conditioned spectral retrieval pathway and an adaptive mechanism for the language backbone. The architecture integrates a parallel frequency branch with the existing VLM backbone (consisting of a CLIP visual encoder, a multimodal projector, and an LLM).

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture (HAFI-VLM)

The system operates by taking an RGB image (I) and a text query (T) as input. The visual stream begins with standard patch embedding for the pretrained vision encoder. In parallel, a dedicated frequency branch processes the same image into low-, mid-, and high-frequency tokens. These frequency tokens are not directly concatenated but are retrieved and injected into the main visual encoder pathway at selected depths. Finally, the frequency-enriched visual tokens are projected and decoded by the LLM.

#### 3.2. DCT Frequency Tokenization

This component provides an explicit spectral basis. The image is first denormalized and converted to YCrCb color space. It is then partitioned into non-overlapping 8x8 patches, and a two-dimensional Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) is applied to each patch. The resulting 64 coefficients per patch are flattened using a zigzag order. These coefficients are then partitioned into three distinct frequency bands: low (coefficients 0-15), mid (16-39), and high (40-63). A 2x2 pooling operation is applied to the spatial grid of DCT patches (e.g., reducing a 42x42 grid to 21x21), yielding `Nf = 441` tokens per band. Band-specific linear projections (`Wf`) and learnable positional/band embeddings are used to map these coefficients into the visual hidden dimension.

#### 3.3. Hierarchical Adaptive Frequency Injection (HAFI)

HAFI modules are strategically inserted at multiple depths within the vision encoder (specifically, at layers L/4, L/2, and 3L/4). Given the depth-dependent nature of the encoder's spectral response, each HAFI module operates independently, allowing for stage-specific frequency evidence retrieval.
- **Residual Text Modulation**: A global text representation, derived from a frozen CLIP text encoder, is used to modulate the visual queries. This text-conditioned bias, projected into a shared bottleneck space, acts as a residual semantic guide for the retrieval process, rather than entirely replacing image-dependent queries. A learnable scalar (`ηi`) controls the influence of this text modulation, initialized to allow training to commence from image-conditioned retrieval.
- **Spatially Aligned Band-wise Retrieval**: For each frequency band, cross-attention mechanisms retrieve relevant frequency tokens. To facilitate the learning of spatial correspondence between the visual and frequency token grids (which may have different resolutions), a lightweight spatial alignment prior (`B_b,i,nm`) is introduced. This prior softly encourages attention to frequency evidence originating from spatially proximate regions, while still allowing global retrieval based on content similarity.
- **Text-Conditioned Band Allocation**: A layer-level gate (`g_i`), conditioned on both the current visual state and the text representation, determines the proportional contribution of each frequency band (low, mid, high) to the final update. This allows the model to dynamically allocate spectral emphasis based on the query.
- **Residual Injection**: The updates from the selected frequency bands (`∆V_b,i`) are then residually injected back into the main visual pathway (`V_i = V_i- + λ_i * Σ(g_b,i * ∆V_b,i)`), preserving the pretrained visual representation while enriching it with task-relevant spectral information. A scalar (`λ_i`) controls the injection strength.

#### 3.4. Visual Enrichment Layer Adapter (VEL-Adapter)

Injecting frequency-enriched tokens alters the distribution of visual tokens presented to the language backbone. To ensure the LLM can effectively utilize this newly exposed evidence, a lightweight adaptation mechanism called VEL-Adapter is introduced. This adapter applies LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) specifically to the query and key projections of the first few shallow LLM layers (e.g., layers 0-3). This targeted adaptation recalibrates the visual-language interface to match the enriched visual tokens without modifying the value projections, feed-forward networks, or deeper language reasoning layers, thereby preserving the LLM's pretrained capabilities.

#### 3.5. Training Strategy and Objectives

The training proceeds in four stages to ensure stable integration and optimization:
1. **Stage 0: Frequency Branch Pretraining**: The frequency encoder and a DCT reconstruction head are trained using a DCT-space reconstruction loss (`L_dct_rec`). This pre-aligns the frequency branch to produce meaningful spectral representations.
2. **Stage 1: HAFI Warm-up**: The pretrained VLM and frequency branch are frozen. Only the HAFI modules (cross-attention and band gates) are warmed up using the standard autoregressive language modeling loss (`L_task`). This allows HAFI to learn to retrieve frequency tokens without immediately altering the core VLM.
3. **Stage 2: Joint Frequency Adaptation**: This stage involves joint optimization of HAFI, the frequency branch, selected visual encoder self-attention projections, and the VEL-Adapter parameters. The objective combines `L_task` with `L_dct_rec` and a gate-balancing loss (`L_bal`) to encourage diverse band usage.
4. **Stage 3: Task Fine-tuning**: All auxiliary objectives are removed. Only HAFI and VEL-Adapter parameters are fine-tuned using `L_task` to align training with inference conditions and consolidate performance.

The training uses AdamW optimizer, bf16 mixed precision, and is performed on NVIDIA A100 GPUs. Datasets are constructed in stage-specific mixtures covering general VQA, OCR-oriented, and knowledge-intensive tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several main findings and empirical results demonstrating the efficacy and mechanistic insights of HAFI-VLM.

#### 4.1. Empirical Performance

HAFI-VLM was evaluated on two representative VLM backbones, LLaVA-1.5-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B, across a suite of six benchmarks, including general VQA (VQAv2, GQA, SQA-I), text-rich understanding (TextVQA, DocVQA, ChartQA), and hallucination robustness (POPE, MMB).
- **Consistent Improvements**: HAFI-VLM demonstrated consistent performance gains across all evaluated benchmarks for both backbones. For instance, HAFI-VLM-LLaVA achieved an 82.8 score on VQAv2, 68.5 on TextVQA, and 89.0 on POPE, surpassing the LLaVA-1.5-7B baseline of 78.5, 58.2, and 86.6 respectively. Similar trends were observed for HAFI-VLM-Qwen.
- **Outperformance of Baselines**: The method outperformed various representation-level enhancement techniques (e.g., Dense Connector, MMFuser, TGIF) that fuse or reweight existing features.
- **Efficiency and Accuracy Trade-off**: HAFI-VLM also surpassed most high-resolution and region-based approaches (e.g., S$^2$, ViCrop, SD-RPN, Q-Zoom) while operating at native resolution and requiring only a single encoder pass. This indicates that task-conditioned frequency injection provides discriminative visual evidence efficiently, often recovering details that are not accessible through mere feature recombination or resolution scaling without its computational overhead.
- **Hallucination Robustness**: The strong performance on the POPE benchmark suggests that providing query-relevant spectral evidence contributes to a more complete perceptual basis for reasoning, which in turn reduces reliance on linguistic priors and improves hallucination robustness.

#### 4.2. Mechanistic Analyses

The study included several mechanistic analyses to understand how HAFI-VLM enhances perception:
- **Task-Adaptive Spectral Rebalancing**: Analysis revealed that HAFI-VLM induces structured, layer-dependent shifts in frequency band allocation. Compared to the base encoder, the enhanced model exhibited a substantially broader distribution of frequency usage across samples and tasks, approaching the diversity observed in input images. Fine-grained and text-rich benchmarks, for example, showed a reduction in low-frequency dominance and an increased emphasis on mid- and high-frequency components. Crucially, while increasing sample-level and task-dependent spectral variation, the overall layerwise frequency trajectories remained close to the pretrained profiles, indicating that HAFI expands spectral evidence without disrupting the core semantic-spectral prior.
- **Text-Conditioned Band Allocation**: The learned frequency injection weights were found to vary significantly with encoder depth and task semantics. Shallow layers tended to retrieve all three bands relatively evenly, intermediate layers emphasized high-frequency evidence, and deeper layers favored mid-frequency components for semantic integration. At the query level, questions related to attributes and categories induced stronger mid- and high-frequency injection, consistent with their reliance on edges and textures. In contrast, relation- and object-oriented questions favored low- and mid-frequency structures. This demonstrates that HAFI performs semantic-aware spectral retrieval, rather than indiscriminate detail amplification.
- **Preservation of Semantic Grounding**: Visual attention visualizations confirmed that HAFI-VLM largely preserved the dominant spatial regions attended to by the pretrained base model under various queries. Observed differences primarily reflected local redistribution of attention within or around these semantically relevant areas, rather than a displacement to unrelated structures. This finding indicates that frequency enrichment expands the available spectral evidence without overwriting the original semantic grounding capability of the VLM.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies provided insights into the contributions of different components of HAFI-VLM:
- **Frequency Bands and Injection Depths**: Jointly injecting all three frequency bands (low, mid, high) yielded the best performance. Low-frequency-only injection generally degraded accuracy, while mid- and high-frequency cues were beneficial but individually inferior to their combination, emphasizing the importance of complementary spectral information. Sparse multi-depth injection was favored over single-layer injection or no injection, suggesting functional specialization across encoder depths.
- **Role of VEL-Adapter**: Removing the VEL-Adapter led to a significant performance drop (an average of 7.8 points across benchmarks). This indicates that the frequency-enriched visual tokens cannot be effectively utilized by a fully frozen language backbone. The VEL-Adapter, by applying LoRA to only the query and key projections of shallow LLM layers, effectively restores the visual-language interface. Fully unfreezing the LLM backbone was less effective than localized adaptation, supporting the targeted approach.
- **Text Conditioning and Layer-Specific Injection**: Removing text conditioning consistently degraded performance, confirming that query semantics are essential for guiding the retrieval of task-relevant spectral evidence. Additionally, sharing HAFI parameters across different injection layers was suboptimal, suggesting that distinct retrieval functions are needed for early, intermediate, and late encoder stages due to their differing levels of visual and semantic abstraction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a notable contribution to the understanding and improvement of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), particularly in their ability to handle fine-grained visual information.

**Fundamental Insight**: The identification and characterization of "spectral response rigidity" in pretrained vision encoders offers a novel theoretical perspective on existing VLM limitations. By demonstrating that vision encoders exhibit persistent, query-invariant spectral allocations despite varied task demands, the work highlights a structural mismatch between static visual encoding and dynamic query requirements. This insight is significant as it diagnoses a previously overlooked cause for perception failures in VLMs, moving beyond mere capacity or resolution arguments.

**Methodological Advancement**: HAFI-VLM introduces a distinct and complementary approach to enhance VLM perception. By integrating a hierarchical, text-conditioned frequency injection pathway with a targeted adaptation of the language backbone, it allows for dynamic, task-relevant spectral evidence retrieval. A key advantage is its ability to operate at native image resolution without requiring additional high-resolution encoding or multiple passes through the vision encoder, offering a favorable accuracy-efficiency trade-off compared to many existing methods. This represents a substantial methodological step towards more flexible and efficient visual information processing in VLMs.

**Performance Enhancement**: The consistent and substantial performance gains observed across various general VQA, text-rich understanding, and hallucination robustness benchmarks validate the practical utility of HAFI-VLM. These improvements indicate that supplementing the pretrained semantic pathway with query-conditioned spectral evidence leads to more accurate and robust visual reasoning. The enhanced robustness against hallucinations is particularly impactful, as it addresses a critical challenge in deploying VLMs reliably.

**Mechanistic Understanding**: The detailed mechanistic analyses, including the demonstration of task-adaptive spectral rebalancing and text-conditioned band allocation, provide transparency into how HAFI-VLM operates. The finding that HAFI selectively amplifies spectral variation without disrupting the pretrained semantic grounding is crucial. It confirms that the method does not destructively modify the core VLM capabilities but rather augments them with necessary fine-grained perceptual cues, establishing frequency enrichment as a distinct and effective route for improving VLM perception.

**Future Implications**: This research opens new avenues for developing more advanced and versatile VLMs. It suggests that dynamic spectral adaptation, guided by linguistic queries, could be a crucial component for future models aiming for truly human-like fine-grained perception. Future work could explore more sophisticated frequency transformations, adaptive spatial-frequency integration strategies, or even extend the concept of "rigidity" to other latent dimensions within vision encoders. Ultimately, this work is likely to inspire further research into dynamic and query-aware visual encoding mechanisms that go beyond static feature extraction.
