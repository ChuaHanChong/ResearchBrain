# Exploring the Adversarial Vulnerabilities of Vision-Language-Action Models in Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> This research demonstrates that Vision-Language-Action models in robotics are highly susceptible to novel adversarial attacks, leading to substantial task failure rates and the exhibition of unsafe behaviors in both simulated and physical environments. The work introduces specific attack objectives and metrics to quantify these vulnerabilities effectively.

## Problem

- Generalist robots utilizing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, while offering unprecedented versatility, introduce new and underexplored attack surfaces.
- Existing adversarial attack methods are not tailored for the unique integrated nature of VLA models, which combine visual, linguistic, and temporal action components for end-to-end control.
- There was a lack of systematic evaluation for VLA model robustness to adversarial manipulation, especially considering real-world robotic dynamics and the safety implications of physical actions.

## Method

- Designed three novel, robot-specific adversarial attack objectives — ==Untargeted Action Discrepancy Attack== (**UADA**), ==Untargeted Position-aware Attack== (**UPA**), and ==Targeted Manipulation Attack== (**TMA**) — tailored for VLA models' spatial, functional, and temporal characteristics.
- Employed ==physically realizable patch-based adversarial attacks==, enhanced with ==inner-loops== and ==geometric transformations== for robustness in real-world viewing conditions.
- Introduced the ==Normalized Action Discrepancy== (**NAD**) metric to quantify the physical severity of adversarial perturbations at the action level, moving beyond simple task failure rates.

## Results

- Untargeted attacks (UADA, UPA) achieved average task failure rates up to **100%** in simulation and nearly **100%** in physical settings, drastically increasing from benign success rates.
- The proposed attacks yielded higher Normalized Action Discrepancy (NAD) values (e.g., UADA DoF1 **32.6%** in the physical setup) compared to baselines, indicating greater physical deviation from intended actions.
- Targeted Manipulation Attacks (TMA) successfully steered robots towards specific erroneous trajectories or forced specific degrees of freedom to zero, causing up to **100%** failure rates for targeted DoF combinations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLA models exhibit severe adversarial fragility, with the proposed attacks causing significant and often complete task failures, along with dangerous, erratic robot movements in real-world scenarios.
> - Attack objectives tailored to robotic actions are highly effective, demonstrating that simply misclassifying visual inputs is insufficient for evaluating VLA model robustness.
> - Adversarial patches generated can transfer effectively between different VLA models and from simulation to physical settings, proving robust against common defense strategies like JPEG compression or blurring.
