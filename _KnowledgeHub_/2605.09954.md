# JODA: Composable Joint Dynamics for Articulated Objects

> [!summary] Summary
> JODA is a framework that infers and refines structured, joint-level internal dynamics for articulated objects using multimodal inputs, capturing complex behaviors like detents and soft closing. The system quantitatively reduced the RMSE against real-world force measurements for objects such as refrigerator and cabinet doors, showing improved realism over simpler dynamic models.

## Problem

- Current articulated-body asset pipelines in physical simulation and robotics do not capture fine-grained dynamical effects, leading to unrealistic mechanical behaviors.
- Complex real-world joint interactions (e.g., frictional holding, detents, snap latching) are often omitted from automatically generated assets, requiring costly manual authoring.
- Existing simulation models rely on simplistic dynamics, such as basic target-position control or global friction/damping, which limits expressive capacity and contributes to a "sim-to-real gap".

## Method

- JODA models joint-level dynamics using three position-dependent fields (conservative force, dry-friction, damping) along a joint's degree of freedom, constructed by composing local effect components via piecewise cubic interpolation.
- A Vision-Language Model (VLM) is prompted to propose semantic effect compositions from a predefined template library, guided by joint context and multi-state visual inputs of the object.
- The framework includes a compiler to translate semantic VLM proposals into concrete dynamics profiles and supports iterative refinement through diagnostic feedback, direct editing, and differentiable simulation optimization against real-world trajectories.

## Results

- JODA generated non-monotonic force profiles for a lighter button ("push-through and pop-back," matching real-world observations) and modeled magnetic-seal effects for a refrigerator door and a spring-loaded latch for a microwave door, improving realism over baselines.
- Quantitative comparisons showed JODA's generated quasi-static opening forces achieved significantly lower RMSE (e.g., 0.121 vs. 0.240 for a refrigerator door) against real-world measurements compared to linear spring baselines.
- Differentiable optimization decreased trajectory Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss from 4.10 × 10⁻² to 1.56 × 10⁻³ for a cabinet door, enabling natural opening while maintaining appropriate support.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex internal mechanical behaviors of articulated objects can be effectively represented and abstracted as equivalent joint-level, position-dependent force, friction, and damping fields.
> - VLMs can accurately propose structured dynamical primitives by separating semantic choice from numerical realization, making the VLM's task manageable and improving reproducibility.
> - Combining VLM-guided semantic proposals with a composable, differentiable dynamics representation enables automatic inference, iterative refinement, and optimization for physically plausible and behaviorally accurate articulated objects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of physical simulation, robotics, and embodied AI relies heavily on articulated-body assets. Contemporary asset pipelines have made strides in recovering or synthesizing detailed visual geometry and kinematic structures, enabling the automatic separation of parts, assignment of joint axes and ranges, and loading into physics engines. However, a significant limitation persists: these pipelines generally do not capture the fine-grained dynamical effects crucial for realistic mechanical behavior. Real-world articulated objects exhibit complex interactions such as frictional holding at intermediate angles, detents, soft closing mechanisms, and snap latching. These effects, which govern how objects dynamically behave, are typically absent from automatically generated assets, often requiring manual authoring or extensive tuning at substantial cost. This deficiency contributes to a "sim-to-real gap," where simulated interactions do not accurately reflect real-world physics.

Existing approaches in this landscape either entirely disregard detailed dynamics or employ simplistic models with limited expressive capacity. For instance, common simulation-ready assets in benchmarks like ManiSkill2 and BEHAVIOR-1K often rely on basic target-position control (equivalent to simple springs) and global friction/damping parameters, which lack the specificity needed for nuanced joint behaviors. While physics simulators (e.g., MuJoCo, Brax, Dojo, Isaac Gym) provide primitives for motors, tendons, and generalized forces, articulating complex, everyday object dynamics like detents or endpoint attraction often requires designing intricate, hand-tuned force laws.

JODA addresses this specific gap by introducing a framework designed to generate the structured, joint-level internal dynamical effects that dictate motion beyond mere rigid-body inertia, gravity, and hard joint limits. Rather than attempting to reconstruct the exact hidden mechanical assemblies within an object, JODA models their *equivalent joint-level effects*. This distinction is central, as it allows for the representation of complex behaviors (e.g., from magnetic catches or spring latches) as position-dependent forces, friction, or damping fields along a joint's degree of freedom (DOF), abstracting away low-level mechanical intricacies. This aligns with engineering mechanics principles that use reduced or equivalent force models to represent complex joint interfaces without detailed contact simulation, and with industrial catalogs that classify such effects into recurring families (e.g., detent, bi-stable, soft-down).

The work leverages recent advancements in vision-language models (VLMs), which are shown to implicitly capture physical priors about object behavior. JODA's contribution lies in grounding these latent, unstructured VLM intuitions within a structured, composable representation suitable for direct simulation, editing, and optimization, thus extending articulated assets beyond just geometry and kinematics to executable, behavior-aware interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed limitations in current articulated-body asset generation, particularly the lack of realistic, fine-grained dynamical effects. While pipelines excel at geometry and kinematics, they often omit crucial behavioral aspects that define how articulated objects interact in the real world. This deficiency hinders the fidelity of physical simulations, robotics applications, and embodied AI systems, contributing to a persistent discrepancy between simulated and real-world behaviors—the "sim-to-real gap."

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the JODA framework are:

1. **To develop a novel representation for joint-level dynamics:** The aim is to move beyond simple kinematic descriptions by capturing complex interaction behaviors such as frictional holding, detents, soft closing, and snap latching. This requires a structured, expressive, and interpretable representation that can encode position-dependent generalized forces, dry friction, and damping along a joint's degree of freedom. This representation should abstract away the underlying mechanical complexity, focusing instead on observable, equivalent joint-level effects.
2. **To enable automatic inference of joint dynamics from multimodal inputs:** A significant challenge is to initialize these structured dynamics without manual supervision or explicit design. The objective is to leverage multimodal inputs, specifically visual observations of an object across various joint configurations and associated joint context (e.g., type, range), combined with the implicit physical priors captured by vision-language models (VLMs). The goal is for VLMs to propose structured dynamical primitives that can be composed into a unified dynamics field.
3. **To ensure compatibility with physical simulation and differentiability:** The generated dynamics representation must be executable within standard physics simulators (e.g., MuJoCo/MJX) to facilitate realistic interactions. Furthermore, the representation should be compatible with differentiable simulation, allowing for gradient-based optimization and refinement of the dynamic profiles against various supervision signals, including real-world interaction trajectories.
4. **To provide a unified interface for inference, editing, and optimization:** The framework aims to integrate the processes of generating, modifying, and refining joint dynamics. This includes supporting direct user manipulation of dynamic parameters, language-guided semantic editing via VLM feedback, and automated optimization through differentiable simulation. This unified approach seeks to offer flexibility and control over the generated behaviors.
5. **To create a composable and interpretable dynamics model:** The underlying formulation should be compact and allow for the composition of various local effects into a global dynamics field. This composability, combined with an interpretable function space (e.g., piecewise cubic interpolation), is intended to make the generated dynamics transparent and amenable to targeted adjustments.

By achieving these objectives, JODA seeks to elevate the realism and utility of articulated-body assets, enhancing their applicability across simulation, robotics, and embodied AI, and ultimately contributing to a reduction of the sim-to-real gap.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

JODA is a framework designed to infer and refine structured joint-level dynamics for articulated objects from multimodal inputs. The pipeline operates in several stages: input processing, dynamic representation, VLM-guided proposal, compilation, iterative refinement, and optimization.

**Input Formulation:**
The input to JODA for a selected target joint includes:
- A visual model of the articulated asset.
- Its kinematic hierarchy and finite joint range.
- **Joint Context:** Automatically extracted from standard simulation tools (e.g., MuJoCo), this includes joint type (e.g., revolute, prismatic), parent and child bodies, joint axis, joint range, equivalent inertia or mass, and an estimation of gravitational effects along the degree of freedom (DOF).
- **Multi-state images:** Rendered images of the object at various joint configurations.
- **Effect template previews:** Visualizations of predefined dynamical primitive behaviors.

**Joint-Level Dynamical Representation:**
JODA represents the internal dynamical structure of a joint using three position-dependent fields over a normalized joint coordinate `s` (from 0 to 1):
1. **Conservative channel (`F_cons(s)`):** Models position-dependent generalized forces or torques, capturing effects like spring return, magnetic attraction, detent wells, and snap-through barriers.
2. **Dry-friction channel (`F_fric,max(s)`):** Models the maximum local static friction magnitude.
3. **Damping channel (`C_damp(s)`):** Models a velocity-dependent resistive coefficient, producing a force proportional to `-C_damp(s) * dq/dt`.

Each channel is constructed by composing local effect components. A component is defined by an active interval `[a_j, b_j]`, local control point positions `x_j,k`, and corresponding values `y_j,k`. Shape-constrained piecewise cubic interpolation (PCHIP) is used to generate smooth curves over these control points, preserving physically plausible profiles. Outside its active interval, a component contributes zero. The final channel-level field is obtained by summing or taking the maximum over active components. This representation is designed to be low-dimensional, local, interpretable, and editable.

**Effect Template Library:**
A library of composable effect templates provides the behavioral vocabulary for JODA. These templates are mechanism-agnostic, defining equivalent joint-level behaviors rather than specific internal designs. Each template contains semantic metadata (name, description, typical placement priors) and prototype curves for the conservative, friction, and damping channels in local normalized coordinates. Examples include `constant_friction_hinge`, `spring_return_to_low_end`, and `detent_internal`.

**VLM-guided Effect Proposal:**
The Vision-Language Model (VLM) stage receives joint context, multi-state rendered images, and effect-template previews. Prompted as a mechanics analyst, the VLM reasons about the *felt behavior* a user would experience. Crucially, the VLM is instructed not to output numerical control points directly but rather a JSON object containing semantic effect proposals. Each proposal specifies an `effect_name` from the template library, an `active interval` (`start_ratio`, `end_ratio`), and qualitative `strength` labels (e.g., "weak," "medium," "strong," "dominant") for each of the three channels. This separation of semantic choice from numerical realization simplifies the VLM's task.

**Compilation to Dynamics:**
A compiler translates the VLM's semantic proposals into a concrete, multi-component PCHIP field model (`composed.json`). This involves:
- Converting normalized intervals from VLM proposals to real joint intervals.
- Mapping qualitative strength labels to numerical multipliers by sampling within predefined bands, ensuring some variability while maintaining repeatability through stable keys.
- Computing reference magnitudes for physical scaling based on joint context. For conservative force and friction, this uses an estimated gravity scale and an inertial scale (derived from equivalent inertia/mass, joint range, and reference travel time). Damping reference is chosen to yield reference force at a reference speed.
- Scaling each template channel's prototype curve so its peak matches the target channel reference multiplied by the sampled qualitative multiplier.

**Diagnostic Feedback and Iterative Proposal:**
After compilation, JODA generates a diagnostic visualization of the dynamics profile (e.g., force, friction, damping curves) across the joint DOF. This visualization, which provides an interpretable link between the generated dynamics and resulting behavior, is fed back to the VLM. The VLM can then iteratively revise its proposals (adjusting effect types, intervals, strengths, or refinement factors) to align the generated dynamics more closely with the desired interaction behavior, operating in a closed-loop.

**Interactive and Differentiable Refinement:**
JODA supports multiple modes for refining the generated dynamics:
1. **Direct editing:** Experienced users can manually adjust control-point values, channel strengths, active interval anchors, and joint-limit damping parameters.
2. **Human-supervised VLM refinement:** Users provide natural language feedback (e.g., "the closing pull is too strong"), prompting the VLM to revise its proposals semantically.
3. **Differentiable simulation optimization:** For higher physical accuracy, differentiable simulation (implemented on MuJoCo/MJX backend) allows for gradient-based optimization of joint profiles. Leveraging real-world interaction trajectories as supervision targets, this mode can automatically tune parameters to match observed motion, using JAX-compatible PCHIP interpolation and appropriate smoothing for friction/limits.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates JODA on diverse articulated objects from datasets like PartNet-Mobility and Lightwheel Sim-Ready Assets, demonstrating its capability to generate plausible and controllable joint behaviors.

**Qualitative Case Studies:**
- **Lighter button (prismatic):** JODA successfully generated a profile that included a full-range spring-like return combined with an internal snap-like structure. This non-monotonic force profile resulted in characteristic "push-through and pop-back" high-speed motion intervals during a virtual-hand interaction, which qualitatively matched observations from a real lighter and its measured trajectory data. A simple linear spring baseline failed to reproduce these transients.
- **Refrigerator door (revolute):** The framework captured a magnetic-seal effect, causing the door to automatically close when nearly shut. A baseline lacking this structure failed to close under similar conditions, highlighting JODA's ability to model helpful closing assist mechanisms.
- **Microwave door (revolute):** JODA generated a profile with a spring-loaded latch near the closed configuration, creating an energy barrier that required an additional push to fully latch. In contrast, a constant drag baseline allowed the door to close unrealistically easily under a slight push, demonstrating the importance of JODA's detailed dynamics for faithful interaction. These results indicate that JODA can reduce sim-to-real discrepancies by embedding physically accurate behaviors.

**Quantitative Comparison with Real-World Data:**
To quantitatively assess the generated dynamics, JODA's derived quasi-static opening forces were compared against real-world measurements from prior work (Jain et al., 2010) for objects like refrigerator and kitchen cabinet doors.
- The comparison involved normalizing the force curves and evaluating them over a 0–60° grid.
- JODA's generated profiles exhibited a closer match to the measured curves than a linear spring baseline. Specifically, for a refrigerator door, JODA achieved an RMSE of 0.121 compared to the spring baseline's 0.240. For a kitchen cabinet door, JODA's RMSE was 0.180 versus the spring's 0.386.
- This demonstrates that JODA's expressive joint-level dynamics are capable of reproducing the structured, nonlinear force profiles observed in real-world objects, unlike simpler models which are constrained to linear force variations.

**Computational Cost:**
- JODA introduces a modest simulation overhead, averaging 0.062 ms per simulation step at `dt = 0.005s`, which is approximately 1.37 times the cost of the original MuJoCo simulation.
- For VLM proposal, each iteration consumed an average of 25.5K input tokens and produced 4.7K output tokens (including reasoning).

**Refinement Experiments:**
- **Differentiable Optimization:** In a cabinet door example, an initial VLM-generated profile provided excessive support, preventing natural opening under gravity. By optimizing against three sampled real-world trajectories, the trajectory MSE loss significantly decreased from 4.10 × 10⁻² to 1.56 × 10⁻³ after 9 Adam iterations. The optimized profile allowed the door to open naturally while retaining appropriate support, demonstrating the efficacy of differentiable refinement.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Absence of Template Library:** Directly prompting the VLM to output numerical control points without the structured template library significantly degraded performance. The VLM struggled with quantitative reasoning, often failing to choose appropriate magnitudes and introducing behavioral defects (e.g., weak magnetic attraction, weak conservative forces that drop to zero at balance points). This ablation also compromised reproducibility.
- **VLM Iteration Step:** Iterative refinement was found to be crucial, especially for objects influenced by gravity. For a dishwasher door, the initial VLM proposal (Round 1) exhibited insufficient near-closed attraction and excessive opening-end assist. After an iteration (Round 2), these issues were resolved, with increased near-closed attraction and decreased far-end assist, matching the intended effect. Gravity-affected scenes averaged 2.2 proposal rounds to satisfy the initial effect specification.

These findings collectively indicate that JODA provides a robust and efficient framework for generating, simulating, and refining realistic, expressive joint dynamics for articulated objects, outperforming simpler baselines and demonstrating the value of its structured representation, VLM integration, and iterative refinement capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

JODA represents a step in addressing a fundamental limitation in the development and utilization of articulated-body assets for virtual environments and robotic applications. By synthesizing fine-grained joint-level dynamics, the framework significantly enhances the physical fidelity of simulated objects, moving beyond basic geometric and kinematic descriptions to capture nuanced interaction behaviors.

The potential impacts of this work include:

1. **Reducing the Sim-to-Real Gap:** By enabling the generation of more physically accurate and behaviorally consistent articulated objects, JODA can lead to more realistic simulations. This is crucial for robotics, where policies trained in simulation often struggle to transfer to the real world due to discrepancies in physical interaction. Improved dynamics can facilitate more effective sim-to-real transfer for robotic manipulation and interaction tasks.
2. **Scalable Asset Generation:** The automation of joint dynamics generation, leveraging vision-language models and a structured representation, mitigates the need for costly and time-consuming manual authoring or expert tuning. This allows for the scalable creation of large datasets of behaviorally rich articulated objects, which is essential for training data-hungry embodied AI agents and populating complex virtual environments.
3. **Enhanced Realism in Virtual Environments:** For applications like interactive scene generation, gaming, and virtual reality, JODA can provide objects that respond more authentically to user interaction, thereby increasing immersion and user experience.
4. **Unified and Interpretable Framework:** JODA integrates representation, inference, and optimization into a cohesive framework. The composable, PCHIP-based representation is designed for interpretability, allowing researchers and developers to understand and directly manipulate specific aspects of joint behavior. This provides a versatile tool for both automated generation and expert-guided design.
5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a methodology for grounding implicit physical priors from VLMs into actionable, structured dynamical models. This could inspire further research into leveraging large language and vision models for more complex physical reasoning, beyond single-joint dynamics. Future extensions could include modeling hidden-state effects, non-linear damping, history-dependent friction, multi-dimensional DOFs, and multi-joint couplings, broadening the scope of physical phenomena that can be automatically synthesized.
6. **Improved Benchmarking and Evaluation:** With richer dynamic models, benchmarks for embodied AI and robotics can incorporate more challenging and realistic interaction scenarios, leading to more robust evaluations of agent capabilities.

In summary, JODA transforms joint dynamics into an authorable and composable representation, extending articulated assets beyond geometry to executable interaction behavior. This advancement contributes to the development of physically grounded, behavior-aware assets vital for the progression of simulation, robotics, and interactive systems.
