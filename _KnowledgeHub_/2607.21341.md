# Grasp, Handover, Rotate: Bimanual Object Reorientation via Compositional Diffusion and Energy-Based Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework from researchers at The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology and Shenzhen Loop Area Institute, BiCompoDiff, unifies grasp selection and trajectory planning for bimanual object reorientation by combining compositional diffusion models and energy-based optimization. The system demonstrated an 81.7% success rate and a 12.5% reduction in joint displacement compared to a baseline, enabling efficient and robust robotic manipulation in both simulated and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Directly achieving a target object pose from an initial grasp is often impractical due to collisions, kinematic limitations, or an unsuitable final orientation, necessitating an intermediate reorientation step.
- Existing single-arm reorientation methods often rely on external fixtures or stable planar surfaces, restricting operational flexibility and general applicability.
- Prior dual-arm approaches typically decompose the problem into sequential stages, using rejection sampling or heuristic methods, which can lead to kinematically infeasible transitions or suboptimal reorientation by overlooking the complete trajectory cost.

## Method

- Developed **BiCompoDiff**, a unified ==energy-based optimization== framework for bimanual object reorientation that jointly optimizes 6-DoF grasp poses for pick, handover, regrasp, and place in cluttered environments.
- Integrated a pretrained ==grasp diffusion model== with ==compositional energy-based models (EBMs)== that encode bimanual planning constraints such as collision avoidance, trajectory smoothness, handover feasibility, and regrasp safety.
- Employed ==gradient-guided MCMC sampling== during the ==reverse diffusion process==, combining diffusion scores and EBM gradients, to iteratively refine grasp poses and balance multiple competing objectives.

## Results

- BiCompoDiff-Full achieved an **81.7%** success rate in simulated bimanual reorientation tasks, representing a **23.4%** absolute increase compared to an adapted ReorientBot baseline.
- The framework demonstrated superior motion efficiency, achieving a **12.5%** smaller total joint displacement and **7.0%** lower execution duration compared to the baseline, indicating smoother and more efficient robot trajectories.
- Real-world experiments validated successful sim-to-real transfer, showing significant reductions in total joint displacement (e.g., **59.7%** for a T-shape brick and **46.4%** for a cup) compared to an ablation without EBM guidance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying grasp planning and motion objectives through compositional diffusion and energy-based optimization allows for simultaneous optimization of high-dimensional grasp and key poses for bimanual reorientation.
> - A learned, differentiable inverse kinematics model (SubnetIK) provides stable and effective gradients for joint-space motion costs, enabling robust optimization even for kinematically challenging or unreachable poses.
> - Annealed MCMC refinement within the reverse diffusion loop significantly improves sampling from the constrained posterior distribution, leading to more feasible, higher-quality bimanual manipulation sequences compared to direct denoising.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rearrangement of objects into specific poses is a fundamental requirement in robotic systems, applicable to both domestic and industrial contexts. However, directly achieving a target pose from an initial grasp is frequently impractical due to factors such as potential collisions, kinematic limitations of the robot, or an unsuitable final object orientation. In such instances, object reorientation becomes an essential intermediate step in the manipulation sequence.

Prior research has predominantly addressed object reorientation within a single-arm robotic framework. These methods often necessitate external fixtures or the identification of a stable intermediate pose on a planar surface, which restricts the operational flexibility of the robot. Dual-arm configurations offer enhanced flexibility by mitigating gravitational constraints and providing more options for intermediate poses. However, this flexibility introduces complexities in simultaneously optimizing grasp selection, trajectory length, and intermediate pose choices. Existing dual-arm reorientation approaches typically decompose the problem into sequential stages, relying on rejection sampling or heuristic methods to generate solutions. Such modular pipelines can lead to kinematically infeasible transitions or suboptimal reorientation due to heuristic grasp sampling that overlooks the complete trajectory cost.

This work differentiates itself by treating bimanual object reorientation as a unified energy-based optimization problem, rather than a sequence of discrete stages. It integrates energy-based models (EBMs) and diffusion models, which have demonstrated capabilities in compositional generalization and unifying grasp generation with trajectory planning. By extending compositional EBMs to the domain of bimanual object reorientation, this research aims to achieve tighter integration of constraints such as dual-arm coordination, collision avoidance, and grasp quality across the entire reorientation sequence, addressing the limitations of prior modular or single-arm techniques.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from the inherent challenges in robotic object manipulation, specifically when a direct pick-and-place operation is insufficient. A grasp suitable for initial acquisition often proves incompatible with the requirements for final placement due to collision risks, kinematic constraints, or an undesirable final orientation, as illustrated in Figure 2. This necessitates an intermediate reorientation step. While single-arm solutions exist, they frequently depend on external aids or stable planar surfaces, limiting their versatility.

Bimanual manipulation, mirroring human behavior, offers a more flexible approach to reorientation. One arm can grasp and reorient an object, hand it over to the second arm, which then performs the final placement (Figure 1). This dual-arm approach expands the possibilities for intermediate object poses but simultaneously introduces a complex multi-objective optimization problem involving grasp quality, trajectory efficiency, and the selection of an appropriate handover configuration. Traditional sequential planning methods, which often use rejection sampling or heuristics, struggle with the coupled nature of these objectives, leading to inefficiencies and suboptimal manipulation outcomes.

The key objectives of this work are to:
- Develop a unified optimization framework for bimanual object reorientation that jointly identifies feasible manipulation trajectories and enables efficient execution.
- Integrate multiple competing objectives, including grasp quality, motion planning, collision avoidance, handover feasibility, and regrasp safety, into a single optimization process.
- Encode these objectives as differentiable energy-based models (EBMs) and combine them with diffusion scores from a pretrained grasp generation model.
- Utilize gradient-guided MCMC sampling to generate bimanual grasp poses from a constrained posterior distribution, effectively balancing the various competing objectives.
- Demonstrate that this unified framework can achieve higher success rates and generate smoother, more efficient robot trajectories compared to existing sampling-based baselines in simulated and real-world scenarios.
- Introduce a learning-based inverse kinematics model to facilitate swift evaluation of motion costs and feasibility within the optimization loop.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, termed BiCompoDiff (Bimanual Compositional Diffusion and Energy-based Optimization), is designed to jointly optimize 6-DoF grasp poses for dual-arm pick-and-place tasks in cluttered environments. It achieves this by integrating a pretrained grasp diffusion model with compositional energy-based guidance during the reverse diffusion process.

**Problem Formulation:**
The task involves optimizing a full reorientation sequence: pick with one arm (g_PK), handover to the second arm (g_HO), regrasp with the second arm (g_RG), and place at the target (g_PL). The framework optimizes a batch of grasp tuples, g = (g_PK, g_HO, g_RG, g_PL), where g_RG is deterministically computed from the other three poses and the object's initial and target poses. The goal is to minimize a composite objective combining a pretrained diffusion prior (L_diff) and various task constraints (∑ λ_c * L_c).

**Overview of the Pipeline (Figure 3):**
BiCompoDiff operates in three tightly coupled phases:
1. **Initialization:** Noisy initial poses for pick (g_t_PK), place (g_t_PL), and handover (g_t_HO) are randomly sampled in the world frame. The regrasp pose (g_t_RG) is derived deterministically. Object and environment point clouds (P_initial, P_target, P_scene) are provided.
2. **Iterative Refinement:** During reverse diffusion, a pretrained grasp diffusion model (GraspGen) predicts epsilon-scores for g_t_PK and g_t_PL (converted to the object frame). Concurrently, differentiable Bimanual Planning EBMs compute energy gradients for collision avoidance, trajectory smoothness, handover feasibility, and regrasp safety. These scores and gradients are combined to provide guidance, iteratively refining the noisy poses (g_t_PK, g_t_PL, g_t_HO) at each timestep.
3. **Selection and Execution:** At the final timestep (t=0), collision-free pose pairs are filtered. The remaining candidates are ranked using a composite score that balances grasp quality (from GraspGen's confidence discriminator) and planning costs (smoothness, regrasp safety). The highest-scoring pair is selected, and its corresponding key poses are passed to a motion planner (cuRobo) to generate the complete bimanual reorientation trajectory.

**Core Components:**
1. **Grasp Diffusion Model:** The framework utilizes a pretrained GraspGen model (specifically, the `robotiq 2f 140` checkpoint). This DiffusionTransformer generates multi-modal 6-DoF grasp pose distributions conditioned on object point clouds. During inference, it performs guided reverse diffusion, simultaneously generating g_PK (conditioned on P_initial) and g_PL (conditioned on P_target), keeping them within the learned grasp manifold. The model's discriminator also provides scalar grasp confidence scores, which contribute to the final composite score.

2. **Bimanual Planning EBMs:** These energy functions provide differentiable gradients that guide the reverse diffusion process towards bimanual manipulation constraints. The gradients are computed with respect to the pick, handover, regrasp, and place grasp poses.
 - **Grasp Collision Avoidance:** Grippers are approximated by collision spheres. Signed distances to the scene point cloud (P_scene) are queried using a KDTree. A quadratic penalty is applied if any sphere is below a specified safety margin (δ), generating smooth repelling gradients near obstacles.
 - **Trajectory Smoothness (c_smooth):** This objective minimizes the total joint motion between key configurations (pick, handover, regrasp, place). A wrapped L1 norm is used as a proxy: `c_smooth = ||∆q_PK→HO||_1 + ||∆q_RG→PL||_1`. To compute joint configurations (q) and their differentiable gradients, the authors developed **Joint-Decoupled SubnetMLP (SubnetIK)**. SubnetIK is a trained differentiable inverse kinematics (IK) model for the UR12e robot. For each key pose, it predicts multiple joint candidates. Valid solutions are filtered by forward kinematics (FK) pose error, and the set minimizing `c_smooth` is selected. SubnetIK provides exact gradients via the chain rule (`∂c_smooth/∂g_k = ∂c_smooth/∂q_k ⋅ J_f(g_k)`), offering stability and guidance even for unreachable poses, unlike analytical IK.
 - **Handover Feasibility and Safety:**
 - Feasibility is enforced through a soft penalty for positions outside a defined workspace bounding box and an orientation cost `c_orient`. This cost penalizes inward-pointing gripper orientations and near-planar alignments of the gripper's z-axis with the robot's xz-plane, ensuring a stable and accessible handover.
 - Safety is maintained using signed-distance field penalties from collision spheres at the handover pose to scene obstacles.
 - **Regrasp Safety (c_regrasp):** This prevents collisions between the pick and placement grippers during the regrasp phase. The cost penalizes insufficient clearance based on the minimum surface-to-surface distance between collision spheres of the two grippers.

3. **Iterative Compositional Energy-Based Optimization with Annealed MCMC (Algorithm 1):** Grasp poses are optimized through annealed MCMC-guided reverse diffusion. For the final `k` denoising steps, `M` (default 5) MCMC refinements are performed at each timestep. In these refinements, the diffusion model's predicted mean (`μ_t`) is combined with weighted task constraint gradients (`∇g`), and noise is injected. Only the final refinement advances to the next timestep. This MCMC loop improves sampling from the constrained posterior, leading to grasps that satisfy both the diffusion prior likelihood and task constraints.

**Post-Optimization Scoring:**
After the optimization, candidate pose pairs are filtered by a collision checker (cuRobo). The remaining valid pairs are ranked using a composite score: `composite = w_conf ⋅ conf_norm + w_plan ⋅ (1 - planning_norm)`. `conf_norm` is the normalized average grasp confidence from GraspGen for g_PK and g_PL. `planning_norm` is the normalized planning cost, combining trajectory smoothness and regrasp cost. The highest-scoring pair is then used to generate the final bimanual trajectory.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of BiCompoDiff was evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulated and real-world environments.

**Implementation Details:**
- **Simulation Pipeline:** A modular simulation pipeline was developed, featuring a task sandbox for scene definition (using Fusion 360 models and point clouds), simplified physics for grasping/placement (rigid attachment/detachment of objects to grippers), and a motion planning backend (cuRobo for minimum-jerk, collision-free joint trajectories between key poses).
- **Task Benchmark:** A benchmark comprising 60 pick-and-place reorientation tasks was created, categorized into 20 easy, 20 medium, and 20 hard scenarios to assess performance across varying levels of scene complexity and clutter.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Performance was measured by:
 - **Motion Efficiency:** Total joint-space displacement (Traj. ∆ in rad, primary metric), end-effector trajectory length (Traj. Len. in m), and execution duration (Dur in s).
 - **Grasp Quality:** Pick and place confidence scores (PK Conf., PL Conf.) from GraspGen, and percentage of gripper finger pad contact area (PK CA %, PL CA %).
 - **Safety & Feasibility:** Regrasp cost (RG Cost, penalizing gripper-to-gripper collisions), path success rate (Succ.%, for fully collision-free trajectories), and average number of collision-free grasp pairs (CF).
 - Computational cost (inference time).
- **Baselines:**
 - **NoEBM:** An ablation of BiCompoDiff where bimanual planning EBM gradients are removed, relying solely on the GraspGen diffusion score for denoising.
 - **ReorientBot (adapted):** An adaptation of a single-arm reorientation method to a bimanual setting. It samples 100 pick, 100 place, and random handover grasps (using GraspGen), followed by heuristic filtering (IK solutions, scene collision, joint-space validity, smoothness scoring). This baseline uses exhaustive grasp pairing (100^3 candidates).

**Ablation Studies (Table I):**
1. **Optimization Modes:**
 - **BiCompoDiff-Full:** Full joint optimization (all poses g_PK, g_PL, g_HO receive smoothness and task-specific gradients). This variant achieved the best overall motion efficiency: 37% lower joint delta (4.569 rad vs 7.293 rad for NoEBM), 20% shorter trajectory length, and 23% shorter duration. It also significantly reduced regrasp cost by 84% and increased success rate by 22% (absolute), with 77% more collision-free pairs.
 - **BiCompoDiff-HO:** Handover-only mode, where smoothness gradients are applied only to g_HO, while g_PK and g_PL receive other task gradients. This mode attained the highest success rate (70.0%) and the lowest regrasp cost (0.208).
 - Conclusion: BiCompoDiff-Full demonstrated superior overall trajectory efficiency, while BiCompoDiff-HO prioritized maximizing success and minimizing regrasp costs. Full mode achieved a balanced performance across various metrics. Figure 4 qualitatively illustrates the trajectory differences.
2. **Effect of MCMC Guidance:** Enabling MCMC refinement steps within the reverse diffusion loop (in Full mode, fixed k=3) led to improvements across all evaluated metrics (motion efficiency, grasp quality, safety) compared to the Full-NoMCMC variant, with a moderate increase in inference time. Consequently, MCMC was used by default.
3. **Effect of Gradient Application Steps (k):** (Figure 6, Appendix) Increasing `k` (the number of denoising steps where EBM gradients are applied) from 1 to 10 generally improved trajectory quality, grasp confidence, contact area, and collision-free pairs. The primary gains were observed up to `k` ≈ 5-6, with saturation thereafter. Success rate showed a mild peak at low-to-mid `k` before a slight decline at very high `k`. Regrasp cost did not exhibit a consistent trend. Inference time increased with `k`. The authors selected `k=3` as the default to balance quality and efficiency.

**Comparison to ReorientBot (Table II):**
BiCompoDiff-Full substantially outperformed the adapted ReorientBot baseline:
- **Success Rate:** 81.7% for BiCompoDiff-Full compared to 58.3% for ReorientBot, representing a 23.4% absolute increase.
- **Motion Efficiency:** BiCompoDiff-Full achieved smoother motions with a 12.5% smaller joint delta, 2.1% shorter trajectory length, and 7.0% lower execution duration.
- While ReorientBot generated a much higher number of collision-free grasp pairs (479.3 vs. 52.7), it exhibited lower grasp quality and incurred significantly higher computational costs due to its exhaustive search strategy.
- An ablation study (Full w/o ∇g_coll) indicated that the efficiency gains of BiCompoDiff primarily stemmed from the injection of differentiable bimanual smoothness gradients rather than solely from grasp collision avoidance. This demonstrates the advantage of end-to-end gradient guidance over traditional sample-and-filter methods.

**Real-World Experiments:**
Validation on a real-world bimanual platform (two UR12e arms with Robotiq Hand-E grippers) confirmed successful sim-to-real transfer. Qualitative observations (supplementary video) showed smooth and collision-free bimanual coordination in complex scenes. Quantitative evaluation on representative scenes (T-shape brick and cup) demonstrated significant improvements in trajectory efficiency: the T-shape brick task improved its total joint displacement from 10.850 rad (NoEBM) to 4.371 rad (a 59.7% reduction), and the cup task improved from 10.126 rad to 5.427 rad (a 46.4% reduction). These results indicate the practical applicability and benefits of the diffusion-guided compositional energy optimization approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces BiCompoDiff, a framework that addresses the complex problem of bimanual object reorientation in robotics by unifying grasp selection, handover planning, regrasp, and motion planning. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating the efficacy of compositional diffusion and energy-based optimization for co-optimizing high-dimensional grasp and key poses while explicitly accounting for motion-level objectives. By tightly integrating multiple constraints—including trajectory smoothness, collision avoidance, handover feasibility, and regrasp safety—into a single, gradient-guided optimization process, BiCompoDiff overcomes the limitations of sequential and heuristic-based planning approaches. The development of SubnetIK, a differentiable inverse kinematics model, further contributes to this capability by enabling effective gradient-based refinement, which is critical for generating smooth and kinematically feasible robot movements.

The potential impact of BiCompoDiff is substantial across several domains:
- **Robotics Manipulation:** The framework offers a more robust, efficient, and human-like approach to bimanual manipulation in cluttered and constrained environments. This could significantly enhance robot capabilities in various applications, such as domestic service robotics (e.g., household chores), industrial assembly lines requiring intricate object reorientation, and logistics tasks involving complex rearrangement.
- **Planning Efficiency and Reliability:** By guiding the generative process towards feasible solutions through energy-based gradients, BiCompoDiff reduces the reliance on extensive and often costly rejection sampling inherent in traditional methods. This leads to more efficient planning and a higher success rate in task execution, translating to more reliable robotic systems.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful validation of BiCompoDiff in real-world experiments underscores its practical applicability. This robust sim-to-real transfer capability is crucial for deploying advanced manipulation strategies from simulation to physical robotic platforms.
- **Future Research Directions:** This work opens avenues for future research. The current framework assumes perfect perception (known object poses and geometries) and optimizes trajectories indirectly via key poses. Future work could explore more direct trajectory optimization methods, enhance robustness to noisy or partial observations (e.g., from real-world sensors), and integrate more sophisticated task-level reasoning to handle even more complex and adaptive manipulation scenarios. Such advancements could further bridge the gap between human-like dexterity and robotic capabilities.
