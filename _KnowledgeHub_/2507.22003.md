# See Different, Think Better: Visual Variations Mitigating Hallucinations in LVLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai University developed ViHallu, a vision-centric framework that mitigates hallucinations in Large Vision-Language Models by generating visual variations and tailored instructions to improve visual-semantic alignment. The approach resulted in up to a 14.4-point overall performance gain on LLaVA-Bench and reduced hallucination rates by up to 5.9% on MMHal-Bench across various LVLM architectures.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently hallucinate, generating text inconsistent with visual content, especially when dealing with fine-grained visual distinctions.
- Existing
- text-centric
- hallucination mitigation methods often fail to fundamentally enhance visual comprehension, instead focusing on language generation adjustments.
- There is a scarcity of naturally occurring datasets with sufficient fine-grained visual variations needed to train LVLMs to discriminate subtle visual features.

## Method

- The ViHallu framework generates high-quality ==visual variation images== using ==ControlNet++== by editing captions and preserving original image structure, including ==counterfactual interventions==.
- It constructs tailored visual instructions using an LLM (DeepSeek-chat V2) for question generation and a VQA model (InternVL2.5) for ground-truth answers based on original and varied image pairs.
- A ==multi-model consensus approach==, employing LLaVA-1.5, MiniCPM-V 2.6, and mPLUG-OWL3, validates the quality of the generated Question-Answer pairs for the ViHallu-Instruction dataset.

## Results

- Improved F1 scores on the POPE dataset across LLaVA-1.5, MiniGPT4 v2, and Qwen2-VL, with MiniGPT4 v2 showing the most significant gains of **2.93-5.23%** in accuracy and outperforming other text-centric methods.
- Enhanced overall performance on the LLaVA-Bench (in-the-wild) dataset, with Qwen2-VL achieving an impressive **14.4-point** increase and MiniGPT-4 V2 gaining **10.4 points** in conversation.
- Reduced hallucination rates on the MMHal-Bench dataset by up to **5.9%** (Qwen2-VL) and **5.7%** (LLaVA-1.5), alongside increased overall scores across all evaluated models, particularly in challenging categories like Adversarial and Environment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly enhancing visual-semantic alignment through synthetically generated and controlled visual variations is more effective for hallucination mitigation than relying solely on text-centric adjustments.
> - Counterfactual interventions in visual data generation, such as placing objects in uncommon contexts, force LVLMs to learn from visual evidence rather than spurious linguistic or statistical priors.
> - A robust pipeline leveraging advanced generative models and multi-model consensus for quality control can create high-quality, targeted instruction datasets to improve fine-grained visual understanding in LVLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) have emerged as powerful tools, demonstrating impressive capabilities in multimodal tasks such as image description, visual question answering, and complex reasoning. Models like LLaVA, BLIP-2, and InstructBLIP have showcased significant advancements in bridging the gap between visual and linguistic modalities. However, a pervasive and critical challenge for LVLMs is the phenomenon of "hallucination," where models generate textual responses that are inconsistent with the visual content. This can manifest as identifying non-existent objects, misattributing properties, or incorrectly describing spatial relationships. Such inaccuracies severely undermine model reliability and hinder their deployment in sensitive applications like medical diagnosis or autonomous driving.

The root cause of hallucination in LVLMs is often attributed to weak visual-semantic alignment, especially when dealing with fine-grained visual distinctions. Models frequently struggle to differentiate subtle visual cues, leading them to rely on linguistic priors or statistical associations rather than actual visual evidence. For instance, an LVLM might describe a "black horse" when presented with a "black-and-white spotted appaloosa horse," highlighting its inability to capture fine details.

Previous research efforts to mitigate hallucination have predominantly adopted "text-centric" approaches. These include improving dataset quality by eliminating noisy or erroneous labels, incorporating negative textual examples (e.g., "hallucinatory captions" as hard negative samples), or reframing mitigation as a preference optimization task. While these methods have shown some success in refining text generation capabilities, they often fall short in genuinely enhancing the model's fundamental visual comprehension, particularly concerning fine-grained distinctions. Studies like LRV-Instruction and HalluciDoctor, for example, primarily focus on manipulating textual instructions or filtering hallucinated text content.

This paper, "See Different, Think Better," addresses this critical gap by proposing a novel **vision-centric** approach. It positions itself as a direct counterpoint to text-centric methods, arguing that true hallucination mitigation requires strengthening the model's ability to precisely interpret visual inputs. By introducing and leveraging synthetically generated visual variations, the authors aim to improve visual-semantic alignment at a foundational level, thereby reducing reliance on statistical priors and enhancing the model's capacity for accurate visual reasoning. This work builds upon recent advancements in controllable Text-to-Image (T2I) generation models (e.g., ControlNet), demonstrating how these generative capabilities can be repurposed to create targeted training data for robust LVLM fine-tuning, thereby pushing the boundaries of hallucination research beyond mere textual manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind "See Different, Think Better" is to overcome the limitations of existing text-centric hallucination mitigation strategies in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). The authors observe that while LVLMs excel in many multimodal tasks, their susceptibility to hallucination—generating textual content inconsistent with visual input—remains a significant impediment to their reliability and practical application. This issue is particularly acute when models encounter fine-grained visual details, where weak visual-semantic alignment leads to incorrect identifications, attribute misattributions, or misrepresentations of spatial relationships.

The core problem, as identified by the researchers, is that existing methods often treat hallucination as a language generation problem rather than a visual understanding deficit. They fail to sufficiently enhance the model's ability to discriminate between subtle visual features, causing LVLMs to rely on misleading statistical correlations or linguistic priors. The absence of naturally occurring datasets with sufficient visual variations to train models on these fine distinctions further exacerbates this problem.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of this research are multi-fold:

1. **Develop a Vision-Centric Hallucination Mitigation Framework (ViHallu):** The overarching goal is to shift the paradigm from text-centric to vision-centric approaches by directly improving visual-semantic alignment.
2. **Generate High-Quality Visual Variation Images:** To address the scarcity of suitable training data, ViHallu aims to create novel visual variation images. These images should feature controllable visual alterations (e.g., changes in scene elements, object categories, or attributes) while meticulously preserving the overall structure and context of the original image. This generation process includes introducing "counterfactual interventions" by placing objects in uncommon contexts to force models to rely on visual evidence rather than spurious correlations.
3. **Construct Tailored Visual Instructions:** The framework seeks to design and construct specific instruction data that leverages these generated visual variations. These instructions, combined with paired original and variation images, are intended to enable LVLMs to learn fine-grained discriminative features more effectively during fine-tuning.
4. **Release a Dedicated Dataset:** A significant objective is to contribute a valuable resource to the research community by releasing ViHallu-Instruction, a visual instruction dataset specifically designed for hallucination mitigation and visual-semantic alignment.
5. **Empirically Validate Effectiveness:** The final objective is to demonstrate, through extensive experimentation on multiple benchmarks and across various LVLM architectures, that ViHallu can significantly mitigate hallucination tendencies and enhance the models' fine-grained visual understanding and overall robustness.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to lay a foundation for more robust and reliable LVLMs that can truly "see different" and "think better," making them more trustworthy for real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **ViHallu (Vision-Centric Hallucination mitigation framework)**, consists of two main phases: Visual Variation Image Generation and Visual Instruction Construction. The core idea is to create and leverage synthetically generated visual variations and corresponding instructions to enhance LVLMs' fine-grained visual-semantic alignment.

#### 3.1. Visual Variation Image Generation

This phase focuses on creating high-quality, controlled visual variations of original images.

1. **Original Image Caption and Segmentation Mask Generation:**
 - **Image Captioning:** The `Tag2Text` model is used to extract image tags and generate comprehensive captions for original images.
 - **Segmentation Mask Extraction:** `MobileSAM` is employed to obtain precise segmentation masks for objects within the original images. These masks are crucial for controlling local alterations during the generation process.

2. **Caption Editing Through Concept Substitution:**
 - An **LLM-based mechanism**, specifically using `DeepSeek-chat V2`, is designed to edit original image captions.
 - The mechanism generates "variant captions" by replacing specific target object categories or attributes while keeping other contextual information constant.
 - A key aspect is the introduction of **counterfactual interventions**: objects are placed in uncommon or unexpected contextual settings (e.g., "zebras grazing in a sandy desert"). This forces the model to learn from visual features rather than relying on spurious correlations or statistical priors of co-occurring objects.

3. **Visual Variation Image Generation and Quality Evaluation:**
 - **Image Generation:** `ControlNet++` is utilized as the controllable Text-to-Image (T2I) generation model. It takes both the original segmentation masks (for structural consistency and object positioning) and the edited variant captions (for content guidance) as dual inputs. This ensures that the generated visual variation image maintains the global structure of the original but features controlled local alterations seamlessly integrated.
 - **Quality Assessment:** To ensure the generated images are high-quality and accurately reflect the variant captions, the `VQAScore` metric is used. This metric, based on a Visual Question Answering (VQA) model (`LLaVA-1.5-13B` in implementation), evaluates image-text alignment. A quality threshold of 0.6 is applied, filtering out low-scoring images to maintain high generation standards. The final image set comprises these high-quality visual variation images alongside their original counterparts.

#### 3.2. Visual Instruction Generation

This phase focuses on constructing high-quality, fine-grained visual instruction data based on the original and generated images.

1. **Detailed Description and Object Tag Generation:**
 - **Detailed Descriptions:** An LVLM (the baseline model undergoing fine-tuning) is used to generate detailed image descriptions. The authors acknowledge that these descriptions might contain hallucinations, which paradoxically helps in identifying areas prone to hallucination.
 - **Object Tags:** To ensure comprehensive coverage of all objects, `Grounded-SAM` is used for object detection and segmentation, extracting explicit object tags. This prevents descriptions from solely focusing on primary objects in complex scenes.

2. **Question-Answer Generation:**
 - **Question Generation:** `DeepSeek-chat V2` processes the detailed descriptions (including potential hallucinations) and object tags to generate a diverse set of questions. The prompt template is designed to elicit questions that target areas prone to hallucination (category, attribute, relation) and cover all identified objects. Questions about hallucinated elements (e.g., "Are there trees in the field?" if the description falsely mentioned trees) are specifically encouraged. Negative questions are also incorporated.
 - **Answer Generation:** `InternVL2.5` is employed to generate accurate answers to these questions. Crucially, InternVL2.5 generates responses based on the *actual visual content* of the image, rather than the potentially hallucinated descriptions, thereby providing ground truth answers.
 - **Inter-image Questioning:** A subset of questions generated from original images is also applied to their corresponding visual variation images. This strategy highlights the fine-grained visual differences, as the answers to identical questions will naturally differ between the original and varied image, aiding the model in distinguishing these subtle cues during fine-tuning.

3. **Quality Assessment of QA Pairs:**
 - To ensure the reliability of the generated Question-Answer (QA) pairs, a multi-model consensus approach is adopted using a "panel of LVLM experts" (`LLaVA-1.5`, `MiniCPM-V 2.6`, and `mPLUG-OWL3`).
 - Each QA pair and its corresponding image are fed to these expert models, which provide a binary "Yes"/"No" assessment of whether the answer accurately aligns with the image content.
 - A majority voting mechanism is applied: only QA pairs endorsed by at least two out of the three expert models are retained, while those rejected by two or more are discarded. This mitigates individual model biases and ensures high-quality instruction data.

The final **ViHallu-Instruction dataset** comprises 6,770 images (1,719 original and 5,051 visual variations) paired with approximately 50,000 tailored instructions, serving as the training data for fine-tuning LVLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of ViHallu was evaluated through extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks and with three representative open-source LVLMs: LLaVA-1.5 (7B), MiniGPT4 v2, and Qwen2-VL (7B).

#### 4.1. Evaluation on Benchmarks

**a) POPE (Polling-based Object Probing Evaluation) Dataset:**
POPE evaluates object hallucination across random, popular, and adversarial negative sampling strategies.
- **MiniGPT-4 v2:** Showed the most significant improvements, with accuracy gains of 2.93% (random), 5.23% (popular), and 2.70% (adversarial). F1 scores also improved substantially across all settings. This confirms ViHallu's ability to reduce spurious correlations by forcing the model to rely on visual evidence.
- **LLaVA-1.5:** Also demonstrated performance enhancements, with accuracy improvements of 2.23% (random), 1.17% (popular), and 0.54% (adversarial). This is notable as LLaVA-1.5 already had robust visual instruction fine-tuning.
- **Qwen2-VL:** Starting with the strongest baseline performance, it saw further modest improvements in random (0.40% accuracy, 0.52% F1) and popular (0.07% accuracy, 0.22% F1) settings. A slight decrease in the adversarial setting (-0.34% accuracy, -0.13% F1) suggests its inherent robustness to counterfactuals, making additional training in this specific area less impactful or prone to minor biases. Despite this, Qwen2-VL fine-tuned with ViHallu still outperformed all other models.
- **Comparison with Other Methods:** ViHallu outperformed other state-of-the-art text-centric hallucination mitigation methods (HA-DPO, HACL, w/VH, w/RAR) when applied to LLaVA-1.5-7B, achieving the highest average accuracy and F1 score on POPE.

**b) LLaVA-Bench (In-the-Wild) Dataset:**
This benchmark assesses comprehensive LVLM capabilities (conversation, detailed description, complex reasoning) beyond hallucination.
- All models fine-tuned with ViHallu showed improved overall performance.
- **MiniGPT-4 V2:** Achieved gains of 10.4 (conversation), 5.4 (detailed description), and 9.2 (complex reasoning) points.
- **LLaVA-1.5:** Saw enhancements of 4.0, 7.4, and 7.5 points respectively.
- **Qwen2-VL:** Demonstrated the most substantial improvements, with gains of 19.0, 7.0, and 15.7 points, leading to an impressive 14.4-point increase in overall performance.
These results indicate that ViHallu not only mitigates hallucinations but also broadly enhances models' conversational abilities and fine-grained understanding, strengthening overall visual-semantic alignment.

**c) MMHal-Bench Dataset:**
Designed specifically for evaluating hallucination across eight categories (e.g., object attributes, counting, spatial relations).
- **LLaVA-1.5:** Overall score increased from 1.93 to 2.11 (+9.3%), and hallucination rate decreased from 0.70 to 0.66 (-5.7%).
- **MiniGPT4 V2:** Overall score improved from 1.54 to 1.91, with hallucination rate dropping from 0.76 to 0.70.
- **Qwen2-VL:** Maintained its leading performance, increasing its score from 2.94 to 3.64 and further reducing its already low hallucination rate from 0.51 to 0.48 (-5.9%).
Radar charts revealed expanded polygons for ViHallu-enhanced models across most hallucination categories, particularly in challenging areas like Adversarial and Environment, confirming its broad effectiveness.

#### 4.2. Further Analysis

**a) Case Study (Qualitative Analysis):**
Qualitative examples demonstrated that ViHallu-tuned LLaVA-1.5 generated more concise, hallucination-free, and factually accurate responses compared to the baseline. For instance, the baseline might hallucinate non-existent objects or make spatial errors, while the ViHallu-tuned version accurately identified the absence of objects and focused on verifiable visual details, indicating a reduced reliance on language priors.

**b) Impact of Dataset Size:**
Experiments with varying sizes of the ViHallu-Instruction dataset (50 to 2,000 images) showed a consistent upward trend in performance (F1 score on POPE), gradually plateauing as the dataset size increased. This confirms that ViHallu effectively mitigates hallucination and its benefits scale with more training data.

**c) Consistency Analysis with Different Subsets:**
Evaluating LLaVA-1.5 fine-tuned on four independent subsets of ViHallu-Instruction revealed consistent performance improvements over the baseline, with minimal variance (within 0.7 F1 score) between subsets. This demonstrates the robustness and consistent quality of the ViHallu-Instruction dataset.

In summary, the results conclusively show that ViHallu significantly improves LVLMs' fine-grained visual understanding and substantially reduces hallucination across diverse benchmarks and model architectures, often outperforming existing text-centric mitigation methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The paper "See Different, Think Better: Visual Variations Mitigating Hallucinations in LVLMs" introduces ViHallu, a pioneering vision-centric framework that marks a significant step forward in addressing the persistent problem of hallucination in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). Its contributions and potential impact are substantial, both for academic research and practical applications.

**1. Novel Vision-Centric Paradigm:** The most significant contribution of ViHallu is its shift from predominantly text-centric hallucination mitigation methods to a novel vision-centric approach. By directly manipulating and generating visual inputs with fine-grained variations, the framework targets the root cause of hallucination: weak visual-semantic alignment. This paradigm shift offers a more fundamental solution to enhance LVLMs' understanding of visual content, moving beyond mere linguistic adjustments.

**2. Innovative Visual Variation Generation:** The methodology for creating visual variation images is highly innovative. By integrating text guidance with segmentation mask control (using ControlNet++) and incorporating counterfactual interventions (placing objects in uncommon contexts), ViHallu effectively generates high-quality, controlled visual data. This addresses the critical challenge of lacking natural datasets with sufficient fine-grained visual differences necessary for robust hallucination training. The ability to precisely control alterations while maintaining structural consistency is a key technical achievement.

**3. Tailored Visual Instruction Construction:** The development of tailored visual instructions for these original and varied image pairs is another unique aspect. The use of LLMs to generate questions that probe potential hallucinations (even those present in initial model descriptions) and leveraging a robust VQA model for ground-truth answers ensures the training data is highly relevant and accurate. The multi-model consensus approach for quality assessment further bolsters the reliability of the ViHallu-Instruction dataset.

**4. Enhanced LVLM Robustness and Reliability:** The comprehensive experimental results demonstrate ViHallu's effectiveness across various LVLMs (LLaVA-1.5, MiniGPT4 v2, Qwen2-VL) and benchmarks (POPE, LLaVA-Bench, MMHal-Bench). The consistent reduction in hallucination rates and significant improvements in fine-grained visual understanding, conversational capabilities, and complex reasoning underscore its ability to make LVLMs more robust and reliable. The particular improvement in adversarial settings, where models typically struggle with spurious correlations, validates ViHallu's core hypothesis.

**5. Release of a Valuable Dataset (ViHallu-Instruction):** The open release of the ViHallu-Instruction dataset is a major contribution to the research community. This carefully curated dataset, comprising thousands of image pairs with high-quality instructions, provides a much-needed resource for future research in visual-semantic alignment, hallucination mitigation, and general LVLM fine-tuning. It lowers the barrier for other researchers to explore and build upon this vision-centric approach.

**6. Potential for Broader Impact:** By mitigating hallucination, ViHallu enhances the trustworthiness of LVLMs, opening doors for their deployment in critical real-world applications where factual accuracy is paramount. This includes domains such as:
 - **Medical Imaging:** Accurate diagnosis requires precise visual understanding without hallucinated findings.
 - **Autonomous Driving:** Misidentifying objects or misinterpreting scenes can have severe safety implications.
 - **Scientific Research:** Reliable interpretation of complex visual data.
 - **Accessibility:** Generating accurate descriptions for visually impaired users.

In conclusion, ViHallu represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal AI. By pioneering a vision-centric approach to hallucination mitigation, it provides a powerful framework and a valuable dataset that will likely inspire and facilitate future research into more robust, reliable, and factually grounded Large Vision-Language Models.
