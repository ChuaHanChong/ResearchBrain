# B-spline Policy: Accelerating Manipulation Policies via B-spline Action Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> B-spline Policy (BSP) introduces a continuous action representation using B-spline curves, enabling robots to execute manipulation tasks significantly faster and with smoother motions. This method reduced task completion times by up to 50% in real-world scenarios while preserving or enhancing task success rates.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation policies are significantly slower than human performance, highlighting a bottleneck in execution efficiency.
- Traditional 'action chunking' methods suffer from uniform temporal resolution and discontinuities when stitching action chunks, leading to inefficient control and tracking errors.
- These discontinuities become problematic at higher execution speeds, causing instability and potential task failures.

## Method

- The **B-spline Policy (BSP)** represents actions as ==continuous B-spline curves==, directly predicting their ==control points== and ==knot vectors==.
- An ==adaptive B-spline fitting== procedure converts discrete demonstration trajectories into compact and smooth B-spline representations, optimizing ==knot placement==.
- An ==inference-time segment alignment== mechanism ensures smooth transitions between consecutively predicted B-spline segments, crucial for stable high-speed execution, replacing conventional ==action chunking==.

## Results

- BSP consistently reduced average task completion times by up to **50%** (e.g., from **23.57s** to **11.80s** for Table Cleaning at **4X** speedup) across various real-world manipulation tasks.
- The approach maintained or improved task success rates in **14 out of 18** comparison settings, demonstrating enhanced robustness and producing noticeably smoother robot executions.
- Inference-time segment alignment proved critical for maintaining trajectory continuity and high success rates at accelerated speeds, though aggressive speedups could still exceed low-level controller capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continuous B-spline action representations intrinsically provide smoothness and temporal flexibility, enabling adaptive execution speeds and high-frequency robot control.
> - Adaptive fitting of B-splines to demonstrations allows for compact and accurate trajectory encoding, focusing representational capacity on critical motion segments.
> - The combination of continuous action curves, temporal rescaling, and inference-time segment alignment is essential for achieving robust and accelerated robotic manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visuomotor policy learning has advanced in recent years, enabling robots to perform complex manipulation tasks by learning directly from sensory observations. However, a significant challenge in this domain is the comparatively slow execution speed of robotic tasks compared to human performance. State-of-the-art robotic systems often require considerably more time than humans to complete everyday manipulation tasks, indicating a bottleneck in efficiency rather than mere success rate.

Many contemporary visuomotor policies utilize "action chunking," where actions are parameterized as fixed-length sequences sampled uniformly over time. While this approach has been shown to stabilize long-horizon prediction and improve learning performance, it introduces two limitations: uniform temporal resolution and chunk discontinuities. Uniform temporal resolution fails to account for the varying control granularity required throughout a task, such as fast reaching movements versus precise contact-rich phases. Chunk discontinuities arise when independently predicted chunks are stitched together, leading to potential tracking failures or task failures, particularly at higher execution speeds.

This research draws inspiration from classical motion planning and computer graphics, where continuous spline-based representations (such as B-splines and Bézier curves) are commonly employed due to their intrinsic smoothness, compactness, and mathematical tractability. Prior work in visuomotor policy acceleration, such as DemoSpeedup, has explored identifying parts of demonstrations that can be downsampled, or full-stack engineering approaches like SAIL that consider policy consistency, robot dynamics, and latency. This paper distinguishes itself by identifying action chunking as a fundamental algorithmic limitation and proposing a continuous action representation based on B-splines to address it directly. This approach is distinct from B-spline-encoded action tokenizers like BEAST or dynamical movement primitives (DMPs), as it uses B-splines as a control-oriented continuous action representation within an end-to-end visuomotor policy pipeline.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to accelerate robot manipulation policies by addressing the inefficiencies inherent in conventional discrete-time action representations. The authors aim to enable robots to execute complex manipulation tasks more quickly while maintaining or improving task success rates.

The motivation for this research stems from several identified limitations of current visuomotor policy learning paradigms:

1. **Slow Task Completion Times:** Despite advances in success rates, modern robotic systems are significantly slower than humans at everyday manipulation tasks. For example, a task a human completes in approximately 10 seconds might take a robot close to a minute. This performance gap highlights a need to optimize for execution speed in addition to task success.
2. **Limitations of Discrete Action Chunking:**
 - **Uniform Temporal Resolution:** Existing methods typically parameterize actions as fixed-length chunks sampled at a uniform rate. This implies that all phases of a manipulation task require the same level of control granularity. In reality, tasks involve phases requiring rapid, coarse motions (e.g., reaching) and phases demanding fine-grained precision (e.g., grasping, alignment, insertion). Uniform chunking leads to inefficient allocation of representational capacity, potentially slowing down fast phases or making precise phases difficult.
 - **Chunk Discontinuities:** The process of stitching together independently predicted discrete action chunks at inference time can introduce discontinuities at chunk boundaries. These abrupt changes in commanded actions become problematic during high-speed execution, where they can lead to controller tracking errors, overshoots, or complete task failure.
3. **Need for Continuous Trajectories:** The aforementioned issues motivate a shift from discrete waypoint chunks to continuous action curves. Continuous representations, particularly splines, are inherently smooth, compact, and mathematically amenable, properties that are beneficial for generating fluid and adaptable robot movements. B-splines, in particular, offer advantages due to their constructive smoothness, compact parameterization, and ability to approximate continuous functions.

By introducing a B-spline-based action representation, the authors seek to overcome these limitations by providing a mechanism for adaptive temporal resolution, intrinsic smoothness, and temporal rescaling, thereby enabling faster and more stable policy execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is the B-spline Policy (BSP), which redefines the action space from discrete-time action chunks to continuous B-spline curves. This involves several methodological components:

#### 3.1. B-spline Action Representation

Instead of predicting a sequence of discrete waypoints, BSP directly predicts the parameters of a continuous B-spline trajectory. A B-spline trajectory `a(u)` is defined by a knot vector `U` and a set of control points `C = {c0, ..., cN}`. The continuous action at a normalized time `u` is given by the weighted sum of control points, where the weights are B-spline basis functions `Ni,p(u)` of degree `p`. The experiments utilize cubic B-splines (p=3) to ensure smooth trajectories with continuous velocity and acceleration profiles.

The use of B-splines provides several properties relevant for fast robot manipulation:
- **Temporal Flexibility:** A continuous trajectory allows sampling at arbitrary control frequencies, enabling the low-level controller to receive dense commands even when the policy operates at a lower inference rate.
- **Temporal Rescaling:** The execution speed can be adjusted by scaling the mapping between real time and the B-spline parameter. For a speedup factor `n`, the robot executes `a_exec(t) = a(nt)`, traversing the same geometric path faster.
- **Intrinsic Smoothness:** B-splines are smooth by construction, avoiding the sharp changes inherent in discrete action chunks that can cause tracking errors at high speeds.
- **Local Error Isolation:** Each control point influences only a limited segment of the trajectory, which contributes to stability and compactness, preventing global distortions from local errors.

#### 3.2. B-spline Policy Implementation

- **Adaptive B-spline Fitting from Demonstrations:** To train BSP, discrete demonstration trajectories are first converted into continuous B-spline representations. An adaptive fitting procedure, based on the FITPACK strategy (Algorithm 1), is employed. This method iteratively inserts knots into regions of the trajectory with the largest reconstruction error, continuing until the error is below a specified tolerance `ε` or a maximum number of knots is reached. This adaptive knot placement prioritizes representational capacity in high-curvature segments while using fewer knots in smooth regions, resulting in a compact and accurate representation.
- **Fixing the Policy Output Size:** The policy is designed to predict a fixed-size vector of local B-spline parameters `[U; C]`, rather than an entire trajectory, at each step. This fixed-size output, which includes control points and knot values, allows seamless integration with standard imitation learning architectures. Boundary support knots and control points are handled by appending future values or repeating final points as needed.
- **High-frequency Execution and Temporal Rescaling:** During inference, the visuomotor policy operates at a relatively low frequency, predicting future B-spline segments. The low-level robot controller then samples this continuous segment at a much higher frequency (e.g., 100 Hz), generating dense robot commands. This decoupling is crucial for fast manipulation, as it ensures the controller receives smooth, high-frequency commands even when the policy itself is not inferring as rapidly. The temporal rescaling factor `n` allows direct adjustment of execution speed.
- **Pipelined Execution with Segment Alignment:** In long-horizon tasks, the policy continuously predicts new B-spline segments. A naive approach of switching to the new segment directly can lead to discontinuities due to policy noise, latency, or tracking errors. To mitigate this, an inference-time segment alignment mechanism is introduced (Algorithm 2). When a new segment `S_new(t)` is predicted, the system searches for a point `t*` on `S_new` that best matches the `a_last` (most recently executed action). This alignment, performed by minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE), ensures smooth transitions between consecutive B-spline segments, preventing the accumulation of discontinuities crucial for stable high-speed execution.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Policy Backbones:** BSP was integrated with two existing imitation learning backbones: Diffusion Policy (referred to as Diff.+BSP) and Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) (referred to as Reg.+BSP).
- **Metrics:** Evaluation focused on task success rate and average completion time (averaged over successful rollouts).
- **Real-world Tasks:** Three diverse tasks were used:
 - **Cube Picking:** Requires precise object manipulation (3 cm cube into a box).
 - **Table Cleaning:** A long-horizon task involving sequential picking and placing of cans and bowls.
 - **Speed Stacking:** A bimanual, contact-rich task involving building and collapsing a cup pyramid.
 Experiments used 6-DoF ARX5 robot arms with single or multiple cameras.
- **Baselines:** Comparisons included:
 - Standard Diffusion Policy and Regression Policy (ACT) at original demonstration/control frequency (1X).
 - Naively accelerated versions (nX) where execution frequency is increased directly.
 - DemoSpeedup, which accelerates by temporally downsampling demonstration trajectories.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** Each real-world method was evaluated on 20 rollouts with distinct, fixed object layouts. Grasping actions in some tasks allowed up to three recovery attempts. Completion time was measured from the start at the home pose to the final gripper opening.
- **Simulation Benchmarks:** BSP was also evaluated on Push-T, RoboMimic, and RoboCasa environments to assess general performance and acceleration capabilities in a reproducible setting. Push-T was specifically used for acceleration studies due to its modifiability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations were conducted across both simulated and real-world manipulation tasks, demonstrating consistent performance improvements.

#### 4.1. Real-world Experiments

- **Finding 1: BSP Consistently Shortens Task Completion Time.**
 Table 1 indicates that integrating BSP consistently reduced the average task completion time across all configurations and tasks when paired with either Diffusion Policy or Regression Policy. For example, in the long-horizon Table Cleaning task, Reg.+BSP at 4X speedup reduced the average completion time from 23.57s (baseline Regression Policy) to 11.80s, representing a 50% reduction in duration.
- **Finding 2: BSP Preserves Task Success, but Aggressive Speedup Can Exceed Controller Limits.**
 BSP generally maintained or improved task success rates. Across 18 comparison settings, BSP matched or improved success rates in 14 cases. For instance, with the Regression backbone, BSP improved Table Cleaning success from 13/20 to 18/20 at 1x and 2x speedup, and Speed Stacking from 8/20 to 16/20 at 1x and 4/20 to 13/20 at 2x speedup. However, the results also suggest a trade-off between execution speed and task robustness. In Speed Stacking, Regression+BSP achieved 0% success at 4x speedup, indicating that the rapid temporal scaling pushed the robot's low-level controller beyond its tracking capabilities.
- **Finding 3: BSP Produces Smoother Trajectories.**
 Qualitative observations and video analysis (Figure 5) showed that B-spline policy produced notably smoother robot executions compared to baseline methods. The discrete action chunks often resulted in jerky movements due to discontinuities, whereas BSP's continuous representation facilitated fluid and faster motions.
- **Finding 4: Inference-time Segment Alignment Is Critical for BSP.**
 An ablation study on the Speed Stacking task (Figure 6) demonstrated the importance of the inference-time segment alignment mechanism. Aligning adjacent B-spline segments improved trajectory continuity, which was particularly crucial at higher execution speeds. Without alignment, success rates became highly sensitive to execution speed, indicating that discontinuities effectively created out-of-distribution inputs, forcing the policy to re-plan or leading to task failure.

#### 4.2. Simulation Benchmarks

- **General Performance (Table 2a):**
 B-spline policy largely matched or outperformed corresponding base policies across Push-T, RoboMimic, and RoboCasa tasks, with one exception (RoboMimic Lift task for Reg.+BSP). On the more complex RoboCasa tasks, BSP consistently achieved higher success rates, suggesting improved robustness in long-horizon manipulation scenarios. These results indicate that B-spline action representations do not compromise standard imitation learning performance and can offer gains over discrete action chunking.
- **Acceleration in Simulation (Table 2b - Push-T Speedup):**
 For the Push-T task, BSP consistently improved both the average score and task completion time across all target speedups. At 4x speedup, Diff.+BSP achieved a higher average score (0.73 vs. 0.59 for Diff. 4x) and reduced completion time by more than half (from 7.19s to 2.87s). This was attributed to the faster execution allowing the agent to perform more corrective actions within the given rollout time, leading to higher final coverage scores.

#### 4.3. Ablation on Fitting Error Tolerance (Table 3)

The study on fitting error tolerance `ε` revealed that increasing `ε` led to a higher compression ratio (fewer knot-control-point pairs relative to original actions) while maintaining a relatively stable success rate. This suggests that performance is not overly sensitive to `ε` within a reasonable range, allowing for potential computational cost reductions with a moderately larger tolerance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing a key limitation: the slow execution speed of visuomotor policies. By introducing the B-spline Policy (BSP), the authors offer a novel action representation that moves beyond conventional discrete action chunking.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Accelerated Robotic Manipulation:** The primary impact is enabling robots to perform manipulation tasks significantly faster. This capability is crucial for deploying robots in real-world scenarios where efficiency is paramount, such as manufacturing, logistics, or household assistance, where task completion times need to align more closely with human performance.
- **Enhanced Trajectory Smoothness and Robustness:** B-spline trajectories are inherently smooth, which reduces jerky movements and the potential for controller tracking errors that can lead to task failure, especially at high speeds. The proposed inference-time segment alignment mechanism further enhances robustness by mitigating discontinuities between successive predicted segments, leading to more stable high-speed control.
- **Adaptive Temporal Resolution:** By predicting continuous curves, BSP allows for flexible temporal scaling and sampling, providing the low-level controller with dense, high-frequency commands. This adapts to varying precision requirements throughout a task, unlike uniform discrete chunking.
- **Compatibility with Existing Methods:** The "plug-and-play" nature of BSP, allowing integration with various imitation learning backbones (like Diffusion Policy and ACT), suggests its broad applicability and ease of adoption within the current research landscape.
- **Improved Efficiency in Learning and Execution:** The adaptive B-spline fitting procedure provides a compact representation of demonstration trajectories, potentially leading to more efficient learning. The ability to accelerate execution without retraining for each target speed further enhances efficiency.
- **Advancing Robot Autonomy:** By making robots faster and more reliable, this work contributes to the development of more capable and autonomous robotic systems that can perform complex tasks in dynamic environments with greater agility and less human intervention.

A noted limitation involves the physical constraints of low-cost robotic arms, where aggressive acceleration could exceed the tracking limits of their low-level controllers. Future work could focus on integrating BSP with more advanced or purpose-built low-level controllers to further enhance tracking robustness at extreme speeds. Overall, BSP represents a step toward overcoming a fundamental challenge in visuomotor policy learning, making robotic manipulation more efficient and practical for a wider range of applications.
