# Sample from What You See: Visuomotor Policy Learning via Diffusion Bridge with Observation-Embedded Stochastic Differential Equation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ShanghaiTech University introduced BridgePolicy, a generative visuomotor policy that embeds observations directly into the stochastic dynamics of a diffusion bridge to enhance action precision. This method achieved the highest average success rates across 52 simulation tasks and five real-world robotic manipulation tasks, demonstrating improved control reliability.

## Problem

- Existing generative policies for visuomotor control underutilize observations, treating them as high-level conditioning signals external to the diffusion process.
- The reverse sampling process in conventional diffusion models often starts from uninformative random noise, weakening the coupling between robot perception and generated actions.
- Applying diffusion bridge formulations to robotics is challenging due to the heterogeneous modalities (e.g., 3D point clouds, robot states) and inherent mismatch in dimensionality and distribution between observations and actions.

## Method

- BridgePolicy redefines visuomotor policy learning as a diffusion bridge problem, where the forward process transforms the robot action into its corresponding observation.
- A data prediction neural network is trained with a reconstruction loss that explicitly integrates the observation as the terminal state, enabling precise action generation from an observation-informed prior.
- A multi-modality fusion pipeline, employing Cross-Attention, unifies heterogeneous observations, and a contrastive (CLIP) loss semantically aligns the fused observation representation with the action space.

## Results

- BridgePolicy achieved the highest average success rates across 52 simulation tasks in the Adroit, DexArt, and MetaWorld benchmarks, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art generative policies.
- In real-world experiments with a Franka Emika Panda robot, the method demonstrated superior performance on five manipulation tasks, achieving an average success rate of **0.90**, outperforming the next best baseline (DP3) at **0.76**.
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of the diffusion bridge formulation, the Cross-Attention mechanism for modality fusion, and robust performance even with fewer expert demonstrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly embedding observations into the stochastic dynamics of a diffusion bridge fundamentally strengthens the coupling between perception and action, leading to more precise control compared to external conditioning.
> - A dedicated multi-modality fusion and semantic alignment mechanism is crucial for successfully applying diffusion bridge formulations to heterogeneous robotic data, bridging the gap between diverse input types and action space.
> - Reframing policy learning as a diffusion bridge provides a better-conditioned generative process, allowing action sampling to initiate from a meaningful, observation-informed prior rather than random noise.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work is situated within the field of robotic learning, specifically focusing on imitation learning for visuomotor control. Imitation learning is a paradigm where robots learn tasks by mimicking expert demonstrations. Recent advancements in generative models, such as diffusion models and flow matching, have been applied to robotic control due to their capability to capture multi-modal action distributions and model temporal dependencies.

Existing generative policies, including Diffusion Policy (DP) and 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3), typically frame policy learning as a conditional generative process. These methods perturb action sequences into random noise through a forward Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) or Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) and then train a neural network to reverse this process, converting noise into actions, conditioned on observations. A common characteristic of these approaches is that observations are treated as high-level conditioning signals external to the diffusion process itself. This typically forces the sampling process to begin from uninformative random noise (e.g., Gaussian noise), potentially weakening the coupling between perception and control and leading to suboptimal performance.

Diffusion bridge formulations have gained traction in other domains, such as image restoration and translation, by modifying the forward diffusion process to align its endpoint distribution with a desired conditioned distribution. This allows sampling to commence from a more informative prior rather than random noise. This paper builds upon these concepts, proposing to adapt diffusion bridge theory to visuomotor policy learning, thereby directly integrating observations into the stochastic dynamics of the action generation process. This contrasts with concurrent works like BRIDGER and VITA, which also aim to reduce reliance on random noise but often employ multi-stage or latent-space approaches, which might introduce dependencies on coarse policies or generalization errors from decoders.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a generative visuomotor policy that more effectively integrates sensory observations into the action generation process, leading to more precise and reliable robot control.

The motivation for this objective stems from identified limitations in current generative policies:
- **Underutilization of Observations:** Existing methods primarily use observations as high-level conditioning signals for the denoising network. This approach does not fully embed the rich information contained within observations into the fundamental stochastic dynamics of the action generation process.
- **Uninformative Starting Point for Sampling:** Because observations are treated as external conditions, the reverse sampling process in conventional diffusion models is typically forced to start from uninformative random noise (e.g., Gaussian noise). This "cold start" weakens the inherent coupling between the robot's perception of its environment and the actions it needs to generate. This can lead to a less precise and less reliable control policy.
- **Inspiration from Diffusion Bridge:** The concept of diffusion bridge, which connects two arbitrary distributions by modifying the forward process, offers a mathematically grounded approach to initiate the reverse process from a more informative prior. Applying this to visuomotor control is hypothesized to enhance action precision by allowing sampling to start directly from meaningful observation representations.

However, formulating policy learning as a diffusion bridge presents specific challenges:
- **Heterogeneous Data Modalities:** Robotic observations (e.g., 3D point clouds, proprioceptive states) and actions are fundamentally heterogeneous in nature. Standard diffusion bridge formulations typically assume that the connected endpoint distributions share the same dimensionality and are naturally aligned. This assumption does not hold for diverse robotic data.
- **Dimensionality and Distribution Mismatch:** Observations often comprise various data types (proprioceptive states, visual inputs, possibly language instructions) that do not readily map to the action space in a simple distributional manner, making classical bridge formulations difficult to apply directly.

To address these motivations and challenges, the paper proposes "BridgePolicy," aiming to explicitly embed observations into the diffusion SDE trajectory and develop a mechanism to align heterogeneous robotic data for effective diffusion bridge application.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, BridgePolicy, reframes visuomotor policy learning as the problem of learning a diffusion bridge. This involves explicitly integrating observations into the stochastic dynamics of the diffusion process.

#### 3.1. BridgePolicy Formulation

BridgePolicy adopts a unified diffusion bridge framework based on Stochastic Optimal Control (SOC) theory, specifically leveraging insights from UniDB (Zhu et al., 2025).

- **Forward Process Definition:** Unlike standard diffusion models where the terminal state `a_T` is typically Gaussian noise, BridgePolicy defines the endpoints of its forward diffusion bridge process as:
 - Initial state `a_0`: The actual robot action `a`.
 - Terminal state `a_T`: The robot observation `o`.
 This formulation constructs a process that transforms the action `a` into the observation `o`. The forward SDE is given by Equation (2), which includes terms related to `a_T - a_t`, signifying the influence of the target observation on the action's evolution.

- **Training Objective:** To learn the reverse process of this bridge, a data prediction neural network, `a_θ(a_t, a_T, t)`, is trained. This network aims to directly map any intermediate action state `a_t` back to the clean action `a_0`. The training objective, `L_DB` (Equation 3), is a reconstruction loss based on the L1 norm, minimizing the difference between the network's prediction and the actual action `a`. This objective explicitly incorporates the observation `a_T = o` into the learning process.

- **Sampling Algorithm:** After training, the policy inference in BridgePolicy is implemented as an iterative generation procedure. It starts from the given observation `a_T = o` and iteratively transforms it into the final action `a_0`. The updating rule (Equation 4) is derived from the UniDB++ solver, which is a training-free acceleration algorithm. This rule calculates `a_t` from `a_s`, `a_T`, and `a_θ(a_s, a_T, s)`, incorporating Gaussian noise `ϵ`. Although complex in appearance, the coefficients are in closed form and can be computed efficiently.

#### 3.2. Modality Fusion and Alignment

A critical challenge in applying diffusion bridge to robotic data is the heterogeneity of observations (e.g., 3D point clouds, robot states) and their mismatch in shape and distribution with the action space. BridgePolicy addresses this through a dedicated pipeline:

- **Encoding Robot State and Point Cloud:** Depth images are first converted to 3D point clouds, which are then downsampled. A lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) encodes the robot state (`o_s`) into `z_s` and another MLP encodes the point cloud (`o_pc`) into `z_pc` (Equation 5). These latent representations are designed to be compatible for fusion.

- **Multi-Modality Fusion:** A Cross-Attention mechanism (Equation 6) is employed to integrate the `z_pc` and `z_s` features into a unified observation representation, `z_obs`. This `z_obs` is then assigned as the terminal state `a_T` of the diffusion bridge. This step unifies the diverse inputs into a single feature vector, ensuring it has the same shape as the action chunk, making it compatible with the diffusion bridge formulation. The paper includes Theorem 3.1 and its empirical evaluation (Appendix B), which asserts that errors in this observation MLP would lead to linearly bounded errors in the generated action, indicating robustness.

- **Modality Alignment:** To address remaining distributional differences and enhance the sampling capability of the learned observation representation, a contrastive learning loss, specifically CLIP loss (Equation 8), is introduced. This `L_align` term semantically aligns the observation representation `z_obs` with the actual action `a`, encouraging semantic proximity between the two distributions.

- **Overall Training Objective:** The final training objective (Equation 9) combines the diffusion bridge loss `L_DB` with the alignment loss `L_align`, weighted by `α`. This combined loss enables the model to learn the diffusion bridge dynamics while ensuring semantic alignment between heterogeneous observations and actions.

Algorithms 1 and 2 detail the training and inference procedures, respectively. During training, observations are fused and reshaped, and then both `L_DB` and `L_align` are computed. During inference, the fused observation latent vector serves as the starting point for the iterative sampling process, transforming it into executable actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

BridgePolicy was evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world robotic tasks, demonstrating consistent performance advantages over state-of-the-art generative policies.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **Benchmarks:** Evaluations were conducted on 52 simulation tasks across three benchmarks: Adroit, DexArt (dexterous hand manipulation), and MetaWorld (robotic arm tasks with varying difficulty). Expert demonstrations were collected using script policies (MetaWorld) or RL algorithms (VRL3 for Adroit, PPO for DexArt).
- **Baselines:** BridgePolicy was compared against Diffusion Policy (DP), 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3), Simple DP3 (lightweight DP3), FlowPolicy, and VITA. The Number of Function Evaluations (NFE) was set to 10 for most baselines, except FlowPolicy which used 1 NFE to avoid error accumulation issues.
- **Performance:** As reported in Table 1, BridgePolicy achieved the highest average success rates across all evaluated benchmarks.
 - **MetaWorld:** BridgePolicy consistently outperformed baselines, especially in "Hard" and "Very Hard" difficulty levels, indicating its robustness in complex tasks. For instance, in MetaWorld, BridgePolicy achieved 0.91±0.03 (Easy), 0.75±0.03 (Medium), 0.58±0.02 (Hard), and 0.79±0.02 (Very Hard), which were higher than all other methods.
 - **Adroit and DexArt:** BridgePolicy also showed superior average success rates (0.60±0.05 and 0.81±0.04 respectively) compared to baselines.
 - VITA, while competitive, showed performance between DP3 and FlowPolicy, potentially due to generalization errors from its two-stage, joint latent image-action space approach.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- **Setup:** Five real-world tasks (Oven-Closing, Oven-Opening, Pick Place, Pour, Unplug) were performed using a Franka Emika Panda robot with a ZED-2i camera for point cloud acquisition. Expert demonstrations (50 episodes per task) were collected using the GELLO human teleoperation system.
- **Performance:** Table 2 presents the real-world success rates. BridgePolicy achieved the highest success rate on all tasks, with an average of 0.90. DP3 was second with 0.76, while FlowPolicy showed a significant performance drop (0.56 average), particularly on tasks requiring location generalization like "Pour" and "Pick-and-Place."
- **Qualitative Analysis:** Visualizations (Figure 3, 14, and 7 in Appendix F.1) for tasks like Oven-Opening and Unplug demonstrated BridgePolicy's ability to execute more precise and complete actions. For instance, in Oven-Opening, BridgePolicy accurately completed both stages (grabbing and opening halfway, then pushing down), whereas FlowPolicy failed to fully open the door, and DP3 failed to finish the task.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Sample Efficiency:** (Figure 4) BridgePolicy exhibited stable and robust performance across varying numbers of expert demonstrations, maintaining superiority even with fewer training samples compared to baselines.
- **Parameter Sensitivity (α):** (Table 4 and Appendix F.2) The weight `α` for the alignment loss `L_align` was explored. Results suggested that a relatively small `α` (e.g., 0.5-1.0) generally yielded better performance, indicating that excessive emphasis on alignment loss could be detrimental.
- **Modality Fusion Method:** (Table 3) Comparing Cross-Attention with simple concatenation for modality fusion, Cross-Attention consistently led to higher success rates across evaluated tasks, affirming its effectiveness in integrating 3D point cloud and robot state.
- **Effectiveness of Diffusion Bridge:** (Table 5) A direct comparison of the policy head formulations showed that Regression performed poorly (near-zero success), standard Diffusion significantly improved performance, and Diffusion Bridge achieved the highest success rates. This outcome supports the core hypothesis that the diffusion bridge formulation provides a better-conditioned generation process, yielding more precise actions.
- **Parameter Sensitivity (λ, γ) for UniDB Framework:** (Appendix F.3, Table 11) Experiments on Adroit tasks showed that `λ=50` and `γ=10^7` were relatively optimal hyperparameters for the underlying UniDB framework.
- **L1 vs. L2 Norm for `L_DB`:** (Appendix F.4, Table 12) Using L1 norm (MAE) for the diffusion bridge training objective `L_DB` generally resulted in slightly higher success rates compared to L2 norm (MSE).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces BridgePolicy, a novel approach to visuomotor policy learning that significantly advances the capabilities of generative models in robotics. The key significance lies in its direct integration of observations into the stochastic dynamics of the diffusion process through a diffusion-bridge formulation, a departure from conventional methods that treat observations as external conditions.

Potential impacts include:
- **Enhanced Precision and Reliability:** By enabling action sampling to start from an observation-informed prior, BridgePolicy demonstrates improved precision and reliability in robot control, as evidenced by its superior performance across a wide range of simulated and real-world manipulation tasks.
- **Effective Handling of Heterogeneous Data:** The semantic aligner and multi-modality fusion pipeline provide a robust mechanism for unifying and aligning heterogeneous robotic data (e.g., 3D point clouds, robot states) with the action space, extending the applicability of diffusion bridge models to complex, multi-modal inputs.
- **Generalizability:** The consistent outperformance over state-of-the-art generative policies, particularly in more challenging scenarios and with varying amounts of demonstration data, suggests improved generalizability of the learned policies.
- **Advancement of Diffusion Models in Robotics:** This work demonstrates a principled way to leverage the mathematically exact framework of diffusion bridges for robotic control, opening new avenues for research in generative policy learning beyond traditional conditioning approaches.

A stated limitation of BridgePolicy is that its current sampling process remains constrained by its SDE formulation. This constraint limits the direct application of distillation methods that could potentially enable faster, one-step action generation, which is an area for future research.
