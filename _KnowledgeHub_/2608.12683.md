# FUSE: Active Functional Affordance Grounding through Adaptive Semantic-Geometric Evidence Acquisition

> [!summary] Summary
> FUSE addresses active functional affordance grounding: an agent moves its camera to find and spatially localize an object satisfying a functional query. It fuses SAM3 pixel-level semantic evidence with 3D Gaussian Splatting SSNR geometric uncertainty, and gates by action entropy between a fast learned amortized planner and explicit uncertainty-driven exploration. On a new Habitat benchmark it reaches 72% success, 1.33x faster than fully explicit search.

## Problem

- Passive affordance grounding predicts from a single fixed viewpoint, so it commits to a target before occluded or incomplete functional cues have been resolved.
- Active object search and navigation acquire extra views but pursue a predefined category or instance, not an unnamed object specified only by its function.
- Task-oriented next-best-view and active grasping planners assume the target object or manipulation hypothesis is already selected, skipping the discovery stage entirely.

## Method

- A ==functional uncertainty field== decomposes into ==SAM3== pixel-level semantic evidence over query-induced candidate hypotheses plus ==SSNR geometric uncertainty== from an incrementally refined ==3D Gaussian Splatting== scene representation.
- ==Explicit exploration== scores each camera action by averaging the fused ==evidence-acquisition map== over the image half that action would reveal, executes the argmax, then registers and refines the splats.
- An ==amortized planner== (CLIP backbone plus three-layer MLP) predicts action values in one pass; ==entropy== below the gate uses it, otherwise explicit search fires, with ==patience-based stopping== on SAM3-plus-depth score.

## Results

- FUSE reaches **72%** success and **70.91%** IoU across **100** Habitat episodes, above Explicit Exploration **70%**, Amortized **66%**, VLM-Active **65%**, Random Active **56%**, Static **42%**.
- Gating invokes explicit search for only **33.41%** of decisions (**2.97** splat refinements per episode), cutting wall time from **10.17** to **7.62** hours, a **1.33x** speedup.
- Ablations: geometry-only **65%** versus semantic-only **53%**; replacing SAM3 pixel masks with patch-level CLIP collapses success from **70%** to **34%**; depth-proximity stopping adds ~**5** points.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Confidence-gated switching between amortized prediction and deliberate search can beat both pure modes, paying deliberation cost only on genuinely ambiguous decisions.
> - Reducing geometric under-observation is a stronger exploration signal than semantic confidence; semantics is better used to restrict where geometry-driven search looks.
> - Better-framed views are not better evidence: viewpoint-quality metrics improved while grounding did not, and weak upstream hypotheses cap achievable success regardless of sensing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a challenge in embodied artificial intelligence (AI), specifically concerning how agents interact with objects in complex, partially observed environments. Traditional approaches to object interaction often fall into several categories, each with limitations that this research aims to address:

- **Passive Affordance Grounding:** Existing methods in this category focus on predicting task-relevant objects, parts, or interaction regions from a single, fixed observation (Chen, Lin, and Aakur 2025; Chen et al. 2025b; Sawatzky et al. 2019; Li et al. 2022). While these have advanced functional reasoning, they operate under the assumption that sufficient visual evidence has already been observed. This can lead to premature commitment when functional cues are occluded or incomplete.
- **Active Recognition and Object Search:** These methods involve acquiring additional observations to improve recognition or localization, but they typically seek a *predefined category or instance* (Lei, Jiang, and Daniilidis 2026; Chaplot et al. 2020; Batra et al. 2020). The target object's identity is known a priori, and the sensing objective is to find or better observe that specific item. This differs from scenarios where the agent must first discover *which* object satisfies a functional requirement.
- **Task-Oriented View Planning and Grasping:** Some research improves visibility or grasp confidence (Shi et al. 2025; Tong et al. 2026) or repositions cameras for better grasp synthesis (Lei, Jiang, and Daniilidis 2026; Shi et al. 2025). However, these approaches generally commence with an already selected target object or manipulation hypothesis, rather than actively discovering the functional target itself.
- **Foundation Models and Multimodal Reasoning:** While large vision-language models (VLMs) and neuro-symbolic methods (Yu et al. 2025; Ahn et al. 2022) have improved functional reasoning from available observations, they generally do not inherently provide mechanisms for actively deciding where to look when crucial evidence is obscured or ambiguous.

The authors identify a gap where an embodied agent needs to: (1) infer potential functional targets from a high-level language query (e.g., "cut a piece of rope"), (2) identify candidate objects in a partially observed scene, and (3) actively acquire additional observations to resolve ambiguity and spatially ground the most suitable object, all without a predefined target identity. This work introduces "Active Functional Affordance Grounding" as a new task that specifically addresses this sequence of challenges, distinguishing itself from prior work that typically assumes a known target or sufficient initial observation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enable embodied agents to efficiently identify and spatially ground objects based on their function within partially observed environments. This requires an agent to actively gather visual information when initial observations are insufficient to resolve functional ambiguities.

The key motivations driving this work are:

- **Necessity for Functional Understanding in Embodied AI:** Functional understanding is fundamental for advanced embodied tasks such as long-horizon planning and problem-solving. An agent needs to interpret high-level functional queries (e.g., "scoop food") and identify objects in the environment that can fulfill that function, even if the specific object identity (e.g., "spoon" vs. "ladle") is not explicitly given or immediately obvious.
- **Limitations of Passive Perception:** Current passive affordance grounding methods often commit to a functional hypothesis from a fixed viewpoint. However, crucial evidence about an object's properties or suitability for a function may be occluded, poorly localized, or simply unavailable from a single initial perspective. This necessitates active sensing.
- **Inadequacy of Existing Active Perception Paradigms:** While active perception exists for tasks like object recognition or navigation, these typically involve searching for a *pre-specified* object category or instance. In contrast, functional grounding requires the agent to first infer *candidate* objects from a query and then actively explore to gather evidence to differentiate among these candidates and confidently select the most appropriate one. The target object's identity is not known in advance.
- **Computational Efficiency vs. Reliability:** Actively acquiring observations, especially with detailed scene representations, can be computationally intensive. A method is needed that can balance the reliability gained from deliberate, evidence-driven exploration with the computational efficiency required for real-time operation in dynamic environments.
- **Development of a Standardized Benchmark:** To facilitate progress in this new task, a standardized benchmark and evaluation protocol are required. This includes defining functional queries, sensing trajectories, stopping criteria, and metrics to enable consistent comparison of different active functional grounding methods.

To address these motivations, the paper introduces FUSE, an adaptive framework designed to combine fast, learned planning with more deliberate, explicit exploration to efficiently acquire the semantic and geometric evidence necessary for reliable functional affordance grounding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FUSE (Functional Understanding through Semantic–Geometric Exploration) is an adaptive evidence-acquisition framework designed for active functional affordance grounding. Its methodology can be broken down into several interconnected components:

- **Problem Formulation:** The task is defined as minimizing unresolved functional uncertainty (Ufunc) while accounting for the cost of active perception. Given a functional query (q), an affordance-generation module first produces candidate object hypotheses (Oq). The agent, starting from a partial observation, selects sensing actions to identify and spatially ground an object o* ∈ Oq. The process terminates when o* can be grounded with sufficient confidence.
- **Functional Uncertainty:** This is a spatial field indicating where evidence for grounding functionally relevant candidates remains unresolved. It is decomposed into two components:
 - **Semantic Uncertainty (Usem):** Derived from SAM3 (Segment Anything with Concepts), which predicts pixel-level masks and confidence scores for each candidate object in Oq. A dense semantic evidence map S_t(Oq) is created, and Usem is computed as 1 - S_t(Oq) (after normalization). Low values in S_t(Oq) indicate high semantic uncertainty.
 - **Geometric Uncertainty (Ugeo):** Quantifies regions poorly explained by the current spatial scene representation. It is calculated using a Structural Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SSNR) map, which measures discrepancies between observed image gradients and gradients of an image rendered from the scene representation. Low SSNR (high discrepancy) corresponds to high Ugeo, indicating areas where more geometric observation is beneficial.
- **Spatial Evidence Representation:** A persistent 3D spatial evidence representation, denoted as B_t, accumulates observations over time. This representation is instantiated using an incrementally refined 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) model. It allows the agent to integrate evidence across viewpoints, reason about visibility and occlusion, predict observations, and localize incomplete geometric information. The 3DGS model is initialized from a few seed observations using Structure-from-Motion (COLMAP) and updated incrementally with new registered observations.
- **Functional Evidence Exploration (Explicit Planning):** This mode of planning actively selects viewpoints based on the combined semantic and geometric uncertainty.
 - An **evidence-acquisition map E_t(Oq)** is generated by fusing S_t(Oq) and Ugeo_t. This map prioritizes regions that are both semantically promising (i.e., associated with candidate objects) and geometrically under-observed.
 - For each feasible sensing action (a), an associated image region Ω(a) that the action is expected to reveal is defined.
 - The quality of an action Q_t(a; Oq) is evaluated by averaging the evidence-acquisition map over Ω(a).
 - The explicit policy selects the action that maximizes Q_t(a; Oq). Crucially, in this mode, the 3DGS representation is refined at *every* step to accurately reflect all accumulated observations and update geometric uncertainty.
- **Evidence Accumulation and Termination:** As the agent explores, it computes a fused target score (r_t,i) for each candidate object at each visited viewpoint. This score combines the SAM3 semantic grounding score with a mask-level depth-proximity score, favoring closer, well-resolved targets. The highest fused score encountered determines the best grounding so far. Exploration terminates if the best score does not improve for K consecutive observations (patience) or after a maximum sensing budget T is exhausted.
- **Amortized Evidence Planning:** To mitigate the computational cost of explicit planning, an amortized planner is introduced. This learned model directly predicts the value of candidate sensing actions from the current observation, a candidate object hypothesis, and the action, *without explicitly updating the 3DGS representation*. It utilizes a frozen CLIP visual backbone and a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) trained on sampled scene transitions to predict expected evidence gain.
- **FUSE: Adaptive Evidence Acquisition (Adaptive Strategy):** This is the core adaptive mechanism. At each step:
 - The amortized planner predicts action values and computes the entropy (H_t) of the resulting action distribution, which serves as a measure of decision ambiguity.
 - If H_t is below a predefined threshold (η), FUSE uses the amortized planner's prediction, executing the highest-valued action and *deferring 3DGS refinement*. This is the fast, efficient path.
 - If H_t is above η (high ambiguity), FUSE invokes the more deliberate, explicit exploration. This involves refining the 3DGS representation over all registered observations and computing explicit semantic-geometric scores to select the next action. This allows for reliable decision-making in ambiguous states at a higher computational cost.

The system is implemented in a Habitat-based simulation environment, using procedurally assembled tabletop scenes. The discrete action space includes horizontal camera motions and tilt adjustments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of FUSE was conducted on a Habitat-based benchmark comprising 100 independently randomized scene instances, evaluating various metrics including Success Rate, Average IoU, Average Steps, and viewpoint quality metrics (Target Pixel Ratio, Center Score, Distance Score, Final SAM3 Score).

- **Active Perception Improves Functional Grounding:**
 - Passive methods (Static: 42.00% success, Canonical View: 32.00%) exhibited limited performance, indicating that a single, fixed viewpoint or a simple canonical view is often insufficient for reliable functional grounding.
 - Active exploration, even random (Random Active: 56.00%), yielded improved success rates, demonstrating the inherent benefit of acquiring more observations.
- **Task-Relevant Sensing Outperforms Unguided Exploration:**
 - Explicit Exploration (70.00% success) and FUSE (72.00% success) significantly outperformed Random Active (56.00%), confirming the effectiveness of task-directed sensing based on semantic and geometric evidence.
 - VLM Active, which received oracle object labels, achieved 65.00% success, indicating that while large models can perform coarse task-directed exploration, they did not match FUSE's performance in acquiring evidence for reliable grounding, despite moving toward and centering targets effectively.
- **FUSE's Performance and Efficiency Trade-off:**
 - FUSE achieved the highest observed non-oracle grounding performance with a 72.00% success rate, 70.91% Average IoU, and 66.61% Final SAM3 Score.
 - Compared to Explicit Exploration, FUSE maintained comparable grounding performance (72% vs. 70%, McNemar p=0.688) while reducing end-to-end wall-clock runtime by 1.33x (Table 5). This efficiency gain stems from FUSE invoking explicit exploration for only 33.41% of decisions (average 2.97 3DGS refinements per episode), effectively using the faster amortized planner for routine decisions.
 - The Amortized Planner alone achieved 66.00% success, demonstrating its efficiency (2.57x speedup over Explicit Exploration) but also highlighting that its predictions can be ambiguous, leading to lower accuracy than FUSE. FUSE's selective fallback strategy improved success by 6 percentage points over the purely amortized approach.
- **Robustness to Affordance Knowledge Sources:**
 - FUSE demonstrated robustness across various upstream knowledge sources (neuro-symbolic, vision-language, large language models), maintaining its adaptive evidence acquisition strategy.
 - CRAFT-E and Gemini achieved the highest non-oracle success rates (both 72.00%), while an Oracle-Label variant reached 77.00%. This indicated that FUSE's benefits persist across different ways of generating candidate functional hypotheses. The differences in success rates between knowledge sources primarily reflected the quality of the initial hypotheses they provided.
- **Ablation Studies and Component Contributions:**
 - Semantic evidence alone (Semantic Only: 53.00%) was insufficient, showing that SAM3's pixel-level grounding needs to be combined with geometric exploration.
 - Geometric uncertainty alone (Geometry Only: 65.00%) provided a stronger exploration signal than semantic confidence alone, demonstrating its value in revealing unseen regions.
 - Combining both semantic and geometric evidence (Explicit Exploration: 70.00%) confirmed their complementary roles in viewpoint selection.
 - Pixel-level SAM3 grounding (70.00% success) significantly outperformed patch-level CLIP evidence (34.00% success), highlighting the importance of precise semantic information.
 - Incorporating depth proximity into the stopping criterion improved success by approximately 5 percentage points, by favoring views where the target was at a more useful distance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research contributes to the field of embodied AI and robotic perception in several ways:

- **Novel Task Formulation:** The introduction of "Active Functional Affordance Grounding" addresses a previously underexplored problem in embodied intelligence. It formalizes the challenge of discovering and spatially grounding functionally relevant objects from high-level queries in partially observed environments, without prior knowledge of the target's identity. This formulation moves beyond passive perception and object-specific active search.
- **Adaptive Exploration Framework:** FUSE provides a practical and effective solution to the accuracy-efficiency trade-off inherent in active perception. By adaptively switching between a fast, learned amortized planner and a more deliberate, explicit semantic-geometric exploration, FUSE optimizes computational resources while maintaining or improving grounding reliability. This adaptive strategy has implications for deploying embodied agents in real-world scenarios with computational constraints.
- **Synergistic Use of Semantic and Geometric Cues:** The framework demonstrates the critical synergy between semantic information (from models like SAM3) and geometric uncertainty (derived from 3D scene representations like 3DGS). The fusion of these cues enables more intelligent and efficient viewpoint selection, guiding the agent towards regions that are both functionally relevant and geometrically under-observed.
- **Robustness Across Knowledge Sources:** The evaluation shows that FUSE's active evidence acquisition strategy is robust to variations in the upstream functional knowledge sources, from neuro-symbolic to large language models. This suggests the framework can be broadly applicable, complementing diverse approaches to functional reasoning by improving the perceptual grounding phase.
- **Advancement of Embodied Agents:** By enabling more reliable and efficient functional object discovery, FUSE contributes to the development of more capable embodied agents. This capability is essential for long-horizon planning, complex manipulation tasks, and problem-solving in unstructured environments, where agents must identify and interact with objects based on their properties rather than their specific class.
- **Benchmark for Future Research:** The release of a Habitat-based benchmark and evaluation protocol for active functional affordance grounding provides a standardized platform for the community to develop and compare new methods, fostering further research in this area.

Future work includes validating FUSE on real robotic platforms to assess its transferability to physical systems and extending it to dynamic environments and more complex manipulation tasks. The research also opens avenues for agents that jointly learn functional reasoning, active perception, and action execution, leading towards more integrated and intelligent robotic systems.
