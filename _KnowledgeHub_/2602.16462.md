# Reactive Motion Generation With Particle-Based Perception in Dynamic Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> The SMART framework enables robot manipulators to generate reactive, collision-free motions in dynamic and unstructured environments by tightly integrating a real-time particle-based occupancy map with an uncertainty-aware Model Predictive Path Integral planner. This approach demonstrated higher success rates, reaching 96% for fast-moving obstacles, and more consistent collision avoidance compared to baseline methods in both simulated and real-world experiments.

## Problem

- Reactive motion generation for high-dimensional manipulators often assumes static environments or lacks real-time global mapping and dynamic obstacle propagation.
- Most reactive planning approaches for manipulators do not explicitly model dynamic obstacle characteristics like velocity and uncertainty, leading to collisions or over-conservative behaviors.
- There is a limited unified integration of real-time sensor feedback with motion generation for manipulators operating in dynamic and uncertain settings.

## Method

- A framework named SMART integrates a Global Dual-Structure Particle-based (G-DSP) map with a Dynamic Obstacle-Aware STORM (D-STORM) planner for real-time, reactive motion generation.
- The G-DSP map models obstacle positions, velocities, and uncertainties using a particle-based continuous occupancy representation, optimized via tensor operations on GPUs.
- The D-STORM planner extends ==MPPI== to incorporate dynamic robot-obstacle propagation and uncertainty-aware collision costs, ensuring proactive avoidance strategies.

## Results

- The G-DSP map yielded smoother velocity estimations with lower RMSE and higher occupancy estimation accuracy (e.g., AUC of **0.910** at 0.02m voxel resolution) compared to other particle-based maps.
- The D-STORM planner achieved a **96%** success rate for collision avoidance with fast-moving obstacles (**0.2** m/s), outperforming baseline planners (73-**87%** success rates).
- The integrated SMART framework demonstrated significantly higher success rates and consistent collision-free motion in dynamic simulations, and successfully avoided parcels and human interaction in real-world UR5 robot experiments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tightly integrating a global particle-based continuous occupancy map (G-DSP) with an MPPI-based planner (D-STORM) enables robust reactive motion generation for manipulators in dynamic, unstructured environments.
> - Explicitly modeling obstacle dynamics (velocity, uncertainty) within both real-time perception and motion planning modules allows for more proactive and safer collision avoidance.
> - Tensor optimization and GPU acceleration are crucial for achieving real-time performance in high-dimensional manipulator tasks by efficiently handling batch operations in both mapping and stochastic control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses challenges in reactive and sensor-based planning for robot manipulators operating in dynamic and unstructured environments. Traditional reactive planning methods for manipulators often assume static, perfectly perceptive, or known environments. While significant progress has been made in solving high-dimensional problems for manipulators, these methods frequently overlook the complexities introduced by dynamic obstacles and real-time sensor feedback.

A notable line of research in robotics focuses on equipping manipulators with environmental awareness by using on-board sensors to reconstruct surroundings via voxels or point clouds. However, many of these perception-planning pipelines are offline, thereby limiting their application to static environments. For mobile robots and unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), advancements in particle-based maps have enabled the modeling of dynamic and arbitrary-shaped obstacles, offering a foundation for integrating perception with model predictive control (MPC). Nevertheless, adapting these advancements to high-dimensional articulated manipulators, which have specific kinematic constraints, real-time requirements, and necessitate global mapping tailored to manipulation tasks, remains an area of ongoing research.

Existing reactive motion generation approaches include field-based methods (e.g., Artificial Potential Fields, Vector Fields, Dynamical System Modulation), tree-based methods (e.g., Monte Carlo Tree Search, Partially Observable Monte-Carlo Planning), and optimization-based methods (e.g., Model Predictive Control, Model Predictive Path Integral - MPPI). While some of these address dynamic environments, they frequently require exact geometry representations, struggle with high-dimensional joint spaces, or do not explicitly account for the dynamic characteristics and uncertainties of obstacles derived from real-time sensing. Specifically, while MPPI has been extended for high-dimensional joint spaces, its application to manipulators in unstructured, dynamic environments, particularly with explicit dynamic obstacle propagation, has been limited. This paper positions itself at the intersection of real-time dynamic mapping and reactive planning, seeking to bridge the gap by developing a tightly coupled, sensor-based control framework for manipulators in such challenging scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations of current reactive motion generation systems for manipulators in dynamic and unstructured environments. Specifically, the authors identify three core challenges:
1. **High-dimensional Kinematic Constraints and Real-time Requirements for Manipulators:** Articulated manipulators possess numerous degrees of freedom, complex arm geometries, and demanding real-time operational requirements. Existing perception methods are often restricted, leading to reactive planning that primarily addresses static or structured environments, rather than dynamic scenarios where global mapping and dynamics propagation for both the manipulator and obstacles are critical.
2. **Lack of Explicit Dynamic Obstacle Modeling:** Most reactive planning for manipulators does not explicitly model the dynamic characteristics (e.g., velocity and covariance) of obstacles. Reliably representing moving obstacles and optimizing collision-free trajectories under the inherent uncertainty of perception and control is a significant challenge. This deficiency can lead to collisions or overly conservative behaviors when faced with fast-moving or unpredictable elements in the workspace.
3. **Limited Unified Integration of Sensing and Planning:** While real-time perception and real-time planning have been studied individually, their unified integration, particularly the incorporation of depth sensor feedback into motion generation for manipulators in dynamic and uncertain environments, remains an underdeveloped area. A tightly coupled approach is necessary to enable effective real-time reactivity.

To address these motivations, the paper sets forth several key objectives, summarized as the development of a framework called Simultaneous Mapping And Reactive Planning Using Tensor Optimization (SMART):
1. **Develop a Global Particle-Based Continuous Occupancy Map (G-DSP):** This objective aims to create a map tailored for arm manipulations that operates in the global frame, explicitly models obstacle velocities and covariance, and can handle dynamic environments efficiently through batch operations and tensorization.
2. **Extend MPPI for Dynamic Collision Avoidance:** The research aims to generalize the MPPI formulation to account for dynamic collision avoidance by propagating robot-obstacle dynamics and incorporating obstacle uncertainty into the planning process.
3. **Propose a Tensorized Reactive and Sensor-Based Planning Paradigm:** The goal is to tightly integrate the G-DSP map with MPPI-based control, leveraging tensor optimization to enable real-time performance for dynamic collision avoidance tasks in manipulators.
4. **Implement and Evaluate the Framework:** The final objective is to develop a real-time GPU-accelerated implementation of SMART and empirically evaluate its performance in both simulated and noisy real-world environments, benchmarking it against state-of-the-art baselines for avoiding multiple static and dynamic obstacles.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, SMART (Simultaneous Mapping And Reactive Planning Using Tensor Optimization), is designed to generate reactive collision-free motion for manipulators in dynamic and unstructured environments. It consists of a real-time perception module, the Global Dual-Structure Particle-based (G-DSP) map, and a reactive motion generation module, Dynamic Obstacle-Aware STORM (D-STORM). The entire system leverages tensor optimizations on GPUs for parallel computation.

#### 3.1. Global Particle-Based Mapping for Manipulators (G-DSP Map)

The G-DSP map is a particle-based continuous occupancy map operating in the global world frame, specifically designed for manipulators. It explicitly models the positions, velocities, and uncertainties (covariance) of static and moving obstacles.

- **Global Dual-Structure Map:** The map maintains point objects as a Random Finite Set (RFS), where each particle stores 3D position, 3D velocity, weight, and indices for its associated voxel and pyramid subspaces. The mapping space is a cuboid divided into `N_v` voxel subspaces for occupancy estimation and `N_p` pyramid subspaces (based on camera FOV) for observational updates. Occupancy probability, voxel center position, and average velocity for each voxel are estimated by summing and weighting particle information within that voxel (Equations 15-17). The uncertainty of voxel position and velocity is explicitly modeled by Gaussian covariance matrices (Equation 18).
- **Robot Geometry Integration:** To prevent the robot from perceiving its own body as an obstacle, observations inside or near the manipulator's surface are ignored using a signed distance function (SDF) based on forward kinematics.
- **Tensor Representation and Batch Operations:** Particle states are stored in a main particle tensor. Auxiliary pyramid and voxel tensors are used to facilitate efficient querying and updates. A key methodological aspect is the extensive use of batch operations and tensor manipulations (e.g., circular shifts and comparisons) to accelerate particle assignment to subspaces (Algorithm 1), prediction (Equation 20), and weight updates (Algorithm 2, Equations 22-25) on GPUs. This reduces the computational complexity of SMC-PHD filtering, making it quasi-linear with respect to the maximum number of particles, significantly faster than traditional sequential processing.
- **SMC-PHD Filter:** The core of the map is a Sequential Monte-Carlo Probability Hypothesis Density (SMC-PHD) filter.
 - **Prediction:** Valid particles in voxel subspaces are propagated using a constant velocity model with added Gaussian noise. Newborn particles are also introduced.
 - **Update:** Weights of observable particles (within the camera's FOV) are updated based on noisy real-world measurements using a Gaussian likelihood model. Occluded particles' weights are maintained or attenuated. The update process is parallelized using tensor operations (Algorithm 2).
- **Trilevel Model:** The map operates on three levels (object/cluster, subspace/voxel, and particle) to provide robust estimations. Velocity estimation for new point clouds (at the cluster level) uses finite differences and the Munkres algorithm for matching.
- **Output:** The G-DSP map outputs a tensor of occupied voxel primitives, each containing its center position, velocity, position covariance, velocity covariance, and voxel length, which serves as the input to the planning module.

#### 3.2. Reactive Collision-Free Motion Generation (D-STORM)

D-STORM extends the Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) approach, specifically the STORM variant, to handle dynamic obstacles by jointly propagating robot and obstacle dynamics.

- **Stochastic Optimal Control Problem:** The planning problem is formulated as minimizing a cost functional over a control sequence, where the control input (joint accelerations) is a random variable sampled from a Gaussian distribution. The objective function includes a control cost and a state-dependent immediate cost that considers both robot and obstacle states (Equations 26-29).
- **Robot Dynamics Propagation:** An approximated dynamical model is used to roll out `K` sampled control trajectories over a planning horizon `H` (Equation 30). This process, which involves integrating joint accelerations to obtain velocities and positions, is parallelized using tensor operations and a lower triangular matrix. A variable time step is used, with smaller steps near the current state and larger ones further in the horizon.
- **Obstacle Dynamics Propagation:** For each of the `N_o` perceived obstacle primitives (voxels), a constant velocity model is employed to predict its future positions along the planning horizon (Equation 31). Crucially, the position uncertainty (covariance `Σ_p_o`) of obstacles is also propagated. A simplified analytical formula (Equation 43), derived from the Extended Kalman Filter under the assumption of a block-diagonal initial covariance and negligible process noise for short horizons, enables batch calculation of covariance propagation, maintaining real-time performance.
- **Uncertainty-Aware Cost Function:** The immediate cost for collision avoidance (`c_col`) is designed to be uncertainty-aware. Instead of a fixed radius, an adaptive radius `r_o` for each obstacle is computed based on its maximum position uncertainty (maximum eigenvalue of `Σ_p_o`) (Equation 37). This allows the planner to adapt its avoidance strategy based on the perceived uncertainty of obstacle positions, leading to safer trajectories. For real-world deployment, a softmax-based sum is used for collision cost (Equation 44) to provide a smooth, risk-sensitive approximation robust to perception noise.
- **Other Cost Terms:** In addition to obstacle collision cost, the total cost function (Equation 42) incorporates:
 - **Self-Collision Avoidance:** A neural network (jointNerf) is used to predict minimum distances between robot links, penalizing penetrations (Equation 38).
 - **Joint Limits:** Penalizes joint positions exceeding specified safety thresholds (Equation 39).
 - **Manipulability:** Penalizes configurations with low manipulability index to avoid singularities (Equation 40).
 - **Goal Reaching:** Encourages the end-effector to reach the target configuration (Equation 41).
- **Implementation:** D-STORM runs on a GPU, leveraging parallel execution for `K` trajectories. A multiprocessing approach is employed to mitigate control latency by streaming desired controls to a low-level admittance controller at high frequency (Algorithm 4). Collision evaluation is restricted to the top `N_c_o` closest obstacles (e.g., 20) to manage computational load.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the SMART framework was evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulated and real-world environments, benchmarking its G-DSP map and D-STORM planner against existing state-of-the-art methods.

#### 4.1. Mapping and Perception Comparison

The G-DSP map was compared against Ewok, K3DOM, and DSP map using metrics of velocity estimation, binary classification (occupancy), and computation time in arm manipulation tasks.

- **Velocity Estimation:** In real-world tests with constant-velocity and random-velocity objects, G-DSP consistently yielded smoother velocity estimations with lower Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) and standard deviation compared to baselines like Munkres-Diff and DSP map (Table I, Figure 7). For constant-velocity objects, estimation stabilized within 1 second, and recovery from perturbations occurred within approximately 2 seconds. The DSP map showed intermediate performance, attributed to imprecise particle weight updates and non-particle-weighted voxel velocity calculations.
- **Occupancy Estimation:** In simulated environments with complex-shaped obstacles, G-DSP achieved the highest average AUC (Area Under Precision-Recall Curve) and F1-score across various voxel resolutions (Table II). At a voxel resolution of 0.02m, G-DSP showed an AUC of 0.910 compared to DSP map's 0.757 and Ewok's 0.845. Qualitatively, G-DSP and DSP maps effectively captured dynamic characteristics, while Ewok and K3DOM suffered from trail noise and missed small objects, particularly with fast-moving obstacles (Figure 8).
- **Computation Time:** G-DSP demonstrated significantly lower total step latency compared to other particle-based maps (DSP and K3DOM), with prediction and update steps being notably faster (Figure 9). While Ewok had the shortest mapping time, its static nature limited its applicability in dynamic scenarios. G-DSP maintained total perception times below 100ms for typical voxel sizes, crucial for reactive control.

#### 4.2. D-STORM Planning Comparison

D-STORM was compared to standard STORM and DS-MPPI in known dynamic environments with varying obstacle sizes and velocities (0.0 to 0.2 m/s).

- **Success Rate:** D-STORM achieved a substantially higher success rate, particularly in dynamic environments. For six obstacles moving at 0.2 m/s, D-STORM had a 96% success rate, compared to STORM's 73% and DS-MPPI's 87% (Table III). This indicates improved safety in environments with rapid changes.
- **Execution Time and Path Length:** D-STORM generally incurred longer round-trip execution times and joint-space path lengths compared to baselines. For example, with six obstacles at 0.2 m/s, D-STORM's round-trip time was 17.97s, longer than STORM's 13.63s and DS-MPPI's 5.95s. This suggests a trade-off where D-STORM's proactive prediction leads to safer but potentially less direct paths.
- **MPC Frequency:** STORM and D-STORM maintained comparable MPC frequencies, generally above 25 Hz when restricting the number of considered obstacles to 20, demonstrating real-time capability despite increased computational load from dynamics propagation.

#### 4.3. Simulation Pipeline Comparison (SMART vs. Baseline)

The integrated SMART framework (G-DSP + D-STORM) was compared against a conventional pipeline (raycast-based Ewok + vanilla STORM) in simulated unstructured dynamic environments with T-shaped obstacles and multiple obstacles.

- **Success Rate and Consistency:** SMART achieved significantly higher success rates in dynamic scenarios, especially with moderate (0.1 m/s) and fast (0.2 m/s) moving obstacles, where the baseline's success rates dropped significantly (e.g., to 74% and 59% respectively for the baseline, while SMART maintained higher rates) (Figure 11). SMART also demonstrated a tighter interquartile range for path length, indicating more consistent collision avoidance behavior.
- **Qualitative Performance:** Visualizations showed the baseline failing to anticipate obstacle motion or suffering from trail noise in its perception, leading to collisions or near-collisions with fast or multiple dynamic obstacles (Figure 12, 13). SMART, by contrast, consistently achieved collision-free motion by explicitly incorporating dynamic characteristics into both perception and planning.

#### 4.4. Real-World Applications

Real-world experiments on a 6-DOF UR5 robot validated SMART's performance in environments with multiple static and dynamic obstacles (e.g., parcels on a conveyor belt, human interaction).

- The robot successfully perceived and avoided moving parcels and human partners (Figure 14, 15), demonstrating its capability for safe obstacle avoidance in physically human-robot interaction (pHRI) and factory applications. The use of a softmax-based sum for collision cost contributed to robustness against real-world sensor noise.
- The implementation leveraged GPU acceleration for both perception and planning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, SMART, that addresses a critical gap in the field of reactive motion generation for manipulators: operating safely and efficiently in dynamic and unstructured environments. By establishing a tight connection between continuous particle-based mapping and MPPI-based planning, the work offers an integrated approach to a problem often tackled by separate, less dynamically-aware modules.

The development of the G-DSP map is significant as it provides manipulators with real-time, global awareness of obstacle positions, velocities, and their associated uncertainties. The tensorized, batch-operated design enables this rich perception to run at high frequencies on GPUs, which is crucial for reactive systems. This is a notable advancement over traditional static or less dynamic mapping methods that often lead to delayed reactions or collisions in volatile environments.

The D-STORM planner, which explicitly propagates robot-obstacle dynamics and incorporates uncertainty-aware costs, enhances the safety and reactivity of manipulator control. Its ability to anticipate obstacle movements, rather than merely reacting to current positions, is a key enabler for robust collision avoidance. The demonstrated superior success rates in dynamic scenarios compared to state-of-the-art baselines support its practical utility.

The unified tensor optimization formulation, applied across both perception and planning modules, represents a methodological contribution towards highly efficient, real-time robotic systems. This integration provides an insightful alternative to conventional perception-planning pipelines, particularly for high-dimensional manipulators.

The potential impact of SMART is considerable across several domains:
- **Safe Human-Robot Interaction (pHRI):** By enabling manipulators to react effectively to human movements and other dynamic elements in a shared workspace, the framework contributes to safer and more fluid human-robot collaboration.
- **Autonomous Exploration and Factory Applications:** The ability to navigate and interact in unstructured and dynamic settings extends the applicability of manipulators in tasks ranging from logistics and assembly to inspection, where environments can change unpredictably.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework provides a robust foundation for future extensions, such as incorporating multi-resolution mapping, persistent memory for static obstacles, multi-modal sensor fusion (e.g., visual and tactile feedback), and active viewpoint generation for complex or partially observed obstacles.

While the current implementation operates within "trivially unstructured environments" and focuses on contact-free planning, the theoretical analysis and empirical validation demonstrate the efficacy of SMART in addressing complex real-world challenges, offering a step towards more intelligent and robust collision-free planning for advanced robotic applications.
