# Harder Is Better: Boosting Mathematical Reasoning via Difficulty-Aware GRPO and Multi-Aspect Question Reformulation

> [!summary] Summary
> MathForge, developed by researchers from Renmin University of China and Alibaba Group, enhances mathematical reasoning in large language models by integrating Difficulty-Aware Group Policy Optimization (DGPO) and Multi-Aspect Question Reformulation (MQR). This framework improves performance on the MATH dataset by 4.56% over the GRPO baseline, achieving an average score of 42.17%.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods, particularly GRPO, algorithmically neglect challenging questions, suppressing policy updates for both very easy and very hard problems.
- Current data augmentation strategies for mathematical reasoning primarily focus on rephrasing or generating new problem-solution pairs, lacking a systematic approach to increase intrinsic question difficulty while preserving original answers.

## Method

- Difficulty-Aware Group Policy Optimization (DGPO) modifies GRPO's ==advantage estimation== using Mean Absolute Deviation (MAD) and applies difficulty-aware question-level weighting to prioritize harder, valid questions during training.
- Multi-Aspect Question Reformulation (MQR) automatically augments training data by reformulating existing questions across three dimensions (adding story background, abstract terminology, nesting sub-problems) to increase difficulty while preserving gold answers.
- MathForge integrates DGPO and MQR into a synergistic framework where MQR provides challenging data and DGPO efficiently learns from it, fostering a 'train harder, test better' learning paradigm.

## Results

- MathForge achieves a **42.17%** average score on the MATH dataset with Qwen2.5-Math-**7B**, representing a **4.56%** improvement over the GRPO baseline (**37.61%**).
- DGPO alone improves performance by **2.18%** over GRPO, demonstrating its effectiveness in targeted RL training and compatibility with other policy optimization methods.
- MQR-augmented data, when combined with GRPO, boosts performance by **3.43%**, confirming the value of systematically harder training examples and showing strong generalizability across various base models and to multimodal tasks like GeoQA-8k.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - GRPO's standard deviation-based advantage estimation inherently suppresses update magnitudes for very easy and very hard questions, peaking at moderate difficulty.
> - Replacing standard deviation with Mean Absolute Deviation (MAD) in GRPO's advantage calculation can mathematically rectify this implicit imbalance, ensuring all valid questions contribute equally.
> - Systematically increasing question difficulty through controlled reformulation, while preserving original solutions, creates a qualitatively superior training environment that drives robust model improvement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper situates itself within the rapidly evolving landscape of Large Language Models (LLMs) and their reasoning capabilities, particularly in the domain of mathematical problem-solving. It builds upon the success of **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**, which has emerged as a promising paradigm for enhancing mathematical reasoning in LLMs. RLVR distinguishes itself from other RL approaches by utilizing rule-based rewards (e.g., binary accuracy) instead of neural reward models, thereby reducing computational overhead and mitigating "reward hacking" issues.

At the core of RLVR, **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** is identified as a representative and widely used algorithmic approach. GRPO estimates relative advantages within a group of responses to the same question, offering an efficient critic-less alternative to methods like PPO. The paper acknowledges a growing body of work aimed at refining GRPO's stability and performance, citing examples such as Dr.GRPO (addressing length bias), GPG and DAPO (improving reward design and advantage estimation), GSPO and GMPO (introducing alternative objectives), and GRPO-AD (recognizing question difficulty).

However, the authors identify two critical, systematic limitations in the existing literature that their work aims to address:
1. **Algorithmic Neglect of Challenging Questions in GRPO:** They mathematically demonstrate that GRPO inherently suppresses update magnitudes for both very easy and very hard questions, prioritizing those of moderate difficulty. This overlooks challenging-yet-solvable questions, which are crucial for pushing model capabilities beyond its current limitations.
2. **Data Augmentation Gaps:** Existing data augmentation techniques for reasoning often focus on generating entirely new question-answer pairs (which can be difficult to quality-control, especially for complex problems) or merely rephrasing questions for diversity (e.g., MetaMath, PersonaMath). They argue that these methods lack a systematic approach to *increasing intrinsic question difficulty* while preserving the original answer, a critical aspect for robust skill development.

MathForge directly tackles these gaps. Algorithmically, it extends GRPO to explicitly emphasize harder questions, correcting its inherent imbalance. Data-wise, it introduces a novel augmentation strategy that systematically increases question difficulty in a controlled manner. This dual approach distinguishes MathForge from prior work that typically addresses either algorithmic improvements or data augmentation in isolation. By simultaneously enhancing *how* the model learns (DGPO) and *what* it learns from (MQR), MathForge aims to provide a more comprehensive and synergistic solution to advance mathematical reasoning in LLMs. The "harder is better" principle advocated by the paper aligns with curriculum learning ideas, but applied specifically to RLVR for mathematical tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from an observed systematic deficiency in how current Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods, particularly those based on Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), handle questions of varying difficulty in mathematical reasoning tasks. The authors argue that while challenging questions are crucial for refining underdeveloped capabilities in large language models (LLMs), existing approaches fail to emphasize them sufficiently from both algorithmic and data perspectives.

The key objectives are thus to:

1. **Rectify Algorithmic Imbalance in GRPO:** The authors identify and mathematically prove that GRPO's advantage estimation function leads to an "implicit imbalance." This imbalance results in policy updates being suppressed for both very easy and, critically, very hard questions, peaking instead for questions of moderate difficulty. This neglect prevents LLMs from fully leveraging challenging-yet-solvable problems, which are ideal for targeted skill improvement. The objective is to design an algorithm that ensures harder questions receive appropriately higher update magnitudes.
2. **Systematically Increase Question Difficulty in Data Augmentation:** Current data augmentation strategies primarily focus on enhancing data diversity through rephrasing or generating new problem-solution pairs. However, these methods often do not systematically increase the intrinsic difficulty of questions while maintaining answer integrity. The objective is to develop a data augmentation strategy that reformulates questions to inherently increase their complexity across multiple dimensions, thereby pushing the model's reasoning boundaries without requiring new solution generation.
3. **Develop a Synergistic Framework:** The overarching objective is to integrate these algorithmic and data-centric solutions into a cohesive framework where they mutually reinforce each other. The goal is for the augmented, more challenging data to provide richer learning opportunities, and for the difficulty-aware algorithm to efficiently learn from these enhanced challenges, creating a powerful "synergistic loop."

In summary, the motivation is to overcome the limitations of current RLVR methods by explicitly focusing on the "harder is better" principle. The authors aim to enable LLMs to more effectively learn from their most challenging solvable weaknesses, fostering more robust and generalized mathematical reasoning capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **MathForge**, a two-dual framework that addresses the identified limitations by enhancing mathematical reasoning through a concerted focus on challenging questions from both algorithmic and data perspectives. MathForge comprises two core components: Difficulty-Aware Group Policy Optimization (DGPO) and Multi-Aspect Question Reformulation (MQR).

#### 3.1. Difficulty-Aware Group Policy Optimization (DGPO)

DGPO is designed to counteract the implicit imbalance in GRPO's update magnitudes and further prioritize harder questions during the learning process.

1. **Difficulty-Balanced Group Advantage Estimation (DGAE):**
 - **Problem with GRPO:** The paper mathematically proves that GRPO's advantage function, which normalizes rewards by the *standard deviation* of responses, causes the total update magnitude for a question to peak at a 50% accuracy rate (p=0.5) and decrease as accuracy approaches 0% or 100%. This means very easy or very hard questions have less impact on policy updates.
 - **DGAE Solution:** DGPO replaces the standard deviation with the **Mean Absolute Deviation (MAD)** in the denominator of the advantage calculation. The authors mathematically prove that this change (Theorem 2) normalizes the total update magnitude for any single question to a constant value (specifically, G, the group size), regardless of its accuracy rate. This effectively rectifies the implicit imbalance, ensuring that all valid questions (those with non-uniform rewards) contribute equally to the overall update magnitude.

2. **Difficulty-Aware Question-Level Weighting (DQW):**
 - **Purpose:** Building on DGAE's balanced updates, DQW then explicitly prioritizes harder questions within a batch.
 - **Mechanism:** It assigns a weight (λ_s) to each question (q_s) using an exponential function: λ_s = B_v * exp(D_s/T) / sum(exp(D_s/T)). Here, D_s is the "difficulty score," defined as the negative mean reward (negative mean accuracy) across all responses for that question. A lower mean accuracy (i.e., higher D_s) implies a harder question, which then receives a higher weight.
 - **Hyperparameter:** T (temperature) controls the sharpness of the weighting distribution, allowing for tuning the emphasis on harder questions.
 - **Validity:** This weighting is applied only to "valid queries" (questions where not all responses are uniformly correct or incorrect), preventing gradient fluctuations and focusing on constructive learning signals.

#### 3.2. Multi-Aspect Question Reformulation (MQR)

MQR is a data-centric approach that automatically augments the training dataset by reformulating existing questions to increase their intrinsic difficulty and diversity, crucially **while preserving the original gold answer**. This avoids the need for regenerating solutions, reducing complexity and ensuring mathematical correctness. A large reasoning model (e.g., OpenAI o3) serves as the "reformulator."

MQR reformulates questions across three specific aspects:

1. **Adding Story Background:** Introduces irrelevant narrative details that do not affect the core mathematical problem. This challenges the model's ability to identify critical information amidst noise and filter out distractions.
2. **Introducing Abstract Terminology:** Invents new, abstract mathematical terms to define concepts central to the question, then restates the question using this new terminology. This tests the model's capacity to grasp abstract concepts and adapt to novel problem representations.
3. **Nesting Sub-problems:** Converts a key numerical condition within the original question into an independent sub-problem. This forces the model to perform multi-step reasoning and potentially integrate knowledge from different mathematical domains to solve the nested sub-problem before addressing the main question.

#### 3.3. Synergistic Loop

MathForge operates as a synergistic loop: MQR expands the data frontier by generating more diverse and challenging questions that expose the model's weaknesses, while DGPO efficiently learns from this augmented data by focusing policy updates on mastering these increased difficulties. This dual-pronged approach aims to push the boundaries of mathematical reasoning performance more effectively than either component in isolation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments demonstrate the significant effectiveness and generalizability of the MathForge framework and its individual components.

1. **Overall Performance of MathForge:**
 - MathForge achieves the best overall performance, outperforming all existing methods on the MATH dataset using Qwen2.5-Math-7B, with an average score of **42.17%**. This represents a substantial **4.56% improvement** over the strong GRPO baseline (37.61%). This highlights the powerful synergy between DGPO and MQR.

2. **Effectiveness of DGPO (Algorithmic Component):**
 - When applied alone, DGPO raises the average score to **39.79%**, a **2.18% gain** over GRPO. This confirms that rectifying the update magnitude imbalance and prioritizing harder questions through DGAE and DQW leads to more efficient and targeted RL training.
 - DGPO also surpasses other advanced policy optimization techniques (Dr.GRPO, GPG, DAPO, GSPO, GRPO-AD), indicating the superior design of its difficulty-aware mechanisms.
 - **Ablation Studies on DGPO:**
 - The valid token-level loss averaging, DGAE, and DQW components contribute average performance improvements of 0.10%, 0.94%, and 1.14%, respectively. This breakdown confirms that both DGAE (balancing updates) and DQW (prioritizing harder questions) are crucial.
 - Temperature hyperparameter T=2.0 for DQW yields optimal performance, balancing focus on hard questions without over-sharpening the distribution.
 - **Training Dynamics:** DGPO consistently achieves higher accuracy during training and produces more concise responses, suggesting improved correctness and efficiency in reasoning.
 - **Compatibility:** DGPO demonstrates broad compatibility, yielding consistent performance improvements when integrated with other policy optimization methods like GPG, DAPO, and GSPO.
 - **Multimodal Applicability:** DGPO significantly improves performance in a multimodal mathematical reasoning task (GeoQA-8k), achieving 59.95% accuracy (+2.52% over GRPO), showcasing its domain-agnosticism.

3. **Effectiveness of MQR (Data-Centric Component):**
 - Using MQR-augmented data with GRPO achieves an average score of **41.04%**, a **3.43% increase** over the GRPO baseline. This validates that systematically increasing question difficulty through reformulation effectively creates a more challenging and diverse learning environment.
 - **Model-Agnosticism:** MathForge, including MQR, consistently delivers superior results across various base models (Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, Qwen2.5-3B, DeepSeek-Math-7B), underscoring its broad generalizability.
 - **Data Quality vs. Volume:** MQR-augmented data consistently outperforms training on original data (even when scaled for equivalent computational budget), confirming that its effectiveness stems from qualitative enhancement, not just increased volume.
 - **Difficulty Assessment:** Direct evaluation confirms MQR-reformulated questions are indeed harder (e.g., Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct accuracy on Original: 79.77% vs. Sub-Problem: 72.04%).
 - **Ablation Studies on MQR:** Each of the three reformulation strategies (Background, Term, Sub-Problem) individually improves performance over baselines (Original and MetaMath-Rephrasing). Their combination in full MQR achieves the highest score (42.17%), demonstrating synergy.
 - **Training Dynamics:** MQR-augmented data initially shows lower training accuracy, but ultimately leads to superior evaluation accuracy ("train harder, test better"), indicating robust and generalizable reasoning capabilities.
 - **Generality of Reformulators:** Even less capable, open-source models (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking) can effectively generate challenging reformulations that significantly improve performance, making MQR broadly applicable.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MathForge framework represents a significant advancement in the field of mathematical reasoning for Large Language Models (LLMs) by offering a principled and highly effective approach to learning from challenging examples. Its contributions are substantial, with broad implications for future AI development:

1. **Principled Approach to Difficulty-Aware Learning:** The core significance lies in rigorously formalizing and addressing the neglected importance of question difficulty in reinforcement learning for reasoning tasks. By demonstrating that "harder is better," the paper provides a new guiding principle for designing more effective training regimes.

2. **Fundamental Algorithmic Correction:** DGPO rectifies a critical, mathematically proven imbalance in GRPO, a foundational algorithm for RLVR. By replacing standard deviation with mean absolute deviation, DGAE ensures that all valid questions contribute meaningfully to policy updates, rather than having their impact diminished due to extreme ease or difficulty. This is a fundamental improvement that can make GRPO and its variants more robust and efficient.

3. **Generalizable Algorithmic Enhancement:** DGPO is not merely a standalone algorithm but a general enhancement that is compatible with and improves other leading policy optimization methods (GPG, DAPO, GSPO). This demonstrates its versatility and potential to serve as a modular component in a wide array of RL systems for reasoning. Its applicability also extends beyond text-only tasks to multimodal domains, further broadening its potential impact.

4. **Novel and Practical Data Augmentation:** MQR introduces an innovative strategy for generating more challenging training data. Crucially, it achieves this by *reformulating existing questions while preserving their original gold answers*. This circumvents the immense challenge and quality control issues associated with generating new, complex problem-solution pairs, making it highly practical and cost-effective. The multi-aspect reformulation strategies (story background, abstract terminology, nested sub-problems) systematically target distinct reasoning skills, leading to more comprehensive model development.

5. **Powerful Synergistic Framework:** The synergy between DGPO and MQR is a key highlight. MQR provides the "harder" training data that exposes model weaknesses, and DGPO provides the "better" learning mechanism to effectively absorb lessons from these challenges. This combined data-centric and algorithmic approach offers a more holistic solution than advancements in either domain alone.

6. **Enhanced Model Robustness and Generalization:** The "train harder, test better" phenomenon observed with MQR-augmented data suggests that confronting more complex problem formulations leads to models with more robust, less brittle reasoning capabilities that generalize better to unseen problems. This is vital for deploying LLMs in real-world applications requiring high-stakes accuracy.

7. **Ethical Considerations and Reproducibility:** The commitment to the ICLR Code of Ethics, public availability of code and augmented data, and detailed reproducibility statements reinforce the scientific rigor and trustworthiness of the research, facilitating further exploration and building upon these findings.

In conclusion, MathForge provides a comprehensive and empirically validated framework for significantly boosting mathematical reasoning in LLMs. By explicitly embracing the principle that learning from harder, well-chosen examples is more effective, this work paves the way for the development of more intelligent, robust, and versatile AI systems capable of tackling increasingly complex analytical challenges.
