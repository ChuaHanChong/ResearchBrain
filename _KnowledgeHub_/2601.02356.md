# Talk2Move: Reinforcement Learning for Text-Instructed Object-Level Geometric Transformation in Scenes

> [!summary] Summary
> TALK2MOVE, developed by researchers at AWS Agentic AI, introduces a reinforcement learning framework for precise, text-instructed geometric transformations of objects within scenes. This method leverages flow-based Group Relative Policy Optimization and spatially grounded reward models, outperforming state-of-the-art models in translation, rotation, and resizing accuracy while maintaining scene coherence.

## Problem

- Existing graphics-based editing tools require significant human intervention and specialized expertise for object manipulation.
- General text-based image editing models struggle with precise geometric commands (e.g., 'rotate 90 degrees') and disentangling specific objects from the background.
- Supervised learning for fine-grained geometric transformations is hampered by the scarcity of high-quality paired 'before-and-after' training data.
- Reinforcement learning approaches for diffusion models are often computationally expensive due to dense rollout generation.

## Method

- The work formulates text-guided geometric object transformation as a reinforcement learning problem within a diffusion framework, utilizing ==flow-based Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==.
- It introduces ==spatially grounded reward models== that use specialist off-the-shelf tools (e.g., object localization, depth estimation, orientation estimation) to provide fine-grained feedback on object displacement, rotation, and scaling.
- The method incorporates =='off-policy step evaluation'== and =='active step sampling'== mechanisms to significantly improve GRPO training efficiency by focusing on the most informative denoising steps.
- A two-stage training pipeline starts with a small SFT 'cold start' using synthesized paired data, followed by extensive GRPO training with input-only data.

## Results

- TALK2MOVE consistently achieved superior performance across translation, rotation, and resizing tasks, outperforming state-of-the-art models like GPT-Image-1, with a **76.67%** translation accuracy and **29.55%** rotation accuracy on synthetic data.
- Human evaluation confirmed the method's superiority, with TALK2MOVE achieving the highest winning rates across all three tasks (**57.50%** translation, **68.75%** rotation, **63.89%** resizing) due to better editing accuracy and scene coherence.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that the active step sampling mechanism reduced training iteration time by **49%** compared to full sampling and improved editing correctness, indicating both efficiency and performance gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formulating geometric object transformation as an RL problem, particularly with GRPO, allows effective learning without extensive paired 'before-and-after' data, addressing a major data scarcity challenge.
> - Spatially grounded reward models, which directly evaluate object-level geometric metrics, are crucial for achieving precise and semantically faithful transformations.
> - Computational efficiency of RL for diffusion models can be significantly improved by identifying and actively sampling the most informative denoising steps, accelerating convergence and performance.
> - A small amount of supervised pre-training (SFT cold start) provides beneficial initial priors, improving the stability and quality of subsequent reinforcement learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visual content generation and editing has seen rapid advancements, largely driven by diffusion models and the integration of large language and vision-language models (LLMs/VLMs). TALK2MOVE positions itself at the intersection of these advancements, specifically addressing a critical gap: **precise, text-instructed, object-level geometric transformation within scenes.**

**Current Landscape and Limitations:**
1. **Graphics-based Editing (2D/3D primitives):** Traditional methods like DragGAN and similar approaches (e.g.,) offer explicit control over objects through points or 3D models. However, they demand significant human intervention, requiring users to specify control points or possess domain expertise in 2D/3D graphics. Furthermore, they are typically data-hungry, relying on vast amounts of high-quality paired data for supervised fine-tuning (SFT), which is scarce and expensive for specific geometric manipulations. These methods also often use pixel-level Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss, which struggles to disentangle foreground objects from complex backgrounds, leading to limited spatial control.
2. **General Text-based Image Editing (Diffusion models, LLM/VLM-connected frameworks):**
 - **Diffusion Models (e.g., Flux, Flux.1 Kontext):** Excelling at general image generation and style/appearance editing, these models often fall short when it comes to fine-grained spatial instructions. Their reliance on text tokenizers limits their ability to interpret and execute precise geometric commands like "move 90 degrees counter-clockwise" or "scale down to 0.5x."
 - **LLM/VLM-connected Frameworks (e.g., Bagel, QwenImageEdit):** These models integrate powerful pre-trained LLMs/VLMs with diffusion decoders to enhance prompt following. While improving semantic alignment, they can still struggle with maintaining visual coherence or executing highly precise spatial manipulations, particularly if they primarily rely on ViT-based encoders for visual understanding.
3. **Reinforcement Learning for Visual Generation:** Recent works have adapted Reinforcement Learning (RL) to diffusion models, framing the denoising process as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) (e.g., DDPO, FlowGRPO, DanceGRPO, MixGRPO). These methods enable models to learn from reward signals, shifting the generation process towards desired outcomes without explicit paired data. However, existing RL-based approaches for image generation or editing are often general-purpose and do not specifically focus on the unique challenges of object-level geometric transformations. Moreover, they typically face significant computational overhead due due to the dense rollout generation required for GRPO training.

**TALK2MOVE's Contribution to the Landscape:**
TALK2MOVE is the **first to explicitly formulate the text-guided geometric object transformation problem within a reinforcement learning framework.** It directly tackles the limitations of previous methods by:
- **Overcoming Data Scarcity:** By leveraging GRPO, it reduces the dependence on costly, scarce paired data that plagues SFT-based methods.
- **Achieving Geometric Precision:** It introduces novel spatially grounded reward models that move beyond pixel-level or holistic image metrics. These rewards directly evaluate object displacement, rotation, and scaling, providing fine-grained feedback crucial for precise geometric control.
- **Improving RL Efficiency:** It addresses the computational expense of GRPO by introducing "off-policy step evaluation" and "active step sampling," which prioritize informative denoising steps and use shortcuts to accelerate training.

By focusing on an intuitive, natural language interface for precise geometric editing, TALK2MOVE bridges the gap between high-level human intent and low-level pixel manipulation, pushing the boundaries of controllable generative AI. Its industrial origins suggest a strong potential for practical application in areas like digital content creation, virtual reality, and robotics simulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of TALK2MOVE is to enable **precise, consistent, and semantically faithful object-level geometric transformations within existing scenes, guided solely by natural language instructions.** This encompasses manipulating an object's position (translation), orientation (rotation), and size (resizing) while maintaining the integrity and coherence of the surrounding scene.

The motivation behind this work stems from several critical challenges and unmet needs in the current landscape of image editing:

- **Demand for Intuitive Interaction:** Natural language offers the most intuitive and low-barrier interface for users to express their intent for image modifications. Current sophisticated graphics-based tools, while powerful, often require specialized knowledge (e.g., 3D modeling, manual control points), making them inaccessible to general users. The goal is to allow users to simply say "move the mug to the left, onto the laptop" and have the system execute that command accurately.
- **Limitations of Existing Text-based Editing:** While modern text-to-image and image editing models excel at altering style, appearance, or generating entirely new content, they largely struggle with executing precise geometric commands. Their underlying mechanisms, often based on global text embeddings or pixel-level reconstruction losses, are not designed to disentangle individual objects and apply specific spatial transformations while preserving scene context. This leads to issues like object deformation, lack of precision, or unwanted alterations to the background.
- **Scarcity of Paired Data for Geometric Transformations:** Training models for precise geometric editing typically requires vast datasets of "before" and "after" image pairs, showing an object undergoing a specific translation, rotation, or scaling. Such paired data, especially high-quality and diverse examples for geometric changes, is exceptionally rare and expensive to curate manually or even synthesize at scale. This data scarcity severely hampers the development of supervised fine-tuning (SFT) approaches for this task.
- **Inadequacy of Pixel-level Optimization:** Standard pixel-level loss functions (e.g., MSE) used in many SFT methods struggle to isolate and optimize for object-specific geometric changes. They average errors across the entire image, making it difficult for the model to learn to precisely manipulate an object without affecting the background or other scene elements. This often results in "blurry" or "inconsistent" edits from a geometric perspective.
- **Computational Cost of RL-based Diffusion:** While Reinforcement Learning offers a promising avenue to learn without paired data by optimizing directly for desired outcomes, its application to diffusion models (e.g., GRPO) can be computationally intensive due to the need for numerous rollouts at each denoising step. Improving the efficiency of such training is crucial for practical deployment.

By addressing these motivations, TALK2MOVE aims to deliver a robust, user-friendly, and data-efficient solution for object-level geometric scene editing, unlocking new possibilities for creative content generation and human-AI interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TALK2MOVE proposes a novel reinforcement learning (RL) framework based on flow-based Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to enable text-guided geometric transformations of objects in scenes. Its methodology is characterized by a multi-stage training pipeline, efficient GRPO adaptations, and a sophisticated spatially grounded reward system.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Standardized Transformation Templates

The problem is defined as mapping an input scene image $I$ and a text instruction $T$ to an edited image $I'$. To constrain the complex instruction-action space, the authors use predefined templates for translation, rotation, and resizing. These templates specify:
- **Translation:** Movement direction (e.g., left, right) and target spatial reference (e.g., "on top of the sofa").
- **Rotation:** Axis (x, y, z), direction (clockwise/counter-clockwise), and angle (e.g., 90 degrees). Axes are defined in the object's local coordinate frame.
- **Resizing:** Scaling ratio (e.g., 1.5x, 2x).
These templates provide a structured and verifiable space for learning and evaluation.

#### 3.2. Geometric Transformation Data Curation

Recognizing the scarcity of paired data, the authors design a two-pronged data curation strategy:
- **GRPO-style RL Training Data (Input-only):**
 - **Reference Image Generation:** An LLM generates textual scene descriptions (e.g., "a boy doll sits by the window beside a laptop and a red mug"). Open-source text-to-image models (e.g., Flux.1 Kontext, QwenImageEdit) then synthesize corresponding reference images.
 - **Instruction Generation:** A VLM annotates spatial editing instructions using the predefined templates for each generated image. These instructions are parsed into a structured format for reward computation (e.g., object, movement direction).
 - **Data Efficiency:** The dataset is expanded by varying textual prompts for each reference image, substantially reducing data creation costs compared to paired supervision. They construct 3,200 samples over 800 unique images for translation.
- **Target Image Synthesis for SFT Cold Start (Paired Data):**
 - To provide basic spatial-editing priors, a small amount of high-quality paired data is synthesized.
 - **Translation & Rotation:** An API-based video generation model (e.g., Seedream 3.0) simulates physically plausible object motion. Short videos depicting the desired transformation are generated and filtered for accuracy and consistency, yielding 800 translation pairs and 43 rotation pairs.
 - **Resizing:** Open-source image editing models (e.g., Flux.1 Kontext, QwenImageEdit) synthesize coarse up/down scaling, which are then paired with references and filtered via perceptual matching, resulting in 110 resizing pairs.

#### 3.3. Training Pipeline

TALK2MOVE employs a two-stage training process:
1. **SFT Cold Start:** Lightweight LoRA-based fine-tuning is applied to a diffusion backbone (Qwen-Image-Edit) using the small, synthesized paired dataset. LoRA adapters are inserted into attention, normalization, and linear layers, keeping text encoder, VAE, and ViT backbones frozen. This stage imbues the model with coarse spatial priors, improving stability and quality for subsequent GRPO training.
2. **RL Stage (GRPO Training):**
 - **Flow-based GRPO:** The denoising process of the diffusion model is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). Stochastic perturbations are injected at each ODE step during denoising, creating groups of diverse sampling trajectories (rollouts). The GRPO objective updates the policy parameters based on group-relative advantages derived from these rollouts.

#### 3.4. Efficient GRPO with Early Exit

To mitigate the high computational cost of dense GRPO rollouts, TALK2MOVE introduces novel efficiency mechanisms:
- **Off-policy Step Evaluation:** The method measures the "step importance" by computing the variance of rollout rewards at each denoising step. High reward variance indicates greater exploration potential and learning utility. This allows identification of an "optimal exit step" – the last perturbed step that yields maximum reward variance, signifying the most informative stages for geometric transformation. This calibration is performed efficiently on a small subset of images.
- **Active Step Sampling:** Once the optimal exit step $K$ is identified, ODE shortcuts are taken from this step to the final step $T$. This means policy updates and stochastic perturbations only occur for the first $K$ steps, and the remaining $T-K$ steps are completed deterministically using model predictions. This significantly reduces the total computation from $T(t_{sample} + t_{optim})$ to approximately $K(t_{sample} + t_{optim})$, improving training efficiency without sacrificing performance.

#### 3.5. Spatially Grounded Reward Design

A crucial component for fine-grained geometric control, the reward model moves beyond holistic image metrics. It directly evaluates object-level transformations using off-the-shelf specialist models:
- **Object Localization:** Text-driven segmentation models (e.g., Segment Anything, Grounding DINO) are used to localize the prompted object in both the reference and edited images, providing segmentation masks and bounding boxes.
- **Task-Specific Rewards:**
 - **Translation:** Reward is based on the relative displacement of object centers (following GenEval) and depth estimation (e.g., Moge, Depth Anything) to account for forward/backward movements.
 - **Rotation:** Orient-Anything estimates object orientations, and the reward evaluates alignment with the specified axis, direction, and angle.
 - **Resizing:** Reward is calculated from the normalized size ratio differences of the object's bounding boxes.
These rewards provide fine-grained, interpretable feedback, guiding policy updates towards precise and accurate geometric manipulation in normalized coordinate space.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

TALK2MOVE demonstrates superior performance in text-guided object-level geometric transformation through comprehensive quantitative and qualitative evaluations, including ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluation

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** On both synthetic (100 samples per task) and real-image (85 samples from OpenImages-V6) benchmarks, TALK2MOVE significantly outperforms state-of-the-art image editing models, including diffusion-based Flux-Kontext, unified models like Bagel, the baseline QwenImageEdit, and the proprietary GPT-Image-1.
 - **Translation:** Achieves higher Translation Distance and Accuracy (e.g., 0.6667 distance and 76.67% accuracy on synthetic data vs. 0.5416 and 64.29% for GPT-Image-1, and much lower for others) (Table 1 & 2).
 - **Rotation:** Shows lower Rotation Error and higher Accuracy (e.g., 0.2861 error and 29.55% accuracy on synthetic data vs. 0.4293 and 2.33% for GPT-Image-1) (Table 1 & 2).
 - **Resizing:** Achieves lower Scale Error and higher Accuracy (e.g., 0.3894 error and 9.17% accuracy on synthetic data vs. 0.3501 and 5.08% for GPT-Image-1, with others performing worse) (Table 1 & 2).
- **Human Evaluation:** A user study with 15 experts confirmed TALK2MOVE's superiority, achieving the highest winning rates across all three tasks (57.50% for translation, 68.75% for rotation, 63.89% for resizing), indicating better editing accuracy and scene coherence as perceived by humans (Table 1).

#### 4.2. Qualitative Evaluation

- TALK2MOVE consistently produces more accurate instruction execution and better preserves original scene details compared to baselines (Figure 5). While GPT-Image-1 performs transformations well, it often alters scene details, exhibits warmer tones, and brighter lighting, leading to less fidelity to the original image. Other open-source models often struggle with the precision required for geometric changes.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **SFT vs. RL Contribution:**
 - SFT provides an initial performance boost, but RL on top of an SFT cold-start significantly further improves translation distance and accuracy for both Flux-Kontext and QwenImageEdit backbones (Table 3).
 - **Data Efficiency:** When training data is reduced to 1/10th (∼80 annotated pairs), SFT alone struggles, but RL built upon the small-data SFT checkpoint still achieves comparable performance to the full-data setting, demonstrating the superior data efficiency of the RL approach.
- **Background Scene Preservation:** TALK2MOVE maintains good scene consistency, achieving L1 distances comparable to other state-of-the-art editing models (Table 5). This indicates that object manipulation does not compromise the unedited background. GPT-Image-1 shows notably lower CLIP similarity due to its tendency to heavily refine or alter the entire image.
- **Off-policy Step Evaluation:** The analysis revealed task-specific optimal "exit steps." For translation and resizing, step 4 (out of 10) had maximum reward variance, while for rotation, it was step 10 (Figure 4a). This highlights that different tasks benefit from focusing on different stages of the denoising process for exploration.
- **Active Step Sampling:**
 - Significant **computational efficiency gains**: Reduces overall iteration time by 14% compared to the sliding-window baseline and by 49% compared to full sampling (Table 4).
 - **Improved performance**: Despite using fewer sampled steps, the shortcut strategy achieves higher editing correctness (e.g., 0.6667 Translation Distance and 76.67% Accuracy) compared to full sampling or sliding window, and accelerates convergence (Figure 4b). This suggests that focusing on the most informative steps is beneficial.
- **Spatially Grounded Reward Model:**
 - Comparing VLM-based rewards with specialist-model-derived spatial rewards, the latter provided a more reliable supervision signal. VLM rewards, while improving scores, were often overly optimistic and unstable, leading to higher rotation error and lower accuracy (e.g., 0.3294 error, 11.63% accuracy). In contrast, the spatial reward led to lower error (0.2861) and much higher accuracy (29.55%), demonstrating its critical role in guiding precise spatial manipulation (Figure 4c).

In summary, TALK2MOVE represents a significant leap forward in text-guided geometric image editing, demonstrating superior performance, data efficiency, and computational efficiency, underpinned by novel RL techniques and specialized reward design.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TALK2MOVE introduces a paradigm shift in text-guided image editing, particularly for geometric object transformations, with profound significance and potential impact across various domains.

#### 5.1. Novelty in Formulation

The most significant contribution is the **first formulation of text-guided geometric object transformation as a reinforcement learning problem within a diffusion framework**, specifically utilizing GRPO. This approach deviates from traditional supervised learning (SFT) methods that rely on expensive paired data, opening up new avenues for controllable visual generation.

#### 5.2. Enabling Precise and Intuitive Control

TALK2MOVE empowers users with an intuitive, low-barrier natural language interface to perform highly precise geometric manipulations. This capability is crucial for:
- **Creative Content Generation:** Artists, designers, and marketers can rapidly iterate on visual ideas, adjusting object positions, orientations, and sizes within complex scenes without requiring specialized graphic design software or expertise. This democratizes advanced image editing.
- **Virtual Reality and Gaming:** Developing interactive virtual environments where users can naturally manipulate objects with voice commands or text inputs.
- **Education and Training:** Creating dynamic visual aids where elements can be repositioned or resized on the fly to illustrate concepts.

#### 5.3. Overcoming Data Scarcity through Data Efficiency

By leveraging an RL-based framework, TALK2MOVE drastically reduces the reliance on costly and scarce paired "before-and-after" datasets for geometric transformations. The ability to learn effectively from input-only data and a reward signal is a game-changer, making this approach more scalable and applicable to real-world scenarios where creating diverse, high-quality paired examples is impractical. This addresses a major bottleneck in the development of highly controllable generative models.

#### 5.4. Enhanced Geometric Precision and Semantic Fidelity

The introduction of **spatially grounded reward models** is a critical innovation. Moving beyond generic image-level metrics or pixel-level losses, these rewards explicitly evaluate object displacement, rotation, and scaling. This fine-grained feedback disentangles object behavior from the scene background, leading to:
- **Interpretable Optimization:** The model learns directly to understand and execute geometric commands.
- **Coherent Transformations:** Edits are more precise, consistent, and maintain scene integrity, avoiding unwanted artifacts or alterations to unedited regions.
This level of precision is unmatched by previous text-guided editing systems.

#### 5.5. Improved Computational Efficiency for RL-Diffusion

The proposed "off-policy step evaluation" and "active step sampling" mechanisms significantly enhance the computational efficiency of GRPO training. By focusing on the most informative denoising steps and taking shortcuts, TALK2MOVE makes RL for diffusion models more feasible and less resource-intensive. This innovation can inspire similar efficiency improvements in other RL-based generative models.

#### 5.6. Broader Impact and Future Directions

- **Foundation for Agentic AI:** Developed by AWS Agentic AI, this work directly contributes to building more intelligent and autonomous AI agents. The ability to understand and execute precise spatial commands is fundamental for agents interacting with virtual or physical worlds, for example, in robotics, simulation, or AI assistants capable of visual task execution.
- **Extensibility to Other Generative Frameworks:** The paper suggests that the RL-based recipe can be extended to other generative frameworks like GANs or autoregressive models for verifiable and controllable visual generation. This could spur innovations in areas beyond diffusion models, driving towards a future of highly controllable and semantically aligned generative AI.
- **Robotics and Simulation:** For Amazon Robotics, this research could be instrumental in developing more intuitive interfaces for robot programming, allowing human operators to instruct robots using natural language for object manipulation tasks or to generate diverse training data for robot learning in simulated environments.

In conclusion, TALK2MOVE represents a significant advancement in the field of controllable generative AI, addressing critical limitations in data scarcity, precision, and efficiency. Its implications extend beyond image editing, offering a powerful blueprint for creating more intelligent, intuitive, and versatile AI systems capable of understanding and interacting with our visual world.
