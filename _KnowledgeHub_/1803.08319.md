# Learning to Detect and Track Visible and Occluded Body Joints in a Virtual World

> [!summary] Summary
> THOPA-net is an end-to-end bottom-up network that jointly detects multi-person body joints and links them over short temporal spans in surveillance video. Four branches predict visible-joint heatmaps, occluded-joint heatmaps, part affinity fields, and temporal affinity fields from a 3D-convolution time-linker. Trained only on JTA, a 460,800-frame photorealistic GTA-V dataset with ~10M poses, it reaches 59.3 mAP and 56.0 MOTA on MOT-16.

## Problem

- Multi-person pose methods handle single frames only, ignoring temporal cues, and depend on separate person detectors that degrade badly in cluttered, heavily occluded urban scenes.
- Video pose work applies temporal smoothing or optical flow to a single person, so it does not resolve multiple overlapping people whose limbs mutually occlude across frames.
- No public dataset joins surveillance-scale crowds with pose, tracking identity, occlusion flags, and 3D scale; PoseTrack lacks surveillance context and MOT-16 gives only boxes.

## Method

- **THOPA-net** extends a VGG-19 multi-stage bottom-up pose network with a dedicated occluded-joint heatmap branch, so hallucinated invisible joints are learned separately from visible ones yet share stage-wise features.
- ==Temporal affinity fields==, a new vector-field representation, link the same joint type between consecutive frames; a 3D-convolution time linker with temporal max pooling fuses N frames into one feature tensor.
- **JTA** dataset is captured through a Grand Theft Auto V game mod that scripts pedestrian flows and reads the renderer for 2D/3D joint, identity, and occlusion labels automatically.
- Ground-truth heatmap Gaussian variance is conditioned on the joint's true metric camera distance, giving scale-aware supervision that removes the need for multi-scale input branches.

## Results

- On JTA joint detection, adding the occlusion branch lifts mAP from **50.9** to **56.3** and the full temporal model reaches **59.3** mAP, **92.1** precision, **83.9** F1.
- JTA tracking gives **59.3** MOTA and **63.2** IDF1, beating a Hungarian baseline on the same detections (**57.4** MOTA, **57.3** IDF1) and on DPM detections (**31.5** MOTA).
- On MOT-16 the fine-tuned model scores **56.0** MOTA, above four published online trackers, though **4064** ID switches expose the missing appearance model; PoseTrack transfers using synthetic training alone.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Occluded and visible parts are related but distinct tasks: giving occlusion its own prediction branch improves visible-joint accuracy too, since silhouette context helps both.
> - Photorealistic game engines can substitute for real annotation when labels are otherwise impossible; transfer hinges on image resolution and sharpness, not on shape realism.
> - Temporally consistent detections with many control points make even trivial frame-to-frame association workable, shifting the hard problem to long-term re-identification.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Multi-People Tracking (MPT) is a well-established domain within computer vision. Traditional MPT approaches frequently employ a "tracking by detection" paradigm, where pedestrian detectors identify candidate objects, and a subsequent association mechanism links these detections into temporally consistent trajectories. However, recent discourse in the field has questioned whether detection and tracking should be considered separate or integrated components of the same problem. The observed strong correlation between detection accuracy and tracking performance suggests benefits from an integrated, end-to-end approach, particularly for short-term tracking scenarios.

Existing research in human pose estimation has made significant strides, but many techniques focus on single-person pose estimation and are not directly applicable to videos featuring multiple, often occluding, individuals. Multi-person pose estimation methods have emerged to address varying numbers of people, truncations, and occlusions. However, these often rely on separate person detectors or exhibit limitations in cluttered environments with significant scale variations. Similarly, single-person pose estimation in videos typically leverages temporal smoothing or optical flow but does not extend to scenarios with multiple overlapping subjects.

Online multi-target tracking has also advanced, with methods employing deep learning for appearance features, encoding long-term temporal dependencies, or using spatial-temporal attention mechanisms to mitigate drift due to occlusions. While some early works combined multi-person tracking with graph partitioning approaches, they often did not concurrently address pose estimation or struggled with the complexities of urban scenarios characterized by frequent occlusions, clutter, and scale changes.

A critical limitation across these subfields has been the scarcity of comprehensive publicly available datasets that combine precise pose estimation annotations (including visible and occluded body parts), tracking information, and realistic urban surveillance contexts. Datasets like Penn Action, JHMDB, and YouTube Pose focus on single-person pose. Posetrack offers multi-person pose and tracking but primarily in diverse, non-surveillance contexts. MOT-16 provides challenging urban tracking sequences with occlusions but lacks pose annotations, offering only bounding boxes. This gap in annotated data hinders the development of robust models for integrated multi-person pose estimation and tracking in complex, real-world urban environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the field of multi-people tracking by proposing an integrated, end-to-end solution that jointly performs multi-person pose estimation and short-term tracking, specifically addressing the challenges posed by occluded body parts in crowded urban scenarios. The authors advocate for an approach where detection and short-term tracking are considered two interdependent aspects of a unified problem, providing a foundational layer for more complex, long-term association or re-identification techniques.

The specific motivations and objectives include:

- **Integrated Detection and Tracking:** To develop an end-to-end deep network architecture, named THOPA-net (Temporal Heatmaps and Occlusions based body Part Association), that simultaneously localizes individual body parts of multiple people and associates them over short temporal durations. This aims to leverage modern deep learning capabilities for body part localization while incorporating temporal coherence.
- **Explicit Occlusion Handling:** To implement a mechanism within the network that explicitly addresses occluded body parts. This involves the network's ability to "hallucinate" plausible locations for non-visible joints, leveraging contextual cues to improve overall pose estimation accuracy, particularly in crowded scenes where occlusions are prevalent.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity with Synthetic Data:** To overcome the significant lack of surveillance-style datasets with comprehensive annotations for tracking, body parts, and occlusions. This is achieved by creating a large-scale Computer Graphics (CG) dataset, named JTA (Joint Track Auto), using a photorealistic video game environment. The goal is for this virtual dataset to serve as a high-quality proxy for real-world scenarios, enabling the training of deep models that can generalize to actual footage. The JTA dataset is designed to provide unprecedented detail, including visible/occluded joint labels and 2D/3D tracking coordinates, which are difficult and labor-intensive to obtain in real-world settings.
- **Demonstrating Generalization Capabilities:** To empirically evaluate the generalization potential of a model trained exclusively on synthetic data, assessing its performance on real-world tracking benchmarks and determining the extent to which fine-tuning on real data is necessary for practical application.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes an approach that integrates intra-frame (spatial) and inter-frame (temporal) information to jointly address multi-person pose estimation and tracking.

**JTA Dataset Creation:**
To address the data scarcity, the authors created the JTA (Joint Track Auto) dataset by leveraging the photorealistic video game Grand Theft Auto V. A custom game modification was developed to synchronously interact with the game engine, allowing for manual direction of scenes and the generation of natural pedestrian flows in various urban environments under diverse illumination and viewpoints. This module enabled automatic, precise annotation of 14 body parts (consistent with and) for every pedestrian. Each annotation included a unique identifier for tracking, 2D image coordinates, 3D world coordinates, and crucial occlusion flags (distinguishing between occlusion by objects/other pedestrians and self-occlusion). The dataset comprises 512 Full HD videos (30 seconds each, 30 fps), totaling 460,800 densely annotated frames with 0 to 60 people per frame (averaging over 21), accumulating nearly 10 million annotated body poses. This provides accurate ground truth for visible and occluded joints, tracking, and precise scale information via 3D coordinates.

**THOPA-net Architecture:**
The core of the methodology is THOPA-net, an end-to-end deep network designed for joint pose estimation and tracking.

1. **Feature Extractor:** The initial feature extraction relies on the first 10 layers of VGG-19, pre-trained on the COCO 2016 keypoints dataset.

2. **Single Image Pose Prediction:** This component extends the architecture from with a three-branch multi-stage Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) for individual frames:
 - **Visible Heatmaps (H):** Predicts heatmaps for visible body parts.
 - **Occluded Heatmaps (O):** A dedicated branch to predict heatmaps for occluded body parts, explicitly differentiating from visible parts and leveraging contextual cues.
 - **Part Affinity Fields (PAFs) (P):** Predicts 2D vector fields that encode the orientation and location of limbs, used to spatially link detected body parts belonging to the same person within a single frame.
 Each branch is an iterative predictor that refines predictions across multiple stages, employing intermediate supervision to mitigate the vanishing gradient problem. Information flows across branches, allowing visible and occluded joint detections to be mutually informed. The loss function for each branch is a Sum of Squared Errors (SSE) loss between predictions and ground truth, masked for visible joints to avoid penalizing occluded ones.

3. **Temporal Consistency Branch:** To integrate temporal coherence and achieve joint detection and short-term tracking, THOPA-net adds a fourth branch.
 - The model takes N RGB frames as input. Features extracted by VGG-19 for each frame are concatenated along the temporal dimension.
 - A cascade of 3D convolution blocks processes these spatio-temporal features, capturing temporal patterns and distilling them via temporal max pooling.
 - **Temporal Affinity Fields (TAFs) (T):** This novel branch predicts 2D vector fields that link the *same* body parts across *subsequent* frames (e.g., connecting a person's head at time *t* to their head at time *t-1*). TAFs serve as a proxy for body part motion, enhancing detection accuracy and enabling temporal association.
 - The temporal information from the TAF branch flows into all other branches (visible, occluded, PAFs) after the first stage, acting as a prior for body part estimation and spatial association. The overall objective function includes an additional SSE loss for TAF predictions.

**Training Procedure:**
Ground truth heatmaps (H* and O*) are generated by placing 2D Gaussians at keypoint locations. Critically, the variance (σ) of these Gaussians is dynamically conditioned by the true metric distance of the keypoint from the camera, thereby inducing scale awareness in the network and eliminating the need for multi-scale input processing. Ground truth PAFs (P*) are unit vectors along limb segments. Ground truth TAFs (T*) are 2D vector fields that point from a body part's location at time *t* to its corresponding location at time *t-1*. The network is trained using the ADAM optimizer with a learning rate of 10⁻⁴ and a batch size of 16.

**Spatio-Temporal Multi-Person Joints Association:**
After obtaining heatmaps and affinity fields, discrete joint locations are extracted using non-maxima suppression. An association algorithm then connects these joints into skeletons by maximizing a score that combines spatial coherence (via PAFs) and temporal consistency (via TAFs). For each candidate limb, a line integral is computed along the predicted PAFs and TAFs. The association process iteratively maximizes the overall score, considering both current-frame PAFs and "warped" PAFs from the previous frame (based on the best TAF-derived past joint locations), within a localized search radius to maintain computational efficiency. This allows for constructing complete skeletons that are consistent in both space and short-term time.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on the proprietary JTA dataset and public real-world benchmarks (MOT-16 and PoseTrack) to evaluate both detection and tracking capabilities, as well as the generalization performance of the proposed model trained on synthetic data.

**Experiments on JTA Dataset:**

- **Joints Detection:** An ablation study quantified the contribution of different network components.
 - The "Single Image no occ" model achieved a mean Average Precision (mAP) of 50.9% for joints and an F1-Score of 71.6% for bounding box detection.
 - Adding the explicit "occlusion estimation branch" ("Single Image + occ") improved performance significantly, raising mAP to 56.3% and F1-Score to 78.4%. This improvement was attributed to the network's ability to estimate occluded parts, which in turn simplified the clustering of joints into complete skeletons, even when visually obstructed.
 - The "Complete" THOPA-net model, incorporating the temporal branch, further boosted performance to 59.3% mAP and 83.9% F1-Score. This enhancement was attributed to the TAFs' contribution, which provided short-term temporal consistency and aided in disambiguating joint associations based on target direction in crowded scenes.
 - The THOPA-net (Complete) also outperformed a retrained version of (which lacked scale-aware heatmaps) on JTA, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed architectural modifications and training strategy.

- **Tracking Experiment:** The THOPA-net was evaluated on JTA's test set for tracking performance using Clear MOT metrics.
 - THOPA-net achieved a MOTA (Multiple Object Tracking Accuracy) of 59.3% and an IDF1 score of 63.2%.
 - Compared to a Hungarian-based baseline using THOPA-net's detections (MOTA 57.4%) and DPM detections (MOTA 31.5%), THOPA-net demonstrated competitive tracking performance.
 - The model showed a high number of ID switches (IDs) and fragmentations (FRAG). This was acknowledged by the authors as a consequence of using a relatively simple, frame-by-frame association method that lacks a robust appearance model for re-associating targets after prolonged occlusions and does not employ complex long-term dynamics. The authors suggested that these tracklets could serve as input for subsequent batch data association or re-identification modules.

**Tracking People in Real Data:**

- **MOT-16 Benchmark:** THOPA-net was tested on the MOT-16 Challenge Benchmark, a challenging urban surveillance dataset with varying resolutions.
 - When fine-tuned on automatically annotated real data (using ground truth detections and inferring skeletons), THOPA-net achieved a MOTA of 56.0%.
 - This placed it competitively among published online (causal) trackers on the benchmark, albeit with higher ID switches and fragmentations compared to some state-of-the-art methods that employ strong appearance cues or complex recurrent architectures.
 - The results suggested that providing temporal consistency to target detections, even with a simple association method leveraging multiple control points (joints), can yield satisfactory short-term tracking. The comparison highlighted that the absence of strong appearance models for re-identification was a primary factor for the higher IDs/FRAG.
 - An additional experiment on the MOT-16 training set demonstrated that the model trained solely on JTA generalized well to real images with high resolution and sharpness (e.g., MOT16-09, MOT16-11), but its generalization diminished with decreasing image quality. Fine-tuning, even with a self-annotated dataset, was shown to effectively adapt the features to real data.

- **PoseTrack Dataset:** Evaluation on a subset of PoseTrack (with surveillance-like features) demonstrated the generalization capabilities of THOPA-net when trained *solely on CG data*.
 - The model achieved satisfying MOTA and mAP results on PoseTrack sequences, suggesting that the JTA dataset can be a viable training source for fostering research in joint tracking, especially for urban scenarios where dedicated real datasets for joint tracking are lacking.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant contributions to the fields of multi-person pose estimation and tracking:

- **Novel Dataset (JTA):** The introduction of the JTA dataset is a major contribution. It addresses a critical data gap by providing a massive, highly detailed, and automatically annotated synthetic dataset for multi-person pose estimation and tracking in urban environments. Its comprehensive annotations, including visible and occluded joints, 2D/3D coordinates, and tracking IDs, are difficult to obtain in real-world settings and are expected to facilitate research into challenging scenarios like occlusions and scale variations.
- **End-to-End Architecture (THOPA-net):** The proposed THOPA-net architecture represents an advance in integrating detection and short-term tracking. By jointly learning body part localization and temporal association, it moves beyond traditional tracking-by-detection paradigms.
- **Explicit Occlusion Handling:** The explicit inclusion of an "occluded heatmaps" branch allows the network to reason about and "hallucinate" non-visible body parts. This is particularly impactful for robust tracking in crowded scenes where occlusions are common, improving the completeness and accuracy of pose estimation.
- **Temporal Coherence via TAFs:** The introduction of Temporal Affinity Fields (TAFs) provides a novel and effective mechanism for incorporating short-term temporal consistency directly into the network's output. TAFs act as a proxy for motion, enhancing the disambiguation and association of body parts across frames.
- **Demonstrated Generalization of Synthetic Data:** The research provides empirical evidence that models trained solely on photorealistic synthetic data can exhibit good generalization capabilities to real-world scenarios, particularly when image resolution and sharpness are high. This finding underscores the potential of large-scale synthetic datasets to accelerate deep learning research where real-world data collection and annotation are prohibitive.
- **Foundation for Advanced Tracking:** THOPA-net provides reliable, short-term "tracklets" that can serve as an optimized input for subsequent, more complex batch optimization or re-identification modules. This modularity simplifies the overall multi-object tracking pipeline by providing high-quality, temporally consistent detections.

In conclusion, this work offers both a novel dataset and a sophisticated architecture that collectively push the boundaries of joint multi-person pose estimation and tracking, particularly in challenging urban environments with occlusions. Its emphasis on integrated detection and temporal association, coupled with the potential of synthetic data, is likely to influence future research directions in surveillance, human-computer interaction, and autonomous systems.
