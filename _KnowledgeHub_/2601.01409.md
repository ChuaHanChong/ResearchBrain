# Sampling Strategy Design for Model Predictive Path Integral Control on Legged Robot Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> This study systematically investigates various control sampling strategies for Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control applied to legged robot locomotion. It demonstrates that structured sampling methods, particularly cubic splines with a moderate number of knot points, drastically improve robustness, stability, and convergence speed across diverse and challenging locomotion tasks compared to unstructured sampling.

## Problem

- Standard Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control often generates temporally unsmooth control sequences when applied directly to complex, high-dimensional legged robot systems.
- Unsmooth control signals can lead to unstable behaviors, excessive actuator effort, and inconsistent contact interactions in contact-rich environments.
- A systematic understanding of the comparative advantages and trade-offs among different control parameterization choices for MPPI in dynamic legged locomotion was lacking.

## Method

- A ==Model Predictive Path Integral== (MPPI) whole-body control framework was developed and applied to a Unitree quadruped robot in simulation.
- Multiple sampling strategies were implemented and compared: unstructured (Normal), cubic splines (with 4 and 8 knot points), Bézier curves (with 4 control points), and linear interpolation (with 10 waypoints).
- Strategies were evaluated across three locomotion tasks—flat-ground walking, stair climbing, and large obstacle traversal—using success rate, steps to goal, and computation time as metrics.

## Results

- Structured sampling methods, especially `CubicSpline-k4`, consistently yielded higher success rates (up to **100%**) and faster convergence across all locomotion tasks compared to unstructured sampling (0-**60%** success rate).
- Unstructured (Normal) sampling consistently failed in complex tasks like stair climbing and obstacle traversal, achieving a **0%** success rate.
- `CubicSpline-k4` demonstrated the most favorable balance of performance, robustness, and computational efficiency, achieving **100%** success and fastest convergence in flat-ground and stair climbing, and superior performance in obstacle traversal while maintaining efficient computation times (around 20-23 ms).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Temporally structured sampling significantly enhances the robustness, stability, and convergence speed of MPPI for legged locomotion compared to unstructured sampling, which often fails in complex tasks.
> - An optimal balance between expressiveness and regularization is achieved with a moderate number of knot points in spline parameterizations, avoiding issues of under- or over-parameterization.
> - The choice of sampling strategy fundamentally impacts the controller's ability to manage discontinuous contact events and highly coupled dynamics, which are inherent in challenging environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control is a prominent sampling-based optimal control method recognized for its capability to manage complex, nonlinear, and high-dimensional systems. Its ability to avoid explicit gradient computations, accommodate nonlinear dynamics and non-convex cost functions, and leverage parallel computing makes it suitable for intricate systems like legged robots. However, direct application of standard MPPI to legged locomotion systems encounters challenges, primarily due to the temporal unsmoothness of control sequences generated by independent sampling at each time step. This can lead to unstable behaviors, high actuator effort, and inconsistent contact interactions in contact-rich environments.

Previous research has explored structured control parameterizations, such as splines or basis functions, within sampling-based and Model Predictive Control (MPC) frameworks to enhance smoothness and sample efficiency. Notable work has demonstrated MPPI implementations on legged robots using cubic spline parameterization to reduce sampling dimensionality and enforce temporal smoothness, thereby enabling stable whole-body control.

This paper contributes to this landscape by systematically investigating and comparing multiple sampling strategies within the MPPI framework specifically for legged robot locomotion. It extends beyond using a single structured representation by contrasting unstructured sampling with various structured approaches, including cubic splines, Bézier curves, and linear interpolation. The work aims to quantify the impact of these different sampling designs on key performance indicators for legged robot tasks, thereby providing insights into the practical considerations for deploying MPPI on such complex systems. It addresses a gap in understanding the comparative advantages and trade-offs of different control parameterization choices for MPPI in the challenging domain of dynamic legged locomotion.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically investigate the role of sampling strategy design within the Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) framework when applied to legged robot locomotion. This investigation is driven by the recognition that while MPPI offers significant advantages for complex, nonlinear, and high-dimensional systems, its direct application to legged robots is hindered by issues arising from the control sampling process.

Specifically, the key motivations and objectives include:

- **Addressing Control Smoothness and Stability Issues:** Standard MPPI typically samples control inputs independently at each time step, which often generates temporally uncorrelated and non-smooth control sequences. For legged robots operating in environments with significant ground contact and hybrid dynamics, such non-smooth signals can induce unstable behaviors, demand excessive actuator effort, and compromise contact consistency. The paper aims to explore how structured sampling strategies can mitigate these issues by enforcing temporal coherence and smoothness in the control outputs.
- **Improving Sample Efficiency:** Sampling-based methods like MPPI rely on a multitude of trajectory rollouts to estimate optimal control actions. The dimensionality of the sampling space directly affects the number of samples required for effective exploration and convergence. By exploring structured control parameterizations that reduce the effective dimensionality of the sampling space (e.g., through knot points or control points instead of every time step), the research aims to enhance the sample efficiency of MPPI for legged robot control.
- **Quantifying Impact on Task Performance and Robustness:** The study seeks to evaluate how different sampling strategies influence the robot's ability to successfully complete various locomotion tasks, its robustness to environmental complexities, and its overall control performance. This involves analyzing metrics such as success rate, convergence speed (steps to goal), and control variability across different terrains and maneuver types (e.g., flat ground, stair climbing, obstacle traversal).
- **Providing Practical Insights for MPPI Deployment:** Ultimately, the research is motivated by the need to develop practical guidelines for implementing MPPI on real-world legged robot platforms. By systematically comparing multiple structured and unstructured sampling approaches, the authors aim to identify which strategies offer the most favorable balance of smoothness, performance, robustness, and computational efficiency, thereby informing future deployments of MPPI for complex robotic systems.
- **Building upon Prior Structured Control Parameterization Work:** The research is explicitly inspired by prior work that utilized cubic spline parameterization within MPPI for legged robots. This paper expands on that foundation by conducting a comparative analysis of cubic splines alongside other structured parameterizations (Bézier curves, linear interpolation) and a baseline unstructured approach to comprehensively understand their respective merits and limitations.

In essence, the paper aims to isolate and quantify the impact of sampling structure on MPPI performance for legged locomotion tasks, moving beyond the simple adoption of a single structured method to a comparative investigation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research systematically investigates the impact of different sampling strategies within the Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) framework on a legged robot platform.

**A. Experimental Platform and Tasks:**
All experiments were conducted in simulation using a Unitree quadruped robot model. This robot is equipped with torque-controlled joints and onboard state estimation, providing full-state feedback for whole-body control. The performance of the sampling strategies was evaluated across three representative locomotion scenarios of increasing complexity:
1. **Flat-ground walking:** A baseline task involving steady-state locomotion on level terrain to assess nominal tracking performance and control smoothness.
2. **Stair climbing:** A task requiring traversal of a staircase with discrete height changes, demanding precise foot placement and coordinated whole-body motion over a longer horizon.
3. **Large obstacle (box) traversal:** A task involving stepping over a tall box obstacle, which necessitates abrupt changes in contact configuration and expressive, highly coordinated control trajectories within a shorter horizon.

For a fair comparison, all sampling strategies were evaluated under identical task settings, cost functions, control constraints, system dynamics model, temperature parameter, planning horizon, number of rollouts, and control frequency.

**B. Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) Control Framework:**
The work utilizes an MPPI-based whole-body control framework, building upon prior research (specifically) that applied MPPI directly to joint-space control of legged robots. The core MPPI algorithm operates in a receding-horizon fashion:
- **Nominal Control Sequence:** A nominal control sequence ($\bar{u}_{0:H-1}$) is maintained over a prediction horizon $H$.
- **Stochastic Rollouts:** For each control iteration, $N$ stochastic control trajectories are generated by adding Gaussian noise ($\epsilon^{(k)}_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma)$) to the nominal control sequence: $u^{(k)}_t = \bar{u}_t + \epsilon^{(k)}_t$.
- **Trajectory Simulation and Cost Evaluation:** Each perturbed trajectory $u^{(k)}$ is simulated forward using the system dynamics to obtain a state trajectory $x^{(k)}$. A cumulative cost $S^{(k)} = \sum_{t=0}^{H-1} \ell(x^{(k)}_t, u^{(k)}_t) + \phi(x^{(k)}_H)$ is computed, where $\ell$ is the running cost and $\phi$ is the terminal cost.
- **Nominal Control Update:** The nominal control sequence is updated using a cost-weighted average of the noise realizations: $\bar{u}_t \leftarrow \bar{u}_t + \sum_{k=1}^N w^{(k)} \epsilon^{(k)}_t$, where $w^{(k)}$ are importance weights derived from the exponential of the negative trajectory costs, scaled by a temperature parameter $\lambda$.
- **Receding Horizon:** The first control input of the updated sequence is executed, and the horizon is shifted.

**C. Implemented Sampling Strategies:**
The paper's novelty lies in comparing how the stochastic perturbations are applied and how the full control trajectories are reconstructed. The following strategies were investigated:

1. **Normal (Unstructured) Sampling:** This serves as a baseline, where Gaussian noise is independently sampled and applied to each control input at every time step across the horizon. This method does not impose any explicit temporal structure.
2. **Cubic Spline Sampling:** Control values are sampled at a small number of uniformly spaced knot points ($K < H$). Gaussian noise is applied to these knot points. The full control trajectory is then reconstructed by interpolating these perturbed knot values using cubic splines, enforcing C2 continuity. This reduces the sampling dimension from $H \times m$ to $K \times m$ (where $m$ is action dimension) and ensures smoothness. Two configurations were tested: `CubicSpline-k4` (4 knot points) and `CubicSpline-k8` (8 knot points).
3. **Bézier Curve Sampling:** Similar to splines, this method perturbs a reduced set of control points ($K$). Gaussian noise is applied to these control points. The complete control trajectory is then reconstructed using Bernstein polynomial basis functions. This provides a global parameterization, reduces sampling dimension to $K \times m$, and guarantees trajectory confinement within the convex hull of control points. One configuration was tested: `Bezier-cp4` (4 control points).
4. **Linear Interpolation–Based Sampling:** As a low-complexity structured baseline, control values are sampled at $K$ uniformly distributed waypoints. Gaussian noise is applied to these waypoints, and the full control trajectory is reconstructed via piecewise linear interpolation between adjacent perturbed waypoints. This reduces the stochastic sampling dimension to $K \times m$ but provides less smoothness (only C0 continuity) than spline-based methods. One configuration was tested: `LinearInterp-w10` (10 waypoints).

**D. Performance Metrics:**
For each task and sampling strategy, five independent trials were conducted. A trial was successful if the robot reached the target position without falling or violating constraints. The following metrics were recorded:
- **Success rate:** Percentage of successful trials.
- **Steps to goal:** Number of control steps required to reach the target, averaged over successful trials.
- **Computation time:** Average wall-clock time per MPPI iteration, measured on the same hardware.

These metrics collectively characterize the robustness, control efficiency, and computational cost, which are crucial for real-time whole-body control of legged robots.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The simulation results provide a comparative evaluation of the different control sampling parameterizations within the MPPI framework, highlighting their impact on task performance, robustness, and efficiency across various locomotion scenarios.

**A. Flat-Ground Walking:**
- **Baseline (Normal) Performance:** The unstructured MPPI with independent Gaussian sampling (Normal) achieved a 60% success rate. It exhibited the slowest convergence, requiring an average of 1358.0 ± 571.8 steps, and showed high variance, indicating unstable rollouts.
- **Structured Sampling Improvement:** Structured methods consistently improved robustness and convergence.
 - `CubicSpline-k4` achieved a 100% success rate and the fastest convergence (444.0 ± 30.2 steps), demonstrating an effective balance of expressiveness and regularization.
 - `CubicSpline-k8` (higher dimensionality) saw its success rate drop to 80% and increased variance (687.0 ± 661.0 steps), suggesting potential over-parameterization issues for a given sampling budget.
 - `Bezier-cp4` and `LinearInterp-w10` both achieved 100% success rates but required more steps (1264.0 ± 163.8 and 1125.4 ± 174.7 respectively) than `CubicSpline-k4`.
- **Computational Efficiency:** `LinearInterp-w10` was the most computationally efficient (21.14 ± 4.17 ms per step), approximately half the time of spline-based approaches, but at the cost of slower convergence. Spline-based methods (`CubicSpline-k4`, `CubicSpline-k8`) had similar computation times around 40 ms.

**B. Stair Climbing:**
- **Failure of Unstructured and High-Cost Structured Methods:** Both `Normal` sampling and `Bezier-cp4` failed entirely, achieving a 0% success rate, consistently reaching the maximum episode length (4000 steps). `Bezier-cp4` also incurred the highest computational cost. This indicates that lack of temporal structure or overly complex global parameterization can be detrimental for long-horizon, coordinated tasks.
- **Superiority of Spline-Based Sampling:** `CubicSpline-k4` and `CubicSpline-k8` both achieved 100% success rates.
 - `CubicSpline-k4` converged slightly faster (1010.6 ± 232.8 steps) with lower variance than `CubicSpline-k8` (1031.6 ± 332.4 steps).
 - These results emphasize that moderate-dimensional spline parameterizations provide sufficient expressiveness and temporal consistency for complex maneuvers like stair climbing.
- **Partial Success of Linear Interpolation:** `LinearInterp-w10` achieved a 60% success rate but required significantly more steps (2817.2 ± 1032.3) and showed high variability, suggesting its piecewise-linear structure was often insufficient for reliable stair climbing despite lower computation time.

**C. Big Box Obstacle Traversal:**
- **Failure of Unstructured Sampling:** `Normal` sampling again resulted in 0% success, consistently failing to produce coordinated motions to clear the obstacle.
- **Cubic Spline Performance:**
 - `CubicSpline-k4` demonstrated the best overall performance, achieving a 100% success rate and the fewest steps (955.4 ± 739.8). The variance was higher than flat-ground walking, which is attributed to the discontinuous contact events inherent in obstacle traversal. It remained computationally efficient (23.25 ± 5.01 ms).
 - `CubicSpline-k8` achieved an 80% success rate and required more steps (1332.4 ± 1379.3), reinforcing the observation that higher dimensionality can reduce robustness for short-horizon, contact-rich maneuvers.
- **Other Structured Methods:** `LinearInterp-w10` had a 60% success rate with high variance, indicating inconsistent obstacle negotiation. `Bezier-cp4` performed the worst among structured approaches, succeeding in only 40% of trials and often failing to generate necessary aggressive or timed motions.

**D. Simulation Summary:**
The comprehensive simulation results demonstrate that the sampling parameterization within MPPI significantly impacts task performance and robustness across diverse locomotion scenarios. Structured sampling strategies, particularly cubic splines with a moderate number of knot points (e.g., `CubicSpline-k4`), consistently yielded lower task costs, improved tracking accuracy, and reduced control variability, especially in contact-rich and highly nonlinear dynamic environments. These strategies also showed superior sample efficiency, enabling more stable behaviors without increasing computational budget. The findings suggest that informed sampling design, focusing on structured or low-dimensional sampling spaces, is crucial for effectively deploying MPPI on complex legged robotic systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research highlights the critical importance of sampling strategy design in the Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) framework for achieving effective and robust locomotion in legged robots. The findings underscore that while MPPI offers a versatile stochastic optimal control formulation, its practical efficacy on complex, contact-rich systems is highly dependent on how control perturbations are parameterized and how trajectories are reconstructed.

The key significance and potential impact areas include:

- **Advancing MPPI for Legged Locomotion:** The study systematically demonstrates that unstructured sampling in MPPI is often insufficient for demanding legged locomotion tasks, leading to instability, high variability, and failure. In contrast, structured sampling strategies, particularly cubic splines, significantly improve success rates, convergence speed, and control smoothness. This provides a clear direction for researchers and practitioners in applying MPPI to legged robots, suggesting that intelligent parameterization of the control space is not merely beneficial but often essential for success.
- **Guiding Control System Design:** The comparative analysis offers practical guidelines for selecting appropriate sampling strategies. The results suggest that moderate-dimensional spline parameterizations strike a favorable balance between expressiveness (required for complex maneuvers) and regularization (ensuring smoothness and stability). This can inform the design of more robust and efficient MPPI-based controllers for quadruped and bipedal robots, reducing trial-and-error in implementation.
- **Improving Sample Efficiency and Computational Performance:** By demonstrating that structured sampling can achieve superior performance with fewer effective sampling dimensions, the research points towards more sample-efficient MPPI implementations. This can translate into reduced computational demands, enabling faster control rates or longer prediction horizons, which are critical for real-time control on embedded hardware with limited resources. The identified trade-offs between computational cost and convergence speed (e.g., `LinearInterp-w10` being fast but less robust for complex tasks) provide further valuable insights for system optimization.
- **Enhancing Robustness in Complex Environments:** The improved performance of structured sampling in challenging environments like stair climbing and obstacle traversal indicates a pathway to developing more robust legged robot controllers. By generating smoother and more coherent control trajectories, these methods can better handle the discontinuous contact events and highly coupled dynamics inherent in such tasks, leading to more reliable navigation and manipulation capabilities.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper explicitly sets the stage for future work, encouraging broader investigations into how sampling parameterizations affect the exploration-exploitation trade-off, convergence properties, and computational efficiency of stochastic optimal control algorithms. It also motivates the exploration of adaptive and learning-based sampling mechanisms, which could further optimize performance by adjusting strategies online based on task requirements or observed dynamics. This could lead to more intelligent and autonomous robotic systems.

In summary, this research moves beyond validating a single structured sampling approach by providing a systematic comparative analysis, yielding concrete evidence for the necessity and benefits of informed sampling strategy design in MPPI for legged robot locomotion. Its findings are poised to impact the development of more capable, robust, and efficient control systems for advanced robotic platforms.
