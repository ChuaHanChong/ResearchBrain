# DeCAL: Towards Physically-Grounded Dexterous Vision-Language-Action Models via Contact-Aware Latent Co-Imagination

> [!summary] Summary
> DeCAL is a physically-grounded dexterous vision-language-action model built on a Mixture-of-Transformers with separate understanding, generation, and action experts. It adds contact-aware gating for adaptive visuo-tactile fusion plus joint visual-tactile latent co-imagination that predicts future interaction dynamics. On six real-world contact-rich dexterous tasks it reaches 71% average success rate and 83.4% progress success rate at 0.27s per action chunk.

## Problem

- Vision-only VLA policies fail on contact-rich dexterous manipulation because multi-fingered hands cause severe self-occlusion and visual input alone leaves contact forces physically unobservable.
- Existing tactile-equipped policies treat touch as an auxiliary signal and fuse modalities homogeneously, so constant tactile injection perturbs visual attention and weakens visual grounding.
- Prior visuo-tactile methods are largely reactive, lacking explicit modeling of future interaction dynamics, which limits reasoning about evolving contact transitions during fine-grained manipulation.

## Method

- **DeCAL** uses a ==Mixture-of-Transformers== with understanding (Qwen3-VL), generation, and action experts linked by blockwise ==Cross-Modal Joint Attention== propagating information unidirectionally from understanding to generation to action.
- ==Adaptive Visuo-Tactile Fusion==: per-block cross-attention with fingertip deform-map tokens as keys and values, scaled by a ==Contact-Aware Gate== derived from a global tactile token.
- ==Visuo-Tactile Latent Co-Imagination== predicts future Cosmos-VAE visual latents and force-conditioned tactile latents decoded to images, deform maps, 6-DoF forces; ==Factorized Flow Matching== decouples arm and hand actions.

## Results

- Averaged over six real-world tasks DeCAL reaches **71%** success and **83.4%** progress rate, beating the strongest baseline by more than **15 points** at **0.27s** per action chunk.
- Under out-of-distribution shifts on Twist Cap, DeCAL scores **75%** on unseen objects and **70%** on unseen lighting, versus **45%** and **55%** for the tactile baselines.
- Ablations on Assemble Parts fall from **65%** to **25%** without Factorized Flow Matching, to **20%** without both latent generation branches, and to **50%** without tactile gating.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tactile signal helps only when gated: contact is intermittent, so always-on fusion biases action generation and corrupts visual grounding.
> - Predicting future contact alongside future pixels improves both; tactile conditioning also raised future-frame generation quality over a vision-only MoT baseline.
> - Arm and hand dynamics differ enough that a shared denoising process degrades coordination; factorizing the action head gave the single largest ablation gain.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which leverage large-scale pre-trained foundation models to achieve capabilities in visual perception and semantic understanding. Recent efforts have extended these VLA models to dexterous manipulation tasks, often relying on large-scale egocentric human demonstrations. These approaches have shown promise in multi-fingered manipulation.

However, existing VLA models primarily remain vision-centric. This reliance on visual inputs alone presents limitations in scenarios involving contact-rich and fine-grained interactions. Challenges such as severe visual occlusions, complex contact dynamics, and limited physical observability from visual inputs hinder the performance of these models in tasks requiring close physical interaction.

To mitigate these limitations, researchers have explored incorporating tactile feedback. Tactile sensing provides direct access to crucial physical interaction signals, including contact state, force variation, slip, and object deformation, which are essential for dexterous manipulation. While some studies have integrated tactile sensing to improve contact awareness and manipulation robustness, most existing approaches treat tactile inputs as auxiliary signals, employing homogeneous multimodal fusion. This often results in a lack of adaptive tactile interaction modeling and limited capacity for explicitly modeling future interactions or reasoning about evolving contact transitions.

This work, DeCAL, positions itself within this landscape by addressing these identified gaps. It aims to develop a physically-grounded VLA model that effectively leverages tactile signals by moving beyond auxiliary fusion. The paper seeks to provide adaptive tactile integration and explicit modeling of future visuo-tactile dynamics within a unified VLA framework, thereby enhancing the policy's understanding of physical interactions and improving performance in contact-rich dexterous manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

Dexterous manipulation tasks, which are prevalent in human daily life, involve intricate contact-rich and fine-grained interactions with the physical environment. Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models face considerable difficulties in these scenarios. A primary motivation for this research is to overcome the limitations of existing VLA models, which struggle with contact-rich tasks due to factors such as severe visual occlusions, complex contact dynamics, and insufficient physical observability provided by visual inputs alone.

Furthermore, while tactile sensing has been introduced to enhance robotic manipulation, existing methods often treat tactile information as a secondary input, performing homogeneous multimodal fusion. This approach typically lacks adaptive integration of tactile signals and explicit modeling of the physical dynamics governing interactions. Consequently, current systems tend to be reactive, with limited foresight into how future interactions will unfold, which is critical for successful dexterous manipulation.

To address these challenges, the authors outline the following key objectives for the DeCAL framework:

1. **Develop a Physically-Grounded Dexterous VLA Model**: The primary objective is to create a unified framework that integrates understanding, imagination, and action generation specifically for contact-rich dexterous manipulation. This model is designed to be "physically-grounded" by incorporating direct physical interaction signals.
2. **Introduce Adaptive Visuo-Tactile Fusion**: A core aim is to enable the model to dynamically regulate the importance of tactile interactions. This involves implementing a contact-aware gating strategy that allows the policy to selectively emphasize tactile cues when physical interactions are occurring, and reduce their influence otherwise.
3. **Propose Visuo-Tactile Latent Co-Imagination**: The research aims to explicitly model future interactions by jointly predicting visual and tactile dynamics in a latent space. This "co-imagination" capability is intended to equip the policy with implicit knowledge of the physical world, enabling it to anticipate the outcomes of its actions and reason about evolving contact transitions.

By pursuing these objectives, the research seeks to enhance the robustness, precision, and generalization capabilities of VLA models in complex dexterous manipulation tasks that demand a fine-grained understanding of physical contact.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DeCAL is presented as a unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework designed for physically-grounded dexterous manipulation. Its primary goal is to predict an action chunk while jointly maximizing the likelihood of future visuo-tactile latent representations and future actions, conditioned on current multimodal observations and language instructions.

**Robot System Setup:**
The hardware consists of a pair of 6-DoF UR5 robotic arms and two 22-DoF SharpaWave five-fingered dexterous hands. Visual observations are captured from two wrist-mounted cameras and one egocentric camera, all utilizing Intel RealSense D435 sensors. Each fingertip is equipped with a high-resolution (320 × 240) vision-based tactile sensor, which provides three forms of tactile signals: raw tactile images (R), 6-DoF net force (F), and deformation maps (M). Demonstrations are collected via a teleoperation system using MetaGlove Pro and VIVE Trackers.

**Model Architecture: Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)**
DeCAL adopts a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture comprising three specialized, collaborative experts:

- **Understanding Expert**: Built upon Qwen3-VL, this expert processes language instructions and multi-view visual observations, encoding them into textual and visual tokens. It integrates tactile observations via a dedicated cross-attention mechanism, where tactile features serve as keys and values, and visual-language features act as queries. This produces contextual embeddings for joint reasoning across modalities.
- **Generation Expert**: This expert explicitly models visuo-tactile dynamics by predicting future visual and tactile observations. It utilizes a parallel decoding strategy, which is non-autoregressive, to maintain computational efficiency required for real-time robotic control. The Qwen3 model serves as its backbone.
- **Action Expert**: Conditioned on the latent features from the understanding and generation experts, along with robot proprioceptive states, this expert predicts an action chunk using a flow matching objective. It also employs Qwen3 as its backbone.

**Factorized Flow Matching:**
To address the distinct dynamics of arm and hand motions, the action expert incorporates Factorized Flow Matching. This mechanism explicitly decouples the generation of arm and hand motions. Actions are initialized from independent noise distributions, projected into separate token sequences, processed jointly by transformer blocks for coordination, and then decoded into clean action trajectories.

**Cross-Modal Joint Attention:**
Information flow among the experts is managed through a blockwise attention mask. Data propagates unidirectionally from the understanding expert to the generation expert, and subsequently to the action expert. Within the understanding and generation experts, tokens are bidirectionally attended. The action expert's state tokens can attend to themselves and all preceding blocks, while action tokens can attend to themselves, state tokens, and all preceding blocks, ensuring full conditioning on understanding, imagined future interactions, and robot states.

**Adaptive Visuo-Tactile Fusion:**
This component facilitates the adaptive integration of tactile information:

- **Tactile Feature Extraction**: Multi-finger deformation maps are used as primary tactile inputs. A shared ResNet-based encoder extracts features from all fingertips, followed by spatial pooling and projection into tactile tokens. A cross-finger transformer captures interactions, yielding local tactile tokens (fine-grained details) and a global tactile token (overall contact state).
- **Adaptive Visuo-Tactile Cross-Attention**: A cross-attention mechanism in each transformer block integrates tactile feedback. Local tactile tokens act as keys and values, while visual-language embeddings serve as queries. The fused output is added residually.
- **Contact-Aware Gating Mechanism**: To prevent continuous tactile input from perturbing visual attention, a gating mechanism dynamically modulates tactile input weights. A gating weight ($\sigma$) is derived from the global tactile token, allowing the policy to selectively emphasize tactile cues only when meaningful physical contact occurs.

**Visuo-Tactile Latent Co-Imagination (within Generation Expert):**
This mechanism models future visuo-tactile dynamics:

- **Tactile Latent Prediction**: The model predicts future 6-DoF contact forces. Forces are mapped to embeddings, processed to produce a tactile latent, which can be decoded into raw images, deformation maps, and 6-DoF force vectors for supervisory training.
- **Visual Latent Prediction**: Future visual latents are generated from historical and current observations using the Cosmos VAE tokenizer. A token compression mechanism (32x32 to 4x4) enhances efficiency, and compressed tokens are decoded in parallel using KV cache.

**Training Objectives (Appendix A.1):**
DeCAL is trained with a combined objective function comprising:
- An L2 loss for visual foresight, comparing predicted visual latents with COSMOS VAE targets.
- A combined reconstruction loss for tactile foresight, supervising predicted raw tactile images, deformation maps, and 6-DoF force vectors.
- A flow matching objective for action prediction, learning a velocity field to transport noisy actions to target actions, conditioned on the understanding and generation expert features.

The total loss is a weighted sum of these three components. During inference, reconstruction decoders for visual and tactile latents are omitted for computational efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of DeCAL was evaluated through extensive real-world experiments on six contact-rich dexterous manipulation tasks: Wipe Vase, Erase Whiteboard, Assemble Parts, Twist Cap, Pipetting, and Screw Light Bulb. Each task involved 100 high-quality teleoperated demonstrations and 20 trials for evaluation. The performance was assessed using Success Rate (SR) and Progress Success Rate (PSR).

**Real-World Task Performance:**
DeCAL consistently achieved high performance across all tasks. As shown in Table 1, it attained an average success rate of 71%, outperforming all baselines, including GR00T N1.6 (56%), InternVLA-A1 (32%), ViTacFormer (34%), DECO (69%), and InternVLA-A1$_t$ (34%). DeCAL also achieved an average progress success rate of 83.4%. The results indicate that while vision-based policies performed adequately on simpler tasks, they struggled with the fine-grained physical interactions inherent in contact-rich manipulation. Naive integration of tactile signals (InternVLA-A1$_t$) did not yield significant improvements and sometimes disturbed visual representations. DeCAL's architecture, incorporating adaptive visuo-tactile fusion and joint visuo-tactile dynamics modeling, demonstrated enhanced physical interaction awareness while maintaining robust visual grounding.

**Tactile Latent Analysis:**
Analysis of the global tactile tokens using t-SNE (Figure 5) revealed distinct clusters corresponding to different contact modes (e.g., twisting, insertion, wiping). This indicates that the tactile encoder effectively captures meaningful and structured physical interaction patterns.

**Adaptive Tactile Gate Effectiveness:**
Visualization of the tactile gate outputs (Figure 6) demonstrated its dynamic behavior. The gate values remained low when the robot hand was not interacting with objects, indicating a visual-dominated policy. However, upon meaningful physical contact, the gate values increased significantly, allowing tactile information to contribute more prominently to action generation.

**Tactile Force Prediction:**
Comparison of predicted 6D tactile forces with ground-truth values (Figure 7) showed a high degree of alignment across different contact stages. This suggests that the visuo-tactile latent co-imagination mechanism effectively models underlying contact dynamics from multimodal observations.

**Generalization Capabilities:**
DeCAL exhibited robust generalization to four out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios: unseen background, cluttered environment, unseen lighting, and unseen object (Figure 8, 9). Taking the Twist Cap task as an example, DeCAL achieved a 75.0% success rate under the unseen object setting, substantially surpassing all baselines. This generalization ability is attributed to the joint modeling of visuo-tactile dynamics, which enables the policy to learn transferable interaction patterns less reliant on specific visual cues.

**Ablation Studies:**
Ablation studies (Table 2) confirmed the contribution of each proposed component:
- **Factorized Flow Matching**: Its removal led to noticeable inconsistencies and poor coordination between arm and hand motions, highlighting its importance for dexterous manipulation.
- **Visual and Tactile Latent Generation**: Policies without these components struggled in contact-sensitive tasks, indicating that future-oriented visuo-tactile prediction provides crucial predictive representations for modeling motion and contact dynamics.
- **Adaptive Tactile Gating Mechanism**: Removing this mechanism reduced the policy's ability to adaptively balance visual-language features, suggesting that it effectively mitigates noise or interference when tactile contact is weak.

**Generation Results:**
DeCAL demonstrated improved future visual generation quality compared to InternVLA-A1 (Table 3, Figure 10), achieving higher Cosmos feature similarity and lower LPIPS scores. The incorporation of tactile feedback and joint visuo-tactile dynamics modeling contributed to more accurate and realistic future-frame predictions. Similarly, generated tactile deformation maps (Figure 13), while not perfectly reconstructing fine details, preserved contact-related information, indicating effective capture of tactile interaction evolution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DeCAL framework represents an advancement in the development of physically-grounded Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for dexterous manipulation. Its primary significance lies in its integrated approach to unifying understanding, imagination, and action generation within a collaborative Mixture-of-Transformers architecture, specifically tailored for contact-rich tasks.

The introduction of **Adaptive Visuo-Tactile Fusion** provides a mechanism for dynamically modulating the influence of tactile feedback based on the relevance of physical contact. This adaptive strategy allows the robot policy to selectively prioritize visual or tactile cues, addressing a limitation of prior approaches that often relied on homogeneous multimodal fusion. This can lead to more robust and context-aware control in complex interaction scenarios.

Furthermore, the concept of **Visuo-Tactile Latent Co-Imagination** enables the model to explicitly forecast future visual and tactile dynamics. By generating predictive latent representations, DeCAL endows the policy with implicit physical world knowledge, allowing it to anticipate interaction outcomes and reason about evolving contact states. This foresight capacity is a crucial component for planning and executing fine-grained dexterous actions that are sensitive to physical contact.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Dexterous Manipulation**: DeCAL's demonstrated state-of-the-art performance and robust generalization across diverse real-world contact-rich tasks indicate its potential to significantly improve the capabilities of dexterous robots in practical applications. Tasks requiring precise physical interaction, such as assembly, delicate object handling, and complex tool use, could benefit from this advancement.
- **More Robust Robot Policies**: By effectively integrating and adaptively leveraging tactile information, DeCAL contributes to the development of more resilient robot policies that can operate reliably even under challenging conditions like visual occlusion or varied environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The framework provides a blueprint for developing more physically-grounded VLA models. The successful integration of explicit dynamics modeling and adaptive multimodal fusion highlights promising directions for future research, particularly in scaling visuo-tactile pretraining and improving sensor robustness.
- **Real-time Operability**: With an average inference latency of 0.27 seconds per action chunk, DeCAL demonstrates efficiency suitable for real-time control, which is essential for dynamic and interactive robotic applications.

The authors acknowledge certain limitations, including the reliance on accurate tactile perception which can be affected by sensor noise, calibration errors, and model drift. The teleoperation system used for data collection also lacked fingertip force feedback to the operator, potentially limiting the quality of tactile-aware demonstrations. Additionally, the current framework does not leverage large-scale visuo-tactile pretraining, an area identified for future research. Addressing these limitations could further enhance the model's capabilities and broader applicability.
