# CRAFT: Counterfactual-to-Interactive Reinforcement Fine-Tuning for Driving Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> CRAFT, a framework from Tsinghua University and Li Auto Inc., refines autonomous driving policies by decomposing closed-loop optimization into a dense counterfactual proxy and a grounded residual correction term. This approach consistently improves driving performance and safety across diverse policy architectures, demonstrating enhanced efficiency and stability compared to existing fine-tuning methods.

## Problem

- Autonomous driving policies trained via open-loop imitation learning suffer from 'distribution shift' when deployed in interactive closed-loop environments, potentially leading to unsafe or unreliable behavior.
- Existing closed-loop reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning methods are sample-inefficient due to sparse, high-variance rewards from rare informative events.
- Counterfactual fine-tuning provides dense supervision but is prone to bias because the simulated 'counterfactual world' cannot perfectly capture complex real-world interaction dynamics.

## Method

- **CRAFT** formulates closed-loop post-training as a ==proxy-residual optimization== problem, combining dense, local feedback with sparse, grounded corrections.
- A ==dense counterfactual proxy== is created by evaluating groups of candidate trajectories in a counterfactual world and normalizing their returns, providing a rich, continuous learning signal.
- Grounded residual correction uses event-driven rewards from real closed-loop rollouts (e.g., collisions, rule violations) to correct biases in the proxy, along with on-policy ==asymmetric KL self-distillation== to stabilize policy adaptation.

## Results

- CRAFT consistently achieved the strongest closed-loop gains in Driving Score (DS) and Success Rate (SR) across three diverse policy architectures: HiP-AD, MindDrive, and SparseDriveV2.
- It outperformed all tested RL fine-tuning baselines (PPO, REINFORCE++, GRPO), showing substantial improvements in safety-critical behaviors such as VRU Interaction, Obstacle Avoidance, and Intersection Turning.
- The framework demonstrated superior training stability and efficient scaling, being the only method to show consistent performance improvement with increasing amounts of fine-tuning data without saturation or instability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The real closed-loop policy gradient can be exactly decomposed into a proxy term and a residual term, allowing for a strategic combination of dense and sparse feedback.
> - Dense counterfactual supervision can efficiently reduce variance in learning signals, while grounded residual approximation specifically corrects for the proxy's bias, especially in interaction-critical scenarios.
> - On-policy self-distillation with asymmetric KL penalties stabilizes fine-tuning, preserving valuable knowledge from the pre-trained policy while enabling adaptation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Modern autonomous driving policy architectures have evolved from traditional modular perception-planning pipelines to unified end-to-end models, vision-language-action (VLA) models, and world action models. While these advanced architectures enhance policy capabilities, their training predominantly relies on an open-loop imitation learning (IL) paradigm. This approach involves training policies on expert demonstrations, which, when deployed in closed-loop scenarios, frequently leads to a "distribution shift." This shift occurs when the policy generates states outside the training distribution, potentially resulting in unsafe or unreliable behavior. Consequently, improving the closed-loop performance of pre-trained driving policies is a critical area of research for real-world deployment.

Current post-training methodologies aimed at mitigating distribution shift typically fall into two categories, each with inherent trade-offs:
1. **Closed-loop Reinforcement Learning (RL) Fine-tuning:** This paradigm involves fine-tuning pre-trained policies within interactive simulators. It provides grounded feedback based on executed actions, which is valuable for learning real-world dynamics. However, informative events (e.g., critical safety incidents, complex interactions) are often sparse across diverse driving scenarios. This sparsity limits training efficiency, making pure closed-loop RL sample-inefficient and prone to high-variance updates, as value estimation can be brittle.
2. **Counterfactual Fine-tuning:** This approach evaluates multiple candidate future trajectories from a given state and derives dense supervisory signals from their relative performance differences, often using group-relative objectives. While offering dense supervision, counterfactual estimates of the future are susceptible to bias. This bias arises because the "counterfactual world" (the simulator or model used for evaluation) cannot perfectly capture the complex interaction dynamics of the real closed-loop environment.

This work, CRAFT, aims to address these fundamental trade-offs by combining the strengths of both paradigms. It seeks to leverage the density of counterfactual supervision while grounding it with real-world feedback from closed-loop interaction, thus providing a more robust and efficient fine-tuning mechanism for autonomous driving policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel on-policy post-training framework, Counterfactual-to-Interactive Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (CRAFT), designed to significantly enhance the closed-loop performance of pre-trained autonomous driving policies. This objective is driven by a detailed understanding of the limitations in existing post-training paradigms:

**Motivation for Addressing Distribution Shift:**
Autonomous driving policies, regardless of their architectural complexity (e.g., hierarchical planning, vision-language-action, vocabulary scoring), are predominantly trained via open-loop imitation learning. This method typically trains policies by mimicking expert behavior in a fixed, recorded dataset. During real-world, closed-loop deployment, the policy's own actions influence future states. If these actions lead to states not well-represented in the training data, the policy may encounter out-of-distribution observations, leading to unpredictable, potentially unsafe, or suboptimal behavior. This phenomenon, known as distribution shift, is a major impediment to the safe and reliable deployment of autonomous driving systems. Therefore, a robust post-training strategy to refine policies in closed-loop environments is crucial.

**Motivation for Combining Strengths of Existing Paradigms:**
The existing post-training paradigms, closed-loop RL and counterfactual fine-tuning, each possess distinct advantages and disadvantages:
- **Closed-loop RL Fine-tuning:**
 - **Advantage:** Provides physically grounded feedback from executed actions, allowing the policy to learn from its own interactions within an interactive simulator.
 - **Disadvantage:** Suffers from sample inefficiency. Informative events (e.g., near-miss collisions, traffic rule violations) are rare in typical driving scenarios, resulting in sparse, high-variance rewards. This sparsity makes it difficult for standard RL algorithms to efficiently learn effective behaviors, especially for safety-critical situations, as a vast amount of interaction data is required to gather meaningful gradient estimates. Value function estimation also becomes brittle due to this variance.
- **Counterfactual Fine-tuning:**
 - **Advantage:** Offers dense supervisory signals by evaluating multiple candidate futures from a single visited state. This approach provides rich, local comparative feedback, which can significantly improve learning efficiency compared to sparse real-world rewards.
 - **Disadvantage:** Inherits bias from imperfect future estimates. The "counterfactual world" used to simulate these candidate futures (e.g., a simplified simulator or a predictive model) may not accurately capture the nuanced interaction dynamics of the real closed-loop environment. This discrepancy can lead to the policy optimizing for behaviors that appear good in the counterfactual world but are suboptimal or unsafe in reality.

**CRAFT's Core Motivation: Proxy-Residual Optimization:**
The central motivation for CRAFT stems from the observation that these two paradigms are complementary. The dense, local feedback from counterfactual evaluation could serve as a broad optimization signal, while sparse, grounded feedback from real closed-loop interactions could correct the biases inherent in the counterfactual estimates. This leads to the key research question: "Can counterfactual supervision serve as a dense proxy, while closed-loop interaction corrects its residual mismatch with the real closed-loop world?"

CRAFT addresses this by formulating closed-loop post-training as a proxy-residual optimization problem. In this framework:
- **Dense Counterfactual Proxy:** Group-normalized counterfactual advantages are used as a dense proxy for real closed-loop advantages. This provides a rich, continuous stream of learning signals over states visited by the current policy.
- **Grounded Residual Correction:** Feedback from interaction-critical events observed during actual closed-loop rollouts is used to provide grounded residual correction. This aligns the potentially biased counterfactual proxy with the reality of the closed-loop world, focusing on situations where the counterfactual model is most likely to be inaccurate (e.g., collisions, rule violations).
- **Stabilization:** To prevent unconstrained policy updates from deviating too far from reliable pre-trained behaviors, CRAFT incorporates asymmetric KL self-distillation, regularizing the online policy towards an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) teacher. This ensures that adaptation is stable and preserves valuable knowledge from the initial policy.

The theoretical underpinnings further motivate this approach by showing that the real closed-loop policy gradient can be decomposed into a proxy term and a residual term under the same visited-state distribution. This decomposition suggests that an aligned proxy can reduce residual variance, while grounded residual approximation can mitigate proxy bias, leading to more efficient and stable learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CRAFT is presented as a general, on-policy framework for closed-loop post-training of supervised driving policies. Its core methodology involves optimizing a surrogate objective composed of a dense counterfactual proxy, a grounded residual correction term, and a self-distillation regularization component. The framework operates on an on-policy state distribution, meaning it continually collects data from the policy it is currently optimizing.

#### 3.1. Real Closed-Loop Objective and Proxy–Residual Decomposition

The framework first formalizes the real closed-loop objective, $J_{real}(\pi_{\theta})$, which represents the expected discounted sum of rewards collected by policy $\pi_{\theta}$ in the real closed-loop environment. The policy gradient of this objective, $\nabla_{\theta} J_{real}(\pi_{\theta})$, involves the real advantage function $A^{real}_{\pi_{\theta}}(s, \tau)$. Direct estimation of this gradient is challenging due to the sparsity of informative events.

CRAFT introduces a dense counterfactual proxy, $\Phi_{\pi_{\theta}}(s, \tau)$, which approximates the real advantage for candidate trajectories. The difference between the real advantage and this proxy is defined as the residual mismatch, $\Delta_{\pi_{\theta}}(s, \tau) = A^{real}_{\pi_{\theta}}(s, \tau) - \Phi_{\pi_{\theta}}(s, \tau)$.
- **Proposition 3.1 (Exact Counterfactual-Residual Decomposition):** This proposition states that the real closed-loop gradient can be exactly decomposed into two terms: one involving the proxy $\Phi_{\pi_{\theta}}(s, \tau)$ and another involving the residual mismatch $\Delta_{\pi_{\theta}}(s, \tau)$. Both terms are defined on the same on-policy state and trajectory distribution, ensuring no state-distribution mismatch is introduced. This decomposition allows for a variance reduction strategy if the proxy is sufficiently aligned with the real advantage.
- **Proposition 3.2 (Conditional Variance Reduction):** This proposition demonstrates that if the proxy is sufficiently correlated with the real advantage (specifically, if $\text{Cov}_{\tau}[Y(s, \tau), C(s, \tau)] > \frac{1}{2} \text{Var}_{\tau}[C(s, \tau)]$), then the variance of the residual term, $\text{Var}_{\tau}[Y(s, \tau) - C(s, \tau)]$, will be less than the variance of the real advantage, $\text{Var}_{\tau}[Y(s, \tau)]$. This implies that the proxy need not be unbiased, only aligned enough to reduce the variance of the learning signal that the residual term needs to estimate.

The overall surrogate objective for CRAFT, based on this decomposition, is:
$\max_{\pi_{\theta}} \mathbb{E}_{s \sim d^{real}_{\pi_{\theta}}} [R_{cp}(s; \pi_{\theta})] + \mathbb{E}_{\xi \sim (\pi_{\theta}, \kappa, P^{real})} [\sum_{t} \gamma^t r^{gr}_t]$
where the first term is the Dense Counterfactual Proxy and the second is the Grounded Residual Correction.

#### 3.2. Dense Counterfactual Proxy

- **Candidate Evaluation:** For each on-policy state $s_i$ collected during closed-loop interaction, a "counterfactual world" is employed to evaluate a group of $G$ candidate trajectories $\{\tau_g\}_{g=1}^G$ over a finite virtual horizon. These evaluations consider efficiency, safety, and rule compliance, yielding a scalar return $\tilde{R}_{i,g}$ for each candidate.
- **Group Normalization:** These raw returns are then converted into local, relative scores, $\tilde{A}_{i,g}$, by normalizing them within each candidate group using their mean and standard deviation (Equation 6). This normalized score serves as the proxy value $\Phi_{\pi_{\theta}}(s_i, \tau_g)$.
- **Objective Function ($L_{cp}$):** The counterfactual objective is formulated as the negative expected sum of the policy's probability of selecting a candidate multiplied by its group-normalized advantage (Equation 7). This provides dense, group-wise comparative supervision at each state.
- **Acknowledged Bias:** The paper notes that biases can still exist due to unrealistic traffic interactions and dynamics in the counterfactual world, which motivates the grounded residual correction.

#### 3.3. Grounded Residual Correction

- **Interaction-Critical Feedback:** Instead of attempting to estimate the full residual mismatch, CRAFT focuses on approximating the interaction-critical components of the residual. This is achieved using grounded feedback from traffic events (e.g., collisions, rule violations) observed in real closed-loop rollouts, which are typically under-represented or inaccurately modeled by the counterfactual proxy.
- **Corrective Rewards and Advantages:** For a selected trajectory $\tau_t$ and its executed action $a_t = \kappa(s_t, \tau_t)$, an event-driven corrective reward $r^{gr}_t$ is computed from the observed closed-loop transition. These rewards are then converted into bounded corrective advantages, $\hat{A}^{gr}_t$.
- **Dual-Clipped Update:** The policy update for the residual correction utilizes an importance sampling ratio $\rho_t(\theta)$ and a dual-clipped objective (Equations 8 and 9). The dual clipping mechanism, with a constant $c > 1$, is crucial for stabilizing learning. It helps preserve informative gradients from rare but catastrophic events (e.g., collisions) by preventing them from dominating the update, while still keeping the overall update bounded.
- **Theorem 3.3 (Gradient Bias under Residual Approximation):** This theorem quantifies the bias introduced when approximating the true residual with $b\Delta_{\pi_{\theta}}(s, \tau)$. It establishes that the norm of the difference between the true and approximate gradients is bounded by the product of the expected norm of the log-policy gradient and the expected difference between the true and approximated residuals. This highlights that grounded feedback focuses on correcting the dominant mismatch, specifically for interaction-sensitive parts of the objective.

#### 3.4. On-Policy Self-Distillation

- **Stabilizing Adaptation:** To prevent the online policy from drifting too far from its reliable pre-trained behavior due to the fine-tuning process, CRAFT employs on-policy self-distillation. An Exponential Moving Average (EMA) teacher policy, $\pi_T$, is maintained (Equation 12). The teacher is initialized from the pre-trained policy and subsequently tracks a smoothed version of the online policy.
- **Asymmetric KL Penalties:** Self-distillation is implemented using asymmetric KL divergence penalties (Equation 13):
 - $L_{dist} = \mathbb{E}_{s \sim d^{real}_{\pi_{\theta}}, \tau \sim \pi_{\theta}(\cdot|s)} [\log \frac{\pi_{\theta}(\tau|s)}{\pi_T(\tau|s)}]$: This reverse KL term acts as the primary constraint, discouraging significant deviations from behaviors supported by the EMA teacher, thus preserving reliable pre-trained knowledge.
 - $L_{KL} = \mathbb{E}_{s \sim d^{real}_{\pi_{\theta}}, \tau \sim \pi_T(\cdot|s)} [\log \frac{\pi_T(\tau|s)}{\pi_{\theta}(\tau|s)}]$: This weaker forward KL term helps prevent the policy from collapsing to a narrow subset of trajectories by encouraging it to explore alternatives that the teacher also supports.
- **Overall Loss:** The final objective function for CRAFT combines all these components with weighting coefficients (Equation 14): $L_{all} = \lambda_{cp}L_{cp} + \lambda_{gr}L_{gr} + \beta_r L_{dist} + \beta_f L_{KL}$.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Benchmark:** Experiments are conducted on Bench2Drive, a CARLA-based benchmark, using a refined protocol (Bench2Drive*) to improve efficiency and reduce noise.
- **Metrics:** Driving Score (DS), Success Rate (SR), and Multi-Ability scores (Route Completion, Infraction Score, and five advanced urban driving skills).
- **Driving Policies:** Three diverse architectures are used to demonstrate generality: HiP-AD (hierarchical planning), MindDrive (vision-language-action), and SparseDriveV2 (vocabulary scoring). Only planning-related modules are fine-tuned.
- **Baselines:** Performance is compared against the pre-trained policy and three RL fine-tuning baselines: PPO (clipped surrogate, learned value baseline), REINFORCE++ (critic-free, batch-normalized return-based updates), and GRPO (group-relative counterfactual rewards).
- **Implementation Details:** CaRL-style reward formulation, RIFT infrastructure for asynchronous rollouts and updates, specific hyperparameter values for all components (Table 5). The counterfactual world uses a batched PID trajectory tracker with a kinematic bicycle model for other agents.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of CRAFT across various dimensions of autonomous driving policy fine-tuning.

#### 4.1. Main Closed-Loop Results (Table 1, Figure 3)

- **Consistent Performance Gains:** CRAFT consistently achieves the strongest closed-loop gains in Driving Score (DS) and Success Rate (SR) across all three diverse policy architectures: hierarchical planning (HiP-AD), vision-language-action (MindDrive), and vocabulary scoring (SparseDriveV2). This suggests the generality of the CRAFT framework, independent of specific model architectures.
 - For HiP-AD, CRAFT improved DS from 73.46 to 75.79 and SR from 53.64% to 56.36%.
 - For MindDrive, CRAFT improved DS from 62.60 to 67.45 and SR from 40.45% to 46.36%.
 - For SparseDriveV2, CRAFT improved DS from 66.43 to 71.10 and SR from 45.45% to 50.45%.
- **Superiority over Baselines:** CRAFT consistently outperforms all tested baselines (REINFORCE++, PPO, GRPO) in aggregate closed-loop metrics. Even GRPO, which shares the concept of group-based counterfactual updates, performs below CRAFT, indicating the crucial role of the additional components in CRAFT.
- **Safety-Critical Improvements:** Fine-grained ability profiles (Figure 3) reveal that CRAFT leads in most specific driving abilities, showing substantial gains in safety-critical behaviors such as VRU Interaction, Obstacle Avoidance, and Intersection Turning. These are abilities that strongly depend on true closed-loop interaction feedback and are less likely to be fully captured by a dense counterfactual proxy alone.

#### 4.2. Ablation and Generalization Analysis (Table 2, Table 3)

- **Ablation Study (Table 2):** The analysis on SparseDriveV2 clarifies the contribution of each CRAFT component:
 - The pre-trained policy (baseline without any CRAFT components) has a DS of 66.43 and SR of 45.45%.
 - Adding self-distillation (SD) alone provides minor improvements (DS 66.49, SR 46.36%).
 - Adding the dense counterfactual proxy (CP) alongside SD (SD+CP) significantly boosts performance (DS 68.40, SR 48.18%), indicating its role in providing broad closed-loop gains.
 - Including grounded residual correction (GR) with SD and CP (full CRAFT) yields the best overall results (DS 71.10, SR 50.45%), particularly improving interaction-related metrics like Infraction Score (IS) and Success Rate (SR). This validates the proxy-residual design, where GR refines the CP's signal with real interaction feedback.
- **Generalization Across Datasets (Table 3):** CRAFT demonstrates positive generalization under scenario shifts. While in-domain fine-tuning on Bench2Drive yields the largest improvements (+4.67 DS, +5.00 SR), cross-scenario transfer to Longest6 v2 and LEAD scenarios still produces consistent positive gains, albeit smaller. A limitation observed is reduced improvement in Route Completion under cross-scenario transfer, suggesting a potentially more conservative policy in unseen scenarios.

#### 4.3. Scaling and Training Dynamics (Figure 4, Figure 5)

- **Scaling Behavior (Figure 4a):** CRAFT is the only method that shows consistent performance improvement with increasing amounts of fine-tuning data (Bench2Drive Scale). Baselines like PPO and REINFORCE++, which rely solely on real interaction, exhibit saturation or instability at larger scales, suggesting inefficient learning from sparse feedback. GRPO, relying only on proxy supervision, shows limited improvement and can even deteriorate with more data, indicating that proxy bias becomes a bottleneck. This highlights that dense proxy supervision is crucial for efficient scaling, while grounded residual correction is necessary for sustained gains.
- **Training Stability (Figure 5):** CRAFT demonstrates superior training stability. It shows larger gains in KL stability for policies with broader action spaces (HiP-AD, SparseDriveV2) compared to those with more constrained spaces (MindDrive), suggesting that broader action spaces enhance counterfactual proxy stability. Furthermore, CRAFT reduces expected-advantage variation across policy architectures compared to GRPO, attributing this stability to the dual-clipped grounded residual correction which prevents extreme events from destabilizing updates.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Scenario Analysis (Figure 6)

- In a pedestrian-crossing scenario, fine-tuning SparseDriveV2 with CRAFT results in a clear and favorable shift in the policy's action distribution.
- Initially, the pre-trained policy, as well as PPO and REINFORCE++ fine-tuned policies, assign high probability to an unsafe left-turn accelerating mode. This indicates that sparse closed-loop updates alone are insufficient to shift dominant unsafe behaviors.
- GRPO manages to shift some probability mass towards a braking-compatible mode, but this mode remains secondary, illustrating the bias inherent in solely relying on counterfactual proxy signals.
- CRAFT induces the most pronounced and safety-oriented shift, making the braking-compatible mode dominant. It achieves this while retaining the original peak of the pre-trained policy at a lower probability, indicating that it grounds corrective behavior while preserving beneficial aspects of the pre-trained policy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents CRAFT, a framework for closed-loop post-training of supervised driving policies, with notable significance and potential impact on autonomous driving development:

- **Addressing a Core Challenge:** CRAFT directly addresses the fundamental problem of distribution shift that arises when policies trained via open-loop imitation learning are deployed in closed-loop interactive environments. By providing a principled method to fine-tune policies in an interactive setting, it contributes to bridging the gap between training and deployment realities.
- **Novel Theoretical Formulation:** The introduction of an exact proxy-residual gradient decomposition on the real closed-loop state distribution is a significant theoretical contribution. This decomposition formalizes the trade-off between dense but biased counterfactual supervision and sparse but grounded closed-loop interaction, providing a solid foundation for the proposed method. The theoretical analyses on variance reduction and gradient bias further strengthen this foundation.
- **Enhanced Performance and Robustness:** Empirically, CRAFT consistently achieves superior closed-loop performance gains across a diverse range of state-of-the-art autonomous driving policy architectures (hierarchical planning, vision-language-action, vocabulary scoring). This generalizability suggests that the framework's principles are broadly applicable to various driving policy designs. The observed improvements in safety-critical behaviors such as VRU interaction and obstacle avoidance are particularly impactful for real-world reliability.
- **Improved Training Efficiency and Stability:** By strategically combining dense counterfactual proxy supervision with grounded residual correction, CRAFT offers a more sample-efficient and stable fine-tuning process compared to methods relying solely on sparse real-world feedback or biased counterfactual estimates. The scaling behavior analysis confirms that CRAFT's performance consistently improves with more data, addressing a key bottleneck in RL-based fine-tuning. The dual-clipping mechanism and self-distillation contribute to stable adaptation, preventing policy drift and preserving valuable pre-trained knowledge.
- **Practical Implications for Deployment:** The ability to effectively fine-tune pre-trained policies in closed-loop environments, particularly for safety-critical scenarios, moves autonomous driving systems closer to robust and reliable real-world deployment. The framework's capacity to induce explicit shifts towards safer behaviors in challenging scenarios, as demonstrated qualitatively, indicates its potential to build more trustworthy autonomous agents.
- **Catalyst for Future Research:** CRAFT's proxy-residual optimization paradigm, combining the strengths of different learning signals, could inspire further research in areas where data sparsity and model bias are prevalent. This includes not only other robotic control tasks but also broader applications in decision-making under uncertainty, where efficient and grounded policy improvement is desired.

In summary, CRAFT presents a robust and theoretically grounded framework that significantly advances the state of the art in fine-tuning autonomous driving policies, addressing crucial challenges related to distribution shift, sample efficiency, and stability, with clear implications for improving the safety and reliability of autonomous vehicles.
