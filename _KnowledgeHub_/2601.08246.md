# FSAG: Enhancing Human-to-Dexterous-Hand Finger-Specific Affordance Grounding via Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework leverages pretrained text-to-image diffusion models to infer finger-specific affordances from human demonstrations, yielding robust, human-intuitive, and hardware-agnostic dexterous grasp synthesis. It achieves high success rates for complex objects and generalizes across different high-DOF robot hands with minimal data.

## Problem

- The challenge of robust dexterous grasp synthesis for multi-fingered robot hands, requiring simultaneous satisfaction of functional intent and physical feasibility.
- Existing methods face high data collection costs and often produce brittle grasps due to reliance on privileged state information or limited generalization across robot embodiments.
- Coarse affordance predictions from prior work fail to provide the fine-grained, finger-specific contact strategies needed for stable multi-contact dexterous grasps.

## Method

- Leverages ==pretrained text-to-image diffusion models== (Stable Diffusion U-Net) to extract multi-scale semantic ==hyperfeatures== encoding object-centric functional geometry.
- Predicts a ==Finger-Specific Affordance Field (FSAF)== consisting of five per-finger likelihood maps, which are learned from human demonstration videos.
- Integrates the FSAF into a ==grasp refinement optimization== as an explicit ==affordance alignment term==, guiding each robot fingertip to its predicted functional contact region.

## Results

- Outperformed prior affordance grounding methods on metrics like KLD (**2.491** vs. **11.184** for CMKA), indicating superior fine-grained localization accuracy using Stable Diffusion hyperfeatures.
- Achieved high real-robot grasp success rates (e.g., **85%** on unseen bottles, **90%** on tomatoes) for various objects and tools, producing human-intuitive and physically stable grasps.
- Demonstrated hardware-agnostic generalization by achieving comparable grasp success rates when transferred to a different dexterous hand (Linker Hand L20) without retraining.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pretrained text-to-image diffusion models encode deep semantic knowledge about object parts and functional geometry, which is crucial for precise, finger-specific affordance grounding.
> - Integrating these finger-specific affordances directly into grasp optimization significantly enhances convergence, stability, and human-intuitiveness of generated grasps by guiding contact points to functional regions.
> - The learned finger-specific affordance representation effectively disentangles grasp semantics from specific hand kinematics, allowing for robust cross-embodiment generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reliable grasp synthesis for dexterous robot manipulation represents a significant challenge within robotics. While multi-fingered dexterous hands offer increased kinematic expressivity and potential for rich contact interactions compared to simpler grippers, this complexity also amplifies the difficulty of learning and deploying robust grasping strategies. The broader research landscape often addresses dexterous grasping by decoupling two primary aspects: "how to grasp" (coordination of fingers, contacts, approach direction) and "where to grasp" (spatial localization of functionally exploitable object regions). This separation frequently results in policies that are brittle, tightly coupled to specific robot embodiments, and limited in their ability to generalize across different objects.

Current approaches to "how to grasp" often involve reinforcement learning or large-scale supervised training in simulation. These methods typically assume access to privileged, noise-free geometric states, which are rarely available in real-world environments, leading to substantial sim-to-real gaps. Challenges arise from realistic sensing artifacts such as partial views, self-occlusions, depth discontinuities, and sensor noise. Alternatively, direct large-scale real-world data collection for dexterous grasping is prohibitively expensive and typically fails to generalize to unseen dexterous hand embodiments due to representational overfitting, where action parameterizations are tied to a specific hand model’s joint configuration and actuation limits.

Regarding "where to grasp," existing affordance research commonly provides coarse region-of-interest predictions, such as bounding boxes or masks. While these provide a spatial prior for potential contact areas, they lack the fine-grained, per-finger engagement strategies, contact assignments, and geometry-conditioned sequencing required for stable multi-contact closure with a dexterous hand. Approaches that recast contact reasoning as keypoint detection are similarly limited, as reliable contact locations often lack distinct RGB cues and depend on object-part semantics and inter-finger relationships that appearance-only models struggle to capture.

This work addresses these limitations by leveraging the observation that internet-scale text-to-image diffusion models, such as Stable Diffusion, have internalized multi-level knowledge about objects, parts, materials, and functional geometry. Prior research indicates that intermediate features from these generative models can encode semantic priors relevant to grasping. The paper builds on this by proposing a data-efficient framework that bypasses extensive robot grasp data collection, instead exploiting these object-centric semantic priors. It also aims to bridge the embodiment gap between human and robot hands, a persistent challenge in learning from human demonstration videos, by extracting manipulation structure directly consumable by robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent difficulties in dexterous grasp synthesis, particularly the need to jointly satisfy functional intent and physical feasibility. Existing methods often fail to achieve this holistically, leading to unstable or non-functional grasps, especially when encountering variations in object pose or instances. The high dimensionality and diverse kinematics of multi-fingered hands exacerbate this problem, often compelling researchers to rely on large, hardware-specific datasets acquired through costly simulation or real-world trials.

The specific objectives and motivations addressed by this work are:

- **To overcome data collection burden:** The paper is motivated by the prohibitive cost and complexity of collecting large, hardware-specific robot grasp datasets. It aims to develop a data-efficient framework that leverages existing knowledge from pretrained generative models, thus bypassing the need for extensive robot grasp data collection.
- **To achieve finger-specific semantic grounding:** A key objective is to move beyond coarse affordance regions by inferring a Finger-Specific Affordance Field (FSAF). This FSAF is intended to unify the "where" and "how" of dexterous contacts by assigning each object surface location a finger-conditioned likelihood and role descriptor, thereby capturing both feasible contact placement and inter-finger compatibility. The motivation is that Internet-scale text-to-image diffusion models, such as Stable Diffusion, possess deep semantic understanding of objects and their functional geometry, which can be repurposed for this fine-grained grounding.
- **To integrate semantics into grasp refinement:** The research seeks to directly incorporate these learned finger-specific affordance regions into the grasp refinement objective. This explicit guidance is motivated by the need to steer each fingertip toward its predicted functional region during optimization, thereby ensuring generated grasps are not only physically stable but also human-intuitive and functionally aligned.
- **To enable cross-hand generalization:** A significant motivation is to develop a framework that supports the transfer of grasp strategies across heterogeneous dexterous hand embodiments without requiring hardware-specific retraining. The goal is to disentangle grasp semantics from specific kinematic models, thereby promoting scalability and reducing deployment costs for new robot hands.
- **To demonstrate robust performance with minimal sensing:** The paper aims to establish that a single depth modality can suffice for high-performance grasp synthesis when effectively coupled with semantic priors from foundation models. This objective is driven by the desire to simplify sensing requirements for robotic systems in real-world scenarios.
- **To produce human-intuitive and physically stable grasps:** Ultimately, the work is motivated by the goal of generating multi-contact grasps that are both stable under physical constraints and align with human interaction logic across a variety of common objects and tools, including generalization to unseen instances and pose variations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is a perception-to-optimization pipeline designed to learn finger-specific affordances from human demonstrations and subsequently synthesize executable dexterous grasps.

**A. Data Collection**
The process begins with constructing a finger-wise contact affordance dataset from human demonstration videos. For each grasp sequence, a data acquisition pipeline automatically detects 2D hand keypoints using the RTMPose estimator. Two frames are identified for each training sample: an object-only keyframe providing a clean visual context, and the earliest stable grasp frame where clear human hand-object contact is established. Fingertip locations (µk) in the grasp frame are used to generate ground-truth Gaussian channels (Hk) over the image lattice, which serve as supervisory signals for finger-specific contact localization.

**B. Finger-Specific Affordance Representation from Diffusion Models**
The core of the methodology lies in leveraging the semantic grounding capabilities of large pretrained text-to-image diffusion models.
1. **Feature Synthesis and Hyperfeature Aggregation:** A frozen Stable Diffusion U-Net is employed as a semantic backbone. Given an image-text pair (e.g., "Pick up the drill"), the input image is encoded into a latent representation (z0) and subjected to a standard latent forward noising process. The diffusion network, conditioned on text embeddings from a frozen CLIP tokenizer, produces multi-scale activations from its U-Net blocks across a subset of denoising timesteps. These activations, termed hyperfeatures, are then aggregated into a global affordance descriptor (Ag). This aggregation employs lightweight bottleneck layers and learned mixing coefficients to summarize complementary semantics across different scales and timesteps.
2. **Finger-Specific Affordance Heatmap Prediction:** An FPN-style decoder is used to map the dense hyperfeature Ag to five per-finger likelihood maps (Ĥ). This decoder progressively upsamples Ag while integrating lateral features from shallower resolutions, generating a fused feature map. A final convolutional projection outputs the per-finger heatmaps, where each map corresponds to a specific finger. The decoder is trained using a mean squared error (MSE) loss, comparing the predicted heatmaps (Ĥ) against the ground-truth Gaussian heatmaps (H) derived from human fingertip labels.

**C. Finger-Specific Affordance Matching for Dexterous Grasping**
1. **Object Reconstruction and Affordance Grounding:** The target object is first segmented from RGB-D images using SAM3, and the masked depth data is back-projected into a partial object point cloud. An object surface reconstruction pipeline, based on SAM3D, is then applied to obtain a posed object surface, with pose refinement performed via ICP. The object's point cloud is re-projected onto the image plane. For each predicted finger keypoint from the affordance heatmaps, the nearest N point-cloud projections on the image are identified, forming a set of robust 3D fingertip contact candidates.
2. **Grasp Refinement and Integration of Finger-Specific Affordances:** The grasp refinement module is adapted from GraspQP, but with key innovations to incorporate finger-specific affordance cues. Grasp synthesis is formulated as an optimization problem minimizing an energy function (E) that includes:
 - **Force-closure term (Efc):** Optimizes contact force distributions for physical stability.
 - **Distance term (Edis):** Penalizes distances between active contact points and the object surface.
 - **Regularization terms (Ereg):** Prevents hand-object penetration, self-penetration, and joint-limit violations.
 - **Affordance alignment term (Eaff):** This novel term explicitly incorporates the Finger-Specific Affordance Fields (FSAF). It minimizes the squared distance between each optimized contact point and its corresponding finger-specific affordance region (Sg(j)), ensuring contacts align with the predicted functional areas.
3. **Initialization (Warm Start):** To improve convergence and reduce sensitivity to local minima, the refinement solver is warm-started. For each finger, its fingertip contact is initialized close to its predicted affordance region (within a 3 cm radius), effectively narrowing the search space to role-consistent configurations.
4. **Execution:** After the optimization converges to a refined grasp posture, a three-phase motion plan (approach, close, hold) is executed to achieve smooth finger trajectories and stable contact establishment on the real robot.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation sought to address three primary research questions (RQ): the performance of the finger-specific grasp representation, its impact on grasp stability and human-intuitive contact placements, and its cross-embodiment generalization.

**A. Experimental Setup**
- **Dataset:** 130 human grasp demonstrations across 13 everyday objects (10 demonstrations per object).
- **Evaluation Set:** Seven unseen objects, including novel instances of previously seen categories and entirely new categories (e.g., wrench, hammer).
- **Implementation:** Trained for 4,000 steps with a batch size of 2 using AdamW on an NVIDIA H100 GPU. Input images were resized to 640 × 320, and heatmaps to 448 × 448.
- **Robotic Platform:** A Franka Emika Panda arm, a RealSense 435 camera, and two dexterous hands: DexHand021 (12+5 DOFs) and Linker Hand L20 (16+5 DOFs).
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Affordance Grounding:** Kullback–Leibler Divergence (KLD, lower is better), Normalized Histogram Intersection (SIM, higher is better), and Normalized Scanpath Saliency (NSS, higher is better).
 - **Dexterous Grasping:** Grasping Success Rate (Suc. R.), defined as lifting the object above 0.1m and holding it stably for over 3 seconds.

**B. Results of Finger-Specific Affordance Grounding (RQ1)**
- **Comparison with Baselines:** The proposed method was compared against CMKA, a prior affordance-based approach, and ablations using CLIP and DINO as feature extractors instead of Stable Diffusion.
- **Performance on Metrics:** The proposed method, utilizing Stable Diffusion (SD) hyperfeatures, significantly outperformed CMKA across all metrics (KLD: 2.491 vs 11.184; SIM: 0.551 vs 0.177; NSS: 5.518 vs 1.861). CMKA showed limitations in fine-grained localization, with predictions often collapsing to object centroids or spilling across boundaries, particularly for objects lacking distinct visual parts.
- **Ablation Study on Feature Extractors:** Replacing SD hyperfeatures with CLIP or DINO led to a noticeable degradation in performance. For instance, KLD increased by 1.33x (DINO) and 2.69x (CLIP), while SIM and NSS dropped. Qualitatively, DINO features tended to merge adjacent fingers and produce broader activations with boundary spill-over, and on unseen tools, biased towards visually salient parts rather than functional grasp regions. The SD hyperfeatures consistently mitigated errors such as centroid collapse, boundary spill-over, and fingertip swapping, indicating their criticality for precise per-finger affordance localization.

**C. Functional Affordance Grasping with High-DOF Hands (RQ2)**
- **Qualitative Grasp Visualization:** The method produced human-intuitive grasp poses, with fingertips aligning tightly with predicted graspable regions, matching the functional geometry of each object. In contrast, GraspQP with random initialization often converged to non-human-like or degenerate configurations. GraspQP* (GraspQP with the same affordance-consistent initialization as the proposed method) showed improvement but still failed on some objects (e.g., banana, drill), suggesting that strong initialization alone is insufficient without explicit affordance guidance. GrainGrasp struggled to generate meaningful grasps due to assumptions about human hand scale and contact representation that did not transfer to robotic hands.
- **Quantitative Grasp Success Rate:** The proposed method, using DexHand021, achieved high success rates across common objects and tools (e.g., Bottle: 100% seen / 85% unseen; Banana: 85%; Tomato: 90%; Screwdriver: 60%; Drill: 70%).
 - Imitation learning baselines (ACT-3D, Diffusion Policy 3D) showed significantly degraded performance, especially on unseen objects or pose variations, as they tend to reproduce demonstrated trajectories without adapting strategies.
 - GrainGrasp achieved near-zero success rates in real-robot trials, indicating challenges in sim-to-real transfer for contact-guided synthesis.
 - CMKA also exhibited limited performance, particularly for objects without salient part delineations (e.g., tomato, banana), and encountered issues with preprogrammed approach motions causing collisions with the table for low-profile objects.
- **Energy Convergence:** During optimization, the proposed method consistently converged faster and reached a lower final energy compared to GraspQP* (GraspQP with identical warm-start initialization). This was attributed to the Eaff term providing dense, region-consistent geometric guidance, which effectively reduced the search space and steered the optimizer away from poor local minima, also indirectly benefiting the force-closure term.

**D. Cross-Embodiment Evaluation (RQ3)**
- **Transferability:** The entire pipeline, including perception and affordance inference, was transferred directly from DexHand021 to a different dexterous hand, Linker Hand L20, by only changing the kinematic model.
- **Grasp Success Invariance:** Grasp success rates remained statistically comparable across both embodiments (e.g., Linker Hand L20 achieved Bottle: 100% seen / 80% unseen; Banana: 90%; Tomato: 100%; Screwdriver: 70%; Drill: 90%). This demonstrated that the finger-specific affordance representation and geometry-aligned waypoint construction effectively decouple grasp semantics from specific hand morphology, enabling deployment without hand-specific retraining.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents several contributions with potential impact on the field of dexterous robot manipulation:

- **Novel Semantic Affordance Extraction:** The introduction of a pipeline that leverages vision-language generative priors from frozen text-to-image diffusion models for finger-specific affordance grounding is a novel approach. This method extracts fine-grained, per-finger contact affordances, bridging high-level interaction semantics with precise contact localization. This offers an alternative to conventional discriminative models for semantic grounding.
- **Data Efficiency:** The framework achieves robust and human-intuitive dexterous grasp synthesis from a relatively small set of human demonstrations. This significantly reduces the data collection burden compared to methods that require extensive robot-specific grasp datasets or large-scale teleoperation, making robotic system deployment more practical and cost-effective.
- **Hardware-Agnostic Generalization:** The demonstrated ability to directly transfer grasp strategies across heterogeneous dexterous hand embodiments without hand-specific retraining is a notable achievement. By disentangling grasp semantics from specific hand kinematics, the approach promotes scalability and reduces the development overhead for new robotic hardware, fostering more universal manipulation capabilities.
- **Monocular Depth Modality Sufficiency:** The research establishes that a single depth modality, when synergistically combined with rich semantic priors from foundation models, can be sufficient for high-performance grasp synthesis. This simplifies sensor requirements and potentially reduces system complexity for real-world robotic applications.
- **Enhanced Grasp Quality and Intuition:** The integration of finger-specific affordances into a physically grounded grasp refinement objective leads to grasps that are not only stable and physically feasible but also align with human interaction logic. This characteristic is valuable for tasks requiring nuanced interaction and for promoting human-robot collaboration.

Collectively, these contributions highlight a path toward scalable, hardware-agnostic dexterous manipulation systems. By effectively harnessing human demonstrations and the knowledge embedded in pretrained generative models, this work offers a foundation for developing more adaptable, robust, and intuitive robotic agents capable of performing complex manipulation tasks in diverse environments.
