# FSAG: Enhancing Human-to-Dexterous-Hand Finger-Specific Affordance Grounding via Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework leverages pretrained text-to-image diffusion models to infer finger-specific affordances from human demonstrations, yielding robust, human-intuitive, and hardware-agnostic dexterous grasp synthesis. It achieves high success rates for complex objects and generalizes across different high-DOF robot hands with minimal data.

## Problem

- The challenge of robust dexterous grasp synthesis for multi-fingered robot hands, requiring simultaneous satisfaction of functional intent and physical feasibility.
- Existing methods face high data collection costs and often produce brittle grasps due to reliance on privileged state information or limited generalization across robot embodiments.
- Coarse affordance predictions from prior work fail to provide the fine-grained, finger-specific contact strategies needed for stable multi-contact dexterous grasps.

## Method

- Leverages ==pretrained text-to-image diffusion models== (Stable Diffusion U-Net) to extract multi-scale semantic ==hyperfeatures== encoding object-centric functional geometry.
- Predicts a ==Finger-Specific Affordance Field (FSAF)== consisting of five per-finger likelihood maps, which are learned from human demonstration videos.
- Integrates the FSAF into a ==grasp refinement optimization== as an explicit ==affordance alignment term==, guiding each robot fingertip to its predicted functional contact region.

## Results

- Outperformed prior affordance grounding methods on metrics like KLD (**2.491** vs. **11.184** for CMKA), indicating superior fine-grained localization accuracy using Stable Diffusion hyperfeatures.
- Achieved high real-robot grasp success rates (e.g., **85%** on unseen bottles, **90%** on tomatoes) for various objects and tools, producing human-intuitive and physically stable grasps.
- Demonstrated hardware-agnostic generalization by achieving comparable grasp success rates when transferred to a different dexterous hand (Linker Hand L20) without retraining.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pretrained text-to-image diffusion models encode deep semantic knowledge about object parts and functional geometry, which is crucial for precise, finger-specific affordance grounding.
> - Integrating these finger-specific affordances directly into grasp optimization significantly enhances convergence, stability, and human-intuitiveness of generated grasps by guiding contact points to functional regions.
> - The learned finger-specific affordance representation effectively disentangles grasp semantics from specific hand kinematics, allowing for robust cross-embodiment generalization.
