# LACY: A Vision-Language Model-based Language-Action Cycle for Self-Improving Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Minnesota developed LACY, a Vision-Language Model that enables self-improving robotic manipulation through a bidirectional language-action cycle. LACY allows robots to both execute commands and explain their actions, leveraging this synergy with a confidence-based active data augmentation strategy to autonomously generate and filter training data, thereby reducing reliance on human supervision and improving task performance.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models primarily use a unidirectional Language-to-Action (L2A) mapping, limiting their contextual understanding.
- Robotic manipulation policies often require extensive, costly, and labor-intensive human-annotated datasets for training.
- Current methods struggle with generalizing to novel scenarios and achieving deeper task comprehension due to reliance on passively collected, large datasets.

## Method

- **LACY** introduces a unified VLM framework that supports three complementary tasks: ==Language-to-Action (L2A)==, ==Action-to-Language (A2L)==, and ==Language-to-Consistency (L2C)==.
- It implements a ==self-improving Language-Action Cycle (L2A2L)== where actions generated from language are described in language, then verified for ==semantic consistency== to autonomously generate new training data.
- A ==confidence-based active data augmentation== strategy, using L2C, selectively generates new data for low-confidence scenarios and filters it via ==majority voting== to ensure quality and efficiency in learning.

## Results

- **LACY** achieved **95%** L2A, **76%** A2L, and **95%** L2C success rates in simulation (with **4,000** demonstrations), significantly outperforming base models and GPT-4o.
- The self-improvement cycle with filtering (LACY-Joint-Filter) boosted L2A to **93%**, A2L to **85%**, and L2C to **95%** with only **1,000** initial demonstrations, demonstrating effective autonomous data generation.
- In real-world robotic experiments, LACY-Joint-Real attained **88%** L2A, **88%** A2L, and **98%** L2C reasoning scores, leading to **72.5%** picking and **60%** pick-and-place success rates with a Franka Emika Panda robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bidirectional grounding between language and action (L2A and A2L) fosters a more holistic understanding and robust internal representations of manipulation tasks.
> - Applying cycle consistency from generative modeling to the language-action domain allows for autonomous, verifiable generation of high-quality training data, mitigating data scarcity.
> - Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting with explicit object grounding is critical for robust reasoning and higher performance in VLM-based robotic manipulation, making the reasoning process more transparent.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation increasingly leverages large-scale Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to enable robots to understand and execute human language instructions. This typically involves learning a unidirectional mapping from language to action (L2A). Prominent examples of this paradigm include frameworks like CLIPort, and large-scale generalist models such as RT-2 and OpenVLA. While these L2A-focused approaches have achieved notable successes in mapping textual commands to robot movements, they face limitations. These limitations include a potential lack of deeper contextual understanding, which can restrict their ability to generalize to novel situations and explain their behaviors. Furthermore, these models often depend on the availability of massive, passively collected datasets, raising concerns about scalability and data efficiency due to the high cost and labor intensity of robot data collection.

This research addresses these limitations by proposing that a complementary skill, mapping actions back to language (A2L), is crucial for developing more robust and holistic language-action grounding in robots. Drawing inspiration from human cognition, where language and action are bidirectionally intertwined, the authors argue that an agent capable of both performing actions and explaining them can form richer internal representations. This bidirectional capability is posited to unlock new paradigms for self-supervised learning, enabling robots to generate and refine their own training data, thereby reducing reliance on external human supervision.

The work connects to existing research in several ways: it builds upon the foundation of VLMs for robotics but extends their functionality beyond a single L2A pathway. It also engages with the broader theme of data generation and self-supervision in robotics, which has explored methods like simulation-based data creation, reinforcement learning for autonomous collection, and various data augmentation techniques. LACY differentiates itself by introducing a novel form of self-supervision based on the principle of cycle consistency, previously applied in generative modeling. In this context, LACY applies cycle consistency to the language-action domain for data generation, with its L2C module acting as a critical verifier, providing a form of intrinsic feedback to enhance learning efficiency and robustness. This approach aims to address the challenge of ensuring the quality of self-generated data, a common issue in self-supervised learning where models might otherwise reinforce their own biases.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a more holistic, robust, and data-efficient approach for robotic manipulation by integrating bidirectional language-action grounding within a unified framework. The specific objectives and motivations underpinning the LACY framework are:

1. **To establish bidirectional language-action mapping:** The paper argues that current L2A-centric approaches in robot learning, despite their advancements, lack "deeper contextual understanding" and "explainability." Motivated by the observation that human actions and language are bidirectionally linked—humans can both execute tasks from language and articulate observed actions—the researchers aim to equip robots with the complementary ability to map observed actions back to language (A2L), in addition to the standard L2A capability. This is intended to foster richer internal representations and improve the robot's understanding of tasks.

2. **To enable autonomous self-improvement through a language-action cycle:** A core motivation is to overcome the dependence on extensive, costly human-annotated datasets. By integrating both L2A and A2L capabilities, the framework aims to create a self-supervised learning paradigm. In this cycle, the robot executes an action based on a language command (L2A), then generates a linguistic description of that executed action (A2L), and subsequently evaluates the semantic consistency between the initial command and its self-generated description (L2C). This closed-loop process is designed to allow the model to autonomously generate, evaluate, and refine its own training data.

3. **To ensure data quality and learning efficiency through semantic consistency verification:** Generating synthetic data without quality control can lead to models reinforcing their own biases or learning from erroneous examples. To address this, a specific objective is to develop a semantic consistency verification module (L2C). This module is tasked with assessing whether the original language instruction and the model's self-generated action description are semantically aligned. This verification step is crucial for filtering out low-quality or inconsistent self-generated data, thereby ensuring that the augmented training dataset is reliable and effective.

4. **To implement a confidence-based active data augmentation strategy:** Naively adding all self-generated data can lead to inefficiencies, such as overfitting to already mastered tasks or incorporating redundant information. Therefore, the researchers aim to develop an active data augmentation strategy that focuses learning on challenging scenarios. This strategy uses the confidence scores from the L2C module to selectively generate new data for cases where the model exhibits low confidence, directing the learning process more efficiently and effectively towards areas requiring improvement.

In summary, the motivation stems from the limitations of unidirectional robot learning, the high cost of data collection, and the potential for a human-like bidirectional understanding to yield more capable, generalizable, and autonomous robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LACY (Language-Action CYcle) framework unifies three synergistic tasks—Language-to-Action (L2A), Action-to-Language (A2L), and Language-to-Consistency (L2C)—within a single Vision-Language Model (VLM). The framework is built upon the LLaVA-NeXT model and employs a two-stage fine-tuning strategy coupled with a self-improving data generation pipeline.

**A. Problem Definition and Notations**
The work defines robotic manipulation tasks using a triplet `ζ_t = (o_t, l_t, a_t)`, comprising an image observation (`o_t`), a natural language task description (`l_t`), and a pick-and-place action (`a_t = (T_pick, T_place)`).
- **L2A Model (`π_l->a`):** Maps an observation `o_t` and language instruction `l_t` to a predicted action `a_hat_t`.
- **A2L Model (`π_a->l`):** Predicts a language description `l_hat_t` from an observation `o_t` and demonstrated action `a_t`.
- **L2C Model (`π_l->c`):** Estimates the consistency `c_t ∈` between two language descriptions (`l_t` and `l_hat_t`) conditioned on the observation `o_t`.

**B. Explaining Robot Actions in Language via A2L**
For the A2L module, LACY generates naturalistic spatial descriptions based on the observed pick-and-place action. The language describes which object to grasp and its placement location. Two types of spatial descriptions are generated:
- **Absolute Spatial Description:** Used when the placement location is not near other objects, referring to a 3x3 grid division of the workspace (e.g., "in the middle left").
- **Relative Spatial Description:** Used when placement is near a reference object (e.g., "to the top right of the mustard bottle").
The selection between absolute and relative descriptions is based on normalized Euclidean distances to the closest object (`d_rel` and `d_abs` thresholds), mimicking human descriptive patterns.

**C. Unified Model via Two-Stage Fine-Tuning**
To address data scarcity and leverage VLM capabilities, LACY utilizes a two-stage fine-tuning process:
1. **Stage 1: Object Grounding Pre-training:** The VLM backbone is initially pre-trained on a dataset of 8,000 images to identify objects, their names, and center coordinates. This foundational skill `O = {(n_i, p_i)}` provides a robust visual understanding for downstream tasks.
2. **Stage 2: CoT-based Multi-task Fine-tuning:** The pre-trained model is then fine-tuned on a smaller, robot-specific dataset (initially 1,000 demonstrations) for the L2A, A2L, and L2C tasks. All three tasks are framed as a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) process, where the model first performs object grounding (`O_hat`) and then uses this context for the specific task. This approach aims to enhance transparency, robustness, and data efficiency. Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is used for efficient fine-tuning.

**D. Self-Improving Data Generation with L2A and A2L**
LACY implements a closed-loop L2A2L pipeline for autonomous data generation. An initial language command `l` is fed into the L2A module to generate an action `a_hat`. This `a_hat` is then passed to the A2L module, along with the observation `o`, to produce a reconstructed language description `l_hat`. This cycle produces a candidate triplet `(o, l, a_hat)`.

**E. Confidence-based Data Augmentation with L2C**
To ensure the quality of self-generated data and focus learning on challenging cases, LACY employs a confidence-based active data augmentation strategy with a majority-voting mechanism (Algorithm 1):
1. **Initial Pass & Consistency Score:** For each sample in the initial dataset, a deterministic L2A2L pass is performed, and the L2C module evaluates consistency (`c`) between the original `l` and reconstructed `l_hat`.
2. **Confidence Extraction:** The L2C module performs binary classification ("1" for consistent, "0" for inconsistent). A continuous confidence score `c` is extracted by applying a sigmoid function to the logit difference between the "1" and "0" tokens from the VLM's final decoding step.
3. **Active Augmentation:** If `c` is below a consistency threshold `τ` (indicating low confidence), the model generates `N` stochastic candidate actions (`A_cand`) using L2A. For each candidate action `a_hat_i` in `A_cand`, `N` language descriptions (`L_cand`) are generated via stochastic A2L.
4. **Majority Voting Filter:** An action `a_hat_i` is added to a new dataset (`D_new`) only if a majority (`≥ ν`) of its `N` corresponding language descriptions in `L_cand` are judged as consistent (`c ≥ τ`) with the original instruction `l_t` by L2C. This filtering mechanism aims to prevent the addition of duplicate or erroneous `(l, a_hat)` pairs and ensures robust explainability.
5. **Re-training:** The model is re-initialized from its base and re-fine-tuned on a merged dataset (`D_aug`), combining the original data with the newly generated, high-quality data.

**F. Experimental Setup**
Experiments are conducted in both a simulated CoppeliaSim environment (32 YCB objects) and a real-world setup (Franka Emika Panda robot, Intel RealSense D415 camera, 12 real objects). The training dataset comprises up to 4,000 successful pick-and-place demonstrations, with smaller subsets (1,000 or 100) used for ablation studies and self-improvement evaluation. Evaluation metrics include L2A success rate, A2L success rate, L2C accuracy, and real-world pick/pick & place success rates. Training is performed on a single NVIDIA A40 GPU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted in both simulation and real-world environments to evaluate LACY's reasoning capabilities, the effectiveness of its components through ablation studies, its self-improvement capacity, and real-world performance.

**A. Reasoning Capabilities in Simulation**
The LACY model, fine-tuned on 4,000 demonstrations, achieved high performance: 95% L2A task success rate, 76% A2L success rate, and 95% L2C accuracy. In comparison, the base LLaVA-NeXT model without specific fine-tuning demonstrated limited performance (6% L2A, 6% A2L, 50% L2C). GPT-4o, when provided with explicit ground-truth object location information, achieved a 90% L2A success rate but struggled with A2L (39%) and L2C (8%). Without ground-truth information, GPT-4o's L2A performance dropped to 28%. These results indicate that large general-purpose models require precise grounding information to perform well in robotic tasks and that LACY's specialized fine-tuning is effective for language-action grounding.

**B. Ablation Studies (1,000 demonstrations)**
A series of ablation studies evaluated the necessity of LACY's components under data-scarce conditions:
- **Effectiveness of Joint Training and Filtering:** LACY-Joint, which incorporates joint training of L2A, A2L, and L2C, achieved 83% L2A, 80% A2L, and 92% L2C. This outperformed LACY-Ind (independently trained modules), which yielded 78% L2A, 78% A2L, and 92% L2C. The integration of the self-improvement cycle (LACY-Joint-SI) further enhanced performance, achieving 93% L2A, 85% A2L, and 95% L2C, demonstrating the benefits of shared representations and the self-improvement mechanism.
- **Necessity of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting:** Models utilizing the CoT strategy (LACY-CoT) consistently outperformed those without it (LACY-non-CoT). LACY-CoT achieved 83% L2A, 80% A2L, and 92% L2C, whereas LACY-non-CoT reached 52% L2A, 43% A2L, and 84% L2C. This indicates that explicitly incorporating object grounding as an intermediate reasoning step is beneficial for task performance.

**C. Self-Improvement Capability (Starting with 100 demonstrations)**
The self-improvement loop was tested by iteratively augmenting a small initial dataset of 100 demonstrations. With each iteration, 100 new high-quality triplets (observation, language, action) were generated and added. LACY-Joint-SI, trained on a mixture of ground-truth and self-generated data, showed consistent improvements in L2A and A2L success rates as the total dataset size increased from 100 to 300 (100 ground truth + 200 self-generated). For example, at 300 data points, LACY-Joint-SI achieved an L2A success rate of 80% and an A2L success rate of 65%, which surpassed LACY-Joint (trained solely on 300 ground-truth data points), which achieved 67% L2A and 44% A2L. The L2C success rate for LACY-Joint-SI was 82% at 300 data points, while LACY-Joint achieved 85%. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of LACY's self-improvement loop in improving performance, particularly in data-limited scenarios, by generating and leveraging additional data.

**D. Real-World Evaluation**
LACY's performance was evaluated on a 7-DoF Franka Emika Panda robot. Initially, models trained only in simulation (LACY-Ind, LACY-Joint) showed reasonable L2A (78-80%) and L2C (94-98%) but lower A2L (28-36%) in real-world settings, primarily due to misnaming objects in real images, highlighting a sim-to-real gap.
Fine-tuning these models with a small real-world dataset (212 demonstrations) significantly improved performance. LACY-Joint-Real achieved 88% L2A, 88% A2L, and 98% L2C, outperforming LACY-Ind-Real (82% L2A, 80% A2L, 94% L2C). For physical robot tasks, LACY-Joint-Real also showed superior performance, achieving a 72.5% pick success rate and a 60% pick-and-place success rate, compared to LACY-Ind-Real's 65% pick and 55% pick-and-place rates. This indicates that the performance gains observed in simulation translate to the real world, and targeted fine-tuning with real data effectively mitigates sim-to-real disparities, especially for object grounding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LACY framework represents an advancement in robotic manipulation by moving beyond the prevailing unidirectional Language-to-Action (L2A) paradigm towards a more comprehensive, bidirectional language-action grounding. This bidirectional capability, encompassing action generation (L2A), action explanation (A2L), and semantic consistency verification (L2C) within a single Vision-Language Model, offers several significant contributions and potential impacts.

One primary significance is LACY's approach to **addressing the data scarcity problem in robot learning**. By enabling a self-improving cycle that autonomously generates and filters its own training data, LACY substantially reduces the reliance on costly and labor-intensive human demonstrations. This capability to scale learning without continuous human supervision is a crucial step towards more scalable and autonomous robot intelligence. The confidence-based active data augmentation strategy further enhances this efficiency by directing data generation towards low-confidence scenarios, which minimizes redundant computation and prevents overfitting.

The framework's **bidirectional grounding capabilities** foster richer internal representations within the robot. This enhanced understanding of the relationship between language, visual observations, and physical actions could lead to more robust policies that generalize better to novel tasks and environments. While not explicitly demonstrated as a direct outcome, the ability to explain actions in language (A2L) could also contribute to more transparent and potentially explainable robot behaviors, facilitating human-robot collaboration and trust.

LACY's performance improvements, observed in both simulation and real-world pick-and-place tasks, underscore the effectiveness of unifying these complementary tasks. The translation of these gains to a physical robot highlights the practical applicability of the framework and its potential to mitigate sim-to-real transfer challenges with targeted real-world data integration.

Despite these advancements, the authors acknowledge limitations, particularly regarding the L2C module's current inability to explicitly evaluate object grounding quality. This implies that errors in object detection or naming could propagate through the Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning process, affecting task success. Addressing this limitation by developing more robust perception and verification modules forms a clear path for future research.

The potential impact of LACY extends to various applications requiring complex manipulation and human-robot interaction. By providing a foundation for robots to learn more autonomously and adaptively, the framework could accelerate the development of robotic systems capable of performing a broader range of complex, long-horizon tasks and acquiring diverse manipulation skills. This bidirectional perspective on language-action interaction could serve as a model for future research in generalizable robot intelligence, where agents not only execute commands but also comprehend and articulate their actions, leading to more intelligent and versatile robotic agents.
