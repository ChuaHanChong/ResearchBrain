# FastStair: Learning to Run Up Stairs with Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> FastStair introduces a planner-guided, multi-stage reinforcement learning framework that enables humanoid robots to run up and down stairs with both high speed and stability. This method leverages parallel foothold planning and achieves robust real-world performance, including winning the Canton Tower Robot Run Up Competition by climbing a 33-step spiral staircase in 12 seconds with a peak velocity of 1.65 m/s.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle to simultaneously achieve high agility (speed and dynamic motion) and strict stability when navigating challenging terrains like stairs.
- Model-free reinforcement learning often leads to overly conservative or unstable behaviors on stairs due to implicit stability rewards conflicting with high-velocity objectives.
- Model-based planners provide explicit stability guarantees but typically result in slow, conservative motions and are computationally intensive for integration with large-scale reinforcement learning.

## Method

- A DCM-based foothold planner is reformulated into a GPU-parallel discrete search, achieving a 25x speedup and enabling real-time, explicit stability guidance within the RL training loop.
- A three-stage reinforcement learning framework (Pre-training, Post-training, ==LoRA Fine==-tuning) is developed: pre-training emphasizes safety via foothold-tracking rewards, post-training specializes policies for low and high-velocity bands, and LoRA fine-tuning unifies these experts for smooth, full-range speed transitions.
- The policy's observation space integrates proprioceptive data with privileged information, including terrain elevation maps and the optimal foothold from the parallel planner, to inform control decisions.

## Results

- The parallel foothold planner achieved a latency of approximately 4 ms for 4096 environments, demonstrating a **25x** speedup compared to recent parallel MPC implementations.
- FastStair consistently achieved the highest success rates and lowest velocity-tracking Mean Absolute Error in simulations compared to baseline and adversarial motion prior (AMP) methods.
- The LimX Oli humanoid robot, utilizing FastStair, successfully navigated a 33-step spiral staircase in 12 seconds with a peak velocity of **1.65** m/s in a real-world competition, exhibiting stable high-speed ascent and emergent step-skipping behavior.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reformulating model-based optimization into a parallel discrete search can overcome computational bottlenecks, making explicit stability guidance tractable for large-scale reinforcement learning.
> - A multi-stage learning approach effectively reconciles the agility-stability trade-off by initially imbuing the policy with planner-derived safety priors, then specializing for speed, and finally unifying policies for seamless performance across velocity ranges.
> - Integrating Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) layers allows for the merging of speed-specialized 'expert' policies into a single network, enabling smooth and robust transitions across diverse velocity commands.
