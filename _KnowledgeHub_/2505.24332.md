# DeepDiver: Adaptive Search Intensity Scaling via Open-Web Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Huawei Language Model Lab introduces DeepDiver, an RL-driven framework, and WebPuzzle, a new open-web benchmark, enabling large language models to dynamically adjust their web search intensity for complex information-seeking. DeepDiver-enhanced 7B-parameter models achieved 37.6-38.1% accuracy on WebPuzzle, matching or exceeding the performance of significantly larger models like the 671B-parameter DeepSeek-R1 (37.1%) and outperforming existing Wiki-based methods.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) struggle with complex information-seeking in open-web environments due to limitations in existing prompting and supervised fine-tuning methods.
- Current benchmarks, largely based on structured, 'clean' datasets like Wikipedia, fail to accurately assess LLMs' ability to navigate noisy, inconsistent, and conflicting information from the live internet.
- Prior reinforcement learning (RL) approaches for LLMs are predominantly trained and evaluated on well-structured datasets, limiting their generalizability to realistic, open-ended information-seeking scenarios.

## Method

- The research introduces ==WebPuzzle==, a novel 24,000-sample training and 275-sample human-annotated test dataset, specifically designed to evaluate LLMs' information seeking on the live internet.
- **DeepDiver**, an RL-driven search and reasoning framework, is developed to cultivate Search Intensity Scaling (SIS) in LLMs, allowing dynamic adjustment of web search frequency and depth based on problem complexity.
- The framework utilizes a curriculum-based approach, combining cold-start supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for iterative RAG and a two-stage reward system (loose then strict graders, plus extra search call rewards).

## Results

- DeepDiver-enhanced **7B** models achieved 37.6-**38.1%** accuracy on WebPuzzle, improving significantly over cold-start SFT (27.9-**30.3%**) and matching or exceeding the performance of much larger models like the **671B**-parameter DeepSeek-R1 (**37.1%**).
- Increased search intensity (more search calls and reasoning rounds) during RL training correlated with performance improvements, with DeepDiver models averaging significantly more search and reasoning rounds per example than SFT baselines.
- DeepDiver demonstrated strong generalization capabilities on out-of-distribution wiki-based problems and open-ended tasks (e.g., **32.72%** on ProxyQA), often outperforming prior Wiki-based methods even when evaluated across language barriers (e.g., **26.1%** on WebPuzzle-en vs. DeepResearcher **15.0%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning can foster emergent adaptive behaviors like Search Intensity Scaling (SIS) in LLMs, enabling them to proactively use external tools to compensate for internal knowledge limitations, even after direct search incentives fade.
> - A carefully designed curriculum, transitioning from a loose to a stricter reward function, is crucial for stabilizing early RL training and effectively overcoming performance plateaus in complex, real-world information-seeking tasks.
> - Training on an open-web dataset like WebPuzzle promotes strong generalization, allowing models to perform well not only on complex real-world queries but also on out-of-distribution wiki-based problems and open-ended generation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Information seeking is a fundamental cognitive skill that involves iterative evidence gathering, reflective reasoning, and the resolution of conflicting information. Despite advancements in artificial intelligence, large language models (LLMs) continue to exhibit limitations in replicating these complex information-seeking behaviors, particularly in open-web question answering.

Existing approaches to enhance LLMs' knowledge-intensive question answering capabilities generally fall into two categories: prompting-based methods and supervised fine-tuning (SFT). Prompting-based strategies, such as iterative Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) frameworks with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, leverage predefined rules or in-context learning to guide the LLM's reasoning flow. However, these methods often suffer from fixed reasoning patterns, which restrict their adaptability to dynamic and complex problems. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) methods train models to improve retrieval and reasoning, generally yielding better performance by internalizing inference patterns from training corpora. A limitation of SFT is that these internalized patterns can restrict generalization to dynamic or unseen scenarios, leading to models that overfit to specific training distributions.

More recently, reinforcement learning (RL) has been applied to enhance LLM inference-time reasoning, enabling iterative refinement and exploration. Several studies have integrated RL into iterative RAG frameworks to encourage exploration of diverse reasoning paths and reward accurate outcomes. However, a notable gap in prior RL-based research is its predominant training and evaluation on well-structured datasets like HotpotQA, which are typically derived from corpora such as Wikipedia. In these "clean" environments, tasks can often be solved using the LLMs' internal knowledge, and the search environments contain minimal noise or conflicting information. This controlled setting limits the generalizability of reported "incentivized search capabilities" to more realistic, open-ended information-seeking scenarios characterized by noisy, inconsistent, and unreliable real-world sources.

This research addresses these limitations by introducing a new benchmark, WebPuzzle, specifically designed to evaluate information-seeking on the live internet across diverse query types. Furthermore, it proposes DeepDiver, an RL-driven framework that cultivates an emergent ability called Search Intensity Scaling (SIS). SIS allows LLMs to dynamically adjust the frequency and depth of their web searches based on problem complexity, moving beyond the constraints of fixed prompting or corpus-tied SFT. By focusing on real-world search environments, this work aims to advance adaptive information seeking in LLMs, providing a more rigorous assessment and development framework for knowledge-intensive problem-solving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the information-seeking capabilities of large language models (LLMs) in real-world, open-web environments, thereby improving their ability to solve complex, knowledge-intensive problems. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Limitations of Existing Benchmarks**: Current benchmarks for information seeking, such as HotpotQA, are largely based on structured, "clean" datasets derived from sources like Wikipedia. In such environments, LLMs can often answer questions using their pre-trained internal knowledge, or they interact with a search environment that is largely devoid of noise, inconsistencies, or conflicting information. This limits the true evaluation of an LLM's ability to navigate the complexities of the live internet. The motivation is to create a more realistic and challenging benchmark that genuinely requires external retrieval, conflict resolution, verification, and reflection.

2. **Bridging the Gap to Real-World Search Environments**: Real-world information seeking involves interacting with the open internet, which is characterized by diverse, noisy, inconsistent, and potentially unreliable sources. LLMs frequently struggle with discerning when and what information to seek, verifying the relevance and trustworthiness of evidence, and reasoning effectively over noisy contexts. A core motivation is to train LLMs to operate robustly in these complex, real-world search environments rather than just structured, academic datasets.

3. **Cultivating Adaptive Information-Seeking Behaviors**: Existing prompting and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) methods tend to enforce a fixed reasoning flow or internalize inference patterns tied to specific training corpora. This rigidity prevents LLMs from adapting their search strategy to the varying demands of different problems. The motivation is to develop a framework that enables LLMs to exhibit emergent, adaptive behaviors, specifically the ability to dynamically scale their search intensity (frequency and depth) in response to perceived information needs or problem difficulty. This contrasts with models that might settle on overconfident, under-evidenced answers due to a lack of adaptive search.

4. **Enhancing Generalization and Robustness**: The research seeks to demonstrate that reinforcement learning (RL) can foster LLM behaviors that generalize better across different problem types (e.g., from closed-ended to open-ended queries) and environments (e.g., from a novel open-web dataset to out-of-distribution wiki-based problems). This aims to overcome the limitations of SFT, which often restricts generalization. The motivation extends to showing that RL can guide LLMs to actively supplement evidence, resolve conflicts, verify content, and engage in self-correction—behaviors crucial for robust information seeking.

To achieve these objectives, the paper introduces two primary components: WebPuzzle, a novel dataset designed to evaluate information seeking in real-world search environments, and DeepDiver, a reinforcement learning framework built upon this dataset to cultivate adaptive search intensity scaling.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research involves the development of a new dataset, WebPuzzle, and an RL-driven framework, DeepDiver, designed to enhance LLMs' adaptive information-seeking capabilities in real-world environments.

#### 3.1. WebPuzzle Dataset

WebPuzzle is a dataset designed to evaluate LLMs' ability to locate and reason over noisy, scattered information on the open web, moving beyond Wikipedia-based datasets where LLMs might rely solely on internal knowledge.

- **Scale and Scope**: The dataset comprises 24,000 training samples and 275 human-annotated test examples. It covers tasks requiring information from Wikipedia content as well as broader open-domain queries from the live internet. Even Wikipedia-related samples are validated to necessitate external retrieval.
- **Candidate Data Generation**: Data is collected from Wiki-corpus and real-user queries coupled with retrieved webpages from a smart assistant service. Two primary generation approaches are used:
 - **Cross-page question generation**: An LLM extracts facts from web pages to generate "inverted" questions, answers, and checklists. This method is applied to open web pages, as Wiki-corpus often yields overly simplistic questions.
 - **Riddle creation**: An LLM selects distinctive entity attributes and applies obfuscation or generalization to formulate challenging problems, with original entities serving as labels.
- **Difficulty Assessment**: To ensure stable RL training by enabling data mixture strategies that prevent consistently low rewards, each problem is tagged with a difficulty level (easy, medium, hard, outlier). This is determined by testing DeepSeek-R1 four times for each problem and counting the number of correct answers (e.g., 4 correct for "easy," 0 for "outlier").
- **Test Set Annotation**: The 275-sample test set is manually annotated by five human experts using an open-web search engine. Annotation principles ensure that answers are definitive, unique, derived from internet search (not common sense), require multiple searches, thorough reasoning, and validation, and are accessible through sufficient search and reasoning rounds. Quality assurance protocols exclude offensive, sensitive, ambiguous, or unsolvable content.

#### 3.2. DeepDiver Framework

DeepDiver is an RL-driven search and reasoning framework trained on WebPuzzle, aiming to foster Search Intensity Scaling (SIS) in LLMs. The framework utilizes a curriculum that progresses from cold-start supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to a custom-designed reinforcement learning (RL) procedure.

- **Initialization of Reasoning and Searching (Cold-Start SFT)**:
 - DeepDiver is first fine-tuned using a diverse dataset to establish foundational reasoning and searching capabilities. This dataset includes 2,000 WebPuzzle samples (across difficulty levels), 300 real-user questions from a smart assistant, 2,200 general reasoning problems, and 1,000 real-user queries concatenated with retrieved documents.
 - Responses for this SFT phase are distilled from DeepSeek-R1, providing initial iterative search and reasoning patterns.
- **GRPO with Iterative RAG**:
 - Following SFT, DeepDiver is enhanced using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), extended for iterative RAG.
 - The model iteratively performs reasoning and searching, making decisions to generate intermediate reasoning, generate search queries for document retrieval, or finalize an answer.
 - A loss mask is applied during GRPO updates, distinguishing between model-generated and externally retrieved tokens. Parameter updates are based solely on model-generated content.
- **Reward Assignment and Scheduling**: A carefully designed reward system maintains stable RL training.
 - **Extra Search Call Rewards**: An additional reward of +1.0 is assigned to successful search-enabled rollouts if a problem cannot be solved by any search-free rollout but is solved by at least one search-enabled rollout. This incentivizes the model to use external tools when necessary for complex problems.
 - **Loose and Strict Rewards**: An LLM-based grader is employed in a two-stage training approach:
 - **Loose Grader**: In early training phases, a loose grader assigns scores from 1 to 10 (scores ≥ 6 yield a 1.0 reward). This provides a more stable and frequent reward signal, beneficial for initial learning.
 - **Strict Grader**: Later in training, a strict grader is used. It conducts three evaluation rounds, requiring at least two out of three positive judgments for a reward. This stricter criterion helps overcome performance plateaus and enhances the model's robustness by preventing exploitation of lenient reward signals.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across various experiments, demonstrating the efficacy of DeepDiver and the WebPuzzle dataset.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparison with Baselines

DeepDiver exhibits substantial performance improvements over distillation-based methods and achieves performance levels comparable to larger state-of-the-art models.
- **On WebPuzzle**: DeepDiver-Qwen2.5-7B achieves 37.6% accuracy, representing a 10-point improvement over the cold-start SFT model (27.9%) and outperforming the R1-distilled model (29.8%). DeepDiver-Pangu-7B shows similar gains, reaching 38.1% from a cold-start of 30.3%.
- **Competitive Performance**: Both DeepDiver-Qwen2.5-7B and DeepDiver-Pangu-7B achieve results on WebPuzzle that are competitive with the 671B-parameter DeepSeek-R1 (37.1%) and the 32B-parameter QwQ-32B (31.4%) when those models use iterative RAG. This suggests that the 7B DeepDiver models can match the performance of significantly larger models on real-web tasks through adaptive search intensity scaling.

#### 4.2. Relationship Between Search Intensity and Performance

Search intensity is positively correlated with performance improvements, indicating that increased search frequency and depth during RL training contribute to better outcomes.
- **Training Phase**: As training rewards increase, the number of search engine calls also rises (Figure 4).
- **Testing Phase**: DeepDiver-Qwen2.5-7B averages 2.51 search and reasoning rounds per example, notably higher than the SFT model's 1.75 rounds. Similarly, DeepDiver-Pangu-7B increases search rounds from 1.84 to 2.89, corresponding to performance gains. This higher search intensity allows the models to explore and verify more information, which is beneficial for complex problems.
- **Isolated Testing**: When evaluating on problems that cannot be solved by internal knowledge alone, DeepDiver exhibits strong information-seeking capabilities. It demonstrates competitive performance against all baselines, even outperforming DeepSeek-R1 by 5.1 points on WebPuzzle in this isolated setting. This suggests DeepDiver's strength lies in external information search and verification, compensating for the smaller 7B model's internal knowledge limitations.

#### 4.3. Generalization to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Wiki-based Problems

DeepDiver, trained primarily on open-web WebPuzzle, demonstrates strong generalization capabilities on Wiki-based problems.
- Both DeepDiver-Qwen2.5-7B and DeepDiver-Pangu-7B significantly outperform their R1-distilled variants and cold-start SFT models on C-simpleQA-500, FRAMES-zh-230, and BamBoogle-zh-71.
- DeepSeek-R1 shows only modest gains with iterative RAG on Wiki-based problems, implying it may have already internalized much of the necessary knowledge, which highlights the need for open-web benchmarks like WebPuzzle.

#### 4.4. Comparisons with Wiki-based Methods

DeepDiver, trained in a real-world Chinese internet setting, generally outperforms prior English-trained, Wiki-based methods when evaluated on English benchmarks.
- **On WebPuzzle-en**: DeepDiver-Qwen achieves a 26.1% accuracy, significantly outperforming DeepResearcher (15.0%) and R1-Searcher (13.7%) by 11.1 and 12.4 points, respectively.
- **Robustness**: Despite the language and environment gap, DeepDiver maintains strong performance on Wiki-based datasets (BamBoogle, FRAMES, HotpotQA), often matching or exceeding baselines. This is attributed to DeepDiver's Search Intensity Scaling (SIS), which facilitates intensive information retrieval and verification, rather than reliance on internal knowledge or language-specific constraints. Wiki-based baselines make fewer search calls due to their "cleaner" training environments.

#### 4.5. Emergence of Search Intensity Scaling (SIS)

The research indicates that SIS is an emergent behavior rather than a direct result of reward shaping.
- The "extra search-call reward" designed to encourage tool use for complex problems exhibits a transient effect. Its trigger frequency significantly drops from 4.5% in early training steps (0-9) to 0.1% in later steps (70-80), fading after step 30.
- Crucially, tool-use rounds (search intensity) continue to increase sharply *after* this auxiliary reward largely fades, demonstrating that the model proactively leverages external tools to compensate for internal knowledge limitations, independent of direct incentives.

#### 4.6. Tolerance of the Reward Function

The design of the reward function impacts RL training trajectory.
- A looser reward function (scoring 1-10, ≥6 = reward) is effective for stabilizing early RL training.
- A stricter reward function (requiring at least 2/3 positive judgments from three evaluation rounds) is necessary to overcome performance plateaus in later training stages. Transitioning from loose to strict rewards resulted in a nearly 9-point performance increase on WebPuzzle, whereas continuing with loose rewards yielded no improvement or even a drop.

#### 4.7. Generalization to Open-ended Problems

DeepDiver, trained exclusively on closed-ended WebPuzzle problems, demonstrates generalization to open-ended tasks like long-form writing.
- On ProxyQA, DeepDiver achieves 32.72%, outperforming the R1-distilled model by 9.47 percentage points.
- DeepDiver also exhibits substantially higher response lengths and search queries, suggesting SIS encourages active information acquisition, leading to more comprehensive answers in open-ended contexts.

#### 4.8. Statistics of Information-Seeking Behaviors

WebPuzzle necessitates more complex information-seeking behaviors compared to wiki-based datasets, and DeepDiver's reasoning chains reflect this complexity.
- DeepDiver exhibits higher average occurrence counts for Reflection & Correction (0.45 on WebPuzzle-en), Conflict Resolution (0.31), and Verification & Denoising (1.72) compared to baselines on wiki datasets. This indicates richer and more sophisticated reasoning patterns.

#### 4.9. Human vs. DeepDiver Performance

WebPuzzle is challenging for human experts as well.
- Human evaluators achieved 44.0% accuracy on a subset, requiring an average of 6.16 search queries and browsing 9.28 web pages.
- DeepDiver achieved 40.0% accuracy with 10.69 search queries across 2.68 rounds, indicating a more comprehensive information-seeking process that aligns more closely with human problem-solving strategies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of large language models and information retrieval by addressing the limitations of existing methods in handling real-world, open-web information-seeking tasks.

#### 5.1. Advancement in Adaptive Information Seeking

The introduction of DeepDiver demonstrates a framework that enables LLMs to develop an emergent ability termed Search Intensity Scaling (SIS). This capability allows LLMs to dynamically adjust their search frequency and depth based on the complexity of the problem, moving beyond predefined prompting rules or static inference patterns learned through supervised fine-tuning. This represents an advancement in how LLMs can approach knowledge-intensive question answering, particularly in environments characterized by noisy and inconsistent information.

#### 5.2. Development of a Rigorous Benchmark

The creation of WebPuzzle, a 24k-sample training and 275-sample test benchmark, provides a rigorous tool for evaluating LLMs' information-seeking behaviors on the live internet. Unlike previous benchmarks that primarily relied on well-structured, "clean" wiki sources, WebPuzzle explicitly demands external retrieval, conflict resolution, content verification, and reflective self-correction. This dataset offers a more realistic assessment of LLM performance in open-domain scenarios, establishing a foundation for future research in this area.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Performance on Real-World Tasks

The findings indicate that DeepDiver-enhanced 7B-parameter models (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Pangu-7B-Reasoner) can achieve performance on real-web tasks comparable to much larger models, such as the 671B-parameter DeepSeek-R1. This suggests that fostering adaptive search behaviors through reinforcement learning can significantly boost the practical utility of smaller LLMs in complex, real-world applications where knowledge retrieval and reasoning are crucial.

#### 5.4. Generalization Across Tasks and Domains

The research demonstrates that the search-seeking policy generalized from closed-ended WebPuzzle queries to open-ended generation tasks, such as long-form writing. This indicates that the adaptive search behaviors learned through DeepDiver are not narrowly confined to the training task but possess broader applicability, potentially enabling LLMs to produce more comprehensive and well-evidenced outputs in various generative scenarios.

#### 5.5. Insight into RL Training Dynamics

The analysis of reward functions and the emergence of SIS provides insights into the dynamics of reinforcement learning for LLMs. The transient role of explicit search rewards and the subsequent growth of intrinsic search intensity suggest that RL can cultivate genuine, proactive tool-use behaviors, rather than merely shaping responses to immediate incentives. The findings regarding loose versus strict rewards also offer practical guidance for designing effective RL curricula for complex tasks.

In summary, this work advances adaptive, verifiable, and scalable information seeking in LLMs by providing a dedicated open-web benchmark and an RL-driven framework that cultivates dynamic search capabilities. It offers contributions to the development of more robust and intelligent LLMs capable of navigating the complexities of real-world information environments.
