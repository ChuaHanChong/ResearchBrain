# Latent Reasoning VLA: Latent Thinking and Prediction for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Xi'an Jiaotong University and collaborators developed LaRA-VLA, a Vision-Language-Action model that integrates multi-modal Chain-of-Thought reasoning into continuous latent representations. This approach achieves efficient real-time robotic control with significantly reduced inference latency and improved performance on complex manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models incur substantial computational overhead and high inference latency due to explicit, lengthy reasoning traces.
- Most CoT formulations rely on discrete representations (language or visual tokens), introducing a representational mismatch with the inherently continuous nature of perception and action in embodied robotics.
- Achieving efficient and robust real-time control for complex, long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks remains challenging with current VLA approaches.

## Method

- **LaRA-VLA** integrates multi-modal ==Chain-of-Thought== reasoning into continuous ==latent representations==, using a ==Qwen3-VL== backbone with dedicated tokens for latent reasoning.
- A three-stage ==curriculum training== paradigm is employed: (1) explicit CoT fine-tuning, (2) curriculum-based replacement of discrete CoT with learnable latent representations, and (3) continuous action generation via a ==Diffusion Transformer== using a ==flow matching loss==.
- An automated annotation pipeline generates structured CoT datasets with detailed multimodal supervision for subtask decomposition, spatial grounding, and motion reasoning.

## Results

- LaRA-VLA achieved an average success rate of **97.9%** on the LIBERO benchmark, with **99.8%** on the Object suite and **96.6%** on the Long suite.
- The model attained the highest average success rate of **68.8%** on the SimplerEnv-WidowX benchmark and consistently outperformed baselines (e.g., ACT, GR00T N1.5) on real-world long-horizon manipulation tasks.
- Inference latency was reduced to **135 ms** per rollout on an NVIDIA A100 GPU, representing up to a **90%** reduction compared to explicit CoT methods, enabling real-time robotic deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Internalizing multi-modal Chain-of-Thought reasoning into continuous latent representations dramatically reduces inference latency and computational overhead, making VLA models viable for real-time robotic control.
> - Structured latent CoT representations, especially when learned jointly across text and visual modalities with predictive supervision, provide more effective and stable guidance for embodied policy learning than explicit textual reasoning.
> - A progressive curriculum-based training strategy is crucial for successfully transitioning from explicit CoT supervision to fully latent embodied reasoning, maintaining structured and non-collapsing latent representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a notable direction for developing scalable and general-purpose robotic manipulation systems. These models aim to establish an end-to-end mapping from rich multimodal observations and natural language instructions to continuous robotic control commands. A critical advancement in VLA model performance and interpretability, particularly for long-horizon robotic tasks, has been the integration of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.

Existing CoT approaches in VLA models can be primarily categorized by their modality. Text-based CoT methods explicitly articulate intermediate reasoning steps in natural language, encompassing task decomposition, high-level planning, and verbalization of visual information. Examples include methods that generate extensive textual traces for reasoning. In contrast, visual CoT methods typically express reasoning through visual reconstruction or prediction, often by modeling future observations or intermediate visual states, frequently relying on discrete visual tokens generated by vector quantization-based tokenizers. A third category combines both textual and visual CoT to leverage multimodal intermediate representations.

Despite the demonstrated effectiveness of CoT, current methods present two primary challenges. First, explicit text-based CoT often necessitates extensive textual reasoning during inference. This process leads to increased token length, substantial KV-cache usage, high memory consumption, and considerable inference latency, rendering real-time robotic control at typical frequencies (e.g., 5 Hz or 1 Hz) impractical. Second, the majority of existing CoT formulations rely on discrete representations, whether language tokens for textual CoT or discrete visual tokens for visual CoT. The authors argue that this discrete representation introduces a mismatch with the inherently continuous nature of perception and action in embodied robotic settings.

The concept of implicit or continuous reasoning in latent space has emerged in large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) as a means to circumvent the verbosity and computational overhead of explicit reasoning while retaining multi-step computation. This approach seeks to preserve reasoning capabilities within continuous hidden states or variables. Fast-ThinkAct, a closely related work, addresses inference cost by distilling explicit textual CoT into latent representations, but it maintains visually grounded reasoning in discrete trace-based forms.

This work contributes to this landscape by extending latent CoT reasoning to embodied VLA models. It aims to internalize both textual and future-oriented visual CoT into continuous latent representations, directly addressing the limitations of high inference overhead and the discrete-continuous representational mismatch inherent in prior CoT-based VLA systems. The proposed method differentiates itself by performing unified reasoning and prediction entirely in a continuous latent space, which contrasts with approaches that selectively latentize only textual CoT.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and effective framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, named Latent Reasoning VLA (LaRA-VLA), that internalizes multi-modal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning into continuous latent representations for embodied action control. This objective is driven by two main motivations addressing the limitations of existing CoT-based VLA approaches:

1. **Mitigate High Inference Overhead:** Existing text-based CoT methods, while effective for reasoning, incur substantial computational costs and high inference latency. The explicit generation of lengthy textual reasoning traces leads to increased token lengths, excessive KV-cache usage, and high memory consumption. This renders these methods unsuitable for real-time robotic control, where low latency is critical (e.g., control frequencies of 5 Hz or 1 Hz are often insufficient). The motivation is to eliminate the need for explicit CoT generation at inference time, thereby significantly reducing latency and computational demands to enable efficient, action-oriented control.

2. **Address Representational Mismatch:** A majority of current CoT formulations rely on discrete representations, whether in the form of language tokens for textual CoT or discrete visual tokens generated by VQ-based tokenizers for visual CoT. The authors contend that this discrete nature of reasoning creates a fundamental mismatch with the continuous perception and action spaces inherent in embodied robotic environments. The objective is to internalize reasoning into continuous latent representations, thereby aligning the reasoning process with the continuous nature of robotic perception and control.

To achieve these motivations, the specific objectives of the LaRA-VLA framework include:
- **Unified Latent Reasoning and Prediction:** To design a VLA model that performs reasoning and prediction entirely within a continuous latent space.
- **Multi-modal Latent Internalization:** To absorb both textual CoT and future-oriented visual CoT into continuous latent representations. This involves using visual goal latents to provide implicit multimodal supervision for textual latent reasoning.
- **Curriculum-Based Training Paradigm:** To develop a structured training strategy that progressively transitions the model from explicit multi-modal CoT supervision to latent embodied reasoning. This curriculum should gradually replace explicit discrete reasoning with compact continuous latent representations and adapt these latent dynamics to directly condition continuous action generation.
- **Creation of Structured CoT Datasets:** To construct automated CoT annotation pipelines and curate datasets (LIBERO-LaRA, Bridge-LaRA, and real-robot demonstrations) featuring structured multi-modal reasoning, including subtask decomposition, movement reasoning, and target object localization, to facilitate the training of the latent reasoning paradigm.
- **Demonstrate Efficiency and Robustness:** To empirically evaluate LaRA-VLA's performance on both simulation benchmarks and real-world long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks, demonstrating its effectiveness, inference efficiency, and robustness compared to existing state-of-the-art VLA methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Latent Reasoning VLA (LaRA-VLA) framework introduces a unified approach for integrating multi-modal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning into continuous latent representations. The methodology encompasses a three-stage curriculum-based training paradigm, a specialized attention mechanism, and an automated data collection pipeline.

**Model Architecture:**
LaRA-VLA utilizes Qwen3-VL as its backbone Vision-Language Model (VLM), leveraging its pre-trained reasoning capabilities and image encoder for consistent visual representations. A dedicated `<img next>` token is introduced to represent predicted visual latents, enabling explicit supervision and alignment during the initial stages of latent reasoning learning. For action prediction, the approach evolves across stages:
- In **Stages I and II**, an autoregressive action token design is employed, similar to prior work (Pertsch et al., 2025), to enable joint learning of action generation with latent reasoning.
- In **Stage III**, explicit action token prediction is removed. Instead, a 16-layer Diffusion Transformer functions as an "action expert," which conditions on the learned latent representations to produce continuous action trajectories.

**Training Procedures (Three Stages):**

1. **Stage I: Explicit CoT Fine-Tuning:**
 - **Objective:** To adapt the VLM to embodied manipulation tasks by leveraging explicit intermediate reasoning provided by CoT annotations.
 - **Inputs:** Visual observations are mapped to visual tokens (`v`) by the image encoder, and language instructions are tokenized into textual tokens (`x`).
 - **Textual CoT Supervision:** The VLM is trained to autoregressively generate a sequence of CoT tokens. Discrete ground-truth textual tokens supervise this generation via teacher forcing, minimizing a negative log-likelihood loss (`L_cot`).
 - **Visual Alignment:** The model predicts the visual latent representation (`ˆz_t+1`) of the next observation. This prediction is supervised by an L1 loss (`L_vis`) against the actual next visual latent (`z_t+1`), which is encoded by the same visual encoder. To stabilize this learning and prevent representation collapse, an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the online encoder parameters is used to compute the target visual latents.
 - **Action Inference:** An inverse dynamics model `f(v_t, v_t+1 | x, c) = a_t` estimates the action from current and next visual states, conditioned on the instruction and intermediate reasoning. Coarse-grained action semantics are propagated across latent reasoning steps using a fast recursive generation framework, and action tokens are trained with an autoregressive objective (`L_act-dis`).
 - **Overall Loss (Stage I):** `L_cot + 0.1 * L_vis + L_act-dis`.

2. **Stage II: Curriculum-based Replacement of Discrete CoT Tokens:**
 - **Objective:** To progressively internalize explicit textual reasoning into a continuous latent space.
 - **Method:** This stage continues to use the same training objectives as Stage I. However, a curriculum-based strategy is applied where subsets of explicit CoT tokens are gradually masked out and replaced with learnable latent representations (referred to as `<thinking>` tokens).
 - **Curriculum Schedule:** The proportion of discrete CoT tokens decreases over training iterations until the entire chain-of-thought is represented fully in the latent space. This process relies on visual prediction objectives and action signals to implicitly supervise the newly learned latent reasoning dynamics.
 - **Overall Loss (Stage II):** `0.2 * L_vis + L_act-dis` (as `L_cot` is progressively annealed to zero).

3. **Stage III: Action Generation via Flow Matching:**
 - **Objective:** To adapt the latent reasoning mechanism to continuous control, enabling efficient continuous action generation.
 - **Method:** The discrete action supervision from previous stages is replaced by a continuous action regression objective using flow matching.
 - **Action Expert:** The Diffusion Transformer (action expert) predicts a velocity field `v_θ(a_τ, τ | h_t)` conditioned on a multimodal latent context `h_t`. This context `h_t` aggregates the latent representation of the current visual observation, language instruction, the intermediate text-based reasoning latent (from Stage II), and the predicted future visual latent.
 - **Overall Loss (Stage III):** `L_act-con` (flow matching loss).

**LaRA Attention Mechanism:**
An attention mechanism tailored for the three-stage paradigm is employed. It operates on multimodal tokens: text (instruction/CoT/latent), current image, future image, and action tokens.
- In **Stages I and II**, future image tokens attend causally to text and current image tokens, with bidirectional interaction among themselves. Action tokens are generated autoregressively, attending to all preceding text, current image, future image tokens, and previously generated action tokens.
- In **Stage III**, action tokens are excluded from the attention computation, and the model focuses solely on text and vision tokens under similar attention constraints.

**Data Collection:**
An automated annotation pipeline is developed to construct structured CoT datasets, addressing the limitations of prior fragmented annotation methods. This "anchor-first, generate-later" paradigm uses semantic and temporal anchors:
- **Semantic Anchors:** Qwen3-VL identifies manipulated objects from the initial frame and task instruction.
- **Temporal Anchors:** Robot trajectories are segmented into atomic manipulation stages based on gripper state changes.
- **Subtask Annotation:** Qwen3-VL generates concise subtask descriptions for each segment.
- **Target Object Bounding Boxes:** GroundingDINO and SAM3 perform open-vocabulary grounding to produce temporally consistent 2D bounding box trajectories for target objects, enhanced by multi-frame ensemble and linear interpolation for robustness.
- **Motion Reasoning:** End-effector trajectories are used to derive global and local motion vectors, which are then discretized into directional descriptors and included in CoT annotations.
This pipeline generated **LIBERO-LaRA** and **Bridge-LaRA** datasets for simulation, as well as a real-robot manipulation dataset.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of LaRA-VLA involved comprehensive experiments across simulation benchmarks and real-world robotic manipulation tasks, focusing on performance, efficiency, robustness, and internal mechanism analysis.

**Simulation Experiments:**
- **LIBERO Benchmark (Table 2):** LaRA-VLA achieved an average success rate of 97.9%, outperforming a broad range of state-of-the-art VLA methods, including those with no CoT, textual CoT, visual CoT, and other latent CoT approaches (e.g., Fast-ThinkAct). Specifically, it reached 99.8% on the Object suite and 96.6% on the Long suite, indicating proficient object-centric reasoning and robustness in extended tasks.
- **SimplerEnv-WidowX Benchmark (Table 3):** LaRA-VLA demonstrated the highest average success rate of 68.8%, surpassing all baseline categories. This result suggests effective real-to-sim generalization under diverse visual conditions. The consistent outperformance across both benchmarks indicates that latent reasoning provides more stable guidance for action prediction and generalizes more effectively than explicit CoT supervision.

**Real-World Experiments:**
- **Performance (Figure 5):** On long-horizon real-world manipulation tasks using an Agilex Cobot Magic platform, LaRA-VLA achieved the highest average success rate among compared methods, exceeding ACT, ECoT, and GR00T N1.5. It showed leading performance on three out of four tasks, particularly benefiting tasks requiring multi-stage reasoning and temporal coordination, implying improved robustness to error accumulation.
- **Subtask-Level Analysis (Table 7):** On tasks with strong sequential dependencies (e.g., "Find the Block and Place It"), LaRA-VLA showed consistent gains over GR00T N1.5, suggesting its latent reasoning mechanism is effective in maintaining cross-subtask coherence.

**Analysis:**
- **Ablation Study (Table 5):**
 - Latent textual CoT supervision (64.6% success rate) yielded larger performance gains than explicit textual CoT (58.3%) over a no-CoT baseline (55.2%).
 - The joint incorporation of latent visual CoT and latent textual CoT achieved the best performance (68.8%), indicating that predictive visual information and multimodal regularization enhance policy learning. This suggests that structured latent CoT, particularly when multimodal, is more effective than explicit textual reasoning.
- **Robustness to Visual Perturbations (Table 4 & Figure 6):**
 - Under various severities of Gaussian noise and blur, LaRA-VLA consistently maintained higher success rates compared to a no-CoT baseline (Qwen-GR00T).
 - Analysis of latent-space distributions (Figure 6) showed that latent reasoning tokens remained semantically consistent with only moderate distributional shifts under corruption, suggesting stability of the learned latent representations.
- **Latent Collapse Analysis (Figure 7):** The study found no evidence of latent representation collapse. Latent tokens associated with different reasoning components formed distinct, semantically coherent clusters, demonstrating functional specialization. Language instruction tokens occupied a separate subspace, indicating that latent CoT did not simply reuse language embeddings. These findings suggest that predictive supervision and action grounding maintain structured latent reasoning.
- **Inference Efficiency (Figure 8):** LaRA-VLA achieved the lowest inference latency at 135 ms per rollout on an NVIDIA A100 GPU. This represents up to a 90% reduction in inference time compared to explicit CoT methods (e.g., ThinkAct-7B at 7513 ms, ECoT-7B at 4434 ms). It was also faster than Fast-ThinkAct (805 ms), which also uses latent CoT, attributed to its compact latent design and lightweight action expert.

**Additional Experiments (Appendix D):**
- **Effect of Action Pretraining (Table 8):** Discrete action supervision during the pretraining stages (I and II) consistently improved the average success rate from 60.4% to 64.2%, suggesting that it helps organize the latent reasoning space for better alignment with the robot's action space.
- **CoT Supervision Without Inference-Time Reasoning (Table 9):** Training with CoT supervision, even if CoT tokens were disabled during inference, improved the average success rate (61.4% vs. 55.2% for no CoT). However, enabling latent CoT reasoning at inference time yielded the best results (68.7%), indicating that explicit latent reasoning provides benefits beyond implicit representation shaping.
- **Effect of EMA on Latent Stability (Figure 13):** The Exponential Moving Average (EMA) target encoder for visual latents improved latent diversity (higher feature-space rank) and semantic separation of different reasoning components, leading to more stable latent CoT learning, even if not strictly necessary to prevent immediate collapse.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper introduces LaRA-VLA, a latent reasoning framework designed for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This framework represents a development in embodied artificial intelligence by addressing several limitations inherent in existing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) based VLA systems.

**Significance:**
- **Addressing Inference Efficiency and Representational Mismatch:** A primary contribution is the demonstration that multi-modal CoT reasoning can be effectively internalized into continuous latent representations. This directly tackles the challenges of high inference overhead and the representational mismatch between discrete CoT tokens and continuous perception-action spaces, which are common in prior VLA models.
- **Enabling Real-time Robotic Control:** By reducing inference latency by up to 90% compared to explicit CoT-based approaches, LaRA-VLA enhances the feasibility of deploying VLA models for real-time robotic control. This efficiency is critical for responsive and reliable operation in dynamic physical environments.
- **Improved Performance and Robustness:** The consistent outperformance of LaRA-VLA against state-of-the-art methods in both simulated and long-horizon real-robot manipulation tasks indicates its effectiveness. Furthermore, its demonstrated robustness to visual perturbations suggests that latent reasoning can contribute to more reliable robotic systems in varied conditions.
- **Advancement in Embodied Reasoning:** The introduction of a curriculum-based training strategy for progressively transitioning from explicit to latent reasoning provides a methodological blueprint for developing more sophisticated implicit reasoning capabilities in embodied agents. The use of visual latent objectives and EMA-stabilized encoders further refines this learning process.
- **Data Generation Paradigm:** The development of an automated CoT annotation pipeline, encompassing subtask decomposition, spatial grounding, and motion reasoning, offers a structured approach to generating rich, multimodal datasets essential for training advanced reasoning models in robotics.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Wider Adoption of VLA Models:** By making VLA models more efficient and robust, this work could facilitate their broader adoption across various robotic applications, including industrial automation, logistics, assistive robotics, and domestic tasks. The ability for robots to execute long-horizon, complex tasks based on natural language instructions in real-time could lead to more versatile and intelligent robotic systems.
- **Future Research Directions:** This research opens avenues for further investigation into the nature of implicit reasoning in AI. Future work could explore increasing the expressiveness of latent CoT without risking latent collapse, optimizing the efficiency of the curriculum training process, and applying similar latent reasoning paradigms to other complex control domains.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** More efficient and capable VLA models could lead to more intuitive and effective human-robot interaction, as robots become better at interpreting high-level commands and executing them with contextual reasoning.

The paper acknowledges that the broader impact of such advances in robotic systems depends on their application and governance. It does not identify any unique societal risks introduced by this work beyond those generally associated with robotic technologies.
