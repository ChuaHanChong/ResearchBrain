# RoboProcessBench: Benchmarking Process-Aware Understanding in Vision-Language Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboProcessBench presents a benchmark for evaluating vision-language models' intrinsic process-aware understanding in robotic manipulation through 12 diagnostic task families and a corpus of 58,000 physically grounded QA pairs. The benchmark highlights current VLM limitations in fine-grained process comprehension but shows that post-training leads to substantial improvements, such as over 40 percentage points in static monitoring.

## Problem

- Vision-language models (VLMs) in robotic manipulation are increasingly employed as judge-like components, requiring fine-grained understanding of manipulation processes beyond just final task success.
- Existing VLM evaluation methods primarily focus on outcome-only evaluations, failing to systematically decompose or assess the VLM's intrinsic capabilities for process-aware understanding.
- There is a lack of dedicated benchmarks and structured data to comprehensively evaluate and train VLMs on critical process-awareness aspects like intermediate states, motion, and temporal progression in robotic tasks.

## Method

- Developed **RoboProcessBench**, a benchmark designed for VLM-side ==process judging==, which decomposes process understanding into 12 diagnostic task families across "static monitoring" and "dynamic reasoning" dimensions.
- Curated ProcessData, a corpus of approximately 58,000 physically grounded process-aware question-answer pairs derived from 260 diverse manipulation tasks and segmented into local process units (LPUs).
- Structured ProcessData into ProcessData-SFT for supervised fine-tuning and ProcessData-Eval for diagnostic evaluation, ensuring distinct splits to prevent data leakage and enable both training and assessment.

## Results

- Zero-shot VLMs exhibit broad limitations and highly uneven performance across the 12 diagnostic task families, with tasks like Primitive-local Progress Recognition (T5) and Temporal Ordering (T8) performing close to random chance.
- Post-training on ProcessData-SFT yielded consistent and substantial performance gains, with improvements of 42.7 to 44.4 percentage points in static monitoring and 21.4 to 23.6 points in dynamic reasoning for respective base models.
- Tasks focused on temporal ordering (T8) remained close to chance even after post-training, indicating that short visual context and current VLM architectures are insufficient for robust temporal reasoning in robotic manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Process-aware understanding in VLMs is not a single generic skill but a structured set of fragmented subskills, with uneven performance across different diagnostic task families.
> - The structured process signals within ProcessData-SFT provide a broadly learnable supervision source, demonstrably improving VLM capabilities in both static monitoring and dynamic reasoning tasks.
> - Despite significant gains from post-training, robust temporal reconstruction (e.g., Temporal Ordering) and comprehensive outcome prediction remain persistent bottlenecks for current VLM architectures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language models (VLMs) have increasingly been integrated into robotic manipulation systems, extending their roles beyond basic perception and language-conditioned reasoning. Contemporary applications position VLMs as active components that function as visual critics, online reward generators, and failure detection systems. This shift implies a requirement for VLMs to evaluate not just the final outcome of a task, but also the physical and temporal progression of manipulation executions.

Existing large-scale robot datasets and embodied benchmarks have advanced manipulation research by providing diverse trajectories and tasks for policy learning and embodied evaluation. These resources often address broad capabilities like visual-spatial reasoning, planning, and language-conditioned manipulation. However, the structured elements of manipulation processes, such as contact, motion states, and local sub-progress, are typically treated as auxiliary information rather than primary evaluation targets.

A growing area of research emphasizes the importance of fine-grained process and reward signals, moving beyond binary success rates. Efforts include step-level and subgoal-level evaluation, execution monitoring, precondition–effect modeling, and the use of intermediate execution signals for policy evaluation and reward generation. Related resources also provide intermediate representations, primitive-level annotations, or hierarchical manipulation structures to support detailed reasoning. While these works highlight the utility of process signals, their primary focus tends to be reward modeling, monitoring, control, or representation learning.

Furthermore, VLMs are being developed specifically as robotic "judges" or feedback modules. Examples include VLM-based action selection, vision-language-action critics for predicting progress, large reward models for online reward generation, and systems for detecting and reasoning over manipulation failures. One particularly relevant work formulates dense robotic auditing with process reward models and introduces metrics for outcome, process, and diagnosis-level evaluation. These initiatives demonstrate the capacity of VLMs to serve as critics and monitors. However, they generally evaluate the judge *within* a downstream pipeline and do not systematically decompose the underlying VLM-side process understanding abilities required for such judgmental roles. The paper identifies this gap as the motivation for developing a benchmark specifically designed to assess and improve VLMs' intrinsic process-aware understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this work stems from the evolving role of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in robotic manipulation. As VLMs are increasingly employed as judge-like components – functioning as visual critics, reward generators, and failure detectors – they are implicitly required to possess a fine-grained understanding of how a manipulation execution unfolds over time and in physical space. This capability extends beyond merely recognizing objects or determining final task success; it necessitates an understanding of intermediate process states, such as whether relevant contact has been established, if motion is active, the stage of a local manipulation primitive, the temporal consistency of actions, and the prerequisites for subsequent primitives. Such requirements define process-understanding problems grounded in the physical evolution of states.

Despite these advanced requirements, existing evaluation methodologies for VLMs in robotics primarily focus on outcome-only evaluations or assume the presence of a capable judge without systematically dissecting its underlying process understanding abilities. Previous work has begun to address subgoal-level evaluation and progress-aware critics, but these approaches tend to instantiate a judge and then assess its utility for tasks like policy auditing or failure analysis, rather than comprehensively evaluating the VLM's intrinsic capacity for process awareness.

To address this identified gap, the paper sets forth several key objectives for RoboProcessBench:

1. **Develop a Comprehensive Benchmark:** To create a dedicated benchmark, RoboProcessBench, specifically for evaluating process-aware understanding in vision-language robotic manipulation.
2. **Decompose Process Understanding:** To systematically decompose the VLM-side abilities required for process judging into 12 diagnostic task families. These families are categorized into two complementary dimensions: "static monitoring" (identifying current local process state) and "dynamic reasoning" (inferring motion, progress, temporal relations, outcome, and primitive-level continuation). This decomposition aims to provide a granular assessment of different subskills.
3. **Construct a Physically Grounded Corpus:** To curate ProcessData, a corpus of approximately 58,000 process-aware question-answer pairs derived from 260 diverse manipulation tasks. This corpus is built from physically grounded execution traces, ensuring relevance to real-world robotic scenarios.
4. **Facilitate Both Evaluation and Training:** To structure ProcessData into distinct splits (ProcessData-SFT for supervised fine-tuning and ProcessData-Eval for evaluation), enabling both diagnostic assessment of existing VLMs and the development of more process-aware models.
5. **Diagnose VLM Limitations:** To conduct an extensive evaluation of various VLMs on ProcessData-Eval to reveal specific limitations in their process understanding across the 12 diagnostic task families.
6. **Demonstrate Learnability of Process Signals:** To demonstrate that the structured process signals within ProcessData-SFT can serve as a learnable supervision source, leading to improved process-aware understanding in VLMs after post-training.

By achieving these objectives, RoboProcessBench aims to not only diagnose the current state of VLM capabilities in process understanding but also to provide a systematic framework and data resource for advancing this critical aspect of robotic intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboProcessBench is designed to evaluate and train VLMs on process-aware robotic manipulation understanding, focusing on how a manipulation execution unfolds from visual observations and optional task context. The methodology involves the decomposition of manipulation episodes, a structured question taxonomy, and a data construction pipeline.

#### 3.1. Benchmark Design

1. **Evaluation Target:** RoboProcessBench evaluates VLM-side abilities for process judging, distinct from action generation or closed-loop policy success. It focuses on intermediate process structure rather than only final task success.
2. **Anchored Local Process Units (LPUs):** A core design principle involves segmenting long manipulation episodes into local process units (LPUs). Each LPU, defined by a start and end time, represents a temporally localized decision context corresponding to a coherent manipulation sub-progress. LPUs are either contact-anchored (derived from contact onset, persistence, or release in contact-rich sources) or primitive-anchored (aligned with explicit low-level action annotations). This abstraction allows for the evaluation of local state, sub-progress, and action-chain reasoning.
3. **Question Taxonomy:** The benchmark comprises 12 question families categorized into two main groups:
- **Static Monitoring:** Tasks that require identifying the current local process state from a single frame or a short ordered clip. These include Phase Recognition (T1), Contact Detection (T2), Bimanual Coordination State Recognition (T4), and Current Primitive Recognition (T10). These tasks assess manipulation-relevant state inference rather than generic object recognition.
- **Dynamic Reasoning:** Tasks that necessitate temporal, predictive, progress-centric, or chain-level inference. These include Motion Direction Prediction (T3), Primitive-local Progress Recognition (T5), Motion State Recognition (T6), Operation Outcome Prediction (T7), Temporal Ordering (T8), Temporal Priority Prediction (T9), Next Primitive Prediction (T11), and Primitive Chain Restoration (T12). These tasks evaluate the VLM's ability to infer process evolution and continuation.
- Tasks T10-T12 are considered "primitive-aware extensions" as they rely on explicit native primitive annotations, while others are built from physical, temporal, or outcome signals.

#### 3.2. Benchmark Construction (ProcessData)

1. **Data Aggregation:** ProcessData aggregates approximately 58,000 process-aware QA items from 260 manipulation tasks, drawing from four complementary robotic manipulation sources:
- **GM-100:** Provides long-tail goal-conditioned manipulation tasks.
- **RH20T:** Contributes contact-rich multimodal traces.
- **REASSEMBLE:** Offers explicit primitive-level action chains.
- **AIST-Bimanual:** Supplies bimanual motion and coordination patterns.
 Each dataset is utilized for the specific process supervision it supports best, ensuring comprehensive coverage across the 12 task families.
2. **Ground-truth (GT) Construction Principles:**
- Preference for source-native supervision over free-form manual annotation.
- Labels are constructed only after identifying task-relevant LPUs.
- Human intervention is limited to calibration and audit, not exhaustive relabeling.
- A deterministic, source-aware GT builder generates each item, mapping source-native signals and metadata to a discrete answer.
- Ambiguous samples near transition boundaries, weak-motion intervals, or unstable states are filtered using task-specific guard margins.
3. **Visual Input Format:** The visual input varies per task family to provide the minimal necessary temporal context. For instance, T1 and T2 use single frames; T3, T4, T5, T6, T7, T10, T11, and T12 use short ordered clips; T8 uses three shuffled frames; and T9 uses a two-frame comparison. Source-native signals (e.g., timestamps, force/torque, gripper state) are used solely for GT construction and are never exposed to the VLM during inference.
4. **Data Splitting:** ProcessData is divided into ProcessData-SFT (for supervised fine-tuning) and ProcessData-Eval (for evaluation) with an approximate 85/15 ratio. Strict episode and recording isolation is maintained between the splits to prevent data leakage and ensure evaluation reflects generalization across execution instances rather than memorization.

#### 3.3. Experimental Protocol

1. **Task Formulation:** All tasks are formatted as multiple-choice visual question answering (MCQA).
2. **Metrics:** Results are reported as accuracy (%) on ProcessData-Eval.
3. **Evaluated Models:** A diverse set of open-source VLMs (QwenVL-family, InternVL-family, RoboBrain, GLM) and closed-source VLMs (Gemini, GPT variants, Claude variants) are evaluated.
4. **Post-training:** Two post-trained VLMs, ProcessData-SFT-Qwen (based on Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and ProcessData-SFT-Intern (based on InternVL-3-8B), are created by supervised fine-tuning on ProcessData-SFT. LoRA (rank 8, alpha 16) is used for fine-tuning the language model while keeping the vision encoder frozen.
5. **Manual Audit:** A compact human reliability check is performed on 50 sampled items per task family from ProcessData-Eval. Two independent annotators achieved average accuracy above 90%, confirming the visual answerability of the tasks for humans. This also provides a human baseline for comparison with VLM performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of various VLMs on ProcessData-Eval and the impact of post-training with ProcessData-SFT revealed specific strengths and limitations in process-aware understanding.

#### 4.1. Diagnostic Results on ProcessData-Eval (Zero-shot Performance)

1. **Broad Limitations and Uneven Performance:** Current VLMs, both open- and closed-source, exhibit broad limitations in process-aware understanding. Performance is highly uneven across the 12 diagnostic families, suggesting that this capability is not a single generic VLM skill but a structured set of subskills.
2. **Fragmented Strengths:** No single zero-shot model consistently outperformed others across all task families. Different VLM families demonstrated varying strengths, indicating they capture different subsets of local state, motion, temporal, or primitive-level cues. This fragmented pattern underscores the value of task-level diagnostics provided by RoboProcessBench.
3. **Local Process-State Grounding vs. Progress Estimation:** Tasks focused on identifying relatively visible local states (e.g., Contact Detection (T2), Motion State Recognition (T6), Primitive Chain Restoration (T12)) were more accessible to zero-shot VLMs. In contrast, Primitive-local Progress Recognition (T5), which requires explicit estimation of how far a local manipulation step has advanced, remained significantly harder, with the strongest zero-shot result at 34.4% (close to the 33.3% random baseline for 3 choices). This gap highlights a key challenge for VLMs.
4. **Temporal Reconstruction as the Hardest Regime:** Temporal Ordering (T8) and Temporal Priority Prediction (T9) proved to be the most challenging tasks, with most zero-shot models performing close to random chance (best T8 at 22.3%, T9 near 50% random baseline). This indicates that short visual context alone does not automatically enable robust temporal reasoning in robotic manipulation, identifying a critical missing capability for VLMs expected to act as process monitors.

#### 4.2. Effect of ProcessData-SFT Supervision (Post-trained Models)

1. **Broadly Learnable Supervision:** Post-training on ProcessData-SFT led to consistent and substantial performance gains for both ProcessData-SFT-Qwen and ProcessData-SFT-Intern, initialized from Qwen2.5-VL-7B and InternVL-3-8B respectively. This indicates that ProcessData-SFT provides a learnable supervision signal that transfers across different VLM backbones.
2. **Significant Gains in Static Monitoring and Dynamic Reasoning:**
- **Static Monitoring:** Both post-trained models significantly improved static monitoring capabilities, with ProcessData-SFT-Qwen and ProcessData-SFT-Intern achieving 77.2% accuracy, marking improvements of 42.7 and 44.4 points over their respective base backbones.
- **Dynamic Reasoning:** Dynamic reasoning also showed substantial improvement, with gains of 23.6 and 21.4 points, reaching 68.9% and 69.5% for ProcessData-SFT-Qwen and ProcessData-SFT-Intern, respectively.
 These improvements were particularly consistent on tasks related to phase recognition (T1), contact detection (T2), bimanual coordination (T4), motion direction prediction (T3), motion-state recognition (T6), current primitive recognition (T10), next primitive prediction (T11), and primitive-chain restoration (T12).
3. **Improved Primitive-Local Progress Recognition (T5):** Post-training notably improved performance on T5, with both ProcessData-SFT-Qwen and ProcessData-SFT-Intern reaching approximately 45.4% accuracy. This represents an improvement of about 11 points over the strongest zero-shot baseline, suggesting that structured process supervision can enable VLMs to recognize some visual evidence of advancement within a manipulation primitive, although this task remains far from saturated.
4. **Persistent Bottlenecks:** Despite overall improvements, temporal ordering (T8) remained close to chance even after post-training. Temporal priority prediction (T9) showed only marginal improvement for one model. Outcome prediction (T7) also improved less consistently compared to local state and primitive-aware tasks. These persistent weaknesses suggest that current process supervision and VLM architectures do not fully address robust temporal reconstruction or reliable outcome prediction.

In summary, RoboProcessBench demonstrated that current VLMs lack uniform process-aware understanding, particularly in tasks requiring fine-grained progress estimation and temporal reasoning. However, the benchmark also proved to be an effective learnable substrate, with supervised fine-tuning significantly improving VLM performance on many process-aware cues, establishing a foundation for developing more capable robotic "judge" modules.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboProcessBench contributes to the field of robotic manipulation and vision-language models in several ways, offering both diagnostic capabilities and a foundation for future development.

1. **Diagnostic Tool for VLM Capabilities:** The benchmark provides a systematic and granular diagnostic tool for assessing the process-aware understanding of VLMs in robotic manipulation. By decomposing this complex capability into 12 distinct task families across static monitoring and dynamic reasoning, RoboProcessBench moves beyond aggregated scores to pinpoint specific strengths and weaknesses of different VLM architectures. This diagnostic ability is crucial for identifying bottlenecks in VLM development and guiding targeted research efforts.
2. **Learnable Supervision Source:** The curated ProcessData corpus, particularly the ProcessData-SFT split, serves as a learnable supervision source. The experimental results demonstrate that supervised fine-tuning on this data consistently improves VLMs' performance on various process-aware cues (local state, motion, progress, primitive-aware). This indicates that many process labels derived from physically grounded traces correspond to visually learnable signals, providing a valuable resource for training more capable models.
3. **Foundation for Robotic "Judge" Modules:** By explicitly evaluating the VLM-side abilities required for judgmental roles in robotics (e.g., visual critics, reward generators, failure detectors), RoboProcessBench establishes a critical capability substrate. Improved process-aware understanding can lead to more effective and reliable VLM-based systems that can monitor robotic manipulation, provide meaningful progress signals, detect failures, and ensure temporal consistency with primitive-level continuations.
4. **Guidance for Future Research:** The benchmark highlights persistent challenges, particularly in robust temporal reconstruction (T8, T9) and comprehensive outcome prediction (T7), even after post-training. These remaining bottlenecks suggest avenues for future research, such as developing stronger video modeling techniques, incorporating explicit temporal objectives, and enhancing long-horizon video memory in VLMs.
5. **Promotion of Fine-grained Evaluation:** RoboProcessBench advocates for a fine-grained evaluation paradigm that scrutinizes intermediate process structures rather than solely relying on final task success. This approach fosters a deeper understanding of VLM performance in complex manipulation tasks and encourages the development of models that can reason about the nuances of physical interactions and temporal evolution.

The authors acknowledge limitations, emphasizing that RoboProcessBench is a diagnostic benchmark and not a direct proxy for closed-loop policy success or a general-purpose VLM leaderboard. However, its structured approach to evaluating process-aware understanding offers a significant step towards developing more intelligent and reliable robotic systems capable of effectively monitoring and responding to the intricacies of manipulation processes.
