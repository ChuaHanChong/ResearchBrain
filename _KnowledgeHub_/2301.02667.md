# Locomotion-Action-Manipulation: Synthesizing Human-Scene Interactions in Complex 3D Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> LAMA (Locomotion-Action-Manipulation) synthesizes long-term human motion in cluttered 3D indoor scenes using only unpaired motion-capture data. A reinforcement-learning action controller optimized at test time drives a motion-matching synthesizer with scene-occupancy state and collision rewards, while an autoencoder motion manifold edits motion for object manipulation. It cuts foot-contact error to 4.34 cm and penetration to 1.30%, and wins perceptual studies against prior work.

## Problem

- Prior scene-interaction methods depend on motion datasets paired with scanned 3D scenes, which are rare, hard to scale, and limit coverage of real-world diversity.
- Earlier work tackles sub-problems in isolation: static pose placement, or interaction with a single target object, without long-term navigation through cluttered rooms.
- Existing systems delegate navigation to separate path planners that approximate the body as a cylinder, breaking down under highly complex cluttered geometry.

## Method

- Scene-aware **Action Controller**: an ==RL== policy optimized per test scene outputs action type, short-term future root forecasts, and posture offsets from body, occupancy-grid, and interaction-cue state.
- **Motion Synthesizer** built on ==motion matching== searches a captured motion database for the next clip, then applies the policy's posture offset; collision and transition-smoothness rewards shape optimization.
- **Task-Adaptive Motion Editor** uses a convolutional-==autoencoder== motion manifold, optimizing a latent vector so hands follow manipulation-cue trajectories while foot and root regularizers preserve naturalness.

## Results

- LAMA reaches **4.34 cm** foot-contact error and **1.30%** penetration across PROX scenes, beating SAMP (**11.75**, **7.18**) and Wang et al. (**6.32**, **2.75** with post-processing).
- Perceptual A/B tests over 50 sequences gave LAMA **215** votes against **35** for Wang et al. (**16.27%** relative) and **176** against **74** for SAMP (**42.04%**).
- Removing the collision reward raises penetrating frames from **1.1%** to **15.7%**; augmentation-generalized policies lift start-point success from **10.5-25.9%** to **40.2-81.7%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Test-time optimization can replace supervised training when paired data is scarce: RL fits a per-scene policy from motion-only capture, sidestepping dataset collection entirely.
> - A learned collision reward over occupancy state beats geometric path planning in clutter, because the policy shapes full body pose, not just root trajectory.
> - Editing inside a learned motion manifold preserves spatiotemporal joint correlations that inverse kinematics destroys, but per-scene optimization costs minutes, limiting interactive use.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human motion synthesis, particularly involving interactions within 3D environments, is a highly active and challenging area within computer graphics and computer vision. Prior research has approached this complex problem by focusing on sub-problems, such as:

- **Static Pose Estimation:** Many methods have concentrated on placing humans realistically within 3D environments or interacting with objects in a static pose, often from diverse inputs like object semantics, images, or text descriptions. These approaches, while foundational, do not address dynamic, long-term movements.
- **Dynamic Human-Object Interactions (HOI) for Specific Tasks:** A significant body of work has tackled dynamic interactions, such as sitting on chairs or carrying boxes. However, these methods typically focus on a single target object or a limited range of interaction types, often neglecting long-term navigation through cluttered scenes or general manipulation. Hand manipulation, while crucial, has also often been treated as a separate problem.
- **Dynamic Human-Scene Interactions with "Scene-Paired" Datasets:** More recent and relevant methods have extended to synthesizing dynamic interactions in real-world 3D scenes. A major limitation of these approaches is their reliance on "scene-paired" motion datasets, such as PROX, where human motion is simultaneously captured with its surrounding 3D environment. Such datasets are exceedingly rare, difficult to acquire, and challenging to scale, fundamentally limiting the generalizability and diversity of synthesized motions. Some works have used synthetic datasets or short motion snapshots as alternatives, but these still face limitations.
- **Navigation through Cluttered Environments:** Methods that do incorporate navigation often rely on separate path planning modules (e.g., A* algorithm) that approximate human and scene geometry (e.g., as cylinders). These approximations can lead to unnatural or collision-prone movements in highly complex or tightly constrained scenarios.
- **Motion Synthesis and Editing:** Techniques leveraging motion graphs, motion matching, and deep learning-based motion manifold learning have been successful in generating and editing natural human motions. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has also emerged as a powerful tool for physics-based and data-driven character control. LAMA integrates these advanced motion synthesis techniques within a broader interaction framework.

LAMA addresses the critical gap left by previous work by proposing a **unified framework** that seamlessly integrates **locomotion, scene interaction, and object manipulation** in complex 3D environments. Crucially, it achieves this **without requiring "scene-paired" motion datasets for supervision**, formulating the problem as a **test-time optimization**. This independence from paired data represents a significant advancement, enabling the system to be applied to arbitrary 3D scenes, including newly scanned ones, without extensive data acquisition.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the LAMA system are multifaceted:

- **Unified Framework for Complex Human Behaviors:** The core objective is to develop a single, coherent framework capable of synthesizing a continuous sequence of everyday human motions. This includes navigating through cluttered indoor environments (locomotion), interacting with various scene elements (scene interaction like sitting), and manipulating objects (e.g., opening a door or a trash can). Current methods tend to address these components in isolation or in fragmented ways.
- **Independence from Scene-Paired Motion Data:** A key motivation is to overcome the severe limitations imposed by the scarcity and difficulty of acquiring "scene-paired" motion datasets. Unlike existing approaches that rely on motion data specifically captured within a target 3D scene, LAMA aims to synthesize high-quality human-scene interactions using **only generic human motion capture data**. This paradigm shift dramatically increases the applicability and scalability of motion synthesis systems to diverse and novel 3D environments.
- **Natural and Plausible Long-Term Motions:** The system is driven by the goal of generating motions that are not only physically plausible (avoiding collisions and maintaining contact) but also appear natural and human-like over extended durations (up to 10 seconds). This requires seamless transitions between different types of actions and robust adaptation to environmental constraints.
- **Test-Time Optimization Paradigm:** To achieve independence from scene-paired data, LAMA formulates the synthesis problem as a "test-time optimization." This means that the system optimizes the character's behavior for a *given specific scene and task* at inference time, rather than relying on a pre-trained model on a large, paired dataset. This allows for adaptability to unique scene geometries and interaction requirements.
- **Handling Complexity and Diversity:** The motivation also stems from the inherent complexity and diversity of real-world 3D environments (clutter, varied object arrangements) and human behaviors within them. LAMA seeks to provide a robust solution that can generalize to these challenges.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LAMA (Locomotion-Action-MAnipulation) is structured as a three-level system designed to synthesize human motions by leveraging a reinforcement learning (RL) framework, motion matching, and manifold-based motion editing. It takes a 3D scene (W), desired interaction cues (Φ), and an initial character state (g_init) as input to produce a sequence of human poses (M).

#### 3.1. Overview of the Three-Level System

The system comprises:
- **Action Controller (A):** An RL-based module responsible for high-level decision-making, guiding the character's locomotion and scene interaction while adapting to the 3D environment and avoiding collisions. It operates as a test-time optimizer.
- **Motion Synthesizer (S):** A motion matching module that translates the action controller's high-level decisions into plausible low-level human postures by searching a motion database.
- **Task-Adaptive Motion Editor (E):** A manifold-based motion editing module that post-processes the synthesized motion for fine-grained adjustments, particularly for object manipulation and adapting actions to diverse object geometries.

#### 3.2. Scene-Aware Action Controller (A)

The Action Controller performs locomotion and desired actions seamlessly, avoiding collisions without requiring scene-paired datasets or prior training for a specific scene. It is directly optimized at test-time to choose the most plausible motion clip from a database.

- **State (s_t):** Represents the current status of the human character, composed of:
 - `s_body`: Joint rotations and velocities, root up-vector, root height, and end-effector positions.
 - `s_scene`: 2D occupancy grid of neighboring cells around the agent on the floor plane and the character's global root position in the grid.
 - `s_inter`: The desired action cue (e.g., target root and foot positions for sitting).
- **Action (a_t):** The control policy outputs a feasible action:
 - `a_type`: Probabilities of the next action type (e.g., walk, sit, stop).
 - `a_future`: Predicted future motion cues, such as root positions for short-term future frames.
 - `a_offset`: Posture offset applied to the raw motion data from the motion synthesizer, allowing for scene-aware pose adjustments.

#### 3.3. Motion Synthesizer (S)

The Motion Synthesizer produces the next plausible posture given the current posture and action signal from the Action Controller. It uses a modified motion matching algorithm.

- **Motion Features (y_t):** For each frame in the motion database, features are extracted, including joint positions and velocities, root up-vector, foot contact cues, and short-term future posture cues (root position and orientation at future timesteps). These are pre-computed for efficiency.
- **Motion Searching and Updating:** Given a query motion feature (x_t) from the Action Controller, the system searches the motion database for the best matching clip using weighted Euclidean distances. The selected raw motion (m_k*) is then updated with the `a_offset` from the Action Controller to generate the next posture (m_t+1). This process is performed periodically to maintain temporal coherence.

#### 3.4. Optimizing Scene-Aware Action Controller

The RL framework optimizes the policy by maximizing a discounted cumulative reward, guiding the character towards desired actions while maintaining physical plausibility and collision avoidance. The total reward (R_total) consists of three main terms:

- **Trajectory Reward (R_tr):** Encourages movement towards the action cue while avoiding collisions.
 - `r_coli`: Penalizes collisions between the character's box-shaped limbs and the 3D scene. This is a crucial scene-aware term.
 - `r_pos`: Rewards proximity to the target action cue's positional constraints.
 - `r_vel`: Encourages the character to move (penalizes stillness).
- **Action Reward (R_act):** Encourages fulfilling the specific action cue.
 - `r_inter`: Rewards meeting the positional constraints of the action cue (e.g., sitting on a chair).
 - `r_∆t`, `r_∆v`: Smoothness rewards that minimize abrupt changes in posture and velocity during transitions.
- **Regularization Reward (R_reg):** Penalizes excessive modification by `a_offset` and maintains temporal consistency between frames.
The system uses early termination and limited action transitions to accelerate policy optimization.

#### 3.5. Generalizing Action Controller

While primarily a test-time optimization, the optimized policy can handle some variations in input. For broader generalization, the policy can be optimized with random variations of initial states and action cues during policy optimization (similar to a learned policy for a fixed scene). This allows a single policy to handle diverse tasks more efficiently for a given scene.

#### 3.6. Task-Adaptive Motion Editing (E)

This module covers the diversity in interactions, particularly for object manipulation, using a manipulation cue (ϕ_M) that specifies desired end-effector trajectories.

- **Motion Manifold Learning:** A convolutional autoencoder is trained on various human motion sequences to compress them into a latent vector within a motion manifold space. This manifold preserves spatiotemporal consistency and naturalness.
- **Editing Process:** The optimal latent vector (z*) is found by minimizing a loss function (L) that constrains the decoded motion to follow the manipulation cue (ϕ_M) while maintaining foot locations and root trajectories of the original motion. This ensures the edited motion remains realistic and coherent.
- **Manipulation Cue Generation:** For articulated objects, cues are semi-automatically generated by simulating the object's motion (e.g., from SAPIEN dataset) and extracting target vertex trajectories for the character's hand.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

LAMA was rigorously evaluated through extensive quantitative metrics and qualitative perceptual studies across diverse real-world 3D scenes.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup and Evaluation Metrics

- **Dataset:** A new motion capture dataset was collected using an IMU-based system (XSens MVN Link), featuring locomotion and interactions (e.g., walk-to-sit). This motion-only data formed the database for the motion synthesizer. PROX and Matterport3D datasets provided 3D scenes, and SAPIEN object meshes were used for manipulation scenarios.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Physical Plausibility:** Measured by `Contact` (penalizes foot movement when in contact, in cm, lower is better) and `Penetration` (percentage of frames where body penetrates scene objects, lower is better).
 - **Naturalness:** Assessed via perception studies (A/B tests) on Amazon Mechanical Turk, where human observers selected more natural motions based on human-likeness and scene plausibility (percentage, higher is better relative to LAMA).
- **Baselines:** Compared against state-of-the-art methods:
 - **Wang et al.:** Long-term 3D human motion synthesis, with and without post-processing.
 - **SAMP:** Generalizable interaction synthesis using explicit path planning for navigation.
 - **Ablation Baselines:** LAMA without collision reward, without action offset, without smoothness rewards, and motion editing replaced by Inverse Kinematics (IK).

#### 4.2. Comparisons with Previous Work

- LAMA generated 50 motion sequences in 4 PROX 3D scenes for comparison, focusing on walk-to-sit motions. Policy optimization per input took 4-20 minutes, with 3-4 minutes for motion editing optimization.
- **Physical Plausibility:** LAMA significantly outperformed both Wang et al. and SAMP. For instance, LAMA achieved a Contact score of 4.34 cm and a Penetration score of 1.30%, compared to Wang et al.'s 6.32 cm / 2.75% (with post-processing) and SAMP's 11.75 cm / 7.18%. This demonstrates LAMA's superior collision avoidance and realistic foot contact.
- **Naturalness:** In perception studies, LAMA was overwhelmingly preferred. Against Wang et al., LAMA received 215 votes versus 35 (relative ratio 16.27% for Wang et al.). Against SAMP, LAMA received 176 votes versus 74 (relative ratio 42.04% for SAMP). This confirms the high perceptual quality and realism of LAMA's synthesized motions.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Action Controller:**
 - Removing the **collision reward (r_coli)** led to a substantial increase in penetration (15.7% of frames vs. 1.1% in original LAMA), confirming its critical role in scene-aware behavior.
 - Without **action offset (a_offset)**, the character failed to make fine-grained limb adjustments (e.g., tilting arms to avoid walls), demonstrating its contribution to detailed scene-aware poses.
 - Lack of **smoothness rewards (r_∆t, r_∆v)** resulted in unnatural and abrupt movements, highlighting their importance for temporal coherence.
- **Task-Adaptive Motion Editing:**
 - Replacing the manifold-based motion editing with traditional **Inverse Kinematics (IK)** for object manipulation led to awkward poses (e.g., for opening a toilet lid). LAMA's manifold approach produced natural movements by reflecting spatiotemporal correlations (e.g., bending knees, tilting hips), while IK struggled to maintain full-body naturalness.
 - The motion editing module also enabled characters to adapt actions (e.g., sitting) to chairs of different shapes.

#### 4.4. Robustness Test of Action Controller

- **Generalization to Varying Inputs (Fixed Scene):** A policy optimized for a single task and initial state could successfully synthesize motions for other distinct initial positions and action cues within the same scene, demonstrating inherent generalization capabilities (e.g., 10.5% to 25.9% success rate across different action cues).
- **Augmentation Strategy for Generalization:** When the policy was optimized with random variations of inputs (g_init and ϕ_A), its robustness significantly increased, covering a much wider range of initial conditions and action cues (e.g., 40.2% to 81.7% success rate). This "generalized policy" could be used for inference only, drastically reducing computation time (0.15s vs. 379s for optimization from scratch).
- **Generalization to Unseen Scenes:** A generalized policy optimized in one scene (W0) showed reasonable generalization to an unseen scene with similar structure (W1, e.g., 70.5% success for one action cue). However, performance decreased substantially when tested on a totally distinct unseen scene (W2), indicating that while robust within a learned scene, direct generalization to vastly different scene layouts remains a challenge without re-optimization or fine-tuning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LAMA system represents a significant step forward in the field of human motion synthesis, offering several key contributions and potential impacts:

- **Breaking the Dependency on Scene-Paired Data:** This is LAMA's most impactful contribution. By formulating motion synthesis as a test-time optimization and relying *only* on generic motion capture data, LAMA bypasses the prohibitive cost and limitations of acquiring scene-paired datasets. This significantly broadens the applicability of such systems to any 3D environment, whether pre-existing public datasets, newly scanned scenes (e.g., via Polycam), or virtual worlds. This unlocks new possibilities for personalized virtual agents and dynamic content creation.
- **Unified and Comprehensive Motion Synthesis:** LAMA provides a holistic solution that seamlessly integrates locomotion, complex scene interactions, and fine-grained object manipulation within a single framework. This contrasts with previous approaches that often tackle these elements in isolation or with limited scope, leading to more complete and believable character behaviors.
- **Enhanced Realism and Plausibility:** Through its sophisticated combination of RL, motion matching, and manifold learning, LAMA achieves state-of-the-art quality in synthesizing natural, physically plausible motions. Superior collision avoidance and realistic interaction (both quantitative and qualitative) lead to more immersive and believable virtual experiences.
- **Adaptability and Robustness:** The test-time optimization paradigm, coupled with the ability to generalize across varying initial conditions and tasks within a scene (especially with augmentation), makes LAMA highly adaptable. While generalization to entirely unseen scenes still poses a challenge, the framework allows for efficient re-optimization or fine-tuning, making it practical for diverse applications.
- **Advancements in Data-Driven Animation and Character Control:** LAMA pushes the boundaries of data-driven animation by demonstrating how existing motion capture libraries can be intelligently repurposed for complex, scene-aware interactions. The integration of deep reinforcement learning with traditional motion matching techniques also offers valuable insights for future research in physics-based character control.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Virtual Reality and Gaming:** LAMA could revolutionize character animation in VR/AR and video games, enabling highly interactive and adaptive NPCs (non-player characters) that seamlessly respond to dynamic environments and user actions.
- **Robotics and Human-Robot Interaction:** The principles of scene-aware, collision-avoiding human motion synthesis could inform the development of more natural and safe human-robot collaboration, particularly in shared spaces.
- **Animation and Content Creation:** Animators could leverage LAMA to rapidly prototype complex interaction sequences, significantly reducing manual effort and increasing the realism of animated characters in film, advertising, and simulations.
- **Digital Twins and Simulation:** Creating realistic digital twins of humans interacting in simulated environments for architectural planning, ergonomic studies, or safety assessments.

**Future Directions:**
The authors themselves identify interesting future research avenues, such as combining this framework with supervised learning for further efficiency gains, and addressing the synthesis of motions for characters with varying body shapes and sizes, which is currently a fixed skeleton assumption. These extensions could further enhance LAMA's versatility and real-world applicability.
