# PhysAlign: Physics-Coherent Image-to-Video Generation through Feature and 3D Representation Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Washington University in St. Louis, Purdue University, and NVIDIA developed PhysAlign, an adapter framework for Image-to-Video (I2V) generation that addresses physical inconsistency in generated videos. The framework leverages a dual alignment strategy in the latent space of existing diffusion models, incorporating learned kinematic priors from a video foundation model and explicit 3D geometry constraints to produce physically coherent and temporally stable videos, demonstrating superior performance in motion plausibility and temporal consistency compared to baselines.

## Problem

- Current video diffusion models often generate content that violates fundamental physical laws, leading to temporally inconsistent or implausible motions and object interactions.
- Existing VDMs frequently lack 3D perceptual fidelity, struggling to maintain consistent 3D spatial structures, perspectives, and realistic object interactions like correct occlusion.
- There is a scarcity of real-world videos explicitly annotated with physics and 3D information, which are crucial for training models to understand fine-grained dynamics and causal motion patterns.

## Method

- **PhysAlign**, an efficient adapter framework, is designed to infuse physical knowledge and 3D geometric priors into existing ==DiT-based I2V models== using ==LoRA-based fine-tuning==.
- A fully controllable synthetic data generation pipeline, built on ==Blender's rigid-body simulation==, produces 3,000 multi-modal videos with RGB, depth maps, and detailed physics metadata for training.
- The framework employs ==dual latent space alignment==: physical knowledge injection via ==Gram-based spatio-temporal relational alignment== with a ==V-JEPA2== teacher model, and 3D geometry injection through a lightweight 3D-convolution head predicting depth with a multi-component loss.

## Results

- PhysAlign achieved the highest Physical Invariance Scores (PIS) on both synthetic (e.g., `a_x` score of **0.632** vs. **0.520** for Wan2.2) and real-world test sets, confirming its effectiveness in producing physics-consistent motion.
- The model consistently outperformed baselines in VBench I2V metrics, showing superior `i2v subject` (**0.911**), `i2v background` (**0.928**), and `motion smooth.` (**0.996**) scores, indicating strong spatial fidelity and smoother motion.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical, complementary contributions of both feature-level representation alignment and 3D geometry alignment, with the full PhysAlign model consistently achieving the strongest performance across all metrics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fine-tuning existing large video diffusion models with a relatively small dataset of 3,000 targeted synthetic clips can effectively internalize complex physical laws and robustly generalize to real-world scenarios.
> - Explicitly aligning the latent space of generative models using both learned kinematic priors from a video foundation model (V-JEPA2) and direct 3D geometry constraints is crucial for achieving physics-coherent and temporally stable video generation.
> - A parameter-free continuous trilinear interpolation for spatio-temporal grid adaptation, combined with a margin-based L1 Gram matrix loss, effectively transfers structural physical knowledge while preserving generative diversity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work addresses a prevalent limitation in current video diffusion models (VDMs): their struggle with physical coherence. While modern VDMs have achieved notable progress in visual quality for tasks like content creation and dynamic environment simulation, they often generate content that violates fundamental physical laws, leading to temporally inconsistent or implausible motions and object interactions. This inconsistency significantly restricts their practical utility in applications requiring strict physical grounding, such as robotics or realistic media generation.

Previous efforts to integrate physical knowledge into video generation models can be broadly categorized by where physics is introduced in the pipeline:
- **Physics in the Input Space**: Methods in this category use external simulation tools (e.g., Material Point Method) to generate physically plausible trajectories or scene states, which then condition a video generator (e.g., PhysGen3D, PhysGaussian). While offering explicit control, these approaches often require scenario-specific rule design and may not generalize across diverse environments.
- **Physics in the Latent Space**: A growing body of work embeds physical principles directly into the latent representations or motion structures of generative models (e.g., PhysDiff, NewtonGen, WonderPlay). This approach allows the generative model itself to maintain plausible dynamics, which is the direction PhysAlign primarily explores.
- **Physics in the Output Space**: Some techniques enforce physics post-generation, using external evaluators like large language models or vision-language models to assess and refine physical consistency (e.g., GPT4Motion, PhyT2V). However, the implicit nature of their physical reasoning can lead to robustness issues in challenging or out-of-distribution scenarios.

Regarding Image-to-Video (I2V) generation specifically, recent advancements, particularly DiT-based architectures (e.g., CogVideoX, LTX-Video, HunyuanVideo, Wan2.2), have improved spatial fidelity and scalability by directly conditioning on a reference image. Nevertheless, these models primarily rely on RGB cues and generally lack explicit 3D or physics modeling, often resulting in physically implausible motions.

PhysAlign positions itself within this landscape by proposing an efficient adapter framework that integrates physical knowledge and 3D geometric priors into *existing* DiT-based I2V models (specifically building upon Wan2.2-14B). Unlike methods that rely on implicit learning or external post-hoc corrections, PhysAlign explicitly grounds the generative model's latent space through a dual alignment strategy involving both learned kinematic priors from a video foundation model and explicit 3D geometry constraints. This approach aims to bridge the gap between raw visual synthesis and rigid-body kinematics, providing a more data-efficient and robust solution for physics-coherent video generation without training a new model from scratch.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed limitations of current video diffusion models (VDMs) in generating physically coherent and temporally stable video content. Despite their ability to produce visually high-quality videos, existing models frequently fail to adhere to fundamental physical laws and often lack 3D perceptual fidelity, leading to several critical issues:

1. **Violation of Basic Physical Laws**: Generated videos often exhibit motions or object interactions that contradict basic physics, such as inconsistent gravitational acceleration, implausible collisions, or non-conserving momentum trajectories. For example, objects might bounce unrealistically, penetrate each other, or accelerate without apparent cause.
2. **Lack of 3D Perceptual Fidelity**: The generated content frequently struggles to maintain consistent 3D spatial structures and perspectives. This can manifest as incorrect occlusion ordering, inaccurate size changes as objects move relative to the camera, or distortions in object appearance over time.
3. **Limited Practical Applicability**: Such physical inconsistencies diminish the reliability and utility of VDMs in real-world applications where physical grounding is crucial. This includes domains such as robotics (for training and simulation), scientific visualization, and professional media production requiring realistic physical dynamics.

The core reason for these deficiencies is that current VDMs primarily learn physical coherence implicitly from vast, unannotated video datasets. These datasets typically lack explicit physical cues such as depth, force vectors, object mass, or detailed object state information, which are vital for understanding and synthesizing fine-grained dynamics and causal motion patterns.

To address these limitations, the PhysAlign research sets forth several key objectives:

- **To Develop a Physics-Coherent I2V Generation Framework**: The central objective is to create an efficient framework capable of synthesizing videos from a single image and text prompt that are consistently physics-coherent, meaning they adhere to real-world physical laws.
- **To Enhance 3D Visual Consistency**: The framework aims to ensure that generated videos maintain 3D perceptual fidelity, respecting spatial structure, perspective, and realistic object interactions (e.g., correct occlusions, appropriate size changes).
- **To Overcome the Scarcity of Physics-Annotated Data**: A significant motivation is to mitigate the critical shortage of real-world videos explicitly annotated with physics and 3D information. This is addressed by constructing a fully controllable synthetic data generation pipeline.
- **To Integrate Physical Knowledge and 3D Priors Efficiently**: The research aims to develop an efficient method to inject explicit physical knowledge and 3D geometric priors into existing video generation foundation models. This is achieved by designing an adapter framework that operates on the model's latent space, avoiding the need to train a new model from scratch and thus promoting data efficiency and robust generalization.
- **To Bridge the Gap between Visual Synthesis and Rigid-Body Kinematics**: Ultimately, the work seeks to establish a practical paradigm that connects raw visual synthesis capabilities of generative models with the principles of rigid-body kinematics, paving the way for more genuinely physics-grounded dynamic scene generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhysAlign is an efficient adapter framework designed to infuse physical knowledge and 3D geometric priors into existing Video Diffusion Models (VDMs), specifically DiT-based architectures, for physics-coherent image-to-video (I2V) generation. The methodology centers on three main components: a physics-aware synthetic data generation pipeline, physical knowledge injection via video representation alignment, and 3D geometry injection.

#### 3.1. Physics Simulator for Data Generation

To circumvent the scarcity of physics-annotated videos, the authors developed a fully controllable synthetic data generation pipeline built upon Blender’s rigid-body simulation engine. This simulator provides fine-grained control over physical parameters and object interactions.
- **Object Diversity**: The simulator generates scenes featuring a range of rigid objects (e.g., balls, cylinders, cubes, cones, icospheres) to approximate diverse real-world object distributions.
- **Parameter Configuration**: For each scene, diverse physical parameters are configured, including object mass, size, elastic coefficients, surface friction, initial drop height, applied force magnitudes, and launch angles. These parameters are sampled from realistic ranges to simulate varied physical conditions.
- **Dynamic Scenes**: Scenes are configured with 3 to 7 objects, assigned initial horizontal velocities based on randomly sampled force directions (0-360 degrees) and random drop heights. This design promotes natural variations in motion trajectories and introduces the probability of object collisions, ensuring the generated videos capture physics-consistent rigid-body dynamics.
- **Multi-Modal Output**: The pipeline renders 90-frame sequences at 512x512 resolution, providing:
 - **RGB Frames**: Photorealistic appearance with ray-traced lighting.
 - **Depth Maps**: Metric distance information from the camera, crucial for 3D geometry conditioning.
 - **Physics Metadata**: Detailed annotations such as object mass, diameter, force in Newtons, starting height in meters, and pixel-space coordinates of applied forces.
 - **Text Annotations**: Automatically generated natural language descriptions integrating all physics parameters, serving as textual conditions.
- **Dataset Size**: The pipeline generates a dataset of 3,000 such synthetic videos for training.

#### 3.2. Physical Knowledge Injection via Video Representation Alignment

This component aims to transfer the physical understanding of dynamic scenes into the VDM by aligning its latent space with a robust, temporally-aware teacher model.
- **Teacher Model**: V-JEPA2, a video foundation model, is employed as a teacher. V-JEPA2 encodes video clips to produce motion-aware patch embeddings that inherently capture temporal evolution and dynamic object interactions, providing rich kinematic priors.
- **Hidden-State Extraction and Projection**: Intermediate hidden states `H^(b)` are extracted from the middle transformer blocks of the DiT model. A lightweight MLP projector `phi^(b)` maps these states to the teacher's embedding space, resulting in projected tokens `Y_hat^(b)`.
- **Parameter-Free Spatio-Temporal Grid Adaptation**: To align student and teacher representations, the projected DiT tokens `Y_hat^(b)` are reshaped into a 5D tensor. A parameter-free continuous trilinear interpolation is then applied to rescale these features to match the teacher’s spatio-temporal grid dimensions. This approach is chosen to preserve fine-grained geometric details without introducing structural biases from parameterized downsamplers.
- **Physical Relation Alignment**: Instead of rigid token-wise alignment, the method aligns the structural knowledge of the physics world using a Gram-based spatio-temporal similarity matrix. For both student and teacher features, a relation matrix `G` is computed, where each element represents the pairwise cosine similarity between spatio-temporal tokens. This matrix encapsulates both intra-frame spatial layouts and inter-frame temporal motion trajectories.
- **Physical Alignment Loss (`L_Phys`)**: A margin-based L1 penalty is used to minimize the discrepancy between the student’s relation matrix `G_S^(b)` and the teacher’s `G_T`. The margin `m` provides a tolerance threshold, penalizing only significant relational discrepancies, which helps preserve the generative diversity of the diffusion model while enforcing kinematic constraints.

#### 3.3. 3D Geometry Injection

To ensure generated videos respect 3D scene geometry, depth information is explicitly integrated into the latent space.
- **Depth Prediction Head**: A lightweight 3D-convolution head is attached to the DiT model. This head takes intermediate hidden states from selected DiT transformer blocks, projects them, and concatenates them with the final video latents to predict depth latents `Z_hat_d`.
- **3D Alignment Loss (`L_3D`)**: A combined objective (`L_3D`) is used to supervise depth prediction from multiple perspectives:
 - **Latent Loss (`L_latent`)**: An L2 loss between predicted depth latents and ground-truth encoded depth maps, enforcing feature-space alignment and enhancing training stability.
 - **Pixel Loss (`L_pixel`)**: A scale-and-shift-invariant (SI) L2 metric on decoded pixel-resolution depth maps. This loss is robust to global scale ambiguities inherent in monocular depth estimation, focusing on relative geometric correctness.
 - **Structure Loss (`L_st`)**: An L1 penalty on the spatial gradients of predicted and target depth maps. This encourages sharp depth transitions at object edges, preserving discontinuities and preventing over-smoothing.
 - **Temporal Loss (`L_temp`)**: An L1 penalty on the frame-to-frame depth changes. This loss ensures temporal consistency in depth evolution, reducing flickering artifacts and enforcing physically plausible motion flow across frames.
- `L_3D` is a weighted sum of these four losses, providing comprehensive supervision for geometrically accurate and temporally stable depth prediction.

#### 3.4. Training Objective and Inference Behavior

- **Training Objective**: The base I2V diffusion transformer (Wan2.2) is fine-tuned using LoRA adapters. The overall training objective `L` combines the standard flow-matching loss (`L_FM`) with the physical alignment loss (`L_Phys`) and the 3D geometry alignment loss (`L_3D`), weighted by hyperparameters `λ_Phys` and `λ_3D`.
- **Inference**: At inference time, the auxiliary branches (video teacher and 3D/depth head) are discarded. The model operates with the learned LoRA adapters on the frozen I2V backbone. This design ensures that PhysAlign's runtime and memory footprint remain identical to the original base model, making it efficient and practical for deployment. The model generates physics-coherent videos conditioned solely on the input image and text prompt, benefiting from the physics-aware latent space shaped during training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PhysAlign demonstrates its ability to significantly enhance physical coherence and 3D perceptual fidelity in image-to-video (I2V) generation, both qualitatively and quantitatively. Comparisons were made against leading I2V baselines: CogVideoX-5B, HunyuanVideo I2V, and Wan2.2.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Comparisons

Figures 1 and 3 illustrate qualitative improvements. PhysAlign consistently produced videos with trajectories adhering to physically valid dynamics across various motion types, including rolling, bouncing, and falling, as well as complex multi-object interactions. The generated videos also maintained subject identity and aligned with the conditioning prompt. In contrast, competing approaches often exhibited issues such as object identity drift, implausible accelerations, or inconsistent collision responses. This suggests that PhysAlign generates motion that respects both initial conditions and underlying physical laws more effectively.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Evaluation Metrics

The performance was assessed using two primary metric families and additional benchmarks:
- **Physical Invariance Score (PIS)**: This metric quantifies the physical plausibility of object motion by analyzing 2D velocity and acceleration profiles derived from segmented object centroids. Higher scores indicate greater physical consistency.
- **VBench I2V**: A comprehensive benchmark evaluating general video generation quality, specifically for I2V tasks. It includes I2V-specific metrics (e.g., `i2v subject` and `i2v background` for consistency with the input image) and general quality metrics (e.g., `motion smooth.`, `dynamic degree`, `aesthetic`, `imaging`).
- **PhysicsIQ Benchmark**: This benchmark evaluates the physical reasoning capabilities of models, particularly in solid mechanics.
- **User Study**: Human participants rated videos based on Motion Physics Quality, Identity Physics Quality, and Overall Video Quality.

#### 4.3. Performance on Synthetic (Blender) Test Set

- **PIS (Table 1)**: PhysAlign achieved the highest PIS across all evaluated dimensions (acceleration in x/y, velocity in x/y, and change in size), approaching the scores of reference videos. For instance, its `a_x` score was 0.632, substantially higher than Wan2.2 (0.520) and CogVideoX-5B (0.350). These results confirm the effectiveness of using physics-aware synthetic data to internalize accurate motion patterns.
- **VBench I2V (Table 3)**: PhysAlign consistently outperformed baselines in both I2V-Metrics and Quality Metrics. It exhibited superior `i2v subject` (0.911), `i2v background` (0.928), and `motion smooth.` (0.996) scores, indicating strong subject and background consistency with the input image, as well as smoother motion. Its `dynamic degree` (0.730) was also notably higher than other models.

#### 4.4. Cross-Domain Generalization on Real-world (WISA-Test) Set

- **PIS (Table 2)**: PhysAlign demonstrated robust generalization, outperforming all baselines on the WISA-Test set in physics metrics. For example, its `a_x` score of 0.604 surpassed HunyuanI2V (0.568) and Wan2.2 (0.444).
- **VBench I2V (Table 4)**: The model achieved the highest scores for `i2v subject` (0.975) and `i2v background` (0.981), indicating strong spatial fidelity with respect to the reference image in complex real-world scenarios. Improved `motion smooth.` (0.995) and `dynamic degree` (0.635) were also observed. These results support the claim that physics kinematics learned from abstract synthetic data, when internalized through unified representation alignment, can effectively transfer to complex real-world dynamics.
- **PhysicsIQ Benchmark (Table 5)**: PhysAlign achieved an overall score of 38.1%, surpassing the base Wan2.2 model (29.6%) and a LoRA-trained Wan2.2 (34.5%). The gains were primarily in the solid-mechanics category, consistent with the focus on rigid objects.

#### 4.5. User Study (Supplementary Table 8)

A user study reinforced the quantitative findings. PhysAlign received higher average human preference scores across all three evaluated aspects: Motion Physics Quality (3.236), Identity Physics Quality (3.343), and Overall Video Quality (3.286), confirming its perceived effectiveness in generating physically coherent and high-quality videos.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies (Tables 6 & 7, Supplementary Tables 9 & 10)

- **Feature Alignment (FA) Contribution**: Removing the feature-level representation alignment resulted in a clear degradation in motion coherence, including irregular velocity profiles and inconsistent acceleration patterns, confirming its crucial role in maintaining consistent latent motion representations.
- **3D Geometry Alignment (3D-A) Contribution**: Removing the 3D geometry alignment reduced depth-awareness and scale consistency, leading to incorrect size changes and weakened physical plausibility. This highlights the necessity of geometric supervision for realistic perspective and motion trajectories.
- **Full Model Effectiveness**: The full PhysAlign model, integrating both semantic (FA) and geometric (3D-A) alignments, consistently achieved the strongest performance across all metrics, validating their complementary contributions.
- **Data Efficiency**: A comparison with a model trained directly on the real WISA dataset (10x more data) showed that PhysAlign, trained on only 3K synthetic videos, achieved superior performance. This suggests that targeted synthetic data with accurate physics annotations can be highly data-efficient for learning physics-aware generation.
- **Teacher Model Impact**: Replacing the V-JEPA2 video teacher with an image-centric DINOv3 model resulted in lower performance, particularly for motion and physics-related metrics. This indicates the importance of a video-native teacher for providing effective spatio-temporal supervision for dynamic scenarios.

Overall, the findings establish PhysAlign as a state-of-the-art method in physics-aware I2V generation, demonstrating enhanced performance in both controlled synthetic environments and challenging real-world scenarios, while also exhibiting data efficiency and robust generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PhysAlign represents a notable advancement in the field of image-to-video (I2V) generation by explicitly addressing the long-standing challenge of physical coherence. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Physical Plausibility and 3D Fidelity**: The core contribution is the development of a framework that significantly improves the physical plausibility and 3D perceptual fidelity of generated videos. By enforcing adherence to fundamental physical laws and maintaining consistent 3D spatial structures, PhysAlign generates videos that are more realistic and perceptually coherent than prior methods. This directly tackles a key limitation that has hindered the practical adoption of video generation models.
- **Data Efficiency and Robust Generalization**: The research demonstrates remarkable data efficiency, showing that fine-tuning with a comparatively small dataset of 3,000 synthetic clips can effectively internalize complex physical laws. More importantly, the model exhibits robust generalization from abstract synthetic shapes and scenarios to complex real-world videos. This finding suggests that large video diffusion models possess untapped latent capacities for physical reasoning, which PhysAlign's structured alignment acts as a "structural catalyst" to unlock. This efficiency could reduce computational costs and data annotation efforts for developing physics-aware generative models.
- **Efficient and Practical Framework**: PhysAlign operates as an adapter framework for existing DiT-based models (e.g., Wan2.2). Its LoRA-based fine-tuning means it does not require training a new model from scratch. Furthermore, at inference time, the auxiliary branches are discarded, ensuring that the runtime and memory footprint are identical to the standard base model. This makes PhysAlign a highly efficient and practical solution for real-world video generation tasks.
- **Methodological Innovation**: The work introduces several methodological contributions:
 - **Controllable Synthetic Data Pipeline**: The creation of a fully controllable rigid-body simulation pipeline that generates paired RGB, dense 3D representations, and granular physics annotations addresses a critical data bottleneck for physics-aware video generation.
 - **Dual Latent Space Alignment**: The proposed unified physical latent space, coupling explicit 3D geometry constraints with a Gram-based spatio-temporal relational alignment, provides a novel approach to deeply ground generative models in both spatial and kinematic principles.
- **Bridging Visual Synthesis and Kinematics**: By effectively connecting raw visual synthesis capabilities with rigid-body kinematics, PhysAlign establishes a practical paradigm for genuinely physics-grounded dynamic scene generation. This moves the field beyond purely aesthetic or semantic generation towards models that implicitly "understand" and simulate physical interactions.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Robotics and Simulation**: The ability to generate physics-coherent videos can significantly benefit robotics research by providing more realistic synthetic environments for training and testing agents, reducing the reliance on costly and time-consuming real-world experiments.
- **Media and Content Creation**: Animators and content creators could leverage PhysAlign to generate more believable and engaging video content, particularly for scenes involving physical interactions or dynamic objects, without requiring extensive manual physics simulation expertise.
- **Scientific Visualization**: Generating physically accurate dynamic scenes can aid in scientific visualization and understanding of complex physical phenomena.
- **Foundation Models**: The insights into decoupling kinematic rules and visual appearance within the latent space could inform the design of future foundation models for more robust world modeling and intuitive physics understanding.

**Future Directions**:
The authors suggest extending PhysAlign's capabilities to more complex physical regimes and scenes beyond rigid-body mechanics. They also propose exploring the integration of a physics agent interacting with the generator via reinforcement learning, aiming to further advance interactive physical reasoning in dynamic scene generation.

While PhysAlign marks a significant step towards realistic physics-aware video generation, the authors appropriately note that it focuses on intuitive physics (perceptual plausibility) rather than precise, scientifically rigorous simulation. Complex physical understanding and high-level reasoning still represent ongoing challenges in this domain.
