# Dynamic Rank Adjustment in Diffusion Policies for Efficient and Flexible Training

> [!summary] Summary
> The DRIFT framework allows for efficient and flexible training of large diffusion policies for robotic motion generation by dynamically adjusting trainable ranks. It reduces batch training time by 11% overall and 18% during online adaptation, making interactive imitation learning with these models practical while maintaining performance.

## Problem

- Overparameterized diffusion policies for robotics incur high computational costs and long training times, limiting practical deployment.
- Existing low-rank parameter-efficient methods (e.g., LoRA) are designed for fine-tuning pre-trained models and are unsuitable for training diffusion policies from scratch.
- The high training time of large diffusion policies renders online interactive imitation learning impractical, despite its potential for improved sample efficiency.

## Method

- Introduces DRIFT, a framework for dynamic rank adjustment in diffusion policies during training, consisting of Rank Modulation and a Rank Scheduler.
- Rank Modulation uses Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) to dynamically partition weight matrices into trainable and frozen components, operating directly on original weights without introducing new parameters.
- A Rank Scheduler coordinates the decay of trainable ranks based on the training epoch, with functions like sigmoid controlling the adjustment, and DRIFT is instantiated within a DAgger-like interactive imitation learning pipeline.

## Results

- The DRIFT-DAgger framework reduced overall batch training time by **11%** and online training time during adaptation by **18%** compared to full-rank baselines.
- DRIFT-DAgger, utilizing Rank Modulation with a sigmoid decay schedule and a terminal rank of 256, achieved comparable performance and sample efficiency to full-rank methods while improving training efficiency.
- Real-world experiments confirmed the simulation findings, demonstrating reduced cumulative training time and robust performance across various robotic tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically adjusting trainable ranks via SVD (Rank Modulation) directly on existing weights offers stability and efficiency for training from scratch, unlike LoRA which adds parameters and is unstable with dynamic changes.
> - A sigmoid-based rank decay schedule effectively balances early exploration with high ranks and later refinement with reduced ranks, optimizing performance and training time.
> - Training diffusion policies from scratch with rank adjustment requires a higher minimum trainable rank (e.g., 256) than typical fine-tuning scenarios (e.g., 4) to retain representational power.
