# EMP: Executable Motion Prior for Humanoid Robot Standing Upper-body Motion Imitation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Executable Motion Prior (EMP) framework, developed at Zhejiang University, enables humanoid robots to stably imitate human upper-body motions by dynamically adjusting desired motion targets to stay within the robot's physical and dynamic limits. This approach achieved a 98.1% success rate in simulations and demonstrated robust real-world performance on physical humanoid robots, successfully reducing self-collisions and improving overall stability.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle to stably execute complex upper-body manipulation tasks due to inherent physical and dynamic limitations.
- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) approaches for motion imitation often face a trade-off between achieving high motion similarity and maintaining robot stability, leading to either unnatural movements or instability.
- Model-based control methods for high-dimensional humanoid dynamics are computationally intensive and necessitate simplifications, compromising motion accuracy and dynamism.

## Method

- A reinforcement learning framework with a decoupled control strategy: an RL policy handles lower-body balance, while the **EMP** module processes upper-body targets.
- A ==Graph Convolutional Network (GCN)== with a VQ-VAE architecture for robust human motion retargeting, converting human motions into a suitable format for the robot and generating a large training dataset.
- The core Executable Motion Prior (EMP) module, a VAE-based network, dynamically adjusts potentially unstable upper-body motion targets based on the robot's current state and predicted future states to ensure executability and stability.

## Results

- The EMP method achieved a **98.1%** success rate in simulations, outperforming baselines like a Decoupled Policy (**97.0%**) and PMP (**97.4%**), while significantly reducing self-collisions (MSC **0.1494** vs. **0.3668** for Decoupled Policy).
- Ablation studies confirmed that removing any of the EMP's key loss components (smoothness, orientation, centroid) drastically reduced success rates and degraded stability metrics, validating their critical contributions.
- The framework successfully deployed on a full-sized physical humanoid robot, demonstrating stable imitation of various human motions and generalizing effectively to a second distinct humanoid platform.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly optimizing motion *targets* through a learned prior, incorporating predicted future states via a world model, effectively resolves the stability-fidelity conflict in humanoid motion imitation.
> - A comprehensive loss function for the EMP module, including terms for orientation, centroid, collision, and smoothness, is crucial for generating physically executable and stable upper-body motions.
> - Combining GCN-based motion retargeting with robust RL training and the EMP module enables successful sim-to-real transfer and generalization of complex upper-body skills across different humanoid robot platforms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots hold immense potential for assisting humans in various tasks, particularly those requiring interaction with human-designed environments and tools. A critical prerequisite for such applications is the ability to perform human-like movements stably and robustly, especially when executing complex upper-body manipulation while standing. This paper directly addresses this fundamental challenge.

Historically, humanoid motion control has been dominated by model-based approaches. While effective, these methods often suffer from high computational burdens, necessitating simplified robot models that limit their applicability to dynamic and complex motions. The advent of reinforcement learning (RL) has offered a promising alternative, initially in physics-based animation and more recently in real-world bipedal locomotion, whole-body control, and teleoperation.

Within the RL paradigm for humanoid motion imitation, a persistent conflict exists between achieving motion similarity (fidelity to the human motion) and maintaining robot stability. Directly imitating complex human upper-body movements can easily push a robot beyond its controllable range, leading to instability or falls. Conversely, overly cautious control sacrifices the naturalness and utility of the imitated motion. Previous RL approaches have explored whole-body control, which can suffer from vibrations, or decoupled policies that struggle with the robot's limited capacity. Some recent works, such as HumanPlus and Exbody, focus on whole-body control, while others, like PMP, introduce predictive motion priors to enhance robustness for decoupled control.

This paper positions itself as a significant advancement by specifically tackling the stability-fidelity trade-off in upper-body motion imitation for standing humanoids. It recognizes that while existing RL policies can generate lower-body actions for balance, directly feeding raw upper-body targets can still lead to instability. Inspired by humans' ability to adapt their movements to maintain balance, the authors introduce the **Executable Motion Prior (EMP)**. This novel module acts as an intelligent filter or adjuster, modifying target upper-body motions in real-time based on the robot's current state to ensure they remain within the robot's executable capabilities and preserve stability, all while minimizing deviation from the original intent. This approach distinguishes it from prior art by explicitly optimizing the motion *target* before execution, thereby enhancing the robustness and safety of RL-based imitation. The work builds upon recent successes in sim-to-real transfer and domain randomization, crucial for deploying RL policies on physical robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the necessity for humanoid robots to stably execute upper-body manipulation tasks in human-centric environments. The authors identify several core challenges:
1. **Limited Controllable Range:** Humanoid robots have physical and dynamic limitations that make direct imitation of all human motions infeasible without risking instability.
2. **Conflict between Stability and Similarity:** In existing reinforcement learning frameworks for motion imitation, policies often face a trade-off: aggressive pursuit of motion similarity can compromise stability, while prioritizing stability can lead to unnatural or distorted movements.
3. **Computational Complexity:** Model-based controllers for high-dimensional humanoid dynamics are computationally intensive, often requiring simplifications that reduce motion accuracy and dynamism.

Given these motivations, the key objectives of the paper are:
- To develop a robust reinforcement learning-based framework that enables humanoid robots to imitate human upper-body motions while simultaneously maintaining overall body stability.
- To design a motion retargeting network that can effectively transfer human motion data into a suitable format for humanoid robot control, thereby generating a large and diverse motion dataset.
- To introduce a novel component, the **Executable Motion Prior (EMP)**, which dynamically adjusts input upper-body motion targets based on the robot's current state. This adjustment aims to ensure the motions are executable and stable, minimizing deviations from the original target amplitude.
- To validate the proposed framework through extensive simulation experiments and demonstrate its practical applicability and effectiveness through real-world deployment on full-sized humanoid robots, including transfer to a different platform.

Essentially, the authors aim to empower humanoids to perform human-like upper-body tasks by developing a "safety mechanism" that pre-validates and adjusts motion commands, ensuring stability without losing the essence of the intended movement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a comprehensive reinforcement learning-based framework, dubbed EMP, for stable upper-body motion imitation. The methodology is structured into three main interconnected components: Motion Retargeting, RL Policy Training for Upper-Body Imitation, and the Executable Motion Prior (EMP) module itself.

#### 3.1. Motion Retargeting

A **Graph Convolutional Network (GCN)** based on a VQ-VAE architecture is designed to transfer human motions to the specific kinematics of the humanoid robot.
- **Architecture:** It comprises a motion encoder (`f_e`), a VQ-codebook layer, and a motion decoder (`f_d`). The encoder embeds source human motion (represented by key node positions and edge features) into a latent space (`z_A`). A transformation network (`f_tf`) then converts `z_A` to `z_B` for the target humanoid skeleton. The VQ-codebook quantizes `z_B` to `z_e`, and the decoder reconstructs the target humanoid joint angles. Key nodes like waist, torso, shoulder, elbow, and wrist are considered.
- **Training Loss:** The retargeting network is trained using a composite loss function that includes end-effector position loss, orientation loss (for wrist rotation), elbow position loss, and VQ-VAE specific losses (embedding and commitment losses) to ensure accurate and physically plausible motion transfer.
- **Dataset:** The GRAB dataset, a part of the larger AMASS dataset, is used as the source for human motion, retargeted to create a large-scale humanoid motion dataset.

#### 3.2. RL Control Policy Training for Humanoid Upper-Body Imitation

The control policy is designed with a **decoupled approach**:
- **`π_lower` (RL-based):** This policy controls the 12 degrees of freedom (DoF) in the humanoid's legs, primarily responsible for maintaining whole-body balance.
- **`π_upper` (Open-loop via EMP):** The upper-body motion targets (15 DoF for arms and waist) are generated and optimized by the EMP network (Section 5) before being sent to the robot.
- **Goal-conditioned Policy:** The RL policy (`π`) takes as input the current robot state (`s_t`) and the upper-body motion target (`g_t`), outputting lower-body actions (`a_t`).
- **State Space:** The observation state (`s_t`) includes current joint positions, previous actions, base Euler angles, and the upper-body motion target. A history of the last 15 frames is incorporated for better context. Actions are 12-dimensional joint position targets for the legs, converted to torques by a PD controller.
- **Reward Design:** A comprehensive reward function is designed to encourage stable standing while enabling upper-body movement. It includes regularization terms for base orientation, projected gravity, base height, linear/angular velocities, and acceleration, as well as leg DoF positions and feet contact/slip. Negative rewards (energy terms) penalize excessive action range, rate, acceleration, torques, and joint velocities/accelerations.
- **Domain Randomization:** To enhance robustness and facilitate sim-to-real transfer, various parameters like friction, link masses, center of mass, link inertia, PD gains, motor torque limits, motor damping, and delay are randomized during training. External disturbances like pushing and hanging are also introduced.
- **Termination Conditions:** Training episodes reset if the projected center of gravity on the x or y axis deviates excessively (exceeds 0.7), preventing prolonged unstable states.

#### 3.3. Executable Motion Prior (EMP) Module

This is the core novelty, a VAE-based network designed to adjust potentially unstable upper-body motion targets:
- **Architecture:** The EMP network consists of an encoder (comprising a state encoder `f_s`, a target encoder `f_t`, and a fusion network `f_ψ`) and a decoder `f_θ`. The state and target are encoded into latent variables (`z_1`, `z_2`), fused into `z`, which is regularized to follow a standard normal distribution. The decoder then generates the adjusted (executable) upper-body motion target (`g_hat_t`).
- **Training Process:** The training is a two-stage process:
 1. **World Model Training:** A separate MLP `f_w` is trained to act as a **world model**, predicting the next state `s_{t+1}` given the current state `s_t` and action `a_t`. This model is crucial because it allows for gradient backpropagation through the robot's dynamics, which is otherwise difficult in a simulator. The world model is trained by minimizing the L2 norm between its prediction and the actual state from the simulator.
 2. **EMP Network Training:** The EMP network is trained to optimize motion targets by minimizing a composite loss function:
 - **Reconstruction Loss (`L_rec`):** Ensures the adjusted target (`g_hat_t`) remains close to the original target (`g_t`).
 - **Orientation Loss (`L_ori`):** Encourages the humanoid's base to remain upright, preventing falls. This loss leverages the world model's prediction of future base orientation.
 - **Collision Loss (`L_col`):** Penalizes self-collisions between simplified spherical models of critical links (torso, hand, sacrum, thigh) at the predicted future state.
 - **Centroid Loss (`L_cen`):** Promotes keeping the robot's centroid projection within the foot support surface. This also leverages the world model's state prediction.
 - **Smoothness Loss (`L_smo`):** Encourages smooth motion trajectories for the adjusted target to avoid sudden, jerky movements.
 - **Regularization Loss (`L_reg`):** Ensures the latent variable `z` conforms to a standard Gaussian distribution, as is typical for VAEs.
- **Generation:** During deployment, the trained EMP network takes the current robot state and the desired upper-body motion target, and outputs an adjusted, "executable" target (`g_hat_t`) that is then fed to the RL policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the EMP framework was rigorously evaluated through extensive simulation experiments, ablation studies, and real-world deployment on two different humanoid robot platforms.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **Comparative Performance:** The EMP (Ours) method consistently demonstrated superior performance across multiple stability metrics compared to several baselines, including a Privileged Policy (upper bound), a Whole-Body Policy, a Decoupled Policy (their RL policy without EMP), and a Decoupled Policy with Predictive Motion Prior (PMP).
 - **Success Rate (SUC):** EMP achieved a high success rate of 98.1%, surpassing the Decoupled Policy (97.0%) and PMP (97.4%).
 - **Stability Metrics (MSC, MBV, MBA, MBO):** EMP significantly reduced self-collision (lower MSC) and improved base velocity, acceleration, and orientation stability (higher MBV, MBA, MBO) compared to all other practical baselines. For instance, MSC for EMP was 0.1494, far lower than the Decoupled Policy (0.3668) and PMP (0.3741), indicating fewer collisions.
 - **Motion Fidelity (MJP) and Smoothness (MUS):** While the mean upper-joint position reward (MJP) slightly decreased for EMP (0.8221 vs. 0.8295 for Decoupled Policy), indicating a small deviation from the original target, this was an acceptable trade-off for significantly enhanced stability. The mean upper-body action smoothness (MUS) remained competitive, demonstrating that adjustments were minimal and purposeful.
- **Whole-Body Policy Limitations:** The Whole-Body Policy, while achieving 100% success and no collisions (due to explicit penalties), exhibited poor performance in other metrics, particularly very low base orientation reward (0.5204) and high upper-body motion smoothness (7.6708, higher is worse). This validates the paper's central premise about the conflict between stability and motion fidelity for whole-body control, highlighting significant vibrations and deviations in upper-body motions.
- **EMP Effectiveness:** Visualizations (Figure 4) clearly illustrate EMP's ability to optimize "dangerous" or unexecutable motions, ensuring the robot remains stable while maintaining the essence of the intended movement. This is particularly evident in motions like hammer use or lightbulb screwing, where EMP prevents falls.
- **Conditional EMP:** Enabling EMP only when motion deviation from the prior distribution exceeded a threshold ("EMP when Danger") yielded very similar results to always-on EMP, suggesting that the system intelligently intervenes when necessary.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

The ablation experiments confirmed the critical role of each loss function in the EMP network's training:
- Removing **smoothness loss** drastically reduced the success rate (27.0%) and worsened smoothness (MUS 5.434).
- Removing **orientation loss** had the most severe impact, reducing success rate to a mere 2.6% and severely degrading all stability metrics.
- Removing **centroid loss** also significantly impacted stability and success rate (10.7%).
This indicates that all three losses are crucial for the holistic stability and executability of the generated motions.

#### 4.3. Real-world Experiments

The framework was successfully deployed on a full-sized humanoid robot (1.65m, 60kg, 27 DoF). The RL policy and EMP operated at 50Hz, with the PD controller at 1kHz. The robot demonstrated successful imitation of various human motions from the AMASS dataset (Figure 6), showcasing the practical applicability and effective sim-to-real transfer of the system.

#### 4.4. Experiments on Another Platform

To further validate robustness and generalization, the system was deployed on a different humanoid platform. The results (Table VI, Figure 7) mirrored those on the primary platform, demonstrating that the EMP framework performs well across different humanoid robot designs. This highlights the adaptability of the proposed method.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "EMP: Executable Motion Prior" framework represents a significant step forward in enabling humanoid robots to perform complex manipulation tasks in dynamic, human-centric environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Stability and Safety:** The most immediate impact is the substantial improvement in humanoid robot stability while performing upper-body motions. By dynamically adjusting motion targets to be within the robot's physical capabilities, EMP drastically reduces the risk of falls and self-collisions. This is paramount for the safe deployment of humanoids in real-world scenarios, where robustness against unforeseen dynamics and challenging motions is critical.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Complex Tasks:** The successful sim-to-real transfer, supported by comprehensive domain randomization and validated on multiple physical platforms, underscores the framework's practical utility. It demonstrates a robust method for developing sophisticated control policies in simulation and reliably transferring them to real hardware, a long-standing challenge in robotics.
- **Foundational for Human-Robot Collaboration:** Enabling humanoids to imitate human upper-body movements naturally and stably is a cornerstone for effective human-robot interaction and collaboration. Robots can better understand and respond to human demonstrations, learn new skills more intuitively, and adapt to human tools and workflows, paving the way for more seamless integration into human society.
- **Generalizable Approach to Constraint Handling in RL:** The concept of an "Executable Motion Prior" that pre-filters and optimizes desired actions based on real-time state and predicted executability offers a valuable paradigm for integrating physical constraints and safety into complex RL systems. This approach could be adapted to other challenging robotic control problems beyond humanoid motion.
- **Accelerated Skill Acquisition:** By providing a reliable method for imitating human movements, the framework facilitates faster and more intuitive learning of complex manipulation skills. Instead of laboriously programming each action, robots can learn from human demonstrations, significantly reducing development time for new applications.
- **Future Whole-Body Control:** While currently focused on upper-body movements, the authors acknowledge the limitations regarding whole-body imitation due to high DoF and complex dynamics. This work lays a strong foundation for future research aiming to extend the EMP concept to full whole-body motion imitation, potentially by integrating similar prior mechanisms for lower-body or contact-rich scenarios. Addressing joint limitations and discrepancies between retargeted and source motions is also highlighted as future work, suggesting continuous improvement toward more natural and capable humanoid robots.

In conclusion, EMP offers a robust, safe, and intelligent solution to a critical problem in humanoid robotics, moving closer to the vision of versatile, human-like robots that can operate effectively in complex human environments.
