# SpatialDreamer: Incentivizing Spatial Reasoning via Active Mental Imagery

> [!summary] Summary
> SpatialDreamer presents a reinforcement learning framework that endows multimodal large language models with active spatial mental simulation capabilities via iterative exploration and visual imagination. This approach, incorporating a Geometric Policy Optimization algorithm, established new top performances on various spatial reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit fragility in complex spatial reasoning tasks demanding mental simulation, such as object counting with occlusions or perspective-taking.
- Current methods either rely on passive observation of spatial data or employ external, manually configured test-time scaling approaches, failing to internalize active spatial mental imagery within the MLLM.
- Training MLLMs for multi-step, long-horizontal reasoning via standard Reinforcement Learning (RL) is hindered by sparse trajectory-level reward signals, making efficient learning of exploration policies difficult.

## Method

- The SpatialDreamer framework implements an agentic reinforcement learning approach that enables MLLMs to actively explore and reason through an iterative ==`think-imagine-answer` loop==, leveraging an external world model (Stable Virtual Camera) to generate novel egocentric views.
- ==Geometric Policy Optimization (GeoPO)== introduces a tree-structured sampling scheme and a ==hybrid reward design==, incorporating dense step-level rewards and geometric penalties, to stabilize training and encourage geometrically plausible action sequences.
- A Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) dataset, ==SpatialDreamer-SFT==, is constructed using single-pass and error-injection-based reflective reasoning samples to bootstrap the MLLM with preliminary agentic imagination behaviors.

## Results

- SpatialDreamer achieved state-of-the-art performance on the SAT benchmark, with **93.9%** average accuracy on SAT-Real and **92.5%** on SAT-Synthesized, surpassing generalist and specialized MLLMs.
- The model established a new state-of-the-art overall score of **84.9%** on MindCube-Tiny, representing a **55.6%** absolute improvement over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline, demonstrating strong active mental imagery capabilities.
- Ablation studies confirmed that GeoPO significantly outperforms standard GRPO (e.g., **+5.5%** on MindCube-Tiny) and that both the geometric penalty and the comprehensive SFT dataset are critical for achieving optimal performance and efficient training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Equipping MLLMs with active mental imagery, where they iteratively imagine and integrate novel views generated by a world model, allows them to overcome the limitations of static single-view perception for complex spatial reasoning.
> - Geometric Policy Optimization (GeoPO)'s hybrid reward system, which provides dense step-level feedback and penalizes geometrically sub-optimal actions, effectively addresses reward sparsity and ensures efficient, stable learning for multi-step reasoning.
> - A carefully curated Supervised Fine-Tuning dataset, including reflective reasoning samples, is essential for pre-training MLLMs to adopt the iterative `think-imagine-answer` agentic reasoning paradigm and develop self-correction capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Artificial Intelligence has seen tremendous advancements with the advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) and their multimodal counterparts (MLLMs). These models excel at tasks involving language understanding, generation, and static image analysis. However, a significant gap remains in their ability to perform complex **spatial reasoning**, particularly tasks requiring **mental simulation** or **active mental imagery**. This work directly addresses this critical limitation.

**Current Landscape and Limitations:**
- **MLLMs' Fragility in Spatial Reasoning:** While MLLMs like GPT-4o show impressive general scene understanding, they "remain fragile when confronted with complex spatial reasoning tasks requiring mental simulation." The paper cites examples like object counting with occlusions or perspective-taking, where current models, relying on static single-view perception, fail to identify objects or relationships that are not immediately visible. Performance on benchmarks like MindCube (GPT-4o achieving only 38.81% accuracy) underscores this deficiency.
- **Passive vs. Active Approaches:**
 - **Passive Observation:** Many existing approaches attempt to improve spatial reasoning through "spatial-aware instruction tuning," exposing MLLMs to synthetic 3D scenes or depth information. However, these methods are fundamentally *passive*, relying on labeled spatial relations rather than actively *imagining*, *moving*, and *updating* their internal representations.
 - **Test-Time Scaling:** Recent works like MindJourney have taken a step towards active exploration by coupling an MLLM with a controllable world model to simulate novel egocentric views. However, these are "test-time scaling approaches," lacking a learnable exploration policy and relying on manually configured hyperparameters. They do not internalize the spatial mental imagery capability within the MLLM itself.
- **Reinforcement Learning for LLMs:** Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for enhancing the reasoning abilities of LLMs, primarily by providing feedback on generated outputs. However, standard RL approaches (e.g., GRPO) typically provide only "trajectory-level reward signals," which are sparse and lack the fine-grained, step-level feedback needed for long-horizontal reasoning tasks like multi-step spatial exploration. This sparsity hinders efficient learning of optimal exploration strategies.

**SpatialDreamer's Contribution to the Landscape:**
SpatialDreamer positions itself as a critical advancement by addressing these limitations. It bridges the gap between passive spatial understanding and active, human-like mental simulation. By proposing a **learnable RL framework** that enables MLLMs to actively explore and imagine, it moves beyond mere observation or external tool use to *internalize* spatial mental imagery as an intrinsic skill. The introduction of **Geometric Policy Optimization (GeoPO)** further refines the RL training process by providing dense, step-level rewards and penalizing geometrically sub-optimal actions, thereby improving learning efficiency and policy quality. This work pushes MLLMs closer to achieving robust, embodied intelligence capable of navigating and reasoning within dynamic 3D environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **endow Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with human-like active spatial mental simulation capabilities** for complex spatial reasoning tasks. The authors aim to move beyond the current passive observation paradigm towards a system that can actively explore, imagine, and integrate visual evidence to solve problems requiring dynamic perspective-taking and hypothetical action outcomes.

The key motivations driving this research are:
- **Addressing MLLM limitations in complex spatial reasoning:** Current MLLMs struggle with tasks where objects are occluded, or the answer requires understanding spatial relationships from varying perspectives or after hypothetical actions. Their reliance on static, single-view perception leads to significant errors, as evidenced by low performance on benchmarks like SAT and MindCube.
- **Mimicking human cognitive abilities:** Humans effortlessly perceive, understand, and reason about a 3D world, mentally simulating actions and predicting outcomes. The motivation is to imbue MLLMs with a similar capacity for "active mental imagery" – an iterative process of imagining novel views and integrating them into reasoning.
- **Bridging the gap between passive observation and active imagination:** Existing methods either passively observe labeled spatial data or rely on complex, manually-configured "test-time scaling" strategies that do not internalize the mental imagery capability within the MLLM itself. The authors are motivated to create a *learnable* framework that makes active exploration an intrinsic skill of the model.
- **Overcoming reward sparsity in long-horizontal reasoning tasks:** Training complex, multi-step reasoning agents with standard reinforcement learning is challenging due to sparse rewards, where feedback is only given at the end of a long sequence of actions. A significant motivation is to develop a more efficient and stable reward mechanism that provides fine-grained, step-level supervision.

In essence, the research aims to transform MLLMs from passive observers into active, imaginative reasoners that can autonomously learn to explore, hypothesize, and integrate information within their internal 3D environment, thereby achieving a more robust and human-like understanding of spatial dynamics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SpatialDreamer introduces a novel reinforcement learning (RL) framework designed to enable spatial reasoning through a closed-loop process of active exploration, visual imagination, and evidence-grounded reasoning. The methodology comprises three main components: the SpatialDreamer framework itself, the Geometric Policy Optimization (GeoPO) algorithm, and the SpatialDreamer-SFT dataset.

#### 3.1. SpatialDreamer Framework (Agentic RL with Active Mental Imagery)

SpatialDreamer operates as an agentic RL framework, mimicking human-like iterative spatial mental reasoning. It works in a closed loop over multiple turns:
1. **Reasoning and Action Selection:** Given an input question and the current visual context (image), the MLLM (within `<think>` tags) analyzes the scene and determines a plausible egocentric action to gain more information.
2. **Imagination via World Model:** The selected action (e.g., `move-forward` by 0.75m, `turn-left` by 27 degrees) is then passed to an external **world model**, specifically the Stable Virtual Camera (SVC). The world model simulates the action's consequence and generates a novel ego-centric view (`<imagine>` tags), which becomes the new visual input for the MLLM.
3. **Evidence Integration and Iteration/Answering:** The MLLM integrates the accumulated visual evidence (original image + generated views) and its ongoing reasoning. This process can either lead to another round of action selection if more exploration is needed, or, if sufficient information is gathered or a maximum iteration limit is reached, it generates the final answer (within `<answer>` tags).

This iterative process allows SpatialDreamer to evolve from passive observation to active, goal-directed imagination, learning *where* to explore, *what* to observe, and *how* to reason within its internal 3D environment.

#### 3.2. Geometric Policy Optimization (GeoPO)

A key challenge in training such a long-horizontal reasoning framework is the sparsity of rewards. To address this, SpatialDreamer proposes **GeoPO**, which combines episode-level and step-level rewards with geometric consistency constraints.
- **Tree-structured Sampling:** Unlike standard RL that samples independent trajectories, GeoPO employs a tree-structured sampling scheme. At each decoding step, the model can sample multiple world-model invocations, expanding N new branches. This structure allows for more diverse exploration of action sequences. It also supports a backtracking mechanism, enabling the model to revert and explore alternative branches if a path proves unpromising.
- **Hybrid Reward Design:**
 - **Episode-level Reward (r_e):** This encompasses a format reward (for correct output structure), a final answer reward (binary, based on correctness), and a tool-call reward (encouraging interaction with the world model).
 - **Step-level Reward (r_s):** This is the core innovation for dense feedback. Leaf nodes (trajectories ending with an answer) receive a binary reward. These rewards are then recursively computed bottom-up: the reward for any intermediate node is the average of its direct child nodes' rewards. This provides credit assignment at each step of the reasoning process.
 - **Geometric Penalty (λ = 0.9):** To encourage efficient and geometrically plausible actions, a penalty coefficient (λ=0.9) is applied to "sub-optimal" action rollouts. These include:
 - **Redundancy:** Consecutive actions in the same direction (e.g., `turn-left` then `turn-left`).
 - **Conflict:** Consecutive actions in opposite directions (e.g., `turn-left` then `turn-right`).
 The overall reward for a step is a combination of episode-level and geometrically penalized step-level rewards.
- **Optimization Objective:** GeoPO adapts the GRPO objective function to incorporate the step-wise reward design, using importance sampling and advantage estimation based on the fine-grained rewards. This encourages stable and efficient policy convergence.

#### 3.3. SpatialDreamer-SFT Dataset Construction

To bootstrap the MLLM with preliminary agentic imagination behaviors, a Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) dataset is constructed from the MindCube training set. This dataset is crucial for teaching the model the desired `think-imagine-answer` reasoning pattern.
- **Single-pass Reasoning Samples:** These samples demonstrate straightforward, monotonic analytical and geometric reasoning without explicit self-correction. They are generated by prompting a high-capacity teacher model (Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct).
- **Reflective Reasoning Samples:** To instill self-correction capabilities, an "error injection strategy" is used. Incorrect arguments are intentionally introduced into tool-call actions at intermediate points in a trajectory. The teacher model is then prompted to simulate an "error–reflection–correction" scenario, where it recognizes the error, backtracks, and suggests a corrective action.
- **Quality Control:** A rigorous manual quality inspection is performed on the generated reasoning traces and world-model produced views, ensuring:
 - **Logical Consistency:** Valid and interpretable logical progression in each step.
 - **Geometric Plausibility:** Consistency between actions and generated views, absence of rendering artifacts.
 - **Goal Relevance:** Each reasoning trace meaningfully contributes to the task.

By combining this sophisticated framework, optimized learning algorithm, and carefully curated dataset, SpatialDreamer aims to equip MLLMs with powerful active spatial reasoning abilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SpatialDreamer demonstrates highly competitive and often state-of-the-art performance across multiple challenging spatial reasoning benchmarks, validating its core hypotheses regarding active mental imagery and fine-grained reward optimization.

#### 4.1. Performance on Spatial Mental Modeling Datasets

- **SAT Benchmark:** SpatialDreamer achieves state-of-the-art performance on both SAT-Real and SAT-Synthesized splits. It scores an impressive 93.9% average accuracy on SAT-Real and 92.5% on SAT-Synthesized. This significantly surpasses all compared generalist MLLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5-Pro), other spatial MLLMs, and even test-time scaling methods like MindJourney. This highlights its superior ability in tasks requiring egocentric movement, object movement, allocentric perspective-taking, and action consequences.
- **MindCube-Tiny Benchmark:** SpatialDreamer achieves a new state-of-the-art overall score of 84.9%. This represents a substantial absolute improvement of 55.6% over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline (84.9% vs. 29.3%). While slightly trailing 3DThinker in the "Among" category, SpatialDreamer exhibits overall superior performance, proving the effectiveness of its active mental imagery mechanism for tasks requiring perspective taking, mental imagination, and dynamic simulation.

#### 4.2. Performance on General Spatial Reasoning Dataset

- **VSI-Bench:** SpatialDreamer achieves an average accuracy of 62.2%, outperforming all generalist and spatial MLLMs by a clear margin. It particularly excels in sub-tasks such as object counting (+0.9% over previous SOTA), relative distance (+2.4%), relative direction (+5.9%), and route planning (+2.1%). This indicates that these categories benefit significantly from SpatialDreamer's active mental imagery and multi-perspective reasoning capabilities, allowing for a more comprehensive spatial understanding than passively observing geometric embeddings.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **GRPO vs. GeoPO:** GeoPO consistently outperforms the standard GRPO method. For instance, on MindCube-Tiny, GeoPO achieves a 5.5% higher average accuracy. This confirms the efficacy of GeoPO's tree-structured sampling and hybrid reward scheme in mitigating reward sparsity and providing dense supervision for multi-step reasoning.
- **Ablations of Geometric Penalty:** Removing the geometric penalty from GeoPO leads to a noticeable performance degradation across all benchmarks (e.g., 1.2% lower on MindCube-Tiny). This demonstrates that penalizing conflicting and redundant spatial trajectories is crucial for regularizing the exploration process and encouraging geometry-consistent reasoning.
- **Ablations of SFT Data:**
 - Training without any SFT data results in a significant performance drop, indicating that the SFT dataset is essential for introducing the `think-imagine-answer` reasoning paradigm.
 - Using both single-pass and reflective samples yields the best overall performance, highlighting their complementary roles in shaping reasoning behaviors.
 - Training with only single-pass data outperforms the reflective-only variant, suggesting that single-pass reasoning provides a stronger foundational exposure to agentic RL.

#### 4.4. Efficiency Analysis of GeoPO

- **Reward Curve:** GeoPO exhibits faster convergence and achieves higher reward values during training compared to GRPO, demonstrating its efficiency and effectiveness.
- **Per-Trajectory Generation Time:** GeoPO requires less trajectory generation time across most training steps. This efficiency gain is attributed to its tree-structured design, which allows for prefix sharing among trajectories.
- **Per-Token Generation Time:** GeoPO consistently achieves lower per-token generation latency than GRPO, indicating superior runtime efficiency during training.

In summary, SpatialDreamer sets new benchmarks in spatial reasoning for MLLMs, showing that active, imagination-driven exploration, coupled with a geometrically-aware and fine-grained RL optimization scheme, is a highly effective approach for developing more robust and human-like AI agents.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SpatialDreamer research represents a significant advancement in the field of Artificial Intelligence, particularly for Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs), with profound implications for their capabilities and future development.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Bridging the Gap to Human-like Spatial Reasoning:** The most critical significance lies in enabling MLLMs to perform "human-like active spatial mental simulation." By moving beyond passive observation to active exploration and imagination, SpatialDreamer equips MLLMs with a cognitive ability akin to how humans mentally manipulate and navigate 3D space. This is a crucial step towards truly intelligent agents that can understand and interact with the physical world in a dynamic, intuitive manner.
- **Addressing Fundamental MLLM Limitations:** The paper directly tackles a well-recognized weakness of current MLLMs: their fragility in complex spatial reasoning tasks, especially those involving occlusions, dynamic scenes, or requiring a change in perspective. By allowing MLLMs to actively "dream" new perspectives, SpatialDreamer overcomes the limitations of static, single-view perception.
- **Novel Reinforcement Learning Paradigm:** The introduction of Geometric Policy Optimization (GeoPO) is a significant methodological contribution. By combining tree-structured sampling with a hybrid reward structure (episode-level, step-level, and geometric penalty), GeoPO provides a solution to the long-standing problem of reward sparsity in multi-step, long-horizontal reasoning tasks. This approach offers a more efficient and stable way to train complex agents, encouraging geometrically consistent and optimal action trajectories.
- **Robust Performance Across Diverse Benchmarks:** SpatialDreamer's state-of-the-art results on challenging benchmarks like SAT, MindCube-Tiny, and VSI-Bench unequivocally demonstrate its effectiveness. Its superior performance over generalist MLLMs, test-time scaling methods, and even other specialized spatial MLLMs highlights the power of its active mental imagery approach.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Enhanced Embodied AI and Robotics:** The ability for MLLMs to actively reason about and simulate physical environments is transformative for embodied AI and robotics. Robots equipped with SpatialDreamer-like capabilities could more intelligently navigate complex spaces, plan multi-step actions to reach occluded objects, or predict the outcomes of their movements in real-time. This could lead to more robust and autonomous robotic systems for various applications, from logistics to service robotics.
- **Improved Human-AI Interaction:** MLLMs with better spatial reasoning can understand human instructions and queries that involve complex spatial relationships more effectively. For instance, an AI assistant could provide more accurate directions, describe obscured objects, or help design physical layouts by understanding spatial constraints and implications.
- **Foundation for Generative Perception:** The paper hints at future work to "internalize the world-model-based imagination capability into MLLMs, enabling unified spatial reasoning and generative perception." This could lead to models that not only understand existing scenes but can also *generate* novel, plausible spatial environments or object arrangements based on textual prompts or internal simulations, paving the way for more creative and intelligent AI systems.
- **Advancements in Cognitive AI:** This research provides a computational model that aligns more closely with human cognitive processes for spatial reasoning and mental imagery. It could inspire further research into how AI models can learn and simulate other higher-level cognitive functions, contributing to our understanding of intelligence itself.
- **Broader Applicability of RL for LLMs:** GeoPO's success in addressing reward sparsity might be transferable to other complex, multi-step reasoning tasks for MLLMs, beyond just spatial reasoning. This could lead to more efficient training methodologies for a wide range of agentic AI applications.

In conclusion, SpatialDreamer pushes the frontier of MLLM capabilities, offering a powerful framework for active spatial reasoning that promises to unlock new levels of intelligence in multimodal and embodied AI systems.
