# AutoFly: Vision-Language-Action Model for UAV Autonomous Navigation in the Wild

> [!summary] Summary
> AutoFly introduces an end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model for autonomous UAV navigation in unknown outdoor environments, operating with minimal language guidance. The model integrates a pseudo-depth encoder to enhance spatial reasoning from monocular RGB inputs, achieving a 47.9% success rate in simulations and demonstrating robust performance in real-world settings.

## Problem

- Existing UAV Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) systems rely on detailed, step-by-step instructions, which are unavailable in real-world outdoor scenarios requiring coarse guidance.
- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models predominantly use RGB inputs, limiting their spatial understanding critical for complex 3D aerial navigation and obstacle avoidance.
- Traditional UAV navigation systems treat primitives like path planning and obstacle avoidance as independent modules, leading to uncoordinated decision-making in dynamic environments.

## Method

- Developed **AutoFly**, an end-to-end VLA model integrating an ==LLaVA-based== Vision-Language Model with a novel Pseudo-Depth Encoder that generates depth-aware features from monocular RGB images using Depth Anything V2.
- Constructed a comprehensive autonomous navigation dataset using SAC-trained agents for trajectory generation, complemented by expert demonstrations and a rebalancing framework to mitigate learning bias.
- Implemented a two-stage training paradigm involving vision-language alignment followed by spatially-informed robot action fine-tuning, utilizing a Siamese MLP projector for robust depth-vision-language integration.

## Results

- AutoFly achieved a **47.9%** success rate, **21.9%** collision rate, and **77.3%** path efficiency rate in simulated environments, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines like OpenVLA by **3.9%** in success rate.
- The model demonstrated robust real-world adaptability, achieving **60%** success indoors and **55%** outdoors, indicating effective sim-to-real transfer capabilities even with limited real-world fine-tuning.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the pseudo-depth encoder improved the success rate by **3.9%** and reduced the collision rate by **2.6%**, and dataset rebalancing increased the success rate from **16.6%** to **47.9%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit integration of depth-aware spatial information via a pseudo-depth encoder significantly enhances VLA model performance for UAV navigation in complex, obstacle-rich 3D environments.
> - A specialized pseudo-depth projector is crucial for effectively extracting and preserving geometric patterns from depth data, outperforming general vision encoders in spatial reasoning tasks.
> - Dataset rebalancing is essential in long-horizon navigation tasks to address imbalances between behaviors like obstacle avoidance and target seeking, preventing learning bias and improving overall success rates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions its work within the field of Embodied AI, specifically focusing on Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) for Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs). VLN tasks involve an intelligent agent navigating an environment by interpreting natural language instructions and visual observations. This capability is relevant for UAVs operating in complex 3D environments, including applications such as search and rescue, environmental monitoring, and autonomous delivery.

Existing UAV VLN research primarily relies on highly detailed, step-by-step instructions that specify predetermined flight paths and maneuvers. These approaches have shown efficacy in controlled, simulated environments, but their applicability is limited in real-world outdoor scenarios, which are often unknown, dynamic, and where only coarse-grained positional or directional guidance is typically available from human operators. Such real-world settings require autonomous capabilities beyond explicit instruction following, including continuous planning and obstacle avoidance.

Furthermore, traditional approaches to UAV navigation primitives (path planning, object recognition, obstacle avoidance) often treat these as independent modular components. This modularity can lead to suboptimal performance in dynamic, unknown environments where coordinated decision-making is necessary. The paper notes that current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which integrate visual, linguistic, and action capabilities, have seen success in ground robotics but predominantly rely solely on RGB inputs, which may limit their spatial reasoning crucial for 3D aerial navigation.

This research aims to bridge the gap between current VLN capabilities and the demands of real-world autonomous UAV navigation by developing an end-to-end VLA model that can operate with concise instructions in unknown outdoor settings, integrate core navigation primitives, and enhance spatial understanding from standard visual inputs. It also addresses deficiencies in existing VLN datasets which are deemed insufficient for real-world autonomous decision-making and exhibit a significant simulation-to-reality gap.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate AutoFly, an end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model designed for autonomous UAV navigation in unknown outdoor environments, utilizing minimal linguistic guidance.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current UAV VLN research and practical deployment challenges:

1. **Reliance on Detailed Instructions vs. Real-world Scenarios**: Existing UAV VLN systems typically assume the availability of explicit, step-by-step instructions and comprehensive environmental knowledge. However, in real-world outdoor exploration, such detailed instructions are frequently unavailable. Instead, operators often provide only coarse-grained positional or directional guidance, requiring the UAV to autonomously plan its route, avoid obstacles, and recognize targets. The objective is to enable UAVs to perform autonomous navigation with simple, concise natural language instructions, moving beyond pre-specified, fixed trajectories.

2. **Insufficient Spatial Reasoning from RGB-only Inputs**: UAV navigation in complex 3D environments demands robust geometric understanding for safe obstacle avoidance, altitude control, and precise positioning. Current VLA models predominantly rely on RGB inputs, which can limit their inherent spatial reasoning capabilities. A key objective is to enhance the model's spatial understanding by integrating depth-aware features derived from standard RGB inputs, without requiring additional depth sensors, thereby reducing hardware complexity and cost.

3. **Fragmented Navigation Primitives**: Traditional UAV navigation systems often implement path planning, object recognition, and obstacle avoidance as separate, independent modules. This modularity can lead to suboptimal, uncoordinated decision-making, particularly in unknown and dynamic environments where integrated deliberation across these high-level components is crucial for adaptive flight. The motivation is to integrate these primitives into a unified, end-to-end VLA architecture to enable joint optimization and coherent decision-making.

4. **Limitations of Existing VLN Datasets**: Current VLN datasets primarily focus on instruction-following rather than autonomous decision-making. They often lack sufficient real-world data, contributing to a "sim-to-real" gap that hinders robust transfer of learned policies to physical UAVs. A significant objective is to construct a novel autonomous navigation dataset that emphasizes continuous obstacle avoidance, autonomous planning, and recognition workflows, incorporating comprehensive real-world data to facilitate robust sim-to-real transfer.

5. **Lack of Effective Training Strategies for Multimodal Alignment**: Aligning visual, depth, and linguistic representations with action policies is a complex challenge. The research aims to develop a progressive two-stage training strategy that effectively achieves this alignment, ensuring the model can leverage multimodal information for generating appropriate control actions.

By addressing these motivations, the research seeks to advance the practical deployment of autonomous UAVs in dynamic, unstructured real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The AutoFly framework is an end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model designed for autonomous UAV navigation. Its methodology encompasses task formulation, VLA model architecture, autonomous navigation dataset construction, and a spatially-informed training paradigm.

#### 3.1. Task Formulation

Autonomous navigation is formulated as learning a control policy, denoted as $\pi$. This policy takes three inputs: the current RGB observation ($o_t$), a natural language instruction ($L$), and coarse positional or directional guidance encoded as an initial action ($a_0$). The policy then outputs a low-level control action ($a_t$). The objective is to derive an optimal policy $\pi^*$ that generates a collision-free planned trajectory $\tau$, respecting UAV kinodynamic constraints, to reach the specified target.

#### 3.2. VLA Model for Autonomous Navigation

AutoFly integrates three core components to address the unique 3D spatial challenges of UAV navigation:

- **Vision-Language Model (VLM)**: The framework utilizes an LLaVA-based configuration, specifically employing the OpenVLA (Kim et al., 2024; Karamcheti et al., 2024) approach. This leverages pre-trained VLMs for scene understanding, language comprehension, and generalization.

- **Pseudo-Depth Encoder**: This novel component generates and encodes depth-aware spatial representations from monocular RGB inputs.
 - **Pseudo-Depth Generator**: It employs Depth Anything V2 (Yang et al., 2024) to produce high-fidelity depth maps from RGB images. This approach avoids idealized depth outputs from simulators like AirSim and eliminates the need for physical depth cameras, reducing payload and cost.
 - **Pseudo-Depth Projector**: The generated depth map is partitioned into patches, linearly embedded into a sequence of depth tokens ($z_d$). The projector matches the dimensionality of depth tokens with vision tokens and encodes inherent spatial information from depth data into the visual feature space, enhancing geometric understanding and multimodal fusion. Technical considerations include addressing the domain gap between depth and RGB data, adapting features, and preserving geometric information critical for navigation.

- **Action De-tokenizer**: To convert model outputs into executable UAV commands, discrete action tokens from the LLaMA2 vocabulary space are mapped to continuous 3D action vectors (e.g., $v_x, v_y, v_z, \omega_{yaw}$). The process involves concatenating depth-projected features from the depth generator and visual features from the vision encoder, feeding them into the Large Language Model (LLM), and then de-tokenizing the LLM's output into an action vector.

#### 3.3. Autonomous Navigation Dataset

To overcome limitations of existing datasets, a novel autonomous navigation dataset was constructed:

- **Trajectory Collection**:
 - **Environment Construction**: Twelve diverse simulated environments (70m x 70m) were built in AirSim, featuring irregular obstacles (trees, walls, rocks, buildings). Object instances (60 selected, with 3-5 distractors per scenario) were strategically placed for recognition tasks.
 - **Trajectory Generation**: To scale data collection beyond expensive human expert demonstrations, specialized data collection agents were developed and trained using the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) reinforcement learning algorithm. These agents, trained for point-to-point navigation using depth-only inputs and CNN-based architectures, achieved a 95% evaluation success rate in their respective scenes. The dataset combines these automatically generated trajectories with expert demonstrations, ensuring both scale and quality. Each trajectory records visual observations, language descriptions, actions, and UAV state information (positional data, attitude angles). The core objective is for the UAV to autonomously navigate from random start points to a designated target using high-level action primitives like planning, obstacle avoidance, and object recognition.

- **Dataset Rebalancing**: Long-horizon navigation trajectories often exhibit an imbalance where obstacle avoidance behaviors are more frequent than target-seeking phases, leading to learning bias. A segmentation and rebalancing framework was applied:
 - Trajectories are segmented into "obstacle avoidance" and "target seeking" phases using Grounding DINO, which queries visual observations with language instructions to detect the target.
 - The imbalance (e.g., 73% obstacle avoidance, 27% target seeking) is quantified via KL divergence.
 - A target uniform distribution is defined, and stratified resampling (downsampling obstacle avoidance, upsampling target seeking) is performed to create a balanced dataset, theoretically grounded in importance sampling.

- **Dataset Analysis**: The dataset characteristics include trajectory length distributions, word cloud analysis of instructions, and target category distributions. A novel metric, "average obstacle encounter," is introduced to quantify environmental complexity and avoidance capability, complementing standard success rates.

- **Simulation-Reality Bridge**: To ensure real-world applicability, 1,000 real-world flight episodes were collected. These episodes serve to validate simulation fidelity and augment training data, enhancing cross-domain generalization and addressing the sim-to-real transfer challenge.

- **Dataset Split**: The training dataset comprises over 13,000 episodes and 2.5 million image-language-action triplets. Evaluation is conducted across 4 testing scenes (2 seen, 2 unseen) with 60 targets (50 seen, 10 unseen), creating 4 evaluation conditions. Additionally, 200 real-world flights validate the model.

#### 3.4. Training Paradigm of AutoFly

AutoFly employs a two-stage training paradigm:

- **Stage 1: Vision-Language Alignment**: The model is initialized using the `prism-siglip-7b` configuration from Prismatic-VLMs, which features a two-layer projector and a 7B-parameter LLaMA2 backbone. This stage establishes a foundation for multimodal understanding.

- **Stage 2: Spatially-Informed Robot Action Fine-tuning**:
 - This stage jointly fine-tunes the pseudo-depth encoder alongside the pre-trained VLA backbone.
 - Three paradigms for integrating depth information were investigated:
 - **(a) Siamese MLP projector**: Maps both depth and visual embeddings into the language space using shared parameters, enforcing consistent cross-modal representations.
 - **(b) Non-Siamese MLP projector**: Uses separate projectors for depth and visual embeddings.
 - **(c) Direct depth integration**: Feeds depth embeddings directly to the LLM without projection.
 - Empirical evaluation found the Siamese MLP projector to be superior. Its parameter sharing mechanism compels both depth and visual branches to learn consistent feature mappings, preserving spatial correspondence and providing implicit regularization, which is crucial for precise spatial reasoning tasks.
 - Fine-tuning uses an autoregressive training paradigm, mapping outputs directly to robot action commands, with cross-entropy loss and specific learning rates for the VLM backbone (2e-5) and pseudo-depth projector (1e-4) over 80K gradient steps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of AutoFly involved comparisons against state-of-the-art VLA baselines in both simulated and real-world environments, complemented by extensive ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Simulation Performance

- **Overall Performance**: AutoFly achieved a success rate (SR) of 47.9%, a collision rate (CR) of 21.9%, and a path efficiency rate (PER) of 77.3% across all test scenarios.
- **Baseline Comparison**: AutoFly outperformed existing VLA baselines:
 - **RT-1**: Exhibited the lowest metrics (24.3% SR, 65.1% CR, 61.1% PER), attributed to its lack of LLM integration for multimodal reasoning.
 - **RT-2**: Showed improved performance (41.9% SR, 26.0% CR, 73.7% PER) due to LLM-based cross-modal understanding.
 - **OpenVLA**: Outperformed RT-2 (44.0% SR, 24.5% CR, 75.1% PER), leveraging implicit spatial reasoning via DINOv2 visual representations.
- **AutoFly's Advantage**: AutoFly surpassed OpenVLA, achieving a 3.9% higher success rate, a 2.6% lower collision rate, and a 2.2% improved path efficiency. This performance gain is attributed to AutoFly's explicit geometric integration through pseudo-depth maps, which proved beneficial in dense, obstacle-rich environments.

#### 4.2. Real-World Performance

- **Environmental Adaptability**: AutoFly was deployed in two distinct real-world environments: a controlled indoor laboratory and an unstructured outdoor campus forest.
- **Consistent Performance**: The model demonstrated comparable performance across environments:
 - Indoor: 60% SR, 30% CR, 76.5% PER.
 - Outdoor: 55% SR, 35% CR, 75.1% PER.
 - The minimal 5% performance gap between indoor and outdoor settings, along with similar path efficiency metrics, indicates robust environmental adaptability and consistent navigation capabilities in the presence of natural elements.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer**: Experiments varying simulation-to-real data ratios confirmed that increased simulation data progressively enhanced real-world deployment performance, even with limited real-world fine-tuning.

#### 4.3. Ablation Experiments

- **Pseudo-Depth Encoder Ablation**: Models incorporating the pseudo-depth encoder achieved 47.9% SR and 21.9% CR, significantly outperforming models without it (44% SR, 24.5% CR). This validates the necessity of introducing spatial information for enhanced navigation.
- **Specialized Depth Projector Validation**: AutoFly's specialized pseudo-depth projector achieved 47.9% SR, 21.9% CR, and 77.3% PER. This substantially outperformed direct application of pre-trained general vision encoders like SigLIP (42.4% SR) or DINOv2 (41.3% SR) to depth maps. This indicates that general methods struggle to extract geometric patterns from depth data, underscoring the value of domain-adapted projection for preserving spatial continuity.
- **Dataset Rebalancing Analysis**: The rebalanced dataset configuration achieved 47.9% SR, 21.9% CR, and 77.3% PER. In contrast, training on the original imbalanced dataset without rebalancing resulted in significantly lower performance (16.6% SR, 32.9% CR, 43.7% PER). This highlights the critical role of trajectory rebalancing in mitigating learning bias caused by data imbalance in long-horizon navigation.
- **Depth-Vision-Language Alignment Analysis**:
 - **(a) Siamese MLP projector (Ours)**: Achieved the highest performance (47.9% SR, 21.9% CR, 77.3% PER).
 - **(b) Non-Siamese MLP projector**: Performed lower (43.3% SR, 25.3% CR, 68.2% PER).
 - **(c) Direct depth integration**: Yielded the lowest success rate (26.7% SR, 31.9% CR, 45.9% PER) due to the modality gap between continuous depth values and discrete LLM tokens.
 The superior performance of the Siamese architecture validated the hypothesis that parameter sharing enforces consistent cross-modal representations and preserves spatial correspondence.
- **Evaluation on Challenging Scenarios (Appendix A.3.3)**: In scenarios with dense cylinders, dense forests, and dynamic obstacles, the pseudo-depth encoder consistently provided significant gains in SR (up to +9.9%) and reductions in CR (up to -9.5%) compared to models without it. The most pronounced gains were observed in dynamic obstacle scenarios, indicating the depth encoder's importance for predictive spatial reasoning.
- **Analysis of Simpler Alternative Approaches (Appendix A.3.4)**: Comparative experiments showed that increasing training data, applying data augmentation, or using a stronger RGB-only vision encoder provided only marginal improvements (e.g., +0.3% to +1.2% SR) and could not replicate the performance gains achieved by the pseudo-depth encoder (+3.9% SR). This indicates the unique and essential contribution of explicit geometric reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AutoFly research presents an end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that contributes to the field of autonomous UAV navigation. Its significance lies in several aspects:

1. **Enabling Autonomous Navigation with Minimal Guidance**: AutoFly addresses a notable limitation in existing UAV VLN by enabling autonomous navigation using coarse-grained linguistic instructions rather than detailed, step-by-step commands. This capability is relevant for practical applications where precise pre-specified routes are unavailable or impractical.

2. **Enhanced Spatial Reasoning from Standard Inputs**: The integration of a pseudo-depth encoder, which derives depth-aware features from monocular RGB inputs, enhances the UAV's spatial understanding without requiring additional depth sensors. This approach reduces hardware complexity, cost, and payload, making it potentially more viable for real-world UAV deployments where payload capacity is a critical constraint.

3. **Comprehensive Dataset for Autonomous Behaviors**: The construction of a novel multimodal dataset, emphasizing continuous obstacle avoidance, autonomous planning, and recognition workflows, shifts the paradigm from instruction-following to autonomous behavior modeling. The inclusion of extensive real-world data specifically aims to bridge the simulation-reality gap, promoting robust sim-to-real transfer and applicability.

4. **Improved Performance and Robustness**: AutoFly demonstrated a 3.9% higher success rate compared to state-of-the-art VLA baselines in simulated environments. Its consistent performance across both simulated and real outdoor environments, including challenging scenarios with dense and dynamic obstacles, suggests a degree of robustness and environmental adaptability.

The potential impact of AutoFly extends to various real-world applications where autonomous UAV operations are desired:
- **Search and Rescue Operations**: UAVs could navigate complex terrain to locate individuals or assess disaster zones with general directional cues.
- **Environmental Monitoring**: Autonomous aerial inspection of vast or difficult-to-access areas, such as forests or farmlands, guided by high-level objectives.
- **Autonomous Delivery Systems**: UAVs could navigate urban or rural landscapes to deliver packages based on general location descriptions, avoiding dynamic obstacles.
- **Inspection and Surveillance**: Autonomous aerial platforms capable of identifying specific targets or monitoring areas with high-level mission directives.

While the research demonstrates capabilities for local obstacle avoidance and target recognition, the authors acknowledge limitations such as restricted global exploration capabilities in large-scale environments and reliance on monocular sensing. Future work plans to integrate LiDAR for 360-degree perception and incorporate Reinforcement Learning for active interaction with dynamic environments, which could further enhance the model's robustness and applicability to more complex real-world scenarios.
