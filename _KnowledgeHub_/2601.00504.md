# MotionPhysics: Learnable Motion Distillation for Text-Guided Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> MotionPhysics introduces a framework for text-guided 3D dynamic simulation, integrating multimodal LLMs for parameter inference with a differentiable Material Point Method simulator. The system employs a novel Learnable Motion Distillation loss to extract pure motion signals from video diffusion models, enabling realistic and physically plausible animations for diverse materials and challenging out-of-distribution objects.

## Problem

- Achieving physically plausible 3D dynamic simulations requires extensive expert knowledge and laborious manual tuning of numerous physical parameters.
- Current pre-trained video diffusion models often generate motions that lack physical common sense and exhibit strong inductive biases from training data, leading to implausible results for novel or unconventional objects.
- Existing methods for automatic physical parameter estimation struggle to generalize across a wide variety of materials and different types of 3D objects.

## Method

- The framework leverages multimodal LLMs (e.g., GPT-4) to infer initial material parameters from natural language prompts, using constraint-aware prompts to embed physically plausible ranges.
- A differentiable ==Material Point Method== (==MPM==) simulator, adapted for 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== (3DGS) representations, handles the core physical dynamics for diverse material behaviors.
- A Learnable Motion Distillation (LMD) loss is introduced to guide the simulation by extracting robust motion priors from video diffusion models while minimizing their appearance and geometric biases.

## Results

- MotionPhysics consistently produced visually realistic and physically plausible dynamic simulations that adhered to user prompts, outperforming baselines on real-world, human-designed, and AI-generated objects.
- A user study showed significant preference for MotionPhysics over baselines in both physical realism and prompt adherence, with preferences exceeding 80% for human-designed and AI-generated scenes.
- The framework achieved the highest average scores across objective metrics (Overall Consistency, CLIPSIM, Energy-Constrained Motion Score) and demonstrated robustness to varying external forces and heterogeneous material compositions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained video diffusion models can be effectively utilized as motion priors for physical simulation if their inherent appearance and geometric biases are actively suppressed during distillation.
> - Grounding LLM-based physical parameter initialization with real-world material property bounds through 'constraint-aware prompts' is crucial for preventing hallucinations and ensuring simulation stability.
> - A fully differentiable pipeline, combining LLM-driven initialization, advanced physical simulation, and an appearance-decoupled motion loss, enables generalizable text-guided dynamic content creation.
