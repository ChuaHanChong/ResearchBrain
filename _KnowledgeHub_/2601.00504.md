# MotionPhysics: Learnable Motion Distillation for Text-Guided Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> MotionPhysics introduces a framework for text-guided 3D dynamic simulation, integrating multimodal LLMs for parameter inference with a differentiable Material Point Method simulator. The system employs a novel Learnable Motion Distillation loss to extract pure motion signals from video diffusion models, enabling realistic and physically plausible animations for diverse materials and challenging out-of-distribution objects.

## Problem

- Achieving physically plausible 3D dynamic simulations requires extensive expert knowledge and laborious manual tuning of numerous physical parameters.
- Current pre-trained video diffusion models often generate motions that lack physical common sense and exhibit strong inductive biases from training data, leading to implausible results for novel or unconventional objects.
- Existing methods for automatic physical parameter estimation struggle to generalize across a wide variety of materials and different types of 3D objects.

## Method

- The framework leverages multimodal LLMs (e.g., GPT-4) to infer initial material parameters from natural language prompts, using constraint-aware prompts to embed physically plausible ranges.
- A differentiable ==Material Point Method== (==MPM==) simulator, adapted for 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== (3DGS) representations, handles the core physical dynamics for diverse material behaviors.
- A Learnable Motion Distillation (LMD) loss is introduced to guide the simulation by extracting robust motion priors from video diffusion models while minimizing their appearance and geometric biases.

## Results

- MotionPhysics consistently produced visually realistic and physically plausible dynamic simulations that adhered to user prompts, outperforming baselines on real-world, human-designed, and AI-generated objects.
- A user study showed significant preference for MotionPhysics over baselines in both physical realism and prompt adherence, with preferences exceeding 80% for human-designed and AI-generated scenes.
- The framework achieved the highest average scores across objective metrics (Overall Consistency, CLIPSIM, Energy-Constrained Motion Score) and demonstrated robustness to varying external forces and heterogeneous material compositions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained video diffusion models can be effectively utilized as motion priors for physical simulation if their inherent appearance and geometric biases are actively suppressed during distillation.
> - Grounding LLM-based physical parameter initialization with real-world material property bounds through 'constraint-aware prompts' is crucial for preventing hallucinations and ensuring simulation stability.
> - A fully differentiable pipeline, combining LLM-driven initialization, advanced physical simulation, and an appearance-decoupled motion loss, enables generalizable text-guided dynamic content creation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of realistic 3D dynamic simulations is a long-standing challenge in computer graphics and animation. Traditionally, achieving physically plausible behavior for 3D objects and diverse materials requires extensive expert knowledge and laborious, time-consuming manual tuning of physical parameters (e.g., Young’s modulus, density, yield stress). MotionPhysics directly addresses this bottleneck by aiming to automate the parameter estimation process from natural language prompts.

**Evolution of Parameter Estimation:**
Early approaches to automatic physical parameter estimation relied on direct observations from real-world phenomena or controlled experiments. More recently, the advent of novel view synthesis techniques like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and particularly 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has provided new avenues. 3DGS, with its explicit representation of scene geometry, has proven highly amenable to integration with differentiable physical simulators such as Material Point Method (MPM), Position-Based Dynamics (PBD), and eXtended Position-Based Dynamics (XPBD). This integration has revived interest in "system identification," where physical parameters are recovered from multi-view videos of synthetic objects.

**Limitations of Generative AI for Physics:**
A critical development in the broader landscape is the rise of large-scale generative models, especially text-to-video diffusion models. These models offer the potential for "zero-shot" parameter estimation by guiding simulations with text or image prompts. However, the paper highlights a significant limitation: current video diffusion models, both open-source and closed-source, often fail to produce videos that adhere to basic physical common sense. This is particularly problematic for "out-of-distribution" objects, such as AI-generated shapes, human-designed models with unconventional textures, or real-world objects subjected to novel forces. These models possess inherent *inductive shape and appearance biases* from their training data, making it challenging to extract pure motion signals divorced from visual specifics. Attempting to build fully physics-grounded video diffusion models from scratch is impractical due to immense computational demands and the need for vast, diverse ground-truth motion data across myriad novel objects.

**MotionPhysics' Position:**
MotionPhysics strategically positions itself to bridge the gap between powerful but often physically naive generative models and accurate, physics-based simulation. Instead of trying to force video diffusion models to directly *generate* physically accurate dynamics, MotionPhysics leverages them as *motion priors* to *guide differentiable physical simulators*. The key insight is to distill robust motion cues from these models while actively minimizing their appearance and geometry biases. Furthermore, the paper improves upon prior LLM-based initialization schemes (e.g., PhysFlow) by introducing *constraint-aware prompts*, which grounds the LLM's parameter estimations within physically plausible ranges, preventing hallucinations and enhancing realism.

By integrating state-of-the-art multimodal LLMs for intelligent initialization, a differentiable Material Point Method (MPM) simulator for diverse material behaviors, and a novel motion distillation technique, MotionPhysics contributes a robust and generalizable framework that pushes the boundaries of text-guided physical simulation, particularly for previously challenging out-of-distribution scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind MotionPhysics is to democratize 3D dynamic simulation, making it accessible to non-experts by automating the intricate process of physical parameter specification. The authors explicitly state their ultimate goal: "what you describe is exactly what you simulate."

Based on this overarching aim, the key objectives of the MotionPhysics framework are:

1. **Automated Physical Parameter Inference:** To automatically infer a plausible and comprehensive set of physical material parameters (density, material class, and class-specific coefficients) directly from a natural language prompt provided by a user. This eliminates the need for expert knowledge, manual tuning, or supervision from ground-truth trajectories or annotated videos, which are often scarce or difficult to obtain.
2. **Support for Diverse Materials and Object Types:** To enable the dynamic simulation of a wide variety of materials, including elastic solids, plasticine, metals, foams, sands, and both Newtonian and non-Newtonian fluids. Critically, the framework must operate effectively across different types of 3D objects, specifically addressing real-world scans, human-designed models (which may lack consistent textures), and AI-generated assets (which can exhibit atypical geometry or appearance).
3. **Overcoming Generative Model Biases:** To address the inherent limitations of pre-trained video diffusion models when used for physical guidance. Specifically, the objective is to extract "pure" motion signals from these models while actively suppressing their inductive biases related to appearance and geometry, which often lead to physically implausible outputs for out-of-distribution objects.
4. **Ensuring Physical Plausibility and Prompt Adherence:** To produce visually realistic and dynamically consistent simulations that faithfully adhere to the high-level motion instructions conveyed in the user's natural language prompt. This includes ensuring that the automatically determined physical parameters result in believable material responses under various force conditions.
5. **Developing a Robust and Efficient End-to-End Framework:** To build a fully automatic, differentiable system that is stable, computationally efficient, and generalize well across a broad spectrum of simulation scenarios.

By achieving these objectives, MotionPhysics seeks to advance the state-of-the-art in physically-grounded generative AI, enabling more intuitive and accurate creation of dynamic 3D content.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MotionPhysics proposes an end-to-end differentiable framework that orchestrates multimodal Large Language Models (LLMs) with a differentiable Material Point Method (MPM) simulator, guided by a novel Learnable Motion Distillation (LMD) loss derived from video diffusion models.

**1. Scene Representation and Simulation:**
- **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS):** The scene and objects are represented as a collection of 3D Gaussians (`G`), allowing for high-quality, real-time rendering and straightforward integration with differentiable simulators.
- **Differentiable MLS-MPM Simulator:** The core physical simulation is handled by a GS-adapted Moving Least Squares Material Point Method (MLS-MPM) simulator built on NVIDIA’s WARP. This simulator can evolve the states of the Gaussians (`Gt`) over time and supports a wide range of complex material behaviors (elasticity, plasticity, fluids) through various constitutive models. All splats share a common material parameter set `θ = {ρ, c, θc}` (density, material class, and class-specific coefficients).

**2. Initialization via Multimodal LLMs with Constraint-Aware Prompts:**
- **LLM Role:** A multimodal LLM (GPT-4) is used to estimate initial material parameters (`θini`) primarily from the user's natural language prompt (`Ptext`) and secondarily from a reference image. This determines the material type (`c`), density (`ρ`), and initial values for class-specific coefficients (`θc`).
- **Constraint-Aware Prompts (Key Innovation):** To combat LLM hallucinations and ensure physical plausibility, the authors provide GPT-4 with structured prompt templates. These templates embed domain-specific parameter bounds (e.g., typical Young’s modulus ranges for metals or foams) sourced from standard material property handbooks. By forcing the LLM to select values within these ranges, the initialization is anchored in real-world material properties, preventing egregious predictions and simulation failures.

**3. Learnable Motion Distillation (LMD) Loss (Core Technical Contribution):**
- **Motivation:** Traditional Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) from video diffusion models often struggles with out-of-distribution (OOD) objects (human-designed, AI-generated) because the diffusion model’s predictions entangle motion with appearance and geometric biases from its training data. The key observation is that even with varying appearance or shape, *identical simulated motions* produce globally consistent *latent codes* from a video encoder, differing only in local details.
- **Approach:**
 - **Augmented Perturbations:** The initial GS representation (`G0`) is augmented with additive Gaussian noise (`ϵ'`) to both kernel centers and color coefficients, yielding `˜G0`. This creates perturbed renderings `{˜Il}` and their corresponding latent embeddings `˜z0` from the video encoder `Eϕ`.
 - **One-Step Denoising:** One-step denoised latents `˜z0k` are computed from `˜zk` (which is `˜z0` perturbed by diffusion noise `ϵ`), effectively projecting `˜zk` back onto the "clean-latent manifold."
 - **Learnable Motion Extractor `M`:** A lightweight two-layer convolutional network, `M`, is introduced. It's initialized as an identity mapping and trained with a small learning rate. `M` is designed to extract *pure motion signals* from the latents.
 - **LMD Loss Formulation:** The LMD loss (`LLMD`) is defined as a Charbonnier variant that minimizes the difference between `y_pred = M(˜z0k)` (motion predicted from noisy latents) and `y_target = M(˜z0)` (motion from clean latents). `M` is synchronized via exponential moving averaging (EMA).
- **Effect:** This setup allows `LLMD` to distill robust motion priors from the pre-trained video diffusion model (CogVideoX) while being minimally affected by appearance and geometry discrepancies, thus guiding the differentiable simulation towards physically consistent motion specified by the text prompt.

**4. Optimization and Implementation:**
- The class-specific coefficients (`θc`) are refined through gradient-based optimization, backpropagating gradients from `LLMD` through the differentiable renderer and the MLS-MPM simulator.
- **Frame Boosting:** To stabilize gradient propagation and accelerate training for long MPM rollouts, a frame boosting scheme is employed. The total simulation frames are split into interleaved subsequences, with gradients computed only at the final step of each subsequence.
- **Boundary Conditions and Forces:** The framework supports diverse manual specifications of boundary conditions (e.g., clamp grid, surface colliders) and external force applications (e.g., constant force, impulse, rotation), enabling precise spatiotemporal control.

**5. Extension to Heterogeneous Materials:**
MotionPhysics can extend to multi-object scenes by combining GS segmentation (e.g., using SAM2) with the multimodal LLM, allowing it to infer distinct material properties for each object from a single prompt. Since MPM particles carry their own material parameters, high-fidelity heterogeneous dynamics are naturally supported.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MotionPhysics was extensively evaluated across more than thirty scenarios, encompassing real-world, human-designed, and AI-generated 3D objects, and a wide array of materials.

**1. Qualitative Evaluation (Figure 1, 4, A1):**
- MotionPhysics consistently produced visually realistic and physically plausible dynamic simulations that accurately adhered to the user's input prompt.
- It demonstrated superior performance over baselines (PhysDreamer, DreamPhysics, OmniPhysGS, PhysFlow), particularly for challenging out-of-distribution objects. For instance, it correctly captured Newtonian viscosity for "jam spreading" and non-Newtonian behavior for "toothpaste dropping," phenomena where baselines often failed to produce accurate deformations or fluid dynamics.
- The framework showed robustness to diverse input forces and conditions (Figure 7), generating appropriate elastic deformations or maintaining structural integrity under increased stress where other methods generated static responses or unrealistic detachments.

**2. User Study (Table 1):**
- A two-alternative forced-choice (2AFC) user study involving 79 participants compared MotionPhysics against baselines on physical realism and prompt adherence.
- MotionPhysics significantly outperformed all baselines. User preferences exceeded 50% across all evaluated datasets (Human-Designed, Real-World, AI-Generated).
- Notably, preferences for MotionPhysics were particularly strong (>80%) on human-designed and AI-generated scenes, confirming its generalization capabilities to novel geometries and textures, a key challenge addressed by the paper.

**3. Objective Quantitative Metrics (Table 2):**
- MotionPhysics achieved the highest average scores across all objective metrics: Overall Consistency (OC, measuring text-video semantic alignment), CLIPSIM (averaging frame-text similarity), and Energy-Constrained Motion Score (ECMS, quantifying motion realism).
- Optimization time for MotionPhysics was competitive (approx. 18.39 minutes for a typical scene) with other NVIDIA WARP-based baselines, significantly faster than PyTorch-based OmniPhysGS (~9 hours).
- **Acknowledged Discrepancy:** The authors noted that while these metrics are useful, they do not perfectly align with human perception, particularly in distinguishing subtle dynamic differences across identical scenes, forces, and prompts. They attribute this to the limitations of current video-text consistency and motion metrics, which may focus more on static appearance or lack the ability to capture diverse material dynamics specified by text.

**4. Ablation Studies:**
- **Impact of LMD Loss:** Experiments comparing `L_LMD` with an optical flow loss (`L_Flow`) and standard Score Distillation Sampling (`L_SDS`) demonstrated the superiority of `L_LMD`. It produced simulations that closely matched manually tuned references and achieved higher objective scores. Crucially, `L_LMD` ensured consistent motion patterns across variations in object texture and geometry, unlike `L_SDS` which led to divergent and less physically accurate simulations (Figure 5, Figure A3).
- **Impact of LLM Initialization:** The ablation study on initialization showed that using *constraint-aware prompts* for the multimodal LLM (ours) provided a stable and plausible starting point. This prevented unrealistic exaggerations or overly rigid behaviors observed with naive LLM initialization (PhysFlow) or median-value initialization, proving the efficacy of grounding LLM predictions in physical ranges (Figure 6).
- **Robustness to Varying Conditions:** MotionPhysics proved robust to changes in external forces and camera viewpoints (Figure 7), accurately simulating material responses (e.g., elastic vibration of a telephone cord, structural integrity of a plane propeller under increased speed) where baselines faltered.
- **Heterogeneous Materials:** The framework successfully extended to multi-object scenes with different materials (e.g., metal axe hitting a rubber toy), yielding high-fidelity heterogeneous dynamics with fewer visual artifacts compared to baselines (Figure 8).

In summary, MotionPhysics demonstrates state-of-the-art performance in text-guided 3D dynamic simulation, establishing its efficacy through comprehensive qualitative, quantitative, and user study evaluations across a diverse range of objects and material types.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MotionPhysics represents a significant step forward in the field of physically-grounded generative AI and 3D simulation. Its core contribution lies in enabling high-fidelity, text-guided dynamic simulations without requiring expert knowledge or ground-truth motion data, a long-standing barrier to entry in this domain.

**Key Significance:**

1. **Democratization of 3D Simulation:** By allowing users to specify complex dynamic behaviors through natural language prompts, MotionPhysics effectively lowers the entry barrier for creating realistic physical animations. This empowers a broader audience, including artists, designers, game developers, and even casual users, to generate rich interactive content without needing to master intricate physical parameters or simulation software.
2. **Robustness to Out-of-Distribution Data:** The novel Learnable Motion Distillation (LMD) loss is a critical innovation. By explicitly decoupling motion signals from the appearance and geometric biases inherent in pre-trained video diffusion models, MotionPhysics achieves remarkable generalization. This means it can generate plausible physical behaviors for unconventional, human-designed, or AI-generated objects that existing diffusion-based methods struggle with, greatly expanding the applicability of generative simulation.
3. **Enhanced Physical Plausibility:** The combination of constraint-aware LLM initialization and the LMD loss leads to genuinely physically plausible simulations. The LLM’s estimates are grounded in realistic material ranges, and the LMD loss steers the differentiable simulator towards accurate motion dynamics, ensuring that the simulated outcome aligns with real-world physics.
4. **Efficiency and Automation:** Automating the parameter estimation and optimization process significantly reduces the time and effort traditionally required for setting up simulations. The differentiable nature of the framework, coupled with optimized implementation details like frame boosting, ensures that this automation is also computationally feasible.
5. **Foundation for Future AI-driven Content Creation:** MotionPhysics contributes to the broader vision of "world models" in AI, where generative models can understand and replicate not just how things look, but how they *behave* physically. This work provides a robust methodology for integrating the vast knowledge encoded in large generative models with the predictive power of physics engines, paving the way for more intelligent, interactive, and consistent AI-generated virtual worlds and cinematic experiences.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Game Development:** Accelerate asset creation and interaction design for games, allowing designers to quickly prototype and test physically realistic behaviors for various in-game objects.
- **Visual Effects (VFX) and Animation:** Provide artists with intuitive tools to generate complex physical effects (e.g., liquid simulations, deformable objects, destruction) from high-level descriptions, reducing reliance on manual keyframing and parameter tweaking.
- **Robotics and Virtual Prototyping:** Enable rapid simulation of materials and object interactions for robotic planning and virtual product testing, especially for novel designs or material compositions.
- **Education and Training:** Create interactive educational content that demonstrates physical principles with realistic simulations based on simple text inputs.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge a few limitations. MotionPhysics currently does not model shadow effects, which could enhance visual realism. More importantly, while the estimated parameters yield plausible simulations, they are not intended for precise real-world material measurement (a task for dedicated system identification).

Future work aims to further automate fine-grained simulation configurations from text and extend the motion distillation loss to other animation tasks, such as character rigging and deformation. These directions suggest a continued commitment to making physically-grounded content creation more accessible and versatile through advanced AI techniques.
