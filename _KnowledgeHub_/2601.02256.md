# VAR RL Done Right: Tackling Asynchronous Policy Conflicts in Visual Autoregressive Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and ByteDance introduced a novel Reinforcement Learning framework specifically designed to resolve asynchronous policy conflicts in Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models. This framework substantially improved text rendering accuracy on CVTG-2K by 41.6% and elevated overall human preference scores by 2.21 absolute points, establishing state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmarks.

## Problem

- Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models suffer from 'asynchronous policy conflicts' during Reinforcement Learning (RL) optimization.
- The multi-scale, parallel token generation in VARs creates significant heterogeneity in action spaces and task similarities across timesteps, leading to unstable RL training and slow convergence.
- Standard RL algorithms, like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), are ineffective due to this varying input structure and poor credit assignment, especially with limited samples.

## Method

- The authors propose a synergistic RL framework, enhancing ==Group Relative Policy== Optimization (GRPO) with three core components: Value as Middle Return (VMR), Per-Action Normalization Weighting (PANW), and Mask Propagation (MP).
- VMR decomposes the long-horizon RL problem at a middle timestep, introducing an intermediate soft return to provide denser, lower-variance feedback to early generation steps without altering the optimal policy.
- PANW normalizes step-wise losses based on token-grid sizes to balance gradient contributions across heterogeneous timesteps, while MP enhances spatiotemporal credit assignment by propagating reward-derived masks to focus updates on causally relevant regions.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved a **41.6%** relative increase in Word Accuracy (from **0.5536** to **0.7841**) and a **16.2%** relative increase in Normalized Edit Distance on the CVTG-**2K** dataset for text rendering.
- NextFlow-RL demonstrated state-of-the-art performance in Human Preference Scores (HPSv3) across multiple categories, with an overall absolute improvement of 2.21 points (from 8.43 to 10.64) compared to the baseline.
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of VMR, PANW, and MP, showing that optimal hyperparameter settings and fine-grained alternating training are crucial for addressing asynchronous policy conflicts and achieving high-quality results.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The unique multi-scale parallel generation of VAR models introduces fundamental 'asynchronous policy conflicts' that hinder robust RL optimization, requiring specific architectural adaptations.
> - Decomposing the RL objective with an intermediate value estimate (VMR) effectively stabilizes training and improves credit assignment in long-horizon, heterogeneous sequential tasks while provably preserving policy optimality.
> - Balancing gradient contributions from varying action space sizes (PANW) and precisely focusing credit assignment on relevant spatial and temporal components (MP) are critical for effective and stable RL in high-dimensional generative processes.
