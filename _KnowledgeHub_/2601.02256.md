# VAR RL Done Right: Tackling Asynchronous Policy Conflicts in Visual Autoregressive Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and ByteDance introduced a novel Reinforcement Learning framework specifically designed to resolve asynchronous policy conflicts in Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models. This framework substantially improved text rendering accuracy on CVTG-2K by 41.6% and elevated overall human preference scores by 2.21 absolute points, establishing state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmarks.

## Problem

- Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models suffer from 'asynchronous policy conflicts' during Reinforcement Learning (RL) optimization.
- The multi-scale, parallel token generation in VARs creates significant heterogeneity in action spaces and task similarities across timesteps, leading to unstable RL training and slow convergence.
- Standard RL algorithms, like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), are ineffective due to this varying input structure and poor credit assignment, especially with limited samples.

## Method

- The authors propose a synergistic RL framework, enhancing ==Group Relative Policy== Optimization (GRPO) with three core components: Value as Middle Return (VMR), Per-Action Normalization Weighting (PANW), and Mask Propagation (MP).
- VMR decomposes the long-horizon RL problem at a middle timestep, introducing an intermediate soft return to provide denser, lower-variance feedback to early generation steps without altering the optimal policy.
- PANW normalizes step-wise losses based on token-grid sizes to balance gradient contributions across heterogeneous timesteps, while MP enhances spatiotemporal credit assignment by propagating reward-derived masks to focus updates on causally relevant regions.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved a **41.6%** relative increase in Word Accuracy (from **0.5536** to **0.7841**) and a **16.2%** relative increase in Normalized Edit Distance on the CVTG-**2K** dataset for text rendering.
- NextFlow-RL demonstrated state-of-the-art performance in Human Preference Scores (HPSv3) across multiple categories, with an overall absolute improvement of 2.21 points (from 8.43 to 10.64) compared to the baseline.
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of VMR, PANW, and MP, showing that optimal hyperparameter settings and fine-grained alternating training are crucial for addressing asynchronous policy conflicts and achieving high-quality results.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The unique multi-scale parallel generation of VAR models introduces fundamental 'asynchronous policy conflicts' that hinder robust RL optimization, requiring specific architectural adaptations.
> - Decomposing the RL objective with an intermediate value estimate (VMR) effectively stabilizes training and improves credit assignment in long-horizon, heterogeneous sequential tasks while provably preserving policy optimality.
> - Balancing gradient contributions from varying action space sizes (PANW) and precisely focusing credit assignment on relevant spatial and temporal components (MP) are critical for effective and stable RL in high-dimensional generative processes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual generation has rapidly evolved, primarily driven by three distinct paradigms: Autoregressive (AR), Diffusion, and Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models. Autoregressive models, inspired by the success of Large Language Models (LLMs) in sequential token prediction, generate images pixel by pixel or token by token in a fixed order (e.g., raster scan). While effective, these models often suffer from slow inference speeds due to the high number of sequential steps required for high-resolution images. Diffusion models, currently a dominant force, synthesize images through an iterative denoising process, achieving remarkable fidelity and diversity. However, they can still be computationally intensive during sampling.

Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models, the focus of this paper, represent a third paradigm. Unlike traditional AR models, VARs operate on a hierarchy of discrete token grids, generating tokens in parallel at progressively finer spatial scales (coarse-to-fine). This "next-scale prediction" strategy offers advantages in both sampling efficiency and image fidelity, aligning well with modern high-resolution image backbones.

The application of Reinforcement Learning (RL) has revolutionized LLMs, enabling significant improvements in reasoning capabilities and human alignment (e.g., RLHF, DPO, GRPO). Naturally, researchers are extending RL techniques to visual generation to enhance output fidelity and controllability. While RL has been applied to flow-based generative models and raster-scan AR models (e.g., T2I-R1, AR-GRPO, SimpleAR), its systematic study for text-to-image VAR models, particularly those employing multi-scale parallel token generation, remains largely unexplored. Previous applications of GRPO to class-conditioned VAR models have not adequately addressed the fundamental structural challenges inherent in VAR's unique generation process.

This paper identifies a critical gap: the "asynchronous policy conflicts" that arise in VAR models when subjected to RL optimization. As VARs generate tokens at varying spatial resolutions across steps, the number of "query tokens" can fluctuate dramatically, creating heterogeneous task similarities across timesteps. This heterogeneity leads to instability, slow convergence, and suboptimal alignment when standard RL methods like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) are directly applied. By focusing on these unique challenges, "VAR RL Done Right" aims to establish a robust RL training recipe for text-to-image VAR models, positioning itself as a pioneering work that bridges the efficiency benefits of VAR with the powerful alignment capabilities of RL. It seeks to unlock VAR's full potential in generating high-quality, semantically aligned images, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with this architecture in a fine-tuned manner.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from a critical challenge in optimizing Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models using Reinforcement Learning (RL): the phenomenon of **asynchronous policy conflicts across timesteps**. While VAR models offer efficient, high-fidelity image generation through their coarse-to-fine, parallel token emission strategy, this heterogeneous generation process creates severe difficulties when applying RL.

Specifically, the number of query tokens (representing different spatial scales) fluctuates by orders of magnitude from coarse to fine generation steps. This dramatic variation in input structure and task similarity across timesteps makes standard RL algorithms, particularly Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), unstable and prone to suboptimal alignment. The RL stage typically operates with far fewer samples than the large-scale pretraining phase, exacerbating this instability and hindering effective fine-tuning. As illustrated by the initial GRPO training curves in the paper (Figure 2), vanilla GRPO on full-scale VAR suffers from instability and underperforms compared to supervised RL at partial-prefix scales, clearly indicating a fundamental issue.

Given this diagnosis, the **key objectives** of this work are threefold:

1. **Systematically diagnose and formalize the asynchronous policy conflicts:** The authors aim to clarify why bandit-style GRPO becomes unstable under the heterogeneous, parallel actions characteristic of VAR models. This involves formulating VAR sequence generation as a deterministic Markov Decision Process (MDP) to provide a solid theoretical basis for addressing the problem.
2. **Develop a novel and effective RL framework for VAR models:** The primary objective is to propose a framework that explicitly manages and mitigates these asynchronous policy conflicts, thereby enabling robust and stable RL optimization for VAR models.
3. **Achieve significant improvements in visual generation performance:** The ultimate goal is to enhance sample quality and objective alignment in VAR models, demonstrated through established benchmarks in text rendering and human preference, by addressing the training instability. This includes outperforming baseline GRPO methods and achieving state-of-the-art results compared to other generative paradigms.

By achieving these objectives, the authors seek to unlock the full potential of VAR models, combining their inherent efficiency with the power of RL for fine-grained control and improved alignment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

To address the asynchronous policy conflicts inherent in Reinforcement Learning for Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models, the authors propose a novel and synergistic framework that enhances Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with three core components: Value as Middle Return (VMR), Per-Action Normalization Weighting (PANW), and Mask Propagation (MP).

#### 3.1. Preliminary: VAR Sequences as MDPs

The authors first formalize VAR sequence generation as a deterministic Markov Decision Process (MDP) (S, A, P, r).
- **State Space (S):** A partial VAR sequence of token grids `s_t = (r_1..., r_t)`.
- **Action Space (A):** Generating the next resolution token grid `a_t = r_{t+1}`.
- **Transition Probability (P):** Deterministic, leading to `s_{t+1}` given `s_t` and `a_t`.
- **Reward Function (r):** A terminal reward `R(s_T)` is provided only at the end of the generation sequence, reflecting typical image generation evaluation.
This formulation allows for the principled application of hierarchical RL and analysis within a KL-regularized RL framework, where the optimal policy is shown to be proportional to the old policy exponentiated by the action-value function `Q*(s_t, a_t)`.

#### 3.2. Value as Middle Return (VMR)

VMR is introduced to stabilize training by decomposing the long-horizon RL problem.
- **Formulation:** The full-horizon, KL-regularized RL objective is split at a middle timestep `m` into two coupled subproblems: a prefix problem (optimizing `π_{1:m-1}`) and a suffix problem (optimizing `π_{m:T-1}`).
- **Mechanism:** An "intermediate soft return" `V*m(s_m)` is inserted at step `m`. The suffix policy is trained to maximize `R(s_T)`, while the prefix policy is trained using `V*m(s_m)` as its sole reward. This provides denser feedback to early steps.
- **Analysis:** The authors provide a theoretical proof (Theorem 2) demonstrating that this two-stage decomposition is "structure-preserving." It provably does not alter the "family-optimal policy" within the VAR family (Mπ), which generates tokens on the same resolution in parallel. This ensures that while training is stabilized, the global objective value is maintained, and credit assignment interference is avoided.
- **Implementation:** `V*m(s_m)` is not learned by a separate critic but is estimated directly from on-policy terminal rewards using a risk-sensitive Monte Carlo estimator: `bV_m(s_m) = η log (1/K * Σ exp(1/η * R(k)(s_T)))`, where `K` is the number of on-policy samples and `η` is a temperature parameter (set to 1, K=2 in practice).

#### 3.3. Per-Action Normalization Weighting (PANW)

To counter the significant step-wise heterogeneity in VAR generation (e.g., varying token grid sizes across resolutions, as depicted in Figure 1), PANW normalizes the contribution of each action.
- **Mechanism:** Each step-`t` loss is weighted by `k_t = 1/(h_t * w_t)^α`, where `h_t * w_t` is the token-grid size at that step, and `α` is a decay exponent. This is followed by a step-level normalization.
- **Benefit:** This scheme balances the gradient scales and KL usage across timesteps, preventing updates from high-resolution (fine-scale) steps, which have many more tokens, from disproportionately dominating the learning process. This leads to improved training stability.

#### 3.4. Mask Propagation (MP)

Inspired by gradient-based reward-feedback practices, MP enhances spatiotemporal credit assignment.
- **Mechanism:** An initial mask is constructed from the output components that directly contribute to the terminal reward (e.g., bounding boxes for OCR). This mask is then propagated backward through the model’s multi-scale hierarchy, from finer to coarser feature scales (Figure 4).
- **Benefit:** The propagated mask gates intermediate rewards and gradients, effectively focusing credit assignment on the causally relevant regions and tokens. This reduces variance in updates across both space and time, leading to more precise and efficient learning.

#### 3.5. Training and Evaluation Setup

- **Base Model:** NextFlow, a VAR model, is used as the backbone, initialized from Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
- **Training Strategy:** On-policy training with group size 16 (candidates per prompt) and batch size 16 (prompts). AdamW optimizer is used.
- **Alternating Optimization:** A fine-grained alternation scheme is adopted, with three prefix GRPO updates for every one suffix GRPO update. This ensures frequent, localized updates.
- **Reward Design:**
 - **Text Rendering:** A composite OCR reward is designed using PaddleOCRv5. It includes:
 - **Completeness:** Confidence-aware measure of ground-truth words found in predictions.
 - **Length Mismatch Penalty:** Penalizes over/under-generation using a character-level multiset distance.
 - **Similarity:** Confidence-weighted normalized Levenshtein distance for best-matched words.
 - **Human Preference:** HPSv3 is used as a direct reward function, deployed as a self-hosted service for efficiency.
- **Evaluation:** Performance is assessed on the CVTG-2K dataset for text rendering (metrics: Word Accuracy, Normalized Edit Distance (NED), CLIPScore) and on HPSv3 benchmarks for human preference.

This comprehensive methodology, combining theoretical foundations with practical algorithmic innovations, aims to provide a robust and effective solution for RL optimization in VAR models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling evidence that the proposed framework significantly enhances the stability and performance of RL optimization for VAR models, addressing the asynchronous policy conflicts effectively.

#### 4.1. Diagnosis Confirmation

The initial diagnosis of asynchronous policy conflicts is visually supported by Figure 2, which demonstrates that vanilla GRPO applied to full-scale VAR generation leads to unstable training curves and suboptimal reward values compared to supervised RL at partial-prefix scales. This underscores the necessity of the proposed solutions.

#### 4.2. Text Rendering Performance

On the challenging CVTG-2K dataset for text rendering, NextFlow-RL (the proposed method) consistently and markedly outperforms the vanilla NextFlow baseline:
- **Word Accuracy (WA):** Improves from 0.5536 to **0.7841**, an absolute gain of 0.2305 and a relative improvement of **41.6%**.
- **Normalized Edit Distance (NED):** Increases from 0.7816 to **0.9081**, an absolute gain of 0.1265 and a relative improvement of **16.2%**.
- **CLIPScore:** Shows a modest but positive increase from 0.8068 to **0.8224**, indicating preserved or improved semantic alignment.

These results are particularly noteworthy as NextFlow-RL not only surpasses its own baseline but also achieves leading or state-of-the-art performance among a wide array of powerful generative models, including diffusion-centric ones like SD3.5, TextCrafter, and FLUX.1 dev, and even some proprietary systems like Seedream 3.0. Visually, Figure 5 demonstrates clear improvements in character order, correctness of glyphs, and reduction of missing or extraneous characters in the RL-refined outputs.

#### 4.3. Human Preference Score (HPSv3) Performance

To assess generalization and overall aesthetic quality, the method was evaluated using HPSv3. NextFlow-RL delivered substantial gains over the baseline NextFlow across all categories:
- **Overall HPS (All):** Improves from 8.43 to **10.64** (+2.21 absolute).
- Consistent improvements are observed across diverse sub-categories such as Animals (10.79 vs. 8.34), Natural Scenery (10.43 vs. 8.22), Plants (10.57 vs. 8.19), and Food (11.18 vs. 8.93).
NextFlow-RL achieved **state-of-the-art** performance in multiple key categories (All, Architecture, Animals, Natural Scenery, Plants, Food, and Others) among strong diffusion-centric competitors (e.g., Flux-dev, Kolors, Playground-v2.5), and remained highly competitive in others (Characters, Design). The visual samples in Figure 6 showcase RL-enhanced images with sharper details, cleaner structures, and stronger adherence to diverse prompts, further validating the broad applicability of the framework.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Comprehensive ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of each proposed component and guide hyperparameter selection:
- **Selection of `m` (VMR split point):** Experimentation revealed that setting `m` to correspond to resolutions of 128x128 or 256x256 (i.e., `m=m_128` or `m=m_256`) yielded the best results (e.g., Word Acc. 0.6677 for `m_128`, 0.6565 for `m_256`). Placing VMR earlier significantly reduces variance and improves early credit assignment. `m=m_256` was chosen as default due to comparable quality and better compatibility with the mask mechanism.
- **Decay exponent `α` (PANW):** An `α` value in the range of [0.6, 0.8] proved optimal, providing a robust balance for gradient normalization without over-suppressing high-resolution updates.
- **Mask Propagation (MP):** Enabling MP consistently improved text fidelity metrics (Word Acc. 0.7071 vs. 0.6855; NED 0.8699 vs. 0.8601), affirming its role in sharpening spatiotemporal credit assignment without impacting CLIPScore.
- **Number of samples `K` (VMR estimation):** `K=2` on-policy samples for VMR estimation performed best, striking an optimal balance between sufficient exploration and managing variance.
- **Alternating training granularity:** A fine-grained alternation scheme (e.g., 3 prefix updates for every 1 suffix update) consistently outperformed coarse-grained alternation, indicating that more frequent, localized updates are crucial for resolving asynchronous policy conflicts efficiently.

In summary, the main findings demonstrate that the proposed VMR, PANW, and MP components synergistically address the unique challenges of RL for VAR models, leading to significant performance gains across both objective and human preference metrics, and establishing VAR models as highly competitive in the generative AI landscape.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the field of visual generative AI, particularly concerning Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models and their optimization with Reinforcement Learning (RL). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Pioneering RL for VAR Models:** This paper presents the first systematic RL framework tailored for text-to-image VAR models. By thoroughly diagnosing and formalizing the "asynchronous policy conflicts" inherent in VAR's multi-scale, parallel generation process, the authors have identified and addressed a previously unstudied critical challenge. This opens up a new avenue for research in optimizing heterogeneous sequence generation.

2. **Robust Methodological Contributions:** The introduction of Value as Middle Return (VMR), Per-Action Normalization Weighting (PANW), and Mask Propagation (MP) constitutes a robust and innovative methodological toolkit.
 - **VMR** offers a principled way to decompose long-horizon RL problems, providing denser and lower-variance feedback without altering the optimal policy, a theoretically profound and practically valuable result. This approach could inspire similar decomposition strategies in other complex sequential decision-making tasks.
 - **PANW** provides a simple yet effective solution for balancing gradients across heterogeneous action spaces, a common problem in models with varying output dimensions across steps.
 - **MP** enhances credit assignment by spatially and temporally focusing updates on reward-relevant tokens, a concept derived from Reward Feedback Learning that is critical for efficiency and stability in high-dimensional output spaces.

3. **Performance Breakthroughs:** The demonstrated state-of-the-art results in text rendering and human preference scores are highly impactful. NextFlow-RL not only drastically improves upon its VAR baseline but also rivals and often surpasses leading diffusion models across multiple challenging benchmarks. This indicates that VAR models, when properly aligned with RL, can achieve superior performance in areas requiring fine-grained control and semantic fidelity, proving their potential beyond just generation speed.

4. **Enabling Practical Applications of VAR:** VAR models are inherently more efficient for high-resolution image synthesis compared to traditional autoregressive or even some diffusion approaches, due to their parallel token generation. By making RL optimization robust and effective for VARs, this work removes a significant barrier to their wider adoption. This could accelerate the development of practical generative AI systems for applications where both high-quality output and efficient inference are crucial, such as content creation, digital design, and personalized media generation. The improved ability to render text accurately within images, for instance, has direct implications for graphic design, advertising, and user interface generation.

5. **Theoretical Advancements:** The formalization of VAR generation as a deterministic MDP and the proofs for VMR's policy invariance provide a strong theoretical foundation. This contributes to a deeper understanding of RL dynamics in complex generative architectures and offers guiding principles for future algorithmic designs.

6. **Catalyst for Future Research:** This work is likely to inspire further research in several directions:
 - Applying the VMR, PANW, and MP concepts to other forms of heterogeneous sequence generation tasks in both vision and language.
 - Exploring more complex reward functions and preference learning techniques for VAR models.
 - Investigating hybrid architectures that combine the strengths of VAR with other paradigms like diffusion.
 - Further scaling of VAR models with RL to even higher resolutions and diverse modalities.

In conclusion, "VAR RL Done Right" significantly advances the state-of-the-art in visual generation by providing a comprehensive and theoretically grounded solution to a critical problem in VAR model optimization. Its impact will be felt across both academic research and industrial applications, pushing the boundaries of what is possible with efficient and highly controllable generative AI.
