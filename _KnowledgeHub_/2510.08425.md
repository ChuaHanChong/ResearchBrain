# Reinforcing Diffusion Models by Direct Group Preference Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Introducing Direct Group Preference Optimization (DGPO), a novel online reinforcement learning algorithm, enables diffusion models to learn from group-level preferences without requiring a stochastic policy. This method achieves up to 30 times faster training and sets new state-of-the-art performance in compositional image generation, visual text rendering, and human preference alignment, while preserving high visual quality.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods for generative AI, like GRPO, require stochastic policies, which are inefficient for diffusion models that primarily use deterministic ODE samplers.
- Previous attempts to apply GRPO-style methods to diffusion models relied on slow, less effective SDE-based samplers, yielding weak learning signals and high computational costs.
- Effectively aligning diffusion models with fine-grained human preferences and improving specific capabilities like complex instruction following remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- ==Direct Group Preference== Optimization (DGPO) is an online RL algorithm designed to learn from group-level preferences, bypassing the need for a stochastic policy for diffusion models.
- The method uses efficient deterministic ODE samplers to generate groups of samples, then calculates advantage scores to partition them into positive and negative sets.
- A novel advantage-based weighting scheme in the objective function mathematically eliminates the intractable partition function, enabling direct optimization.
- A Timestep Clip Strategy is introduced to prevent visual degradation by avoiding training on low-quality early-timestep generated samples.

## Results

- DGPO demonstrates up to 30 times faster training compared to previous state-of-the-art methods like Flow-GRPO, significantly enhancing efficiency.
- The method achieved a **97%** overall score on the GenEval compositional image generation benchmark, **0.96** OCR Accuracy for visual text rendering, and a 23.89 PickScore for human preference alignment, setting new performance benchmarks.
- Models fine-tuned with DGPO exhibit improved instruction following and superior visual quality, while also maintaining or enhancing performance on out-of-domain quality metrics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The power of group preference optimization stems from using fine-grained relative preferences within a sample group, which can be harnessed without a stochastic policy.
> - Efficient, deterministic ODE samplers are effective for training rollouts in diffusion models for RL, offering superior quality and speed over less efficient SDE samplers.
> - A specific advantage-based weighting strategy can mathematically cancel out intractable partition function terms in the preference optimization objective, simplifying and stabilizing training.
