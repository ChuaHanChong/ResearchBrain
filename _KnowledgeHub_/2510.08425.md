# Reinforcing Diffusion Models by Direct Group Preference Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Introducing Direct Group Preference Optimization (DGPO), a novel online reinforcement learning algorithm, enables diffusion models to learn from group-level preferences without requiring a stochastic policy. This method achieves up to 30 times faster training and sets new state-of-the-art performance in compositional image generation, visual text rendering, and human preference alignment, while preserving high visual quality.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods for generative AI, like GRPO, require stochastic policies, which are inefficient for diffusion models that primarily use deterministic ODE samplers.
- Previous attempts to apply GRPO-style methods to diffusion models relied on slow, less effective SDE-based samplers, yielding weak learning signals and high computational costs.
- Effectively aligning diffusion models with fine-grained human preferences and improving specific capabilities like complex instruction following remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- ==Direct Group Preference== Optimization (DGPO) is an online RL algorithm designed to learn from group-level preferences, bypassing the need for a stochastic policy for diffusion models.
- The method uses efficient deterministic ODE samplers to generate groups of samples, then calculates advantage scores to partition them into positive and negative sets.
- A novel advantage-based weighting scheme in the objective function mathematically eliminates the intractable partition function, enabling direct optimization.
- A Timestep Clip Strategy is introduced to prevent visual degradation by avoiding training on low-quality early-timestep generated samples.

## Results

- DGPO demonstrates up to 30 times faster training compared to previous state-of-the-art methods like Flow-GRPO, significantly enhancing efficiency.
- The method achieved a **97%** overall score on the GenEval compositional image generation benchmark, **0.96** OCR Accuracy for visual text rendering, and a 23.89 PickScore for human preference alignment, setting new performance benchmarks.
- Models fine-tuned with DGPO exhibit improved instruction following and superior visual quality, while also maintaining or enhancing performance on out-of-domain quality metrics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The power of group preference optimization stems from using fine-grained relative preferences within a sample group, which can be harnessed without a stochastic policy.
> - Efficient, deterministic ODE samplers are effective for training rollouts in diffusion models for RL, offering superior quality and speed over less efficient SDE samplers.
> - A specific advantage-based weighting strategy can mathematically cancel out intractable partition function terms in the preference optimization objective, simplifying and stabilizing training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the crucial intersection of Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Diffusion Models (DMs), aiming to bridge a significant gap in the post-training alignment of generative models.

**The Landscape of Generative AI Alignment:**
The field of generative AI has seen immense progress, particularly with Large Language Models (LLMs) and Diffusion Models. A key challenge, however, is aligning these powerful models with human preferences, complex instructions, and desired quality metrics. For LLMs, Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) has become a cornerstone, with methods like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) (Rafailov et al., 2024) and Group Relative Preference Optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024) successfully enhancing capabilities like reasoning and safety.

**The Mismatch for Diffusion Models:**
While RLHF has been transformative for LLMs, its direct application to diffusion models has lagged. Diffusion models generate images (or other data) by progressively denoising a random input. Most performant diffusion samplers rely on deterministic Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) for efficiency and high-quality outputs. However, traditional policy-gradient RL methods, including GRPO, fundamentally require a *stochastic policy* to facilitate exploration and effective gradient estimation.

**Prior Attempts and Their Limitations:**
Previous attempts to adapt GRPO-style methods to diffusion models (e.g., Flow-GRPO by Liu et al., 2025; Xue et al., 2025) have resorted to using stochastic Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE)-based samplers. This "forced adaptation" introduces several drawbacks:
1. **Inefficiency:** SDE-based rollouts are less efficient and often produce lower-quality samples under a fixed computational budget compared to ODEs.
2. **Weak Learning Signal:** The stochasticity typically comes from model-agnostic Gaussian noise, providing a weak and inefficient learning signal, especially in high-dimensional image spaces.
3. **High Computational Cost:** Training is often performed over the entire sampling trajectory, making each iteration computationally expensive.

**The Innovation of DGPO:**
This paper, through Direct Group Preference Optimization (DGPO), directly addresses these limitations. It argues that the core strength of GRPO lies in its ability to leverage *fine-grained relative preference information within a group* of samples, rather than its policy-gradient formulation. By disentangling this crucial aspect, DGPO offers a novel online RL algorithm specifically tailored for diffusion models, overcoming the need for a stochastic policy and enabling the use of efficient ODE samplers. Conceptually, it extends DPO by incorporating group-wise information, effectively re-imagining GRPO for diffusion models. This makes DGPO a significant advancement in the efficient and effective alignment of diffusion models with diverse preference signals.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of Direct Group Preference Optimization (DGPO) are:

1. **Overcome the Policy-Gradient Mismatch for Diffusion Models:** The central motivation is to resolve the fundamental conflict between the requirements of existing reinforcement learning methods like GRPO (which necessitate a stochastic policy) and the operational mechanics of diffusion models (which predominantly use efficient, deterministic ODE-based samplers). Prior adaptations using inefficient SDE-based samplers are deemed suboptimal and restrict the potential of RL for diffusion models.

2. **Enable Faster and More Efficient Training:** A critical goal is to significantly accelerate the post-training process for diffusion models. Existing methods are computationally expensive due to reliance on SDE samplers, weak learning signals from model-agnostic noise, and training over entire sampling trajectories. DGPO aims to be substantially faster per iteration and in overall convergence.

3. **Leverage Fine-Grained Group Preference Information:** The authors identify that the true power of methods like GRPO stems from their ability to utilize *relative preference information* among multiple samples generated for a single prompt. The objective is to harness this powerful group-level information without the limitations of a policy-gradient framework.

4. **Achieve Superior Performance:** Beyond efficiency, the goal is to develop a method that delivers superior performance on various evaluation metrics, including complex compositional image generation, visual text rendering, and overall human preference alignment, while also maintaining or improving general image quality.

5. **Develop a "Diffusion-Native" RL Alignment Algorithm:** The motivation is to create an RL algorithm that inherently suits the architecture and sampling mechanisms of diffusion models, moving beyond forced adaptations and instead building on their strengths, such as efficient ODE sampling.

In essence, DGPO seeks to unlock the full potential of reinforcement learning for diffusion models by creating an efficient, high-performing, and intrinsically compatible optimization framework that leverages powerful group-level preference signals.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Direct Group Preference Optimization (DGPO) is an online reinforcement learning algorithm designed to efficiently fine-tune diffusion models by directly learning from group-level preferences, thereby circumventing the need for a stochastic policy.

**Core Idea:**
DGPO’s central insight is that the success of Group Relative Preference Optimization (GRPO) in LLMs lies in its ability to utilize fine-grained relative preference information within groups, not necessarily its policy-gradient formulation. DGPO therefore aims to directly optimize for these group-level preferences.

**Algorithm Steps (Online Setting):**
1. **Sample Conditioning and Generate Group:** For each training iteration, a condition (e.g., a text prompt `c`) is sampled. An *online model* (current `p_theta` or its EMA version `p_theta-`) generates a group of `G` samples (`x_0^1..., x_0^G`) using *efficient deterministic ODE-based rollouts*. This is a crucial departure from prior GRPO-style methods that use SDEs.
2. **Compute Advantages:** A reward function `r_phi(c, x_0)` evaluates the quality of each generated sample `x_0`. To determine relative preference, advantage scores `A(x_i_0)` are computed for each sample `x_i_0` within the group. This involves normalizing individual rewards by subtracting the group mean and dividing by the group standard deviation, similar to GRPO: `A_i = (r_i - mean({r_j})) / std({r_j})`.
3. **Partition into Positive and Negative Groups:** Based on the advantage scores, the group of samples `G` is partitioned into a positive group `G+` (samples with `A_i > 0`, i.e., better than average) and a negative group `G-` (samples with `A_i <= 0`, i.e., worse than or equal to average).
4. **Direct Group Preference Optimization Objective:**
 - The core objective is to maximize the likelihood of the positive group being preferred over the negative group, formulated using a Bradley-Terry-like model: `log sigma(R_theta(G+|c) - R_theta(G-|c))`.
 - The group-level reward `R_theta(G|c)` is parameterized as a *weighted sum* of individual sample rewards `r_theta(c, x_0)` within the group: `Sum_{x_0 in G} w(x_0) * r_theta(c, x_0)`.
 - The individual sample reward `r_theta(c, x_0)` is derived from the optimal density of the RLHF objective (similar to DPO), involving `log (p_theta(x_0:T|c) / p_ref(x_0:T|c))`. This term includes an intractable partition function `Z(c)`.
5. **Advantage-Based Weight Design:** To address the intractable `Z(c)` and leverage fine-grained preferences, a novel weighting strategy `w(x_0) = |A(x_0)|` is proposed. This design is crucial because:
 - It assigns larger weights to samples that deviate more from the average reward, enabling more effective learning of relative preferences.
 - Crucially, due to the zero-mean property of normalized advantages, the sum of weights for `G+` equals the sum of weights for `G-` (`Sum_{x_0 in G+} w(x_0) = Sum_{x_0 in G-} w(x_0)`). This property mathematically cancels out the intractable `Z(c)` term in the objective function.
6. **Final DGPO Loss Function:** After the weighting strategy eliminates `Z(c)` and applying Jensen's inequality (to move expectation outside) and some simplification, the final loss function `L_DGPO(theta)` optimizes the difference in denoising score matching (DSM) losses between the current model `f_theta` and the reference model `f_ref` for positive and negative groups, weighted by their advantages. The calculation is performed for a randomly sampled timestep `t`.
7. **Timestep Clip Strategy:** To address potential degradation in visual quality when generating few-step samples for training (due to their poorer quality), DGPO introduces a "Timestep Clip Strategy." During training, timesteps `t` are sampled only from a range `[t_min, T]` (with `t_min > 0`), preventing the model from overfitting to low-quality artifacts in early timesteps.

**Key Advantages of DGPO's Approach:**
- **No Stochastic Policy Requirement:** Enables the use of efficient deterministic ODE samplers, leading to higher-quality rollouts for training.
- **Direct Group-Level Optimization:** Directly leverages fine-grained relative preferences without the complexities of policy gradients.
- **Efficient Convergence:** Optimization is guided by group preferences, avoiding inefficient exploration from model-agnostic Gaussian noise.
- **Reduced Computational Cost:** Avoids training on the entire sampling trajectory, significantly reducing cost per iteration.
- **Intractable Term Elimination:** The clever advantage-based weighting `w(x_0)` mathematically removes the intractable partition function `Z(c)`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate that Direct Group Preference Optimization (DGPO) significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in efficiency and performance across various diffusion model tasks.

**1. Superior Training Efficiency:**
- **Speedup:** DGPO achieves an astounding ~20 times faster training on average compared to Flow-GRPO, the prior state-of-the-art method. For the challenging GenEval benchmark, DGPO is nearly 30 times faster (Figure 1, Figure 3).
- **Reasons for Efficiency:** This speedup is attributed to several factors: the use of efficient deterministic ODE samplers for rollouts (instead of SDEs), the elimination of inefficient model-agnostic Gaussian noise for exploration, and avoiding training on the entire sampling trajectory.

**2. State-of-the-Art Performance on In-Domain Metrics:**
- **GenEval Benchmark (Compositional Image Generation):** DGPO significantly boosts the base SD3.5-M model's performance from 63% to an impressive 97% overall score. This surpasses Flow-GRPO (95%), GPT-4o (84%), and other leading autoregressive and diffusion models (Table 1). It shows strong performance across all sub-categories including object counting, spatial relations, and attribute binding.
- **Visual Text Rendering (OCR Accuracy):** DGPO achieves an OCR Accuracy of 0.96, outperforming Flow-GRPO (0.92) and the base SD3.5-M (0.59) (Table 2, Figure 3a).
- **Human Preference Alignment (PickScore):** DGPO achieves a PickScore of 23.89, outperforming Flow-GRPO (23.31) and the base SD3.5-M (21.72) (Table 2, Figure 3b).

**3. Robust Performance on Out-of-Domain Metrics:**
- **No Reward Hacking:** Despite optimizing for specific in-domain rewards, DGPO maintains or improves performance on independent out-of-domain image quality metrics not used during training (Aesthetic Score, DeQA, ImageReward, UnifiedReward) (Table 2). This indicates that DGPO is aligning the model with genuine quality improvements rather than merely overfitting to the training reward signal.

**4. Qualitative Improvements:**
- **Instruction Following & Visual Quality:** Qualitative comparisons show that DGPO-trained models not only follow instructions more accurately but also maintain a significantly stronger visual quality compared to Flow-GRPO, which often exhibits visual degradation (Figure 2, 6, 7). This highlights the benefit of using high-fidelity ODE samplers during training.

**5. Ablation Study Insights:**
- **Effectiveness of Timestep Clip Strategy:** Without the proposed timestep clip strategy, while OCR accuracy slightly decreases (0.96 to 0.95), the visual quality seriously degrades (Figure 4). This confirms the importance of clipping timesteps during training to mitigate artifacts from few-step generated samples.
- **ODE vs. SDE Rollout:** Using ODE rollouts (DGPO) significantly outperforms SDE rollouts (DGPO w/ SDE) in both convergence speed and ultimate metrics for visual text rendering (Figure 5b). This strongly supports the claim that SDE-based sampling in prior works was a limitation imposed by the policy-gradient framework, not a benefit for sample diversity or quality.
- **Online vs. Offline DGPO:** Online DGPO performs notably better than offline DGPO, emphasizing the benefits of continuous interaction and updates in the online setting (Figure 5a).
- **Comparison to Diffusion DPO:** DGPO notably outperforms Diffusion DPO in both online and offline settings (Figure 5b). This validates the effectiveness of DGPO's approach in leveraging fine-grained *group relative information*, which Diffusion DPO cannot fully exploit due to its pairwise sample restriction.

In summary, DGPO establishes a new benchmark for efficiently and effectively aligning diffusion models, demonstrating superior speed, quantitative performance across diverse tasks, and improved qualitative output quality, all while avoiding the pitfalls of prior approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Direct Group Preference Optimization (DGPO) represents a significant advancement in the field of generative AI, particularly for diffusion models. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

1. **Democratizing High-Quality Diffusion Model Alignment:** By drastically reducing training time (20-30x faster) and computational costs, DGPO makes it much more feasible for a wider range of researchers and practitioners to fine-tune and align diffusion models with specific preferences or tasks. This could accelerate innovation in text-to-image synthesis and related generative applications.

2. **Unlocking Full Potential of RL for Diffusion Models:** DGPO resolves a long-standing architectural mismatch between traditional policy-gradient RL methods and the efficient sampling mechanisms of diffusion models. By removing the reliance on inefficient stochastic policies and enabling the use of high-fidelity ODE samplers, DGPO paves the way for more effective and direct application of RL techniques to diffusion models. This could lead to the development of even more sophisticated alignment strategies in the future.

3. **Enhanced Model Performance and Fidelity:** The demonstrated superior performance on complex tasks like compositional image generation (GenEval), visual text rendering, and human preference alignment, coupled with improved visual quality, signifies that DGPO can produce more useful and aesthetically pleasing generative AI outputs. This has direct implications for applications in creative industries, content generation, and specialized visual tasks.

4. **Robustness Against "Reward Hacking":** The consistent performance on out-of-domain metrics, even after optimizing for specific in-domain rewards, suggests that DGPO encourages genuine improvements in model quality rather than simply overfitting to the reward function. This robustness is critical for deploying aligned models in real-world scenarios where unseen prompts and preferences are common.

5. **A New Paradigm for Preference Optimization:** DGPO’s ability to leverage fine-grained group relative preference information without the complexities of policy gradients or the limitations of pairwise comparisons (as in DPO) introduces a powerful new paradigm for preference-based learning. This approach could inspire similar innovations in other generative models or even multi-modal systems, where group comparisons might offer richer signals than individual or pairwise judgments.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies its focus on text-to-image synthesis but notes the potential for adaptation to text-to-video synthesis. This suggests DGPO could serve as a foundational method for aligning more complex and resource-intensive generative models, further extending its impact across the broader generative AI landscape. The elegance of its mathematical formulation, particularly the advantage-based weighting for canceling intractable terms, is a novel contribution that could be adopted or extended in other preference learning contexts.

In conclusion, DGPO is not merely an incremental improvement; it represents a significant methodological leap that addresses core efficiency and performance challenges in aligning diffusion models. Its impact will likely be seen in faster development cycles, higher-quality generative outputs, and a more robust framework for human-centric AI alignment.
