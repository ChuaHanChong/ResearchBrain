# LatentUM: Unleashing the Potential of Interleaved Cross-Modal Reasoning via a Latent-Space Unified Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Tsinghua University, and UCSD developed LatentUM, a unified model that integrates visual understanding and generation within a shared semantic latent space, eliminating pixel-space mediation. This approach enhances interleaved cross-modal reasoning for tasks like visual spatial planning and action-conditioned world modeling, achieving state-of-the-art performance on relevant benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing unified models (UMs) rely on distinct visual representations for understanding and generation, necessitating inefficient and error-prone pixel-space mediation.
- The pixel decoding and re-encoding steps in UMs introduce 'codec bias' and 'cross-modal misalignment,' hindering complex reasoning tasks.
- Current UMs often prioritize pixel-level fidelity over semantic correctness, which is less optimal for reasoning-centric tasks like visual planning and self-reflection.

## Method

- **LatentUM** employs ==Model Behavior Aligned Quantization (MBAQ)== to represent visual information as ==semantic tokens==, ensuring these tokens preserve understanding capabilities.
- A ==Mixture-of-Modal Experts (MoME)== autoregressive Transformer processes interleaved text and visual features within a shared ==self-attention== mechanism, allowing generated visual codes to be reasoned over directly.
- A ==decoupled pixel decoder== is optionally used for pixel-level visualization, ensuring the core model's latent space remains focused on semantics rather than pixel reconstruction.

## Results

- **LatentUM** achieved state-of-the-art on the Visual Spatial Planning (VSP) benchmark, with **0.99** average accuracy for fine-grained step-by-step planning, outperforming previous models like **ThinkMorph** (**0.76**).
- On the GenEval benchmark, **LatentUM** with self-reflection achieved a score of **0.92** (with external reward) and **0.87** (with self-reward), demonstrating enhanced instruction-following for visual generation.
- **LatentUM** demonstrated strong capabilities in action-conditioned world modeling, achieving an Average Trajectory Error (ATE) of **1.34** and Relative Pose Error (RPE) of **0.34** on the RECON dataset, showing competitive performance with specialized navigation models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying visual representations in a shared semantic latent space and eliminating pixel-space mediation allows for more fluid and efficient interleaved cross-modal reasoning.
> - Optimizing visual quantization to preserve the behavior of a pre-trained Vision-Language Model (MBAQ) effectively retains semantic information for understanding tasks.
> - Self-referential reasoning, where the model processes its own semantically-represented visual outputs, significantly improves instruction-following and generation quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of multimodal artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on Unified Models (UMs). UMs are designed to process and generate content across diverse modalities, such as text, images, and videos, within a single architectural framework.

Existing UMs have largely concentrated on visual generation tasks, including text-to-image synthesis, image editing, and video generation. While these applications are valuable, the paper identifies limitations in current UM approaches. One such limitation is that the performance of UMs on these generative tasks often does not match that of specialized, task-specific models. A more fundamental issue highlighted is the architectural dependency on "pixel decoding as a bridge" between visual understanding and generation. This necessity arises because current UMs typically employ disjoint visual representations for these two capabilities. This pixel-space mediation is described as both ineffective and inefficient, potentially introducing "codec bias" and "cross-modal misalignment," which can negatively affect the model's ability to follow complex instructions in reasoning-centric tasks.

The authors propose a shift in focus for UMs towards "interleaved cross-modal reasoning." This paradigm involves continuous interaction between different modalities, such as solving visually grounded planning problems through step-by-step visual reasoning, improving visual generation quality via self-reflection, or modeling the dynamics of the physical world based on sequential actions. For such reasoning tasks, the paper argues that semantic correctness is often more critical than pixel-level fidelity.

Related work in UMs includes early approaches that extend pretrained Large Language Models (LLMs) with discrete visual tokens (e.g., Chameleon, Emu3) and more recent efforts exploring improved training strategies and interleaved image-text generation. Research on visual representations in UMs varies, with some adopting separate encoders for understanding and generation, others pursuing unified tokenizers, and some exploring direct semantic features for generation. A growing body of work specifically investigates interleaved reasoning, often referred to as Visual Chain-of-Thought approaches (e.g., ThinkMorph, Uni-CoT, UniCorn). However, the paper points out that even these interleaved reasoning models frequently rely on pixel-space mediation.

Furthermore, the work connects to the domain of "world models," which aim to predict future environmental states conditioned on past observations and actions. Recent unified models like Emu3.5 and Transfusion-RAE have begun to explore this connection. LatentUM aligns with this direction by treating world modeling as an instance of cross-modal reasoning, predicting future visual states using semantic tokens within its shared latent space. This situates LatentUM as an effort to enhance UM capabilities by unifying visual representations and improving their efficacy in complex, multi-step reasoning and world modeling tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a identified limitation in existing unified models (UMs): their reliance on distinct visual representations for understanding and generation. This architectural choice necessitates a "pixel decoding" step to convert internal visual representations to pixel space for generation, followed by a re-encoding step to derive semantic features for subsequent understanding. This "pixel-space mediation" is considered a bottleneck that introduces several inefficiencies and performance limitations.

The key objectives of this work are detailed as follows:

1. **Eliminate Pixel-Space Mediation:** The central goal is to remove the requirement for pixel decoding and re-encoding between visual understanding and generation. This is motivated by the observation that this intermediary step is computationally inefficient and introduces unnecessary complexities in the reasoning process.
2. **Unify Modality Representations within a Shared Semantic Latent Space:** To address the disjoint representation issue, the authors aim to develop a model that represents all modalities, including both visual and textual information, within a single, coherent semantic latent space. This unified representation is intended to facilitate direct interaction and reasoning between modalities.
3. **Enable Flexible Interleaved Cross-Modal Reasoning:** By operating within a shared semantic latent space, the model is designed to naturally support interleaved cross-modal reasoning. This means the model should be able to reason over its own generated visual content (represented semantically) without needing to convert it to pixels first, thereby allowing for more fluid and integrated reasoning processes.
4. **Alleviate Codec Bias and Strengthen Cross-Modal Alignment:** The pixel-space mediation step is hypothesized to introduce "codec bias" and degrade "cross-modal alignment." A unified semantic latent space is intended to mitigate these issues, leading to a more consistent and robust alignment between visual and linguistic concepts.
5. **Prioritize Semantic Correctness for Reasoning-Centric Tasks:** For applications involving dense visual thinking, planning, and self-reflection, the semantic content and logical consistency of visual information are often more critical than photorealistic pixel-level fidelity. The model's design is therefore motivated to preserve and leverage understanding-oriented semantics.
6. **Demonstrate Enhanced Performance across Diverse Tasks:** The research aims to empirically validate the benefits of its proposed architecture by achieving competitive or state-of-the-art performance on specific benchmarks. These include:
 - **Visual Spatial Planning:** Solving complex visually grounded planning problems via step-by-step reasoning.
 - **Visual Generation with Self-Reflection:** Improving the quality and instruction-following ability of visual generation by allowing the model to reflect on its own outputs.
 - **World Modeling:** Supporting the prediction of future visual states and environment dynamics conditioned on actions.

In summary, the motivation is to overcome the limitations of current unified models by creating a more integrated, efficient, and semantically aligned architecture that can unlock more sophisticated cross-modal reasoning capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LatentUM is designed to integrate visual understanding and generation within a shared semantic latent space. The methodology revolves around three core components: a visual tokenizer utilizing model behavior aligned quantization (MBAQ), a Mixture-of-Modal Experts (MoME) autoregressive Transformer, and an optional decoupled pixel decoder.

#### 3.1. Visual Tokenizer via Model Behavior Aligned Quantization (MBAQ)

The foundational aspect of LatentUM is its approach to visual representation. Rather than relying on pixel-reconstruction-oriented features, LatentUM aims to generate understanding-oriented features.
- **Semantic Features:** Visual information is represented as semantic tokens, aligning with language through contrastive pretraining, specifically building upon CLIP features.
- **Discretization:** To enable unified autoregressive modeling with language tokens, continuous CLIP features are discretized. Multi-codebook quantization (MCQ) is employed, where each high-dimensional visual token is split into multiple chunks, and each chunk is independently quantized using separate codebooks. This yields a large effective vocabulary from a smaller number of codebook entries.
- **MBAQ Objective:** The quantization process is not optimized for pixel reconstruction. Instead, MBAQ trains the quantizer `Q_ϕ` to preserve the visual understanding capabilities of the original CLIP features. This is achieved by incorporating a pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) `p_ψ`. The training objective minimizes the KL divergence between the VLM's output distributions when fed original CLIP features versus quantized features (`Q_ϕ(V)`) on a Visual Question Answering (VQA) dataset. This ensures that the de-quantized features (`˜V`) retain semantic meaning for understanding tasks.

#### 3.2. Mixture-of-Modal Experts (MoME)

LatentUM's core autoregressive model is a Transformer structured with a Mixture-of-Modal Experts (MoME) architecture to mitigate conflicting optimization signals between understanding and generation tasks.
- **Parallel Branches:** Within each Transformer layer, two parallel branches are maintained:
 - The **understanding branch (`ψ`)** processes interleaved text and visual features for comprehension tasks.
 - The **generation branch (`θ`)** is dedicated to generating discrete visual tokens.
- **Shared Self-Attention:** Both branches share a common self-attention mechanism, allowing for cross-modal information flow. However, they maintain separate feed-forward networks and projection matrices.
- **Dynamic Routing:** A token routing mechanism, cued by special tokens like `<BOI>`, directs input tokens to the appropriate branch. This enables the model to seamlessly switch between understanding and generation modes.
- **Self-Referential Reasoning:** Crucially, generated visual codes are de-quantized and re-cached within the same context, allowing the model to directly reason over its own outputs without requiring an intermediate pixel decoding step.
- **Initialization:** The understanding branch `ψ` is initialized from a strong off-the-shelf VLM (InternVL3.5-4B), preserving its original visual understanding capabilities, while only the generation branch `θ` is actively trained initially.

#### 3.3. Vision Head

A lightweight causal Transformer serves as the prediction head. It predicts the code indices within each visual token sequentially, capturing internal dependencies and facilitating the Multi-Code Next-Token Prediction (MC-NTP) objective.

#### 3.4. Decoupled Pixel Decoder

For tasks requiring pixel-level visualization (e.g., text-to-image generation), a separate diffusion decoder (`ϵ_η`) is employed.
- **Independent Training:** This decoder is trained independently of the main LatentUM model. It adapts a pre-trained Diffusion Transformer (DiT) by replacing its text conditioning with the quantized visual features (`˜V`) from LatentUM.
- **Conditional Denoising:** The decoder learns to perform conditional denoising, with `˜V` guiding the pixel generation process.
- **Semantic Focus:** The core LatentUM model is never optimized for pixel reconstruction; the decoder is an optional component. This ensures the latent space remains focused on semantics rather than pixel fidelity.

#### 3.5. Pre-training Procedure

- The MBAQ quantizer is trained on the LLaVA-v1.5-665K dataset using the CLIP encoder from InternVL3.5-4B.
- LatentUM\_Base is initialized with InternVL3.5-4B weights for the `ψ` branch, and the `θ` branch is trained on 32M image-text pairs from BLIP3o using the MC-NTP objective.
- The decoupled diffusion decoder is trained separately on the same 32M images.

#### 3.6. Post-training for Reasoning Capabilities

To activate and enhance interleaved cross-modal reasoning, LatentUM undergoes task-specific post-training:
- **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** Both `ψ` and `θ` branches are jointly fine-tuned on interleaved multimodal datasets. The objective is next-token prediction, where generated visual tokens are re-processed by `ψ` to inform subsequent reasoning. A specialized attention mask allows for single-pass training of multi-step visual reasoning.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is applied to improve generation quality. Rewards can be external or derived from a "generate-then-reflect" mechanism where LatentUM's understanding branch `ψ` evaluates its own generated `˜V` via verification questions, generating a self-reward signal.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of LatentUM encompassed its base capabilities in visual understanding and generation, as well as its advanced cross-modal reasoning performance.

#### 4.1. Base Model Capabilities

- **Visual Understanding:** LatentUM\_Base demonstrated competitive performance across several multimodal understanding benchmarks (MME, POPE, SEED-Bench, MMBench, MMMU). Notably, when operating on quantized visual features (LatentUM\_Base†), the performance degradation was minimal across most benchmarks, and in some cases, it surpassed baselines using continuous features. This indicates that the Model Behavior Aligned Quantization (MBAQ) effectively preserves semantic information relevant for understanding.
- **Visual Generation:** Qualitatively, LatentUM\_Base generated semantically coherent images from diverse prompts, including an emergent capability for legible in-image text rendering, attributed to the shared semantic space between visual and language tokens. Quantitatively, on the GenEval benchmark (measuring instruction-following for compositional prompts), LatentUM\_Base achieved an overall score of 0.85, outperforming all other unified models evaluated, despite being trained on a comparatively smaller dataset. This result suggests that semantically aligned visual features enhance instruction-following in visual generation.

#### 4.2. Cross-Modal Reasoning

- **Visual Generation with Self-Reflection:** Through post-training with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) using self-derived rewards, LatentUM\_Vis-Gen achieved a GenEval score of 0.87, surpassing all unified model baselines. When augmented with an external pixel-reward model, its GenEval score further increased to 0.92, establishing a new state-of-the-art among unified models. On GenEval2, which employs a more robust MLLM-as-judge evaluation, LatentUM\_Vis-Gen (using self-reward) significantly outperformed all baselines (TIFA\_GM 31.3, TIFA\_AM 72.9), indicating that self-derived rewards can drive substantial improvements in generation quality.
- **Interleaved Cross-Modal Reasoning for Visual Spatial Planning (VSP):** LatentUM\_Vis-Plan consistently outperformed all prior visual reasoning models on the VSP benchmark.
 - **Coarse-grained planning:** Where the model generates a complete visual plan before deriving a solution, LatentUM\_Vis-Plan achieved an average accuracy of 0.85 (e.g., 1.00 on Level 3, 0.71 on Level 6).
 - **Fine-grained step-by-step planning:** Where the model alternates between textual actions and updated visual states, LatentUM\_Vis-Plan achieved an average accuracy of 0.99 (e.g., 1.00 on Levels 3-5, 0.97 on Level 6). This substantial improvement in the fine-grained setting, particularly over models like ThinkMorph (0.76 average), validates the core hypothesis that unified semantic representations facilitate more effective, deeper interleaved reasoning without pixel-space mediation.

#### 4.3. World Modeling

LatentUM demonstrated its applicability to action-conditioned world modeling for visual navigation. Fine-tuned as LatentUM\_WM on the RECON dataset, it performed latent semantic prediction.
- **Open-loop prediction:** The model successfully rolled out temporally consistent future visual states guided by predefined action trajectories, preserving the spatial layout of the initial scene.
- **Zero-shot language-conditioned navigation:** By leveraging inherited language-vision alignment, LatentUM\_WM translated free-form textual instructions (e.g., "get closer to the left house") into accurate future visual states, illustrating grounded environment dynamics.
- **NWM benchmark:** Quantitatively, LatentUM\_WM achieved an Average Trajectory Error (ATE) of 1.34 and a Relative Pose Error (RPE) of 0.34, surpassing Transfusion-RAE and remaining competitive with specialized Navigation World Model (NWM) baselines.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Semantic Features vs. Pixel-Reconstruction Features:** A comparison with a VQVAE baseline, which uses pixel-reconstruction features, showed that LatentUM's semantically aligned features significantly outperformed the VQVAE baseline on both GenEval and GenEval2. This supports the claim that semantic alignment is crucial for instruction-following in visual generation.
- **MBAQ for Visual Understanding:** MBAQ consistently outperformed alternative quantizers trained with direct MSE or cosine similarity loss for feature reconstruction across five visual understanding benchmarks. This indicates that optimizing for VLM prediction behavior during quantization is more effective than direct feature reconstruction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LatentUM presents a methodological advancement in the design of unified models by proposing a shared semantic latent space for all modalities. This approach directly addresses the inefficiencies and biases associated with pixel-space mediation between visual understanding and generation, which is a common architectural pattern in existing multimodal systems.

The significance of this work can be understood through several aspects:

1. **Fundamental Architectural Innovation:** By unifying visual representations in a shared semantic latent space and eliminating pixel-level decoding for internal reasoning, LatentUM offers a more streamlined and computationally efficient framework for multimodal processing. This conceptual shift provides a foundation for developing more integrated and coherent AI systems.

2. **Enhanced Cross-Modal Reasoning Capabilities:** The demonstrated performance on complex tasks like Visual Spatial Planning, particularly the significant gains with fine-grained interleaved reasoning, indicates that direct reasoning over semantic visual tokens is more effective than methods relying on pixel re-rendering. This capability is crucial for developing AI agents that can perform sophisticated, multi-step problem-solving requiring dynamic interaction between language and visual states.

3. **Improved Visual Generation and Instruction Following:** The research demonstrates that semantically aligned visual features, coupled with a self-reflection mechanism via reinforcement learning, lead to higher-quality visual generations that adhere more closely to complex, compositional instructions. This has implications for content creation tools and systems requiring precise control over generated visual outputs based on textual commands.

4. **Advancements in World Modeling and Embodied AI:** LatentUM's successful application to action-conditioned world modeling, including zero-shot language-conditioned navigation, suggests its potential for embodied AI and robotics. The ability to predict future visual states as semantic tokens, and ground environment dynamics to natural language, is a critical step towards developing intelligent agents that can understand, navigate, and interact with the physical world.

5. **Reduced Codec Bias and Stronger Alignment:** The design of LatentUM, specifically through Model Behavior Aligned Quantization (MBAQ), is shown to alleviate codec bias and strengthen cross-modal alignment. This contributes to the robustness and reliability of multimodal AI systems, as discrepancies between visual and linguistic interpretations are reduced.

6. **Guidance for Future Research:** The identified limitations, such as fixed-resolution generation, modest pretraining scale, and reliance on pixel-space recurrent interfaces for current world modeling, point to promising directions for future work. Scaling the model, extending to variable resolutions, improving temporal consistency, and developing fully latent world-modeling pipelines could further unlock the potential of this paradigm.

Overall, LatentUM contributes to the development of more capable and efficient unified multimodal models, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in interleaved cross-modal reasoning and paving the way for more sophisticated multimodal AI applications. The provision of code further supports reproducibility and encourages continued research in this area.
