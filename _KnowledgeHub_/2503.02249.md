# Natural Selection via Foundation Models for Soft Robot Evolution

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Michigan-Ann Arbor introduce RoboCrafter-QA, a multimodal benchmark designed to assess and enhance large language models' (LLMs) capabilities in soft robot design. They demonstrate that finetuning an LLM allows it to achieve 94.01% accuracy in selecting optimal robot morphologies and to directly generate designs with over 10 times higher average reward compared to generalist LLMs, with these simulated improvements verified through real-world robot experiments.

## Problem

- Designing soft robots is inherently complex due to their infinite degrees of freedom, non-linear material properties, and the intricate coordination required between morphology, actuation, and control, traditionally relying on time-consuming expert intuition and iterative prototyping.
- A quantitative benchmark was missing to evaluate large language models' (LLMs) "embodied design intelligence," specifically their ability to reason about the interplay between high-level task descriptions and low-level physical robot designs.
- General-purpose LLMs have demonstrated limitations in robotics-related physical reasoning, struggling to bridge the gap between abstract task understanding and concrete morphological choices for embodied systems.

## Method

- ==Developed RoboCrafter-QA==, a novel multimodal question-answering benchmark comprising 36,000 question-answer pairs across 12 distinct soft robot tasks, designed to evaluate LLMs' ability to select superior robot morphologies.
- Finetuned an efficient, open-source LLM (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct) using parameter-efficient Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) on the RoboCrafter-QA dataset to significantly enhance its performance in soft robot design selection and direct generation.
- Established a physical pipeline for fabricating modular soft robots and conducted real-world experiments to validate the sim-to-real correlation of LLM-selected and generated designs, confirming their practical utility.

## Results

- The finetuned Qwen-0.5B-Instruct model achieved an average accuracy of **94.01%** on easy tasks and **88.31%** on hard tasks within the RoboCrafter-QA benchmark, far surpassing generalist LLMs like Grok-4 (**59.72%** Easy) and Gemini-**2.5**-Pro (~**52%** Easy).
- The finetuned LLM demonstrated superior generative capabilities, producing robot designs that achieved a mean average reward of **1.48** in simulation, which was over 10 times higher than the next best general-purpose LLM baseline.
- Real-world experiments with fabricated modular soft robots confirmed a strong positive sim-to-real correlation, as designs with higher simulated rewards consistently exhibited faster forward speeds in physical tests for tasks like Walker-v0 and Pusher-v0.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - While general-purpose multimodal LLMs show limited accuracy (e.g., GPT-5 at ~51%) and task-dependent biases in soft robot design selection, targeted parameter-efficient finetuning can enable a compact LLM to achieve state-of-the-art performance.
> - Contextual cues in prompts are critical for larger LLMs to reason effectively, whereas smaller models with limited capacity may benefit from prompt simplification, and logical negation (e.g., selecting suboptimal designs) remains a challenge across all evaluated models.
> - The finetuned LLM not only excels at design selection but also demonstrates strong generative capabilities, producing novel, high-performing soft robot morphologies directly from task descriptions, which can significantly accelerate the design process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The design of soft robots is a multidisciplinary challenge at the intersection of materials science, mechanics, and control. These robots offer advantages such as compliance and adaptability in unstructured environments but present complexities due to their infinite degrees of freedom, non-linear material properties, and the intricate coordination required between morphology, actuation, and control. Traditional design processes rely on expert intuition, iterative prototyping, and computationally intensive simulations.

Previous research in soft modular robot design and optimization has explored reconfigurability and employed evolutionary algorithms for automatic design. More recently, generative AI, including diffusion models and hybrid data-driven/physics-based methods, has been applied to automated morphology discovery.

Concurrently, Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated advanced reasoning capabilities across various domains, primarily through natural language understanding and generation. The potential of LLMs to act as "intelligent co-designers" in robotics has been theorized, suggesting a paradigm shift from human-driven engineering to AI-empowered selection in robot design. However, a comprehensive and quantitative analysis of LLMs for embodied robot design, specifically bridging high-level task descriptions with low-level morphological choices, has remained largely unexplored.

Existing LLM benchmarks primarily focus on general language, code, or non-embodied visual reasoning. These benchmarks do not adequately assess the "embodied design intelligence" required to reason about the complex interplay between a robot's physical morphology, its environment, and a specified task. LLMs have previously shown limitations in robotics-related physical reasoning, underscoring the need for specialized evaluations that integrate high-level planning with real-world physical constraints.

This work addresses this gap by introducing a benchmark specifically designed to evaluate LLMs' ability to reason about soft robot designs, thus contributing a structured method for assessing embodied intelligence in this context. It then demonstrates that a targeted finetuning approach can enhance LLM capabilities for both selecting and directly generating high-performing soft robot morphologies, and validates these simulated results with physical robot experiments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research is the inherent complexity and iterative nature of designing soft robots, which traditionally demands extensive interdisciplinary expertise and experimental validation. The authors identify a gap in the application of foundation models, particularly LLMs, to embodied design tasks, despite their demonstrated reasoning abilities in other domains. The central research question driving this work is: "Can LLMs leverage their latent understanding for the design of embodied soft robots, bridging the gap between high-level task descriptions and low-level morphological choices?"

To address this question, the paper establishes several key objectives:

1. **Develop a Benchmark for Embodied Design Intelligence:** To quantitatively assess LLMs' capabilities in soft robot design, the first objective was to create RoboCrafter-QA, a novel multimodal question-answering benchmark. This benchmark aims to evaluate how LLMs reason about the interplay between task requirements and soft robot morphology by presenting them with design challenges in a simulated environment.
2. **Evaluate State-of-the-Art Multimodal LLMs:** A key objective was to conduct a comprehensive evaluation of current state-of-the-art multimodal LLMs on the RoboCrafter-QA benchmark. This evaluation was intended to reveal the existing capabilities and limitations of these models in the context of embodied design reasoning, particularly concerning fine-grained distinctions between robot designs.
3. **Enhance LLM Performance in Design Selection and Generation:** Recognizing potential limitations in generalist LLMs, an objective was to finetune an efficient, open-source LLM specifically for soft robot design tasks using the generated benchmark data. This aimed to achieve state-of-the-art performance in design selection and explore the finetuned model's capacity for direct generation of novel, high-performing robot morphologies.
4. **Validate Sim-to-Real Correlation:** To confirm the practical utility and relevance of the benchmark and the LLM-driven design process, a final objective was to construct physical replicas of the modular soft robots and conduct real-world experiments. This validation aimed to demonstrate that superior performance observed in the simulation benchmark translates to effective real-world design selection and performance.

Through these objectives, the research aims to demonstrate that LLMs can transition from general linguistic tasks to specialized, embodied design challenges, potentially revolutionizing the robot design process by acting as intelligent co-designers.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers around the development and application of the RoboCrafter-QA benchmark, followed by LLM evaluation, finetuning, and real-world validation.

**A. RoboCrafter-QA Benchmark Development:**
1. **Overview:** RoboCrafter-QA is a multimodal question-answering benchmark comprising 36,000 question-answer (QA) pairs, with 3,000 questions for each of 12 distinct tasks curated from the EvoGym simulator. Each question presents a textual prompt describing a task and environment, along with two candidate soft robot designs represented as 5x5 voxel-based material matrices. The ground truth indicates the design with higher simulated performance. Questions are categorized into "Easy" and "Hard" based on the performance reward difference between candidate designs.
2. **Data Generation:**
 - **Design Space:** Robot morphologies are defined within a 5x5 voxel grid, where each voxel can be one of five material types: empty, rigid, soft, horizontal actuator, or vertical actuator. Structural integrity ensures fully connected components.
 - **Evolutionary Process:** An evolutionary algorithm is used to generate diverse robot designs. Starting with an initial population of 30 random designs, robots are evaluated in EvoGym environments. A Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)-trained controller optimizes each robot's actuation to maximize task-specific performance (reward). The top 50% of robots are retained, and the remainder are replaced by mutation-based offspring with low probability (10%), maintaining uniqueness. This process continues until 3,000 robots are evaluated per task.
3. **Question Generation:** Robot records (morphology and performance) are sorted by reward. Automated pairing constructs questions where LLMs must identify the better-performing robot. Each question includes environment-specific objectives and detailed descriptions of the 5x5 voxel grid representation.

**B. LLM Evaluation Metrics:**
1. **Accuracy (Acc):** Measures the proportion of correct predictions (model output matches ground truth) out of N test queries.
2. **Consistency (Cons):** Assesses the reproducibility of responses. Each query is presented three times, and consistency is calculated as the average proportion of matching responses across trials.

**C. Benchmarking State-of-the-Art LLMs:**
The study evaluated several advanced LLMs, including GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Grok-4, and Qwen-0.5-Instruct, on the RoboCrafter-QA benchmark. A simple actuator-count heuristic served as a baseline. Performance was analyzed across easy and hard tasks within locomotion, object manipulation, and climbing/balancing categories.

**D. Ablation Study on Prompt Design:**
An ablation study was conducted to understand the impact of different components of the textual prompt on LLM reasoning. Settings included removing actuator specifications ("NoAct"), environment descriptions ("NoEnv"), and a "Worse" setting where models were instructed to select the suboptimal design, to test robustness in logical reversal.

**E. Finetuning LLM for Robot Design Selection:**
1. **Model Selection:** Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, an efficient open-source LLM, was selected for finetuning.
2. **Finetuning Strategy:** A parameter-efficient Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) strategy was employed. LoRA adapters were injected into key layers (attention and feed-forward projections) of the transformer block, while other model parameters remained frozen.
3. **Training Data:** All 36,000 QA pairs from the benchmark were used. Data was formatted in the Qwen chat style, including a system prompt, a user query (environment, task, and structure description), and an assistant response indicating the correct answer.
4. **Optimization:** The model was optimized using a causal language modeling objective, predicting the assistant’s next token. Gradients were propagated only through the LoRA components.

**F. LLM for Direct Robot Design (Generative Capability):**
After finetuning, the model's ability to directly generate soft-robot structures was evaluated. It was prompted to design robot morphologies, constrained to a maximum of five actuators for manufacturability, for three selected environments: Walker-v0, Pusher-v0, and Carrier-v0. The generated designs were then evaluated in simulation to determine their rewards.

**G. Real-world Robot Design Verification (Sim-to-Real):**
1. **Fabrication:** A physical pipeline was established to fabricate voxel-based soft robots mirroring the 5x5 grid. Components (soft, rigid, horizontal, vertical actuator, void) were cast using Ecoflex™ 00-50 for soft parts and Dragon Skin™ 30 for rigid parts. Pneumatic actuators included embedded strings for controlled expansion.
2. **Modular Assembly:** Small magnets integrated at the vertices of modules enabled rapid, reconfigurable assembly.
3. **Actuation and Control:** Physical robots were driven by a pneumatic actuation system with three-way valves managed by a PWM-based controller, allowing for gait and movement control.
4. **Sim-to-Real Alignment:** Engineering optimizations, such as adding foot-like structures with anisotropic friction and using a specific testing surface, were implemented to align real-world conditions with simulation parameters (e.g., coefficient of friction).
5. **Experiments:** Selected robot designs (including those with higher and lower simulated rewards) generated by the finetuned LLM were fabricated and tested in real-world environments for Walker-v0, Pusher-v0, and Carrier-v0 tasks. Real-world performance was measured by forward speed (mm/s).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings across LLM evaluation, finetuning, generative design, and real-world validation:

**A. Benchmarking Results of Robot Design Selection (Table II):**
- **Simple Heuristics Insufficient:** A baseline heuristic (actuator-count) achieved an average accuracy of 47.07% on easy tasks and 50.67% on hard tasks, indicating that basic metrics are inadequate for complex design evaluation.
- **Performance Hierarchy:** Grok-4 demonstrated the strongest reasoning capabilities among generalist LLMs, with average accuracies of 59.72% (Easy) and 55.90% (Hard). Gemini-2.5-Pro offered a balanced performance (approx. 52% accuracy, approx. 71% consistency). GPT-5 achieved high consistency (77.19% Easy, 77.42% Hard) but its accuracy (approx. 51%) was comparable to or worse than the heuristic, suggesting consistent selection of suboptimal designs rather than effective reasoning. Qwen-0.5-Instruct performed lowest in both metrics.
- **Task-Dependent Biases:** LLM performance varied significantly by task. For instance, the Jumper-v0 task was particularly challenging for GPT-5 (9.84% accuracy) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (16.84% accuracy), despite high consistency, indicating consistent misinterpretation of impulsive dynamics. Conversely, models showed stronger performance (65-69% accuracy) on tasks like Carrier-v0 and UpStepper-v0, suggesting better reasoning for structured locomotion and balance.

**B. Ablation Study on Minor Prompt Design (Table III):**
- **Contextual Cues are Critical for Larger LLMs:** For GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro, and Grok-4, the full prompt with complete task and environment cues consistently resulted in the highest accuracy (approx. 51-60%). This indicates that these larger models leverage contextual information for effective reasoning.
- **Model Capacity and Prompt Simplification:** The smaller Qwen-0.5B exhibited an inverse trend, performing better when actuator or environment details were removed (e.g., "NoAct" at 53.31% vs. "Full" at 37.61%). This suggests that smaller models with limited capacity might overfit to surface correlations or become confused by overly detailed prompts, benefiting from simplification.
- **Robustness to Logical Reversal:** The "Worse" setting (instructing selection of the suboptimal design) consistently degraded performance across all models, highlighting that logical negation and reasoning reversal remain challenging, even for larger LLMs, which tend to maintain a bias towards selecting optimal designs.

**C. Finetuning LLM for Robot Design Selection (Table IV):**
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The finetuned Qwen-0.5B-Instruct model achieved significantly enhanced performance, reaching an average accuracy of 94.01% on easy tasks and 88.31% on hard tasks across all 12 environments.
- **High Consistency:** This was complemented by strong average consistency metrics of 90.64% (Easy) and 84.50% (Hard).
- **Near-Perfect Performance:** The finetuned model achieved 100% accuracy and consistency on easy tasks for environments such as BridgeWalker-v0 and Pusher-v0. These results indicate that parameter-efficient finetuning effectively enables even a compact LLM to comprehend and accurately evaluate complex soft robot designs.

**D. LLM for Direct Robot Design (Table V):**
- **Superior Generative Capability:** The finetuned Qwen-0.5B model ("Ours") demonstrated superior generative capabilities compared to general-purpose LLMs (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Grok-4). It achieved the highest average and best rewards across Walker-v0, Pusher-v0, and Carrier-v0 environments.
- **Significant Outperformance:** The finetuned model obtained a mean average reward of 1.48, which was over 10 times higher than the next best baseline, and a mean best reward of 3.02. This indicates that instruction-level finetuning not only improves design selection but also enables the generation of high-performing robot morphologies directly.

**E. Real-world Robot Design Verification (Table VI & Fig. 7):**
- **Strong Sim-to-Real Correlation:** Real-world experiments using fabricated modular soft robots confirmed a strong positive correlation between simulated performance and physical reality. Designs that achieved higher rewards in simulation consistently demonstrated faster forward speeds in real-world tests for Walker-v0, Pusher-v0, and Carrier-v0 tasks.
- **Validation of Design Principles:** This physical validation confirms that the design principles learned and selected/generated by the finetuned LLM in simulation translate effectively to real-world performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for leveraging large language models in the embodied design of soft robots, addressing a significant challenge in robotics. The key significance and potential impacts include:

1. **Bridging High-Level Intent and Low-Level Design:** The work demonstrates that LLMs can be trained to effectively bridge the gap between high-level task descriptions (e.g., "walk as far as possible") and low-level morphological and material choices for soft robots. This capability can automate parts of the design process that traditionally rely on human intuition and extensive experimentation.
2. **Introduction of a Standardized Benchmark:** RoboCrafter-QA provides a novel, comprehensive, and quantitative benchmark for evaluating embodied design intelligence in LLMs. This benchmark can serve as a critical tool for future research, enabling standardized assessment and comparison of different models and training methodologies in the domain of robot design.
3. **Demonstration of LLM Finetuning Effectiveness:** The study conclusively shows that while generalist LLMs may struggle with nuanced physical reasoning, targeted parameter-efficient finetuning can unlock and significantly enhance this capability. The achievement of state-of-the-art performance by a compact, open-source LLM (Qwen-0.5B-Instruct) highlights the potential for democratizing advanced robot design tools.
4. **Generative AI for Robot Morphology:** Beyond selection, the finetuned LLM's ability to directly generate novel, high-performing robot morphologies represents a significant step towards autonomous robot design. This could accelerate the prototyping and discovery of new robot designs, potentially leading to morphologies previously unforeseen by human designers.
5. **Validation of Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful fabrication and real-world testing of LLM-generated designs, demonstrating a strong sim-to-real correlation, is crucial for establishing the practical utility of this approach. It provides confidence that advancements in simulation-based LLM design can translate into tangible benefits for physical robot development.
6. **Towards AI as a "Natural Selector":** The conceptualization of LLMs acting as a "natural selector" for robot design suggests a paradigm shift in engineering. This work lays the groundwork for AI systems that can intelligently guide the evolution of robot designs, potentially leading to faster innovation cycles and more adaptable robots.
7. **Open-Sourcing for Community Advancement:** The commitment to open-sourcing the full system (benchmark, code, and finetuned models) aims to foster further research and collaboration in this emerging field, enabling other researchers to build upon these foundations.

In summary, this research advances the field by demonstrating the feasibility and effectiveness of integrating LLMs into the soft robot design pipeline, from conceptualization and selection to direct generation and real-world validation. This approach has the potential to accelerate the development of complex robotic systems and redefine the role of AI in engineering design.
