# Towards Hierarchical Multi-Step Reward Models for Enhanced Reasoning in Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Hierarchical Reward Models (HRM) and Hierarchical Node Compression (HNC) were developed to enhance the reliability and scalability of reward models for multi-step mathematical reasoning in LLMs. HRM combats reward hacking by evaluating reasoning at both individual and consecutive multi-step levels, leading to more stable and generalizable reasoning, while HNC efficiently augments training data for improved robustness.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) frequently struggle with detecting and correcting intermediate errors in complex multi-step reasoning, leading to error propagation.
- Outcome Reward Models (ORMs) suffer from delayed feedback, credit assignment issues, and susceptibility to spurious reasoning.
- Process Reward Models (PRMs) are vulnerable to reward hacking and incur high computational and human annotation costs for training data generation.

## Method

- Introduced ==Hierarchical Reward Model (HRM)== that evaluates reasoning at both ==fine-grained (individual steps)== and coarse-grained (merged consecutive steps) levels, promoting coherence and self-correction.
- Developed ==Hierarchical Node Compression (HNC)==, a data augmentation strategy that merges consecutive nodes in MCTS-generated trees to enhance data diversity and robustness with minimal computational overhead.
- Applied a ==self-training strategy== for policy models, fine-tuning them on high-quality reasoning data identified by HRM and regularized with ==KL divergence== to maintain generalization capabilities.

## Results

- HRM achieved **80%** accuracy on the PRM800K test set, demonstrating stable performance and consistently outperforming traditional ORM and PRM which showed fluctuations.
- HRM, when trained with HNC-augmented data, consistently showed greater robustness and stability across various policy models, with HNC requiring only 30 minutes of CPU for augmentation.
- Policy models fine-tuned using HRM-identified high-quality reasoning data achieved higher maximum accuracy (e.g., **80%** vs. **74.4%** for PRM-trained models on PRM800K) and improved robustness.
- HRM demonstrated superior generalization, achieving **74.2%** accuracy on the out-of-domain MATH500 dataset compared to PRM's **65.6%** at N=64.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Evaluating reasoning hierarchically, by considering both individual steps and multi-step coherence, effectively combats reward hacking and fosters more reliable LLM reasoning.
> - An efficient data augmentation method like HNC can significantly improve the diversity and robustness of autonomously generated training data for reward models without substantial computational cost.
> - Careful regularization with KL divergence is critical during policy model self-training to prevent catastrophic forgetting of general capabilities when fine-tuned on task-specific, high-quality data.
