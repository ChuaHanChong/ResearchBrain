# Adaptive Model-Base Control of Quadrupeds via Online System Identification using Kalman Filter

> [!summary] Summary
> A Kalman filter-based approach is introduced for online identification of a quadruped robot's total mass and center of mass, integrated into an adaptive model-based control architecture. This method enables the robot to maintain consistent base height and orientation tracking performance with varying or unknown payloads, exhibiting improved robustness over nominal and RLS-based controllers.

## Problem

- Model-based control for legged robots relies on fixed plant models, limiting their adaptability to variable or unknown payloads in dynamic environments.
- Existing online system identification methods, such as Recursive Least Squares (RLS), are susceptible to measurement noise and biases, often requiring extensive, ad-hoc heuristics for stable estimation.
- Many adaptive control approaches lack a direct, quantitative measure of the reliability of their parameter estimates, hindering informed decisions about when to apply model updates.

## Method

- A Kalman filter (KF) formulation is developed for online identification of the total mass and the x and y components of the center of mass (COM) of a quadruped's floating base.
- The KF is integrated into an adaptive model-based control architecture comprising a heuristic-based gait sequencer, ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC), and Whole-Body Control (WBC).
- Parameter updates from the KF to the controller are gated by thresholds applied to the estimation covariance matrix, ensuring that only sufficiently reliable estimates are used to adapt the robot's dynamic model.

## Results

- The KF-based adaptive controller significantly reduced base height tracking error by up to **61%** (e.g., from **4.92** cm to **1.89** cm) and its standard deviation by up to **57%** for the heaviest payload (**5.1** kg) compared to the nominal controller.
- During dynamic payload switching experiments, the adaptive controller with KF maintained consistently lower height and orientation errors throughout the 0 kg to 5 kg load changes, effectively adapting to varying payloads.
- The RLS-based adaptive controller did not consistently improve performance and sometimes degraded it, highlighting the KF's superior robustness to noisy and biased contact forces through its reliability assessment capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Kalman filter's inherent estimation covariance matrix provides a direct, quantitative measure of parameter reliability, allowing the controller to make informed decisions on when to apply model updates, reducing reliance on heuristics.
> - Dynamic locomotion, such as stepping or walking, significantly enhances the observability of mass and COM parameters, leading to a substantial decrease in estimation covariance and improved reliability compared to standing still.
> - A simplified robot model, assuming mass concentrated in the floating base and neglecting angular acceleration terms, is sufficient for effective online mass and COM identification during typical quadrupedal gaits without compromising overall performance.
