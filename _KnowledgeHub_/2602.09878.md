# MVISTA-4D: View-Consistent 4D World Model with Test-Time Action Inference for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> MVISTA-4D develops a view-consistent 4D world model for robotic manipulation, generating multi-view RGB-D predictions of dynamic scenes and inferring actions. The system achieves improved manipulation success rates and more accurate 4D scene understanding by integrating explicit cross-modality and cross-view consistency with a trajectory-level action inference strategy.

## Problem

- Robotic world models often provide 2D image predictions lacking geometric consistency or partial 3D geometry that is incomplete and brittle under occlusion, which is insufficient for precise physical interaction.
- Ensuring coherence between generated RGB and depth information, and maintaining geometric consistency across multiple camera views for 4D dynamic scene generation, remains a significant challenge.
- Inferring robust robot actions from predicted future states is an ill-posed problem, as traditional inverse dynamics models struggle with ambiguity and often ignore the temporal structure of manipulation trajectories.

## Method

- MVISTA-4D introduces a latent video diffusion model enhanced with structured tokenization, learnable modality tokens, and local cross-modality attention for consistent RGB-D output.
- A novel spherical camera embedding and geometry-aware deformable cross-view attention mechanism enforce explicit geometric consistency across arbitrary queried viewpoints in the generated 4D scenes.
- Action inference is achieved through test-time optimization of a low-dimensional latent code representing the entire action trajectory, further refined by a residual inverse dynamics module.

## Results

- The model consistently outperforms baselines in 4D scene generation, showing lower depth errors (AbRel, RMSE) and superior 3D point cloud quality (Chamfer Distance, EMD) on both synthetic and real-world datasets.
- MVISTA-4D achieves higher manipulation success rates across various tasks, reaching 72.6% on RLBench and demonstrating superior performance on a real robot compared to existing approaches.
- Ablation studies confirm that the proposed geometry-aware deformable cross-view attention, local cross-modality attention, and trajectory-level action inference mechanisms are critical for enhancing both 4D prediction fidelity and robotic manipulation success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit architectural designs that promote cross-modality fusion and geometry-aware cross-view attention are essential for generating coherent and physically plausible 4D dynamic scene predictions.
> - Representing an entire action sequence as a compact, low-dimensional latent code enables a more stable and robust conditioning for generative models, capturing the temporal rhythm of manipulation.
> - Combining test-time optimization of trajectory latents with a residual inverse dynamics module effectively mitigates the ill-posedness of action inference, leading to more reliable robot control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges within the field of world-model-based robotic manipulation, particularly concerning the "imagine-then-act" paradigm. This paradigm involves a robot forecasting future observations under instructions and then selecting actions that align with these imagined futures, offering advantages in planning and long-horizon control over purely reactive control methods.

Current world models for robotic manipulation primarily operate in one of two ways:
1. **Image-based forecasting:** Many existing models generate future observations as 2D image sequences (e.g., video generation methods). While benefiting from established video generation priors, these models often lack explicit representation of 3D geometry. This limitation can lead to predictions that are visually plausible but geometrically inconsistent, thereby creating a discrepancy between imagined futures and physically executable actions, especially in contact-rich manipulation tasks where 3D spatial relations are critical.
2. **Partial 3D geometry reasoning:** A more recent direction involves modeling dynamics directly in 3D using representations such as point clouds or particle-like structures. These methods offer explicit geometric information but can be sparse and may not provide sufficient appearance or semantic cues for fine-grained manipulation. Furthermore, methods that forecast RGB-D sequences often remain single-view, resulting in incomplete geometry and reduced robustness under occlusion, with monocular depth predictions potentially suffering from scale and temporal drift issues. Other multi-view approaches may be limited in the number of views or sensitive to the choice of source view.

A second challenge in world-model-based manipulation is the conversion of imagined futures into executable actions. Common approaches include:
1. **Joint prediction of observations and actions:** This tightly couples control with generation, but errors in early predicted actions can compound over time.
2. **Geometry-first pipelines:** These methods estimate object poses from imagined geometry and then derive actions. However, they can be vulnerable to inaccuracies in pose estimation, particularly under occlusion or geometric artifacts.
3. **Inverse dynamics models (IDM):** These models map consecutive observations to actions. A significant limitation is their ill-posed nature; multiple actions can yield similar perceptual changes, especially under partial observability and physical contact. Furthermore, these methods often treat actions as discrete, per-step signals, potentially overlooking the trajectory-level structure and temporal correlations inherent in manipulation tasks.

MVISTA-4D positions itself within this landscape by seeking to integrate strong visual priors with geometrically consistent, multi-view 4D predictions. It aims to overcome the limitations of single-view or sparse 3D representations by generating complete RGBD sequences from arbitrary viewpoints. Simultaneously, it addresses the challenges of action inference by introducing a method that leverages trajectory-level structure and test-time optimization, moving beyond the per-step and ill-posed nature of traditional inverse dynamics. This work builds on latent video diffusion frameworks and extends them with specific mechanisms for multi-modality and multi-view consistency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are twofold:
1. **To develop an embodied multi-view 4D generative world model capable of synthesizing dynamic manipulation scenes in RGBD from queried viewpoints.** This model is designed to produce geometrically consistent, arbitrary-view RGBD sequences given only a single-view RGBD observation as input.
2. **To establish a robust method for inferring executable actions from the generated 4D futures, addressing the ill-posed nature of traditional inverse dynamics.** This involves incorporating trajectory-level action conditioning and an action refinement mechanism.

These objectives are motivated by several limitations identified in existing approaches to world-model-based robotic manipulation:

- **Incomplete 3D Scene Dynamics:** Current world models often rely on 2D image-based forecasting, which, despite advancements in video generation, lacks the explicit 3D geometric information essential for understanding physical interactions in manipulation. While some methods attempt 4D generation by forecasting RGB-D, they frequently remain single-view, leading to incomplete geometric understanding and brittleness when objects are occluded. Sparse 3D representations (e.g., point clouds) may offer explicit geometry but lack the rich appearance and semantic cues provided by RGB data. A comprehensive understanding of the physical world necessitates a model that can predict both appearance and complete 3D geometry across multiple viewpoints and over time.
- **Inconsistent Multi-view and Cross-modality Predictions:** The fusion of RGB and depth information, and the consistency across multiple camera views, are critical for accurate 4D scene reconstruction. Existing methods often struggle to maintain consistency between appearance and geometry or across different viewpoints, leading to artifacts, misalignments, or missing information in the reconstructed 3D scene. This inconsistency limits the utility of generated futures for precise robotic control.
- **Ill-posed Action Inference:** Converting an imagined future into a sequence of robot actions remains a significant challenge. Inverse dynamics models, which infer actions from observed state transitions, are inherently ill-posed because multiple actions can result in similar perceptual changes, especially in complex, partially observed, or contact-rich scenarios. Furthermore, many current methods treat actions as isolated per-step signals, neglecting the temporal coherence and low-dimensional structure that characterize effective manipulation trajectories. This lack of trajectory-level understanding can lead to unstable or inefficient action sequences.

To address these motivations, MVISTA-4D proposes a model that explicitly designs cross-modal and cross-view feature fusion for improved appearance-geometry consistency and geometric alignment across views. For action inference, it introduces a trajectory-level latent code to capture temporal structure, enabling action inference via test-time optimization and refinement through a residual inverse dynamics model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MVISTA-4D framework is built upon a latent video diffusion model, utilizing a 3D VAE and a Transformer-based diffusion model with Flow Matching. The system takes a single-view RGBD observation, a set of target camera extrinsics, and a text instruction as input to generate instruction-following future RGBD sequences from multiple views. These sequences are designed to depict the same underlying scene dynamics with geometric consistency.

#### 3.1. Input Format Strategy

A structured tokenization approach is employed to optimize how the Transformer models correlations.
- **Intra-view (Cross-modality) fusion:** Within each view, RGB (I) and Depth (D) latents are tokenized using a VAE. Width-wise concatenation is used to place RGB and depth tokens at the same spatial location adjacently, encouraging consistency between modalities.
- **Inter-view (Cross-view) fusion:** Height-wise concatenation is applied across different views. This promotes structure-level cross-view coherence and supports a variable number of input views. The flattened concatenated latent tensor forms the one-dimensional token sequence for the Transformer.

#### 3.2. Feature Integration Across Modalities

To enhance consistency between appearance and geometry:
- **Learnable Modality Token:** A distinct learnable token is added to appearance (x_app) and geometry (x_geo) features to explicitly distinguish their modality and reduce ambiguity when processed by a shared backbone.
- **Local Cross-Modality Attention:** Before the standard self-attention in each DiT (Diffusion Transformer) block, a lightweight local cross-modality attention module is inserted. This module facilitates feature exchange within a small spatial neighborhood (defined by radius r) between RGB and depth tokens. A gated residual update (γ_app, γ_geo) selectively amplifies or suppresses fusion based on cue reliability, and locality reduces computational cost.

#### 3.3. Learning Geometric Consistency Across Views

To enforce geometric alignment and consistency across views:
- **Camera Embedding as a View Token:** Instead of learning separate view embeddings, camera extrinsics are directly used as discriminative view tokens. The extrinsic matrix is parameterized in spherical coordinates (yaw, pitch, roll, log-distance from a shared look-at point), processed by Fourier features, to create a compact 13-D embedding (e_v). This representation makes scale cues more accessible and disentangles rotation/translation, improving generalization. It also serves as a token to distinguish different viewpoints.
- **Geometry-aware Deformable Cross-view Attention:** This module captures multi-view correspondences efficiently. For a query token in one view, it sparsely samples K candidate key-value locations along the epipolar line induced in other views. This restricts attention to geometrically plausible regions, reducing candidates. A deformable refinement mechanism predicts small 2D offsets for sampled locations based on query and sampled features and their similarity, improving alignment at coarser latent resolutions. Multi-head attention is then applied over these refined sparse sets.

#### 3.4. Trajectory Conditioning as a Style Code

To enable trajectory-level action control and stable action inference:
- **Action Sequence Encoding:** A TCN (Temporal Convolutional Network)-based VAE encodes the entire action sequence (a_1:T) into a compact low-dimensional latent token sequence (z ∈ R^(S×C)). This latent code captures the temporal rhythm, smoothness, and phase structure of the trajectory.
- **Injection into Generator:** The latent (z) is injected into the generative model as "style tokens" via cross-attention.
- **Auxiliary Latent-Consistency Head:** During training, a lightweight head reconstructs the conditioning latent tokens (z) from the generator's final-layer representations. This L_traj objective encourages the model to preserve trajectory-relevant information throughout the generation process.

#### 3.5. Embodied Inference and Planning

The framework includes a two-stage action inference process:
- **Trajectory Latent Inference via Test-time Optimization:**
 1. A plausible dynamic rollout (V-bar) is first generated using only text conditioning.
 2. The generated rollout (V-bar) is then frozen.
 3. A randomly initialized trajectory latent (z) is optimized via backpropagation, searching for the latent code that best reconstructs the fixed V-bar through the frozen generator. This produces z*.
 4. The optimized z* is decoded into an action sequence (a-hat_1:T) using the pre-trained TCN decoder. Optimizing in the low-dimensional latent space contributes to smoother and more executable actions.
- **Residual Inverse Dynamics Module (R-IDM):**
 1. The action sequence decoded from z* serves as a strong prior (a_prior_t).
 2. For each timestep, consecutive 3D point sets (P_t, P_t+1) are extracted by back-projecting cropped RGBD observations from the interaction region.
 3. A PointNet-style encoder processes these point clouds along with the prior action (a_prior_t) to predict a residual correction (∆a_t).
 4. The final, refined action (a_t) is computed as a_prior_t + ∆a_t. This residual formulation stabilizes inverse dynamics learning by anchoring predictions around a plausible prior, allowing the network to focus on local adjustments.

**Datasets and Training:**
Experiments are conducted on two synthetic datasets (RLBench, RoboTwin2) and a newly collected real-world multi-view dataset (14 tasks). The model is trained with SFT-style fine-tuning, employing randomized masking for source RGBD video latents and a trajectory-conditioning dropout schedule. Two inference strategies for variable views are supported: Mode-1 (single run concatenation) and Mode-2 (two-pass masked completion), with Mode-2 being the default for main experiments due to its stability and quality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from evaluations on both 4D scene generation and downstream robotic manipulation tasks across synthetic (RLBench, RoboTwin) and a real-world dataset.

#### 4.1. 4D Scene Generation

- **More Consistent 4D Geometry Prediction:** The proposed method generates view-consistent RGB-D sequences. Back-projecting and fusing these multi-view predictions yield more complete 3D point trajectories with reduced occlusion-induced holes.
 - **Quantitative Metrics (Table 1):** The method demonstrates improved depth metrics (lower Absolute Relative Error (AbRel), lower RMSE, higher δ1) and 3D point cloud metrics (lower Chamfer Distance (CD), lower Earth Mover Distance (EMD)) compared to baselines (UniPi*, 4DGen, TesserAct) across RLBench, RoboTwin, and the real-world dataset. This indicates stronger cross-view geometric consistency. For instance, on RoboTwin, Ours achieves AbRel of 2.60 (vs. 3.00 for 4DGen and 3.71 for TesserAct) and CD of 6.51 (vs. 7.18 for 4DGen and 7.11 for TesserAct).
 - **Qualitative Evidence (Fig. 2, 3):** Visual comparisons illustrate that the fused predictions from MVISTA-4D result in more complete point clouds with fewer misalignments and missing regions than baseline methods.
- **High-quality Appearance Generation:** The method achieves competitive appearance quality metrics.
 - **Quantitative Metrics (Table 1):** The method records comparable PSNR, SSIM, and FVD values to established baselines. For example, on RLBench, Ours achieves an FVD of 18.57, which is lower (better) than UniPi* (19.94), 4DGen (20.51), and TesserAct (27.77).
 - **Attribution:** This performance is attributed to the integration of stronger geometry, which provides more reliable structural cues for consistent appearance rendering, and explicit cross-modality fusion facilitating effective information exchange between RGB and depth.
- **Ablations on Cross-View and Cross-Modality Modeling (Table 2, Fig. 4, 5):**
 - **Cross-View Modeling:** Removing the Geometry-aware Deformable Cross-View Attention (w/o view) or replacing it with simple epipolar attention (EA) consistently degrades both appearance and geometry metrics. The degradation is linked to increased cross-view inconsistency and misalignment, as illustrated by object shifts and depth boundary disagreements in qualitative comparisons.
 - **Cross-Modality Modeling:** Removing explicit modality fusion (w/o Mod) also reduces performance, with qualitative comparisons showing RGB drift from depth and misalignment with object boundaries.

#### 4.2. Embodied Action Planning

- **Superior Manipulation Performance:** The method consistently outperforms comparative methods in robotic manipulation tasks.
 - **Quantitative Metrics (Table 3, 4):** On RLBench, MVISTA-4D achieves a 72.6% success rate, surpassing P-ACT (60.4%), UniPi* (34.6%), 4DGen (47.0%), and TesserAct (67.3%). Similar improvements are observed on RoboTwin (43.0% vs. 20.5%, 16.3%, 40.2%, 33.9% respectively) and a real-world robot platform (e.g., 56% vs. 37% for TesserAct on "Open Drawer").
 - **Attribution:** This performance is attributed to the more complete geometry provided by multi-view generation, the trajectory-level latent optimization for action inference, and the residual inverse dynamics module.
- **Effect of Trajectory Latent Optimization (Table 3):** The "Act-Head" variant, which skips latent optimization and directly uses an action-head prediction as a prior, yields lower success rates (e.g., 72.5% vs. 72.6% on RLBench). This result supports the effectiveness of test-time latent optimization.
- **Ablation of Residual IDM (Table 3):** Variants that either remove the residual IDM entirely ("w/o R-IDM") or use a full inverse dynamics model ("Full IDM") both underperform the complete model. This validates the effectiveness of the residual refinement approach, which predicts corrections to a strong trajectory prior.
- **Manipulation Ablations on Fusion and Trajectory Representation (Table 5):**
 - Removing cross-view or cross-modality fusion consistently degrades manipulation success rates.
 - The "cat depth" baseline, which directly concatenates depth with RGB without explicit fusion mechanisms, performs worse due to suboptimal handling by the pre-trained video backbone.
 - Removing the TCN-VAE, which encodes action sequences into a compact latent, also negatively impacts performance, affirming the benefit of trajectory-level action representation.
- **Efficiency and Auxiliary Action Initialization (Table 6):** The method exhibits comparable inference latency to baselines. An additional variant, "+ActionInit," which initializes the trajectory latent with an action-head prediction, further improves success rates (e.g., 76.5% on RLBench) and reduces total inference time by decreasing the required optimization steps.
- **Effect of Number of Views (Table 11):** Increasing the number of views from 1 to 3 yields notable performance gains (68.6% to 72.6% on RLBench). Further increasing to 4 or 5 views provides only marginal improvements while increasing computational cost, suggesting 3 views as a practical trade-off.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MVISTA-4D research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by advancing the capabilities of world-model-based systems, particularly in scenarios requiring detailed geometric understanding and robust action execution.

- **Enhanced Reliability in Embodied Manipulation:** The primary impact is an observed improvement in the reliability of embodied manipulation. By generating geometrically consistent 4D predictions across multiple views, the model provides a more complete and accurate understanding of the dynamic scene. This is particularly beneficial in complex, cluttered, or occluded environments, where partial observations or geometrically inconsistent predictions can lead to manipulation failures. The ability to "imagine" a fuller 4D future allows for more informed action inference.
- **Improved Action Inference and Execution:** The introduction of trajectory-level action conditioning and a test-time action optimization strategy, followed by a residual inverse dynamics model, addresses the limitations of traditional, ill-posed inverse dynamics. This approach enables the inference of smoother, more executable actions that are closely aligned with the imagined 4D futures, reducing ambiguity and increasing robustness during real-world execution.
- **Geometric Interface for Planning:** The work proposes that view-consistent 4D prediction can serve as an effective geometric interface. This interface bridges the gap between high-level imagined futures (what the robot "sees" will happen) and low-level executable robot actions, offering a principled way to translate visual understanding into physical control.
- **Scalability and Future Directions:** The observed "scale-up behavior" where generating more views leads to more complete and better-aligned 4D reconstructions, indicates a practical path for performance improvements with increased computational resources or more camera views. This suggests potential for deploying the model in more complex and expansive manipulation setups.
- **Insights for Future World Modeling:** The extensive ablations and analyses provide practical insights into the importance of explicit cross-modality and cross-view fusion, as well as trajectory-level action representation. These findings can guide future research in embodied world modeling, particularly in the design of architectures that integrate diverse sensory information and control signals effectively.

**Potential Risks:** The capabilities of generating realistic synthetic visual data and potentially misleading reconstructions present potential risks. The authors advocate for responsible release and safety-aware deployment of such technologies to mitigate these concerns.

**Limitations and Future Work:** The research identifies limitations, including the increased latency associated with test-time optimization, which may impact real-time responsiveness, and the dependence on accurate camera and robot calibration for maintaining consistency and action quality. Future work is suggested to improve spatial precision and contact robustness, potentially through higher-resolution geometry, stronger contact-aware constraints, or closed-loop correction mechanisms.
