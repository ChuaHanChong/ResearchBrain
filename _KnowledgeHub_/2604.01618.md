# Tex3D: Objects as Attack Surfaces via Adversarial 3D Textures for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed Tex3D, a framework for generating adversarial 3D textures that physically attack Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by causing high task failure rates (up to 96.7%). This approach leverages differentiable rendering and trajectory-aware optimization to achieve robustness against viewpoint changes and effective cross-model transferability.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lack robustness to adversarial attacks, which is a critical safety concern for real-world robotic deployment.
- Existing language-based and 2D visual patch attacks have limitations: language attacks are not primary physical attack surfaces, and 2D patches are view-specific, conspicuous, and easily circumvented by viewpoint changes.
- It is challenging to optimize adversarial 3D textures in a differentiable manner within non-differentiable physics simulators and to maintain attack effectiveness over long, multi-timestep manipulation trajectories.

## Method

- **Tex3D**, a framework for end-to-end optimization of ==physically realizable 3D adversarial textures==, was developed to directly manipulate objects within VLA simulation environments.
- ==Foreground-Background Decoupling== (**FBD**) was introduced, using a ==dual-renderer== approach (MuJoCo for background, Nvdiffrast for differentiable target object rendering) to enable gradient propagation for 3D textures.
- ==Trajectory-Aware Adversarial Optimization== (**TAAO**) was employed, using ==latent dynamics-guided frame weighting== and ==vertex-based texture parameterization== to sustain adversarial effectiveness over long task horizons and improve transferability.

## Results

- Tex3D achieved high average task failure rates across four VLA models and various manipulation tasks, increasing failure from baselines (**2.8%–24.1%**) to **69.3%–90.5%** for untargeted and targeted attacks.
- Adversarial textures generated by Tex3D demonstrated strong transferability, causing failure rates up to **75.7%** when transferred across different VLA models, including cross-family architectures.
- Tex3D attacks outperformed 2D patch attacks in robustness against geometric variations and in physical world deployment (maintaining **66.8%–67.6%** failure rates), while also resisting common input-space defenses.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adversarial 3D textures provide a physically grounded, viewpoint-robust, and less perceptible attack surface for VLA models compared to 2D patch attacks.
> - Differentiable optimization of 3D object appearance is achievable in non-differentiable physics simulators by decoupling foreground object rendering with a differentiable renderer and synchronizing parameters.
> - Maintaining effective adversarial influence over long, sequential robotic manipulation tasks requires prioritizing attack optimization during behaviorally critical moments within a trajectory.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a significant advancement in robotic control, integrating visual perception and natural language instructions to generate low-level control signals for robotic manipulation. These models, exemplified by architectures such as OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, $\pi_0$, and $\pi_{0.5}$, have demonstrated proficiency across various complex manipulation tasks. However, as VLA models transition towards real-world deployment, their robustness to adversarial attacks becomes a critical safety concern.

Prior research on adversarial attacks against VLA systems has primarily focused on two modalities:
1. **Language-based attacks**: These methods perturb the natural language instructions provided to the VLA model, leading to erroneous actions. While effective, their impact is limited by their dependence on the language interface, which may not always be the primary attack surface in physical environments.
2. **2D visual (patch-based) attacks**: These attacks involve applying adversarial patches to the input images, inducing task failure through visual perception manipulation. Such methods are more physically realizable than language-based attacks but possess inherent limitations. They are often view-specific, meaning their effectiveness is contingent on precise camera viewpoint and object pose alignment, which is difficult to maintain in dynamic physical interactions. Additionally, 2D patches can be conspicuous and non-naturalistic, potentially leading to easier detection.

This research addresses these limitations by exploring adversarial 3D textures as a more physically grounded and less perceptible attack surface. Unlike 2D patches, 3D textures are intrinsically bound to object surfaces, offering robustness to viewpoint and object pose variations. This object-centric approach is presented as particularly relevant for embodied manipulation and real-world robotic deployment, filling a gap in the existing literature by enabling physically plausible and sustained adversarial influence directly on manipulated objects.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate and demonstrate the vulnerability of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to physically realizable adversarial 3D textures. The motivation stems from the identified limitations of existing adversarial attack paradigms and the increasing deployment of VLA models in safety-critical robotic applications.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations include:

- **Addressing the lack of physical realism and viewpoint robustness in existing visual attacks**: Current 2D patch attacks are often view-specific and conspicuous, making them less representative of real-world threats. The research aims to develop an attack that is intrinsically bound to object surfaces, thereby maintaining effectiveness across various viewpoints and object poses, which is crucial for embodied interaction. Adversarial 3D textures are posited as a more natural and less perceptible form of perturbation suitable for physical environments.

- **Overcoming the challenge of non-differentiable simulation environments**: A significant technical obstacle to optimizing adversarial 3D textures directly within VLA simulation environments (e.g., those built on physics engines like MuJoCo) is the absence of a differentiable path from the VLA objective function back to the object's appearance. Standard simulators typically do not expose gradients for 3D object textures, making end-to-end optimization intractable. A core objective is to establish such a differentiable pathway without altering the core simulation engine.

- **Maintaining adversarial effectiveness over long-horizon manipulation trajectories**: VLA model inference in robotic tasks often spans hundreds of timesteps, involving complex, sequential actions. Existing 3D texture attacks, primarily developed for non-embodied settings, do not inherently account for this temporal aspect. A key objective is to ensure that the adversarial texture sustains its disruptive effect across an entire task trajectory, rather than only at isolated moments. This requires a mechanism to prioritize and optimize perturbations during behaviorally critical phases of a task.

- **Enabling end-to-end optimization of adversarial 3D textures**: The overarching goal is to create a comprehensive framework, named Tex3D, that allows for the direct, end-to-end optimization of physically grounded 3D adversarial textures within the operational VLA simulation environment. This framework is designed to facilitate both untargeted attacks (to degrade overall task success) and targeted attacks (to steer the model towards specific erroneous actions).

- **Exposing critical vulnerabilities and advocating for robustness-aware training**: By demonstrating the high task failure rates induced by Tex3D in both simulation and real-robot settings, the research aims to empirically highlight the fundamental fragility of current VLA systems against such attacks. This exposure is intended to motivate and inform the development of robustness-aware training methodologies and safety measures for future VLA model design and deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology focuses on developing Tex3D, a framework that enables end-to-end optimization of 3D adversarial textures for VLA models. This involves addressing the challenges of differentiability in simulation environments and maintaining adversarial effectiveness over long task trajectories.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The objective is to find an adversarial texture map $T_{adv}$ for a target object mesh $M = (V, F, T)$ that maximizes the deviation of VLA model actions. The VLA model $\pi$ takes an observation $O_t$ at timestep $t$ and a language instruction $l$ to produce an action $a_t = \pi(O_t, l)$. The optimization seeks to maximize action deviation over the entire task trajectory $T$ and across multiple sampled views $M$:

$T_{adv} = \arg \max_{T_{adv}} E_{t \sim T, m \sim U} [ \| \pi(O_{t,m}(M_{adv}), l) - a^*_{t,m} \|^2 ]$

where $O_{t,m}(M_{adv})$ is the observation of the adversarial mesh $M_{adv}=(V, F, T_{adv})$ at timestep $t$ from view $m$, and $a^*_{t,m}$ is the reference action for the clean mesh.

#### 3.2. Differentiable Rendering via Foreground–Background Decoupling (FBD)

To enable gradient propagation back to the object texture within non-differentiable physics simulators like MuJoCo, Foreground–Background Decoupling (FBD) is introduced. This method utilizes a dual-renderer approach, leveraging MuJoCo for background rendering and Nvdiffrast for differentiable target object rendering, with synchronized parameters.

1. **Environmental Background Rendering**: At each timestep $t$, MuJoCo renders the complete scene, excluding the target object, to generate the background image $x_{bg_t}$. This ensures that the physical simulation and environmental context (robot, other objects) remain unmodified and physically accurate.

2. **Target Foreground Rendering**: The target object mesh with the adversarial texture $M_{adv}$ is rendered separately in Nvdiffrast. Nvdiffrast is chosen for its GPU-accelerated, differentiable rasterization capabilities, allowing gradients to be computed with respect to the texture map $T_{adv}$. This step yields the foreground image $x_{fg_t}(T_{adv})$ and a silhouette mask $m_t$ (pre-computed from MuJoCo) that identifies the target object's pixels.

3. **Scene Compositing**: The adversarial observation $O_t(M_{adv})$ is then assembled by combining the foreground and background images using the silhouette mask:
 $O_t(M_{adv}) = m_t \odot x_{fg_t}(T_{adv}) + (1 - m_t) \odot x_{bg_t}$
 This composite image is fed into the VLA model, establishing a differentiable chain from $T_{adv}$ through Nvdiffrast, the VLA model, and the action loss.

4. **Cross-renderer Parameter Alignment**: To ensure photometric and geometric consistency between the two renderers, rendering parameters are synchronized:
 - **Geometric Alignment**: The object's model matrix ($M_t$), camera view matrix ($V_t$), and projection matrix ($P_t$) are extracted from MuJoCo at each timestep and applied to Nvdiffrast. This ensures the target object is rendered with its correct 3D pose, camera viewpoint, and intrinsic parameters.
 - **Lighting Alignment**: Ambient light intensity ($I_a$), diffuse light intensity ($I_d$), and material reflectance ($\rho$) from MuJoCo's scene configuration are mirrored in Nvdiffrast's shading pipeline.

#### 3.3. Trajectory-Aware Adversarial Optimization (TAAO)

To ensure sustained adversarial effectiveness over long manipulation trajectories, Trajectory-Aware Adversarial Optimization (TAAO) is proposed.

1. **Latent Dynamics-Guided Frame Weighting**: This component identifies behaviorally critical frames within a trajectory by analyzing the latent dynamics of the observation sequence.
 - **Latent Feature Extraction**: A pre-trained visual encoder $E$ extracts a latent representation $f_t = E(O_t)$ for each observation.
 - **Latent Velocity and Acceleration**: Temporal rates of change are estimated using central differences: $v_t = \|f_{t+1} - f_{t-1}\|_2 / 2$ and $\alpha_t = |v_t - v_{t-1}|$. These are then normalized across the trajectory.
 - **Criticality Scoring and Frame Weighting**: A criticality score $s_t = \max(\hat{v}_t, \hat{\alpha}_t)$ is computed, and per-frame weights $w_t$ are derived using a temperature-scaled softmax, prioritizing frames with high perceptual change or abrupt shifts in the rate of change.

2. **Vertex-Based Texture Parameterization**: To mitigate overfitting and improve transferability, the high-dimensional texture map $T_{adv}$ is reparameterized by optimizing per-vertex color attributes $c \in R^{N_v \times 3}$. The texture map is then recovered via barycentric interpolation over the fixed mesh geometry, effectively constraining perturbations to a smoother, low-rank manifold.

3. **Adversarial Objectives**:
 - **Untargeted Attack**: Aims to maximize the weighted L2 distance between the VLA model's output actions for the adversarial object and the reference actions for the clean object, averaged over views and weighted by frame criticality:
 $c^* = \arg \max_c \sum_{t=1}^T w_t \cdot \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M \| \pi(O_{t,m}(M_{adv}), l) - a^*_{t,m} \|^2$
 - **Targeted Attack**: Aims to minimize the weighted L2 distance between the VLA model's output actions and a prescribed target action $a^{tgt}_t$, averaged over views and weighted by frame criticality:
 $c^* = \arg \min_c \sum_{t=1}^T w_t \cdot \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M \| \pi(O_{t,m}(M_{adv}), l) - a^{tgt}_t \|^2$
 In the implementation, $a^{tgt}_t$ is defined as actions that redirect the gripper towards an alternative target.

#### 3.4. Physical Attack (Expectation over Transformations - EoT)

To bridge the sim-to-real gap, the Expectation over Transformations (EoT) strategy is incorporated. During optimization, stochastic transformations $g \sim T$ are sampled and applied to the rendered adversarial observations. These transformations include 3D variations (object pose, viewpoint, distance) and 2D image-level augmentations (brightness, contrast, blur). This encourages the adversarial texture to be robust to real-world variations, complementing multi-view optimization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides extensive experimental results in both simulation and real-robot settings, evaluating Tex3D's effectiveness across various VLA models and manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Overall Attack Effectiveness

Tex3D consistently achieved high task failure rates across four VLA models (OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, $\pi_0$, $\pi_{0.5}$) and four LIBERO task categories (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long-horizon), in both untargeted and targeted attack settings (Table 1).
- On **OpenVLA**, Tex3D increased the average task failure rate from a baseline of 24.1% (No Attack) to 88.1% for untargeted attacks and 90.5% for targeted attacks.
- For **OpenVLA-OFT**, the average failure rate rose from 4.7% to 76.0% (untargeted) and 79.3% (targeted).
- **$\pi_0$** saw its average failure rate increase from 4.6% to 71.8% (untargeted) and 73.3% (targeted).
- **$\pi_{0.5}$** experienced an increase from 2.8% to 69.3% (untargeted) and 71.2% (targeted).
- The **Spatial** task often showed the most pronounced increases, with failure rates reaching up to 96.7% for OpenVLA.
- For **targeted attacks**, the average L1 distances between attacked actions and target actions were low (0.0176 for OpenVLA, 0.0237 for OpenVLA-OFT, 0.0305 for $\pi_0$, and 0.0372 for $\pi_{0.5}$), indicating precise control over the model's erroneous behavior (Table 4).

These results suggest that Tex3D's strong performance is attributed to the joint optimization of object-level adversarial textures with temporal consistency, surpassing simple noise injection or single-frame perturbations.

#### 4.2. Cross-Model Transferability

Adversarial textures generated by Tex3D demonstrated transferability across different VLA models (Table 2, bottom).
- **Same-family transfer** was strong: OpenVLA-optimized attacks achieved 61.5%–70.6% failure rates on OpenVLA-OFT, and vice-versa (70.2%–75.7%). Similarly, $\pi_0$ attacks transferred to $\pi_{0.5}$ with 49.2%–58.4% failure rates, and vice-versa (55.8%–63.7%).
- **Cross-family transfer** was also effective: OpenVLA-optimized textures caused 34.5%–44.1% failure rates on $\pi_0$ and 27.8%–36.5% on $\pi_{0.5}$. Conversely, $\pi_0$-optimized textures resulted in 44.8%–61.5% failure rates on OpenVLA and 37.2%–48.4% on OpenVLA-OFT.
These findings indicate that Tex3D learns robust, object-level adversarial patterns rather than overfitting to specific model architectures.

#### 4.3. Ruling Out Non-adversarial Factors

Control studies confirmed that the elevated failure rates were due to adversarial optimization, not ordinary visual variations or rendering artifacts.
- Random Gaussian noise or simple color changes applied to the object texture resulted in much lower failure rates compared to Tex3D. For example, on the OpenVLA Spatial task, these controls caused failure rates between 18.8% and 24.6%, far below Tex3D's 95.8%/96.7% (Figure 1, Table 3).
- The dual-renderer pipeline (MuJoCo + Nvdiffrast) introduced negligible discrepancies, with a maximum 1.2 percentage point difference in task failure rate compared to direct MuJoCo rendering (Table 2, top). This indicates high rendering fidelity.

#### 4.4. Robustness Against 2D Patch Attacks

Tex3D demonstrated superior robustness compared to 2D patch-based attacks under geometric variations and in physical world deployment (Figure 4).
- Under digital camera angle and object rotation perturbations, 2D patch attacks showed significant drops in effectiveness (e.g., from 100% to 63.4%-67.4% failure rate). Tex3D, conversely, maintained high failure rates (80.8%-88.1%).
- In physical world experiments with position offsets, Tex3D sustained failure rates of 66.8%–67.6%, outperforming 2D patch attacks (40.8%–50.8%) and 2D patch + EoT (49.8%–56.6%).
- Qualitative comparisons showed 2D patches were more susceptible to viewpoint changes and placement mismatch, while Tex3D's object-level textures maintained their effect after physical deployment (Figure 5). Tex3D also yielded lower LPIPS scores, indicating better visual quality compared to 2D patch attacks (Figure 7, first column).

#### 4.5. Robustness Against Common Defenses

Tex3D maintained strong attack effectiveness against standard input-space defenses, including JPEG compression, additive noise, median blur, and bit-depth reduction. The adversarial task failure rate remained largely consistent (around 86.6%–87.3%) across these defense settings (Figure 6).

#### 4.6. Rendering Consistency and Attack Trajectory

The dual-renderer composition maintained high visual consistency with the original MuJoCo rendering, as indicated by SSIM scores consistently close to 1 (Figure 7). Despite this visual consistency, Tex3D's adversarial textures induced substantial behavioral deviations, causing the robot to drift from its intended path and lead to task failure (Figure 3).

#### 4.7. Representative Keyframe Weights

TAAO's dynamics-guided weighting allocated optimization effort effectively. Critical stages like grasping received significantly higher weights (e.g., 0.90) compared to more stable phases such as initialization or put-down (e.g., 0.20-0.37). This prioritization ensures adversarial influence is concentrated during decision-sensitive moments (Table 5).

#### 4.8. Ablation Studies

- **FBD Components**: Removing the foreground-background decoupling (rendering the entire scene in Nvdiffrast) increased optimization time per step significantly (from $\sim$7.2s to $\sim$24.8s) and slightly reduced failure rates (88.1% to 84.6%). Removing MVP or Lighting alignment further lowered failure rates (to 65.8% and 76.8% respectively), confirming the importance of cross-renderer alignment for fidelity and effectiveness (Table 6).
- **TAAO Weighting Strategy**: Dynamics-guided weighting achieved the highest task failure rate (88.1%), outperforming random weighting (73.7%) and uniform weighting (82.1%).
- **Perturbation Level**: Increased perturbation budgets (L0 to L3) generally led to higher task failure rates in both white-box and cross-model transfer settings (Figure 8). Even visually natural (L0) perturbations were highly effective, suggesting a subtle trade-off between stealthiness and attack strength (Figure 9).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research demonstrates that adversarial 3D textures constitute a practical, robust, and highly effective attack surface for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The findings have several significant implications:

- **Exposure of Critical Vulnerabilities**: The high task failure rates achieved by Tex3D, even with subtle perturbations, reveal a critical fragility in current VLA systems. This suggests that VLA models, despite their advanced capabilities, are susceptible to physically grounded visual attacks that are challenging to detect and robust across viewpoints and temporal dynamics. This vulnerability is particularly concerning for real-world deployments where physical manipulation and varied environmental conditions are commonplace.

- **Practical and Stealthy Threat Model**: Unlike previous language-based or 2D patch-based attacks, adversarial 3D textures are naturally integrated into objects' appearances, making them less perceptible and more difficult to mitigate in physical environments. Their inherent robustness to geometric variations (viewpoint changes, object rotations) further establishes them as a more potent and realistic threat model for embodied agents.

- **Call for Robustness-Aware VLA Training**: The effectiveness of Tex3D underscores the need for VLA model development to incorporate systematic robustness evaluations and robustness-aware training. The paper suggests that the current vulnerability may stem from VLA models being trained on idealized environments with limited coverage of subtle appearance shifts. Future research and development could focus on:
 1. **Diverse and Realistic Visual Data**: Incorporating more varied and realistic robotic scenes, including those with subtle adversarial visual variations, into training datasets.
 2. **Structural Constraints on Actions**: Enforcing stronger structural constraints or logical reasoning over joint dependencies at the action level to filter out implausible or harmful actions, thereby increasing resilience.

- **Advancement in Adversarial Machine Learning for Robotics**: The introduction of Foreground-Background Decoupling (FBD) provides a novel technical solution for enabling differentiable optimization of 3D object appearance within non-differentiable simulation environments. Similarly, Trajectory-Aware Adversarial Optimization (TAAO) advances the field by demonstrating how to maintain adversarial effectiveness across long, sequential, and behaviorally critical task trajectories in embodied AI.

In conclusion, Tex3D provides a rigorous demonstration of a significant security challenge for VLA models. Its findings necessitate a re-evaluation of current VLA safety protocols and training paradigms, urging the development of more resilient and robust robotic systems capable of operating reliably and safely in complex, potentially adversarial, real-world environments.
