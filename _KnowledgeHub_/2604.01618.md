# Tex3D: Objects as Attack Surfaces via Adversarial 3D Textures for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed Tex3D, a framework for generating adversarial 3D textures that physically attack Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by causing high task failure rates (up to 96.7%). This approach leverages differentiable rendering and trajectory-aware optimization to achieve robustness against viewpoint changes and effective cross-model transferability.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lack robustness to adversarial attacks, which is a critical safety concern for real-world robotic deployment.
- Existing language-based and 2D visual patch attacks have limitations: language attacks are not primary physical attack surfaces, and 2D patches are view-specific, conspicuous, and easily circumvented by viewpoint changes.
- It is challenging to optimize adversarial 3D textures in a differentiable manner within non-differentiable physics simulators and to maintain attack effectiveness over long, multi-timestep manipulation trajectories.

## Method

- **Tex3D**, a framework for end-to-end optimization of ==physically realizable 3D adversarial textures==, was developed to directly manipulate objects within VLA simulation environments.
- ==Foreground-Background Decoupling== (**FBD**) was introduced, using a ==dual-renderer== approach (MuJoCo for background, Nvdiffrast for differentiable target object rendering) to enable gradient propagation for 3D textures.
- ==Trajectory-Aware Adversarial Optimization== (**TAAO**) was employed, using ==latent dynamics-guided frame weighting== and ==vertex-based texture parameterization== to sustain adversarial effectiveness over long task horizons and improve transferability.

## Results

- Tex3D achieved high average task failure rates across four VLA models and various manipulation tasks, increasing failure from baselines (**2.8%–24.1%**) to **69.3%–90.5%** for untargeted and targeted attacks.
- Adversarial textures generated by Tex3D demonstrated strong transferability, causing failure rates up to **75.7%** when transferred across different VLA models, including cross-family architectures.
- Tex3D attacks outperformed 2D patch attacks in robustness against geometric variations and in physical world deployment (maintaining **66.8%–67.6%** failure rates), while also resisting common input-space defenses.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adversarial 3D textures provide a physically grounded, viewpoint-robust, and less perceptible attack surface for VLA models compared to 2D patch attacks.
> - Differentiable optimization of 3D object appearance is achievable in non-differentiable physics simulators by decoupling foreground object rendering with a differentiable renderer and synchronizing parameters.
> - Maintaining effective adversarial influence over long, sequential robotic manipulation tasks requires prioritizing attack optimization during behaviorally critical moments within a trajectory.
