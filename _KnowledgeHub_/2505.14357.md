# Vid2World: Crafting Video Diffusion Models to Interactive World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Vid2World repurposes pre-trained video diffusion models into interactive world models by causalizing their architecture and training objective for autoregressive rollout, then adding frame-level causal action guidance; transferring a 1.1B DynamiCrafter model achieves state-of-the-art results across robot manipulation, 3D game simulation, and open-world navigation.

## Problem

- World models trained only on in-domain action-labeled data require costly, labor-intensive collection and still yield coarse, low-fidelity predictions with limited physical realism.
- Scaling to cross-domain action-labeled data improves efficiency somewhat but the acquisition cost persists and generated visuals still lack fidelity and realism.
- Standard video diffusion models use bidirectional temporal attention and convolutions and video-level conditioning, making them incompatible with causal, action-conditioned autoregressive rollout.

## Method

- ==Weight transfer schemes== for causalizing temporal convolutions, comparing ==Shift==, ==Masked==, and a proposed ==Extrapolative Weight Transfer== that redistributes future-facing kernel weights via local linear feature extrapolation to preserve pre-trained representations.
- ==Diffusion Forcing== training objective with independently sampled per-frame noise levels, enabling the causalized architecture to handle the noise-level distributions seen during autoregressive inference.
- ==Causal Action Guidance==: frame-level action injection via a lightweight ==MLP== plus an ==action-dropout training scheme== enabling ==classifier-free guidance==, proven equivalent to steering a history-consistent posterior toward action alignment.

## Results

- On RT-1 robot manipulation, Vid2World reaches FVD **18.5**/**18.7** and FID around **5.8-5.9**, outperforming ControlNet, Action-Conditioned, Language-Conditioned, and AVID baselines in FVD despite being the only autoregressive variant.
- On CS:GO game simulation, Vid2World beats the best DIAMOND baseline by **71.1%** relative FVD improvement and **79.9%** relative FID improvement.
- Ablations show Extrapolative and Masked Weight Transfer beat Shift Weight Transfer, and adding Action Guidance further improves FVD from **28.6** to **22.4** and FID from **7.52** to **6.16**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Model-level transfer of internet-scale pre-trained video diffusion priors can substitute for expensive in-domain or cross-domain action-labeled data collection in world modeling.
> - Naive causalization (Shift Weight Transfer) can catastrophically fail even on smooth signals; preserving output representations via principled extrapolation is necessary, not just enforcing causal masking.
> - Classifier-free-style causal action guidance formalizes action conditioning as posterior steering, giving a test-time knob to trade off action responsiveness against generation fidelity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

World models (WMs) are foundational components in sequential decision-making, enabling intelligent agents to predict future states and plan actions by simulating environment dynamics. Despite their utility in domains such as game simulation, autonomous driving, and robotics, conventional WMs typically rely on in-domain, action-labeled data. This dependency often necessitates labor-intensive data collection and frequently results in predictions that are low in fidelity and coarse in detail, thereby limiting their application in complex and visually rich environments.

Recent efforts to address the data-hungry nature of WMs have explored pre-training on broader, cross-domain action-labeled datasets, drawing inspiration from the success of foundation models. While this approach has shown some improvements in data efficiency and generation quality, it does not fundamentally resolve the challenge of acquiring costly action-labeled data. The visual realism and fidelity of predictions remain an area for further development.

In parallel, video diffusion models (VDMs), trained on vast internet-scale, action-free video data, have demonstrated considerable capabilities in generating high-quality videos that depict diverse real-world dynamics. These models represent a significant repository of learned physical priors and generative capacities. The integration of these two research directions—leveraging the generative power of VDMs for the predictive capabilities of WMs—has been explored to a limited extent. Previous work has involved co-training WMs with action-free data, but the current paper proposes a more direct model-level transfer.

The transition from passive video generation, characteristic of VDMs, to interactive world modeling presents two primary challenges:
1. **Causal Generation**: Standard VDMs are designed for full-sequence denoising, incorporating bidirectional temporal context. This makes them unsuitable for autoregressive rollouts, where future predictions must be strictly dependent on past observations and actions.
2. **Action Conditioning**: VDMs are typically conditioned on high-level, video-centric inputs (e.g., text prompts) and lack mechanisms for fine-grained, frame-level action conditioning, which is essential for counterfactual reasoning in interactive environments.

Existing diffusion-based world modeling approaches fall into two categories: those treating it as a conditional image generation problem with fixed context windows, limiting long-term reasoning, and those formulating it as full-sequence video generation, which improves temporal coherence but prevents autoregressive interaction. Similarly, prior efforts to adapt pre-trained generative models to WMs have often overlooked the crucial aspects of interactivity and temporal causality. This work aims to bridge this gap by systematically enabling causal and action-conditioned generation within pre-trained video diffusion models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the Vid2World research are articulated as follows:

1. **Mitigate Limitations of Existing World Models**: Current world models often require extensive domain-specific, action-labeled training data, which is expensive and labor-intensive to collect. Despite this investment, the predictions generated by these models frequently exhibit low fidelity and coarse visual details, constraining their utility in complex real-world environments. A core motivation is to overcome these limitations by reducing reliance on such data and enhancing visual realism.

2. **Exploit Internet-Scale Action-Free Video Data**: The research posits that internet-scale video data, characterized by its abundance, ease of collection, and diversity, constitutes a largely untapped resource containing rich world priors. This vast dataset offers an opportunity to imbue world models with a more comprehensive understanding of real-world dynamics without the prohibitive costs associated with action labeling.

3. **Advance from Data-Level to Model-Level Transfer**: While some prior work has explored co-training world models with action-free video data, this paper advocates for a paradigm shift toward directly transferring knowledge from *pre-trained video diffusion models* into interactive world models. This approach aims to bypass the computational expense of training on massive video corpora from scratch and to leverage the physical priors already implicitly learned by VDMs, given that non-causal generative modeling may inherently be a simpler learning task than its causal counterpart.

4. **Enable Causal Generation in Video Diffusion Models**: A fundamental objective is to adapt the inherently non-causal nature of standard video diffusion models, which use bidirectional temporal context for full-sequence denoising, to support causal autoregressive generation. For interactive world modeling, predictions must strictly depend on past observations and actions, necessitating architectural and objective modifications to enforce temporal causality.

5. **Incorporate Fine-Grained Action Conditioning**: Pre-trained video diffusion models typically rely on coarse, video-level conditions (e.g., text prompts) and lack the ability to process fine-grained, frame-level action signals. A key objective is to integrate a mechanism for precise action conditioning, enabling the model to perform counterfactual reasoning—predicting how specific actions influence future states—which is critical for interactive decision-making.

In summary, the motivation is to develop a general, scalable, and effective methodology (Vid2World) that repurposes the high-fidelity generative capabilities of pre-trained video diffusion models into interactive world models, thereby achieving autoregressive, action-conditioned predictions across diverse domains with limited domain-specific interaction data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Vid2World framework addresses the challenges of transforming passive video diffusion models into interactive world models through two primary mechanisms: **Video Diffusion Causalization** and **Causal Action Guidance**.

#### 3.1. Video Diffusion Causalization

This component aims to adapt existing video diffusion models, which inherently use non-causal temporal dependencies, to enable autoregressive generation.

- **Architectural Modifications**:
 - **Temporal Attention Layers**: For non-causal temporal attention layers, causality is enforced by applying causal masks during computation. This restricts the receptive field to only consider past frames, preventing information flow from future frames to the current prediction. No specific parametric changes are required, as attention mechanisms are inherently adaptive to variable sequence lengths.
 - **Temporal Convolution Layers**: Causalizing temporal convolution layers is approached through three distinct weight transfer strategies for pre-trained symmetric kernels:
 1. **Shift Weight Transfer**: This naive approach shifts the entire pre-trained kernel weights into the past. While it preserves all original weights, it introduces a temporal misalignment, potentially leading to suboptimal representations due to altered feature aggregation points relative to their original context.
 2. **Masked Weight Transfer**: This strategy truncates the kernel by retaining only the weights corresponding to past and present timesteps, while setting future-facing weights to zero. This method directly enforces causality but discards information encoded in the original future-facing weights.
 3. **Extrapolative Weight Transfer**: This proposed, more principled mechanism is based on local linear extrapolation of features along the temporal dimension. It models future features (`z_{t+k}`) as a linear combination of `p` past features (`z_{t-j}`). The core principle is to redistribute the weights that originally acted on future frames (`w_i` for `i>0`) back onto the past part of the causal kernel, according to these linear feature relationships. This aims to maximally preserve the output representation of the original non-causal convolution within a causal framework, minimizing representational drift.

- **Training Objectives for Causal Generation**: Architectural changes alone are insufficient. Standard video diffusion models are typically trained with a homogeneous noise schedule across all frames. To enable robust causal rollouts where history frames are clean and future frames are noisy, Vid2World adopts **Diffusion Forcing** (Chen et al., 2024). This involves independently and uniformly sampling noise levels for each frame (`k_t ~ U(0, K)`) during training. This exposure to a diverse set of noise-level combinations across frames prepares the model for the heterogeneous noise levels encountered during autoregressive inference.

#### 3.2. Causal Action Guidance

This component equips the causalized video diffusion model with the ability to perform fine-grained, frame-level action-conditioned generation, transforming it into an interactive world model.

- **Causal Action Injection**: To provide frame-level control, the embedding of the action `a_{t-1}` (the action preceding the predicted frame `o_t`) is integrated into the model’s latent representation at temporal position `t`. This is achieved by feeding action inputs through a lightweight multi-layer perceptron for continuous action spaces or a learned embedding layer for discrete action spaces, and then combining these embeddings with the existing noise conditioning.

- **Training and Sampling with Guidance**: The framework incorporates **classifier-free guidance** (Ho & Salimans, 2021) to enhance action controllability. The model is trained to learn both a conditional score function (conditioned on the action sequence) and an unconditional score function (where the most recent action is masked).
 - **Action Dropout Mechanism**: During training, each action `˜a_t` is independently dropped with a fixed probability `p`. This forces the model to learn score functions that account for various subsets of action sequences, including the impact of the immediate action on the predicted transition.
 - **Guided Sampling**: At inference time, the noise prediction `ϵ_{guided}` is computed as a linear combination of the conditional (`ϵ_{cond}`) and unconditional (`ϵ_{ucond}`) score estimates: `ϵ_{guided} = (1 + λ) · ϵ_{cond} − λ · ϵ_{ucond}`, where `λ` is the guidance scale. This process, termed Causal Action Guidance, acts as an implicit classifier, steering the generative process toward predictions that are aligned with the specified action, while maintaining visual fidelity. The theoretical basis for this is demonstrated as steering the posterior distribution to emphasize action alignment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Vid2World was evaluated by transferring a 1.1B-parameter U-Net-based video diffusion model, DynamiCrafter (Xing et al., 2024), pre-trained on internet-scale videos. Experiments were conducted across robot manipulation, 3D game simulation, and open-world navigation domains, utilizing metrics such as FVD, FID, SSIM, LPIPS, PSNR, and DreamSim for video prediction, and Real2Sim Policy Evaluation for downstream task performance.

#### 4.1. Robot Manipulation (RT-1 Dataset)

- **Performance**: Vid2World, operating in an autoregressive manner, achieved FVD of 18.5 and FID of 5.806. These results were competitive with or superior to various existing transfer methods, including non-autoregressive baselines. For instance, it outperformed the ControlNet and AVID baselines in FVD, and achieved comparable performance across other metrics.
- **Real2Sim Policy Evaluation**: The method demonstrated its utility for downstream decision-making by reliably reflecting performance disparities among different RT-1 policy checkpoints (Begin, 15%, Converged) on a drawer closing task. The simulated policy success rates closely corresponded to real-world trends, indicating the model's capacity for faithfully predicting diverse outcomes under varied policies.

#### 4.2. 3D Game Simulation (CS:GO Dataset)

- **Performance**: Vid2World significantly outperformed state-of-the-art autoregressive world models, specifically both "Fast" and "HQ" configurations of DIAMOND (Alonso et al., 2024), across all evaluation metrics. For example, Vid2World achieved an FVD of 106.6 and an FID of 17.5, which represents a 71.1% improvement in FVD and a 79.9% improvement in FID compared to the best baseline configuration.
- **Qualitative Observations**: The model exhibited reduced error accumulation compared to baselines, maintaining visual sharpness during rapid viewpoint changes. It also demonstrated accurate action alignment, faithfully reflecting specified actions (e.g., "aim-down-sights") in the generated video sequences. Furthermore, Vid2World showed capability for counterfactual reasoning and implicitly captured environmental stochasticity, producing varied plausible outcomes for unobserved elements.

#### 4.3. Open-World Navigation (RECON Dataset)

- **Performance (Single-Step Prediction)**: In a setup where baselines perform single-step predictions of distant future frames, Vid2World, despite using autoregressive inference (which introduces error accumulation), achieved performance on par with NWM (1B) and surpassed NWM + Ego4D (1B) for four out of six metrics. This indicates the strength of the learned priors and generalization capabilities, even when operating beyond its training horizon.
- **Performance (Autoregressive Rollout)**: When compared in an autoregressive rollout setting, Vid2World consistently produced predictions that were superior or comparable to NWM and significantly outperformed DIAMOND baselines, highlighting its robustness against error accumulation in sequential generation.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- **Action Guidance**: The inclusion of causal action guidance consistently improved performance across evaluated metrics for both Extrapolative and Masked Weight Transfer methods, demonstrating its contribution to aligning generation with actions.
- **Weight Transfer Mechanisms**: Both Masked and Extrapolative Weight Transfer mechanisms yielded improved performance compared to the Shift Weight Transfer. Extrapolative Weight Transfer generally showed slightly better outcomes, indicating its effectiveness in preserving representations while enforcing causality.
- **Guidance Scale**: The causal action guidance scale (λ) influenced generation quality, with optimal performance observed at an intermediate scale. Excessive guidance led to degradation, consistent with the behavior of classifier-free guidance in general.
- **Role of Pretraining**: An additional experiment demonstrated that training the identical architecture from scratch resulted in substantially inferior performance compared to all Vid2World variants leveraging the pre-trained video diffusion model, underscoring the critical role of transferring large-scale visual priors.
- **Representational Preservation**: Evaluation via cosine similarity confirmed that the weight transfer mechanisms, especially extrapolative, largely preserved the representational structure of the original video diffusion model within the transformed world model.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a systematic approach to transforming passive video diffusion models into interactive world models, addressing key limitations in existing methodologies. The significance and potential impact of Vid2World are multifaceted:

1. **Advancement in World Model Development**: The research provides a general and effective framework (Vid2World) for leveraging pre-trained video diffusion models, which are trained on abundant internet-scale action-free video data, to create high-fidelity, action-conditioned, and autoregressive world models. This marks a shift from solely relying on expensive, domain-specific action-labeled data, potentially making world model development more scalable and accessible.

2. **Novel Technical Contributions**: The introduction of "video diffusion causalization" and "causal action guidance" represents novel technical contributions. The systematic exploration of weight transfer mechanisms for temporal convolution layers, particularly the "extrapolative weight transfer," and the adaptation of classifier-free guidance for frame-level action conditioning, provide concrete methods for bridging the gap between passive video generation and interactive world modeling.

3. **Establishment of New Benchmarks**: By achieving competitive or superior performance across diverse and challenging domains—robot manipulation, 3D game simulation, and open-world navigation—Vid2World sets new benchmarks for world modeling tasks. This quantitative improvement suggests that the approach can yield more accurate and visually realistic environment predictions.

4. **Support for Downstream Decision-Making**: The demonstration of Vid2World's ability to support "Real2Sim Policy Evaluation" indicates its practical utility in enabling agents to simulate and assess policy outcomes reliably. This capability can accelerate policy learning and evaluation by providing a high-fidelity simulated environment that reduces the need for extensive real-world experimentation.

5. **Enhanced Understanding of Generative Models**: The work contributes to a deeper understanding of how robust visual and physical priors learned by large-scale generative models can be transferred and repurposed for specific causal and interactive tasks, highlighting the potential for foundation models beyond their original training objectives.

**Potential Impact**:
The ability to craft interactive world models from readily available action-free video data can lead to more capable and generalized artificial agents. These agents could operate in more complex and dynamic environments, benefiting applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and synthetic data generation for training. The reduced dependency on costly labeled datasets democratizes access to advanced world modeling capabilities.

**Future Research Directions**:
The paper identifies several avenues for future work, including the exploration of even larger-scale video diffusion models as base architectures, development of more computationally efficient training and inference methods (as current inference latency remains a challenge), and investigation into novel model-based reinforcement learning algorithms that can effectively leverage these high-fidelity world models. Additionally, the development of improved quantitative metrics for evaluating the "interactivity" and "action semantics following" capabilities of world models is highlighted.
