# SafeBench: A Benchmarking Platform for Safety Evaluation of Autonomous Vehicles

> [!summary] Summary
> SafeBench is introduced as a unified benchmarking platform for autonomous vehicle safety evaluation, integrating 8 NHTSA-defined safety-critical pre-crash scenarios with 10 route variations, 4 scenario generation algorithms, and 4 deep reinforcement learning (DRL) AD algorithms. The platform utilizes 10 multi-level metrics for performance assessment and reveals performance discrepancies in AD systems between benign and safety-critical situations, alongside vulnerabilities in perception components to adversarial attacks.

## Problem

- Traditional autonomous driving (AD) evaluation requires extensive real-world testing (hundreds of millions of miles) to encounter rare safety-critical scenarios, which is economically and environmentally inefficient.
- Existing AD evaluation platforms and tools are fragmented, using different simulation environments, scenario definitions, generation methods, and metrics, which hinders standardized and fair comparisons of AD algorithms.
- Machine intelligence-enabled AD systems are vulnerable to adversarial manipulations and natural distribution shifts, necessitating comprehensive and robust safety evaluation before deployment.

## Method

- Developed SafeBench, a unified, modular platform built on the Carla simulator, integrating 8 NHTSA-defined safety-critical pre-crash scenarios with 10 diverse route variations to provide comprehensive test coverage.
- Incorporated four distinct scenario generation algorithms (Learning-to-collide, AdvSim, Carla Scenario Generator, Adversarial Trajectory Optimization) to efficiently create a diverse set of challenging and high-risk testing scenarios.
- Evaluated four deep reinforcement learning-based AD algorithms with varied perceptual inputs (e.g., 4D state, bird's-eye view, camera) using 10 multi-level metrics (Safety, Functionality, Etiquette) for detailed diagnostic reporting.

## Results

- The scenario selection process on SafeBench improved the collision rate of generated scenarios by approximately 30%, with AT achieving an 81.1% collision rate and CS demonstrating an 85.5% selection rate.
- AD algorithms consistently showed a significant performance gap, with overall scores dropping by up to 37.5% (e.g., TD3 from 0.830 to 0.518) between benign and safety-critical scenarios.
- All tested perception models were vulnerable to adversarial attacks, but multi-modal 3D object detection models (CLOCs) exhibited smaller performance drops compared to LiDAR-only models (SECOND), indicating improved robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Autonomous driving algorithms exhibit a substantial performance degradation when moving from benign driving conditions to safety-critical scenarios, exposing current limitations in handling hazardous situations.
> - Different scenario generation algorithms demonstrate varying effectiveness; Adversarial Trajectory Optimization (AT) generates the most challenging scenarios, while the Carla Scenario Generator (CS) produces highly transferable ones.
> - Multi-modal sensor fusion strategies (e.g., 4D+BEV input, multi-modal 3D object detection) contribute to enhanced AD system robustness against adversarial attacks and generally lead to improved safety performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of machine intelligence-enabled systems, particularly in safety-critical domains such such as autonomous driving (AD), has raised concerns due to their demonstrated vulnerability to adversarial manipulations and natural distribution shifts. Traditional methods for evaluating AD systems rely heavily on naturalistic driving, which demands hundreds of millions of miles of testing to encounter sufficiently diverse and rare safety-critical scenarios. This approach is costly, time-consuming, and environmentally intensive.

In response to these challenges, various approaches for autonomous driving evaluation have emerged, often leveraging simulation platforms. However, existing efforts are characterized by a fragmentation of design choices, including different simulation environments, varied definitions and generation methods for safety-critical scenarios, diverse scenario generation algorithms, and inconsistencies in driving route variations and evaluation metrics. This heterogeneity impedes a standardized, fair, and efficient comparison of the effectiveness and efficiency of different AD algorithms and testing mechanisms.

Prior research categorizes existing AD algorithm evaluation into three types:
- **Data-driven generation and testing:** These methods focus on sampling real-world data and estimating distribution density to model actual driving conditions. While providing realism, they require extensive data collection to capture rare safety-critical events.
- **Adversary-based generation and testing:** These approaches model surrounding agents (e.g., vehicles, pedestrians) as adversarial entities to intentionally create challenging driving scenarios.
- **Knowledge-based generation and testing:** These methods integrate domain knowledge, such as traffic rules, as constraints to guide the scenario generation process.

While the latter two categories, adversary-based and knowledge-based methods, have shown efficiency and effectiveness in specific environments, they still suffer from the lack of a unified platform. Different platforms utilize distinct AD algorithms, sensor configurations, and evaluation criteria, making direct comparison challenging. This work addresses this gap by aiming to provide a consolidated framework that integrates these disparate elements, thereby establishing a standardized benchmark for autonomous vehicle safety evaluation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for SafeBench stems from the recognized challenges in evaluating the safety and robustness of Autonomous Driving (AD) algorithms. The increasing complexity of machine learning (ML) models used in AD necessitates comprehensive and quantitative evaluation before their large-scale deployment. The paper outlines several key challenges that SafeBench aims to address:

- **Rarity of Safety-Critical Scenarios:** Safety-critical events are infrequent in real-world driving, occurring roughly every 30,000 miles. This rarity mandates millions of miles of testing, leading to substantial economic and environmental costs. Existing accelerated evaluation methods, such as importance sampling (IS) and cross-entropy (CE), have shown limitations in dealing with the increasing complexity of ML algorithms, sometimes underestimating risk. There is a need for methods that can efficiently generate and evaluate these rare, yet critical, scenarios.
- **Lack of Unified Evaluation Standards:** Current AD evaluation tools and platforms are often developed in isolation, each with its own design choices regarding dataset selection, safety-critical scenario definitions, scenario generation algorithms, evaluation metrics, and input types for AD agents. This fragmentation makes it difficult to conduct fair comparisons between different AD algorithms or to interpret evaluation results consistently across various studies.
- **Need for Comprehensive Coverage:** An effective evaluation platform should cover a wide array of known safety-critical scenarios and accommodate various scenario generation techniques to thoroughly explore an AD system's vulnerabilities. It should also account for environmental and route variations to ensure generalizability.

Given these motivations, the key objectives of the SafeBench platform are:

- **To provide the first unified platform:** Integrate different types of safety-critical testing scenarios, scenario generation algorithms, and variations in driving routes and environments into a single, cohesive framework.
- **To offer comprehensive scenario coverage:** Incorporate 8 National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA)-defined safety-critical pre-crash scenarios, each with 10 diverse driving route variations, ensuring a broad testing landscape.
- **To integrate multiple scenario generation algorithms:** Develop and include four distinct scenario generation algorithms (Learning-to-collide, AdvSim, Carla Scenario Generator, Adversarial Trajectory Optimization) to assess AD algorithms against a range of adversarial and knowledge-based challenges.
- **To enable fair comparison of AD algorithms:** Implement four deep reinforcement learning (DRL)-based AD algorithms with four types of perceptual inputs (e.g., 4D state, bird's-eye view, camera) to facilitate direct and controlled comparisons under standardized conditions.
- **To establish diverse safety measurement metrics:** Utilize 10 evaluation metrics categorized into three levels—Safety, Functionality, and Etiquette—to provide a detailed and multi-faceted diagnostic report on AD performance.
- **To offer a flexible and modularized design:** Construct the platform with a modular architecture that allows for easy replacement, addition, or modification of components, facilitating future research and development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SafeBench platform is designed to provide a unified and systematic approach to evaluating autonomous vehicle safety. It is built upon the open-source Carla simulator and operates within a Docker container, utilizing ROS for inter-module communication.

**Platform Structure:**
SafeBench consists of four interconnected modules:
- **Ego Vehicle:** This module provides the virtual vehicle environment, including sensor configurations (LiDAR, Camera, Radar), global planner, and vehicle appearance. It serves as the deployment site for the AD algorithms under test.
- **Agent Node:** Responsible for training and managing AD algorithms for both the ego vehicle and surrounding traffic participants. It processes observation data from scenarios and outputs control signals.
- **Scenario Node:** The core component for organizing and generating testing scenarios. It dictates the behaviors of traffic participants (e.g., pedestrians, surrounding vehicles) and static environmental elements (e.g., road layouts, traffic light states).
- **Evaluation Node:** This module collects real-time data during testing, providing comprehensive evaluations of AD algorithms across diverse generated scenarios based on a defined set of metrics.

**Safety-Critical Testing Scenarios:**
The platform integrates 8 distinct pre-crash safety-critical scenarios, derived from National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) research, which represent common accident precursors. These scenarios include: Straight Obstacle, Turning Obstacle, Lane Changing, Vehicle Passing, Red-light Running, Unprotected Left-turn, Right-turn, and Crossing Negotiation. To ensure robust evaluation, 10 diverse driving routes are developed for each scenario. These routes vary in environmental features such as the number of lanes, presence of intersections, T-junctions, bridges, and different road signs, aiming to test generalization capabilities.

**Safety-Critical Scenario Generation Algorithms:**
For each driving route, SafeBench employs four distinct algorithms for generating varied testing samples, broadly categorized into adversary-based and knowledge-based methods:
- **Adversary-based Generation:**
 - **Learning-to-collide (LC):** A black-box algorithm that optimizes the initial poses of traffic participants (e.g., cyclists) to induce collisions with the AD algorithm. It formulates scenarios as autoregressive building blocks and uses the policy gradient method REINFORCE for optimization. The method was adapted to apply to all 8 scenarios.
 - **AdvSim (AS):** This algorithm manipulates existing trajectories to perturb the driving paths of surrounding vehicles (SVs), thereby creating hazardous situations. It utilizes the kinematic bicycle model to represent SV trajectories and employs Bayesian Optimization for trajectory parameter optimization.
- **Knowledge-based Generation:**
 - **Carla Scenario Generator (CS):** A module integrated within the Carla Simulator that uses rule-based methods to construct testing scenarios. It adopts standard rules and employs a grid search approach to generate safety-critical scenario parameters across all 8 traffic scenarios.
 - **Adversarial Trajectory Optimization (AT):** This method incorporates explicit domain knowledge, such as traffic rules and physical laws, as constraints to guide the scenario optimization process. It utilizes a PSO-based black-box optimization strategy to generate various testing scenarios while adhering to these constraints.

Initially, 3,140 testing scenarios are generated. A scenario selection process is then applied, retaining only scenarios that cause collisions for at least 2 out of 4 tested AD algorithms, resulting in 2,352 high-risk, transferable testing scenarios.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
Performance is assessed across 10 metrics, grouped into three levels:
- **Safety Level:** Measures serious traffic rule violations. Metrics include Collision Rate (CR), Frequency of Running Red Lights (RR), Frequency of Running Stop Signs (SS), and Average Distance Driven Out of Road (OR).
- **Functionality Level:** Evaluates the AD agent's ability to complete its driving task. Metrics include Route Following Stability (RF), Average Percentage of Route Completion (Comp), and Average Time Spent to Complete the Route (TS).
- **Etiquette Level:** Assesses driving quality and smoothness. Metrics include Average Acceleration (ACC), Average Yaw Velocity (YV), and Frequency of Lane Invasion (LI).
An **Overall Score (OS)** is calculated as a weighted sum of these 10 metrics, with higher weights assigned to safety-critical metrics.

**AD Algorithms Tested:**
SafeBench evaluates four deep reinforcement learning (DRL) algorithms: Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG), and Twin Delayed DDPG (TD3). These algorithms are trained with varied perceptual capabilities, corresponding to four input types:
- **4D:** Basic observation (distance to waypoint, longitude speed, angular speed, front-vehicle detection).
- **4D+Dir:** 4D input augmented with 7 dimensions (command, direction vectors of ego vehicle, current waypoint, target waypoint).
- **4D+BEV:** 4D input combined with a bird’s-eye view (BEV) semantic map rendered from CARLA information.
- **4D+Cam:** 4D input combined with an image from a front-facing camera.
Training occurs in Carla town03, using a consistent reward function and action space mapping to Carla's vehicle controls.

**Robustness Evaluation on Perception Components:**
The modular design allows for evaluating the robustness of AD system components. SafeBench conducts physical semantic attacks on perception, specifically:
- **Point Cloud Segmentation:** Three types of adversarial attacks (Point Attack, Pose Attack, Scene Attack) are applied to four segmentation models (PointNet++, SqueezeSegV3, PolarSeg, Cylinder3D) pre-trained on SemanticKitti.
- **3D Object Detection:** Adversarial physical semantic transformations (changing vehicle types, colors, pedestrian shapes, skin colors, and object rotations) are applied to LiDAR-only (SECOND) and multi-modal (CLOCs) 3D object detection models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation conducted on SafeBench yielded several findings regarding scenario generation effectiveness, AD algorithm performance, and the robustness of perception components.

**Scenario Generation Effectiveness:**
- **Increased Collision Rate:** The scenario selection process, which filters for scenarios causing collisions in at least two AD algorithms, demonstrated an increase in the collision rate (CR) of the retained scenarios. For instance, the LC algorithm's scenarios showed a CR improvement of approximately 30% after selection.
- **Effectiveness of AT:** Adversarial Trajectory Optimization (AT) was identified as the most effective algorithm for generating challenging scenarios, achieving a high initial CR of 73.4% (increasing to 81.1% post-selection) and the lowest overall score (OS) of 0.546 (decreasing to 0.508 post-selection), indicating its ability to create highly safety-critical situations.
- **Transferability of CS:** The Carla Scenario Generator (CS) demonstrated the highest selection rate (SR) of 85.5%, indicating high transferability of its generated scenarios across different AD algorithms.
- **Most Challenging Scenario:** Among the 8 pre-crash scenarios, "Vehicle Passing" consistently resulted in the highest collision rate and lowest overall score, suggesting it is the most difficult scenario for AD algorithms to navigate safely.

**AD Performance on Benign vs. Safety-Critical Scenarios:**
- **Significant Performance Gap:** A substantial performance gap was observed between AD algorithms tested on benign scenarios and those tested on SafeBench's safety-critical scenarios. For example, TD3, which achieved an overall score of 0.830 on benign scenarios, scored only 0.518 on safety-critical ones. This consistent drop across various algorithms and input types indicates the effectiveness of SafeBench in exposing vulnerabilities.
- **Performance Trade-off:** The study identified a trade-off between an AD agent's performance in benign and safety-critical scenarios. Agents excelling in benign conditions often showed reduced performance in critical situations. For instance, PPO obtained the highest overall score in safety-critical scenarios, while SAC achieved the highest score in benign scenarios but performed worst in safety-critical ones.

**Comprehensive Diagnostic Report:**
The detailed diagnostic reports, considering Safety, Functionality, and Etiquette levels, revealed that different AD algorithms exhibited strengths in specific areas:
- **Safety Level:** PPO, particularly with 4D+BEV input, generally demonstrated high safety performance with the lowest collision rate (CR) and average distance driven out of road (OR).
- **Functionality Level:** TD3 exhibited high route following stability (RF), indicating its capability to adhere to predefined routes.
- **Etiquette Level:** SAC and DDPG generally showed better driving etiquette, with lower average acceleration (ACC) and yaw velocity (YV) respectively.
- **Overall Score:** PPO consistently achieved the highest overall scores across different input types, with PPO using 4D+BEV input attaining the best OS of 0.694 on safety-critical scenarios.
- **Trade-off in Metrics:** A trade-off between functionality and safety metrics was observed. Agents prioritizing faster route completion (e.g., SAC with best TS score) sometimes exhibited higher collision rates. Similarly, agents with high route completion (e.g., PPO) sometimes had higher frequencies of running red lights and stop signs.

**Impact of Input Modality:**
- The performance drop between benign and safety-critical scenarios was consistent across all input types (4D, 4D+Dir, 4D+BEV, 4D+Cam), confirming the generalizability of SafeBench's testing scenarios.
- AD algorithms utilizing 4D+BEV input generally achieved lower collision rates compared to those with other input types, suggesting that bird’s-eye view information significantly contributes to safer driving.

**Robustness Evaluation on Perception Components:**
- **Point Cloud Segmentation:** All four tested point cloud segmentation models (PointNet++, SqueezeSegV3, PolarSeg, Cylinder3D) were vulnerable to Point, Pose, and Scene attacks, experiencing varying degrees of Intersection over Union (IoU) degradation. This indicates that current perception models are susceptible to adversarial manipulations.
- **3D Object Detection:** Adversarial physical semantic transformations caused a drop in average precision (AP) for both LiDAR-only (SECOND) and multi-modal (CLOCs) 3D object detection models. Notably, the multi-modal CLOCs model exhibited a smaller performance drop than the LiDAR-only SECOND model under adversarial conditions. This suggests that fusing data from multiple sensors can enhance the robustness of perception systems in AD.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SafeBench provides a novel contribution to the field of autonomous driving research and development by introducing a unified, comprehensive, and modular platform for evaluating the safety and robustness of AD algorithms. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Standardized Benchmarking:** SafeBench addresses the fragmentation in AD evaluation by offering a standardized platform. This enables researchers and practitioners to conduct fair and consistent comparisons of different AD algorithms, scenario generation techniques, and sensor configurations under similar and controlled conditions. Such standardization can accelerate the iterative development and refinement of AD systems.
- **Enhanced Testing Efficiency and Effectiveness:** By integrating 8 NHTSA-defined safety-critical scenarios with 10 route variations and 4 generation algorithms, SafeBench facilitates the efficient creation of a vast number of challenging test cases (2,352 selected scenarios). This focused generation of rare, high-risk scenarios significantly improves testing efficiency compared to traditional real-world mileage accumulation, which can be prohibitively expensive and time-consuming.
- **Detailed Diagnostic Capabilities:** The platform's 10 evaluation metrics, organized across Safety, Functionality, and Etiquette levels, provide a granular diagnostic report for AD algorithms. This detailed feedback can help identify specific weaknesses and areas for improvement in various aspects of AD performance, from accident avoidance to driving smoothness and adherence to traffic rules.
- **Motivation for Algorithm Development:** The observed performance gaps between benign and safety-critical scenarios, as well as the trade-offs between different performance metrics, highlight inherent deficiencies in current AD algorithms. SafeBench is anticipated to motivate the development of more robust, safer AD algorithms that can perform reliably under a wider range of challenging conditions. It also encourages the creation of more sophisticated and effective testing scenario generation algorithms.
- **Guidance for AD System Design:** Findings from SafeBench, such as the improved safety performance with 4D+BEV input and the enhanced robustness of multi-modal perception models against adversarial attacks, provide empirical insights that can guide the design of future AD systems, suggesting beneficial architectural choices and sensor fusion strategies.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The modular and extensible design of SafeBench allows for the integration of new AD algorithms, additional safety-critical scenarios, novel scenario generation techniques, and diverse sensor modalities. This flexibility establishes a foundation for ongoing research into AD safety, including the evaluation of commercial AD systems beyond the current RL-based focus.

While acknowledging that simulation cannot fully replicate real-world conditions and track testing remains essential, SafeBench offers a robust pre-deployment evaluation tool. The authors also address potential negative societal impacts by highlighting the dual-use nature of the platform – it can be used to expose vulnerabilities by malicious actors, but its primary intent is to enable developers to proactively identify and mitigate these weaknesses, ultimately contributing to the safer deployment of autonomous vehicles.
