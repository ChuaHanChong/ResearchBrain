# SafeBench: A Benchmarking Platform for Safety Evaluation of Autonomous Vehicles

> [!summary] Summary
> SafeBench is introduced as a unified benchmarking platform for autonomous vehicle safety evaluation, integrating 8 NHTSA-defined safety-critical pre-crash scenarios with 10 route variations, 4 scenario generation algorithms, and 4 deep reinforcement learning (DRL) AD algorithms. The platform utilizes 10 multi-level metrics for performance assessment and reveals performance discrepancies in AD systems between benign and safety-critical situations, alongside vulnerabilities in perception components to adversarial attacks.

## Problem

- Traditional autonomous driving (AD) evaluation requires extensive real-world testing (hundreds of millions of miles) to encounter rare safety-critical scenarios, which is economically and environmentally inefficient.
- Existing AD evaluation platforms and tools are fragmented, using different simulation environments, scenario definitions, generation methods, and metrics, which hinders standardized and fair comparisons of AD algorithms.
- Machine intelligence-enabled AD systems are vulnerable to adversarial manipulations and natural distribution shifts, necessitating comprehensive and robust safety evaluation before deployment.

## Method

- Developed SafeBench, a unified, modular platform built on the Carla simulator, integrating 8 NHTSA-defined safety-critical pre-crash scenarios with 10 diverse route variations to provide comprehensive test coverage.
- Incorporated four distinct scenario generation algorithms (Learning-to-collide, AdvSim, Carla Scenario Generator, Adversarial Trajectory Optimization) to efficiently create a diverse set of challenging and high-risk testing scenarios.
- Evaluated four deep reinforcement learning-based AD algorithms with varied perceptual inputs (e.g., 4D state, bird's-eye view, camera) using 10 multi-level metrics (Safety, Functionality, Etiquette) for detailed diagnostic reporting.

## Results

- The scenario selection process on SafeBench improved the collision rate of generated scenarios by approximately 30%, with AT achieving an 81.1% collision rate and CS demonstrating an 85.5% selection rate.
- AD algorithms consistently showed a significant performance gap, with overall scores dropping by up to 37.5% (e.g., TD3 from 0.830 to 0.518) between benign and safety-critical scenarios.
- All tested perception models were vulnerable to adversarial attacks, but multi-modal 3D object detection models (CLOCs) exhibited smaller performance drops compared to LiDAR-only models (SECOND), indicating improved robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Autonomous driving algorithms exhibit a substantial performance degradation when moving from benign driving conditions to safety-critical scenarios, exposing current limitations in handling hazardous situations.
> - Different scenario generation algorithms demonstrate varying effectiveness; Adversarial Trajectory Optimization (AT) generates the most challenging scenarios, while the Carla Scenario Generator (CS) produces highly transferable ones.
> - Multi-modal sensor fusion strategies (e.g., 4D+BEV input, multi-modal 3D object detection) contribute to enhanced AD system robustness against adversarial attacks and generally lead to improved safety performance.
