# CoLLMLight: Cooperative Large Language Model Agents for Network-Wide Traffic Signal Control

> [!summary] Summary
> CoLLMLight, developed by The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou), proposes a cooperative Large Language Model (LLM) agent framework for network-wide traffic signal control. It integrates spatiotemporal awareness, complexity-aware reasoning, and simulation-driven fine-tuning to achieve superior generalization and efficiency, outperforming existing methods on various real-world datasets.

## Problem

- Existing LLM-based traffic signal control (TSC) methods focus on localized decision-making, lacking crucial inter-agent coordination for network-wide traffic optimization.
- Traditional and Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based TSC approaches struggle with generalization to dynamic and unseen traffic conditions, despite advancements in cooperative control.
- Deploying computationally intensive generalist LLMs for real-time TSC faces challenges in balancing decision-making accuracy with the required computational efficiency.

## Method

- A ==cooperative LLM agent== framework is proposed that models spatiotemporal relationships and temporal dynamics to enable network-wide decision-making among intersections.
- A complexity-aware reasoning mechanism dynamically adjusts the LLM's reasoning depth based on real-time congestion risk, optimizing computational resources for varying traffic scenarios.
- A simulation-driven fine-tuning approach uses iterative data generation with GPT-4o for pseudo-labeling and environmental feedback for policy refinement, creating a lightweight and specialized LLM.

## Results

- CoLLMLight achieves state-of-the-art or comparable performance in zero-shot evaluations across seven real-world datasets (Jinan, Hangzhou, New York), consistently outperforming traditional, RL-based, and existing LLM-based methods.
- The framework demonstrates superior generalization, evidenced by an up to **25.17%** reduction in Average Waiting Time (AWT) on complex networks like New York 1, showcasing effective cooperative control.
- Ablation studies confirm the critical role of complexity-aware reasoning and the two-stage simulation-driven fine-tuning process, with optimized performance achieved by balancing historical information and communication thresholds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly incorporating spatiotemporal awareness and inter-agent cooperation in LLM-based systems is critical for effective network-wide traffic signal control.
> - Dynamically adapting the reasoning complexity of LLM agents significantly improves both efficiency and performance, allowing for optimal resource utilization in real-time control tasks.
> - A multi-stage fine-tuning process, combining reasoning chain optimization with iterative environmental feedback from simulations, is effective in building specialized and robust LLMs for complex control environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Traffic Signal Control (TSC) is a cornerstone of urban traffic management, constantly evolving to combat congestion and optimize flow. Historically, TSC methods began with **traditional transportation-based approaches** like FixedTime and MaxPressure, which rely on predefined cycles or real-time heuristics. While foundational, these methods often struggle with the dynamic and complex nature of modern urban traffic, requiring extensive manual calibration and lacking generalization capabilities.

The past decade saw a significant shift towards **data-driven methods**, particularly those leveraging Reinforcement Learning (RL). RL-based approaches learn optimal control policies through continuous interaction with the traffic environment, capturing intricate multi-level embeddings of traffic dynamics. To tackle network-wide optimization, recent RL research integrated **Graph Neural Networks (GNNs)**, enabling agents to aggregate spatial information from neighboring intersections and achieve cooperative control, a significant advancement over localized RL. Examples include CoLight and Advanced-CoLight. However, a persistent challenge for RL remains its generalization ability to unseen or highly variable traffic conditions, often due to reliance on limited training data.

The advent of **Large Language Models (LLMs)** has introduced a new paradigm, demonstrating exceptional problem-solving and generalization capabilities across various domains. Recognizing this potential, researchers have recently explored LLMs for TSC, as exemplified by projects like LLMLight. These initial LLM-based TSC approaches showcased the models' ability to perform human-like reasoning and handle complex tasks. However, a critical limitation quickly became apparent: existing LLM-based methods primarily focused on *localized decision-making* for individual intersections, largely neglecting the crucial aspect of **inter-agent coordination** necessary for effective network-wide traffic optimization. This gap is significant, as the paper's introductory figure clearly illustrates that both RL and LLM-based methods benefit substantially from cross-intersection collaboration.

CoLLMLight directly addresses this critical void. It aims to bridge the gap by endowing LLM agents with the capability for network-wide cooperative control, a feature previously more robustly handled by sophisticated RL-GNN architectures. By introducing explicit mechanisms for spatial and temporal awareness and inter-agent coordination, CoLLMLight seeks to combine the strengths of LLMs (generalization, reasoning) with the necessity of cooperative action, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in LLM-based TSC. Furthermore, the paper implicitly contributes to the broader field of **multi-agent LLM systems**. While other multi-agent LLM frameworks exist for domains like software development and robotics, many rely on iterative communication and consensus-building, which can be computationally intensive and too slow for real-time traffic control. CoLLMLight proposes a more efficient cooperative agent model where agents independently analyze collaborators' dynamics and make decisions that optimize global traffic, offering a distinct approach to multi-agent coordination.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop CoLLMLight, a cooperative Large Language Model (LLM) agent framework for network-wide traffic signal control (TSC)**. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations, stemming from the limitations of existing TSC methodologies:

1. **Addressing the Lack of Inter-Agent Coordination in LLM-based TSC:** Existing LLM-based approaches for TSC, while demonstrating impressive reasoning and generalization capabilities, largely operate in isolation, focusing on localized decision-making at individual intersections. This neglects the fundamental requirement for cross-intersection collaboration, which is crucial for alleviating congestion and achieving global traffic optimization across an entire road network. CoLLMLight aims to imbue LLM agents with the ability to reason about and act upon the intricate dependencies among neighboring intersections.
2. **Enabling LLMs to Comprehend Complex Traffic Interactions:** To facilitate effective cooperation, LLM agents need to understand more than just local conditions. This involves two sub-challenges:
 - **Modeling Spatial Relationships:** Identifying lane connectivity and inter-dependencies between intersections.
 - **Reasoning about Dynamic Temporal Interactions:** Understanding how congestion propagates and queue spillbacks occur in real-time. Crucially, agents must predict how their local actions will impact not only their assigned intersection but also adjacent ones, ensuring network-wide improvements.
3. **Balancing Computational Efficiency and Decision-Making Accuracy:** Deploying computationally intensive LLM reasoning uniformly across all traffic scenarios is impractical for real-time applications. Traffic conditions vary drastically, from lightly congested intersections requiring simple adjustments to heavily congested areas demanding sophisticated, deep reasoning. The motivation is to avoid wasting computational resources on simple cases while ensuring adequate reasoning for complex situations, thus striking an optimal balance between efficiency and decision quality.
4. **Developing a Lightweight and Tailored LLM for Cooperative TSC:** Generalist LLMs can be large and inefficient for specialized, real-time control tasks. The goal is to fine-tune a smaller LLM to become highly proficient in cooperative TSC, leveraging domain-specific knowledge and continuous environmental feedback to enhance decision accuracy and operational efficiency.

The authors summarize their contributions as:
- Proposing CoLLMLight, the first work to integrate LLM cooperation into TSC.
- Designing a complexity-aware reasoning mechanism that dynamically adapts reasoning depth based on real-time traffic conditions.
- Proposing an LLM fine-tuning method for cooperative TSC, utilizing iterative simulation and environmental feedback.
- Validating the framework's effectiveness, scalability, and robustness through extensive experiments on diverse datasets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CoLLMLight introduces a comprehensive cooperative LLM agent framework structured around three core components: Spatiotemporal-Aware Cooperative Decision-Making, Complexity-Aware Reasoning, and Simulation-Driven Fine-Tuning.

#### 3.1. Spatiotemporal-Aware Cooperative Decision-Making

This component empowers LLM agents to understand and act upon the spatial and temporal dynamics of traffic across a network.

1. **Observation Collection:** At each timestep, lane-level observations are collected, including the number of queued vehicles ($n_{queue}$), moving vehicles ($n_{move}$), average waiting time ($\tau$), and traffic occupancy ($\rho$). These granular observations are then aggregated to form comprehensive local and neighboring observations ($O_t$) for the agent's assigned intersection and its adjacent ones.
2. **Spatiotemporal Modeling:**
 - **Spatial Relationships:** A directed subgraph $G=(V, L)$ is constructed for each intersection, explicitly representing the connectivity between lanes and intersections, crucial for understanding how traffic flows between them.
 - **Temporal Dynamics:** Historical traffic interactions are captured over a fixed time window $\Delta t$. This history $T_t = \{(O_t^i, a_t^i) | t - \Delta t < t^i < t\}$ includes past observations and the corresponding signal configurations applied at both the assigned and neighboring intersections.
3. **Cooperative Decision-Making:** The collected and modeled information ($O_t$, $G$, $T_t$), along with task instructions $D$ (e.g., preventing downstream congestion, adjusting to upstream flow), is formatted into a human-readable prompt (X) for the LLM. The LLM then follows a detailed Chain-of-Thought (CoT) process:
 - **Analyze Current Traffic Conditions:** It first analyzes the current state at both the assigned and neighboring intersections.
 - **Predict Future Traffic States:** Based on the current analysis, it predicts future traffic states for neighboring intersections under each possible signal configuration ($a \in A$).
 - **Select Optimal Signal:** Finally, it selects the signal configuration ($\hat{a}$) that is predicted to optimize network-wide traffic flow.

#### 3.2. Complexity-Aware Reasoning

To address the trade-off between computational efficiency and decision accuracy, CoLLMLight introduces a complexity-aware reasoning mechanism. It dynamically assesses the decision-making complexity at each intersection and adapts its reasoning depth accordingly. The complexity is classified based on a **congestion risk coefficient ($n_c$)**, which represents the number of critical neighboring lanes that are either congested or at risk.

1. **No Cooperation ($n_c = 0$):** When no critical neighboring lanes are congested, the intersection can operate independently. The LLM directly selects the optimal signal without detailed traffic analysis or future state prediction, minimizing computational overhead.
2. **Simple Cooperation ($n_c = 1$):** If exactly one neighboring lane shows high traffic occupancy, the agent performs a local spatiotemporal analysis of dependencies. However, future traffic state prediction is excluded to maintain efficiency, as the congestion is contained.
3. **Complex Cooperation ($n_c > 1$):** For scenarios with multiple highly congested neighboring lanes, the agent engages in the full cooperative decision-making process, including comprehensive traffic analysis, future traffic state prediction, and systematic signal comparison to account for multi-hop effects and extensive spatiotemporal dependencies.

The LLM is explicitly instructed to identify $n_c$ and select the appropriate reasoning strategy.

#### 3.3. Simulation-Driven Fine-Tuning

This component aims to enhance the LLM's reasoning capability and decision-making accuracy specifically for cooperative TSC, building a lightweight model through an iterative process.

1. **Reasoning Chain Optimization:**
 - **Data Generation:** The CityFlow traffic simulator is used with a synthetic traffic flow dataset (Syn-Train) to generate diverse traffic scenarios.
 - **Pseudo-Labeling with GPT-4o:** GPT-4o, a powerful generalist LLM, is leveraged to generate high-quality pseudo-labels for training. It summarizes current traffic conditions to derive the congestion risk ($n_c$) and traffic analysis ($Y_{ana}$).
 - **Future State Prediction Labels:** The simulator itself is used to roll out different signal configurations and collect the true next-step traffic states ($O_a^{t+1}$), providing ground truth for future state prediction.
 - **Optimal Signal ($a^*$):** The "pseudo-golden signal" ($a^*$) is identified by simulating various actions and selecting the one that minimizes the total number of queued vehicles over a five-timestep horizon in neighboring intersections.
 - **Supervised Fine-Tuning:** The LLM is then fine-tuned via supervised learning, minimizing the negative log-likelihood of generating these synthesized reasoning chains (encompassing $n_c$, $Y_{ana}$, predicted $O_a^{t+1}$, and $a^*$).

2. **Refinement with Environment Feedback:** After initial reasoning chain optimization, the LLM's decisions may still be suboptimal. An iterative refinement process is introduced:
 - **Environmental Evaluation:** The LLM's decisions are simulated, and their effectiveness is evaluated using an environment feedback function $Q$. $Q$ is defined as the inverse of the overall queue length at neighboring intersections after five timesteps, rewarding lower congestion.
 - **Policy Refinement:** The reasoning chain associated with the signal configuration yielding the highest environmental feedback is designated as a "golden pseudo reasoning chain" for further fine-tuning. This iterative process allows the model to continuously improve its decision-making by interacting with and learning from the traffic environment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted on both synthetic (Syn-Train) and seven real-world datasets (Jinan, Hangzhou, New York) validate CoLLMLight's effectiveness, efficiency, and generalizability across diverse traffic scenarios.

#### 4.1. Comparative Performance (RQ1)

- **Superior Generalization:** CoLLMLight consistently achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) or comparable performance against all baselines in zero-shot evaluations on real-world datasets. This highlights its ability to effectively adapt learned policies to varied and unseen traffic conditions.
- **Outperforming Traditional and RL-based Methods:** The framework significantly outperforms traditional methods (FixedTime, MaxPressure) and a wide range of RL-based approaches (MPLight, AttendLight, PressLight, CoLight, Efficient-CoLight, Advanced-CoLight). While RL methods like Advanced-CoLight perform well on training data (Syn-Train), they struggle with generalization to real-world datasets, a common limitation CoLLMLight overcomes.
- **Advancing LLM-based TSC:** CoLLMLight notably improves upon existing LLM-based methods like LLMLight. While LLMLight shows promise in simpler scenarios (e.g., Jinan 1), its localized decision-making limits its effectiveness in complex coordination scenarios, particularly in the New York dataset. CoLLMLight's cooperative approach addresses this, achieving, for instance, a 25.17% reduction in Average Waiting Time (AWT) for New York 1 compared to the second-best performance.
- **Efficiency over Generalist LLMs:** Even when integrated with generalist LLMs (Llama 3.1, Qwen 2.5, Deepseek R1) within the CoLLMLight framework, the fine-tuned CoLLMLight-8B model often outperforms these larger, more resource-intensive models, especially in high-complexity environments like New York. This demonstrates the efficiency and domain-specificity gained through its design and fine-tuning.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study (RQ2)

- **Impact of Complexity-Aware Reasoning:** The ablation study on reasoning strategies (No Cooperation - NC, Simple Cooperation - SR, Complex Cooperation - CR) confirms the efficacy of CoLLMLight's dynamic approach. Variants relying on a single reasoning strategy or only two strategies show compromised performance or efficiency. CoLLMLight, by intelligently identifying cooperation complexity and switching between tailored strategies, achieves superior performance while optimizing computational cost. For example, "w/o SR" performs well in New York (complex) but poorly in Jinan/Hangzhou (simpler), demonstrating the need for adaptable reasoning.
- **Importance of Optimization Stages:** Both stages of the Simulation-Driven Fine-Tuning are critical for performance:
 - **Reasoning Chain Optimization (w/o RO variant):** Omitting this stage leads to performance degradation, particularly in complex scenarios like New York. This indicates that a foundational, structured reasoning process is essential for the LLM to form effective control strategies. Direct refinement without this base can even worsen performance due to a lack of fundamental comprehension.
 - **Policy Refinement with Environmental Feedback (w/o PR variant):** While reasoning chain optimization provides a strong foundation (performing well in simpler networks like Jinan and Hangzhou), it is insufficient for highly complex environments like New York. Policy refinement, through iterative interaction with the environment, significantly boosts performance and generalization, bridging the gap between imitation learning and optimal control.

#### 4.3. Analysis of Spatiotemporal Information (RQ3)

- **Neighboring Information (Communication Threshold $\alpha$):**
 - Aggregating too much information ($\alpha=0$, considering all neighboring lanes) leads to higher processing times and potential errors due to noise.
 - An optimal threshold (found to be $\alpha=0.5$ in experiments) effectively filters out unimportant lanes, allowing the model to focus on critical congested areas, thereby improving both performance and efficiency.
 - Setting $\alpha$ too high risks disregarding critical lanes, leading to decreased performance.
- **Historical Information (Window Size $\Delta t$):**
 - Ignoring historical information completely ($\Delta t=0$) results in the lowest performance, underscoring the importance of temporal dynamics.
 - Even a small historical window ($\Delta t=1$) yields significant performance improvements.
 - While valuable, increasing $\Delta t$ linearly increases computational overhead. Crucially, performance does not continuously improve with larger $\Delta t$; it can degrade when the window becomes too large (e.g., >5 or 10 timesteps), likely due to increased complexity and noise in the input context. An optimal $\Delta t=5$ was identified, balancing performance and efficiency.

In summary, CoLLMLight robustly addresses the challenges of cooperative TSC with LLMs by intelligently incorporating spatiotemporal awareness, adapting reasoning complexity, and employing a sophisticated simulation-driven fine-tuning process. Its superior performance, efficiency, and generalization capabilities make it a promising solution for real-world urban traffic management.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CoLLMLight represents a significant advancement in the field of intelligent transportation systems and the application of Large Language Models (LLMs) to real-world control problems. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Pioneering Cooperative LLM Agents for TSC:** This work is groundbreaking as it introduces the first framework to explicitly integrate multi-agent cooperation into LLM-based traffic signal control. By moving beyond localized decision-making, CoLLMLight addresses a critical limitation of previous LLM applications in this domain, paving the way for truly network-wide optimization strategies.
2. **Enhancing Traffic Management Effectiveness:** The demonstrated superior performance of CoLLMLight, particularly in complex and large-scale urban scenarios like the New York dataset, signifies its potential to significantly reduce average travel and waiting times. This translates directly to reduced urban congestion, lower fuel consumption, decreased emissions, and improved quality of life for city residents. Its robustness and generalization capabilities across diverse real-world datasets indicate a high potential for practical deployment.
3. **Balancing Performance with Computational Efficiency:** The novel complexity-aware reasoning mechanism is a key innovation. By dynamically adjusting the LLM's reasoning depth based on real-time traffic conditions, CoLLMLight offers a practical solution to the inherent computational cost of LLMs. This intelligent resource allocation ensures that the system is both effective in complex situations and efficient enough for real-time responsiveness, a crucial requirement for dynamic traffic environments.
4. **Advancing LLM Fine-tuning for Real-world Control:** The proposed simulation-driven fine-tuning approach, incorporating iterative data collection and environmental feedback, is highly impactful. It provides a robust methodology for developing lightweight, domain-specific LLMs tailored for specific control tasks. This approach helps overcome the limitations of large, generalist LLMs in terms of efficiency and domain specificity, making advanced LLM capabilities accessible for practical, real-time applications where precision and speed are paramount.
5. **Broadening the Scope of Multi-Agent LLM Systems:** Beyond TSC, CoLLMLight contributes to the broader research landscape of multi-agent LLM systems. It offers an effective model for cooperation where agents independently analyze collaborators' dynamics and make decisions that optimize global objectives, without relying on computationally expensive, iterative inter-agent communication. This approach could inspire similar frameworks in other domains requiring real-time, coordinated decision-making.
6. **Future Directions in Intelligent Transportation:** The paper opens exciting avenues for future research, particularly the exploration of hierarchical asynchronous Multi-Agent LLM frameworks. This could involve regional-level planning guiding local intersection agents, leading to even more sophisticated and scalable urban traffic management systems. Such advancements could transform how smart cities manage their transportation networks, moving towards more adaptive, intelligent, and sustainable mobility solutions.

In conclusion, CoLLMLight is a pivotal contribution that not only pushes the boundaries of traffic signal control but also expands the practical applicability and efficiency of large language models in complex, multi-agent control environments. Its impact could be far-reaching, fundamentally changing how urban traffic systems are designed and operated in the era of artificial intelligence.
