# CoLLMLight: Cooperative Large Language Model Agents for Network-Wide Traffic Signal Control

> [!summary] Summary
> CoLLMLight, developed by The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou), proposes a cooperative Large Language Model (LLM) agent framework for network-wide traffic signal control. It integrates spatiotemporal awareness, complexity-aware reasoning, and simulation-driven fine-tuning to achieve superior generalization and efficiency, outperforming existing methods on various real-world datasets.

## Problem

- Existing LLM-based traffic signal control (TSC) methods focus on localized decision-making, lacking crucial inter-agent coordination for network-wide traffic optimization.
- Traditional and Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based TSC approaches struggle with generalization to dynamic and unseen traffic conditions, despite advancements in cooperative control.
- Deploying computationally intensive generalist LLMs for real-time TSC faces challenges in balancing decision-making accuracy with the required computational efficiency.

## Method

- A ==cooperative LLM agent== framework is proposed that models spatiotemporal relationships and temporal dynamics to enable network-wide decision-making among intersections.
- A complexity-aware reasoning mechanism dynamically adjusts the LLM's reasoning depth based on real-time congestion risk, optimizing computational resources for varying traffic scenarios.
- A simulation-driven fine-tuning approach uses iterative data generation with GPT-4o for pseudo-labeling and environmental feedback for policy refinement, creating a lightweight and specialized LLM.

## Results

- CoLLMLight achieves state-of-the-art or comparable performance in zero-shot evaluations across seven real-world datasets (Jinan, Hangzhou, New York), consistently outperforming traditional, RL-based, and existing LLM-based methods.
- The framework demonstrates superior generalization, evidenced by an up to **25.17%** reduction in Average Waiting Time (AWT) on complex networks like New York 1, showcasing effective cooperative control.
- Ablation studies confirm the critical role of complexity-aware reasoning and the two-stage simulation-driven fine-tuning process, with optimized performance achieved by balancing historical information and communication thresholds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly incorporating spatiotemporal awareness and inter-agent cooperation in LLM-based systems is critical for effective network-wide traffic signal control.
> - Dynamically adapting the reasoning complexity of LLM agents significantly improves both efficiency and performance, allowing for optimal resource utilization in real-time control tasks.
> - A multi-stage fine-tuning process, combining reasoning chain optimization with iterative environmental feedback from simulations, is effective in building specialized and robust LLMs for complex control environments.
