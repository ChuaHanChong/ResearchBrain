# Reinforce Adjoint Matching: Scaling RL Post-Training of Diffusion and Flow-Matching Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from TU Munich, MIT CSAIL, King's College London, Microsoft Research, and University of Oxford developed Reinforce Adjoint Matching (RAM), a method that scales RL post-training of diffusion and flow-matching models by preserving their computationally efficient supervised regression structure. RAM achieves higher task rewards and reduces training steps by up to 50 times on a large text-to-image model across compositional, OCR, and human preference tasks.

## Problem

- Existing RL post-training methods for diffusion and flow-matching models suffer from high computational cost due to intractable likelihoods, often requiring extensive SDE rollouts or backward adjoint sweeps.
- These methods typically abandon the efficient supervised regression structure central to the scalability of generative model pretraining, leading to increased computational complexity and numerical instability.
- Many approaches rely on surrogate objectives or require differentiable reward functions, which can introduce inaccuracies or limit applicability with real-world, non-differentiable rewards.

## Method

- Reformulates RL post-training as a ==KL-regularized stochastic optimal control== problem, optimizing for reward maximization while maintaining proximity to the pretrained distribution.
- Derives a ==regression-based objective==, **Reinforce Adjoint Matching (RAM)**, by approximating the adjoint function and applying a ==REINFORCE identity== to handle non-differentiable reward functions.
- Leverages the property that the ==optimal noising law remains invariant==, allowing for efficient generation of multiple noisy training samples from a single clean endpoint via ==analytical noising==, bypassing costly SDE rollouts.

## Results

- Achieved the highest training rewards across all three text-to-image tasks: GenEval (**0.97**), OCR (**0.97**), and PickScore (**23.67**), indicating superior alignment with target reward functions.
- Demonstrated substantial training efficiency, reaching peak performance **34 to 50 times faster** in terms of steps than leading baselines like Flow-GRPO, with comparable per-step computational cost.
- Maintained or improved general image quality on the DrawBench dataset while enhancing task-specific rewards, and remained compatible with classifier-free guidance, unlike some baseline methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The optimal generative process for KL-regularized reward maximization only modifies the clean-endpoint distribution, while the conditional law for noising a fixed clean sample remains unchanged.
> - Approximating the adjoint by omitting the path-cost correction term substantially reduces variance, making the method more stable and scalable for large-scale generative models.
> - Applying the REINFORCE identity allows for the use of non-differentiable reward functions by re-weighting the backward bridge score with the reward, circumventing the need for direct reward gradients.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Diffusion and flow-matching models have established themselves as prominent approaches for high-fidelity generation in continuous data domains. Their scalability during pretraining is largely attributed to a supervised regression structure: clean data samples are analytically noised, and the model is trained to regress against a closed-form target. This process is computationally efficient due to the straightforward construction of training pairs.

However, the capabilities of pretrained generative models are often enhanced through post-training, particularly using reinforcement learning (RL), to align generations with specific objectives, human preferences, or complex conditional prompts. In image generation, this enables tasks such as correct object composition, legible text rendering, or overall aesthetic alignment. In language modeling, analogous RL post-training is foundational to advanced reasoning systems.

The challenge in applying RL post-training to diffusion and flow-matching models stems from the intractability of sample likelihoods, which are essential for traditional policy gradient methods used in tractable likelihood models like language models. Existing methods to overcome this intractability incur various computational costs and complexities:
- **Policy-gradient methods:** These often introduce stochasticity into the denoising process and rely on Gaussian transition densities to optimize variational lower bounds. Examples include methods that utilize stochastic differential equation (SDE) rollouts, which are computationally intensive and require many steps.
- **Stochastic optimal control methods:** These aim to precisely optimize the KL-regularized objective but typically necessitate reward gradients backpropagated through the sampling process, often via adjoint Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs). This approach also involves SDE rollouts and can face numerical instability, especially for flow-matching models where noise schedules may diverge near the noisy end.
- **Surrogate objectives:** Some approaches replace intractable likelihoods with more manageable proxies, such as flow-matching ELBOs or distillation of improvement directions by contrasting positive and negative endpoints. While these can simplify the objective, they diverge from the direct KL-regularized reward maximization.

A common limitation across many of these existing methods is their reliance on costly SDE rollouts, the need for reward gradients (which restricts the type of reward functions that can be used), or the use of surrogate losses, all of which depart from the simple regression structure that makes pretraining scalable. This paper aims to address these limitations by demonstrating that the regression structure of pretraining can be extended to RL post-training, thereby offering a more scalable and efficient alternative.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce a scalable and efficient method for reinforcement learning (RL) post-training of diffusion and flow-matching generative models, specifically designed to address the computational bottlenecks of existing approaches.

The detailed objectives and underlying motivations are as follows:

1. **Extend the supervised regression structure to RL post-training:** The pretraining phase of diffusion and flow-matching models benefits from its simplicity as a supervised regression task, where a model predicts a target (e.g., noise, clean data, or velocity) from a noisy input. The motivation is to retain this efficient, scalable regression paradigm during RL post-training, rather than resorting to complex and costly methods like SDE rollouts or direct policy gradients on intractable likelihoods.

2. **Avoid costly computational components:** Current RL post-training methods often require:
 - **SDE rollouts:** Simulating stochastic differential equations to generate samples is computationally expensive, especially for complex generative processes and when many steps are needed.
 - **Backward adjoint sweeps:** Calculating gradients through the sampling process often involves solving adjoint ODEs backward in time along stored trajectories, which is memory-intensive and numerically delicate.
 - **Reward gradients:** Many methods necessitate a differentiable reward function, which limits the applicability to black-box or non-differentiable reward signals, such as human preferences.
 The motivation is to develop a method that circumvents these computational demands, thereby reducing training time and resource requirements.

3. **Achieve high performance on reward objectives:** The method should effectively align generative models with diverse reward functions, demonstrating improved performance in specific generation tasks. The paper focuses on three distinct image generation tasks:
 - **Compositional Generation:** Ensuring generated images correctly represent specified objects and their spatial relationships within a prompt.
 - **Visual Text Rendering:** Producing legible text within generated images according to the prompt.
 - **Human Preference Alignment:** Generating images that are preferred by humans, as evaluated by learned preference models.
 The motivation is to provide a practical tool for fine-tuning models to produce higher-quality, more controllable, and user-aligned outputs across various criteria.

4. **Significantly improve training efficiency:** A core motivation is to reduce the number of training steps and overall wall-clock time required to achieve target reward levels compared to existing state-of-the-art RL post-training techniques. The aim is to make RL fine-tuning more accessible and practical for large-scale models and complex tasks.

5. **Maintain or improve generic image quality:** Optimizing for a specific reward can sometimes lead to "reward hacking," where a model achieves a high score on the target metric but degrades in general image quality or coherence. A key objective is to ensure that the proposed method aligns with reward functions without sacrificing the overall aesthetic quality, fidelity, or diversity of the generated images, as assessed by a range of independent image quality metrics.

By addressing these objectives, the research seeks to offer a more practical and scalable framework for enhancing the capabilities of continuous-domain generative models through RL post-training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Reinforce Adjoint Matching (RAM) methodology is grounded in stochastic optimal control theory and leverages a specific structural property of optimal generative processes to derive a reward-corrected regression target for RL post-training. The approach bypasses common computational bottlenecks such as SDE rollouts, backward adjoint sweeps, and direct reward gradients.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Foundation

1. **KL-Regularized Reward Maximization:** The problem of RL post-training is formulated as maximizing the expected reward while keeping the post-trained model's output distribution close to the pretrained model's distribution in a KL-divergence sense. This is expressed as targeting a "tilted distribution" $p_0^* \propto p_{ref_0}(x) \exp(r(x))$, where $p_{ref_0}$ is the pretrained clean-endpoint distribution and $r(x)$ is the reward function.

2. **Stochastic Optimal Control Formulation:** This objective is framed as a stochastic optimal control problem. The pretrained backward SDE is steered towards the target tilted distribution using a drift correction $u_t$. The goal is to maximize the terminal reward (from $X_0$) minus a quadratic control cost over the trajectory. This control problem is mathematically equivalent to the KL-regularized reward maximization in path space.

3. **Structure of the Optimal Process (Theorem 3.1):** A central theoretical insight is that an optimal controlled process achieves the reward maximization by tilting the *clean-endpoint distribution* ($p_0^*$) towards higher-reward samples, but critically, it *leaves the conditional law of noisy states given that clean endpoint unchanged*. This means the rule for noising a fixed clean sample remains the same as in pretraining, which implies the analytic noising kernel (equation 1) is preserved. This "memoryless schedule" property is key to RAM's efficiency.

4. **Adjoint Matching (Theorem 3.2):** Stochastic optimal control theory provides a fixed-point condition for the optimal control $u_t$: it is proportional to the negative spatial gradient of the Bellman value function, denoted as the adjoint $A_u_t(x) = \nabla_x V_u_t(x)$, i.e., $u_t(x) = -\sigma_t A_u_t(x)$. This condition is used as a self-consistency target for training.

#### 3.2. Reinforce Adjoint Matching (RAM) Derivation

1. **REINFORCE Identity for Adjoint:** To avoid explicit reward gradients (which may be non-differentiable), a REINFORCE identity is applied to the value function gradient. This replaces the reward gradient with the backward bridge score weighted by the reward:
 $A_u_t(x) = E[r(X_u_0) \nabla_x \log p_{u_0|t}(X_{u_0} | x) | X_{u_t} = x] - \frac{1}{2} E[\nabla_x \int_0^t \|u_\tau(X_{u_\tau})\|^2 d\tau | X_{u_t} = x]$.

2. **Approximation by Dropping Path-Cost Correction:** RAM simplifies the adjoint by dropping the second term, the "path-cost correction," resulting in $A_u_t(x) \approx E[r(X_u_0) \nabla_x \log p_{u_0|t}(X_{u_0} | x) | X_{u_t} = x]$. This approximation is justified because it is exact at initialization (when $u_t \equiv 0$) and under specific conditions (e.g., Gaussian reference, linear reward). A comparison with alternative estimators that retain the path-cost correction (e.g., random jump, full-horizon Malliavin/Bayes, local SDE rollout) shows that this simplified form yields the lowest variance in practice and is more scalable for image generation.

3. **Endpoint Sampling and Analytic Noising (Algorithm 1):** Leveraging Theorem 3.1, RAM avoids SDE rollouts. Instead, it generates a clean endpoint $X_0$ from the *current* generative model using any standard ODE sampler. This $X_0$ is then analytically noised to a noisy state $X_t$ using the same kernel as in pretraining ($X_t = (1-t)X_0 + t\epsilon$). This process generates joint samples $(X_0, X_t)$ that are consistent with the optimal process at initialization and at convergence. A single generated endpoint $X_0$ can be reused to create multiple (K) independent noisy training states $X_t$ by drawing new noise $\epsilon$, amortizing the cost of sampling and reward evaluation.

4. **Bayes Bridge Score (Proposition 4.1):** The backward bridge score $\nabla_x \log p_{0|t}(x_0 | x_t)$ (which represents the influence of $x_0$ on $x_t$) is derived in a closed-form expression in terms of the model's current velocity field $v_t$. This avoids complex computations like Jacobian-vector products through the score.

5. **RAM Objective Function (Equation 17):** Combining the approximate adjoint, the endpoint sampling procedure, and the Bayes bridge score, RAM derives a consistency loss for the velocity field $v_\theta$:
 $L_{RAM}(\theta) = E_t [\|v_\theta_t(X_t) - \text{sg}(v_{ref_t}(X_t) + r(X_0)((\epsilon - X_0) - v_\theta_t(X_t)))\|^2]$.
 Here, $\text{sg}(\cdot)$ is the stop-gradient operator, meaning gradients are only propagated through the left-hand side $v_\theta_t(X_t)$. The terms $X_0 \sim p_{\theta_0}$, $\epsilon \sim N(0, I)$, and $X_t = (1-t)X_0 + t\epsilon$ reflect the on-policy sampling and analytic noising. The $v_{ref_t}$ term acts as an explicit anchor, penalizing deviations from the pretrained model, which helps prevent reward hacking. This objective is a simple, reward-corrected regression, directly parallel to pretraining.

#### 3.3. Relation to KL Optimum

The paper demonstrates that the RAM fixed point relates to the KL-regularized optimum by a path-integral characterization. The RAM objective evaluates the conditional reward-velocity covariance at the right endpoint of a path of exponentially tilted distributions. This formulation is exact for specific cases (Gaussian reference, linear reward) and acts as a first-order approximation to the KL optimum more generally.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates Reinforce Adjoint Matching (RAM) against existing state-of-the-art methods for RL post-training of Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium (SD3.5M) across three distinct text-to-image tasks: compositional image generation, visual text rendering, and human preference alignment.

#### 4.1. Performance on Training Rewards

RAM consistently achieves the highest training reward on all three tasks compared to Flow-GRPO, DiffusionNFT, and AWM.
- **Compositional Image Generation (GenEval):** RAM reaches a GenEval Accuracy of 0.97, surpassing Flow-GRPO (0.95), DiffusionNFT (0.95), and AWM (0.83).
- **Visual Text Rendering (OCR):** RAM achieves an OCR Accuracy of 0.97, matching AWM (0.97) and slightly exceeding DiffusionNFT (0.96) and Flow-GRPO (0.92).
- **Human Preference Alignment (PickScore):** RAM obtains a PickScore of 23.67, outperforming Flow-GRPO (23.31), AWM (23.39), and DiffusionNFT (23.29).

#### 4.2. Training Efficiency

RAM demonstrates significant improvements in training efficiency, particularly in terms of the number of training steps required to reach peak reward, while maintaining comparable per-step computational costs.
- **GenEval:** RAM reaches Flow-GRPO's peak reward in approximately 50x fewer training steps (270 steps for RAM vs. >5k for Flow-GRPO).
- **OCR:** RAM achieves Flow-GRPO's peak reward in approximately 48x fewer steps (60 steps for RAM vs. 1.2k for Flow-GRPO).
- **PickScore:** RAM reaches Flow-GRPO's peak reward in approximately 34x fewer steps (300 steps for RAM vs. >3k for Flow-GRPO).
- **Per-step compute cost:** The average GPU-hours per training step are comparable between RAM and Flow-GRPO (e.g., 0.666 GPU-hours for RAM vs. 0.701 for Flow-GRPO on GenEval), indicating that the step reductions directly translate to wall-clock time savings.
- **Comparison with other efficient methods:** RAM also shows improved efficiency over DiffusionNFT and AWM. Its regression target, derived from an adjoint-matching condition, is noted as a key factor for this advantage over surrogate objectives. The efficiency gains are partly attributed to RAM's ability to generate multiple conditionally independent training states from a single clean endpoint, providing more gradient signal per step than methods relying on correlated states from SDE rollouts.

#### 4.3. Image Quality Evaluation and Reward Hacking

The paper assesses generic image quality using metrics on DrawBench prompts to detect reward hacking.
- **Compositional Generation & Preference Alignment:** For these tasks, RAM's reward improvements are accompanied by comparable or better image-quality metrics (Aesthetic, DeQA, ImageReward, HPSv2, PickScore) compared to baselines.
- **Visual Text Rendering:** While achieving high OCR reward, RAM avoids the severe quality degradation (indicated by lower Aesthetic, DeQA, ImageReward, HPSv2, PickScore) observed in DiffusionNFT and AWM. Flow-GRPO maintained higher image quality metrics for this task but at a substantially lower OCR reward. This suggests a trade-off between OCR legibility and aesthetic quality, which RAM manages to balance effectively.
- **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):** RAM retains compatibility with CFG after post-training, an important feature for controlling generation. In contrast, CFG was observed to lower the task reward for DiffusionNFT and AWM.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

Qualitative examples demonstrate that RAM effectively corrects compositional errors, renders specified text legibly, and produces images that align more closely with human preferences, all while preserving the style and fidelity characteristic of the pretrained SD3.5M model.

#### 4.5. Estimator Variance

On a 2D toy example, a comparison of various estimators for the value-function gradient (including those retaining the path-cost correction) shows that RAM, by dropping the path-cost correction, attains the lowest variance. This suggests that the path-cost gradient can be a dominant source of noise, making RAM's simplified approach more stable and efficient for high-dimensional data like images.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Reinforce Adjoint Matching (RAM) method represents a methodological advancement in the field of generative modeling, specifically for post-training diffusion and flow-matching models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Scalability and Efficiency for RL Post-Training:** RAM demonstrates that the computational efficiency inherent in the supervised regression structure of pretraining can be extended to RL post-training. By avoiding costly SDE rollouts, backward adjoint sweeps, and direct reward gradients, RAM achieves substantial reductions in training time (up to 50x fewer steps than comparable methods). This makes RL fine-tuning of large-scale generative models more feasible and practical, lowering the computational barriers for researchers and developers.

2. **Preservation of Model Quality and Control:** The method enables effective alignment with complex reward functions (composability, text rendering, human preference) without significantly degrading generic image quality. Its compatibility with classifier-free guidance (CFG) after post-training is an important practical advantage, allowing users to maintain control over generation style and adherence to prompts. This indicates that RAM facilitates targeted model improvement without introducing undesirable side effects commonly associated with aggressive fine-tuning.

3. **Methodological Innovation in Optimal Control and RL:** The core innovation lies in leveraging a key theoretical insight: that the optimal controlled generative process primarily tilts the clean-endpoint distribution towards higher reward samples while preserving the conditional noising law given that endpoint. This theoretical foundation, combined with a REINFORCE identity and a closed-form Bayes bridge score, leads to a novel, simpler, and more efficient consistency loss that directly corrects the pretraining target with reward information. This approach offers a departure from traditional policy gradient or adjoint-based methods that often entail more complex computations.

4. **Broader Applicability and Research Directions:** The underlying principles of RAM, particularly the exploitation of the "memoryless schedule" property in stochastic optimal control for generative models, could be generalizable. This suggests potential applications beyond diffusion and flow-matching models, possibly extending to other continuous-domain generative architectures or even flow maps, should their underlying noising or interpolation mechanisms exhibit similar structural properties. The work may also inspire further research into gradient-free or regression-based RL approaches that minimize reliance on complex trajectory sampling and backpropagation.

5. **Facilitating Advanced AI Capabilities:** By enabling more efficient and effective alignment of generative models with human preferences and complex task specifications, RAM contributes to the development of more capable, controllable, and user-friendly AI systems. This could have a substantial impact on applications in creative industries, content generation, and any domain where high-fidelity, customized generative outputs are desired.
