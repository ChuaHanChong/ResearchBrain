# Remember-R1: Mitigating Long-Context Visual Forgetting through Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Remember-R1, a reinforcement learning framework developed by researchers including those from Northwestern Polytechnical University and Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, mitigates long-context visual forgetting in Multimodal Large Language Models. It achieves this by applying direct process-level supervision with a multi-component reward system, improving performance by up to 13.60% on benchmarks like MathVista while preserving inference efficiency.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) experience 'visual forgetting' during long chain-of-thought reasoning, gradually relying less on visual evidence and more on generated textual context.
- This leads to reasoning chains diverging from factual visual information, which is particularly problematic for tasks requiring sustained visual alignment, such as mathematical or logical reasoning.
- Existing solutions for visual forgetting are either computationally expensive, fragment reasoning processes, or provide only indirect supervision, failing to address the core problem of process-level visual dependency effectively.

## Method

- **Remember-R1** introduces a reinforcement learning (RL) framework that applies ==direct process-level supervision== to an MLLM's original reasoning trajectory without modifying its inference procedure.
- A set of three complementary ==process-level rewards== is designed: Visual Vocabulary (`r_voc`) encourages explicit mention of visual keywords; Visual Memory (`r_mem`) promotes sustained visual attention; and Visual Key-Region (`r_region`) guides attention to relevant image areas.
- The model's policy is optimized using ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== with a combined reward signal (answer correctness plus the three visual rewards) to foster visually grounded generative behaviors.

## Results

- Remember-R1 consistently improved MLLM performance across all **seven** evaluated benchmarks, achieving gains up to **+13.60%** on MathVista (for the 3B model) and exhibiting enhanced capabilities in MMVet, MMStar, and RealWorldQA.
- Qualitative and quantitative analysis confirmed that Remember-R1 significantly slows the decay of visual attention over long generation sequences, maintaining adherence to visual facts and preventing reasoning drift.
- A comprehensive ablation study demonstrated the necessity and complementary roles of all three process-level rewards, with each contributing uniquely to mitigating visual forgetting and enhancing overall performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct process-level supervision via reinforcement learning is effective in mitigating visual forgetting in MLLMs and can be implemented without incurring inference-time computational overheads.
> - A multi-faceted reward system that addresses distinct aspects of visual grounding (vocabulary, attention persistence, and regional focus) is crucial for comprehensively improving visual evidence utilization in long-context reasoning.
> - Explicitly shaping the *process* of reasoning through visual grounding rewards is more effective than relying solely on outcome-based rewards (e.g., final answer correctness) for developing robust multimodal reasoning capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are increasingly applied to complex, multi-step reasoning tasks beyond basic perception, such as image captioning. However, a key challenge known as "visual forgetting" emerges during long chain-of-thought reasoning processes. As reasoning sequences extend, MLLMs tend to gradually reduce their reliance on visual evidence, becoming more dependent on the generated textual context. This can lead to the reasoning chain diverging from factual visual information, a phenomenon particularly critical for tasks requiring sustained alignment with visual facts, such as mathematical, logical, and spatial reasoning.

Existing methods for mitigating visual forgetting have limitations. Some approaches involve re-introducing visual information (e.g., the original image or specific regions) during inference. While this can aid long-form reasoning, it increases computational and memory costs and may fragment the reasoning process. Other methods aim to improve visual grounding by inserting visual claim proxies during training. However, the supervision in these techniques is applied to auxiliary interactions rather than directly constraining how visual evidence is maintained along the model's primary reasoning path. This indirect supervision leaves the core problem of process-level visual dependency in long contexts inadequately addressed.

This research introduces Remember-R1, a reinforcement learning (RL) framework, to fill this gap. It proposes applying direct process-level supervision onto the original reasoning trajectory without modifying the model’s inference procedure. This approach aims to encourage sustained visual grounding throughout response generation, directly combating visual forgetting by influencing the model's behavior where it naturally occurs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to mitigate long-context visual forgetting in MLLMs during extended reasoning tasks. This phenomenon describes the diminishing reliance on visual evidence as MLLMs generate lengthy reasoning chains, leading to a drift from visual facts towards accumulated textual context. This is particularly problematic for tasks requiring continuous visual alignment, such as complex mathematical or logical reasoning, where accurate interpretation of visual cues is paramount for correct problem-solving. An illustrative example in the paper (Figure 1) shows how incorrect responses exhibit a sharper late-stage decline in visual attention compared to correct ones, highlighting this issue.

Current solutions are insufficient or come with significant drawbacks. Methods that re-introduce visual information during inference (e.g., through repeated visual processing) are computationally expensive in terms of both memory and processing power, and can also lead to fragmented reasoning chains. Training with visual claim proxies, while providing some visual grounding, offers indirect supervision. It supervises auxiliary interactions rather than directly constraining the model's use of visual evidence along its inherent, untouched reasoning trajectory (Figure 2). This indirectness fails to address the process-level weakening of visual dependence.

Motivated by these limitations and the need for a more direct approach, Remember-R1 aims to:
1. **Apply Direct Process-Level Supervision**: Introduce a reinforcement learning framework that supervises visual evidence use directly on the MLLM’s original reasoning trajectory, fostering sustained reliance on visual evidence throughout the entire generation process.
2. **Preserve Inference Efficiency**: Achieve this mitigation without altering the model’s inference process, thereby maintaining its operational speed and memory footprint during deployment.
3. **Design Comprehensive Rewards**: Develop a set of three complementary process-level rewards to foster specific aspects of visual grounding:
 - **Visual Keyword Coverage**: Encourage the explicit mention and broader integration of matched annotated visual keywords within the reasoning trace.
 - **Visual Attention Persistence**: Promote the sustained maintenance of visual dependence in later reasoning steps, counteracting the natural decay of visual attention.
 - **Question-Relevant Region Focus**: Guide the model's visual attention to concentrate on image regions most pertinent to answering the given question, especially as the reasoning unfolds.

These objectives collectively seek to enhance MLLMs' ability to maintain factual consistency with visual inputs over long and complex reasoning sequences, leading to more robust and accurate multimodal reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Remember-R1 is a reinforcement learning (RL) framework designed for training MLLMs, focusing on process-level supervision to mitigate visual forgetting without altering inference-time behavior.

**Quantifying Visual Forgetting**: The model’s reliance on visual evidence at each generation step `t` is measured by `Ω(t)`, which represents the aggregate attention mass assigned to visual tokens from the final transformer layer. Visual forgetting is quantified by `Δ_vis`, calculated as the difference between the average visual attention in early versus late stages of generation, with a larger value indicating greater forgetting.

**Visual Evidence Annotation**: To provide the necessary supervision for process-level rewards, the ViRL39K dataset was augmented with structured annotations:
- **Visual Keyword Extraction**: For each image `I_i`, a large vision-language model (Qwen-VL-Max) was used to extract a set of concise visual keywords (`K_i`). These keywords encompass object categories, attributes, quantities, and short spatial phrases, grounded in the visible content, and are kept short for compact supervision.
- **Key-Region Box Annotation**: For each sample `i`, Qwen-VL-Max was also employed to identify and annotate bounding boxes (`B_i`) around image regions that provide visual evidence crucial for answering the question `Q_i`. Critically, the correct answer (`A_i`) was provided to the annotation model *only* during this one-time annotation phase for guidance and was never exposed to the policy model during subsequent RL training or inference.
- **Manual Verification**: Both the extracted keywords (`K_i`) and key-region boxes (`B_i`) underwent manual verification to enhance annotation quality, removing redundancies, correcting mismatches, and adjusting inaccuracies. The final dataset comprises 38,657 samples with these verified annotations.

**Reward Design**: Remember-R1 employs three complementary process-level rewards, each targeting a specific aspect of sustained visual evidence utilization during reasoning (illustrated in Figure 4):
1. **Visual Vocabulary Reward (`r_voc`)**: This reward encourages the explicit mention of annotated visual keywords (`K_i`) in the generated reasoning trace. It is computed based on the number of matched keywords in the response, using case-insensitive exact phrase matching. A temporal weighting scheme `(τ(k)/T_eff)` is applied, where `τ(k)` is the last occurrence position of keyword `k`, giving higher reward if keywords appear later in the response, thus promoting sustained reference throughout the reasoning chain.
2. **Visual Memory Reward (`r_mem`)**: Designed to counteract the decay of overall visual attention, this reward encourages the model to maintain visual dependence into later reasoning steps. It quantifies how well visual attention is preserved by comparing the average visual attention (`Ω(t)`) over an initial window of tokens (`μ_start`) with that over a final window of tokens (`μ_end`). The reward is formulated such that it increases when `μ_end` remains close to or exceeds `μ_start`, thereby discouraging a sharp decline in visual dependency.
3. **Visual Key-Region Reward (`r_region`)**: This reward ensures that preserved visual attention is directed towards informative, question-relevant regions. It calculates the proportion of visual attention assigned to the pre-annotated key-region bounding boxes (`B_i`) at each step, forming a region-attention ratio `R_B(t)`. This ratio is then averaged across generation steps with a temporal weight `(t/T)`, assigning higher importance to focused attention in later stages of reasoning.

**Reinforcement Learning**: The total reward for a generated rollout `Y` combines a binary answer correctness reward (`r_acc`) with the three process-level visual rewards: `R(Y, I, Q) = r_acc(Y) + r_voc(Y) + r_mem(Y) + r_region(Y)`. This combined reward signal is used to optimize the MLLM’s policy (`π_θ`) using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). GRPO updates the model parameters by computing a normalized advantage for responses within a sampled group and applying a clipped surrogate objective, along with KL regularization against a reference policy, guiding the MLLM to learn visually grounded generative behaviors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation demonstrated Remember-R1's consistent effectiveness in enhancing MLLM performance and mitigating visual forgetting across diverse tasks and model scales.

**Overall Performance Improvements**: Remember-R1 consistently improved performance over its Qwen2.5-VL base models (at both 3B and 7B scales) across all seven evaluated benchmarks (Table 1). Notable gains were observed in mathematical and logical reasoning (e.g., +13.60 on MathVista for the 3B model), general multimodal capabilities (e.g., MMVet, MMStar), and visual perception (RealWorldQA). These results support the efficacy of the unified reward design.

**Visual Grounding Analysis**:
- **Qualitative Evidence**: A qualitative comparison on a geometric reasoning task (Figure 5) illustrated that the base model could hallucinate non-existent visual elements and drift into text-dependent reasoning, leading to incorrect answers. In contrast, Remember-R1 maintained adherence to visual facts, meticulously tracking relevant visual cues (shape, color, spatial) to provide accurate responses. This demonstrated the framework's ability to prevent reasoning trajectories from detaching from visual information.
- **Quantitative Attention Analysis**: Analysis of the mean visual attention ratio assigned to visual tokens across reasoning steps (Figure 6) showed that while visual attention naturally declines over time for both models, Remember-R1 consistently exhibited a *slower rate of decay* compared to the base model. This difference became more pronounced in the middle and later stages of generation, confirming its ability to preserve the model's reliance on visual evidence throughout long generations.

**Ablation Study**: A comprehensive ablation study (Table 2, Figure 7) validated the necessity and complementary roles of all three process-level rewards:
- **`r_voc` (Visual Vocabulary Reward) Removal**: Led to a reduction in the number of matched visual keywords in the response and performance degradation, particularly in reasoning-intensive benchmarks. This indicates the reward's importance in encouraging explicit integration of visual concepts into reasoning.
- **`r_mem` (Visual Memory Reward) Removal**: Resulted in a noticeably faster decay of visual attention over generation steps and corresponding performance drops across several benchmarks. This confirmed that preserving late-stage visual reliance is critical for maintaining performance in long reasoning chains.
- **`r_region` (Visual Key-Region Reward) Removal**: Caused a substantial decrease in attention aligned with question-relevant regions and clear accuracy degradation across various task types. This highlights that merely maintaining overall visual attention is insufficient; focused attention on salient, relevant regions is essential for effective visual grounding.
- **Comparison with Accuracy-Only RL**: Remember-R1 consistently outperformed a GRPO variant trained solely on the final answer correctness reward (`r_acc`). This finding emphasizes the superiority of explicit process-level supervision in shaping visually grounded reasoning over mere outcome-based rewards.

In conclusion, the findings indicate that Remember-R1's process-level supervision effectively addresses visual forgetting, fostering more accurate and visually grounded reasoning across a broad spectrum of multimodal tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Remember-R1 presents a significant contribution to the field of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) by offering a novel and effective strategy to mitigate long-context visual forgetting. Its core innovation lies in applying direct process-level supervision through reinforcement learning, specifically targeting the model's original reasoning trajectory without altering its inference procedure. This approach provides a more direct and efficient solution compared to prior methods that either introduce computational overheads through re-introducing visual information or offer indirect supervision via proxy tasks.

The consistent performance improvements observed across diverse benchmarks—ranging from complex mathematical and logical reasoning to general multimodal understanding and fine-grained visual perception—and across different model scales, highlight the broad applicability and robustness of Remember-R1. This suggests that the principles of direct process-level supervision for visual grounding can be generalized to a wide array of MLLM applications.

The qualitative and quantitative analyses provide compelling evidence of the framework's mechanism. By preventing reasoning from drifting towards unsupported textual assumptions (hallucinations) and by demonstrably slowing the decay of visual attention during long generations, Remember-R1 enhances the factual consistency and reliability of MLLMs. This is particularly impactful for high-stakes applications where accuracy and adherence to visual facts are paramount.

The ablation study further elucidates the value of each component of the reward system. By showing that each of the visual vocabulary, memory persistence, and key-region focus rewards contributes uniquely and complementarily to the overall performance, the research provides a clearer understanding for designing more effective training objectives. The superior performance of Remember-R1 over accuracy-only reinforcement learning underscores the importance of shaping the *process* of reasoning, not just its *outcome*, for developing truly robust and visually intelligent MLLMs.

Ultimately, Remember-R1 advances the capability of MLLMs for long-horizon multimodal reasoning. By enabling MLLMs to maintain a sustained and focused engagement with visual evidence, it paves the way for more capable and reliable models in domains requiring deep contextual understanding of visual information, such as advanced scientific analysis, detailed document interpretation, or sophisticated robotic perception, while preserving inference efficiency.
