# ThinkMorph: Emergent Properties in Multimodal Interleaved Chain-of-Thought Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified model named ThinkMorph, developed by researchers from National University of Singapore, Zhejiang University, University of Washington, Stanford University, and The Chinese University of Hong Kong, utilizes multimodal interleaved chain-of-thought reasoning to generate progressive text-image steps, leading to substantial performance gains on vision-centric tasks and revealing emergent properties such as unseen visual manipulations and autonomous mode switching.

## Problem

- Multimodal reasoning tasks often require iterative coordination, but existing models struggle to genuinely integrate visual and linguistic thoughts beyond superficial correlation.
- Current interleaved Chain-of-Thought approaches are either brittle (tool-augmented) or produce isomorphic representations, failing to leverage the complementary strengths of text and image.
- There is a lack of a unified, generalizable framework to enable and investigate genuinely iterative and coordinated reasoning between language and vision.

## Method

- Developed ==ThinkMorph==, a unified multimodal model based on Bagel-7B, capable of generating progressive, ==interleaved text and image tokens== for reasoning.
- Constructed a meticulously curated dataset of approximately 24,000 high-quality ==text-image reasoning traces==, emphasizing complementary modality interactions across diverse vision-centric tasks.
- Fine-tuned the model with ==dual loss functions== (Mean Squared Error for images, negative log-likelihood for text) to jointly optimize both visual generation and textual coherence.

## Results

- ThinkMorph achieved an average performance gain of **34.74%** on vision-centric benchmarks compared to its base model, with an **85.84%** improvement on Spatial Navigation.
- The 7B-parameter model performed comparably to or better than models an order of magnitude larger, surpassing Qwen2.5-VL-72B by **34%** on VSP and GPT-4o by **24.67%** on SAT.
- Three emergent properties were identified: the generation of unseen visual manipulations, autonomous switching to text-only reasoning for certain samples (achieving **81.25%** accuracy when switching), and superior test-time scaling through diversified multimodal thoughts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Text and image thoughts are most effective when they function as complementary modalities, each advancing the reasoning process beyond what the other could achieve alone.
> - Fine-tuning on carefully curated interleaved reasoning traces enables generative models to develop and utilize task-effective visual manipulations (e.g., zoom, inpainting) not explicitly trained.
> - Unified multimodal models can develop emergent properties, including autonomous mode switching between textual and visual reasoning, and diversified test-time exploration of solution spaces.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge within multimodal reasoning, specifically the iterative coordination between language and vision. While advancements have been made in textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT), its application to multimodal problems, particularly those requiring more than textual description, has shown limitations. Existing multimodal reasoning models, especially in vision-centric domains like spatial reasoning, often struggle to move beyond image description to interrogation and manipulation of visual elements. This limitation prevents models from effectively tackling complex problems that benefit from a "think-and-sketch" human-like approach.

Prior attempts to implement interleaved Chain-of-Thought fall into two main categories: tool-augmented designs and unified models. Tool-augmented designs, which rely on external visual modules (e.g., cropping tools or specialized sketching models), are often indirect and can be brittle. Unified models, while offering a more integrated approach, have not consistently provided a generalizable mechanism for mutual advancement between text and image reasoning. For instance, some models exhibit isomorphic text and image representations, limiting their generalization beyond training domains. This paper posits that for generalizable multimodal reasoning, text and images should function as complementary modalities that mutually advance the reasoning process, rather than serving as isomorphic representations. ThinkMorph aims to develop a unified model that embodies this principle, thereby contributing a more robust and generalizable approach to interleaved multimodal reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research were to develop a unified multimodal model capable of genuinely interleaved text-image reasoning and to systematically analyze its properties. The specific motivations and objectives include:

- **Addressing Limitations of Current Multimodal Reasoning**: The authors identify that current multimodal reasoning often fails to be an iterative process where language and vision interweave effectively, particularly in vision-centric tasks demanding interrogation and manipulation of visual content. Models frequently default to language-driven reasoning, which is insufficient for tasks requiring visual grounding.
- **Emulating "Think-and-Sketch" Behavior**: Humans often employ a "think-and-sketch" strategy for complex problems, involving both verbal deliberation and visual manipulation. The research is motivated by the desire to enable AI models to mirror this behavior, moving beyond purely textual thought processes.
- **Defining Meaningful Interleaved Chain-of-Thought**: A core challenge is establishing what constitutes meaningful interleaving, especially as externalizing visual thinking (e.g., through sketches) is inherently ambiguous and difficult to scale. The project aims to operationalize this concept by designing specific, verifiable intermediate visual thoughts grounded in concrete visual manipulations.
- **Developing a Unified Model for Complementary Modalities**: The central hypothesis is that text and images should function as complementary modalities that mutually advance reasoning, rather than merely representing the same information. This principle guides the development of ThinkMorph, a unified model designed to integrate both modalities seamlessly.
- **Investigating the Efficacy of Interleaved Reasoning**: A key objective is to systematically study when and how interleaved reasoning surpasses unimodal (text-only or visual-only) approaches. This involves fine-tuning the model under different reasoning modes and evaluating their performance across various tasks.
- **Characterizing Emergent Properties**: Beyond performance metrics, the research seeks to identify and characterize novel emergent properties that arise from interleaved reasoning. These properties are considered indicators of higher-level multimodal intelligence and include behaviors like unseen visual manipulations, autonomous mode switching, and enhanced test-time scaling through diversified multimodal thoughts.
- **Establishing a Generalizable Recipe**: The overall motivation is to establish a generalizable methodology for achieving robust and adaptive multimodal reasoning within unified models, demonstrating that generative processes can directly enhance understanding within an interleaved Chain-of-Thought framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces ThinkMorph, a unified multimodal model developed to facilitate generalizable interleaved reasoning. The methodology encompasses data curation, model training, and evaluation across various reasoning modes.

**Model Architecture and Interleaving Mechanism:**
ThinkMorph utilizes Bagel as its base unified multimodal model. It is designed to generate a sequence of interleaved text and image tokens, allowing for dynamic switching between modalities. Special delimiter tokens (`<image start>`, `<image end>`, `<think>`, `</think>`, `<answer>`, `</answer>`) are introduced to control these transitions, enabling the model to integrate visual manipulation within its verbal reasoning flow.

**Interleaved Training Data Collection:**
A significant aspect of the methodology involves curating a high-quality dataset of approximately 24,000 interleaved reasoning traces. This dataset spans four distinct tasks chosen for their varying demands on visual engagement and cross-modal interaction:

- **Jigsaw Assembly**: Requires reconstructing an image from scrambled patches. The traces include textual descriptions of individual pieces, visual representations of re-arranged pieces, and final textual verification. Data was generated from SAT, ADE20K, and Omni3D using a custom pipeline.
- **Spatial Navigation**: Involves finding a safe path on a grid map. Reasoning traces include textual abstraction of the maze, visual rendering of paths (e.g., with overlaid arrows), and textual articulation of moves. Data was generated using a custom pipeline based on OpenAI Gym.
- **Visual Search**: Focuses on answering questions about a target object in an image. Traces feature textual hypotheses of areas of interest, visual bounding box annotations, and textual attribute descriptions. Data was curated from Visual CoT, GQA, and VSR, with filtering to ensure target object bounding boxes occupy 1-30% of the image area and to remove ambiguous or incorrect instances using MLLMs (Gemini 2.5 Pro and GPT-5).
- **Chart Refocus**: Entails answering questions about data visualizations. Traces include textual identification of relevant data elements, visual highlighting of regions of interest, and textual value extraction/computation. Data was sourced from ChartQA and Refocus, with filtering for single highlighting/drawing operations and MLLM-based error removal.

For data generation, GPT-4.1 was prompted to produce step-by-step textual reasoning, often by first being provided with the ground truth answer and instructed not to reveal this information. Visual components were then integrated, either through custom generation pipelines (Jigsaw, Spatial Navigation) or existing annotations (Visual Search, Chart Refocus). Two unimodal baselines were derived: text-only thoughts (generated by GPT-4.1) and visual-only thoughts (using only the image outputs from the interleaved traces).

**Training and Evaluation:**
- **Training**: ThinkMorph was fine-tuned on the curated interleaved traces using Bagel's official implementation. The training involved dual objectives: Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss for image tokens and negative log-likelihood loss for text tokens.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks**:
 - **In-domain**: VSP-main-task (Spatial Navigation), VisPuzzle (Jigsaw Assembly), and a subset of ChartQA (Chart Refocus).
 - **Out-of-domain**: VStar, BLINK (with BLINK-Jigsaw subset designated as BLINK-J), MMVP, SAT, and CV-Bench.
- **Evaluation Procedure**: All evaluations were conducted using the `vlmevalkit` framework for consistency. For tasks where direct matching was insufficient, GPT-5 was employed as an LLM-as-a-Judge for answer correctness.
- **Reasoning Mode Comparison**: Performance was compared across three reasoning modes: text-only, visual-only, and interleaved reasoning, fine-tuned from Bagel-7B.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding ThinkMorph's performance and emergent capabilities:

**Performance Gains on Multimodal Reasoning Tasks:**
ThinkMorph demonstrated substantial performance improvements, particularly on vision-centric tasks requiring interleaved reasoning.
- **Vision-Centric Tasks**: Interleaved reasoning consistently outperformed unimodal approaches. On average, ThinkMorph achieved a 34.74% improvement over its base model (Bagel-7B) across three vision-centric tasks. Specific gains included 85.84% on Spatial Navigation (from 0.83% to 86.67%), 38.75% on Jigsaw Assembly, and 8.38% on the out-of-domain VStar benchmark for Visual Search. Overall, interleaved reasoning surpassed the next-best unimodal mode by an average of 5.33% on these tasks.
- **Generalization to Out-of-Domain Benchmarks**: Despite being fine-tuned on approximately 24,000 samples, ThinkMorph exhibited robust generalization. It matched or exceeded larger and proprietary Vision-Language Models (VLMs). For example, it outperformed InternVL3.5-38B on spatial reasoning in SAT (52.67% vs. 49.33%) and matched Gemini 2.5 Flash on MMVP perception (80.33%). It also surpassed Qwen2.5-VL-72B on VSP by 34% and on BLINK-J by 10.67%, and outperformed GPT-4o by 24.67% on SAT.

**Conditions for Interleaved Reasoning Necessity:**
The study clarified when interleaved reasoning provides a critical advantage.
- It is most effective when text and images continuously inform each other, such as visualizing re-arranged pieces in Jigsaw Assembly, validating routes with overlaid arrows in Spatial Navigation, or pinpointing objects with bounding boxes in Visual Search.
- For tasks like Chart Refocus (in-domain ChartQA), textual reasoning alone sometimes suffices, making visual manipulation supplementary. However, for out-of-domain perception tasks (e.g., MMVP), interleaved reasoning proved essential for capturing and reasoning about precise visual cues that text alone could not convey.

**Emergent Properties:**
ThinkMorph exhibited three emergent properties indicative of higher-level multimodal intelligence:

1. **Unseen Visual Manipulations**: The model developed the ability to generate accurate and task-effective visual manipulations (e.g., zoom-in, inpainting, multi-box generation, motion forecasting, perspective transformation, region cropping) that were not explicitly present in its training data. These operations, sometimes accounting for up to 10% of visual operations during inference, were contextually appropriate and contributed directly to problem-solving, often triggered by specific textual cues (e.g., "examine closely" for zoom-in). This suggests that pretraining provides the manipulation ability, while interleaved fine-tuning directs it towards reasoning.

2. **Autonomous Mode Switching**: Despite being trained exclusively on interleaved data, the model autonomously switched to text-only reasoning in 5.3% of inference cases when generalizing to out-of-domain tasks. These instances, when the model switched modes, achieved an accuracy of 81.25%, a 7.29% improvement over cases where interleaved reasoning was maintained (73.96%). This switching behavior was task-adaptive: it occurred for tasks where initial visual encoding provided sufficient information for text-only reasoning, leading to efficiency gains (approximately 75% fewer tokens in switched samples). This demonstrates the model's ability to dynamically allocate reasoning effort.

3. **Better Test-Time Scaling via Diversified Thoughts**: Interleaved reasoning improved test-time scaling more effectively than unimodal approaches. Under Best-of-N sampling, it maintained consistent gains (e.g., +5.2% on VSP, +8.0% on BLINK-J), with improvements amplifying under distribution shifts. This advantage is attributed to the exploration of richer and more diverse reasoning trajectories within the broader multimodal solution space, which increases the likelihood of finding a correct answer as the number of samples (N) increases. Scaling patterns varied, with monotonic improvements for reasoning-intensive tasks and U-shaped scaling for perception-focused benchmarks, suggesting task-dependent benefits.

**Additional Analyses:**
- **Computational Cost**: Interleaved reasoning was approximately three times more computationally expensive than text-only reasoning. However, it often demonstrated a superior performance-cost trade-off on challenging vision-centric tasks.
- **Text Representation Enrichment**: Multimodal training enriched the model's text representations, leading to higher entropy and greater vocabulary diversity in textual outputs, including more visual-relational terms, even when the model autonomously switched to text-only mode.
- **Mode Switching with Test-time Scaling**: A hybrid model trained on a balanced dataset across reasoning modes showed that mode diversity further amplifies the benefits of test-time scaling, allowing for flexible alternation between modality strategies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on ThinkMorph contributes to the field of multimodal AI by providing a systematic analysis of interleaved reasoning and demonstrating its capabilities. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancement in Multimodal Reasoning**: The work establishes that treating text and images as complementary modalities that mutually reinforce each other is a generalizable approach for enhancing multimodal reasoning. This moves beyond prior limitations where modalities were often isomorphic or indirectly coordinated through tools.
- **Understanding Emergent AI Capabilities**: The identification of emergent properties—unseen visual manipulations, autonomous mode switching, and diversified test-time scaling—offers insights into how unified models can develop sophisticated, adaptive reasoning skills without explicit supervision. This suggests that interleaved training fosters a deeper internalization of problem-solving strategies, mirroring aspects of human intelligence.
- **Improved Generalizability and Robustness**: ThinkMorph's ability to achieve substantial performance gains on vision-centric benchmarks and generalize to out-of-domain tasks, often matching or surpassing much larger proprietary models, indicates a more robust and adaptable form of multimodal AI. This can lead to more reliable systems for complex visual and linguistic tasks.
- **New Avenues for Model Scaling**: The finding that interleaved reasoning enables superior test-time scaling by exploring broader multimodal solution spaces provides a novel direction for enhancing model robustness and accuracy. This diversity in reasoning trajectories, encompassing both visual and textual paths, offers a method to discover higher-quality solutions, particularly under challenging generalization conditions.
- **Foundation for Future Multimodal AI**: The research provides a framework for future work in several areas:
 - **Adaptive Mode Selection**: Further development of models that can intelligently select the optimal reasoning mode (text-only, visual-only, or interleaved) based on task complexity and data availability.
 - **Cross-Modal Alignment**: Improving objectives and mechanisms for coherent integration and alignment between visual and textual thoughts.
 - **Generative-Understanding Synergy**: Demonstrating that generative capabilities can directly enhance understanding capabilities within an interleaved Chain-of-Thought framework, breaking the perceived trade-off between the two.
- **Practical Applications**: The improved performance on tasks like spatial navigation, visual search, and chart analysis could lead to more capable AI assistants in various domains, including robotics, data analysis, and advanced human-computer interaction, where detailed visual understanding and manipulation are critical.

In conclusion, ThinkMorph represents a step towards creating multimodal AI systems that can reason more adaptively and comprehensively by integrating language and vision in a mutually reinforcing manner.
