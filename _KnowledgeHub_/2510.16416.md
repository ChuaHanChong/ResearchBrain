# SSL4RL: Revisiting Self-supervised Learning as Intrinsic Reward for Visual-Language Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The SSL4RL framework reinterprets self-supervised learning tasks as intrinsic, verifiable reward signals for reinforcement learning, providing a scalable method for post-training vision-language models. This approach consistently improves visual grounding and reasoning capabilities across diverse multimodal benchmarks and also generalizes effectively to graph learning.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often over-rely on linguistic priors, underutilizing visual evidence and resorting to 'textual shortcuts' in reasoning.
- Applying Reinforcement Learning (RL) to VLMs is limited by the scarcity of scalable, reliable, and verifiable reward mechanisms, with human preferences or explicit verifiers being costly or unavailable.
- The traditional use of Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) is primarily for pre-training, leaving its potential as a source of intrinsic, verifiable targets for fine-tuning behavioral alignment largely unexplored.

## Method

- Reformulates established ==SSL tasks== (e.g., Rotation, Jigsaw, Contrastive, Patch Position) to provide dense, automatic, and ==intrinsically verifiable reward signals== for RL-based post-training.
- Utilizes ==Grouped Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO)== to fine-tune multimodal models, maximizing the SSL-derived intrinsic rewards.
- Trains models to generate natural language reasoning paths that accurately predict the ground truth labels of the self-supervised tasks, thereby reinforcing visual grounding and reasoning.

## Results

- SSL4RL models achieve substantial performance gains on vision-language reasoning benchmarks, with average improvements of **7.39%** on MMBench and **8.94%** on SEED-Bench for the Qwen2.5-VL-3B model.
- The framework demonstrates enhanced robustness against various image perturbations and consistently improves performance on open-ended image captioning tasks.
- SSL4RL successfully generalizes to graph-structured data, showing improvements in node classification and link prediction tasks, confirming its domain-agnostic applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised learning tasks can effectively serve as intrinsic, verifiable reward signals for reinforcement learning, addressing the reward scarcity bottleneck in VLM alignment.
> - The effectiveness of SSL tasks as RL rewards adheres to a 'Goldilocks principle,' where task difficulty must be appropriately matched to model capacity for optimal performance.
> - SSL4RL fosters stronger visual grounding by compelling models to derive answers directly from image content, leading to reduced reliance on linguistic priors and more robust reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) represent a significant advancement in multimodal understanding by integrating the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) with visual inputs. This synergy has enabled a range of applications, including visual question answering and interactive dialogue. However, existing VLMs exhibit limitations in effectively utilizing visual evidence. In vision-centric tasks, where visual content is paramount, VLMs often rely on linguistic priors, leading to performance inferior to specialized vision models. Conversely, in vision-language reasoning tasks, VLMs may prioritize textual knowledge over visual grounding, a tendency that becomes more pronounced in long-form generation. These issues highlight a need for training methodologies that can enhance both visual grounding and robust reasoning.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a prominent paradigm for post-training large models, demonstrating its ability to align models with desired behaviors through preference-based signals. Verifier-driven RL, which utilizes automatically checkable rewards, has shown success in domains such as mathematics and programming where explicit programmatic verifiers are available. However, a fundamental challenge remains in domains lacking such verifiers: the scarcity of scalable and reliable reward mechanisms. In these contexts, training pipelines often resort to LLM-as-a-judge heuristics, which are noted for being biased, noisy, and susceptible to adversarial manipulation, thereby compromising their reliability.

Self-supervised learning (SSL) has been instrumental in representation learning across various modalities, including masked language modeling for text and contrastive learning or masked autoencoders for vision. A key characteristic of SSL tasks is their intrinsically verifiable targets. For instance, given a rotated image, the ground-truth rotation angle is unambiguous. These targets are inherent properties of the data itself, offering dense and reliable supervision at scale, unlike preference-based signals.

This research introduces SSL4RL to address the challenge of obtaining abundant and verifiable reinforcement signals for VLMs in domains where external verifiers are absent. It positions SSL tasks not merely as pre-training tools for representation learning, but as a source of intrinsic, verifiable reward functions for RL-based post-training. By reinterpreting corrupted inputs as defining RL trajectories and correctness as defining rewards, SSL4RL aims to bridge perceptual learning and reasoning alignment, specifically by encouraging models to generate natural language reasoning paths for visual tasks. This approach distinguishes itself from prior work like Jigsaw-R1 by generalizing the concept beyond a single task and offering a unified framework for leveraging diverse SSL objectives for multimodal model alignment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the SSL4RL framework are detailed as follows:

- **Address Visual Evidence Utilization Deficiencies in VLMs**: A core motivation stems from the observed weaknesses in how Vision-Language Models (VLMs) process visual information. VLMs often default to linguistic priors in tasks that demand precise visual understanding, leading to suboptimal performance in vision-centric scenarios. Furthermore, in complex vision-language reasoning tasks, they may exploit textual shortcuts rather than grounding their reasoning in visual evidence, particularly in generative tasks requiring longer outputs. The research aims to develop a method that reinforces visual grounding and robust reasoning capabilities simultaneously, ensuring models derive answers from image content rather than solely from learned textual knowledge.

- **Overcome the Scalability and Reliability Bottleneck of RL Rewards**: While Reinforcement Learning (RL) has proven effective for aligning large models, its application to VLMs is significantly constrained by the lack of scalable and reliable reward mechanisms. Many domains lack explicit programmatic verifiers, forcing reliance on human preference data (expensive and limited) or LLM-as-a-judge heuristics (prone to bias, noise, and manipulation). The research is motivated to find an alternative, automatic, and verifiable source of rewards that can be generated at scale without human intervention or external, potentially unreliable, AI evaluators.

- **Repurpose Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) Tasks as Verifiable Rewards**: The central objective is to propose a novel framework, SSL4RL, that reinterprets traditional SSL objectives as dense, automatic, and verifiable reward signals for RL-based fine-tuning. Instead of solely using SSL for pre-training to learn representations, this work aims to leverage the inherent, unambiguous ground-truth targets within SSL tasks (e.g., predicting image rotation angle, reconstructing masked patches) directly as intrinsic reinforcement signals. This eliminates the need for external labels or heuristic evaluators, providing a self-contained and scalable reward generation mechanism.

- **Promote Natural Language Reasoning for Visual Tasks**: Beyond merely improving perceptual accuracy, SSL4RL is motivated to encourage VLMs to articulate their reasoning processes in natural language when solving visual tasks. By framing SSL objectives within an RL context, the framework aims to bridge the gap between low-level perceptual learning and high-level linguistic reasoning, fostering models that not only solve visual problems but also explain their solutions.

- **Systematically Investigate Effective SSL Task Design**: A further objective is to conduct comprehensive ablation studies to identify the key factors influencing the effectiveness of different SSL tasks when used as RL rewards. This includes analyzing the impact of data volume, model scale, specific model architectures, task difficulty, and the semantic alignment of SSL tasks with target downstream domains. The motivation is to derive new design principles for future work in aligning multimodal models using self-supervised objectives.

- **Demonstrate Generality Across Modalities**: The research also aims to establish the broad applicability of the SSL4RL paradigm by extending it beyond the visual domain to other structured data, specifically graph learning. This objective seeks to confirm that the principle of generating verifiable rewards from data transformations is a versatile approach for multimodal alignment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SSL4RL framework is designed to integrate self-supervised learning (SSL) objectives with reinforcement learning (RL) for fine-tuning Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

**Framework Overview:**
The core idea involves transforming an input `x` (primarily images in the VLM context) via a corruption function `c(x)` into a context-target pair `(˜x, y)`. The VLM, acting as a policy `πθ`, conditions on `˜x` to generate a prediction `ˆy`. A verifiable reward `r(ˆy, y)` is then computed by comparing `ˆy` against the ground-truth target `y`. This process reinterprets each SSL task as an RL environment where the corruption defines the environment, the target specifies the verifiable ground truth, and the reward function provides supervision.

**SSL Pretext Tasks:**
Four representative SSL pretext tasks are revisited and adapted to generate RL rewards:

1. **Rotation Prediction**:
 - **Corruption**: An input image `x` is rotated counter-clockwise by a predefined angle `y`. The standard configuration uses angles from {0°, 90°, 180°, 270°}. For task difficulty scaling, a finer interval of 45° is also explored.
 - **Reward**: A binary reward `r = 1[ˆy = y]` is assigned, indicating 1 for correct prediction of the rotation angle and 0 otherwise.

2. **Jigsaw Puzzles**:
 - **Corruption**: An input image `x` is partitioned into a grid (e.g., 2x2, later 3x3 for difficulty scaling), and the resulting patches are randomly shuffled according to a permutation `y`.
 - **Reward**: A binary reward `r = 1[ˆy = y]` is given if the model correctly predicts the original permutation of patches.

3. **Contrastive Learning**:
 - **Corruption**: Standard augmentation pipelines are applied to generate two augmented views from an image. The default weak augmentation includes color jittering, grayscale conversion, Gaussian blur, horizontal flipping, and random resized cropping, each with 0.2 probability. For task difficulty scaling, a stronger augmentation strategy is used (0.8 probability, max crop scale 0.3).
 - **Reward**: A binary classification reward `r` is assigned based on whether two given views originate from the same source image. This imitates the InfoNCE similarity score, promoting high similarity for positive pairs and low for negative pairs.

4. **Patch Position Prediction**:
 - **Corruption**: An image is divided into a grid of quadrants (e.g., 2x2, later 3x3 for difficulty scaling), and a single patch is extracted from a specific location `y`.
 - **Reward**: A binary reward `r = 1[ˆy = y]` is given if the model correctly predicts the original spatial position (quadrant) of the extracted patch.

**Optimization Strategy:**
The framework utilizes Grouped Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO) for training. GRPO is an efficient policy-gradient method designed for large-scale LLM training. It optimizes the policy `πθ` by maximizing a regularized objective function:
`J(θ) = Eτ ∼πθ [R(τ) − β KL(πθ(·|τ) ∥ π0(·|τ))]`
where `R(τ)` is the SSL-derived reward for a trajectory `τ`, and `β` is a coefficient controlling the strength of KL regularization. The KL penalty prevents the fine-tuned policy from diverging significantly from a reference policy `π0` (e.g., the supervised fine-tuned model before RL), which stabilizes training. GRPO employs clipped policy-gradient updates, similar to PPO, but optimized for the computational demands of large batch LLM training. All experiments maintained a consistent GRPO configuration (group size 5, KL loss coefficient 0.01, entropy loss coefficient 0, context length 2048) to ensure that performance differences were attributable to the choice of SSL reward.

**Base Models and Training Infrastructure:**
Experiments primarily employed Qwen-2.5-VL-3B/7B-Instruct models, known for their performance and moderate size. An ablation study also included Gemma3-4B to assess generality. Training was performed on 8xA800 GPUs with a batch size of 512 (adjusted to 256 for Gemma3 due to computational constraints).

**Evaluation Settings:**
Evaluation was conducted using VLMEvalKit, with default sampling parameters (temperature 0.01, top-p 0.001, top-k 1).

**Extension to Graph Domain:**
To demonstrate generality, SSL4RL was extended to graph-structured data using three graph-based SSL tasks:
1. **Attribute Mask**: Randomly masks node descriptions, rewarding reconstruction accuracy.
2. **Neighbor Prediction**: Rewards correct identification of adjacent nodes for a target node in a partial graph.
3. **Link Prediction**: Rewards accurate classification of edge presence/absence between node pairs in a partial graph.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experimental results across various tasks and ablation studies, yielding several key findings.

#### 4.1. Vision-Language Reasoning Tasks

Experiments on benchmarks such as MMBench, SEED-Bench, V*, RealWorldQA, BLINK, and MME-RealWorld-Lite demonstrate that the SSL4RL paradigm substantially improves performance.
- **Performance Gains**: SSL4RL models (3B) outperformed the base model by an average of 7.39% on MMBench and 8.94% on SEED-Bench. Notable improvements included 39.00 percentage points on MMBench's Relation Reasoning task and 19.63 percentage points on SEED-Bench's Visual Reasoning task. Across additional benchmarks, SSL4RL yielded significant gains, such as +8.90% on V* and +9.55% on RealWorldQA.
- **Effective SSL Tasks**: Rotation and Position prediction tasks consistently produced the strongest performance gains. Position tasks fostered integrated spatial understanding by requiring models to combine fine-grained local details with overall scene layout. Rotation tasks were effective by compelling models to engage in "anti-commonsense" learning, challenging pre-trained priors and sharpening relational reasoning.
- **Contrastive Learning Efficacy**: The standard Contrastive task initially showed limited benefits or even degradation on some downstream tasks, hypothesized due to insufficiently challenging augmentations. Subsequent ablations confirmed that stronger augmentation strategies improved its effectiveness.
- **Qualitative Improvements**: Models trained with SSL4RL exhibited sharper attention alignment with text queries and reduced over-reliance on linguistic priors, demonstrating greater dependence on visual evidence.
- **Comparison to Oracle Rewards**: When compared to a strong baseline (Golden-3B) trained with oracle, task-specific rewards, SSL4RL variants achieved competitive performance (e.g., 81.35% vs. 84.93% on MMBench), indicating that self-supervised objectives can effectively close a significant portion of the performance gap without using downstream labels.

#### 4.2. Vision-Centric Tasks (ImageNet-1K Classification)

- SSL4RL fine-tuned models consistently outperformed the base model on ImageNet-1K classification across Completion, Choice-20, and Choice-200 question types.
- The Position task again led to the largest gains, achieving 67.14% vs. 57.10% for the base model on Choice-200.
- The Contrastive task, while less effective in VLM reasoning, showed competitive results here, benefiting from its strength in learning robust semantic representations invariant to augmentations, which is advantageous for instance discrimination.

#### 4.3. Open-ended Vision-Language Task (Image Captioning)

- On the CapArena platform, SSL4RL consistently improved image captioning performance over the base model, with the largest gain of 8.14 points (56.45 vs. 48.31).
- Qualitative analysis indicated enhanced detail capture and improved spatial reasoning in generated captions, aligning with the types of visual understanding reinforced by SSL pretext tasks.
- This demonstrated that self-supervised rewards can provide meaningful learning signals even in complex open-ended tasks lacking verifiable ground truth.

#### 4.4. Robustness Analysis

- **Perturbed MMBench**: SSL4RL models consistently exhibited superior robustness under both weak and strong image perturbations. For example, SSL4RL-Rotation achieved 72.11% average accuracy under weak perturbation compared to 66.80% for the base model.
- **VLM Robustness Benchmarks**: On a comprehensive benchmark with various corruption functions and severity levels, SSL4RL models outperformed the base model, with the performance advantage becoming more pronounced at higher corruption levels (e.g., +5.37% on Contrast-Level5).

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Data Volume Scaling**: Performance demonstrated a clear positive scaling relationship with increasing training data, indicating that SSL4RL effectively leverages larger, more diverse datasets for enhanced performance across most subtasks.
- **Model Size Scaling**: Gains were less pronounced for larger 7B models compared to 3B models. This was attributed to the fixed difficulty of the SSL tasks potentially failing to adequately challenge larger models, suggesting a "difficulty-capacity matching" principle.
- **Base Model Choice**: The benefits of SSL4RL extended to the Gemma3-4B model, yielding consistent average performance improvements (2.88% on MMBench), validating the framework's general applicability across different VLM architectures. The relative efficacy of individual SSL tasks remained consistent.
- **Task Difficulty Scaling**: The impact of increasing task difficulty varied significantly. Stronger augmentations markedly improved the Contrastive task. However, for already challenging tasks like Rotation and Jigsaw, increasing difficulty could be detrimental, potentially leading to over-specialization. This highlights a "Goldilocks Principle" where task difficulty must be appropriately matched to the model's capacity.
- **Task Combination**: A naive combination of all four SSL rewards did not yield significant cumulative improvements over the best single-reward setups. This suggests potential conflicts between distinct SSL objectives or challenges in effectively balancing their contributions.

#### 4.6. Extension to Other Domains (Graph Learning)

- The SSL4RL paradigm successfully generalized to graph-structured data. The 3B model showed substantial improvements (up to 13.79% average gain) on node classification and link prediction benchmarks.
- Consistent with visual domain findings, the 7B model exhibited diminishing returns.
- The relative effectiveness of graph SSL tasks was contingent on the downstream objective; tasks emphasizing structural reasoning performed better on link prediction, while feature reconstruction tasks were more advantageous for node classification. This reinforces the importance of aligning the pretext task's inductive bias with the target application.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SSL4RL framework represents a significant contribution to the field of multimodal learning by introducing a novel paradigm for post-training Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Scalable and Verifiable Reward Mechanism**: SSL4RL addresses a critical bottleneck in applying Reinforcement Learning (RL) to VLMs: the scarcity of scalable and reliable reward signals. By repurposing intrinsically verifiable targets from Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) tasks as dense, automatic rewards, the framework eliminates the reliance on costly human preference data, unreliable AI evaluators, or external programmatic verifiers. This offers a robust and scalable method for obtaining reinforcement signals, which is crucial for the continued development of large multimodal models.

- **Enhanced Visual Grounding and Reasoning**: The experimental results consistently demonstrate that SSL4RL substantially improves VLMs' performance on both vision-centric and vision-language reasoning benchmarks. This enhancement is attributed to improved visual grounding, leading to sharper attention mechanisms and reduced over-reliance on linguistic priors. This indicates a potential to develop VLMs that more effectively integrate and reason with visual evidence, fostering more robust and context-aware understanding.

- **Generality and Modality-Agnosticism**: The successful extension of SSL4RL to graph-structured data, in addition to its performance across diverse vision and vision-language tasks, highlights the framework's generalizability. This suggests that the core principle of deriving verifiable rewards from data transformations is broadly applicable across different data modalities, offering a versatile approach for aligning various types of multimodal models.

- **Insights into Effective SSL Task Design**: The comprehensive ablation studies provide valuable insights into the characteristics of effective SSL tasks within the RL context. The identification of the "Goldilocks Principle" of task difficulty—where difficulty must be appropriately matched to model capacity—and the nuanced impact of different SSL objectives on downstream tasks offer crucial design principles for future research. This challenges the assumption that all self-supervision is equally useful for reasoning alignment and underscores the importance of strategic task selection.

- **Path Towards More Capable and Safer AI**: By providing a method to align models using intrinsic, verifiable signals, SSL4RL opens a path towards developing more capable and potentially safer multimodal foundation models. Without needing external human feedback or potentially biased AI judges, the framework could lead to models that are more aligned with objective data properties and less susceptible to external manipulation or misguidance.

- **Future Research Directions**: SSL4RL lays the groundwork for future advancements, particularly in designing more complex and adaptive SSL objectives that can continue to challenge and refine larger models. It also prompts further exploration into sophisticated strategies for combining multiple SSL rewards to overcome the observed non-additivity, potentially through dynamic weighting, curriculum learning, or meta-learning approaches. The framework establishes a new avenue for unifying self-supervision and reinforcement learning to drive reasoning and alignment in advanced AI systems.
