# VideoWorld 2: Learning Transferable Knowledge from Real-world Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ByteDance Seed and Beijing Jiaotong University developed a dynamics-enhanced Latent Dynamics Model (dLDM) that effectively decouples action dynamics from visual appearance to learn transferable knowledge directly from unlabeled real-world videos. This method demonstrated superior success rates, achieving up to 72.3% on complex paper-folding tasks and significantly improving cross-domain task success on robotic manipulation benchmarks, while also generating high-quality, long-horizon task execution videos.

## Problem

- AI models primarily learning from text lack the rich information about world dynamics, spatial relationships, and physical laws present in visual data.
- Current video generation models and latent action models struggle to extract transferable knowledge for complex, long-horizon real-world tasks, often overfitting to irrelevant visual details.
- The entanglement of action dynamics and visual appearance in previous models hinders generalization and leads to degraded performance in novel environments.

## Method

- A dynamics-enhanced Latent Dynamics Model (dLDM) is proposed to robustly acquire knowledge by explicitly decoupling appearance modeling from action learning.
- A pretrained Video Diffusion Model (VDM) is leveraged for appearance generation, enabling dLDM's latent codes to focus on task-relevant dynamics without encoding fine-grained visual details.
- An autoregressive transformer is then trained to predict sequences of these compact, disentangled latent codes, facilitating long-horizon planning and task execution.

## Results

- Achieved a **68.8%** to **72.3%** success rate on step 7 of paper-folding tasks on the Video-CraftBench benchmark, representing up to a ~**70%** improvement over competing methods.
- Demonstrated superior cross-domain transferability in CALVIN robotic manipulation, achieving an average task success length of 2.88 when pre-trained on OpenX, surpassing other baselines.
- Produced high-quality, long-horizon task execution videos with improved visual fidelity, reflected in SSIM of 0.770 and LPIPS of 0.205, while effectively filtering out irrelevant visual details.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly disentangling action dynamics from visual appearance modeling is crucial for learning transferable knowledge from diverse, real-world video data.
> - Pretrained Video Diffusion Models can effectively handle visual appearance, allowing latent dynamic representations to focus purely on task-relevant actions.
> - Utilizing low-fidelity motion cues to condition the VDM, in a gradient-stopped manner, stabilizes training and enhances long-horizon sequence generation by refining appearance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen remarkable progress, largely driven by large-scale models trained on text data (e.g., LLMs like PaLM, Qwen, Llama, Gemini, Mistral, cited in the paper). However, the authors argue that "text alone cannot fully capture the rich information of the real visual world, including world dynamics, spatial relationships, and underlying physical laws." This paper positions itself at the forefront of a paradigm shift: enabling AI models to acquire **transferable knowledge directly from raw, unlabeled video data**, mimicking how humans and animals learn from visual observations.

VideoWorld 2 addresses several intertwined research areas:

- **Video Generation:** The paper acknowledges the advancements in video generation, particularly diffusion-based models (like Sora, Veo, HunyuanVideo, Wan) for photorealism and autoregressive (AR) models for causal sequence modeling (Lumos-1, VideoPoet). However, it critically observes that while these models generate high-fidelity visuals, they "fail to learn task-relevant dynamics or long-horizon policies" in complex, real-world tasks. VideoWorld 2 attempts to combine the strengths of both by leveraging diffusion models for appearance and AR models for dynamics, but with a novel decoupling mechanism. It also differentiates its "disentanglement" objective from prior works that typically separate motion from appearance for style transfer or editing, aiming instead for task-relevant, transferable dynamics.
- **World Models:** World models, which aim to learn physical dynamics for simulation and planning, are a core inspiration. Current approaches often focus on short-term dynamics for synthesis or sample-efficient planning in reinforcement learning. VideoWorld 2 extends this by tackling "the distinct challenge of learning transferable knowledge for complex, long-horizon tasks directly from unlabeled real-world videos," emphasizing long-term reasoning and planning beyond simple prediction.
- **Learning from Unlabeled Videos / Latent Action Models:** Extracting meaningful and transferable representations of visual dynamics from unlabeled videos is a significant challenge. Existing latent action models (LAMs) often focus on short-horizon tasks, struggle with substantial appearance variation, or rely on reconstruction objectives that entangle dynamics with irrelevant visual details. VideoWorld 2's predecessor, VideoWorld, demonstrated the potential of latent dynamic models in synthetic environments but faltered in real-world complexity. This paper directly addresses the limitations of previous LAMs and VideoWorld by proposing a more robust disentanglement mechanism for real-world scenarios.

In essence, VideoWorld 2 aims to bridge the gap between high-fidelity video generation, robust world modeling, and practical skill acquisition in real-world settings. It critiques the shortcomings of existing models when faced with the diversity, complexity, and long-horizon nature of real-world video tasks, proposing a novel approach to extract truly transferable knowledge.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to answer the fundamental question: **"Can AI models learn transferable knowledge for complex, long-horizon tasks directly from unlabeled real-world videos?"**

The authors are motivated by several critical observations and limitations of current AI paradigms:

1. **Limitations of Text-centric Knowledge:** While large language models excel, they are inherently limited by the symbolic nature of text. The rich, nuanced information about world dynamics, spatial relationships, and physical laws, which is abundant in visual data, remains largely untapped for direct knowledge acquisition. Animals and humans learn extensively through visual observation, suggesting a more natural and scalable path for AI.
2. **Failure of Existing Video Models in Real-world, Long-horizon Tasks:**
 - **Prior VideoWorld:** The original VideoWorld successfully learned knowledge from *synthetic* videos (e.g., Go games, simulated robotics). However, its approach and model design proved inadequate for the "substantial visual diversity, complex action dynamics, and often long-horizon, multi-step interactions" present in real-world videos. It failed to extract core task-solving knowledge or generalize in novel real-world settings.
 - **State-of-the-art Video Generation Models:** Even advanced video generation models, capable of producing high-fidelity visuals, "fail to faithfully represent task execution" for long-horizon tasks. The authors conjecture these models "are unable to disentangle task-core actions embedded in visual changes and motion dynamics, and instead overfit to irrelevant visual details." This leads to "degraded task performance and unstable long-horizon generation."
 - **Latent Action Models (LAMs):** Existing LAMs typically focus on short-horizon tasks or suffer from similar disentanglement issues, as their reconstruction objectives force latent codes to capture extraneous visual details alongside dynamics.
3. **Need for Transferable Knowledge:** The ultimate goal is for AI to acquire "generalizable knowledge" that can be applied across "diverse scenarios," much like a child can adapt paper-folding skills to different paper materials. This necessitates representations that isolate task-critical actions from superficial appearance variations.

To address these motivations, VideoWorld 2's core objective is to **robustly acquire transferable knowledge by explicitly decoupling appearance modeling from action learning.** This disentanglement is hypothesized to enable the learning of concise, semantically meaningful, and transferable latent actions, leading to significantly more robust and generalizable policies for complex, long-horizon tasks in real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VideoWorld 2 introduces a novel approach centered around a **dynamics-enhanced Latent Dynamics Model (dLDM)**, designed to overcome the limitations of previous methods by decoupling appearance modeling from action learning. The overall framework involves two main training stages and an inference stage.

#### 3.1. Generative Knowledge Learning Framework

The research frames knowledge acquisition as a generative problem: training a video generator $\rho$ to model the conditional distribution of future frames $x_{t+1}$ given historical observations $x_{0:t}$. This generative model then acts as a policy $\pi(\cdot|x_{0:t}) : X \rightarrow A$, predicting actions to execute tasks. Traditional video generation models (e.g., VQ-VAE based) are deemed inefficient for this due to high spatiotemporal redundancy in their representations, hindering the extraction of essential task knowledge.

#### 3.2. Latent Dynamic Model (LDM) Limitations

The original VideoWorld introduced an LDM that compressed future visual changes into compact latent codes using a causal VQ-VAE. While effective in synthetic settings, it struggled in the real world. The core issue identified is the **"entanglement of dynamics and appearance"**: its latent codes inadvertently captured irrelevant visual details (background, lighting, texture, camera motion), making the model sensitive to environmental changes and limiting generalization. This led to "scene drift and erroneous actions" in novel real-world environments.

#### 3.3. Dynamics-enhanced Latent Dynamics Model (dLDM)

To address the entanglement problem, VideoWorld 2 proposes the dLDM, a significant innovation over its predecessor. The dLDM aims to robustly acquire knowledge by explicitly decoupling appearance modeling from action learning.

The dLDM comprises the following key components and mechanisms:

- **Causal VQ-VAE for Visual Dynamic Compression:** This component, similar to the original LDM, encodes future visual changes from an input video clip $x_{0:T}$ into a sequence of discrete latent codes $z = \{z_{n_k}\}_{k=1, n=1}^{K,N}$. The causal encoder uses a 3D CNN, and learnable query embeddings capture change information via cross-attention, which are then quantized. The critical difference is *what these latent codes are optimized to represent*.
- **Pretrained Video Diffusion Model (VDM) for Appearance Modeling:** This is the core innovation. Instead of relying on a simple VQ-VAE decoder for reconstruction, VideoWorld 2 leverages a powerful, pretrained VDM (specifically, the NVIDIA Cosmos DiT 2B model) to handle visual appearance modeling. By offloading the high-fidelity visual reconstruction to the VDM, the dLDM encoder and its latent codes are "relieved from encoding fine-grained visual details" and can instead focus exclusively on capturing "task-relevant dynamics" – the abstract, transferable actions required for task completion.
 - The latent codes $z$ are provided to the VDM through a projection layer and causal cross-attention, guiding its generation.
 - Causal attention in the VDM ensures that generated features at time $t$ only depend on information up to time $t$, preserving temporal consistency.
- **Motion Conditioning with Original VQ-VAE Decoder (ControlNet-like Branch):** Directly training the VDM to generate future frames from noise based *only* on latent codes can be slow and prone to incorrect motion, as the VDM isn't inherently trained on target task dynamics. To stabilize training and provide coarse temporal cues, the dLDM reuses the VQ-VAE decoder (from the causal VQ-VAE) to reconstruct *low-fidelity, motion-rich outputs* from the latent codes. This "coarse temporal cues" (e.g., hand movements, object displacements) are fed into the VDM via a gradient-stopped, ControlNet-like branch. This allows the VDM to focus on refining appearance rather than inferring motion from scratch. Gradient stopping prevents the decoder's reconstruction loss from influencing the latent code learning in a way that would reintroduce appearance entanglement.

#### 3.4. Autoregressive Transformer for Long-horizon Reasoning

After the dLDM extracts the compact, disentangled latent codes, an autoregressive transformer (NVIDIA Cosmos AR 4B) models these sequences. The transformer is trained to predict future latent dynamics, conditioned on the initial frame and a task instruction. This allows the model to learn the long-term patterns and policies required for complex, multi-step tasks.

#### 3.5. Inference

During inference, given an initial image and a task instruction from a new, unseen environment, the transformer predicts a sequence of future latent dynamic codes. The dLDM then decodes these codes into a coherent, long-horizon task execution video, demonstrating the transferability of the learned knowledge. The entire process allows for auto-regressive generation of extended action sequences.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VideoWorld 2 demonstrates significant advancements in learning transferable knowledge from real-world videos, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines on challenging benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Results on Video-CraftBench (Handicraft Tasks)

The researchers introduced **Video-CraftBench**, a novel benchmark of first-person videos of long-horizon handicraft tasks (paper folding, block building). This benchmark features fine-grained manipulation, deformable materials, diverse appearances, and long task durations, designed to test knowledge transferability in unseen environments.

- **Superior Task Success Rate:** VideoWorld 2 achieved remarkable improvements in sequential task success rates. For paper folding, it reached a final step (step 7) success rate of **68.8%** when trained solely on Video-CraftBench, and **72.3%** when combined with OpenX pre-training. This represents a substantial improvement of up to **~70%** over competing methods (e.g., Wan 2.2 14B's 0% at step 7, VideoWorld's 0% without OpenX, and 31.9% with OpenX). For block-stacking, it achieved 81.5% (Craft-only) and 85.8% (OpenX+Craft), outperforming others.
- **Robust Long-horizon Execution:** While state-of-the-art video generation models (Cosmos AR/DiT, HunyuanVideo, Wan) achieved reasonable success in early steps, their performance rapidly deteriorated for long sequences, often failing entirely by step 4. VideoWorld 2, in contrast, consistently produced "complete and coherent task sequences in test environments."
- **Enhanced Visual Quality:** Leveraging the pretrained VDM, VideoWorld 2 generated videos with significantly higher visual quality, reflected in improved SSIM (0.770 vs. ~0.72 for best VDM baseline, 0.680 for VideoWorld 1) and lower LPIPS (0.205 vs. ~0.24 for best VDM baseline, 0.351 for VideoWorld 1) scores. This indicates accurate actions coupled with high-fidelity appearance.
- **Efficient Knowledge Extraction:** VideoWorld 2 efficiently extracts core task-relevant information, filtering out extraneous details like background clutter and object variations, leading to effective generalization.

#### 4.2. Results on CALVIN (Robotic Manipulation)

To assess cross-domain generalization and knowledge transfer, VideoWorld 2 was evaluated on the CALVIN robotics environment, using the OpenX dataset for pre-training.

- **Improved In-Domain Latent Pre-training:** When pre-trained on CALVIN's own latent codes (10% data), VideoWorld 2 (1.87 Avg. Len.) significantly outperformed LAPA (1.49 Avg. Len.) and approached the performance of an Oracle model trained on 22k full action labels (2.36 Avg. Len.), demonstrating high data efficiency in learning manipulation skills.
- **Strong Cross-Domain Transferability:** Pre-training on the 1.3M OpenX dataset (diverse robotic demonstrations) substantially boosted VideoWorld 2's performance on CALVIN, achieving an average task success length of **2.88**, outperforming LAPA (2.51) and even an OpenX-pretrained video next-token prediction baseline (2.46). This validates the effectiveness of dLDM's latent codes for transferring manipulation knowledge across different agents and environments.
- **Similar Latent Dynamic Features:** Qualitative analysis showed that similar latent codes in OpenX and Video-CraftBench correspond to similar motion patterns despite differing environments and agents, underscoring the learned representations' transferability.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Key Insights

Ablation studies provided critical insights into the dLDM's design:

- **VDM's Role in Code Transferability:** The incorporation of the VDM prior yielded a **~30% increase in success rate** and improved LPIPS. UMAP visualizations confirmed that with VDM, latent codes for the same action clustered much more tightly across distinct environments (e.g., CALVIN and Bridge), indicating superior cross-environment consistency and transferable dynamics, unlike VideoWorld's codes which diverged strongly by environment.
- **Effect of Original Decoder and ControlNet Guidance:** Stopping the gradient from the VQ-VAE decoder improved success rates by ~20%, indicating it initially introduced noise. However, using the decoder's low-fidelity, motion-rich reconstructions as ControlNet-like conditioning for the VDM (with gradient stopped) significantly stabilized output and yielded further improvements (~0.9 LPIPS, up to ~20% task success), particularly for long-horizon tasks.
- **Hyperparameter Effects:**
 - **Query Embedding Length (N):** N=4 provided the best balance, capturing sufficient information without introducing excessive noise or increasing sequence length too much.
 - **LDM/VDM Interplay:** The combination of a projection layer with causal self-attention and causal cross-attention significantly enhanced LDM-VDM interaction.
 - **dLDM Context Length (T):** Performance improved with increasing context length, plateauing at T=93 frames (approx. 5 seconds), which aligns with the VDM's maximum context.
 - **dLDM Codebook Size:** Complex tasks like Video-CraftBench benefited from larger codebook sizes (e.g., 1000) than simpler tasks (CALVIN), but excessively large codebooks hindered convergence.
- **VDM Training Strategies:** Full fine-tuning of the pretrained VDM was most effective. Random initialization failed, and freezing the VDM (updating only VQ-VAE) underperformed, highlighting the need for VDM adaptation to fine-grained manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VideoWorld 2 represents a significant step forward in the quest to enable AI models to learn from the vast, unstructured visual data of the real world. Its contributions and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Pioneering Real-world Knowledge Learning:** This work is "the first to investigate learning transferable knowledge for complex, long-horizon tasks directly from raw real-world videos." This pushes the frontier beyond synthetic environments, demonstrating that generalizable skills can be extracted from messy, unlabeled visual demonstrations.
- **Critical Insight: Decoupling Dynamics and Appearance:** The core finding that "disentangling action dynamics from visual appearance modeling is critical for effective knowledge acquisition" in real-world scenarios is a profound insight. It offers a new design principle for future embodied AI and robot learning systems, suggesting that models should explicitly separate what to do (dynamics) from how it looks (appearance).
- **Introduction of dLDM:** The dynamics-enhanced Latent Dynamics Model (dLDM) is a novel architectural contribution that effectively implements this decoupling. By leveraging pretrained Video Diffusion Models for appearance and focusing latent codes on abstract dynamics, it offers a robust solution for transferable skill learning. This architecture could inspire new designs for world models, imitation learning, and policy learning from observation.
- **New Benchmark for Fine-grained Reasoning:** The introduction of **Video-CraftBench** addresses a "largely unexplored challenge of fine-grained, long-horizon visual reasoning in real-world handicraft tasks." This benchmark provides a crucial testing ground for future research, fostering advancements in areas like deformable object manipulation, multi-step planning, and generalization across diverse visual conditions.
- **Advancing Embodied AI and Robotics:** The strong results on CALVIN, particularly the cross-domain transfer from OpenX, indicate that VideoWorld 2 can acquire valuable manipulation knowledge for robotics. This paves the way for robots to learn complex skills directly from human demonstrations or other robot videos, significantly reducing the need for expensive, domain-specific data collection and expert labeling. This could accelerate the development of more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems.
- **Scaling Knowledge Acquisition:** By tapping into the "vast abundance of video content available on the internet," this research offers a pathway for AI to scale its knowledge acquisition capabilities beyond text. This aligns with the long-term goal of building general-purpose AI agents capable of understanding and interacting with the physical world.
- **Open Science Contribution:** The commitment to open-sourcing "all code, data, and models" is a substantial contribution to the research community, enabling reproducibility, further development, and wider adoption of the techniques proposed.

In conclusion, VideoWorld 2 presents a compelling vision for learning robust and transferable knowledge from real-world videos. Its success in decoupling dynamics from appearance, demonstrated across complex handicraft and robotic tasks, marks a significant stride toward building more intelligent and capable AI agents that can truly learn from observation.
