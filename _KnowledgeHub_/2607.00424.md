# Robust Operational Space Control with Conformal Disturbance Bounds for Safe Redundant Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Houston developed a robust operational space control framework for redundant manipulators, integrating an Extended State Observer (ESO) for real-time disturbance estimation with Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) enhanced by sliding-window conformal prediction for probabilistic safety guarantees. This approach achieves millimeter-level task-space tracking accuracy and ensures real-time safety at 1 kHz, outperforming conventional methods by providing robust compensation and less conservative safety enforcement.

## Problem

- Classical Operational Space Computed Torque Control (OSCTC) experiences significant performance degradation due to model uncertainties and external disturbances.
- Existing methods for OSCTC robustness, such as data-driven or adaptive control, often require full-state measurements, lack rigorous theoretical guarantees, or struggle with unstructured disturbances.
- Providing rigorous yet practical safety guarantees for redundant manipulators under dynamic uncertainties is challenging, as robust CBF designs typically rely on conservative, pre-defined disturbance bounds.

## Method

- An ==Extended State Observer (ESO)== is integrated into the operational space control for each task-space dimension to estimate lumped disturbances using only position measurements, avoiding noisy numerical differentiation.
- A robust **Operational Space Computed Torque Controller (OSCTC)** is designed to actively compensate for these real-time disturbance estimates, ensuring accurate task-space tracking.
- A robust high-order ==Control Barrier Function (CBF)== is employed for safety, with its disturbance-variation bound adaptively estimated online using a ==sliding-window conformal prediction== mechanism, providing probabilistic and less conservative safety guarantees.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved stable, millimeter-level task-space tracking errors (mean-square error **1.712e-05 m**) on a 7-DoF Franka manipulator, demonstrating superior robustness compared to conventional OSCTC (**2.033e-04 m**) and residual learning (**7.531e-05 m**) under sudden payload changes.
- Hardware experiments confirmed guaranteed safety at **1 kHz**, with the robust CBF and adaptive conformal prediction preventing any constraint violations, unlike conventional CBF or ESO-only CBF which experienced violations.
- The online estimated adaptive conformal disturbance bounds provided significantly tighter and less conservative uncertainty estimations, dynamically adapting to changes in disturbance variance and enabling more precise CBF enforcement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Extended State Observers can effectively estimate complex, lumped disturbances in operational space using only position measurements, offering smooth and theoretically convergent estimates superior to raw residuals or some data-driven approaches.
> - Combining model-based disturbance estimation (ESO) with data-driven, distribution-free uncertainty quantification (conformal prediction) enables the provision of statistically rigorous yet practically less conservative safety guarantees for control barrier functions.
> - Online, adaptive estimation of disturbance variation bounds via sliding-window conformal prediction allows for dynamic adjustment of safety parameters, leading to tighter bounds and improved performance compared to relying on fixed, worst-case assumptions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Redundant robotic manipulators are increasingly utilized in complex environments, particularly those involving human interaction or spatial constraints, due to their inherent dexterity and flexibility. These robots can exploit their additional degrees of freedom to perform secondary tasks such as obstacle avoidance, posture optimization, and facilitating human-robot collaboration while executing primary tasks.

Control strategies for such manipulators are typically formulated either in joint space or operational (task) space. Joint-space control requires complex inverse kinematics solutions for redundant systems, necessitating additional redundancy resolution techniques to coordinate task-space objectives with constraints. In contrast, Operational Space Computed Torque Control (OSCTC) offers a direct method for regulating end-effector motion in task space, while retaining the null-space freedom for secondary objectives.

A significant challenge for classical OSCTC is its reliance on precise knowledge of the robot's dynamic model. When model uncertainties (e.g., imperfect dynamic modeling, payload variations, unmodeled friction) or external disturbances (e.g., unpredictable human forces) are present, the performance of classical OSCTC degrades, leading to reduced tracking accuracy and robustness.

Current approaches to enhance OSCTC robustness have limitations:
- **Data-driven or learning-based methods (e.g., residual learning):** These techniques aim to approximate unknown dynamics. However, they typically require full-state measurements, which often involve noisy numerical differentiation of position signals in real systems. Furthermore, they may lack rigorous theoretical guarantees and can be sensitive to shifts in data distribution, introducing additional design complexity related to network architecture and training.
- **Adaptive control schemes:** These methods are designed to estimate structured uncertainties. While effective for known forms of uncertainty, their performance can be limited when confronted with unstructured or unmodeled disturbances.

This research positions itself within this landscape by seeking to combine the strengths of model-based control with data-driven uncertainty quantification. It aims to develop a robust OSCTC framework that integrates an Extended State Observer (ESO) for model-based disturbance estimation and Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) for safety, enhanced by a data-driven Conformal Prediction mechanism to manage uncertainty bounds. This approach specifically addresses the challenge of providing rigorous safety guarantees under dynamic uncertainties while maintaining accurate task-space tracking. The work distinguishes itself from related research, such as that integrating tracking control and CBFs in task space, by explicitly incorporating dynamic uncertainties into its formulation for safe task-space control.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust operational space control framework for redundant robotic manipulators that can achieve accurate task-space tracking and provide rigorous safety guarantees even in the presence of dynamic uncertainties.

This overarching objective is motivated by several specific challenges and desired improvements:

1. **Overcoming limitations of classical OSCTC under uncertainty:** Classical OSCTC's performance degrades when the accurate dynamic model assumptions are violated by internal disturbances (e.g., imperfect modeling, friction, payload changes) or external disturbances (e.g., human interaction). The motivation is to enhance OSCTC's robustness to these uncertainties.
2. **Addressing shortcomings of existing robustness methods:**
 - **Residual learning:** While promising, residual learning often requires full-state measurements, where derivative terms (velocities, accelerations) can be noisy in practice. It also frequently lacks formal theoretical guarantees regarding convergence and robustness, and its design complexity can be significant. The motivation is to provide a method that avoids full-state measurements, offers theoretical support for state convergence, and maintains a simpler control structure.
 - **Adaptive control:** Adaptive methods are effective for structured uncertainties but less so for unstructured disturbances. The motivation is to create a framework capable of handling both structured and unstructured disturbances.
3. **Ensuring rigorous and practical safety guarantees:** Safety is paramount for redundant manipulators operating in constrained and human-interactive environments. Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) offer a formal approach to enforce safety, but robust CBF designs typically require a known, fixed bound on disturbance variations to guarantee absolute safety. This often leads to overly conservative control actions, hindering performance. The motivation is to reduce this conservatism while maintaining high confidence in safety.
4. **Integrating model-based robustness with data-driven adaptability:** The paper aims to leverage the benefits of both model-based control (e.g., explicit theoretical guarantees, structured design) and data-driven methods (e.g., adaptability to unknown dynamics, online quantification of uncertainty). The motivation is to combine these paradigms to create a comprehensive framework that is both robust and flexible.
5. **Online estimation of disturbance variation bounds:** A key limitation in robust CBF design is the requirement for a pre-specified disturbance-variation bound. The motivation is to develop a method for estimating this bound online, in a data-driven and distribution-free manner, to enable more practical and less conservative probabilistic safety guarantees.

In summary, the core motivation is to develop a control strategy that can enable redundant manipulators to operate reliably and safely in dynamic, uncertain, and human-interactive environments by integrating robust disturbance estimation, formal safety mechanisms, and adaptive, data-driven uncertainty quantification.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The proposed methodology integrates an Extended State Observer (ESO) for real-time disturbance estimation, a robust Operational Space Computed Torque Controller (OSCTC) for task-space tracking, and a Control Barrier Function (CBF) enhanced by sliding-window conformal prediction for probabilistic safety guarantees.

**A. System Dynamics and Operational Space Control:**
The robotic system dynamics are initially described by the Euler-Lagrange formulation in joint space (Equation 1). These dynamics are then transformed into task space, defining the end-effector acceleration `¨x` in terms of joint quantities (Equations 2-4). The classical OSCTC structure is adopted, decomposing the control torque `τ` into a task-space force `F` and a null-space torque `τ0` for secondary objectives (Equation 5). This leads to task-space dynamics (Equation 6) where `a_cmd` is the desired commanded acceleration for exponential trajectory tracking (Equation 8), and `F` is computed to achieve this (Equation 9).

**B. Operational Space ESO-Based Disturbance Estimation:**
The presence of unknown disturbances `f` (arising from model uncertainties, friction, payload variations, external interactions) is explicitly considered by reformulating the task-space dynamics (Equation 15). For each dimension `i` of the task space, the uncertain dynamics are treated as a second-order system (Equation 17). An Extended State Observer (ESO) (Equation 18) is designed for each dimension to estimate the lumped disturbance `f_i`.
- The ESO augments the system state with the disturbance `f_i` (defined as `x_3i`).
- It relies only on position measurements (`x_1i`), avoiding noisy numerical differentiation for velocities and accelerations.
- The observer gains (`L_1i`, `L_2i`, `L_3i`) are selected to ensure Hurwitz stability of the error dynamics (Equation 19), with observer bandwidth (`ω_oi`) as the primary tuning parameter.
- The paper contrasts this with residual learning, highlighting ESO's advantages in requiring only position measurements, providing explicit exponential error convergence, and maintaining a simpler design with minimal tuning.

**C. Robust OSCTC Design:**
With the estimated disturbance `ˆf` from the ESO, the task-space force `F` is reformulated into a robust form (Equation 22) to actively compensate for these disturbances. A key aspect is the demonstration (Lemma 1) that the null-space torque does not affect task-space tracking, even under uncertain inertia, ensuring decoupling.

**D. Robust High-Order CBF for Safe Control:**
Safety is enforced using a Control Barrier Function (CBF). The system with disturbance is formulated as a control-affine system (Equation 26). A continuously differentiable function `h(x)` defines a safety set `C_s = {x | h(x) ≥ 0}`. For systems with relative degree `r`, a higher-order CBF is used (Definition 1).
- **Disturbance Variation Bound:** The robust CBF design (Theorem 1) incorporates a bound `Γ` on the ESO estimation error `|f_i(k) - ˆf_i(k)|`. This bound depends on observer bandwidth (`ω_oi`), sampling time (`T_s`), and a crucial, but typically unknown, disturbance-variation bound `l_fi` (Assumption 1).
- **QP-CBF Formulation:** The control problem is then formulated as a Quadratic Program (QP) (Equation 34), where the objective is to minimize the deviation from a nominal control law `k(x)` subject to the CBF constraint, which ensures `h(x) ≥ 0` and its derivatives remain non-negative.
- **Specific Safety Constraint:** For experimental validation, wall-like safety constraints along Cartesian axes are defined using a scalar projection `ζ` (Equation 29). The barrier function `h(ζ)` (Equation 32) is designed for a second-order relative degree system, requiring the condition `¨h + k_1 ˙h + k_0h ≥ 0` (Equation 33).

**E. Probabilistic Robust CBF via Sliding-Window Conformal Disturbance Bounds:**
To address the practical challenge of the unknown `l_fi`, a data-driven, distribution-free method using sliding-window conformal prediction is employed.
- **Residual Derivative Estimation:** The derivative of the disturbance `f` is approximated by computing the discrete-time derivative `r(k)` of the algebraic residual `f_res_i(k)` (Equations 35-36). The residual `f_res_i(k)` is calculated directly from observed acceleration and nominal dynamics.
- **Conformal Prediction:** A sliding window `R(k)` of recent absolute residual derivative values is maintained (Equation 37). The empirical `(1 − α)` quantile `l_α_i(k)` of `R(k)` is computed online (Equation 38).
- **Probabilistic Bound:** Under the exchangeability assumption within the window, this quantile provides a probabilistic guarantee `P(|˙f_res_i(k)| ≤ l_α_i(k)) ≥ 1 − α` (Equation 39).
- **Adaptive Error Bound:** This `l_α_i(k)` is then used as the `l_fi` in the ESO error bound `Γ_i` (Equations 40-41), generating an online, probabilistic bound on the ESO estimation error. This adaptive approach aims to provide less conservative safety guarantees than using a fixed, worst-case `l_fi`.
- **Safety Guarantee:** With `l_α_i(k)`, the forward invariance of the safe set is guaranteed with a probability of at least `1 − α`.

**F. Experimental Validation:**
The framework is validated on a 7-DoF Franka Research 3 (FR3) manipulator, both in simulation and hardware, operating at 1 kHz.
- **Disturbance Injection:** A specific nonlinear state-dependent disturbance model (Equation 42) is used to evaluate robustness.
- **Safety Setup:** A wall-like constraint along the y-axis (`y ≤ 0.3m`) is chosen for safety evaluation.
- **Comparisons:** The proposed method is compared against conventional OSCTC, residual learning (with a neural network), conventional CBF without disturbance compensation, and robust CBF with fixed disturbance bounds.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research presents several key findings from both simulation and hardware experiments conducted on a 7-DoF Franka Research 3 manipulator.

**A. Simulation: Disturbance Estimation**
- **ESO Accuracy and Smoothness:** In simulation, where the ground truth injected external disturbance is known, the proposed ESO estimate closely tracked the true external disturbance. The ESO output demonstrated significantly reduced noise and variation compared to the raw computed residual signal. This indicates the ESO's effectiveness in filtering high-frequency components while accurately estimating the underlying disturbance trend.
- **Residual vs. NN:** The raw computed residual exhibited the same overall trend as the injected disturbance but was noticeably noisier, capturing both internal model mismatch and external disturbances, and being sensitive to state measurement noise. The neural network (NN) prediction, while following the residual trend, also showed a noticeable bias.

**B. Hardware: Tracking Under Sudden External Disturbance**
- **Enhanced Tracking Accuracy:** In an experiment involving a lemniscate trajectory with a sudden payload attachment (a partially filled water bottle at t=15s), the proposed method achieved superior tracking performance.
 - Before the disturbance, all controllers (Conventional OSCTC, Residual learning, Proposed method) tracked similarly.
 - After the payload attachment, conventional OSCTC exhibited noticeable deviations from the reference trajectory.
 - Residual learning showed some improvement over conventional OSCTC but still displayed greater deviations.
 - The proposed method maintained a stable, millimeter-level tracking error throughout the experiment, demonstrating robust compensation against sudden and time-varying disturbances. The mean-square error for the proposed method (1.712e-05 m) was substantially lower than conventional OSCTC (2.033e-04 m) and residual NN (7.531e-05 m).
- **Hardware Disturbance Estimation:** Consistent with simulation, the ESO estimate in hardware was significantly less noisy than the raw residual, even though it followed its general trend. The residual variation substantially increased after the payload was attached. The NN estimate, similar to simulation, tracked the residual trend but presented a noticeable bias.

**C. Hardware: Safety Evaluation**
- **Guaranteed Safety with Adaptive Conformal Prediction:** The safety evaluation, focused on a constraint `y ≤ 0.3 m` under model uncertainty, demonstrated the effectiveness of the proposed robust CBF with adaptive conformal prediction.
 - **Conventional CBF Failure:** A conventional CBF without disturbance compensation failed to maintain safety, violating the `h(x) ≥ 0` condition, meaning the robot crossed the safety boundary.
 - **ESO Improvement:** Incorporating ESO for disturbance compensation improved safety performance, but some violations still occurred due to unquantified estimation errors.
 - **Reduced Conservatism and No Violations:** The proposed CBF method, which integrates ESO with adaptive sliding-window conformal prediction for disturbance bounds, successfully guaranteed safety, preventing any violations of the constraint. Moreover, this approach was less conservative compared to robust CBF versions employing fixed, pre-specified confidence levels (e.g., 90% or 100% fixed bounds), leading to a narrower interval between the upper and lower disturbance bounds.
- **Adaptive Bound Performance:** The online estimated adaptive conformal disturbance bound (`l_α(t)`) effectively bounded the residual errors, demonstrating its ability to adapt to changes in disturbance variance. In contrast, static conformal prediction bounds calculated from offline data did not adapt to these variances. The ability to estimate a dynamic, tighter bound `Γ` from `l_α(t)` allowed for more precise and less conservative CBF enforcement.

In summary, the experiments confirmed that the proposed framework delivers millimeter-level tracking accuracy and ensures real-time safety at 1 kHz under various disturbances. It outperforms conventional model-based and data-driven residual learning approaches in terms of robustness, achieved performance, and reduced conservatism in safety guarantees.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for redundant manipulators operating in complex, uncertain, and human-interactive environments. The core significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Robustness to Dynamic Uncertainties:** The integration of an Extended State Observer (ESO) into the operational space control framework provides a robust mechanism for compensating for both structured and unstructured dynamic disturbances. This is critical for real-world applications where perfect dynamic models are impractical and external interactions are common. The ESO's ability to estimate disturbances using only position measurements, combined with its theoretical convergence guarantees, offers a more robust and simpler alternative to data-driven methods that require full-state information and complex model training.
2. **Rigorous and Practical Safety Guarantees:** By incorporating a robust high-order Control Barrier Function (CBF), the framework provides formal safety guarantees. Crucially, the introduction of sliding-window conformal prediction to estimate disturbance-variation bounds online addresses a major limitation of traditional robust CBF designs, which often rely on conservative, pre-defined bounds. This innovation enables probabilistic safety guarantees that are both statistically rigorous and practically less conservative, fostering higher performance without compromising safety.
3. **Reduced Conservatism in Control:** The adaptive, data-driven estimation of uncertainty bounds directly impacts control performance. By providing tighter, online-adapted bounds, the QP-CBF controller can make less conservative control decisions, allowing the robot to operate closer to its performance limits while maintaining safety. This is a key enabler for high-dexterity and agile manipulation in constrained spaces.
4. **Simplicity and Real-time Implementation:** The proposed framework maintains a relatively simple control structure compared to complex machine learning approaches. The ESO design involves minimal parameter tuning (primarily observer bandwidth), and the use of efficient QP solvers like qpOASES allows for real-time operation at high frequencies (demonstrated at 1 kHz). This simplicity makes the framework more amenable to industrial deployment and research applications.
5. **Benchmarking and Comparison:** The direct comparison with conventional OSCTC and a residual learning approach highlights the tangible benefits of the proposed methodology in terms of tracking accuracy and disturbance rejection on hardware. This provides valuable insights into the trade-offs between model-based, data-driven, and hybrid control strategies.
6. **Broader Applicability:** The principles developed in this paper are transferable to a wide range of robotic systems beyond redundant manipulators, particularly those requiring robust control and safety guarantees in uncertain environments. This includes collaborative robots, mobile manipulators, and other complex mechatronic systems.

In conclusion, this work contributes a robust, safe, and efficient control framework that effectively bridges the gap between model-based control's theoretical rigor and data-driven methods' adaptability. Its potential impact lies in enabling the more widespread and safer deployment of advanced robotic systems in challenging, dynamic, and human-centric operational contexts.
