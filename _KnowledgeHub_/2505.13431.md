# A Practical Guide for Incorporating Symmetry in Diffusion Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford and Northeastern Universities presented practical methods for integrating symmetry into diffusion policies, showing that simpler techniques such as relative trajectory actions and Frame Averaging can enhance robotic manipulation performance. These approaches achieved results comparable to or exceeding complex equivariant architectures, improving sample efficiency and generalization, for instance, with relative actions yielding up to a 7.4% average performance increase.

## Problem

- Existing equivariant neural networks, while beneficial for sample efficiency and generalization, are complex to implement due to specialized mathematical formulations and custom network designs.
- The intricate nature of equivariant architectures often leads to incompatibility with modern, widely-used policy learning frameworks like Diffusion Policy, eye-in-hand perception, and pretrained vision encoders.
- Robotics researchers face a dilemma between leveraging the theoretical benefits of symmetry and the practical challenges of its implementation in contemporary, easy-to-use systems.

## Method

- Investigate and provide practical, straightforward approaches to incorporate symmetry into diffusion policies without incurring substantial design and implementation overhead.
- Explore how specific observation (eye-in-hand perception) and action (relative trajectory) representations can inherently induce SE(3)-invariance in the policy's denoising network.
- Integrate equivariant vision encoders into standard diffusion policy frameworks or leverage ==Frame Averaging== to apply rotational equivariance to powerful pretrained vision encoders without architectural modification.

## Results

- Using relative trajectory actions consistently improved performance, outperforming absolute trajectory actions by an average of **5.9%** to **7.4%** across robotic manipulation tasks.
- Integrating an equivariant vision encoder yielded a **9.1%** average performance improvement, while applying Frame Averaging to a pretrained encoder resulted in a **4.1%** average gain over baselines.
- The proposed methods achieved performance comparable to or exceeding complex fully equivariant architectures, for example, performing only **2.5%** below a multi-camera, RGBD-based voxel equivariant policy while using only a single eye-in-hand RGB image.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining eye-in-hand perception with relative trajectory actions inherently induces SE(3)-invariance in the policy's denoising network, offering a practical way to achieve symmetry benefits by simply changing action representation.
> - Explicitly enforcing symmetry in the vision encoder, either via equivariant CNNs or through Frame Averaging of pretrained models, significantly enhances policy learning and generalization capabilities.
> - Frame Averaging provides an effective and modular strategy to imbue powerful, off-the-shelf pretrained vision encoders with rotational equivariance, enabling their use in symmetry-aware robot learning without extensive architectural changes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of symmetry into neural network architectures has demonstrated improvements in sample efficiency and generalization within robotic policy learning. Equivariant neural networks, which are mathematically constrained to transform predictably under specific group operations (e.g., rotations, translations), have been applied across various robot learning domains, including reinforcement learning, imitation learning, and grasp learning. However, the practical adoption of these models faces significant barriers due to their implementation complexity. Equivariant architectures typically necessitate specialized mathematical formulations, custom network designs, and structured inputs, which are often incompatible with modern, widely-used policy learning frameworks.

One such modern framework is Diffusion Policy, which leverages denoising diffusion probabilistic models for visuomotor control to generate smooth, multimodal robot actions. While powerful, diffusion policies are data-intensive. Previous research has attempted to inject domain symmetries into diffusion models to mitigate data requirements, often by implementing complex equivariant denoising networks. This typically introduces significant architectural complexity that diverges from the standard components of diffusion policies, eye-in-hand perception, or pretrained vision encoders.

This work addresses the existing trade-off between the theoretical benefits of symmetry and the practical challenges of its implementation in contemporary robot learning systems, specifically focusing on diffusion-based visuomotor policies. It aims to develop practical, less complex methods to integrate symmetry benefits without requiring a complete overhaul of established policy frameworks or sacrificing the advantages of modern components.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to provide practical and straightforward approaches for incorporating the benefits of symmetry into diffusion policies without incurring the substantial design and implementation overhead associated with fully equivariant architectures. This is motivated by several factors:

- **Complexity of Existing Equivariant Designs:** Current state-of-the-art equivariant models often require specialized layers, unique mathematical formulations, and domain-specific symmetry reasoning. This complexity creates a steep learning curve for practitioners and hinders the widespread adoption of symmetry in diverse policy learning frameworks, such as Q-learning, actor-critic methods, and diffusion models.
- **Integration Challenges with Modern Policy Components:** The inherent structure of many equivariant architectures, which often demand specialized inputs or layer designs, frequently clashes with modern policy components. This includes diffusion-based action generation, eye-in-hand perception, and the use of powerful pretrained vision encoders, which are common in current robotics research.
- **Balancing Performance and Practicality:** Robotics researchers are often faced with a dilemma: choose complex equivariant architectures to gain symmetry advantages (like improved sample efficiency and generalization), or opt for simpler, more practical implementations at the cost of these benefits. This work aims to demonstrate that this trade-off can be mitigated, allowing both symmetry benefits and implementation practicality to be achieved simultaneously. This dilemma is particularly pronounced in diffusion-based visuomotor policies, where implementing equivariant structures within the denoising process has proven difficult.

To achieve its primary objective, the paper investigates three specific approaches:
1. Exploring invariant representations through the use of relative trajectory actions and eye-in-hand perception.
2. Integrating equivariant vision encoders into standard diffusion policy frameworks.
3. Leveraging pretrained vision encoders by applying Frame Averaging techniques to achieve symmetric feature extraction.

The overarching goal is to enable diffusion policies to achieve performance levels comparable to or exceeding fully equivariant architectures while maintaining a significantly simpler implementation profile.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research frames the problem as behavior cloning for visuomotor policy learning, where a policy maps an observation (including visual information, gripper pose, and aperture) to an action (gripper target pose and open-width command). The policy is trained using Diffusion Policy, which models the problem as learning a Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) over action trajectories. The core methodology involves investigating three distinct strategies to inject symmetry into this framework.

#### 3.1. Representing Actions as Absolute, Relative, and Delta Trajectory

The paper examines the impact of action representation on policy learning, specifically considering SE(3) (3D rigid-body transformation group) symmetries.

- **Absolute Trajectory Action:** Actions are defined as a sequence of future gripper poses directly in the world frame ($A_{t+i} \in SE(3)$).
- **Relative Trajectory Action:** Actions are defined as a sequence of future gripper poses relative to the current gripper frame at time $t$ ($A^r_{t+i} \in SE(3)$). The absolute poses are recovered by $A_{t+i} = T_t A^r_{t+i}$, where $T_t$ is the current gripper pose in the world frame.
- **Delta Trajectory Action:** Actions are defined as a sequence of incremental transforms, each expressed relative to the gripper’s frame at the preceding time step ($A^d_{t+i} \in SE(3)$). Absolute poses are reconstructed multiplicatively as $A_{t+i} = T_t \prod_{j=0}^{i-1} A^d_{t+j}$.

The paper formally proves (Proposition 1) that under a global SE(3) transformation, absolute trajectory actions transform equivariantly (i.e., $g \cdot a = \{gA_t, gA_{t+1}, \dots\}$), while both relative and delta trajectory actions are invariant (i.e., $g \cdot a_r = a_r$ and $g \cdot a_d = a_d$).

#### 3.2. SE(3)-Invariant Policy Learning

This section explores how specific choices of observation and action representation can inherently induce SE(3)-invariance in the policy's underlying denoising network.
- **Eye-in-Hand Perception:** When a camera is mounted on the robot’s end-effector, the visual input (image $I_t$) remains unchanged under an arbitrary global SE(3) transformation of the world, as the relative pose between the camera and the scene objects does not vary.
- **Proposition 2:** The paper proves that if a policy utilizes eye-in-hand observations and either relative or delta trajectory actions, and if the policy's mapping from observation to the local action sequence does not explicitly depend on the global gripper pose ($T_t$), then the resulting policy, which reconstructs absolute actions, is SE(3)-equivariant. This implies that the underlying denoising network becomes invariant to global transformations. In practice, the gripper pose $T_t$ is still included as an input to the policy for contextual information, leading to an *approximately* invariant property rather than strict invariance.

#### 3.3. Equivariant Vision Encoders

To enhance the extraction of symmetry-aware features without resorting to a full end-to-end equivariant policy, the paper proposes integrating an equivariant vision encoder into the diffusion policy framework.
- **Equivariant CNNs:** A standard CNN vision encoder (e.g., ResNet-18) is replaced with an equivariant CNN, such as one implemented with `escnn` library enforcing C8-equivariance (rotations by multiples of 45 degrees). This encoder maps the input image to a feature vector that transforms according to the regular representation of the chosen cyclic group, providing richer, symmetry-aware features to the subsequent non-equivariant diffusion head.

##### 3.3.1. Incorporating Pretrained Vision Encoders with Frame Averaging

Recognizing the power of large-scale pretrained models in computer vision, the paper employs Frame Averaging to enable the use of these models in an equivariant manner without modifying their internal architecture.
- **Frame Averaging:** This technique transforms an arbitrary function $\Phi(x)$ into a G-equivariant function $\Psi(x)$ by averaging its output over transformations from a group G: $\Psi(x) = \frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{g \in G} \rho_y(g) \Phi(\rho_x(g)^{-1}x)$. When applied to a pretrained vision encoder (e.g., ImageNet-1k pretrained ResNet-18), this approach leverages powerful general-purpose representations while explicitly enforcing rotational equivariance.

**Experimental Setup:**
The proposed approaches are systematically evaluated on 12 robotic manipulation tasks from the MimicGen benchmark and additional experiments on Robomimic environments. Policies are trained with 100 expert demonstrations. Success rate is the primary metric, averaged over three random seeds and 60 evaluation episodes. Training employs a standard Diffusion Policy setup, including RGB images, 6 DoF SE(3) action space, 2 past observations, 16 future action steps (8 executed), AdamW optimizer, EMA, and a cosine learning rate scheduler. Various encoder and UNet architectures are used depending on the specific experiment (e.g., ResNet-18 for CNN and Equivariant Encoders, with different output dimensions and UNet hidden channels).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation provides a systematic comparison of the proposed methods for incorporating symmetry into diffusion policies.

#### 4.1. Action and Observation Representation

(Data from Table 1 and accompanying text)

- **Relative Trajectory Performance:** Using relative trajectory actions consistently outperformed absolute trajectory actions in 10 out of 12 tasks. Across both Large FOV In-Hand and In-Hand + External observation settings, relative trajectory provided an average improvement of 5.9% and 7.4% over absolute trajectory, respectively. This supports the theoretical analysis regarding the SE(3)-invariance property of relative trajectories.
- **Delta Trajectory Performance:** Delta trajectory actions generally performed poorly, underperforming absolute trajectory by an average of 2.9%. This is attributed to potentially less temporal and structural information for the denoising process, a finding consistent with prior observations on velocity control in diffusion policies.
- **Observation Setting Comparison:** While Large FOV In-Hand perception often performed comparably to or better than In-Hand + External views, it slightly underperformed by 2.7% on average. This was primarily due to a significant drop in performance on tasks like "Coffee Preparation" and "Threading," where the single eye-in-hand view suffered from occlusion or insufficient information for long-horizon tasks.
- **Overall Baseline Improvement:** The combination of invariant observation and action representations improved performance by 4.7% compared to the original Diffusion Policy, which typically uses In-Hand + External views and absolute trajectory.

#### 4.2. Integrating Symmetry into the Vision Encoder

(Data from Table 2 and Appendix sections D, E, F, G, H)

- **Equivariant Encoder Benefits:** The use of an equivariant encoder (Equi Enc) yielded a 9.1% average performance improvement over a standard CNN encoder (CNN Enc) across 11 out of 12 tasks. This indicates that explicit symmetry constraints in the vision encoder component enhance diffusion policy learning.
- **Frame Averaging with Pretrained Encoders:** Applying Frame Averaging (Pretrain + FA) to a pretrained encoder (Pretrain) resulted in a 4.1% average performance improvement, demonstrating that symmetry benefits can be obtained even when leveraging powerful pretrained representations without modifying the underlying network architecture.
- **Comparison to Equivariant Diffusion Policy (EquiDiff):**
 - The Equi Enc approach, utilizing a single eye-in-hand RGB image, outperformed the image-based EquiDiff (Im) on average.
 - The Pretrain + FA approach achieved results only 2.5% below the voxel-based EquiDiff (Vo), despite EquiDiff (Vo) using RGBD inputs from four cameras and a more complex architecture, while Pretrain + FA used a single eye-in-hand RGB image.
- **Robomimic Experiments (Appendix D):** On Robomimic environments, the Pretrain + FA method generally outperformed baselines, achieving an average improvement of 3% compared to EquiDiff.
- **Frame Averaging with External View (Appendix E):** The benefits of Frame Averaging were consistent even with In-Hand + External views. Pretrain + FA with this setup yielded a 6.7% improvement over Pretrain without FA, and a 15.5% improvement over training from scratch. Notably, in this configuration, Pretrain + FA outperformed EquiDiff (Im) in all tasks and achieved an average 1% higher performance than EquiDiff (Vo), further highlighting the effectiveness and practicality of the approach.
- **Frame Averaging with Absolute Action and Multi-Camera (Appendix F):** The Pretrain + FA encoder demonstrated plug-and-play capability by providing significant improvements (21.5% for Diffusion Policy and 9.7% for EquiDiff) even when used with absolute actions and multi-camera inputs.
- **Ablation Studies (Appendix G):**
 - **Component Isolation:** Separately ablating the relative trajectory and symmetric feature extraction components revealed complementary benefits. Replacing relative trajectory with absolute trajectory reduced performance by 11.1%, while replacing the symmetric encoder with a non-pretrained CNN reduced it by 19.6%.
 - **Proprioception:** Removing the gripper pose from the policy input reduced performance, indicating that this proprioceptive information, despite being symmetry-breaking, provides valuable context.
 - **Symmetry Group:** Experiments with different SO(2) discretization orders (C8, C4, C2) showed that C8 yielded the best performance, aligning with prior research suggesting it is a practical sweet spot for 2D rotational symmetry.
- **EquiDiff with In-Hand Only (Appendix H):** When EquiDiff was restricted to a large FOV eye-in-hand camera, its mean success rate dropped by 12.8% compared to its original multi-camera configuration, indicating its reliance on external viewpoint signals.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a set of practical methodologies for incorporating symmetry into diffusion policies, offering a pathway to achieve performance levels competitive with or exceeding complex, fully equivariant architectures while significantly reducing implementation complexity. The work addresses the long-standing trade-off between architectural complexity and sample efficiency in robot learning by demonstrating that both benefits can be realized without a complete architectural redesign.

**Key Contributions and Practical Implications:**

- **Simplified Symmetry Integration:** The primary significance lies in providing straightforward, modular approaches to leverage symmetry. This lowers the barrier to entry for practitioners seeking to improve robotic policy learning with symmetry, avoiding the need for deep mathematical expertise or specialized framework development.
- **SE(3)-Invariance with Practical Representations:** The theoretical proof and empirical validation that eye-in-hand perception combined with relative trajectory actions yields inherent SE(3)-invariance is a significant contribution. This finding is practical because merely changing the action representation coordinate frame can result in a 5-7% performance improvement, enhancing generalization without complex architectural changes.
- **Effective Equivariant Feature Extraction:** The demonstration that integrating equivariant vision encoders, or using Frame Averaging with pretrained encoders, significantly enhances performance (up to 9.1% and 4.1% respectively) without requiring end-to-end equivariant policy structures is impactful. Frame Averaging, in particular, offers an elegant solution to harness the representational power of large-scale pretrained models while endowing them with explicit rotational equivariance.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance with Reduced Overhead:** The method achieves state-of-the-art results on the MimicGen benchmark for RGB eye-in-hand diffusion policies. Notably, it performs only 2.5% below voxel-based EquiDiff (which uses four RGBD cameras and a more complex architecture) using only a single eye-in-hand RGB image, and can even outperform it with multi-camera inputs. This suggests a more efficient and scalable solution for real-world robotic deployment.
- **Modular and Generalizable Design:** The proposed techniques are designed to be integrated modularly, allowing for flexibility and potential applicability to other policy learning frameworks beyond diffusion models.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**

- **Limited Field of View:** The reliance on a single eye-in-hand camera, even with an enlarged FOV, can lead to failure modes due to occlusion or insufficient information for certain long-horizon tasks. Future work could explore fish-eye cameras or memory mechanisms to address this.
- **Scope of Application:** The current investigation is limited to diffusion policies and specific benchmarks (MimicGen, Robomimic). Extending these approaches to other policy learning frameworks, such as ACT, is a potential avenue for future research.
- **Computational Cost:** While more efficient than some fully equivariant models, Frame Averaging can increase training time compared to vanilla Diffusion Policy (approximately double the GPU hours). Optimizing this computational overhead could be a future goal.
- **Real-World Validation:** Although designed for practicality and compatibility with systems like UMI, the methods have not yet been demonstrated on physical robots. Validating their transferability to real-world hardware remains a crucial next step.
