# A Practical Guide for Incorporating Symmetry in Diffusion Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford and Northeastern Universities presented practical methods for integrating symmetry into diffusion policies, showing that simpler techniques such as relative trajectory actions and Frame Averaging can enhance robotic manipulation performance. These approaches achieved results comparable to or exceeding complex equivariant architectures, improving sample efficiency and generalization, for instance, with relative actions yielding up to a 7.4% average performance increase.

## Problem

- Existing equivariant neural networks, while beneficial for sample efficiency and generalization, are complex to implement due to specialized mathematical formulations and custom network designs.
- The intricate nature of equivariant architectures often leads to incompatibility with modern, widely-used policy learning frameworks like Diffusion Policy, eye-in-hand perception, and pretrained vision encoders.
- Robotics researchers face a dilemma between leveraging the theoretical benefits of symmetry and the practical challenges of its implementation in contemporary, easy-to-use systems.

## Method

- Investigate and provide practical, straightforward approaches to incorporate symmetry into diffusion policies without incurring substantial design and implementation overhead.
- Explore how specific observation (eye-in-hand perception) and action (relative trajectory) representations can inherently induce SE(3)-invariance in the policy's denoising network.
- Integrate equivariant vision encoders into standard diffusion policy frameworks or leverage ==Frame Averaging== to apply rotational equivariance to powerful pretrained vision encoders without architectural modification.

## Results

- Using relative trajectory actions consistently improved performance, outperforming absolute trajectory actions by an average of **5.9%** to **7.4%** across robotic manipulation tasks.
- Integrating an equivariant vision encoder yielded a **9.1%** average performance improvement, while applying Frame Averaging to a pretrained encoder resulted in a **4.1%** average gain over baselines.
- The proposed methods achieved performance comparable to or exceeding complex fully equivariant architectures, for example, performing only **2.5%** below a multi-camera, RGBD-based voxel equivariant policy while using only a single eye-in-hand RGB image.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining eye-in-hand perception with relative trajectory actions inherently induces SE(3)-invariance in the policy's denoising network, offering a practical way to achieve symmetry benefits by simply changing action representation.
> - Explicitly enforcing symmetry in the vision encoder, either via equivariant CNNs or through Frame Averaging of pretrained models, significantly enhances policy learning and generalization capabilities.
> - Frame Averaging provides an effective and modular strategy to imbue powerful, off-the-shelf pretrained vision encoders with rotational equivariance, enabling their use in symmetry-aware robot learning without extensive architectural changes.
