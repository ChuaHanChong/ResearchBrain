# CoT-VLA: Visual Chain-of-Thought Reasoning for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> CoT-VLA integrates visual chain-of-thought reasoning into Vision-Language-Action models by having them predict subgoal images before generating actions. This approach leverages large-scale action-less video data for training, enabling the model to outperform state-of-the-art VLA models by 17% in real-world manipulation and 6% in simulation benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models primarily use direct end-to-end mapping from observations to actions, lacking explicit intermediate reasoning steps crucial for complex, multi-stage manipulation tasks.
- Current VLA models struggle with effective temporal planning and foresight, limiting their performance on tasks requiring a sequence of sub-goals.
- Abundant, action-less video data from the internet remains largely underutilized by robot learning models, which often rely exclusively on expensive action-annotated robot demonstrations.

## Method

- Introduces ==visual chain-of-thought (CoT)== reasoning where the **CoT-VLA** model first predicts a future image frame as a ==visual subgoal==, then generates a sequence of actions conditioned on this subgoal.
- Leverages a 7B-parameter unified multimodal foundation model, ==VILA-U==, for both understanding visual observations and generating image and action tokens.
- Employs a two-stage training approach, using robot demonstrations for both visual and action prediction, and large-scale ==action-less video== datasets (e.g., EPIC-KITCHENS) specifically for training visual subgoal generation.

## Results

- CoT-VLA surpasses state-of-the-art VLA models, achieving a **17%** improvement in real-world manipulation tasks and a **6%** improvement in simulation benchmarks.
- The model demonstrates superior instruction following on the LIBERO benchmark and robust adaptation to novel environments in Franka-Tabletop experiments, especially for complex multi-instruction tasks.
- Ablation studies confirm the individual and combined effectiveness of visual chain-of-thought, action chunking, and a novel hybrid attention mechanism in boosting task performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit visual chain-of-thought reasoning, using predicted subgoal images, significantly enhances the reasoning and generalization capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.
> - Large-scale action-less video datasets can effectively be leveraged for pre-training the visual reasoning component of robot policies, reducing reliance on costly action-annotated robot demonstrations.
> - Improvements in the visual reasoning capabilities of VLA models directly translate to higher robotic task success rates, particularly for challenging out-of-distribution and long-horizon tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning has seen impressive progress, particularly with the advent of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models aim to leverage the rich understanding capabilities of pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), originally developed for general image and text understanding, and fine-tune them on diverse robot demonstrations to achieve generalizable sensorimotor control. VLAs inherit the VLMs' ability to understand diverse scenes, objects, and natural language instructions, offering a promising direction for robust robot policies.

However, a critical limitation of most existing VLA models is their focus on direct, end-to-end mapping from observations to actions. This approach often bypasses explicit intermediate reasoning steps, which are crucial for complex, multi-stage manipulation tasks and for improving model interpretability. Such direct mappings can lead to a lack of temporal planning or sophisticated reasoning capabilities, hindering performance on tasks requiring more than immediate reactive control.

This paper directly addresses this identified gap by introducing **visual chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning** into VLA models. The concept of Chain-of-Thought reasoning originates from the natural language processing (NLP) domain, where it has significantly improved the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs) by prompting them to generate explicit, step-by-step intermediate thoughts before arriving at a final answer. Applying this powerful paradigm to robotics, particularly for grounding reasoning in visual observations and physical actions, is a rapidly evolving area.

Previous efforts to integrate CoT into embodied AI have explored various forms of intermediate representations, such as textual plans, keypoints, or bounding boxes. While effective, these often require additional pre-processing or explicit annotation pipelines. CoT-VLA distinguishes itself by proposing the use of **subgoal images** as the intermediate reasoning step. This is a novel and highly practical choice because subgoal images:
1. Are naturally available within existing robot demonstration datasets (simply future frames).
2. Require minimal additional pre-processing.
3. Are interpretable, providing a clear visual representation of the model's planned state.
4. Crucially, allow the leveraging of *action-less video datasets* (e.g., EPIC-KITCHENS, Something-Something V2) for training the visual reasoning component. This is a significant breakthrough for data scalability, as unlabeled video data is vastly more abundant than action-annotated robot demonstrations, enabling the model to learn visual dynamics and instruction following from a much broader base of experience.

The technical foundation of CoT-VLA is built upon **VILA-U**, a state-of-the-art unified multimodal foundation model capable of both understanding and generating image and text tokens. This positions CoT-VLA at the forefront of combining large-scale multimodal pre-training with specific robotic control applications, moving towards more generalist and capable embodied AI systems. By integrating generative image modeling with action prediction, CoT-VLA contributes to the growing trend of developing unified models that can perceive, reason, and act in complex environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the reasoning and generalization capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robot manipulation by incorporating explicit visual chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning.

The motivation for this objective stems from several limitations observed in current VLA paradigms:
- **Lack of Intermediate Reasoning:** Existing VLA models typically operate on a direct input-output mapping, taking current visual observations and language instructions to directly predict robot actions. This "black box" approach lacks the crucial intermediate reasoning steps that humans naturally employ for complex tasks. Without such steps, VLAs struggle with tasks requiring multi-stage planning or deep understanding of temporal dynamics.
- **Limited Temporal Planning:** The direct mapping nature of current VLAs inhibits their ability to perform effective temporal planning. For tasks that unfold over time and require a sequence of sub-goals, these models often lack the foresight to anticipate future states, leading to suboptimal or failed executions.
- **Difficulty with Complex Manipulation Tasks:** Complex manipulation tasks often involve subtle environmental changes, object interactions, and intricate sequences of actions. Without explicit reasoning about intermediate states, current VLAs find it challenging to achieve high success rates and robust generalization in such scenarios.
- **Underutilization of Abundant Data:** While robot demonstrations are valuable, they are costly and time-consuming to collect. Concurrently, vast amounts of action-less video data exist. Existing VLAs, by focusing solely on action-annotated data, cannot fully leverage this abundant source of visual information to improve their understanding of object dynamics and interactions.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of CoT-VLA are explicitly defined:
1. **Introduce Visual Chain-of-Thought Reasoning:** Develop a novel method where VLAs first predict a future image frame as a visual subgoal (an explicit intermediate reasoning step) before generating actions. This allows the model to "think visually" about the desired future state.
2. **Integrate Subgoal Generation with Action Prediction:** Design a two-phase prediction process: first, auto-regressively generate a subgoal image conditioned on current observation and language instruction; second, generate a short sequence of actions conditioned on the current observation, language instruction, *and* the generated subgoal image.
3. **Leverage Action-Less Video Data:** Enable the visual reasoning component of the VLA to be trained not only on robot demonstrations but also on large-scale, action-less video datasets (like EPIC-KITCHENS and Something-Something V2). This aims to enhance the model's visual reasoning capabilities by leveraging more abundant data, thus improving generalization.
4. **Develop a State-of-the-Art VLA System:** Build CoT-VLA, a 7B parameter model, leveraging a unified multimodal foundation model (VILA-U) that can understand and generate both visual and action tokens, demonstrating its superior performance in real-world and simulation benchmarks.
5. **Validate the Impact of Key Components:** Empirically demonstrate the individual and combined effectiveness of visual CoT reasoning, a novel hybrid attention mechanism (causal for pixels/text, full for actions), and action chunking on overall task performance and generalization.

By achieving these objectives, CoT-VLA aims to push the boundaries of robot learning towards more intelligent, interpretable, and data-efficient policies capable of handling increasingly complex manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CoT-VLA introduces a novel visual chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, moving away from direct observation-to-action mapping towards a more explicit, two-stage reasoning process.

**Core Idea: Visual Chain-of-Thought**
Instead of directly predicting actions from visual observations and language instructions, CoT-VLA operates in two sequential phases:
1. **Subgoal Image Generation:** The model first predicts a future image frame, $\hat{s}_{t+n}$, as an intermediate visual reasoning step. This image represents a desired future state in pixel space, $n$ frames ahead of the current observation.
 - **Equation:** $\hat{s}_{t+n} \sim P_\theta(s_{t+n} | s_t, l)$
2. **Action Sequence Generation:** Subsequently, conditioned on the current observation, the language instruction, *and* the newly generated subgoal image, the model generates a sequence of $m$ actions, $\{\hat{a}_t..., \hat{a}_{t+m}\}$, designed to achieve the predicted subgoal state.
 - **Equation:** $\{\hat{a}_t..., \hat{a}_{t+m}\} \sim P_\theta(\{a_t..., a_{t+m} | s_t, l, \hat{s}_{t+n})$

This approach enables the model to "think visually" by explicitly reasoning about desired future states before committing to a sequence of actions.

**Base Vision-Language Model**
CoT-VLA is built upon **VILA-U**, a 7B-parameter unified multimodal foundation model. VILA-U is pre-trained on interleaved text-image data and is capable of both understanding and generating image and text tokens. Key features of VILA-U that are leveraged include:
- A unified vision tower encoding visual inputs into discrete tokens aligned with textual information.
- Autoregressive image and video generation capabilities.
- Utilization of residual quantization to enhance the representational capacity of discrete visual features.

**Training Procedures**
The training involves two main stages: pretraining and adaptation (fine-tuning). The model's LLM backbone, projector, and depth transformer are optimized, while the vision tower remains fixed.

1. **Data Types:**
 - **Robot Demonstrations ($D_r$):** Curated subset of the Open X-Embodiment dataset, containing language instructions, sequences of robot actions, and visual observations. This data is used for both visual and action prediction training.
 - **Action-less Videos ($D_v$):** Datasets like EPIC-KITCHENS and Something-Something V2, consisting of language descriptions and images *without* action annotations. This data is exclusively used for training the visual subgoal image generation.

2. **Training Objectives:** The overall training objective combines two key components:
 - **Visual Tokens Prediction ($L_{visual}$):** For subgoal image generation, the model predicts residual tokens autoregressively. This loss function ensures that the generated subgoal image accurately reflects a plausible future state given the current observation and language instruction.
 - **Action Tokens Prediction ($L_{action}$):** For action generation, each action dimension is discretized into 256 bins, and the model predicts sequences of these discrete action tokens. The loss minimizes cross-entropy for action predictions.

3. **Key Architectural Enhancements:**
 - **Hybrid Attention Mechanism:** This is a novel contribution. CoT-VLA uses **causal attention** for autoregressive generation of image pixels and text tokens (where each token can only attend to previous tokens). In contrast, it employs **full attention** for predicting all dimensions of action tokens simultaneously within an action chunk, allowing all action dimensions to interact with each other. This is proposed to improve action coherence and performance.
 - **Action Chunking:** Instead of predicting a single action at each timestep, CoT-VLA predicts a sequence of actions (an "action chunk," e.g., 10 actions) at once. This improves inference speed and can lead to smoother, more purposeful movements.

**Deployment Strategy (Closed-Loop Control)**
At test time, CoT-VLA operates in a closed-loop manner:
1. Given the current visual observation ($s_{obs_t}$) and language instruction ($l$), the model first samples a subgoal image ($\hat{s}_{t+n}$).
2. Then, it samples a sequence of actions ($[\hat{a}_t..., \hat{a}_{t+m}]$) conditioned on the current observation, instruction, and the generated subgoal.
3. The robot executes this chunk of $m$ actions.
4. After execution, new observations ($s_{obs_{t+m+1}}$) are captured, and the process repeats, allowing for continuous adaptation and progress towards the task goal.

This methodology systematically integrates visual reasoning, leverages diverse data sources, and incorporates architectural improvements to enhance VLA performance and generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across simulation benchmarks and real-world robot manipulation tasks consistently demonstrate the effectiveness and state-of-the-art performance of CoT-VLA.

**Overall Performance:**
- CoT-VLA significantly outperforms the state-of-the-art VLA model by **17% in real-world manipulation tasks** and **6% in simulation benchmarks**, as highlighted in the abstract.

**1. LIBERO Simulation Benchmark:**
- **Superior Performance:** CoT-VLA achieved the best or competitive success rates across all four LIBERO task suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long) compared to Diffusion Policy, Octo, and OpenVLA.
- **Improved Instruction Following:** Analysis of failure cases revealed that baseline methods sometimes overfit to visual cues, disregarding language instructions when initial states were visually similar across tasks. CoT-VLA, by explicitly reasoning visually about desired actions via language-grounded subgoal generation, exhibited **better instruction following ability**.

**2. Bridge-V2 Real-Robot Experiments:**
- **Competitive Generalization:** CoT-VLA demonstrated competitive performance across all four generalization categories (visual, motion, semantic, and language grounding) compared to Octo, OpenVLA, and SUSIE.
- **Challenges with Action Chunking:** While strong overall, CoT-VLA showed slightly lower success rates in some visual and language generalization tasks compared to OpenVLA. The authors attribute this not to errors in visual reasoning but to **grasping failures caused by action chunking**. This suggests a trade-off or an area for refinement in the action generation aspect.
- **Comparison to SUSIE:** Despite SUSIE generating visually higher-quality goal images (due to its diffusion-based prior), CoT-VLA achieved higher task success rates, particularly in tasks involving novel objects or complex language grounding. This indicates that task success is not solely dependent on visual fidelity of generated goals but also on robust action generation conditioned on those goals.

**3. Franka-Tabletop Real-Robot Experiments:**
- **Strong Adaptation to Novel Environments:** In this setting, where models were fine-tuned on a relatively small number of demonstrations (10-150 per task) on an unseen robot setup, CoT-VLA achieved the **highest average performance**.
- **Robustness in Multi-Instruction Tasks:** While Diffusion Policy performed well on simpler single-instruction tasks, CoT-VLA, alongside other OpenX-pretrained models (Octo, OpenVLA), showed significantly better adaptation and performance on complex **multi-instruction tasks** where accurate language grounding is critical.

**4. Ablation Studies:**
- **Impact of CoT-VLA Components (LIBERO-Spatial & LIBERO-Goal):**
 - **Action Chunking:** Consistently improved performance over single-action prediction.
 - **Hybrid Attention:** Further boosted performance when combined with action chunking.
 - **Visual CoT:** The full CoT-VLA model (with visual CoT, hybrid attention, and action chunking) yielded the best results, empirically validating the effectiveness of the proposed visual chain-of-thought reasoning.
- **Importance of Pretraining (Franka-Tabletop):**
 - Pretraining CoT-VLA on OpenX and action-less video data (EPIC-KITCHENS, Something-Something V2) resulted in a **46.7% relative improvement** in performance (from 53.7% to 78.8%) compared to directly fine-tuning the base VILA-U model on Franka-Tabletop demonstrations. This strongly emphasizes the crucial role of large-scale multimodal pretraining for better downstream task adaptation.

**5. "Better Visual Reasoning Helps" Study:**
- To directly test the hypothesis that improved visual reasoning leads to better robot performance, CoT-VLA was tested on challenging out-of-distribution, long-horizon tasks (e.g., "move green scallion to apple-covered book").
- Using **ground-truth goal images** (simulating perfect visual reasoning) resulted in a **40% absolute success rate improvement** for both sub-tasks compared to using the model's own generated goal images. This striking result provides strong evidence that advancements in visual reasoning and goal image generation capabilities will directly translate to enhanced robotic task performance.

In summary, CoT-VLA not only achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse benchmarks but also provides clear empirical evidence for the benefits of integrating visual chain-of-thought reasoning, robust architectural choices (hybrid attention, action chunking), and large-scale multimodal pretraining for generalizable robot manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of CoT-VLA marks a significant advancement in the field of robot learning, particularly for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its implications and potential impact are multi-faceted:

1. **Enhanced Reasoning and Generalization:** By incorporating explicit visual chain-of-thought reasoning, CoT-VLA moves beyond simple reactive policies. The ability to "think visually" about future subgoal states before acting endows robots with a higher level of scene understanding and temporal planning. This leads to significantly improved generalization capabilities across diverse tasks, objects, and environments, as demonstrated by its state-of-the-art performance in both simulation and real-world settings. This is crucial for deploying robots in unstructured and dynamic real-world scenarios.

2. **Improved Interpretability and Debuggability:** The subgoal images generated by CoT-VLA serve as interpretable intermediate reasoning steps. Unlike abstract representations (e.g., embeddings, keypoints), these images provide a clear visual trace of the model's thought process. This interpretability is invaluable for researchers and engineers to understand *why* a robot behaves in a certain way, identify failure points, and debug complex policies more effectively.

3. **Data Efficiency through Action-less Videos:** One of the most impactful contributions is the method's ability to leverage abundant *action-less video data* for pre-training the visual reasoning component. Traditional robot learning heavily relies on costly and laborious human-teleoperated demonstrations with action annotations. By learning visual dynamics and instruction following from readily available, unannotated video footage, CoT-VLA significantly reduces the dependency on expensive robot-specific data. This approach opens up vast new avenues for scaling up robot learning with much larger and more diverse datasets, accelerating the development of truly generalist robots.

4. **Foundation for More Capable Embodied AI:** CoT-VLA builds upon and integrates recent advancements in large multimodal foundation models (like VILA-U). This strategy aligns with the broader trend in AI of developing unified models capable of processing and generating various modalities (text, image, video, actions). This work provides a strong blueprint for how these powerful foundation models can be effectively grounded in embodied agents, paving the way for robots that can understand complex instructions, reason about their environment, and execute intricate manipulation tasks with greater autonomy.

5. **Benchmarking and Future Research Directions:** The comprehensive evaluation across multiple benchmarks (LIBERO, Bridge-V2, Franka-Tabletop) provides a robust comparison against existing state-of-the-art methods, setting new performance standards. Furthermore, the paper candidly discusses its limitations, which serve as clear signposts for future research:
 - **Computational Overhead:** The real-time generation of image tokens is currently a bottleneck. This highlights the need for faster image generation techniques or more efficient LLM inference methods to improve throughput for real-time robot control.
 - **Visual Quality and Coherence:** The autoregressive image generation can produce lower visual quality compared to diffusion models. Integrating with advanced generative models could enhance the fidelity of subgoal images, potentially leading to even better action execution.
 - **Action Chunking Refinements:** Issues like discontinuous actions between chunks point towards future work on temporal smoothing techniques or more sophisticated per-step prediction mechanisms.
 - **Out-of-Distribution Generalization:** While CoT-VLA improves generalization, true zero-shot reasoning for entirely novel tasks remains a challenge. The finding that better visual reasoning *directly* translates to better task performance suggests that advancements in world models and large-scale video/image models (which can generate more accurate and diverse future states) will be critical for unlocking further robotic capabilities.

In essence, CoT-VLA represents a critical step towards creating more intelligent, robust, and adaptable robotic systems. By endowing robots with the ability to reason visually about their future actions and leverage vast amounts of passive visual data, this work pushes the boundaries of what is possible in generalizable robot manipulation.
