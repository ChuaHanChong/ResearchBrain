# Spatiotemporal System Forecasting with Irregular Time Steps via Masked Autoencoder

> [!summary] Summary
> The Physics-Spatiotemporal Masked Autoencoder (P-STMAE) directly forecasts high-dimensional dynamical systems from irregularly sampled data, leveraging a convolutional autoencoder for spatial compression and a masked transformer for temporal dynamics. It achieved superior or competitive performance across simulated and real-world datasets, including a 6.16e-5 MSE on Shallow Water Equations and 8.02e-5 MSE on NOAA Sea Surface Temperature data.

## Problem

- Accurate prediction of high-dimensional dynamical systems (e.g., from PDEs) where observations occur at irregular time intervals.
- Limitations of traditional time series methods and existing deep learning models, like RNNs, which typically require regularly sampled data, leading to biased preprocessing (interpolation/imputation).
- Computational complexities and memory demands when applying transformer architectures directly to high-dimensional physical fields for spatiotemporal modeling.

## Method

- P-STMAE integrates a ==Convolutional Autoencoder (CAE)== to efficiently reduce high-dimensional spatial states into a compact latent representation.
- A ==masked transformer== operates in this latent space, directly handling irregular time steps and missing data through ==learnable masking tokens== and ==sine-cosine positional embeddings==.
- The model employs a ==non-autoregressive, single-pass prediction== mechanism to reconstruct the entire physical sequence, including future states, mitigating cumulative error propagation.

## Results

- P-STMAE achieved the lowest MSE (**6.16e-5**), highest SSIM (**0.9538**), and highest PSNR (**43.90**) on the Shallow Water Equations dataset, outperforming ConvRAE and ConvLSTM.
- It demonstrated strong generalization on real-world NOAA Sea Surface Temperature data, securing the lowest MSE (**8.02e-5**), highest SSIM (**0.9817**), and highest PSNR (**41.03**).
- The model maintained consistently low prediction errors and stable performance across increasing missing data ratios and sampling dilations, demonstrating robustness where baselines deteriorated.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining spatial compression via CAE with a latent-space masked transformer effectively addresses both high dimensionality and irregular temporal sampling in complex physical systems.
> - Directly processing irregular time steps with a masked autoencoder, rather than relying on interpolation, preserves true temporal dynamics and avoids preprocessing biases.
> - A unified framework for sequence reconstruction and forecasting enables parallel, non-autoregressive prediction, offering computational efficiency and mitigating error accumulation.
