# EquiForm: Noise-Robust SE(3)-Equivariant Policy Learning from 3D Point Clouds

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Purdue University developed EquiForm, a framework for SE(3)-equivariant policy learning that effectively handles noisy 3D point cloud observations. It enhances robotic manipulation performance and spatial generalization in simulated and real-world environments by explicitly correcting geometric distortions and enforcing consistent feature representations.

## Problem

- Existing SE(3)-equivariant robotic manipulation methods assume clean 3D point cloud observations but are sensitive to real-world sensor noise, partial occlusions, and sampling inconsistencies.
- Noise and geometric distortions cause "equivariance deviations," disrupting theoretical transformation rules and leading to inconsistent policy behavior and degraded performance.
- Current approaches primarily encode symmetry but lack mechanisms to explicitly correct noise-induced geometric deviations or enforce equivariant consistency under noisy conditions.

## Method

- A ==geometric denoising module== recovers stable 3D structures from noisy point clouds by correcting ==normal-direction deviations== and enforcing local sampling consistency through ==tangent-direction correction==.
- An ==SE(3)-equivariant encoder== is regularized with a ==contrastive InfoNCE loss==, promoting robust feature consistency under rigid transformations and stochastic geometric perturbations.
- An integrated pipeline combines denoising, equivariant feature encoding, ==canonicalization==, and action prediction (using a ==diffusion model==) to ensure policies are robust to imperfect geometric inputs.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **66.6%** across **16** simulation benchmarks, outperforming the Canonical Policy by **10.0%** and DP3 by **24.3%**, with notable gains on noisy MimicGen tasks.
- Demonstrated superior robustness under increasing Gaussian noise levels and maintained consistent performance under rigid transformations combined with partial observations in simulation.
- Consistently achieved the highest success rates in real-world robotic tasks, averaging **28.1%** improvement over baselines, particularly on challenging long-horizon and geometrically varied manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Noise-induced geometric distortions directly lead to "equivariance deviations," which destabilize canonicalization and cause inconsistent observation-to-action mappings.
> - Explicitly correcting noise through a two-stage geometric denoising process (normal and tangent direction) significantly improves the stability and temporal smoothness of point cloud observations.
> - Enforcing equivariant consistency in the feature space via contrastive learning helps maintain the SE(3) structure even with partial visibility and augmented noise, ensuring robust generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation through imitation learning has demonstrated capabilities in acquiring complex behaviors from demonstrations. Initial approaches often relied on 2D visual observations, but these policies frequently faced limitations in generalization due to sensitivity to changes in illumination, texture, and camera viewpoints. To address these issues, a shift towards 3D point clouds as policy inputs has been observed. Point clouds offer a geometry-aware, appearance-invariant representation, providing increased robustness to visual distractors and enabling policies to better capture the structural relationships within an environment.

Despite the advantages of point clouds, challenges persist in generalizing across diverse scene layouts, which often involve variations in object orientations and poses. This has motivated the integration of SE(3) equivariance into policy learning. SE(3) equivariance ensures that a policy's response to rigid transformations (rotations and translations) of the input is predictable and consistent, thereby enhancing sample efficiency and spatial generalization in 3D manipulation tasks. Existing SE(3)-equivariant approaches primarily encode these symmetry constraints directly into neural network architectures or operate in canonicalized reference frames.

However, a significant gap in the current research is the implicit assumption of clean and stable geometric observations. In practical scenarios, point clouds are frequently corrupted by sensor noise, partial occlusions, missing data, and inconsistencies from sparse or irregular sampling. These imperfections introduce geometric distortions that disrupt the theoretical correspondence between rigid transformations in the input space and their induced transformations in the feature space, leading to deviations from the expected equivariance. Current methods do not explicitly model or provide mechanisms to preserve SE(3) equivariance under such unreliable geometric conditions.

EquiForm addresses this specific challenge by proposing a framework that explicitly accounts for noise-induced geometric deviations and enforces equivariant consistency in learned representations. It builds upon prior work in point cloud policy learning (e.g., diffusion-based policies), equivariant robot learning (e.g., canonicalization approaches), and noise-robust 3D perception. Unlike previous methods that either treat noise as a decoupled preprocessing step or implicitly rely on generic encoder robustness, EquiForm integrates geometric denoising with a contrastive equivariant alignment objective. This approach aims to provide a unified solution for maintaining SE(3)-consistent control even when the underlying geometric structure of observations is perturbed, thus enabling more reliable deployment of point cloud-based policies in realistic, noisy environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a noise-robust SE(3)-equivariant policy learning framework, named EquiForm, designed to operate reliably on noisy 3D point cloud observations for robotic manipulation tasks. The motivation stems from several limitations observed in current point cloud-based and equivariant policy learning methods:

1. **Sensitivity to Real-World Noise**: While 3D point clouds offer improved robustness to appearance variations compared to 2D images, they remain highly sensitive to sensor noise, pose perturbations, and occlusion-induced artifacts. These real-world imperfections distort the geometric structure of observations.
2. **Breakdown of Equivariance Assumptions**: Existing SE(3)-equivariant approaches typically assume clean and stable geometric inputs. However, noise, partial observations, and sampling inconsistencies prevent observations from strictly adhering to ideal rigid-body transformations. This leads to what the authors term "equivariance deviations," where input perturbations break the theoretical transformation rules of equivariant architectures, resulting in inconsistent policy behavior and degraded performance.
3. **Lack of Explicit Noise Correction**: Current methods primarily focus on encoding symmetry constraints into neural architectures but do not explicitly correct noise-induced geometric deviations or enforce equivariant consistency in learned representations under noisy conditions. This leaves a critical gap in bridging theoretical equivariance with practical robustness.
4. **Formal Analysis of Noise Impact**: The paper provides a formal analysis detailing how noise-induced geometric distortions specifically lead to equivariance deviations in observation-to-action mappings. This analysis highlights that noise not only corrupts the input point cloud but also destabilizes the estimation of canonical transformations, which are crucial for SE(3) canonicalization-based policies.

Based on this analysis, EquiForm aims to address two fundamental challenges:
- How to obtain a canonical representation that remains stable despite noisy observations, ensuring feature consistency.
- How to guarantee that the estimated transformation maintains consistency with the ideal SE(3) structure required for policy equivariance.

To achieve these objectives, EquiForm introduces specific components:
- A **geometric denoising module** is proposed to restore consistent 3D structure from noisy or incomplete observations, providing a more stable foundation for downstream equivariant reasoning.
- A **contrastive equivariant alignment objective** is designed to enforce representation consistency under both rigid transformations and noise perturbations. This objective explicitly promotes noise-robust equivariance, enabling the learned representation to maintain symmetry even with imperfect inputs.

Ultimately, the motivation is to enhance the reliability, spatial generalization, and sample efficiency of robotic manipulation policies by enabling them to effectively reason with geometry under realistic, noisy sensing conditions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EquiForm integrates two primary components: geometric denoising and equivariant contrastive learning, into a unified pipeline for noise-robust SE(3)-equivariant policy learning. The overall approach processes raw, noisy point cloud observations to produce stable, canonicalized representations for action prediction.

**A. Point Cloud Noise Analysis**
The authors first characterize common sources of point cloud imperfections, including depth sensing noise (points deviating from true surface), projection and discretization effects (distorting fine details), sampling inconsistency (different point sets for the same static object), and occlusion/partial visibility (missing or incomplete observations due to viewpoint changes). These disturbances lead to corrupted observations ($X_\delta$) that deviate from the underlying surface and exhibit varying sampling patterns, disrupting SE(3)-equivariant canonicalization.

**B. Geometric Denoising Module**
To mitigate noise, a geometric denoising module is introduced, designed to recover a cleaned point set ($X^\star$) from a noisy input ($X_\delta$). The module addresses two components of observation noise:
1. **Normal-direction correction**: This addresses points deviating from the underlying surface. For each noisy point, a local surface reference ($\bar{x}_\delta$) is computed by averaging its neighbors. The correction projects the noisy point back towards the approximated implicit surface by removing its normal-direction deviation. This involves estimating a local surface normal ($n_\delta$) and updating the point using the formula $x = x_\delta - n_\delta n_\delta^\top (x_\delta - \bar{x}_\delta)$.
2. **Tangent-direction correction**: This addresses sampling variations and local density irregularities along the surface, which persist even after normal-direction correction. This step aims to enforce local sampling consistency by applying a spatial low-pass filtering operation within the tangent plane. The update encourages the correction displacement to align with the local neighborhood offset while remaining within the tangent plane, using the formula $x^\star = x - (I - nn^\top) (x - \bar{x})$.

These two steps are applied progressively to yield a geometrically stable and temporally smooth point cloud, providing a reliable input for subsequent processing.

**C. Equivariant Consistency via Contrastive Learning**
Even after geometric denoising, occlusion and partial visibility can still introduce inconsistencies in point clouds observed from different viewpoints. To promote equivariant consistency under these conditions, a contrastive learning strategy is employed.
1. **Encoder**: The core of this component is an SE(3)-equivariant encoder, implemented using the Vector Neuron (VN) framework. This encoder maps point clouds to latent equivariant feature representations.
2. **Augmentation**: From a denoised point cloud ($X^\star$), an augmented view ($\tilde{X}^\star$) is generated by applying stochastic geometric perturbations. These include Gaussian noise injection, random cropping, point dropout, and point insertion, simulating realistic viewpoint changes and sensing variations.
3. **Contrastive Objective**: Both $X^\star$ and $\tilde{X}^\star$ are processed by the shared equivariant encoder. An InfoNCE loss is applied to the resulting equivariant features. This loss encourages the features of positive pairs (original and augmented views of the same underlying point cloud) to be similar, while pushing them apart from features of other samples (negative examples) in the batch. This regularization in the equivariant feature space improves robustness to noise and partial observations while preserving the SE(3) structure.

**D. EquiForm Policy Learning Pipeline**
The complete pipeline operates as follows:
1. **Denoising**: A raw, noisy point cloud observation ($X_\delta$) is first processed by the geometric denoising module to produce a stable point cloud ($X^\star$).
2. **Feature Encoding and Canonicalization**: The denoised point cloud ($X^\star$) is fed into the SE(3)-equivariant encoder ($\Psi$). During training, this encoder is regularized by the contrastive learning objective, which enhances feature consistency under perturbations. The encoder then predicts a rigid transformation ($T \in SE(3)$), serving as the canonicalization transform.
3. **Canonical Representation**: The inverse transformation ($T^{-1}$) is applied to the denoised observation to obtain a canonicalized point cloud representation ($\hat{O}$). This aligns the input to a shared reference frame, invariant to viewpoint and observation-specific artifacts.
4. **Action Prediction**: The canonicalized representation ($\hat{O}$), combined with robot proprioceptive information, is input to a policy network (e.g., a diffusion model, as used in this work) to predict canonical control commands.
5. **Action Transformation**: Finally, these canonical actions are transformed back to the original observation frame using the corresponding SE(3) forward transformation ($T$), yielding the final robot control commands.

This integrated approach aims to ensure that the policy remains equivariant and robust by first stabilizing the geometric input and then enforcing consistency in the equivariant feature space, thereby improving generalization under realistic operating conditions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

EquiForm was evaluated on a comprehensive set of 16 simulated manipulation tasks and 4 real-world robotic tasks, assessing both overall performance and robustness under various noise and transformation conditions.

**A. Simulation Experiments**
1. **Overall Task Performance (Table II)**:
 - EquiForm achieved an average success rate of 66.6% across 16 simulation benchmarks, demonstrating an improvement of 10.0% over the Canonical Policy (56.6%) and 24.3% over DP3 (42.3%).
 - The most significant performance gains were observed on the MimicGen benchmark tasks, which feature substantial pose variation, cluttered scenes, and noisy point cloud observations. EquiForm outperformed DP3 by 34.5% and the Canonical Policy by 17.0% on these tasks.
 - On RoboTwin tasks (bimanual manipulation), EquiForm showed competitive performance, achieving the best results on the majority of tasks, with an average improvement of 15.8% over DP3 and 4.7% over Canonical Policy. In some RoboTwin tasks, Canonical Policy still outperformed EquiForm, suggesting specific task characteristics like emphasis on local symmetries or tool-use interactions might reduce the benefit of additional robust canonicalization.
 - For the sparse 3D Push-T task, EquiForm also demonstrated better performance than both baselines.

2. **Robustness under Noisy Inputs (Gaussian Noise)**:
 - **Qualitative Visualization (Fig. 12)**: Geometric denoising was shown to preserve surface continuity and spatial coherence of point clouds even under severe isotropic Gaussian noise, unlike Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) which failed to recover coherent geometry.
 - **Temporal Chamfer Distance (Fig. 13)**: Geometric denoising improved frame-to-frame geometric consistency, indicated by lower Chamfer distances between adjacent point cloud frames, especially under noisy conditions ($\sigma = 0.08$). This suggested improved sampling consistency over time.
 - **Task Success Rates (Fig. 14)**: EquiForm maintained consistently higher success rates across increasing levels of Gaussian observation noise compared to baseline methods, which experienced rapid performance degradation. This demonstrated the effectiveness of the geometric denoising module in stabilizing point cloud observations.

3. **Robustness under Rigid Transformations and Partial Observations**:
 - **Equivariant Feature Robustness (Fig. 16)**: UMAP visualizations of equivariant feature embeddings showed that models trained with contrastive learning maintained compact and coherent feature clusters for the same point cloud instance, even when subjected to rigid rotations combined with Gaussian jitter, cropping, dropout, and insertion. In contrast, features without contrastive learning became scattered under these perturbations, indicating a breakdown of robustness.
 - **Task Success Rates (Fig. 17)**: EquiForm achieved higher success rates than the Canonical Policy across a wide range of in-plane rotation angles, particularly when coupled with random translation and partial observation effects. DP3, lacking built-in equivariance, showed significant performance collapse. These results indicate that the combination of geometric denoising and contrastive learning effectively stabilized equivariant features under coupled rigid transformations and partial observability.

4. **Ablation Study (Table III)**:
 - Both the geometric denoising module and equivariant contrastive learning individually contributed to performance improvements on subsets of tasks, suggesting complementary benefits.
 - The full EquiForm model, combining both components, achieved the highest overall average success rate of 67.3%, confirming their aggregate effectiveness. However, the combined model did not achieve the best performance on every single task, indicating that in some constrained scenarios, simpler canonicalization might suffice without the need for additional smoothing or regularization.

**B. Real-Robot Experiments**
1. **Benchmarking on Diverse Tasks (Table IV)**:
 - EquiForm consistently achieved the highest success rates across four real-world manipulation tasks (Block Stacking, Shoe Alignment, Table Organization, Cloth Folding) compared to DP3 and the Canonical Policy. For instance, in Shoe Alignment, EquiForm achieved an 8/10 success rate, while DP3 and Canonical Policy achieved 2/10 and 3/10, respectively.
 - The policy demonstrated improved robustness, particularly on long-horizon and geometrically challenging tasks like Table Organization and Cloth Folding, where baselines largely failed.

2. **Validation under Rigid Transformations (Table V, Fig. 20)**:
 - EquiForm maintained higher success rates under scene-level SE(3) layout variations (random translations and in-plane rotations) compared to baselines.
 - Qualitative analysis for Cloth Folding (Fig. 20) showed that DP3 failed to adapt to scene transformations, while the Canonical Policy, though equivariant, suffered from inaccurate grasp localization due to noisy point clouds. EquiForm, by contrast, produced grasp trajectories that transformed consistently with the scene while remaining accurate under noisy observations.

3. **Overall Real-World Improvement**: EquiForm demonstrated an average improvement of 28.1% in real-world experiments compared to baselines.

**C. Failure Case Analysis**:
- Identified limitations included under-representation of small objects after uniform point cloud downsampling, leading to insufficient geometric support for grasping (e.g., Table Organization).
- Ambiguity between thin, deformable objects (e.g., cloth) and supporting surfaces in geometry-only point clouds, making accurate grasp localization difficult (e.g., Cloth Folding).

In summary, the results indicate that EquiForm significantly enhances the noise robustness and spatial generalization of SE(3)-equivariant policies, both in simulation and real-world environments, by explicitly addressing noise-induced geometric distortions and enforcing consistent feature representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of EquiForm lies in its direct addressal of a critical vulnerability in current 3D point cloud-based and equivariant robotic manipulation policies: their sensitivity to real-world sensor noise, partial observations, and geometric inconsistencies. By formalizing how these factors break SE(3) equivariance and providing a principled solution, EquiForm offers several potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Reliability in Real-World Robotics**: The primary impact is enabling more reliable and robust deployment of imitation learning policies on physical robots. Real-world sensing is inherently imperfect, and policies that can tolerate noisy inputs are crucial for practical applications. EquiForm's demonstrated performance gains in real-world tasks under standard and varied layouts suggest a step towards more dependable autonomous manipulation.

2. **Improved Spatial Generalization**: By stabilizing the canonical representation and ensuring equivariant consistency under perturbations, EquiForm improves the ability of policies to generalize to unseen object poses, viewpoints, and scene layouts. This means robots can operate effectively in more diverse and unstructured environments without requiring extensive retraining for every new configuration.

3. **Advancement in Equivariant Learning**: The work contributes to the field of equivariant machine learning by highlighting and addressing the practical challenges of applying theoretical equivariance in noisy domains. The proposed geometric denoising module and contrastive equivariant alignment objective offer generalizable components that could be adapted to other 3D perception and control tasks benefiting from robust equivariance.

4. **Flexible Policy Integration**: EquiForm functions as a preprocessing and representation learning framework that can integrate with various generative policy heads, such as diffusion models. This modularity allows researchers to leverage advancements in action prediction models while benefiting from EquiForm's noise robustness and equivariance.

5. **Data Efficiency Implications**: While not explicitly a core claim, improved generalization often correlates with enhanced data efficiency. By learning more stable and consistent representations, the policy might require fewer demonstrations to learn robust behaviors across different scene variations, which is a key challenge in imitation learning.

6. **Future Research Directions**: The identified limitations, such as the under-representation of small objects and ambiguity for thin deformable structures, point towards important avenues for future research. This could spur development in adaptive point sampling strategies, higher-resolution geometric representations, or the integration of multi-modal sensing (e.g., tactile feedback) to overcome challenges where geometry-only point clouds are insufficient.

In conclusion, EquiForm represents a notable advancement in making SE(3)-equivariant robotic manipulation policies more practical and reliable for deployment outside of controlled laboratory settings, thereby broadening the scope of what imitation learning can achieve in real-world applications.
