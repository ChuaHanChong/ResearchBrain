# ToolScope: An Agentic Framework for Vision-Guided and Long-Horizon Tool Use

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Renmin University of China developed ToolScope, a training-free multimodal agent framework, that integrates global planning with local multimodal perception for complex Vision Question Answering tasks. It consistently outperformed existing baselines across four VQA datasets, achieving gains such as +9.12% on MAT-Search and +4.67% on ScienceQA, by using a strategic navigator and a native visual "Perceive" tool to manage long-horizon reasoning and visual context.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with complex Vision Question Answering (VQA) tasks requiring dynamic reasoning and external knowledge.
- Existing training-free multimodal agents suffer from "local reasoning traps" due to a lack of high-level global planning for long-horizon decision-making.
- Multi-step multimodal reasoning often leads to "visual context degradation," where crucial visual information is lost or not revisited dynamically.

## Method

- Introduces ==ToolScope==, a plug-and-play, training-free framework comprising a ==Global Navigator==, ==Agentic Executor==, and Response Synthesizer.
- The Global Navigator performs high-level task analysis to select relevant tools and generate a strategic, long-term reasoning plan.
- The Agentic Executor employs specialized tools, including a novel native =="Perceive Tool"== for dynamic visual re-attention, a Search Tool for knowledge retrieval, and a Code Tool for computation.

## Results

- ToolScope consistently outperformed all prompt-based and tool-augmented baselines across four VQA benchmarks (VQA 2.0, ScienceQA, MAT-Search, MathVista).
- Achieved performance gains up to **+9.12%** on MAT-Search, **+4.67%** on ScienceQA, and **+1.4%** on VQA 2.0 and MathVista when integrated with MiMo-VL-7B-RL.
- Demonstrated strong generalization across different MLLM backbones and scales, with an average accuracy improvement of **+6.69%** for MiMo-VL-7B-RL and **+6.33%** for InternVL3-78B, validating its robustness and scalability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating explicit global planning with local multimodal perception significantly improves coherence and effectiveness in complex VQA tasks.
> - A native "Perceive Tool," allowing the MLLM to dynamically query and re-attend to visual information, effectively mitigates visual context degradation without external vision models.
> - A modular, training-free design can achieve robust and generalizable performance gains across various MLLM backbones and tasks, making advanced agentic capabilities more accessible.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "ToolScope" is situated at the intersection of **Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)**, **Agentic AI**, and **Visual Question Answering (VQA)**. This is a rapidly evolving and highly competitive field, driven by the remarkable capabilities of foundational models like GPT-4V, Gemini, and Claude.

Initially, MLLMs excelled at a wide range of vision-language tasks through end-to-end inference. However, their inherent limitations became apparent when faced with complex scenarios requiring dynamic reasoning, access to external, up-to-date knowledge, or multi-step computational processes. This led to the emergence of **multimodal agents**, a paradigm that extends MLLMs' capabilities by enabling them to interact with and invoke external tools. This agentic approach allows models to go beyond their parametric knowledge and perform more sophisticated reasoning.

Within the agentic AI landscape, two main approaches have evolved:
1. **Training-based agents**: These methods, often employing supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or reinforcement learning (RL), train MLLMs to use tools. While powerful, they incur substantial resource costs due to the need for large amounts of high-quality multimodal training data and extensive model training.
2. **Training-free agents**: Offering greater flexibility and lower deployment costs, these methods prompt MLLMs to interact with tools without explicit training. However, existing training-free frameworks frequently suffer from "local reasoning traps," lacking a global perspective for long-horizon decision-making and coherent strategic planning. Furthermore, many remain predominantly text-centric, neglecting the unique challenges of multimodal cognition, particularly **visual context degradation**—the problem of losing or failing to revisit critical visual information during multi-step reasoning. Approaches attempting to integrate visual foundation models as external tools often increase system complexity.

ToolScope addresses these critical gaps in the training-free agent paradigm. It specifically aims to overcome the limitations of **limited global planning** and **visual context degradation**, which are identified as core challenges in both training-based and training-free agentic approaches. By proposing a framework that unifies global planning with local multimodal perception, ToolScope directly contributes to making multimodal agents more robust, generalizable, and efficient for complex VQA tasks, pushing the boundaries of what MLLMs can achieve in dynamic, knowledge-intensive, and visually-grounded environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the ToolScope framework is to enable Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to flexibly and efficiently utilize external tools for sophisticated, long-horizon reasoning in complex Vision Question Answering (VQA) tasks. This overarching goal is driven by the recognition of two fundamental limitations in current MLLM-based agentic systems:

1. **Limited Global Planning**: Existing agentic frameworks, particularly training-free ones, often operate in a purely reactive, step-by-step manner. They lack a high-level strategic module that can analyze the task, decompose it into sub-problems, and outline a long-term reasoning trajectory. This absence of global guidance frequently leads to suboptimal tool selection, redundant invocations, and disjointed reasoning paths, especially in tasks requiring multi-step, cross-modal interactions. ToolScope aims to integrate a global deliberative module to provide coherent strategic planning.
2. **Visual Context Degradation**: In multi-step multimodal reasoning, visual information tends to be processed once during initial encoding and then largely forgotten or implicitly relied upon. This leads to a degradation of visual context over extended inference steps, preventing the MLLM from dynamically re-attending to specific image regions or extracting fine-grained visual details as new reasoning steps emerge. As illustrated in the paper's example, this can result in misidentifying crucial visual information (e.g., mistaking a specific Harry Potter novel for the entire series). ToolScope seeks to develop mechanisms for retaining, referencing, and reusing visual information throughout the reasoning process.

To address these limitations, the authors explicitly frame their research questions as:
- "How can global planning be integrated with localized multimodal perception to enable more coherent and effective decision-making in multimodal agents?"
- "How can visual information be retained, referenced, and reused across multi-step inference to mitigate the problem of visual context degradation?"

By tackling these challenges, ToolScope is motivated to advance multimodal agents beyond basic tool integration towards truly adaptive, strategic, and visually-grounded reasoning capabilities, all within a resource-efficient, training-free paradigm.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ToolScope is introduced as a **plug-and-play, training-free multimodal agent framework** designed to solve complex VQA tasks by integrating global planning with local multimodal perception. The framework is composed of three interconnected modular components: the Global Navigator, the Agentic Executor, and the Response Synthesizer.

#### 3.1. Global Navigator

The Global Navigator serves as the strategic brain of the agent. Given a multimodal input (image `I`, question `Q`), it performs a high-level analysis to understand the task's intent and complexity. Its key functions include:
- **Toolkit Selection**: From a predefined pool of tools (`T`), it selects a relevant subset (`T'`) necessary for the task. This step is crucial for reducing the model's decision space and focusing tool use. For simple tasks, it can even decide that no external tools are needed.
- **Global Reasoning Plan Generation**: It formulates a high-level strategic plan (`g`) that outlines the sequence of major reasoning steps and how the selected tools will be employed.
The output of the Global Navigator is a structured guidance prompt (`G`) encapsulating both the plan and the selected toolkit, which steers the subsequent execution.

#### 3.2. Agentic Executor

The Agentic Executor is the core reasoning engine, responsible for carrying out multi-step problem-solving in an iterative, tool-augmented manner. It dynamically generates tool invocation calls and integrates their results into the ongoing reasoning context. To equip the MLLM with essential capabilities for diverse VQA tasks, ToolScope introduces three specialized tools:

1. **Perceive Tool**: This is a novel component designed specifically to combat visual context degradation. It allows the agent to dynamically re-attend to the image, formulate targeted visual sub-questions, and extract localized visual information on demand. Crucially, the Perceive tool is implemented *natively by the backbone MLLM itself*, without relying on external detectors, OCR engines, or specialized perception models. It treats the image as a "queryable perceptual memory," enabling active and recursive visual understanding.
2. **Search Tool**: This tool facilitates multimodal knowledge acquisition beyond the MLLM's internal parameters.
 - **Textual Retrieval**: It employs a classic BM25 algorithm on a Wikipedia dump to retrieve factual and background knowledge.
 - **Multimodal Retrieval**: It constructs a multimodal knowledge base from the training set of the target dataset, using CLIP-based cross-modal retrieval to find relevant visual examples. A cosine similarity threshold (τ=0.9) is applied to filter out noisy or irrelevant visual content, ensuring semantic relevance.
3. **Code Tool**: This module enables the agent to write and execute Python code for numerical computations, logical inferences, or algorithmically complex subproblems. A feedback loop is implemented to capture and return execution errors (e.g., syntax or runtime errors) to the MLLM, allowing it to revise the code until a valid result is obtained.

The Agentic Executor iteratively generates a reasoning trace (`R`), where each step includes a thinking process, tool invocation, and the corresponding tool's execution result.

#### 3.3. Response Synthesizer

After the iterative reasoning process, the Response Synthesizer aggregates the intermediate steps in the reasoning trajectory (`R`) and generates the final answer (`A`). Its role is to condense the entire reasoning process by filtering out redundant attempts, unsuccessful tool invocations, and irrelevant detours. It then refines the final response to ensure coherence, completeness, and faithful alignment with the original question and image, providing a concise and user-friendly output.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

All experiments are conducted in a **strictly training-free setting** using off-the-shelf MLLMs. The Perceive capability is intrinsic to the backbone MLLMs. The agent's reasoning budget is capped at 10 turns, chosen for a favorable accuracy-latency trade-off. Standard decoding hyperparameters are used, and for retrieval, a chunked English Wikipedia dump is employed with CLIP (ViT-B/16 backbone) as the retriever. Detailed prompts for each component are provided in the appendix.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of ToolScope was rigorously evaluated across four diverse Visual Question Answering (VQA) benchmarks: VQA 2.0 (general VQA), ScienceQA (scientific knowledge and visual reasoning), MAT-Search (retrieval-based VQA), and MathVista (mathematical reasoning with visual inputs). The evaluation used official accuracy metrics for MathVista and VQA 2.0, and Exact Match (EM) accuracy for ScienceQA and MAT-Search. ToolScope was compared against both prompt-based baselines (Direct Prompting, Chain-of-Thought, Program-of-Thought) and tool-augmented agent baselines (Naïve MRAG, Cantor).

The key findings and results are summarized as follows:

- **Consistent Superior Performance**: ToolScope consistently outperformed all tested baselines across all four diverse datasets. For instance, when integrated with MiMo-VL-7B-RL, ToolScope achieved significant performance gains, including up to +9.12% on MAT-Search, +4.67% on ScienceQA, and +1.4% on both VQA 2.0 and MathVista. These results underscore its robustness and general applicability without requiring task-specific fine-tuning.

- **Strong Generalization Across MLLM Backbones and Scales**: ToolScope demonstrated remarkable generalization capabilities, maintaining its performance advantage across different MLLM backbone families (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3, MiMo-VL series) and scaling effectively with model capacity. Average accuracy improvements across all datasets were +6.69% for MiMo-VL-7B-RL, +6.33% for InternVL3-78B, +5.50% for InternVL3-8B, and +4.81% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B. This indicates that ToolScope's benefits are not tied to a specific architecture and that it effectively leverages the increasing capabilities of larger models.

- **Inconsistent Performance of Existing Baselines**: The study revealed that existing baselines often exhibit inconsistent performance across different VQA datasets, struggling to balance generality with specialized needs. For example, Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Cantor showed moderate gains on reasoning-heavy datasets but underperformed on tasks requiring visual grounding or external knowledge. Naïve MRAG, while improving on most datasets, showed reduced performance on ScienceQA, highlighting its limited robustness to noisy retrieval.

- **Ablation Study Insights**: An ablation study using Qwen2.5-VL-7B on MathVista and MAT-Search elucidated the contribution of each ToolScope component:
 - The **Search tool** was identified as the most impactful, with its removal leading to a substantial performance drop (e.g., from 39.40% to 33.10% on MAT-Search), emphasizing the critical role of external knowledge.
 - The **Code tool** proved crucial for quantitative reasoning, with its absence causing the largest drop on MathVista.
 - The **Perceive tool** also contributed to performance, confirming its effectiveness in mitigating visual context degradation.

- **Scaling with Top-k Retrieval Documents**: Analysis of the number of retrieved documents (`k`) showed that ToolScope consistently outperformed baselines across various `k` values. It highlighted a trade-off: a small `k` provides insufficient context, while a large `k` introduces noise. Performance peaked at `k=8`, indicating an optimal balance. Naïve RAG, in contrast, exhibited instability and performance deterioration with increasing `k` on ScienceQA.

- **Scaling with Reasoning Max Turns**: Investigating the maximum number of reasoning turns revealed that increasing reasoning steps improved ToolScope's performance, validating the benefits of iterative reasoning. However, diminishing returns were observed beyond 4-5 turns, and excessive turns could even degrade performance on simpler tasks due to "overthinking."

- **Scaling with Model Size**: Across various InternVL3 model sizes (2B to 78B), ToolScope consistently outperformed baselines, demonstrating its scalability and ability to leverage larger backbone capacities for more effective tool use and global-local coordination.

In summary, ToolScope achieves significant and consistent improvements, demonstrates strong generalization, and effectively addresses the core challenges of global planning and visual context degradation through its modular design and specialized tools.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ToolScope framework represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal AI agents, offering several layers of significance and promising substantial potential impact:

- **Novelty in Agentic Design**: ToolScope introduces a modular, training-free agentic framework that uniquely unifies global planning with local multimodal perception. This top-down strategic guidance (Global Navigator) combined with iterative, context-aware execution (Agentic Executor) addresses a critical limitation of previous reactive agentic systems that often lacked coherent long-horizon reasoning.

- **Effective Mitigation of Key Challenges**:
 - **Global Planning**: The Global Navigator component effectively tackles the problem of limited global planning by providing high-level strategic guidance and intelligent toolkit selection, leading to more focused and efficient reasoning trajectories.
 - **Visual Context Degradation**: The introduction of the native Perceive tool is a crucial innovation. By treating the image as a dynamic, queryable perceptual memory and allowing for active re-attention to visual details, ToolScope directly overcomes the common issue of visual context degradation in multi-step multimodal reasoning. This makes the agent's visual understanding more robust and consistent over time.

- **Strong Performance and Generalizability**: The consistent and significant performance improvements across diverse VQA benchmarks (VQA 2.0, ScienceQA, MAT-Search, MathVista) demonstrate ToolScope's effectiveness and robustness. Its ability to generalize across different MLLM backbone architectures (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3, MiMo-VL) and scale with model capacity further highlights its versatility and potential as a foundational agentic design.

- **Practicality and Resource Efficiency**: As a training-free and plug-and-play framework, ToolScope is highly practical. It does not incur the substantial resource costs associated with training large multimodal agents, making it more accessible and easier to deploy and adapt to various MLLM backbones and tasks. This lower barrier to entry can accelerate research and application development in agentic AI.

- **Blueprint for Future Research**: ToolScope provides a valuable blueprint for building more capable and intelligent multimodal agents. It opens up promising avenues for future research in several directions:
 - **Advanced Planning and Reasoning**: Further development of sophisticated global planning mechanisms, including learning to plan or more adaptive planning in dynamic environments.
 - **Enhanced Multimodal Perception**: Exploring more advanced and efficient ways to integrate visual, auditory, and other sensory modalities for truly comprehensive perception.
 - **Broader Applications**: Extending the framework beyond VQA to other complex multimodal reasoning tasks, such as robotics, embodied AI, scientific discovery, and medical diagnosis, where long-horizon reasoning and precise tool use are paramount.
 - **Robust Tool Integration**: Investigating methods for more seamless and robust integration with heterogeneous external knowledge sources and complex, stateful code execution environments.

- **Ethical and Safety Considerations**: The paper acknowledges limitations regarding open-world scenarios, safety-critical applications, and ethical considerations surrounding tool access (e.g., hallucinated citations, unsafe code). This highlights a critical area for future work, underscoring the responsibility required in deploying such powerful agentic systems.

In conclusion, ToolScope represents a significant stride towards more intelligent, adaptive, and reliable multimodal AI agents. By strategically addressing fundamental challenges in global planning and visual context, it sets a new standard for training-free agentic frameworks and paves the way for a new generation of multimodal reasoning systems capable of tackling increasingly complex real-world problems.
