# RAGEN: Understanding Self-Evolution in LLM Agents via Multi-Turn Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Northwestern University, Stanford, NYU, and Microsoft developed StarPO, a reinforcement learning framework, and RAGEN, a modular system, to train self-evolving large language model agents in multi-turn environments. The work identifies a new instability called the "Echo Trap" and proposes StarPO-S, which uses uncertainty-based filtering and gradient shaping to achieve more robust training and higher success rates across various interactive tasks.

## Problem

- Current LLM training and single-turn RL methods are insufficient for multi-turn, interactive, and stochastic environments requiring sequential decision-making and continuous self-improvement.
- LLM agents face significant training instability, including managing complex reward signals and achieving generalization across diverse environments.
- Understanding how to enable LLM agents to learn effectively and stably in self-evolutionary settings, especially concerning internal reasoning processes, is an open challenge.

## Method

- Introduce ==StarPO (State-Thinking-Actions-Reward Policy Optimization)==, a trajectory-level reinforcement learning framework that optimizes entire multi-turn interaction histories, including an agent's internal reasoning.
- Develop ==RAGEN==, a modular system built on StarPO, serving as a comprehensive platform for training and evaluating LLM agents in controlled, multi-turn, stochastic environments.
- Implement ==StarPO-S==, a stabilized variant, utilizing uncertainty-based trajectory filtering and asymmetric gradient clipping to address training instabilities observed in multi-turn LLM agent learning.

## Results

- Vanilla reinforcement learning methods like PPO and GRPO consistently lead to performance collapse in multi-turn LLM agent environments, often due to the "**Echo Trap**" phenomenon.
- The proposed StarPO-S variant significantly enhances training stability and achieves higher final success rates across diverse environments by employing uncertainty-based filtering and gradient shaping techniques.
- Task diversity, moderate interaction granularity, and frequent rollout updates improve generalization; while reasoning aids simple single-turn tasks, its multi-turn utility is limited without fine-grained, reasoning-aware reward signals.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-turn LLM agent training with standard RL methods introduces a new instability, termed the "Echo Trap," characterized by agents overfitting to repetitive reasoning patterns and collapsing behavioral diversity.
> - Training stability can be significantly improved by filtering training data based on reward uncertainty and using asymmetric gradient shaping (StarPO-S).
> - Explicit reasoning in LLM agents does not consistently improve generalization in complex multi-turn tasks and can even degrade or become "hallucinated" if reward signals are not fine-grained and reasoning-aware.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) has rapidly progressed from static text generation to enabling sophisticated autonomous agents capable of interacting with complex environments. This paradigm shift, however, introduces unique and formidable challenges that are not adequately addressed by traditional LLM training or single-turn reinforcement learning (RL) methods.

This paper directly tackles the underexplored domain of **multi-turn reinforcement learning for LLM agents**. Prior work in RL for LLMs, such as RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback), often focuses on single prompt-response interactions or static tasks like mathematical problem-solving and code generation (as illustrated in Figure 1). While such methods have shown promise in improving specific capabilities, they fall short in scenarios requiring sequential decision-making, long-term memory, adaptation to stochastic feedback, and self-improvement through continuous interaction—all critical components for truly autonomous agents in dynamic environments like planning assistants, robotics, or tutoring systems.

The authors identify several key challenges specific to this multi-turn agent setting: training instability, difficulty in managing complex reward signals, and limited generalization across diverse environments. A central open question motivating their research is: "what design factors make self-evolving LLM agents learn effectively and stably?"

By proposing `StarPO` (State-Thinking-Actions-Reward Policy Optimization) as a general trajectory-level RL framework and `RAGEN` as a modular training and evaluation system, the authors aim to systematically investigate these issues. Their work extends the current landscape by moving beyond single-turn optimizations (like PPO and GRPO, which are adapted here) to handle full interaction trajectories, including the agent's internal reasoning processes. The identified "Echo Trap" phenomenon, where models overfit to self-generated, repetitive reasoning, aligns with broader concerns in the AI community regarding model collapse when recursively trained on their own outputs (Shumailov et al., 2024). Thus, this research contributes significantly by pinpointing and addressing novel instability patterns in self-evolving LLM agents, paving the way for more robust and generalizable AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically investigate and improve the self-evolution capabilities of Large Language Model (LLM) agents within interactive, multi-turn, and potentially stochastic environments. The authors aim to enable LLMs to learn effectively and stably as autonomous agents, a domain that remains largely underexplored despite its critical importance for real-world applications.

The key motivations driving this work stem from the inherent limitations of current LLM training paradigms when applied to agentic settings:
1. **Complexity of Interactive Environments:** Unlike static tasks (e.g., single-turn math or code generation), interactive agent tasks demand sequential decision-making, the ability to maintain context and memory across multiple turns, and adaptation to unpredictable or stochastic feedback from the environment. Existing methods often fail to adequately capture these dynamics.
2. **Lack of Self-Evolution Capabilities:** While LLMs can be fine-tuned for specific tasks, empowering them to genuinely "self-evolve" through experience in an RL setting, particularly with rule-based rewards, has received insufficient attention. This self-improvement is crucial for agents operating in dynamic, open-ended scenarios.
3. **Training Instability:** A significant challenge in applying reinforcement learning to LLMs for agent training is the observed instability. This includes issues like complex or sparse reward signals, and difficulties in ensuring generalization as the agent learns. The paper explicitly poses a central question: "what design factors make self-evolving LLM agents learn effectively and stably?"

To address these challenges, the authors set forth the following key objectives:
- **Develop a General RL Framework:** Introduce `StarPO` (State-Thinking-Actions-Reward Policy Optimization) to provide a unified approach for multi-turn, trajectory-level agent training, offering flexible control over reasoning, reward assignment, and prompt-rollout structures.
- **Build a Modular Training System:** Create `RAGEN` as a practical, modular system to implement the full training loop (rollout generation, reward assignment, trajectory optimization) under the StarPO framework, serving as a research infrastructure for systematic analysis.
- **Identify Core Challenges and Design Principles:** Through systematic experimentation across diverse environments, discover and characterize the unique instabilities (like the "Echo Trap") in multi-turn LLM agent RL, and propose effective design principles and stabilization strategies.
- **Understand the Role of Reasoning and Reward Signals:** Investigate how reasoning emerges (or fails to emerge) in LLM agents during multi-turn RL, and the critical role of fine-grained, reasoning-aware reward signals in shaping intelligent behavior.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to lay a robust foundation for building more reliable, adaptable, and intelligent LLM agents capable of learning and improving in complex interactive settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce `StarPO` (State-Thinking-Actions-Reward Policy Optimization), a novel reinforcement learning framework, and `RAGEN`, a modular system built upon it, to facilitate the training and evaluation of LLM agents in multi-turn, stochastic environments.

#### 3.1. MDP Formulation for Agent Training

The problem of LLM agents interacting with dynamic environments is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) M={S, A, P}.
- **States (S):** Represent observation sequences or interaction histories (e.g., current environment state and past turns).
- **Actions (A):** Token sequences generated by the LLM (e.g., `move left`, `search for product`).
- **Transition Dynamics and Reward (P):** The environment's response to an action, yielding a new state and a reward.
The agent's policy `π_θ` generates actions conditioned on the current state and interaction history, unfolding over a maximum horizon K to form a full trajectory `τ`. This trajectory serves as the primary learning material.

#### 3.2. StarPO: Reinforcing Reasoning via Trajectory-Level Optimization

Unlike previous methods that optimize single prompt-response pairs, StarPO treats the *entire multi-turn trajectory*—including observations, intermediate reasoning traces, actions, and environmental feedback—as a coherent unit for rollout and model optimization.
- **Objective:** Maximize the expected cumulative reward over the entire trajectory: `J_StarPO(θ) = E_M,τ~π_θ [R(τ)]`.
- **Reasoning-Guided Structured Output:** At each step `t`, the agent generates a structured output `a_T_t = <think>...</think><answer>a_t</answer>`, where `a_T_t` includes the intermediate reasoning and `a_t` is the executable action. Both are jointly optimized.
- **Optimization Procedure:** StarPO interleaves rollout generation and policy updates. In each iteration, `N` trajectories are generated from `P` initial states. Updates are performed with a batch size `E`. The policy probability `π_θ(τ)` is decomposed into token-level likelihoods, making it compatible with autoregressive LLMs.
- **Modular Optimization Strategies:** StarPO is designed to support various RL algorithms. The paper specifically details its instantiation with:
 - **PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization):** Utilizes a critic to estimate token-level value and advantages, which helps stabilize training.
 - **GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization):** A critic-free approach that assigns a scalar reward `R(τ_i)` to each trajectory and normalizes advantages across a group of trajectories. This relies on the relative performance within a batch.

#### 3.3. The RAGEN System

`RAGEN` is the practical implementation of the StarPO framework. It's a comprehensive, modular system for training and evaluating LLM agents in controlled environments.
- **Functionality:** RAGEN handles structured rollouts, customizable reward functions, and integration with multi-turn, stochastic environments.
- **Purpose:** It serves as both the execution backend for StarPO and a research platform for systematically studying training stability, generalization, and learning dynamics of reasoning agents. Its extensibility allows for easy integration of new environments, reward schemes, or rollout strategies.

#### 3.4. Experiment Setup

- **Environments:** Four environments with varying complexities are used:
 - `Bandit`: Single-turn, stochastic, testing risk-sensitive reasoning.
 - `Sokoban`: Multi-turn, deterministic puzzle requiring irreversible planning.
 - `Frozen Lake`: Multi-turn, stochastic navigation with probabilistic transitions.
 - `WebShop`: Multi-turn, open-domain, realistic web-based shopping task involving natural language grounding.
- **Models:** Qwen-2.5 Instruct 0.5B for symbolic tasks, 3B for WebShop. Appendix D also includes evaluations on 3B, 7B, and 72B models, and zero-shot comparisons with GPT-4o.
- **Training Configuration:** Models trained on NVIDIA H100/A100 GPUs for 100-200 rollout–update iterations. Standard RL parameters are used (e.g., GAE with γ=1.0, λ=1.0, Adam optimizer, entropy bonus, response-format penalty).
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Success rate, rollout entropy, in-group reward variability (to detect behavioral diversity collapse), response length (reasoning verbosity), and gradient norm (training stability).

#### 3.5. StarPO-S: Stabilized Multi-turn RL

To mitigate the observed instability, `StarPO-S` introduces three key modifications:
- **Uncertainty-Based Trajectory Filtering:** Prompts are sorted by the standard deviation of rewards from repeated rollouts, and only the top `p%` most uncertain prompts are retained for training. This active learning principle focuses training on informative, high-variance examples.
- **KL Term Removal:** The KL divergence penalty, typically used in PPO to keep the new policy close to the old, is removed, allowing for more aggressive exploration.
- **Clip-Higher (Asymmetric Clipping):** The PPO clipping range is modified with a higher upper bound than the lower bound (e.g., ε_high=0.28, ε_low=0.2), allowing more aggressive learning from positive advantages.

#### 3.6. Rollout Quality Investigation

The paper investigates how factors affecting rollout quality influence training stability and generalization:
- **Task Diversity:** The number of distinct initial states (prompts) used per rollout-update cycle.
- **Interaction Granularity:** The number of actions allowed per turn.
- **Rollout Frequency:** How frequently new rollouts are collected (Online-k strategy).

#### 3.7. Reasoning Analysis

The study examines the evolution of explicit reasoning (`<think>` tokens) during training and its relationship with generalization, especially under varying reward designs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several critical findings that shed light on the challenges and design principles for training self-evolving LLM agents with multi-turn reinforcement learning:

1. **Multi-turn Agent RL Training Introduces New Instability Pattern:**
 - Vanilla adaptations of single-turn RL methods like PPO and GRPO, while showing initial gains, consistently collapse in multi-turn agent settings (e.g., Bandit and Sokoban tasks).
 - PPO, with its critic, offers slightly more stability than GRPO in some deterministic settings but still fails, suggesting that a critic alone is insufficient to prevent reasoning degradation. GRPO, surprisingly, can be more stable in highly stochastic environments like Frozen Lake, where value estimation is difficult for PPO.
 - **"Echo Trap":** A recurring instability pattern is identified where agents overfit to locally rewarded reasoning patterns. This leads to a collapse in behavioral diversity, with responses becoming repetitive and deterministic, hindering long-term generalization.

2. **Collapse Dynamics and Early Warning Signals:**
 - Model collapse is directly reflected by plateaus or drops in average reward and spikes in gradient norms, indicating unstable updates.
 - Crucially, **reward standard deviation** (within a group of rollouts from the same prompt) and **output entropy** (of generated tokens) serve as early warning signals. A sharp drop in reward standard deviation or erratic changes in entropy often precede performance degradation and signal the onset of the "Echo Trap." Gradient norm spikes typically mark the point of irreversible collapse.

3. **StarPO-S Stabilizes Multi-turn RL with Instance Filtering and Gradient Shaping:**
 - **Uncertainty-Based Filtering:** `StarPO-S`, a stabilized variant, significantly improves training robustness. By retaining only the top `p%` of high-variance prompts (where the agent exhibits outcome uncertainty), collapse is delayed or entirely mitigated, particularly for PPO. This validates the active learning principle that uncertain examples are most informative.
 - **Gradient Shaping:** Techniques like KL Term Removal (eliminating the KL divergence penalty from PPO's objective) and Clip-Higher (asymmetric clipping allowing more aggressive learning from positive rewards) further boost success rates and extend stable training phases.
 - Overall, StarPO-S consistently delays collapse and achieves higher final success rates across all tested environments compared to vanilla StarPO, attributed to more selective training data and balanced optimization signals.

4. **Generating Useful Trajectories for RL Training:**
 - **Task Diversity with Response Comparison:** Higher task diversity (achieved by having multiple responses per initial state, e.g., 4 responses per prompt) consistently yields better generalization across environments. This allows the agent to contrast different outcomes under similar conditions.
 - **Moderate Interaction Granularity:** Allowing 5-6 actions per turn achieves optimal performance, balancing sufficient planning space with avoiding noise from overly long or diluted reward sequences. Too many actions can degrade performance.
 - **Frequent Rollout Updates:** An "Online-k" strategy (where k is the number of policy updates per rollout collection) shows that fresher rollouts (Online-1, i.e., new rollouts collected every iteration) lead to faster convergence and better generalization. This emphasizes the importance of aligning optimization targets with the agent's most current behavior.

5. **Reasoning Improves Generalization but Fades Without Fine-Grained Rewards:**
 - **Reasoning Benefits Single-Turn Tasks:** In single-turn Bandit tasks, explicit reasoning (`<think>` blocks) enhances generalization, even in counter-intuitive reward scenarios (BanditRev), suggesting it helps internalize symbolic cues beyond rote memorization.
 - **Reasoning Degradation in Multi-Turn Tasks:** In complex multi-turn environments like Sokoban and Frozen Lake, reasoning provides limited benefits and tends to shrink over training iterations, even when prompted for.
 - **Hallucinated Reasoning:** Critically, models can produce incoherent or "hallucinated" reasoning traces yet still achieve high rewards if the reward signal is solely outcome-based. This "spurious reward" makes it difficult for RL to reliably reinforce genuinely useful intermediate reasoning, leading agents to discover shallow strategies that bypass reasoning.
 - This highlights the critical need for **fine-grained, reasoning-aware reward design** that explicitly values coherent thought processes, not just final task success.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "RAGEN: Understanding Self-Evolution in LLM Agents via Multi-Turn Reinforcement Learning" makes significant contributions to the field of AI agents, particularly in the context of large language models and reinforcement learning. Its potential impact spans both theoretical understanding and practical application:

1. **Advancing LLM Agent Capabilities:** By introducing `StarPO` and `RAGEN`, the paper provides a foundational framework and system for training LLMs as interactive, self-evolving agents in multi-turn, stochastic environments. This represents a crucial step beyond traditional single-turn fine-tuning, pushing LLMs closer to truly autonomous behavior in dynamic settings like robotics, complex problem-solving, and adaptive assistants.

2. **Identifying and Mitigating Core Instabilities:** The discovery and characterization of the "Echo Trap" phenomenon is a major finding. This instability, common in training on self-generated data, highlights a fundamental challenge in deep reinforcement learning with LLMs. The proposed `StarPO-S` with uncertainty-based filtering and gradient shaping offers concrete, effective solutions, enabling more robust and stable training of LLM agents. This directly addresses the critical need for reliable self-improvement mechanisms in AI.

3. **Establishing Design Principles for Agent RL:** The systematic analysis of rollout quality factors (task diversity, interaction granularity, rollout frequency) provides invaluable design principles for future LLM agent training. These insights offer practical guidance for researchers and practitioners on how to structure data collection and policy updates to maximize learning efficiency and generalization. For instance, the emphasis on frequent, diverse rollouts underscores the importance of an "online" learning paradigm for adaptive agents.

4. **Underscoring the Importance of Meticulous Reward Design:** The finding that reasoning can degrade or become "hallucinated" without fine-grained, reasoning-aware reward signals is profoundly significant. It challenges the assumption that outcome-based rewards alone are sufficient to cultivate complex, interpretable reasoning in LLMs. This calls for a paradigm shift in reward modeling for agents, necessitating sophisticated mechanisms that can evaluate and reinforce intermediate thought processes, not just final actions. This is a critical area for future research, impacting fields from AI safety to explainable AI.

5. **Benchmarking and Research Infrastructure:** `RAGEN` itself, as an open-source system, provides a valuable research infrastructure. It enables systematic experimentation, replication of findings, and further exploration of LLM agent training dynamics. This lowers the barrier to entry for researchers wanting to work on multi-turn agent RL, accelerating progress in the field.

6. **Insights into Model Scaling and Generalization:** The comparison of model scales (0.5B to 72B) reveals that while larger models benefit from pre-training priors in language-rich tasks (Bandit, WebShop), careful RL training on smaller models can achieve comparable generalization on symbolic tasks (Sokoban, Frozen Lake). This suggests that algorithmic advancements and meticulous training methodologies can be as crucial as raw model scale in certain domains, potentially enabling efficient development of capable agents even with resource constraints (e.g., demonstrated by the efficiency of LoRA fine-tuning).

In conclusion, this paper provides a timely and comprehensive investigation into the complex landscape of multi-turn RL for LLM agents. By identifying new instabilities, proposing effective stabilization techniques, and detailing crucial design considerations, it paves the way for the development of more intelligent, robust, and truly autonomous language-based AI systems.
