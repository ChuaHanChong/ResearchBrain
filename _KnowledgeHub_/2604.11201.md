# CocoaBench: Evaluating Unified Digital Agents in the Wild

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced COCOABENCH, a benchmark evaluating unified digital agents on their ability to integrate vision, search, and coding to solve complex, long-horizon tasks in open digital environments. Evaluations reveal that current state-of-the-art agents achieve a maximum success rate of 45.1%, indicating substantial room for improvement in compositional reasoning.

## Problem

- Existing agent evaluation benchmarks focus on assessing individual capabilities in isolation (e.g., GUI-only, CLI-only) or within fixed tool ecosystems.
- A lack of systematic evaluation exists for unified digital agents on tasks explicitly demanding the adaptive composition of multiple core capabilities (vision, search, coding) in open-ended, real-world digital environments.
- Reliable and scalable evaluation for complex, long-horizon tasks, especially those involving multi-step interactions, is challenging to achieve without relying on subjective human or LLM judges.

## Method

- The **COCOABENCH** dataset was constructed, comprising 153 human-authored tasks designed for ==multi-capability integration== across nine diverse domains, with minimal instruction specification for infrastructure agnosticism.
- Each COCOABENCH task includes an ==automatic, outcome-based evaluation script== to ensure reproducible and scalable assessment without human or LLM judgment.
- A lightweight, modular agent scaffold called **COCOA-AGENT** was developed, utilizing an ==AIO Sandbox== and a ==ReAct-based== approach with general-purpose tools for browser interaction, file manipulation, execution, and control, enabling controlled comparisons across LLM backbones.

## Results

- GPT-5.4 under the Codex scaffold achieved the highest success rate at **45.1%**, demonstrating its strong performance across different configurations.
- Leading open-source multimodal models (Kimi-k2.5 and Qwen3.5-397B-A13B) showed significantly lower success rates (**11.8%** and **9.8%** respectively) compared to proprietary models.
- Error analysis revealed that reasoning and planning failures accounted for the largest share of issues (approximately **53%**), followed by visual grounding (**28%**) and tool/execution errors (**19%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art unified digital agents exhibit limited reliability on complex compositional tasks, with the best-performing system achieving only a 45.1% success rate.
> - Agent scaffold design significantly influences performance and cost efficiency, with coding-centric scaffolds showing effective generalization for broader problem-solving.
> - Stronger agents strategically leverage programmatic execution (coding tools) extensively for multi-step reasoning, data processing, and structured output formatting, demonstrating a positive correlation between coding tool usage and success rates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language model (LLM) agents has progressed significantly, demonstrating strong performance in specialized domains such as software engineering, graphical user interface (GUI) automation, and deep research for report generation. Concurrently, a trend in agentic frameworks and models, exemplified by systems like OpenClaw, Claude Cowork, GPT-5.4, Claude Sonnet 4.6, and Seed-2.0, aims to integrate these diverse capabilities into single, unified digital agents. These unified agents are envisioned as comprehensive assistants capable of tackling complex, real-world tasks that necessitate the flexible combination of multiple interaction modalities.

However, the existing landscape of agent evaluation benchmarks predominantly focuses on assessing individual capabilities in isolation. For instance, benchmarks like OSWorld and TerminalBench-2 specialize in computer use in virtual machine environments and command-line interface (CLI) tasks, respectively. SWE-bench Pro targets repository issue resolution, largely within software engineering contexts. Other benchmarks, such as MCP Atlas and Tool Decathlon, evaluate tool use but within fixed, predefined tool ecosystems. BrowseComp focuses on open-web research but adheres to a specific iterative search pattern. While GDPval covers a broad range of occupation-grounded tasks, its evaluation relies on human expert judgment, which can introduce scalability and reproducibility challenges. This specialized nature of current benchmarks creates a gap in systematically evaluating the performance of unified digital agents on tasks that explicitly demand the adaptive composition of multiple core capabilities—namely, vision (for GUI interaction and visual interpretation), search (for information seeking and synthesis), and coding (for problem-solving, quantitative analysis, and structured tool invocation) in open-ended environments.

COCOABENCH addresses this gap by providing a benchmark specifically designed for unified digital agents. It aims to evaluate agents not on isolated skills, but on their ability to integrate and compose vision, search, and coding capabilities to solve long-horizon, human-designed tasks in diverse, real-world digital environments. This work contributes to the broader research goal of developing and assessing general-purpose digital agents that can effectively assist humans with complex, multi-modal tasks, moving beyond single-modality or domain-specific solutions. The benchmark's design, which emphasizes minimal task specification and automatic outcome-based evaluation, further distinguishes it from existing methods that may rely on constrained environments or subjective assessments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and establish COCOABENCH, a benchmark specifically designed to evaluate unified digital agents. This evaluation focuses on the agents' ability to flexibly compose core capabilities, namely vision, search, and coding, to address complex, long-horizon tasks in open digital environments.

The motivation for developing COCOABENCH stems from several observations within the current state of LLM agent research:

1. **Emergence of Unified Agent Systems:** Recent advancements have led to the development of LLM agents that integrate multiple functionalities, aiming to serve as comprehensive digital assistants. These systems promise to assist humans with complex tasks requiring diverse interactions with digital environments.
2. **Limitations of Existing Benchmarks:** Despite the rise of unified agents, existing evaluation benchmarks primarily test agent capabilities in isolation. They often confine agents to single domains (e.g., software engineering, GUI-only tasks, CLI-only tasks) or fixed tool ecosystems, pre-specifying the available tools or interfaces. This narrow scope prevents a comprehensive assessment of how effectively an agent can combine different interaction modalities and tools in a flexible, adaptive manner to achieve a complex goal.
3. **Need for Compositional Evaluation:** Real-world digital tasks frequently require agents to seamlessly transition between and compose various capabilities. For example, a task might involve searching the web for information, then interpreting a visual chart from a webpage, and finally writing code to process and present the extracted data. Current benchmarks are ill-equipped to measure performance on such compositional challenges.
4. **Desire for Open-World Reasoning and Infrastructure Agnosticism:** Many benchmarks couple agents to specific runtimes, interfaces, or tool environments. This limits the assessment of an agent's ability to reason about and select appropriate tools in an unconstrained, "open-world" setting. COCOABENCH aims to decouple task evaluation from specific agent infrastructures, requiring agents to infer tool use from high-level instructions.
5. **Reliable and Scalable Evaluation:** Evaluating complex, long-horizon tasks, especially those involving multi-step interactions, can be resource-intensive and prone to subjectivity. A key motivation was to design an evaluation system that is both reliable (producing consistent results) and scalable (allowing for evaluation across many agents and tasks) without relying on human or LLM judges for final correctness. This led to the design of automatic, outcome-based evaluation functions.
6. **Facilitating Controlled Comparison:** To enable analytically interpretable comparisons across different LLM backbones, the authors were motivated to develop a lightweight, shared agent scaffold (COCOA-AGENT). This scaffold standardizes the agentic components, allowing researchers to isolate the performance impact of the underlying language models.

In summary, the work is motivated by the need for a robust and comprehensive evaluation framework that mirrors the complexity and multi-modality of real-world digital tasks, thereby advancing the development of truly unified and general-purpose digital agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involved the construction of the COCOABENCH dataset, the design of a controlled evaluation scaffold (COCOA-AGENT), and the execution of extensive experiments using both existing agent systems and various LLM backbones.

#### 3.1. COCOABENCH Task Construction

COCOABENCH comprises 153 human-authored tasks designed to assess unified agents on complex problem-solving. The task creation process adhered to three key criteria:
- **Multi-Capability Integration:** Each task was required to necessitate the integration of multiple core capabilities.
- **Nontrivial Challenge:** Tasks were designed to present a significant challenge for humans in realistic settings.
- **Stable External Resources:** Dependencies on external resources were chosen to ensure long-term stability and validity.

**Inclusive Task Settings:** Tasks are minimally defined by an instruction and an evaluation function over the agent's final output, making them agnostic to specific agent infrastructures, runtimes, or tool ecosystems. This approach necessitates agents to reason about tool use in an open-world setting rather than relying on predefined toolsets. For tasks requiring external multimodal inputs, such as images or documents, their URLs are embedded directly within the instructions.

**Automatic Evaluation Functions:** Each task includes an associated evaluation script for automated and reproducible assessment. Where feasible, tasks demand outputs in specific structured formats (e.g., string, list, dictionary). For tasks involving multi-step environmental interactions, outcome-based proxy evaluators are utilized. These evaluators verify the final result, assuming that a correct outcome strongly implies successful execution, thereby balancing task realism with scalable and reproducible evaluation.

**Quality Control:** A rigorous peer-review process was applied to all tasks, reference answers, and evaluation functions. Reviewers ensured clarity of instructions, well-defined output formats, correctness of reference answers, absence of trivial shortcuts, and accessibility of external resources. Pilot experiments with initial agent versions were conducted to identify and refine tasks based on recurring failure patterns, distinguishing agent errors from benchmark design issues.

#### 3.2. Task Diversity and Composition Analysis

The constructed benchmark exhibits diversity across several dimensions:
- **Task Domains:** COCOABENCH spans nine diverse domains: Business, Culture, Education, Life, Logic & Puzzles, Science, Sports, Technology, and Travel, mirroring everyday digital challenges.
- **Task Resources:** Tasks leverage a variety of resources, including webpages, videos, images, and documents (CSV, PDF, BibTeX files). Task designers also hosted 17 custom websites and contributed artifacts like Weights & Biases logs and Costco receipts to create realistic scenarios.
- **Target Capabilities:** Each task was human-annotated for its primary reliance on Vision (extracting visual information), Search (accessing and analyzing internet information), and Coding (writing code for efficient problem-solving). Analysis indicated that 98% of tasks require multiple capabilities, with coding being identified as important for 56.2% of tasks.

#### 3.3. Experiment Settings

**Existing Agentic Systems:** A selection of representative agent systems was evaluated, covering various designs and capability profiles:
- ChatGPT Agent Mode (OpenAI, 2025b)
- OpenClaw (OpenClaw, 2026) instantiated with GPT-5.4 and Claude Sonnet 4.6
- Codex (OpenAI, 2026) with GPT-5.4
- Claude Code (Anthropic, 2026a) with Claude Sonnet 4.6
- OpenAI Deep Research (OpenAI, 2025a) (o4-mini version)
Each run was allocated a 30-minute wall clock budget and a maximum of 50 interaction turns.

**COCOA-AGENT Scaffold:** To enable controlled comparisons across LLM backbones, COCOA-AGENT, a lightweight and modular agent scaffold, was developed. It uses an AIO Sandbox (Docker container integrating browser, shell, file system) for isolated and scalable execution. The scaffold adopts a ReAct-based approach, providing general-purpose tools for:
- **Browser Interaction:** Both DOM-level APIs and screenshot-based GUI control (17 tools).
- **DOM Tools:** Programmatic access to page structure (11 tools).
- **File Manipulation:** Reading, writing, listing, searching, and image reading (9 tools).
- **Execution:** Shell commands and sandboxed Python/JavaScript execution (2 tools).
- **Control:** Task completion signal (1 tool).
This structured tool interface supports the three core capabilities (vision, search, coding) while minimizing tool hallucination.

**Models under COCOA-AGENT:** The following models were evaluated using the COCOA-AGENT scaffold:
- Proprietary models: Claude Sonnet 4.6, GPT-5.4, Gemini-3.1-pro, Gemini-Flash-3.0.
- Open-source multimodal models: Kimi-k2.5 (MoE, 1T parameters), Qwen3.5-397B-A13B (MoE, 397B parameters).

#### 3.4. Error Analysis

A structured error analysis was conducted on 712 failed trajectories across all six models evaluated under COCOA-AGENT. An LLM (Claude Sonnet 4.6) was used as a judge to annotate failure causes based on a defined taxonomy. Failures were categorized into three main classes:
- **Reasoning & Planning (E1):** Failures related to devising an effective approach, imprecise reasoning, or losing track of task requirements.
- **Tool & Execution (E2):** Breakdowns in interacting with tools or interfaces, including execution errors, infinite loops, or issues with anti-bot barriers.
- **Visual Grounding (E3):** Fundamental perceptual disconnects, such as misreading, overlooking, or misinterpreting visual information.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations revealed several insights into the capabilities and limitations of current unified digital agents.

#### 4.1. Overall Results

- **Limited Reliability:** Current agents demonstrate limited reliability on COCOABENCH tasks. The top-performing system, GPT-5.4 under the Codex scaffold, achieved a success rate of 45.1%.
- **Model Backbone Impact:** GPT-5.4 consistently performed strongly across different scaffolds, achieving 45.1% with OpenClaw and Codex, and 36.6% with COCOA-AGENT. Claude Sonnet 4.6 was competitive in some settings (34.0% with OpenClaw) but exhibited less stable performance elsewhere (25.5% with Claude Code, 15.7% with COCOA-AGENT).
- **Open-Source Performance:** Leading open-source multimodal models, Kimi-k2.5 and Qwen3.5-397B-A13B, achieved significantly lower success rates of 11.8% and 9.8%, respectively, indicating a considerable performance gap compared to proprietary models.
- **Scaffold Influence:** Agent scaffold design was observed to play a substantial role. Scaffolds originally developed for coding, such as Codex and Claude Code, demonstrated effective generalization beyond their initial domain, functioning as general problem solvers on COCOABENCH. OpenClaw also proved to be a robust scaffold. COCOA-AGENT, despite its lightweight design, provided a foundation for meaningful base model comparisons.

#### 4.2. Cost and Model Performance

- **Efficiency vs. Performance:** An analysis of average cost (USD) and task completion time (seconds) per task revealed no clear direct correlation between higher costs or longer times and better performance. Codex, utilizing GPT-5.4, demonstrated the most efficient balance of cost and performance, positioning it on the Pareto frontier.
- **Scaffold-Dependent Cost:** Cost efficiency was significantly influenced by scaffold design. For example, using GPT-5.4 as the base model, Codex incurred an average cost of $0.75 per task, compared to $1.09 for OpenClaw and $2.31 for COCOA-AGENT.

#### 4.3. Tool Statistics (COCOA-AGENT)

- **Dominance of Coding Tools:** Across the six models evaluated under COCOA-AGENT, coding tools (`code execute` and `shell execute`) accounted for the largest proportion of total tool invocations. Browser-level actions (`browser navigate`, `image read`) were also frequently used, with DOM-level interaction tools appearing at moderate frequencies. This distribution reflects the benchmark's compositional demands, requiring agents to both acquire and process information programmatically.
- **Tool Usage and Performance Correlation:** A positive correlation was observed between the proportion of tool calls allocated to coding and overall success rates. GPT-5.4 and Gemini 3.1 Pro, which achieved the highest success rates (36.6% and 26.1% respectively), allocated over 60% of their tool calls to coding. In contrast, lower-performing models like Kimi-k2.5 (11.8% SR) and Qwen3.5-397B-A13B (9.8% SR) assigned around 30% of their calls to coding. This suggests that programmatic execution serves as an effective strategy for multi-step reasoning, data processing, and structured output formatting on COCOABENCH tasks, allowing stronger models to separate information acquisition from downstream processing.

#### 4.4. Error Analysis

An analysis of 712 failed trajectories identified three main classes of errors:
- **Reasoning & Planning (E1):** This category accounted for the largest share of failures, approximately 53% across all models. These errors involve incorrect logical paths, goal displacement (solving a sub-problem instead of the main task), imprecise computations, or failures to adhere to output format requirements.
- **Visual Grounding (E3):** Approximately 28% of failures were attributed to visual grounding issues. These include failures to resolve fine-grained visual details, lack of prior world knowledge to map visual features to semantic concepts, or missing content that is visually rendered but not exposed through DOM queries.
- **Tool & Execution (E2):** This category comprised about 19% of failures, relating to structural breakdowns in the agent's interaction loop, such as infinite loops (repetitive actions without recovery), being blocked by anti-bot barriers without recognition, or tool result hallucination (fabricating outputs or suffering from context truncation).

**Comparison between GPT-5.4 and Kimi K2.5:**
- **Reasoning and Planning:** Kimi K2.5 exhibited more frequent failures in incorrect reasoning (E1.1) and format errors (E1.3) compared to GPT-5.4, indicating challenges in procedural knowledge and maintaining task instruction adherence over long horizons.
- **Tool & Execution:** Kimi K2.5 was more susceptible to infinite loops (E2.1), suggesting a weaker ability to self-correct or recover from unexpected tool outputs.
- **Visual Grounding:** Kimi K2.5 showed noticeable underperformance in visual detail (E3.1) errors, indicating less reliable perception of subtle visual information critical for task completion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of COCOABENCH and COCOA-AGENT carries several significant implications for the advancement and evaluation of artificial intelligence.

First, this work addresses a critical **gap in the evaluation landscape** for LLM agents. By creating a benchmark focused on the flexible composition of vision, search, and coding capabilities for complex, long-horizon tasks in open digital environments, COCOABENCH moves beyond the limitations of existing benchmarks that largely assess capabilities in isolation or within constrained settings. This shift is crucial for fostering the development of truly unified digital agents that can effectively interact with the diverse and dynamic nature of the real world.

Second, the findings provide a **realistic assessment of current agent capabilities**, demonstrating that even state-of-the-art systems remain far from reliable on compositional digital tasks, with the best-performing agent achieving only a 45.1% success rate. This quantified insight highlights the substantial room for improvement required before these agents can serve as robust general-purpose assistants.

Third, the detailed **error analysis illuminates specific weaknesses** in current agent designs. The prominence of failures in reasoning and planning, tool interaction and execution, and visual grounding offers clear, actionable directions for future research. This includes enhancing agents' ability to formulate robust multi-step plans, interpret complex visual information, recover from unexpected tool outputs, and manage long interaction histories without losing context.

Fourth, by open-sourcing COCOABENCH (including task instructions and evaluation scripts) and COCOA-AGENT (a lightweight, shared scaffold), the research **promotes standardized, reproducible evaluation** within the AI community. This facilitates controlled comparisons across different agent architectures and underlying LLM backbones, accelerating progress by enabling researchers to benchmark their innovations against a common, challenging standard. The COCOA-AGENT scaffold specifically offers a practical foundation for future research, including data engineering and reinforcement learning-based training for unified digital agents.

Finally, the observation that **coding is a critical ingredient for strong agent performance** on complex compositional tasks emphasizes the importance of programmatic processing. Stronger agents leveraged code execution extensively not only as an action space but also as an analytical tool for multi-step reasoning and structured output formatting. This suggests that integrating robust coding capabilities is vital for general-purpose digital agents, beyond traditional software engineering applications.

In sum, COCOABENCH and COCOA-AGENT are positioned to serve as foundational tools for understanding, evaluating, and driving the development of more capable and reliable general-purpose digital agents, bridging the gap between specialized agentic systems and the vision of human-level digital assistance.
