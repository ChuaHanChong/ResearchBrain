# Native Reasoning Models: Training Language Models to Reason on Unverifiable Data

> [!summary] Summary
> Native Reasoning Training (NRT) is a framework that trains language models to reason using only question-answer pairs, circumventing the need for human-annotated reasoning traces or external verifiers. This method, which intrinsically rewards reasoning that increases the model's confidence in correct answers, boosted average performance by 10.2 points over SFT on Llama-3.1-8B across nine benchmarks, achieving a 76.0 score on GSM8K compared to SFT's 29.0.

## Problem

- Traditional Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) depend on expensive human-annotated reasoning data and external verifiers, limiting scalability and applicability.
- RLVR's reliance on objective external verifiers restricts its use to domains like mathematics and coding, leaving many tasks with subjective or unverifiable correctness unaddressed.
- Existing verifier-free reinforcement learning approaches often suffer from instability, mode collapse, or introduce biases through proxy reward mechanisms like LLM judges.

## Method

- Native Reasoning Training (**NRT**) treats reasoning as a ==latent variable==, employing reinforcement learning to explore and intrinsically reward reasoning traces that enhance the model's predictive confidence in ground-truth answer tokens.
- The framework optimizes using off-policy RL with importance sampling, generating both trace policy gradients and weighted token prediction gradients to refine trace generation and answer prediction simultaneously.
- NRT introduces robust reward aggregation functions (e.g., Geometric Mean, Weighted Sum based on inverse or log-probability) and reward stabilization techniques to prevent policy collapse and ensure stable learning.

## Results

- NRT-WS (
− log p) achieved an average score of 56.2 on the Llama-3.1-**8B** model across nine benchmarks, outperforming the SFT baseline by 10.2 points and the strongest prior method (RLPR) by 5.4 points.
- The method demonstrated substantial performance gains in complex reasoning tasks, with NRT-WS (
− log p) increasing the Llama-3.1-**8B** model's GSM8K score from 29.0 (SFT) to 76.0.
- NRT effectively mitigated mode collapse and policy degradation, maintaining diverse and high-quality reasoning processes throughout training, and improved prediction confidence for high-entropy tokens by up to **15%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Principled reward shaping, particularly weighted-sum aggregation that targets difficult tokens, is crucial for cultivating robust reasoning abilities without explicit trace supervision.
> - Language models can intrinsically learn to resolve their own predictive uncertainties by generating reasoning that specifically boosts confidence in previously high-entropy, hard-to-predict tokens.
> - NRT enables the autonomous emergence of a structured reasoning vocabulary and format, allowing models to clearly differentiate between internal thought processes and final answers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) into Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) has largely been driven by a two-stage training paradigm: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) followed by Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). SFT typically involves training models to imitate human-written reasoning processes, often in the form of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) demonstrations. This phase provides a foundational ability to structure thoughts. Subsequently, RLVR refines and generalizes this ability by using an external verifier (e.g., a unit test for code, a final answer check for a mathematical problem) to reward any reasoning trace that leads to a correct output, thus moving beyond strict imitation of human-provided paths.

Despite the successes of the SFT+RLVR paradigm in domains like mathematics and coding, it faces two fundamental limitations. Firstly, the SFT phase necessitates extensive, high-quality, and costly human-annotated reasoning data. This reliance can introduce human biases and limit the model's exploration of novel reasoning strategies. Secondly, the dependency on external verifiers restricts the applicability of RLVR to objectively assessable domains, leaving a substantial range of tasks where reasoning is crucial but correctness is subjective or unverifiable (e.g., open-ended question answering, creative writing, summarization).

To address this gap, alternative approaches employing "proxy rewards" or "self-rewarding" mechanisms have emerged. Proxy reward methods often utilize a powerful LLM judge to score outputs based on a rubric or human preferences. However, these methods introduce complexities in rubric design and are constrained by the judge model's inherent capabilities and potential biases. Self-rewarding approaches train a model to generate its own feedback, typically by rewarding high-confidence or consistent answers. A common challenge for these methods is "mode collapse," where the policy converges to simple, low-entropy outputs, hindering exploration and diversity.

This paper's work, Native Reasoning Training (NRT), positions itself within the "Reinforcement Learning on Unverifiable Data" category. It directly addresses the core limitations of the SFT+RLVR paradigm and existing verifier-free methods. NRT seeks to cultivate reasoning abilities without requiring human-annotated reasoning traces or external verifiers, instead leveraging only question-answer pairs. By reframing the reasoning process as a latent variable and intrinsically rewarding traces that increase the model's likelihood of generating the ground-truth answer, NRT offers a principled framework to extend robust reasoning training to a broader spectrum of tasks, particularly those lacking objective verifiers. It aims to overcome the instability and mode collapse observed in some prior verifier-free RL approaches by systematically designing more robust reward aggregation functions.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent limitations of the prevailing SFT+RLVR paradigm for training large reasoning models. These limitations restrict the scalability, applicability, and potential for novel reasoning discovery in LLMs.

**Key Motivations:**

1. **High Data Collection Costs and Human Bias:** The initial SFT stage critically depends on expensive, high-quality human-annotated reasoning data. This data collection is resource-intensive and can inadvertently embed human cognitive biases and priors into the model, potentially constraining its ability to discover more effective or creative reasoning strategies beyond human imitation.
2. **Limited Applicability to Verifiable Domains:** The RLVR stage's reliance on external verifiers confines its application to objectively assessable domains such as mathematics and code generation. This leaves a significant portion of real-world tasks where reasoning is essential but correctness is subjective and unverifiable, such as open-ended question answering, summarization, or creative writing, beyond the scope of this powerful reinforcement learning approach.
3. **Drawbacks of Existing Verifier-Free Approaches:** Attempts to extend reasoning to unverifiable domains using proxy rewards (e.g., LLM judges) face challenges in rubric design complexity and are bottlenecked by the judge's own capabilities and biases. Other self-rewarding approaches have demonstrated susceptibility to issues like mode collapse, where the model's policy converges to simple, low-entropy outputs, thus limiting exploration and the generation of diverse, complex reasoning.

**Key Objectives:**

In response to these motivations, the authors established several objectives for the Native Reasoning Training (NRT) framework:

1. **Cultivate Reasoning without Explicit Trace Supervision:** To develop a novel framework that can foster complex reasoning abilities in language models without requiring human-written reasoning demonstrations. The goal is for reasoning to "emerge" as the model learns to connect questions to answers.
2. **Eliminate Reliance on External Verifiers:** To enable reasoning training using only standard question-answer pairs, thereby obviating the need for external verifiers and making the methodology applicable to tasks where objective correctness is elusive.
3. **Formalize Reasoning as a Latent Optimization Problem:** To treat the reasoning process as a latent variable to be discovered and to formulate the training objective as an optimization problem. This involves intrinsically rewarding reasoning paths that increase the model’s likelihood of producing the ground-truth answer.
4. **Design Robust Reward Aggregation Functions:** To systematically analyze intrinsic failure modes of prior verifier-free methods (e.g., policy collapse) and design more robust reward aggregation functions within the unified NRT framework. This aims to create a self-reinforcing feedback loop that allows the model to resolve its own uncertainty effectively.
5. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** To empirically demonstrate that NRT achieves superior performance compared to standard SFT baselines and existing verifier-free RL methods across diverse reasoning benchmarks, particularly in complex domains.
6. **Ensure Robustness to Training Instabilities:** To confirm that NRT exhibits high robustness to common training instabilities, such as policy collapse, which affect other reinforcement learning approaches.
7. **Provide a General and Scalable Training Path:** To offer a scalable and broadly applicable method for building more powerful reasoning systems that can operate across a wider range of tasks, unconstrained by data annotation or verifier availability.

### 3. Methodology and approach

Native Reasoning Training (NRT) is designed to cultivate reasoning abilities using only question-answer pairs, without explicit supervision on the reasoning trace itself. It treats the reasoning process as a latent variable that the model must discover.

#### 3.1. Core Principle: Latent Reasoning and Intrinsic Rewards

NRT employs reinforcement learning to explore potential reasoning traces. The central idea is to intrinsically reward traces that enhance the model's own predictive confidence in the ground-truth answer. This creates a self-reinforcing feedback loop where the model learns to generate reasoning that resolves its internal uncertainty regarding the correct answer.

The method formalizes this at the token level. For a given input `x` and reference answer `y* = (y*1..., y*T)`, a per-trace reward `R(z, θ)` is defined. This reward is derived by applying a differentiable aggregation function `f` to the model's conditional probabilities `c_i(z, θ) = π_θ(y*i | x, z, y*<i)` for each token `y*i`. The overall NRT objective is the expectation of this reward over sampled traces `z`.

#### 3.2. Optimization Process

The NRT objective is optimized using off-policy reinforcement learning with importance sampling. The gradient calculation yields two complementary learning signals:
1. **Trace Policy Gradient:** This term reinforces holistically useful traces (`z_k`) based on the overall `Trace Reward R(z_k, θ)`. It updates the policy for generating reasoning traces.
2. **Token Prediction Gradient:** This term is a weighted, per-token policy gradient for answer prediction. It updates the model to better predict answer tokens (`y*i`) given the generated trace (`z_k`), with each token's gradient weighted by a `Token Reward Signal`. This dual mechanism jointly refines both trace generation and answer prediction, enabling self-correction of the latent reasoning process.

#### 3.3. Intrinsic Reward Shaping via Aggregation Functions

The choice of the differentiable aggregation function `f` is critical, as it shapes the intrinsic reward landscape. Different functions prioritize different aspects of the prediction task. The paper analyzes several functions:

- **Sequence Log-Probability (logP):** `f(c) = Σ log c_j`. This corresponds to prior work (JLB) and results in a `Token Reward Signal` of `1`, effectively standard maximum likelihood for answer tokens.
- **Sequence Probability (P):** `f(c) = Π c_j`. This corresponds to prior work (Verifree) and results in `Token Reward Signal` equal to `R(z, θ)`.
- **Geometric Mean (GM):** `f(c) = (Π c_j)^(1/T)`. NRT-GM is more robust than arithmetic mean because a single near-zero probability token collapses the entire reward, forcing the model to perform well on all tokens.
- **Arithmetic Mean (AM):** `f(c) = (1/T) Σ c_j`. This corresponds to prior work (RLPR). This method can be dominated by high-probability (easy) tokens, potentially rewarding trivial or null traces that do not help with difficult tokens, which can lead to policy collapse.
- **Weighted Sum (WS):** `f(c) = Σ w_j c_j`. This scheme offers granular control over token importance.
 - **Inverse-Probability (NRT-WS (1/p)):** Weights `w_i` are inversely proportional to baseline probabilities `c_i,base` from a baseline model (e.g., `π_old`) with an empty trace. This encourages reasoning that targets difficult tokens.
 - **Log-Probability (NRT-WS (− log p)):** Weights `w_i` are proportional to `-log c_i,base`, further focusing on the most challenging tokens.

#### 3.4. Reward Stabilization

To stabilize policy gradient updates, the reward is replaced with a robust advantage estimate `A_k(θ)`, inspired by GRPO. This involves:
1. **Clipped Reward:** `R'k(θ) = max(0, R(z_k, θ) - R_base)`, where `R_base` is the reward from an empty trace `(z = ∅)`. This focuses the learning on traces outperforming a baseline.
2. **Group-wise Normalization:** The clipped rewards are normalized within their group to have zero mean and unit variance, reducing gradient variance and focusing optimization on relative effectiveness.

#### 3.5. Structural Format Supervision

A supplementary format-supervision loss `L_format` is applied only to special tokens (`<|think start|>`, `<|think end|>`) to encourage the model to explicitly distinguish the reasoning trace `z` from the final answer `y`. This guides the model to adopt a specific output format without rigid constraints. Incomplete traces are manually appended with `<|think end|>` to maximize data utility.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup

- **Models:** Llama-3.2-3B, Mistral-7B-v0.3, and Llama-3.1-8B.
- **Dataset:** A 200K-sample random subset of the `tulu-3-sft-mixture` dataset, containing only question-answer pairs (`x, y*`), without expert-written reasoning traces.
- **Methods Evaluated:**
 - **Baselines:** SFT, and re-implementations of prior work within the NRT framework: JLB (Sequence Log-Probability), Verifree (Sequence Probability), and RLPR (Arithmetic Mean).
 - **NRT Variants:** NRT-GM (Geometric Mean), NRT-WS (1/p) (Weighted Sum with inverse-probability), and NRT-WS (-log p) (Weighted Sum with negative log-probability).
- **Evaluation:** Nine diverse benchmarks (BBH, MMLU, DROP, PopQA, TruthfulQA, GSM8K, MATH, HumanEval, IFEval) using the standardized OLMES framework.
- **Implementation:** All RL methods trained using GRPO with a learning rate of 1e-5, batch size of 256, generating up to 2048 reasoning tokens, and a format-supervision loss weight of 0.3.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research presents several key findings regarding the effectiveness, robustness, and internal mechanisms of Native Reasoning Training (NRT).

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance in Verifier-Free Reasoning

NRT achieved the highest average performance across all evaluated model families (Llama-3.2-3B, Mistral-7B-v0.3, Llama-3.1-8B) compared to standard SFT baselines and prior verifier-free methods.
- On the Llama-3.1-8B model, the best NRT variant, NRT-WS (− log p), attained an average score of 56.2 across nine benchmarks. This represents a 10.2-point improvement over the SFT baseline (46.0) and a 5.4-point improvement over the strongest prior method, RLPR (50.8).
- For the smaller 3B model, NRT-WS (− log p) achieved a 39.9 average, outperforming the SFT baseline by 3.5 points, while other verifier-free RL methods (JLB, Verifree, RLPR) failed to surpass SFT.
- NRT demonstrated pronounced performance gains in complex, reasoning-intensive domains. For instance, on GSM8K (8B model), NRT-WS (− log p) boosted the score from 29.0 (SFT) to 76.0, significantly exceeding RLPR’s 65.0. Similarly, NRT-GM improved the BBH score by 13.1 points over RLPR to 54.3. These results indicate NRT's ability to elicit latent reasoning skills for multi-step problems.

#### 4.2. Efficacy of Principled Reward Shaping

The success of NRT is attributed to its principled reward shaping strategies, particularly the weighted-sum (WS) schemes.
- While NRT-GM showed significant improvements, the NRT-WS variants consistently yielded the best results. This supports the hypothesis that guiding the model to generate reasoning that resolves its own uncertainty is an effective training mechanism.
- NRT-WS (− log p), by prioritizing tokens with low baseline probability, emerged as the top-performing method, securing the highest scores on GSM8K, MMLU, DROP, and HumanEval on the 8B model. This superior performance over methods with simpler aggregation functions (e.g., RLPR's Arithmetic Mean, JLB's Sequence Log-Probability) highlights the importance of the intrinsic reward structure.

#### 4.3. Robustness to Mode Collapse and Policy Degradation

NRT effectively mitigated common reinforcement learning pitfalls, such as mode collapse and policy degradation, maintaining diverse, lengthy, and high-quality reasoning throughout training.
- In contrast, the RLPR baseline exhibited rapid degradation in both 3B and 8B models, converging to short, low-entropy reasoning traces with consistently low quality scores. This indicated that RLPR settled into a poor local optimum, generating trivial content.
- All NRT variants (GM, WS) sustained high-entropy and long reasoning processes while maintaining or improving semantic quality. This suggests NRT models engage in substantive reasoning steps rather than merely "babbling."
- This stability is linked to NRT’s reward mechanism, which, unlike the Arithmetic Mean reward (used in RLPR) that can reward trivial traces, either penalizes poor performance on any single token (Geometric Mean) or forces the model to address its weakest predictions (Weighted Sum schemes).

#### 4.4. Targeted Resolution of Predictive Weaknesses

NRT's targeted reward shaping was shown to effectively teach the model to resolve its own predictive weaknesses, leading to increased confidence in the most difficult tokens.
- Analysis of ground-truth token probabilities, categorized by the SFT model’s prediction entropy, revealed a skewed distribution with a long tail of highly uncertain tokens.
- Weighted-sum NRT schemes (NRT-WS (1/p) and NRT-WS (− log p)) exhibited a strong positive correlation between baseline token entropy and relative improvement in token probability (P/P_SFT). For the highest-entropy tokens, NRT-WS (1/p) increased assigned probability by up to 15%.
- Conversely, RLPR and NRT-GM showed negligible gains or even dips in confidence for these high-entropy tokens. This provides direct evidence that NRT’s targeted reward shaping enables the model to generate reasoning that addresses its most critical points of confusion.

#### 4.5. Emergent Vocabulary of Reasoning

Qualitative analysis of the NRT-WS (− log p) model revealed the autonomous development of a distinct "language of reasoning."
- Generated reasoning traces were rich with procedural and meta-cognitive terms such as "let," "step," "solve," "premise," "developed," "reasoning," and "explanation." This indicates active problem-solving and externalization of internal queries.
- The model successfully disentangled reasoning from the final answer, suppressing answer-specific formatting tokens (e.g., "boxed") from the reasoning trace. This demonstrates NRT’s ability to teach the model to differentiate between the "thinking" and "answering" parts of a task, leading to clean, step-by-step logic.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The Native Reasoning Training (NRT) framework introduces a significant advancement in the field of large language models, offering several key implications and potential impacts:

1. **Broadened Applicability of Reasoning Training:** NRT liberates reinforcement learning-based reasoning training from its current confinement to domains with objectively verifiable answers and costly human-annotated reasoning traces. By enabling effective reasoning training solely on question-answer pairs, NRT unlocks a vast array of "unverifiable" tasks, such as open-ended question answering, creative writing, summarization, and ethical deliberation, where explicit ground truth for intermediate reasoning steps or external verifiers are unavailable. This significantly expands the practical utility and scope of powerful reasoning models.

2. **Reduced Data Costs and Mitigated Human Bias:** The elimination of the need for expert-written reasoning demonstrations drastically reduces the financial and labor costs associated with data collection for SFT. Furthermore, by allowing models to discover their own reasoning paths rather than imitating human-authored ones, NRT lessens the risk of embedding human cognitive biases and priors. This could lead to more novel, efficient, and less biased reasoning strategies that are less constrained by human cognitive patterns.

3. **Enhanced Model Robustness and Stability:** NRT's principled reward shaping, particularly the Geometric Mean and Weighted Sum schemes, demonstrates a robust ability to prevent training instabilities such as policy collapse, a common issue in other RL-based fine-tuning methods. By intrinsically incentivizing the generation of genuinely helpful and comprehensive reasoning that resolves the model's own uncertainty, NRT fosters more stable and reliable learning dynamics, leading to higher quality and more consistent reasoning outputs.

4. **Improved Model Confidence and Reliability:** The core mechanism of NRT, which rewards reasoning that increases the model's predictive confidence in ground-truth tokens, directly addresses a critical aspect of model reliability. By teaching models to actively resolve their internal uncertainties, especially for difficult tokens, NRT can lead to models that are not only more accurate but also more "aware" of their own reasoning processes, potentially offering more trustworthy and explainable outputs.

5. **Foundation for More Capable AI Systems:** NRT provides a general, scalable, and robust training paradigm that could contribute to the development of a new generation of inherently more capable reasoning systems. The principles demonstrated by NRT, such as treating reasoning as a latent variable and leveraging intrinsic self-reinforcing feedback loops, could be extended to other stages of model development, including pre-training. This could instill foundational reasoning abilities from inception, leading to LLMs that are not just language processors but truly "native reasoners."

6. **Theoretical Advancements in Verifier-Free RL:** The unified framework presented by NRT not only offers practical solutions but also provides a theoretical lens to analyze and understand the reward structures and failure modes of prior verifier-free RL methods. This theoretical grounding can guide future research in designing even more sophisticated and effective intrinsic reward mechanisms for complex AI tasks.
