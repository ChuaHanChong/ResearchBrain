# v1: Learning to Point Visual Tokens for Multimodal Grounded Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Yonsei University and Seoul National University developed `v1`, a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) that uses a "point-and-copy" mechanism to dynamically re-access specific visual information during reasoning, mitigating "visual grounding decay." The `v1` model achieves improved performance on multimodal mathematical reasoning benchmarks, outperforming comparable 7B-scale MLLMs and approaching the performance of larger 72B-scale models.

## Problem

- Current MLLMs experience "visual grounding decay," where attention to visual tokens weakens and focus on relevant regions declines during extended reasoning chains.
- Existing MLLMs typically process an image once at the beginning of inference, relying on a static, cached visual representation for subsequent multi-step reasoning.
- This limitation hinders performance on tasks requiring precise, iterative consultation of visual details, such as complex multimodal mathematical reasoning.

## Method

- Introduces `v1`, a lightweight MLLM architecture featuring a =="point-and-copy" mechanism== that augments the model's output space to allow direct referencing and copying of input visual patch embeddings.
- Develops `v1g`, a large-scale dataset of ==300K multimodal reasoning traces== with fine-grained visual grounding annotations, constructed using an automated pipeline leveraging LLMs and MLLM cross-attention for robust object grounding.
- The model learns to dynamically predict whether to generate a standard text token or a special =="pointer token"== that, when selected, injects the referenced continuous visual embedding into the subsequent decoding step.

## Results

- `v1` consistently outperformed comparable 7B-scale general-purpose and reasoning-specialized MLLMs across MathVista, MathVision, and MathVerse multimodal mathematical reasoning benchmarks.
- Ablation studies confirmed the crucial role of the dynamic pointing mechanism, with `v1` achieving **34.5%** accuracy on MathVision testmini compared to **23.6%** for the base Qwen2.5-VL model.
- The `v1g` dataset's attention-based grounding method achieved **83.3%** correctness in human evaluations, and the trained `v1` model demonstrated high pointing accuracy (**82.7%** correct, **87.7%** appropriate).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs demonstrably suffer from "visual grounding decay," an empirical finding showing declining overall visual attention and reduced focus on salient regions as inference chains lengthen.
> - A lightweight "point-and-copy" mechanism for dynamically re-accessing and incorporating visual information can significantly improve MLLM performance and interpretability for complex grounded reasoning tasks.
> - High-quality, fine-grained visual grounding datasets can be effectively generated through automated pipelines leveraging powerful LLMs for trace decomposition and MLLMs for attention-based region grounding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a limitation within the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly concerning their ability to perform complex, multi-step reasoning that requires sustained interaction with visual information. Recent advancements in MLLMs (e.g., Liu et al., 2023; Bai et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025) have facilitated progress in complex reasoning tasks, with mathematical reasoning serving as a common benchmark due to its demands for long-horizon grounding and unambiguous solutions.

However, a fundamental architectural characteristic of many current MLLMs is that they process visual inputs only once at the beginning of an inference task. Image embeddings are typically encoded into a key-value cache, and subsequent reasoning largely occurs in the textual domain over this frozen visual context. This approach contrasts with human cognitive processes, where individuals often revisit and re-examine visual evidence multiple times throughout a reasoning task, especially when dealing with complex diagrams or images where intermediate steps depend on localized cues.

The paper identifies this disparity as "visual grounding decay," where the model's attention to relevant image regions diminishes as the reasoning chain extends. This phenomenon manifests as a general weakening of attention to all image tokens and a decline in the relative focus on semantically important regions.

Existing research on MLLM reasoning includes:
- **Text-only LLM reasoning**: Significant improvements in text-based reasoning through methods like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, reinforcement learning, and inference-time scaling (e.g., Jaech et al., 2024; Guo et al., 2025). However, extending these directly to multimodal settings without explicit visual re-engagement mechanisms remains challenging.
- **Multimodal CoT**: Models like LLaVA-CoT (Xu et al., 2025) and TVC (Sun et al., 2025) have adapted CoT reasoning for multimodal inputs, achieving strong performance on benchmarks like MathVista (Lu et al., 2024). Yet, these models still treat visual inputs as static context throughout the reasoning process.
- **Explicit Visual Reference**: A developing area focuses on equipping MLLMs with mechanisms for more explicit interaction with images. This includes predicting structured spatial references (e.g., bounding boxes or points), generating intermediate visual artifacts (e.g., sketches), or using hybrid tool-use pipelines (e.g., Gupta & Kembhavi, 2023; Hu et al., 2024).

This work specifically aligns with methods that enable dynamic visual re-access during decoding. It extends the "pointer-generator" concept (See et al., 2017), originally for selective text copying, to the domain of visual tokens. By allowing the model to "point and copy" relevant visual embeddings back into the reasoning stream, the proposed system aims to mitigate visual grounding decay and enhance multimodal reasoning capabilities, offering a lightweight and interpretable approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from an observed discrepancy between human and current MLLM reasoning processes when visual information is involved, particularly in mathematical problem-solving. Humans frequently revisit diagrams to extract specific details or re-evaluate visual evidence as their reasoning unfolds. In contrast, most MLLMs process an image once at the outset, encoding its features into a static cache. Subsequent reasoning then primarily relies on this frozen representation, making it challenging to re-ground intermediate steps or re-focus on localized visual cues (e.g., angles, tangency points, symmetries) as required by complex multi-step problems.

The authors empirically validated this limitation, observing "visual grounding decay" in existing MLLMs. As reasoning chains lengthen, the models exhibit a progressive reduction in overall attention to image tokens. Furthermore, even the relative weight placed on genuinely relevant visual tokens decreases, suggesting a loss of focus on the most informative regions. This decay hinders the model's ability to maintain a strong connection between its textual reasoning and the visual input, potentially leading to errors in complex tasks that demand precise visual grounding.

Given this motivation, the key objectives of this research were:
1. **Develop a Mechanism for Active Visual Referencing**: To design and implement a lightweight extension for MLLMs that enables them to dynamically "point and copy" relevant image patches back into the reasoning stream during inference. This mechanism is intended to allow models to actively re-access and reuse visual information as needed.
2. **Ensure Semantic Alignment**: To ensure that the point-and-copy mechanism retrieves image patches using their semantic representations as keys, thereby maintaining perceptual evidence in alignment with the model's reasoning space.
3. **Create a Grounded Reasoning Dataset**: To construct a large-scale dataset, v1g, comprising approximately 300,000 multimodal mathematical reasoning traces with fine-grained, interleaved grounding annotations. This dataset is designed to provide the necessary supervision for training the active visual referencing behavior.
4. **Empirically Validate Performance Improvement**: To evaluate the proposed model, v1, on established multimodal mathematical reasoning benchmarks and demonstrate that dynamic visual access, facilitated by the point-and-copy mechanism, leads to improved reasoning capabilities compared to existing baselines.
5. **Mitigate Visual Grounding Decay**: To provide evidence that the v1 architecture, through its dynamic visual reference, effectively mitigates the problem of visual grounding decay observed in other MLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on introducing a "point-and-copy" mechanism as a lightweight extension to existing MLLMs, enabling dynamic visual re-access during reasoning. This is complemented by the creation of a specialized dataset for training this behavior.

**v1 Model: Pointing for Multimodal Grounded Reasoning**
The v1 model operates as a self-contained MLLM, integrating textual reasoning, visual localization, and point-and-copy operations without external tools. It extends standard autoregressive MLLM token prediction.

1. **Formulation**: Standard MLLMs autoregressively predict the next discrete token conditioned on input `c` (embedded text or visual features) and previously generated tokens `x_<t>`. The challenge is that multimodal inputs often consist of continuous feature sequences (image embeddings), which do not map to discrete vocabulary tokens.
2. **Augmented Output Space**: v1 addresses this by augmenting the output space to $\bar{V} = V \cup C$, where $V$ is the standard vocabulary and $C = \{c_1, c_2, \dots, c_K\}$ is the set of continuous input vectors (e.g., embeddings of image patches). A pointer to an input vector $c_k$ is denoted as $\langle\text{ptr}: c_k\rangle$, treated as a discrete token during decoding.
3. **Dual Distribution**: At each decoding step, the model computes two distributions:
 - A generation distribution over the vocabulary $V$, producing logits `logit_gen`.
 - A pointing distribution over the input positions $C$, producing logits `logit_ptr`.
 These are concatenated to form the final output logits.
4. **Pointing Logit Computation**: Pointing logits are calculated by attending over the image sequence. Specifically, `logit_ptr^(k)` for input $c_k$ is computed as `L_q(h_t) ⋅ L_k(c_k)^⊤ / √D`, where `h_t` is the decoder hidden state, `L_q` and `L_k` are learned linear projections (lightweight linear heads added to the MLLM backbone), and $D$ is the latent dimensionality.
5. **Point-and-Copy Operation**: During inference, if the model selects an index in $V$, a vocabulary token is emitted. If an index $k \in C$ is selected, the token is represented as $\langle\text{ptr}: c_k\rangle$. In the subsequent decoding step, the input embedding at that position is replaced with the continuous vector $c_k$, effectively "copying" the visual information directly into the attention mechanism for the next step.
6. **Lightweight Integration**: v1 uses Qwen2.5-VL as the MLLM backbone and adds only two linear layers (`L_q`, `L_k`), incurring minimal computational overhead. This approach reuses existing input embeddings rather than generating new image tokens, avoiding extensive pretraining.

**v1g Dataset Construction**
To train the v1 model, a dataset of 300,000 multimodal mathematical reasoning paths, v1g, was constructed with interleaved grounding annotations linking reasoning steps to specific image regions. The pipeline is fully automated and comprises three stages:

1. **Oversampling Textual Reasoning Traces**: Initial reasoning traces were sourced from the training set of TVC (Sun et al., 2025), which leverages the QvQ model (Qwen Team, 2024).
2. **Decomposing Reasoning Traces into Visual Reference Steps**: An off-the-shelf LLM (Gemini-2.0-flash) was used to extract visual grounding cues from the text-based reasoning traces. Each visual reference was rewritten as a `detect` call, which includes a natural-language description and returns a corresponding image region. Retrieved regions are assigned symbolic identifiers (`<objX>`). The LLM also generates a key-value list of visual components. Few-shot prompts guide this process. Invalid outputs (e.g., mismatches, non-unique labels, insufficient object count, ill-formed reasoning) are filtered.
3. **Grounding Visual References to Image Regions**: For each visual reference, bounding box annotations are generated. Instead of relying solely on the coordinate generation interface of MLLMs (which can struggle with abstract or unconventional regions like charts or geometry), the authors estimated the model's visual focus using a *relative attention mechanism* built on Qwen2.5-VL's grounding capacity. This method involves:
 - Preparing multimodal input with an image and a visual-grounding instruction prompt.
 - Extracting the cross-attention map (`A`) over image tokens from the MLLM's final decoding position, averaged across selected layers and heads.
 - Extracting a baseline attention map (`A'`) after removing the object name from the prompt.
 - Computing a contrastive relevance (`R = A / A'`) for each image token.
 - Deriving a bounding region by identifying the peak in `R` and sweeping over candidate crop ratios to select the box maximizing contrast sharpness. This region is then converted into image-coordinate bounding box `b`.

**Implementation Details**
- **Preprocessing**: Images are converted into flattened patch sequences. Bounding boxes are mapped to sequences of pointer tokens (e.g., `<ptr4>`, `<ptr32>`) that index the enclosed patches. These tokens are added to the tokenizer vocabulary, and their embeddings are dynamically replaced by the corresponding image patch embeddings during preprocessing before entering the transformer.
- **Training**: Qwen2.5-VL (7B) is used as the backbone. Training involves a base learning rate of $3 \times 10^{-5}$, batch size of 2, gradient accumulation over 4 steps, AdamW optimizer, DeepSpeed for distributed training, and 5 epochs. Z-loss regularization stabilizes the softmax partition function.
- **Inference**: v1 uses additional caches for key features (for pointing logits) and image patch features (for copying). The added model cost is minimal due to only one linear projection over image embeddings. Copied visual tokens increase sequence length, but typically remain below 60% of the text token count, resulting in an average total length of about 1.6 times that of text-only reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the limitations of current MLLMs and the efficacy of the proposed v1 model.

**Empirical Confirmation of Visual Grounding Decay**
Before introducing v1, the authors empirically analyzed the attention dynamics of TVC-7B on the RefCOCO benchmark. The findings indicated:
- **Total Attention Decline**: A consistent decrease in the cumulative attention scores directed towards all visual tokens as the generation steps progressed. This suggested a reduced overall reliance on the input image over time.
- **Relative Focus Decline**: Even when considering attention to semantically relevant visual regions (e.g., target bounding boxes), the ratio of attention on these specific regions relative to all image tokens tended to converge to a lower value (around 0.8 by mid-generation), indicating a weakening relative focus on salient visual content.
These observations supported the hypothesis of "visual grounding decay" and underscored the need for mechanisms enabling dynamic visual re-access.

**Downstream Evaluation on Multimodal Mathematical Reasoning**
v1 was evaluated on three established benchmarks: MathVista (mini), MathVision (mini/full), and MathVerse (mini).
- **Performance Leadership**: The v1 model (7B) consistently outperformed comparable 7B-scale baselines, including both general-purpose MLLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL 7B, InternVL2.5 8B) and reasoning-specialized models (e.g., LLaVA-CoT 11B, Mulberry 7B, TVC 7B).
- **Gap Reduction to Larger Models**: The 7B-scale v1 model significantly narrowed the performance gap with several much larger 72B/78B-scale MLLMs, particularly on tasks requiring precise visual grounding.
- **Strongest Gains on MathVision**: Improvements were notably pronounced on the MathVision benchmark, which is characterized by a strong demand for accurate visual grounding. For example, on MathVision mini, v1 achieved 34.5%, substantially higher than the 23.6% of the base Qwen2.5-VL 7B and the 22.7% of TVC 7B (full set). The average performance across benchmarks for v1 (38.4%) also surpassed that of competing 7B-scale models.

**Qualitative Results**
Qualitative comparisons with LLaVA-CoT on MathVision examples illustrated v1's capabilities:
- **Bar Graph Task**: In a problem requiring percentage calculation from a bar graph, v1 explicitly identified and processed the correct bar ("Candy E") via its pointer mechanism, leading to the correct answer. LLaVA-CoT, relying on text-only reasoning, misidentified the tallest bar.
- **Hexagon Pathfinding Task**: For a spatial reasoning task involving connectivity in hexagons, v1 actively attended to structural differences between options, facilitating the elimination of invalid candidates.
- **Complex Visual Reasoning (Appendix G)**: Additional examples showed v1 effectively handling attribute-based counting in synthetic domains (CLEVR-like) by localizing objects and sequentially reasoning through attributes (e.g., "red," "tiny matte balls"). It also performed comparative reasoning in chart comprehension, grounding key data series and revisiting them for nuanced comparisons of visual trajectories. These examples demonstrated how active visual reference supports more precise, interpretable, and robust grounded reasoning compared to text-only Chain-of-Thought methods.

**Ablation Study**
An ablation study on MathVision (testmini) quantified the contribution of the point-and-copy mechanism:
- **Backbone (Qwen2.5-VL 7B)**: Achieved a score of 23.6%.
- **Ours (no pointing)**: This variant was trained with the v1g dataset but had the point-and-copy mechanism disabled during inference. It scored 25.3% (+1.7% over the backbone), indicating that the specialized training data itself offered some improvement.
- **Ours (v1)**: With the point-and-copy mechanism enabled during inference, v1 achieved 34.5% (+10.9% over the backbone, and +9.2% over the "no pointing" variant). This established that the dynamic point-and-copy operation at inference time is a primary driver of the observed performance gains.

**Utilization of Pointed Visual Regions**
Analysis of attention patterns during generation after the first copy operation (Figure 5) showed:
- **Localization Phase**: Before a copy operation, attention to original image tokens increased, indicating the model's process of identifying relevant regions.
- **Focused Processing**: Immediately after copying, intermediate transformer layers (e.g., layers 2 and 14) exhibited dominant "Copy Attention" directed towards the copied tokens. This suggested focused, post-retrieval processing of the specifically referenced visual content.
- **Stable Reference**: Across all layers, copied tokens consistently received higher attention than the original image tokens, implying that copied regions serve as stable and readily accessible references for the model.
- **Integration**: In higher layers (e.g., layer 27), attention to both input and copied tokens became more balanced, suggesting a late-stage integration of retrieved visual information into the broader reasoning context.

**Human Evaluation of Grounding Quality (Appendix D)**
- **v1g Dataset Quality**: Human evaluation confirmed that the attention-based grounding method used for v1g achieved 83.3% correctness, significantly outperforming GroundingDINO (29.3%) for semantically complex and context-dependent visual references.
- **v1 Pointing Accuracy**: The trained v1 model demonstrated high pointing accuracy during inference, with 82.7% correctness for its pointed regions. An "appropriateness" score of 87.7% indicated that the model selectively invoked the pointing mechanism when it was contextually justified and beneficial for reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in equipping Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with more dynamic and human-like visual reasoning capabilities.

**Addressing a Core Limitation**: The primary significance of this work lies in its direct address of "visual grounding decay"—a previously identified limitation where MLLMs struggle to maintain focus on relevant visual information over extended reasoning sequences. By introducing the point-and-copy mechanism, v1 enables MLLMs to actively re-access and reuse visual evidence, thereby maintaining a stronger link between textual reasoning and visual context throughout multi-step tasks.

**Improved Multimodal Reasoning**: The consistent performance gains demonstrated across multiple mathematical reasoning benchmarks, particularly on tasks requiring precise visual grounding, indicate that dynamic visual access significantly enhances MLLMs' reasoning capabilities. The ability of a 7B-scale v1 model to approach the performance of much larger 72B/78B models suggests that architectural modifications for dynamic visual interaction can yield substantial benefits beyond simply scaling model size.

**Lightweight and Compatible Architecture**: The v1 model is designed as a lightweight extension, adding only minimal parameters (two linear layers) to existing MLLM backbones. This design choice avoids the computational expense and extensive pretraining required for models that generate new image tokens, making v1 a practical and energy-efficient solution for integrating dynamic visual referencing into current MLLM architectures. Its compatibility with popular MLLMs operating on continuous image embeddings facilitates broader adoption.

**Creation of a Valuable Dataset**: The v1g dataset, comprising 300,000 multimodal mathematical reasoning traces with fine-grained visual grounding annotations, is a significant contribution. This dataset provides a scalable and high-quality resource for training MLLMs in visually grounded reasoning, particularly in domains involving abstract visual elements like charts and geometric diagrams, where traditional object detectors often fall short. The automated pipeline for its construction also offers a template for generating similar datasets for other complex visual reasoning tasks.

**Enhanced Interpretability**: The explicit nature of the point-and-copy mechanism introduces a degree of interpretability into the MLLM's reasoning process. By observing which visual tokens the model chooses to copy at each step, researchers can gain insights into the specific visual evidence the model is leveraging, making its internal operations more transparent.

**Future Research Directions**: This work encourages further exploration into dynamic visual access as a fundamental component of multimodal reasoning. Potential future impacts include:
- **Generalization to Broader Domains**: Applying the v1 framework to other complex visual domains such as scientific diagrams, medical images, or visual commonsense reasoning, where sustained visual grounding is critical.
- **Advanced Control Mechanisms**: Investigating more sophisticated control mechanisms for visual retrieval, possibly incorporating weak supervision or reinforcement learning to enable more flexible and efficient strategies without dense annotations.
- **Deeper Integration**: Exploring how the integration of active visual referencing can be further optimized to achieve even tighter coupling between visual perception and language generation across diverse multimodal tasks.

Overall, v1 presents a pragmatic and effective method for improving MLLM performance on complex visual reasoning tasks by directly addressing a known architectural limitation, offering a pathway toward more robust and human-like multimodal AI systems.
