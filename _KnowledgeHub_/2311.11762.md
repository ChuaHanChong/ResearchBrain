# MUVO: A Multimodal Generative World Model for Autonomous Driving with Geometric Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> MUVO is a self-supervised, action-conditioned world model for autonomous driving that encodes front-camera images and lidar range views, fuses them with transformer self-attention instead of a Bird's-Eye-View bottleneck, and rolls a GRU-based probabilistic transition model forward in a 2D latent space. Decoders reconstruct future RGB frames, point clouds, and optional 3D occupancy voxels; transformer fusion with lossless range views proved the strongest configuration.

## Problem

- Most driving world models consume camera data alone, and the few lidar or multimodal ones ignore the combined stereo-camera-plus-lidar setup real autonomous vehicles actually deploy.
- Existing camera-lidar world models fuse through Bird's-Eye-View features, an acknowledged bottleneck that discards height information needed for accurate spatial prediction.
- Occupancy-predicting world models rely on vision only or operate purely in occupancy space, so nobody measured how multimodal fusion and actionable 3D occupancy prediction interact.

## Method

- ==Transformer sensor fusion==: image and range-view lidar feature maps flatten to tokens with 2D sinusoidal positional plus learnable sensor embeddings, then pass through a multi-head self-attention encoder, avoiding BEV projection. This defines **MUVO**.
- Two-dimensional latent space in the ==GRU==-based transition model, replacing fully connected GRU layers with convolutions and modelling prior/posterior distributions with a transformer decoder queried by learnable state embeddings.
- Multimodal decoder heads with a multi-scale self-supervised loss: L1 on RGB, L1 plus L2 on range-view point clouds, and ==Scene-Class Affinity Loss== on an optional 192x192x64 binary voxel head.

## Results

- Across **eight** encoder-fusion combinations on CARLA data from **four** towns and **300,000** frames, range-view lidar plus no-BEV images plus transformer fusion matched or beat all alternatives on PSNR and Chamfer Distance.
- The 2D latent state gave the largest gain for camera and voxel occupancy prediction, while an added perceptual loss and a MobileViT-V2 backbone showed little or no improvement beyond camera images.
- Camera-lidar pre-training for **50,000** steps sped up occupancy learning and beat training from scratch early; frozen pre-trained weights still improved, showing partial spatial information already encoded.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bird's-Eye-View fusion is not free: dropping it for token-level transformer fusion over lossless range views measurably improves future-prediction quality, especially for camera modality.
> - Representational bottlenecks matter more than backbone choice or auxiliary perceptual losses, so widen the latent state before reaching for fancier encoders.
> - Cheap sensor-reconstruction pre-training transfers usable geometric structure to expensive 3D occupancy heads, and adding occupancy prediction only mildly helps raw sensor forecasts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

World models represent a significant area of research in machine learning, particularly in autonomous driving, where they offer the potential to enhance the reasoning capabilities of systems by predicting future scenarios based on observations and actions. Current world models for autonomous driving predominantly focus on camera-based inputs, with a smaller number utilizing lidar data. While the fusion of camera and lidar data is recognized as crucial for comprehensive environmental perception in autonomous vehicles, only a limited number of recent world models integrate both modalities. A common practice in these multimodal approaches is to rely on Bird’s-Eye-View (BEV) features, which are identified as a potential bottleneck due to their inherent lack of height information, thus compromising the full 3D understanding of the environment.

Additionally, existing world models often predict raw sensor data, which can be less directly actionable for autonomous driving tasks compared to predictions of 3D occupancy. While some works have explored predicting 3D occupancies, they typically rely solely on visual inputs or operate exclusively in occupancy space, without integrating multimodal sensor data directly into the world model's predictive framework.

This work positions itself by addressing these identified gaps. It proposes a multimodal world model that leverages both camera images and lidar point clouds, moving away from BEV-centric sensor fusion strategies to avoid the loss of height information. Furthermore, it explicitly investigates the benefits of combining multimodal sensor data prediction with the prediction of actionable 3D occupancy, a combination not extensively explored in previous research. By systematically evaluating different sensor fusion strategies and the impact of 3D occupancy prediction, MUVO contributes to understanding the optimal architectural choices for developing more robust and capable world models for autonomous driving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and experimentally evaluate a multimodal generative world model for autonomous driving, termed MUVO (MUltimodal World Model with Geometric VOxel representations), to gain a deeper understanding of effective sensor fusion strategies and the benefits of integrating 3D occupancy prediction.

The specific motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

- **Address limitations of existing world models:** Current world models for autonomous driving primarily rely on camera data, neglecting the typical multi-sensor setups (camera and lidar) of autonomous vehicles. The few existing multimodal models often use BEV features, which are considered a bottleneck due to the loss of height information. MUVO aims to overcome this by exploring fusion strategies that do not rely on BEV features.
- **Evaluate sensor fusion strategies:** The research seeks to systematically evaluate various sensor fusion strategies for combining camera and lidar data. This involves comparing naive fusion approaches (e.g., feature averaging, concatenation) with more advanced transformer-based methods, and assessing their impact on the prediction quality of raw sensor data (camera images and lidar point clouds). The motivation is to identify optimal strategies for effectively integrating diverse sensor modalities.
- **Analyze the impact of different latent space representations:** The authors investigate the effects of modeling the latent space as two-dimensional compared to the more common one-dimensional representations, and also examine the influence of perceptual loss and vision transformer backbones on prediction performance.
- **Examine the benefits of 3D occupancy prediction:** A significant motivation is to explore the effects of additionally predicting 3D occupancy grids. While raw sensor predictions can be less actionable, 3D occupancy predictions offer direct spatial and geometric information. The research aims to understand how integrating a 3D occupancy decoder component affects the overall world model, specifically:
 - Whether 3D occupancy prediction can benefit from a model pre-trained solely on camera and lidar data.
 - Whether the knowledge encoded through 3D occupancy prediction can, in turn, improve the quality of camera and lidar predictions.
- **Inform future world model development:** By conducting extensive experiments with a relatively simple model architecture, the authors intend to "shine some light on how sensor fusion strategies impact the prediction quality of world models" and to provide insights into potential weaknesses of current sensor fusion approaches. This systematic evaluation is motivated by the goal of guiding the design of more robust and effective world models for autonomous driving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a Multimodal World Model with Geometric Voxel Representations (MUVO), designed to process and fuse RGB camera images and lidar point clouds to predict future observations, conditioned on actions. The model architecture is based on the fundamental structure of MILE, but with modifications to accommodate multimodal sensor fusion and 3D occupancy prediction.

#### 3.1. MUVO Architecture Overview

The MUVO architecture consists of four main components:
1. **Observation Encoder:** Processes raw RGB images and lidar point clouds.
2. **Multimodal Fusion:** Combines the latent representations from different sensors.
3. **Transition Model:** Predicts future latent states conditioned on actions.
4. **Multimodal Decoder:** Decodes future latent states into raw RGB images, point clouds, and optionally 3D occupancy grids.

#### 3.2. Observation Encoder

- **Inputs:** RGB images (600x960 pixels from a front camera) and lidar point clouds (up to 60,000 points from a top-mounted lidar).
- **Lidar Representation:** 3D point clouds are projected into a 2D cylindrical projection for lossless bijective representations.
- **Feature Extraction:** A pre-trained backbone (ResNet18 as baseline) is used to extract feature maps from different model layers, which are then fused to produce image features ($F_c \in R^{C \times H_c \times W_c}$) and point cloud features ($F_L \in R^{C \times H_L \times W_L}$).

#### 3.3. Multimodal Fusion

- **Primary Approach (Transformer-based):** Employs a Transformer's self-attention mechanism to fuse features.
 - Features ($F$) are flattened into sequences of tokens ($f \in R^{C \times HW}$).
 - 2D sinusoidal positional embeddings and learnable sensor embeddings are added to each token to introduce spatial inductive biases and sensor categorization.
 - Tokens from all sensors are concatenated and fed into a Transformer encoder with *k* layers, yielding new fused tokens ($t_{new}$).
- **Comparative Approaches:** For evaluation, two naive fusion methods were also examined:
 - **Averaging (AVG):** Averaging 1D features from each sensor.
 - **Concatenation (FC):** Concatenating 1D features, followed by a fully connected layer to reduce dimensionality.
- **Encoder Variants for Fusion Evaluation:**
 - **Image Features:** Standard encoder (WOB - without BEV mapping) vs. BEV mapping approaches (BEV - mapping features to BEV space).
 - **Lidar Features:** Range View (RV - cylindrical projection with ResNet backbone) vs. PointPillars (PP - segmentation into pillars, feature extraction, 2D BEV pseudo-image).

#### 3.4. Transition Model

- **Inputs:** Fused observation features ($o_{0:t}$) and encoded actions ($a_{0:t}$) (simple MLP).
- **State Representation:** Unlike common 1D vectors, stochastic hidden states ($s_t$) and deterministic historical states ($h_t$) are modeled as 2D latent states (shape $C_h \times (\sum_j T_j)$).
- **Deterministic State:** $h_{t+1} = f_\theta(h_t, s_t)$ modeled by a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) with convolutional layers replacing fully connected layers to handle 2D states.
- **Probabilistic States:**
 - Posterior hidden state distribution: $q(s_t | o_{\le t}, a_{<t})$ (sampled from $N_\phi(o_t, h_t, a_t)$).
 - Prior hidden state distribution (for prediction): $p(s_t | h_t, a_{t-1})$ (sampled from $N_\theta(h_t, a_{t-1})$).
 - $N_\phi$ and $N_\theta$ are probability models implemented as transformer decoders, querying state information.

#### 3.5. Multimodal Decoder

- **Inputs:** Latent dynamic state ($s_t, h_t$) from the transition model.
- **Outputs:** Raw camera images, lidar point clouds, and an optional head for 3D occupancy.
- **Decoding Process:** Tokens are divided by modality and reshaped.
 - Camera and lidar: Reshaped to $C \times H_0 \times W_0$, then upscaled using 2D convolutional networks.
 - 3D occupancy: Decoded into voxel grids ($C \times X \times Y \times Z$) using 3D convolutions.

#### 3.6. Training Setup

- **Self-Supervised Training:** No labels are required during training.
- **Losses:** Multi-scale losses computed at different resolutions (1, 2, 4).
 - **Image Loss ($L_{img}$):** L1 loss for RGB data.
 - **Point Cloud Loss ($L_{pcd}$):** L2 loss for Euler distance ($L_{p,xyz}$) and L1 loss for range ($L_{p,r}$) on range view images.
 - **3D Occupancy Loss ($L_{V,scal}$):** Scene-Class Affinity Loss (SCAL) for binary occupancy voxel grids.
 - **Total Loss:** $L = \sum_i (\lambda_{img}L_{img_i} + \lambda_{pcd}(L_{pxyz_i} + L_{pr_i}) + \lambda_V L_{V,scal_i})$.
- **Dataset:** Collected in CARLA simulation environment (Town01, Town03, Town04, Town06 across various weather conditions) using an expert reinforcement learning agent. Total 300,000 frames at 10 FPS.
- **Validation Datasets:**
 - **$D_{RL_{val}}$:** Same cities/weather as training, randomized routes, to evaluate representation learning.
 - **$D_{DS_{val}}$:** Same cities, *different* weather conditions, randomized routes, to evaluate performance under domain shifts.
- **Training Parameters:** Sequences of length 12 (6 for voxel experiments), 0.2s intervals, batch size 16, AdamW optimizer (learning rate 10$^{-4}$, weight decay 0.01).
- **Backbone:** Pre-trained ResNet18 as baseline.

#### 3.7. Evaluation Metrics

- **Image Predictions:** Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR).
- **Point Cloud Predictions:** Chamfer Distance.
- **3D Occupancy Predictions:** Intersection over Union ($IoU^+$ for occupied, $IoU^-$ for empty), Precision, and Recall.

#### 3.8. Experimental Design for Evaluation

- **Sensor Fusion Strategies:** Comparison of 8 encoder-fusion combinations (e.g., RV-WOB-TR, PP-BEV-FC) using the defined metrics on both validation datasets.
- **Latent Space Analysis:** Comparison of 1D vs. 2D latent spaces, with and without perceptual loss, and using ResNet18 vs. MobileVit-V2 (Vision Transformer) backbones.
- **3D Occupancy Prediction Effects:**
 - **Pre-training Effects:** Experiments with pre-trained weights (PTF - frozen, PTO - open) vs. no pre-training (NPT) for 3D occupancy.
 - **Impact on Sensor Predictions:** Assessing if including occupancy prediction improves camera and lidar prediction quality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding sensor fusion strategies, latent space configurations, and the integration of 3D occupancy prediction in a multimodal world model.

#### 4.1. Latent Space Configuration

- **2D Latent Space:** Modeling the latent space as two-dimensional (2D) demonstrated a significant benefit for camera image predictions and spatial voxel occupancy predictions compared to a 1D latent space.
- **Lidar Predictions:** Lidar predictions showed no significant benefit from a 2D latent space.
- **Perceptual Loss:** The inclusion of an additional perceptual loss term did not show strong effects and, in some cases, resulted in visually poorer reconstructions without significant advantages.
- **Vision Transformer Backbone:** Utilizing a Vision Transformer (MobileVit-V2) as an encoder provided advantages for camera image prediction but had no notable effect on other metrics.
- **Conclusion:** The 2D latent space itself provided the largest performance boost, with other modifications having comparatively minor effects.

#### 4.2. Sensor Fusion Strategies

- **General Performance Trends:** A drop in performance was observed for all networks on the $D_{DS_{val}}$ dataset (domain shift) compared to $D_{RL_{val}}$ (familiar environments) for camera predictions, while relative network performance remained consistent. For point cloud predictions, there was no significant performance disparity between the two validation datasets.
- **Transformer-Based Fusion:** The transformer-based architecture (TR) generally performed on par with or better than naive fusion methods (AVG, FC).
 - **Image Prediction:** The RV-WOB-TR combination (Range View lidar, Without BEV camera, Transformer fusion) consistently performed on par or better for camera image predictions. The effectiveness of the transformer was observed to depend on the encoder used.
 - **Point Cloud Prediction:** For point clouds, the RV-WOB-TR approach performed on par or better on $D_{RL_{val}}$ but experienced a drop in performance on $D_{DS_{val}}$. Overall, transformer-based architectures generally outperformed other fusion techniques for point cloud predictions.
- **Lidar Encoder Representations:** Range view-based lidar encodings (RV) consistently demonstrated clear advantages over PointPillars (PP) for both camera and point cloud predictions. Lossless range-view representations were determined to be an optimal strategy.
- **Image Encoder Representations:** Methods incorporating an additional BEV mapping for image features performed worse for camera predictions, and combinations with PointPillars suffered particularly. This supports the hypothesis that BEV features act as a bottleneck by lacking height information.
- **Optimal Fusion Strategy:** A transformer-based architecture with a 2D latent space and lossless range-view representations for point clouds was identified as an optimal fusion strategy, with performance benefits being more pronounced for camera predictions.

#### 4.3. 3D Occupancy Prediction

- **Benefits of Pre-training for 3D Occupancy:**
 - **From Scratch (NPT):** The model trained from scratch showed similar performance across both validation datasets.
 - **Pre-trained Open (PTO):** The model using pre-trained camera-lidar weights with open updates ($PTO$) showed early advantages, suggesting valuable spatial knowledge from pre-training. In later stages, NPT surpassed PTO in Precision, while PTO maintained superiority in $IoU^-$ and Recall. This indicates that the non-pre-trained model might adopt a more conservative prediction strategy.
 - **Pre-trained Frozen (PTF):** Even with frozen pre-trained weights, training only the voxel decoder ($PTF$) led to performance improvements over time, demonstrating that the camera and lidar pre-trained weights already encode some, albeit limited, spatial information relevant to discrete world geometry.
 - **Overall:** Pre-training strategies using only camera and lidar data are generally recommended for 3D occupancy prediction, as they accelerate training and result in overall superior performance.
- **Impact of 3D Occupancy on Sensor Predictions:**
 - Including 3D occupancy prediction led to only slightly increased performance gains for both camera and lidar predictions.
 - The benefit was more pronounced for camera predictions under the $D_{RL_{val}}$ setting. This indicates that while occupancy data is actionable, its direct feedback to raw sensor prediction quality is modest within this model architecture.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the development of world models for autonomous driving by systematically evaluating critical architectural choices related to multimodal sensor fusion and geometric representations. The findings have several implications for the design and capabilities of future autonomous driving systems:

- **Overcoming BEV Bottlenecks:** The demonstration that fusion strategies *without* reliance on Bird’s-Eye-View (BEV) features, particularly those using lossless lidar representations and transformer-based fusion, can outperform BEV-centric approaches, addresses a key limitation in existing world models. This suggests a pathway towards models that retain full 3D spatial information, which is critical for robust environmental understanding and planning in complex driving scenarios.
- **Enhanced Multimodal Fusion:** The identification of a transformer-based architecture combined with a 2D latent space and range-view lidar representations as an effective fusion strategy provides concrete guidance for integrating diverse sensor data. This can lead to world models that leverage the strengths of both cameras (texture, semantics) and lidar (precise depth, robustness to lighting) more effectively, resulting in more accurate and comprehensive future predictions.
- **Actionable Geometric Representations:** The investigation into 3D occupancy prediction highlights its value as an actionable output for autonomous driving. While computationally intensive, the results indicate that pre-training on camera and lidar data can significantly boost the efficiency and performance of learning 3D occupancy. This suggests that future world models can be designed to intrinsically understand and predict the physical geometry of the environment, moving beyond raw sensor data to provide directly usable information for planning and control.
- **Improved Training Efficiency:** The finding that pre-training with camera and lidar data benefits 3D occupancy prediction implies that complex 3D reasoning can be bootstrapped from more readily available 2D sensor data. This could reduce the data requirements and computational cost associated with training models for full 3D scene understanding.
- **Foundational Insights for Future Research:** By isolating and evaluating the effects of different fusion strategies and 3D occupancy, this work provides foundational insights for developing more capable world models. It encourages further exploration into architectures that can seamlessly integrate multimodal inputs and produce rich, actionable 3D outputs, potentially leading to safer and more intelligent autonomous systems. The identified need for large-scale real-world data experiments and considerations for computational efficiency in more complex sensor setups also points to crucial directions for future research in this field.
