# Symbolic Learning Enables Self-Evolving Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Agent symbolic learning treats a language agent as a neural network whose learnable weights are its prompts, tools and pipeline structure. A prompt-based language loss is back-propagated node by node as textual gradients, and symbolic optimizers rewrite prompts, tools and pipeline jointly. It beats DSPy and prompt-optimization baselines on benchmarks, software development and creative writing, without needing ground truth.

## Problem

- Building language agents needs heavy manual engineering of prompts, tools and pipelines by human experts, so agent research is engineering-centric rather than learning from data.
- Prior optimizers such as DSPy, GPTSwarm, Agent-pro and AgentOptimizer tune isolated prompts, tools or nodes separately, which risks local optima and hurts whole-system performance.
- Search-based methods need numerically codable metrics, so they fail on open-ended real-world tasks like software development or creative writing, and they cannot add nodes or write new tools.

## Method

- Agent pipeline as ==computation graph==: nodes act as layers, prompts and tools as weights. A forward pass stores inputs, outputs, prompts and tool usage in a ==trajectory==, like an autograd graph.
- ==Language loss== and ==language gradients==: a prompt-based LLM loss (with or without ground truth) scores the trajectory, then ==textual gradients== are ==back-propagated== from the last node to the first.
- Three ==symbolic optimizers== (**PromptOptimizer**, **ToolOptimizer**, **PipelineOptimizer**) apply gradients to edit prompts, tools and pipeline nodes, with retry, rollback, learning-rate prompts and batched updates that mimic ==mini-batch SGD==.

## Results

- On MATH with GPT-3.5, accuracy reaches **38.8** versus **23.8** for the hand-built agent and **17.3** for DSPy. With GPT-4 it reaches **60.7** on MATH and **54.0** F1 on HotPotQA.
- Software development score averages **3.8** out of 4, against **2.4** for the Agents baseline and **1.6** for GPTs. The optimized agent scores **4** on four of five games.
- Creative writing GPT-4 score is **7.4** with GPT-4 and **6.9** with GPT-3.5 (baseline **4.2**), beating Tree-of-Thoughts at **6.8** with GPT-4 and **3.8** with GPT-3.5. Prompt-only optimizers sometimes degrade.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimize all components of an agent jointly by propagating credit through the pipeline, as in backprop; per-node optimization risks local optima, just as layer-wise training did.
> - Initialization matters: starting from the simplest agent and letting the optimizer build structure is stable, while over-engineered starting agents make optimization unstable, echoing neural network initialization.
> - Language-based loss needs no ground truth, so deployed agents can keep self-evolving. Gains are larger on complex real-world tasks than on accuracy benchmarks, so agent-learning evaluation should target such tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "language agents"—complex pipelines leveraging large language models (LLMs), prompting techniques, and tool usage—is a rapidly expanding frontier in the pursuit of artificial general intelligence (AGI). These agents have demonstrated impressive capabilities across various real-world tasks, effectively bridging connectionist AI (the LLM backbone) with symbolic AI (structured pipelines of prompts and tools).

However, a fundamental limitation acknowledged by the authors, and indeed a widespread challenge in the field, is that current language agent research is predominantly "model-centric" or "engineering-centric." This implies that significant progress hinges on substantial manual effort from human experts to engineer prompts, design tools, and construct intricate pipelines. Such manual intervention limits the robustness, versatility, and scalability of these agents, making it difficult to adapt them to new tasks or rapidly changing data distributions.

This paper positions itself as a critical step towards overcoming this limitation by advocating for a transition to "data-centric" learning. Prior efforts in automated agent optimization have focused on specific modules, such as prompt engineering (e.g., DSpy, GPTSwarm, Agent-pro, AgentOptimizer). While effective in isolated scenarios, these approaches often optimize individual components (like a single prompt or tool) in a piecemeal fashion. The authors draw a compelling analogy to early neural network training, where optimizing layers separately led to suboptimal performance compared to modern end-to-end back-propagation. They argue that optimizing isolated components in an agent system is similarly prone to local optima, hindering the overall system's performance.

"Symbolic Learning Enables Self-Evolving Agents" directly addresses this gap by proposing a systematic framework for *holistic* and *joint optimization* of all symbolic components within an agent system. By mimicking the principles of connectionist learning—specifically back-propagation and gradient descent—but applying them to natural language "weights," "loss," and "gradients," this work seeks to provide a principled, data-driven mechanism for language agents to autonomously learn and evolve. This moves beyond merely automating prompt generation or tool selection; it aims for an agent that can introspect, evaluate its own performance, and systematically refine its internal structure and logic, including its entire computational pipeline. This paradigm shift from manual engineering to autonomous self-optimization represents a significant advancement in the pursuit of truly intelligent and adaptable AI agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable a fundamental shift in language agent development: from an "engineering-centric" model, heavily reliant on human expertise, to a "data-centric" paradigm where agents can autonomously learn and evolve. This transition is identified as a crucial pathway towards achieving Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

The specific key objectives driving this work are:

1. **Introduce Agent Symbolic Learning:** To develop and formalize a systematic framework, termed "agent symbolic learning," that facilitates the self-optimization of language agents.
2. **Enable Autonomous Self-Optimization:** To empower language agents to optimize their own internal components—prompts, tools, and the architectural pipeline that integrates them—in a data-driven manner.
3. **Mimic Connectionist Learning in Symbolic Space:** To adapt the core principles of neural network training (back-propagation and gradient descent) to the symbolic domain of language agents, using natural language to represent "weights," "loss," and "gradients."
4. **Demonstrate Practical Effectiveness:** To provide proof-of-concept experimental evidence that the proposed framework can effectively enhance agent performance on both established LLM benchmarks and more complex, real-world agentic tasks.
5. **Facilitate Future Research and "Self-Evolving Agents":** To open-source the entire framework, including code and prompts, thereby encouraging further research and development of agents that can update themselves after deployment, leading to truly "self-evolving agents."

The primary motivation stems from the inherent limitations of the current, manually intensive approach to developing language agents. The sheer complexity of decomposing tasks, designing prompts, and integrating tools demands substantial manual engineering, which limits an agent's robustness, versatility, and adaptability to new scenarios or data. The authors strongly believe that for language agents to approach AGI, they must acquire the ability to autonomously learn from their experiences and continuously improve, much like biological intelligence. By drawing an analogy to the success of end-to-end learning in neural networks, they underscore the need for a holistic optimization scheme that transcends the local optima inherent in component-wise optimization, ultimately enabling agents to learn and adapt "in the wild."

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of Agent Symbolic Learning is to draw a direct analogy between a language agent system and a neural network, then re-implement the principles of connectionist learning (forward pass, loss computation, back-propagation, and gradient-based weight updates) using natural language.

**Key Analogies:**
- **Agent Pipeline (A):** Corresponds to the computational graph of a neural network, representing the sequence of nodes and their connections. These pipelines can be dynamic, adapting based on input.
- **Node (N):** An individual step in the agent pipeline, analogous to a layer in a neural network. Each node processes natural language input ($I_n$) using an LLM, guided by specific prompts ($P_n$) and equipped with tools ($T_n$), to produce natural language output ($O_n$).
- **Prompts ($P_n$) & Tools ($T_n$):** These are considered the "learnable weights" of a node, defining its behavior and capabilities.
- **Trajectory ($\tau$):** A record of the agent's execution during a "forward pass," storing all inputs, outputs, prompts, and tool usages at each node. This serves a similar purpose to the computational graph's intermediate activations and states in deep learning frameworks, enabling back-propagation.
- **Language Loss ($L_{lang}$):** Instead of a numerical scalar, the loss is a textual evaluation generated by an LLM. A carefully designed prompt template ($P_{loss}$) is fed the trajectory to holistically assess the agent's performance, providing both natural language comments and an optional numerical score. Crucially, this can be computed with or without ground truth, enabling both supervised and unsupervised "self-evolving" learning.
- **Language Gradient ($\nabla_{lang}$):** These are textual analyses and reflections on the symbolic components within each node, indicating how they contributed to the overall language loss. An LLM, guided by a prompt template ($P_{gradient}$), computes these gradients.

**The Agent Symbolic Learning Procedure (Algorithm 1):**

1. **Forward Pass:** The agent executes a given task. For each node in its pipeline, it records the input ($I_n$), generated output ($O_n$), the prompts ($P_n$) used, and any tool usages ($T_n$). All this information is accumulated in the trajectory ($\tau$).
2. **Language Loss Computation:** After the forward pass, the complete trajectory ($\tau$) is fed into an LLM using the $P_{loss}$ prompt template. The LLM then generates a natural language loss ($L_{lang}$), which includes evaluative comments and potentially a numerical score, reflecting the agent's overall performance against task requirements.
3. **Back-propagation of Language Gradients:** This step iterates through the nodes in the agent pipeline in reverse order (from last to first). For each node, an LLM (using the $P_{gradient}$ prompt) computes its language gradient ($\nabla_{n}^{lang}$). This computation considers the language gradient of the *subsequent* node (if any), the current node's execution details (input, output, prompts, tools from the trajectory), and the overall language loss. This ensures that each node's gradient reflects its contribution to the holistic system's performance, akin to how gradients in neural networks flow through the computational graph.
4. **Language Gradient-based Update:** Finally, the computed language gradients are used to update the agent's symbolic components. This is achieved via specialized "symbolic optimizers," which are themselves carefully designed prompt pipelines:
 - **PromptOptimizer:** Optimizes the prompts ($P_n$) within each node by suggesting modifications to components like task descriptions, few-shot examples, principles, and output formats, based on the language gradients.
 - **ToolOptimizer:** Decides whether to improve (edit descriptions), delete, or create new tools ($T_n$) based on the feedback in the language gradients. It then invokes specific prompts for each operation.
 - **PipelineOptimizer:** Modifies the agent pipeline structure ($A$) itself. Using an "agent programming language" (based on [Zhou et al., 2023b]), it instructs an LLM to analyze how the pipeline can be improved and then implement changes such as adding, deleting, or moving nodes.

**Robustness and Training Mechanisms:**
- **Error Handling:** Illegal updates (e.g., syntactically incorrect code from ToolOptimizer) are retried up to three times.
- **Rollback Strategy:** After an optimization step, the agent re-runs the current example. If the performance, as evaluated by the language loss function, degrades, the optimization is rolled back.
- **"Learning Rate":** A component in the optimizer prompts controls the aggressiveness of the LLM in suggesting modifications.
- **Batched Training:** Analogous to mini-batch stochastic gradient descent, individual forward passes, loss computations, and back-propagations are performed for a batch of examples. The collected language gradients for a specific node across the batch are then fed to the LLM to guide a more stable and holistic update.

The entire framework relies on large language models (specifically GPT-3.5 and GPT-4) as the computational engine for generating loss, gradients, and optimized components, effectively treating the LLM as a meta-optimizer operating in natural language space.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted a series of proof-of-concept experiments to evaluate the effectiveness of the Agent Symbolic Learning framework, covering both standard LLM benchmarks and complex agentic tasks.

**Results on Standard LLM Benchmarks (HotpotQA, MATH, HumanEval):**
The framework consistently demonstrated superior performance compared to several baselines, including a simple GPT-based approach, a generic language agent built with the Agents framework, an agent with automated prompt engineering (Agents w/ AutoPE), and the DSPy LLM pipeline optimization framework.
- **Consistent Improvements:** Across HotpotQA (multi-hop QA), MATH (competition-level mathematics), and HumanEval (code synthesis), the proposed method ("Ours") achieved higher scores than all compared baselines.
- **Significant Gain on MATH:** The most pronounced improvement was observed on the MATH dataset, indicating the framework's ability to enhance reasoning and problem-solving for complex, logic-intensive tasks.
- **Robustness:** Unlike "Agents w/ AutoPE" and "DSPy," which sometimes showed performance degradation in certain scenarios, Agent Symbolic Learning proved more stable and robust in optimizing the overall performance of language agents. This supports the hypothesis that holistic, gradient-based optimization is more effective than isolated component optimization or search-based approaches that struggle with non-numeric metrics.

**Results on Complex Agent Tasks (Creative Writing, Software Development):**
The framework showed even more significant advantages on open-ended, real-world tasks where metrics are harder to define numerically.
- **Creative Writing:** The "Ours" method achieved a GPT-4 score of 7.4 (with GPT-4 as the backbone), notably outperforming baselines like "GPTs" (6.0), "Agents" (6.0), "Agents w/ AutoPE" (6.5), and even the specialized "Tree-of-Thought" (ToT) (6.8) method. This indicates its ability to foster sophisticated planning, writing, and revision capabilities within the agent.
- **Software Development:** On tasks requiring the development of executable software from product requirement documents, the framework yielded an average score of 3.8, significantly higher than "GPTs" (1.6) and "Agents" (2.4). The scores range from 1 (execution failure) to 4 (flawless alignment with expectations). The authors noted that their approach successfully recovered standard operation procedures similar to those found in expert-designed frameworks like MetaGPT, highlighting its capacity for emergent, structured problem-solving.

**Case Study and Analysis:**
A case study on the creative writing task visually demonstrated the optimization process (Figure 3), showing how the framework could refine prompts (e.g., adding detailed instructions for narrative flow) and even restructure the agent pipeline (e.g., adding an "editor" agent and a "Check" node to an initial "Write" node). This exemplifies the framework's ability to emulate human expert development of agents.

**Key Observations from Analysis:**
- **Initialization Sensitivity:** The initial state of the agent system influences final performance. Simpler initializations, allowing the symbolic optimizers to drive evolution, tended to be more stable than over-engineered starting points. This suggests potential for "pre-training" agents on diverse tasks for robust initialization.
- **Superiority on Complex Tasks:** The framework's benefits were more pronounced and stable on complex, real-world tasks where success metrics are not easily quantifiable by equations. This underscores its potential for applications beyond traditional benchmarks and highlights the need for new benchmarks focused on complex agent learning evaluation.

In summary, the main findings strongly support the efficacy of Agent Symbolic Learning in enabling language agents to autonomously optimize their symbolic components and overall pipeline. The framework provides a robust and effective method for developing "self-evolving agents," particularly for challenging, open-ended tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Agent Symbolic Learning" framework represents a significant conceptual and practical advancement in the field of language agents, with profound implications for the future of artificial intelligence.

**1. Paradigm Shift Towards Data-Centric AI:** The most crucial impact is its potential to drive a transition from "model-centric" or "engineering-centric" agent development to a truly "data-centric" approach. This means moving away from extensive manual design and tuning by human experts towards systems that can autonomously learn, adapt, and improve from their own experiences and data. This shift is widely considered essential for advancing towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

**2. Enabling Self-Evolving Agents:** The framework's ability to learn from experience *without requiring ground-truth labels* for its language-based loss function means agents can continue to optimize themselves even after deployment in dynamic, real-world environments. This "self-evolving" capability allows for continuous improvement and adaptation, a hallmark of intelligent systems. Such agents would be more robust, versatile, and less reliant on continuous human intervention for maintenance and updates.

**3. Holistic System Optimization:** Unlike prior work that often focuses on optimizing isolated components (e.g., individual prompts or tools), Agent Symbolic Learning provides a systematic method for *jointly optimizing all symbolic components*—including prompts, tools, and the entire agent pipeline structure. By mimicking connectionist back-propagation, it enables a holistic optimization scheme that considers the interdependencies of components, thereby avoiding local optima and leading to genuinely improved system-level performance. This addresses a critical limitation of previous approaches.

**4. Principled Approach to Agent Learning:** Drawing direct analogies to well-understood connectionist learning procedures (like back-propagation and gradient descent) provides a principled and systematic methodology for agent optimization. This moves beyond ad-hoc prompt engineering or limited combinatorial search strategies, offering a more theoretically grounded pathway to building intelligent agents. It provides a formal "learning algorithm" for symbolic AI structures.

**5. Enhanced Performance on Complex Real-World Tasks:** The experimental results demonstrate significant performance gains, particularly on complex, open-ended tasks like creative writing and software development. These are tasks where traditional numerical metrics are challenging to define, and manual engineering is extremely labor-intensive. The framework's effectiveness in these domains highlights its potential to unlock new applications and push the boundaries of what AI agents can autonomously achieve.

**6. Open-Source Contribution:** The decision to open-source the entire framework, including code and prompts, is a critical contribution to the research community. It will accelerate further research and development in data-centric agent learning, allowing other researchers to build upon this foundation, test new hypotheses, and contribute to the evolution of self-optimizing AI systems.

**Potential Future Impact:**
- **Democratization of Agent Development:** By automating the optimization process, the framework could lower the barrier to entry for developing sophisticated AI agents, allowing more users to build and deploy capable systems without deep prompt engineering expertise.
- **More Adaptive and Robust AI:** Agents developed using this framework would be inherently more adaptive to changing environments, new tasks, and unforeseen challenges, leading to more resilient and trustworthy AI systems.
- **Foundation for Meta-Learning and AGI:** The concept of agents learning *how to learn* and optimize their own internal architectures is a crucial step towards meta-learning and could serve as a foundational building block for more general-purpose AI systems.

In conclusion, "Symbolic Learning Enables Self-Evolving Agents" offers a transformative approach to language agent development. By bridging the gap between connectionist and symbolic AI through a novel learning framework, it lays important groundwork for the creation of truly autonomous, adaptive, and self-improving AI agents, marking a meaningful stride towards AGI.
