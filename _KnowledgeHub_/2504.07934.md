# SoTA with Less: MCTS-Guided Sample Selection for Data-Efficient Visual Reasoning Self-Improvement

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces ThinkLite-VL, a family of visual reasoning models that achieve state-of-the-art performance using an order of magnitude fewer training samples by leveraging Monte Carlo Tree Search for data-efficient sample selection. The models achieve 75.1% on MathVista with only 11k samples (7B model) and 79.7% with 7.5k samples (72B model), demonstrating that data quality over quantity can drive VLM self-improvement.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) methods for vision-language models (VLMs) in reasoning tasks are often ineffective or rely on cumbersome pipelines involving knowledge distillation from larger models.
- Achieving strong VLM reasoning performance typically requires very large datasets (e.g., 60k to 260k samples), which are resource-intensive and computationally expensive.
- Reliably and scalably quantifying sample difficulty to identify high-quality, challenging training data for VLMs is non-trivial.

## Method

- The ==ThinkLite-VL== framework re-purposes ==Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)== as a mechanism to estimate the difficulty of visual reasoning tasks, defined by the number of MCTS iterations a VLM requires to solve them.
- A small, high-quality training dataset is created by filtering **70,000** open-source samples to select those requiring more than **5** MCTS iterations or remaining unsolved after **50** iterations.
- The curated, small subsets of data are used for ==Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT)== with the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) loss function on base Qwen2.5-VL models, without prior supervised fine-tuning or knowledge distillation.

## Results

- ThinkLite-VL-7B achieved a new state-of-the-art score of **75.1%** on MathVista and outperformed existing 7B-level models across **six** of eight visual reasoning benchmarks.
- ThinkLite-VL-72B established new state-of-the-art with **79.7%** on MathVista and **64.3%** on MathVerse, outperforming all existing open-source VLMs across six benchmarks.
- The models achieved these results using significantly fewer training samples (**11k** for 7B, **7.5k** for 72B), an order of magnitude less than prior models, without relying on knowledge distillation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sample difficulty, specifically measured by MCTS iterations, critically influences the effectiveness of reinforcement fine-tuning for visual reasoning.
> - A carefully curated mix of 'unsolved' (most difficult) and 'medium-difficulty' samples, requiring more than 5 MCTS iterations, is optimal for driving VLM self-improvement.
> - Optimal challenging samples are model-specific; training a model with data curated for a different-sized model significantly reduces performance gains, highlighting the need for tailored data curation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language models (LLMs) and, more recently, vision-language models (VLMs) has seen rapid advancements, particularly in complex reasoning tasks. A key trend in LLMs has been the use of reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) to enhance reasoning capabilities, sometimes even without initial supervised fine-tuning (SFT). However, applying RFT effectively to VLMs has been challenging due to the inherent "modality gap"—VLMs are often pre-trained on text-heavy objectives but need to perform multimodal reasoning post-training.

Previous attempts to improve VLM reasoning via RFT often relied on cumbersome pipelines involving knowledge distillation from larger, often proprietary, models or extensive supervised format alignment. These methods are data-intensive and computationally expensive, limiting the models' ability to truly "self-improve." This paper directly addresses these limitations, proposing a method that enables significant VLM self-improvement *without* knowledge distillation or large-scale supervised pre-training data, relying solely on RFT with a carefully curated, small dataset.

The broader landscape includes:
- **LLM Reasoning:** Methods like Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Tree of Thoughts, Graph of Thoughts, process reward models (PRMs), and search techniques (MCTS, beam search) for enhancing reasoning. This paper uniquely repurposes MCTS, typically an inference-time search algorithm, for *training data selection*.
- **VLM Reasoning:** Adapting CoT to VLMs, creating multimodal datasets for SFT/knowledge distillation, and more recently, applying RL training to VLMs. This paper contrasts sharply with many VLM RFT works (e.g., Vision-R1, OpenVLThinker) that depend on knowledge distillation and massive datasets (hundreds of thousands of samples).
- **Data Filtration:** A critical area in training large models, aiming to identify high-quality, diverse, and relevant data to optimize efficiency and generalization. This work extends data filtration to the RFT phase of VLMs, specifically for reasoning tasks. It differentiates itself from concurrent works like MM-Eureka by using a more fine-grained, search-based difficulty metric (MCTS iterations) rather than simple zero-shot accuracy, allowing it to identify "unsolved but informative" samples.

In essence, this work represents a significant step towards more data-efficient and genuinely self-improving VLMs for complex reasoning, by demonstrating that *quality* of training data (specifically, appropriate challenge) can outweigh *quantity*, and that sophisticated search algorithms can be leveraged for data curation, not just inference. It challenges the conventional wisdom that VLMs need massive, distilled datasets to achieve strong reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop a data-efficient self-improvement method for visual reasoning in VLMs** that achieves state-of-the-art (SoTA) performance using significantly fewer training samples, and crucially, **without relying on knowledge distillation or extensive supervised fine-tuning.**

The core motivation stems from several observations and challenges:
- **Limited RFT Effectiveness in VLMs:** While RFT has proven highly effective for LLMs in reasoning tasks, its impact on VLMs has been less pronounced due to the inherent modality gap between pre-training objectives and multimodal reasoning tasks. Existing solutions often involve complex, multi-stage pipelines with knowledge distillation, which is cumbersome and resource-intensive.
- **The Data Quantity vs. Quality Dilemma:** Current VLM reasoning models often require very large, often distilled, datasets (e.g., 60k to 260k samples). This raises questions about the efficiency of training and the necessity of such vast data. The authors hypothesize that not all data points contribute equally to reasoning improvement.
- **The Importance of Sample Difficulty:** The central insight driving this work is that **sample difficulty critically influences RFT effectiveness**. Appropriately challenging examples can drive substantial reasoning improvements, even in low-data regimes. However, reliably and scalably quantifying sample difficulty is non-trivial.

Therefore, the paper aims to:
1. Prove that high-quality, appropriately challenging training samples are sufficient for VLM self-improvement via RFT, eliminating the need for knowledge distillation.
2. Develop a novel, scalable method to identify these "appropriately challenging" samples.
3. Demonstrate that models trained on such curated, small datasets can achieve competitive, even SoTA, performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **ThinkLite-VL**, is a data-efficient visual reasoning system that achieves self-improvement through RFT on a small, difficulty-curated dataset. The methodology consists of three main phases: Data Collection, MCTS-based Sample Selection, and Visual Reasoning Training.

#### 3.1. Data Collection

The initial pool of training data comprises 70,000 open-source samples spanning three core visual reasoning domains:
- **Mathematical Reasoning:** Geometry3K, GeoQA, Geos.
- **Natural Image Understanding:** FigureQA, ScienceQA, OK-VQA.
- **Chart Understanding:** IconQA, TabMWP.
A crucial step taken was to reformulate several multiple-choice datasets (IconQA, FigureQA, Geometry3K, TabMWP, OK-VQA) into an **open-ended format**. This forces the VLM to derive answers through reasoning rather than mere selection, thereby enhancing the reliability of difficulty assessment and improving training effectiveness.

#### 3.2. MCTS-based Sample Selection (Core Contribution)

This is the innovative heart of ThinkLite-VL. The authors repurpose **Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)**, typically an inference-time search algorithm, to estimate the difficulty of each training instance.
- **Difficulty Definition:** Difficulty is defined as the number of MCTS reasoning iterations a VLM requires to solve a task. A higher number of iterations indicates a greater challenge for the model.
- **MCTS Process:** Using the base VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct or Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct) as the policy model ($\pi_\theta$), MCTS simulates reasoning traces for each of the 70k samples. The process involves:
 - **Selection:** At each node (representing a reasoning step prefix), the next step is selected based on a UCB1-like formula that balances exploration and exploitation, influenced by visitation frequency ($N(s_t)$). Notably, it prioritizes computational efficiency and comprehensive exploration, not employing separate process reward models.
 - **Expansion:** From the current step, the VLM generates $k=3$ distinct candidate actions (next reasoning steps) using temperature decoding (temperature = 0.5).
 - **Simulation:** For each selected node, the policy model generates several reasoning steps until a final answer is produced or a preset step limit (50 iterations) is reached.
 - **Evaluation (Self-Rewarding):** A separate critic model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) compares the generated final answer with the ground truth to determine correctness. If correct, the MCTS process terminates, and the number of iterations (K) is recorded. If incorrect, the visit count of the node is updated, and the next iteration begins.
- **Data Filtration:** After applying MCTS to all 70k samples, the dataset is filtered based on the recorded 'K' values. The final training sets are selected as:
 - All samples with K > 5 (i.e., those requiring more than 5 iterations to solve).
 - All samples that remained unsolved after 50 iterations.
This procedure yielded highly curated, small subsets: 11k samples for the 7B model and 7.5k samples for the 72B model.

#### 3.3. Visual Reasoning Training

The selected high-quality samples are then used for **Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT)**.
- **No Knowledge Distillation/SFT:** A key aspect is that this RFT is applied directly to the base Qwen2.5-VL models, without any prior supervised fine-tuning or knowledge distillation from larger models.
- **Loss Function:** The training uses the **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** loss function, which is designed to optimize policy directly. The specific training prompt template includes instructions for "thinking" step-by-step as an internal monologue, followed by the final answer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields impressive empirical results, demonstrating the efficacy of MCTS-guided sample selection for data-efficient VLM self-improvement.

- **State-of-the-Art (SoTA) Performance:**
 - **ThinkLite-VL-7B:** Achieves a new SoTA score of 75.1% on MathVista, surpassing much larger models including GPT-4o, O1, and Qwen2.5-VL-72B. It consistently outperforms all existing 7B-level models across eight visual reasoning benchmarks, leading in six out of eight.
 - **ThinkLite-VL-72B:** Further advances the SoTA frontier, achieving 79.7% on MathVista and 64.3% on MathVerse, establishing new SoTA on both. It outperforms all existing open-source VLMs across six benchmarks.
- **Significant Performance Improvement over Base Models:**
 - ThinkLite-VL-7B improves the average performance of Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct from 59.69% to 64.18% (a 4.49% absolute increase, or ~7.5% relative).
 - ThinkLite-VL-72B increases the average accuracy of Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct from 68.25% to 72.67% (a 4.42% absolute increase, or ~6.5% relative).
- **Data Efficiency and Elimination of Knowledge Distillation:**
 - ThinkLite-VL-7B achieves its results using only 11k training samples, and ThinkLite-VL-72B with 7.5k samples. This is an *order of magnitude fewer* samples than prior reasoning models (which often use 60k-260k samples) and importantly, without any knowledge distillation from larger, external models.
 - The model outperforms MM-EUREKA-Qwen-7B, which also avoids SFT knowledge distillation but uses more data (15k), emphasizing the quality of MCTS-filtered data.
- **Effectiveness of MCTS-based Sample Selection:**
 - **Superior to Random Selection:** ThinkLite-VL-7B and ThinkLite-VL-72B clearly outperform baselines trained on an equal number of randomly selected samples (ThinkLite-VL-Random11k and ThinkLite-VL-Random7.5k), demonstrating average performance improvements of 3.29% and 3.06% respectively.
 - **Optimal Difficulty Mix:** Ablation studies reveal that combining "unsolved" (most difficult) samples and "Iter5Only" (medium-difficulty, solvable with >5 iterations) samples yields the best performance. Neither subset alone is sufficient. This validates the hypothesis that an "appropriately challenging" mix is crucial.
 - **Superior to Self-Consistency Filtering:** MCTS-based filtering consistently outperforms both offline and online self-consistency-based data selection strategies. This is attributed to MCTS's explicit tree search providing a more expressive and fine-grained estimate of difficulty, identifying informative cases beyond simple solved/unsolved statuses.
- **Model-Specific Difficulty:** Analysis shows that the optimal difficult samples are model-specific. Training a 72B model on samples curated for a 7B model, and vice-versa, leads to significantly reduced performance gains compared to training on a model's *own* curated set. This suggests that "one-size-fits-all" difficulty sets are ineffective and emphasizes the need for model-tailored data curation.
- **Training Reward vs. Benchmark Performance:** The study notes that models trained on full or randomly selected datasets achieve higher training rewards but lower actual benchmark performance. This highlights that simply maximizing reward on easy samples doesn't improve true reasoning ability; challenging, "unsolved" samples, despite lower immediate rewards, are critical for deeper learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings of this paper carry significant implications for the future development and training of large multimodal models, particularly in the domain of complex reasoning.

- **Paradigm Shift in VLM Training:** ThinkLite-VL challenges the prevailing notion that achieving SoTA reasoning in VLMs necessitates either massive, costly knowledge distillation or extremely large datasets. It demonstrates that a carefully curated, smaller set of challenging examples, combined with effective RFT, can be profoundly more efficient and effective. This could lead to a shift in VLM training pipelines, prioritizing intelligent data curation over sheer data volume.
- **Enhanced Data Efficiency:** The ability to achieve SoTA with an order of magnitude fewer samples (11k/7.5k vs. 60k-260k) translates directly into substantial cost savings (compute, data storage, labeling) and faster iteration cycles for VLM development. This democratizes access to high-performance VLM training, making it feasible for researchers and organizations with more limited resources.
- **True Self-Improvement:** By eliminating reliance on external knowledge distillation, ThinkLite-VL moves closer to genuine "self-improvement" in VLMs. The model learns from its own iterative reasoning process to identify and tackle its weaknesses, leading to more robust and autonomously developed reasoning capabilities.
- **Novel Application of MCTS:** Repurposing MCTS from an inference-time search algorithm to a powerful data selection mechanism for training is a novel contribution. This opens up new avenues for using sophisticated search techniques in data curation for various machine learning tasks beyond reasoning, and potentially across different modalities.
- **Deeper Understanding of Learning Signals:** The paper's insight into "difficulty as a learning signal" and the empirical validation that a mix of medium-to-hard samples is optimal for RFT provides valuable theoretical and practical guidance for curriculum learning and reinforcement learning in complex domains. The observation that "unsolved" challenging samples lead to stronger models, despite lower immediate training rewards, reinforces the importance of tackling hard problems for true intellectual growth in AI.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The release of the full ThinkLite-VL model family and the MCTS-filtered training sets for both 7B and 72B models is a crucial contribution to the open-source community. This enables reproducibility, fosters further research, and allows other researchers to build upon these data-efficient self-improvement techniques.

In conclusion, "SoTA with Less" presents a compelling case for data quality over quantity in VLM reasoning, offering a scalable, efficient, and highly effective methodology that pushes the boundaries of multimodal AI self-improvement.
