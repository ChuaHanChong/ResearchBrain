# MotionCraft: Physics-based Zero-Shot Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Politecnico di Torino developed MOTIONCRAFT, a zero-shot video generation method that animates static images with physically plausible motion by warping the latent space of a pre-trained image diffusion model using optical flows derived from physics simulations. The system generates temporally coherent videos, achieving an average Motion Consistency of 0.8655, which is notably higher than the baseline's 0.6740.

## Problem

- Existing video diffusion models require extensive training data and computational resources, limiting accessibility and flexibility.
- Current text-to-video models struggle with fine-grained control over specific, physically accurate motion dynamics from simple text prompts.
- Zero-shot video generation methods often face challenges in achieving both temporal consistency and synthesizing novel, coherent content within dynamic scenes.

## Method

- Utilizes a pre-trained image diffusion model (==Stable Diffusion==) in a zero-shot, ==autoregressive== pipeline for frame-by-frame video generation.
- Generates ==optical flow== from physics simulators (e.g., fluid dynamics, rigid body motion, ==multi-agent== systems) and applies it to warp the noise latent space of the diffusion model during the reverse DDIM sampling process.
- Integrates Multiple Cross-Frame Attention (MCFA) for enhanced long-range and short-range temporal consistency and Spatial Noise Map Weighting (Spatial-η) to selectively enable stochastic sampling for new content generation.

## Results

- Achieved an average Motion Consistency of **0.8655** (± **0.12**), significantly outperforming the Text2Video-Zero baseline (**0.6740** ± **0.23**), indicating better preservation of content details under motion.
- Qualitatively generated realistic and temporally coherent videos with prescribed fluid dynamics, rigid body motion, and multi-agent behaviors, successfully synthesizing new, plausible scene elements (e.g., new continents) without explicit prompting.
- Ablation studies confirmed that Multiple Cross-Frame Attention (MCFA) and Spatial Noise Map Weighting (Spatial-η) are essential for maintaining temporal consistency and accurately creating novel content, respectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The optical flow in pixel space shows a strong correlation with flow in the noise latent space of image diffusion models, enabling effective motion application within this abstract representation.
> - Decoupling content generation from motion dynamics by leveraging physics simulators for motion and a powerful image prior for content synthesis leads to more controllable and plausible video outputs.
> - Careful management of attention mechanisms (MCFA) and stochastic noise sampling (Spatial-η) within the latent space is critical for maintaining consistency and generating new, coherent content in zero-shot video generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of realistic and temporally coherent videos remains a significant challenge within computer vision. Recent advancements in generative models, particularly Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) and Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs), have achieved compelling results in static image generation, producing images almost indistinguishable from real photographs. The natural progression of this research area is toward video generation, which introduces the additional complexity of temporal consistency and motion dynamics.

Existing approaches to video generation often fall into several categories. Large-scale models such as Sora, Video Diffusion Models, Imagen-Video, and Align your Latents demonstrate high temporal consistency and photorealism. However, these models typically require extensive computational resources and vast amounts of paired text-video data for training, making them computationally intensive and potentially limited by their training datasets. Other works explore few-shot or unsupervised learning to reduce data requirements, such as Make-a-Video, which uses unsupervised training with videos and a retrieval strategy, or latent flow diffusion models that train an optical flow generator.

A key limitation of many current video generation models, particularly those driven solely by text prompts, is the difficulty in achieving fine-grained control over specific motion dynamics, especially those governed by physical laws. Synthesizing a video according to precise physical behaviors (e.g., fluid dynamics, rigid body motion) is often not possible with a simple text prompt. This is where the presented work, MOTIONCRAFT, positions itself.

MOTIONCRAFT addresses this gap by focusing on "zero-shot" video generation. This means it operates without requiring any additional training or finetuning of the core generative model and does not rely on reference videos or pre-trained optical flow estimators. The primary zero-shot video generators in the existing landscape include Text2Video-Zero (T2V0) and Generative Rendering. T2V0, a baseline for comparison in this paper, applies a constant shift to the initial latent noise of a pre-trained image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion). Generative Rendering, a concurrent work, uses depth-conditioned ControlNet and manually animated 3D meshes, which limits its ability to render complex phenomena like fluids.

Furthermore, the field of diffusion-based video and image *editing* has seen methods that exploit the prior of text-to-image models. Examples include Tune-A-Video (finetuning for video editing), Pix2Video (self-attention injection for video editing), Motion Guidance (user-defined optical flow for image editing), and LatentWarp/TokenFlow (using estimated optical flow from reference videos to warp latent space for consistent editing). These methods differ from MOTIONCRAFT in their focus on editing existing content rather than generating novel video sequences from scratch with prescribed physical motion.

MOTIONCRAFT distinguishes itself by decoupling temporal evolution (motion) from content generation. It proposes to explicitly inject the physics of the real world as motion dynamics, using physics simulators as the source of motion information (optical flows). This approach aims to create more parsimonious models that do not need to implicitly learn complex physical laws from data. By operating in the noise latent space of a pre-trained image diffusion model and employing a physics-based optical flow, MOTIONCRAFT seeks to achieve both realistic content generation and precise, user-controllable physical motion, without the computational burden of training large video-specific models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a zero-shot video generation model capable of producing realistic and physically plausible videos with fine-grained control over motion dynamics. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and sub-objectives:

1. **Address Limitations of Existing Video Diffusion Models:** Current state-of-the-art video diffusion models, while achieving high quality, are often limited by:
 - **Heavy Training and Computational Requirements:** Models like Sora require enormous computational resources and large datasets, making them inaccessible for many researchers and applications.
 - **Data Bias:** Videos generated by these models can be biased towards their training datasets, limiting the diversity and novelty of generated content.
 - **Lack of Fine-grained Control over Motion:** Simple text prompts are often insufficient to specify precise physical dynamics. If a user desires a video conforming to specific physical laws (e.g., fluid behavior, rigid body mechanics), current models struggle to deliver this level of control.

2. **Decouple Temporal Evolution from Content Generation:** The authors motivate that explicitly controlling motion dynamics allows for a separation of the temporal aspects from the content generation aspects. This can lead to more efficient and controllable models, as the system does not need to "brute-force" learn complex physical laws from data. Instead, it can leverage pre-existing knowledge from physics simulations.

3. **Develop a Zero-Shot Approach:** A core objective is to achieve video generation without the need for extensive training or finetuning. This minimizes computational cost and data requirements, making the method more accessible and adaptable. The authors aim to utilize only a pre-trained still image generator (specifically, Stable Diffusion) and knowledge of physical laws.

4. **Incorporate Physical Laws for Motion Control:** The paper advocates for physics simulators as a suitable source for generating motion. These simulators provide user-controllable, plausible, and explainable motion dynamics in the form of optical flows. The objective is to devise a method that can effectively incorporate these physical laws into the generative process to animate a starting image.

5. **Leverage Latent Space Warping:** A specific technical objective is to demonstrate that warping the *noise latent space* of an image diffusion model with an optical flow derived from physics simulations can lead to coherent application of desired motion. This approach is posited to overcome limitations of applying flow directly in pixel space, which would struggle to synthesize novel, consistent elements needed to adapt to complex motion, potentially leading to artifacts or missing content. The hypothesis is that the latent space, being a more abstract representation, allows the powerful image prior of the diffusion model to "fill in" missing information consistent with the scene's evolution.

In summary, the motivation stems from the desire for a more controllable, efficient, and physically plausible video generation method that can animate images with complex dynamics prescribed by physical laws, without the high training overhead of large, end-to-end video diffusion models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MOTIONCRAFT is presented as a physics-based zero-shot video generator that leverages a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model, specifically Stable Diffusion (SD), to create videos. The methodology is characterized by its reliance on optical flow derived from physical simulations to warp the latent space of the diffusion model, coupled with specialized attention and noise sampling mechanisms.

#### 3.1. Core Principle: Optical Flow in Latent Space

The foundation of MOTIONCRAFT is a key observation: the optical flow estimated between two frames in pixel space exhibits a strong correlation with the flow estimated between their corresponding noise latent representations in SD. The authors empirically support this by computing the average cosine similarity between RGB and latent space optical flows on the MSU Video Frame Interpolation Benchmark dataset, reporting a value of 0.727. This correlation suggests that applying motion transformations in the latent space can effectively translate to desired motion in the generated pixel space, while potentially allowing the diffusion model to synthesize new content.

#### 3.2. Physics-based Optical Flow Generation

Instead of learning motion from data, MOTIONCRAFT sources optical flow from physics simulations. Various libraries are employed to simulate different physical phenomena:
- **Fluid Dynamics:** Using the Φ-flow library to numerically solve Navier-Stokes equations, allowing for simulations of smoke, fire, or melting effects. The velocity field from these simulations serves as the optical flow.
- **Rigid Body Motion:** Simulating the movement of rigid objects, such as a satellite orbiting or Earth rotating.
- **Multi-agent Systems:** Implementing models like the Boids model (using the agentpy library) to simulate flocking behavior.

These simulations provide user-controllable, plausible, and explainable motion descriptions as a sequence of optical flows ($W_{f-1 \to f}$).

#### 3.3. Autoregressive Video Generation Pipeline

MOTIONCRAFT generates videos autoregressively, frame by frame, starting from an initial image ($I_0$) which can be real or generated. The process for generating frame $I_f$ from $I_0$ and $I_{f-1}$ is as follows (illustrated in Algorithm 1):

1. **DDIM Inversion:**
 - The previous frame $I_{f-1}$ (or $I_0$ for the first iteration) is encoded into its latent representation $z_{f-1,0}$ using the VAE encoder $E$ of Stable Diffusion.
 - A reverse DDIM sampling scheme (DDIM inversion) is applied to $z_{f-1,0}$ for a fixed number of timesteps $\tau$ (e.g., 400). This process, without Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), progressively adds noise to $z_{f-1,0}$ to obtain its noisy latent representation $z_{f-1,\tau}$. This step essentially finds the noise vector that, when denoised, would produce $I_{f-1}$.

2. **Latent Warping:**
 - The optical flow warping operator $W_{f-1 \to f}$, derived from the physics simulation, is applied to the noisy latent $z_{f-1,\tau}$. This operation, $\zeta_{f,\tau} = W_{f-1 \to f}(z_{f-1,\tau})$, warps the latent representation according to the prescribed motion, effectively moving elements in the latent space.

3. **Frame Generation (Reverse Diffusion):**
 - From the warped noisy latent $\zeta_{f,\tau}$, $\tau$ steps of reverse diffusion (DDIM sampling) are performed to generate the next frame $I_f$. This involves progressively denoising $\zeta_{f,\tau}$ back to the image latent space $z_{f,0}$, which is then decoded by the VAE decoder $D$ to produce the final RGB image $I_f$.
 - During this generation phase, Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) is employed to enhance image quality, utilizing both positive ($P$) and negative ($P_\emptyset$) prompts.

#### 3.4. Novel Mechanisms for Consistency and Content Generation

Two key mechanisms are introduced to maintain temporal consistency and facilitate novel content generation:

1. **Multiple Cross-Frame Attention (MCFA):**
 - This technique generalizes the standard Cross-Frame Attention (CFA) by allowing the currently generated frame to attend to an arbitrary number of reference frames. In MOTIONCRAFT, the model attends to both the *first frame* ($I_0$) and the *previous frame* ($I_{f-1}$).
 - MCFA replaces the keys and values, which are typically computed from the generating frame's features in the UNet's self-attention blocks, with those computed from the attended frames. This ensures long-range consistency with the initial scene (via $I_0$) and short-range consistency with the immediate past (via $I_{f-1}$), helping to maintain global scene elements and local color/texture coherence.

2. **Spatial Noise Map Weighting (Spatial-η):**
 - This technique enables a pixel-by-pixel choice between deterministic DDIM sampling ($\eta=0$) and stochastic DDPM sampling ($\eta=1$) during the reverse diffusion process.
 - The spatial map $\eta_f$ is derived from the physical simulation. It is set to 1 in regions where new content needs to be created (e.g., where the optical flow points out of bounds, indicating a new part of the scene entering, or where the flow has discontinuities). In other regions, where content is merely moving, $\eta_f$ is set to 0 to maintain consistency and determinism. This allows the model to "fill in" novel plausible content in required areas while preserving the integrity of existing, moving elements.

This combination of physics-driven latent warping and specialized attention/sampling strategies allows MOTIONCRAFT to generate videos with complex, prescribed motion while maintaining visual coherence and synthesizing new content where necessary.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MOTIONCRAFT involved both quantitative metrics and qualitative comparisons against the state-of-the-art zero-shot video generator, Text2Video-Zero (T2V0).

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

The paper introduces a novel "Motion Consistency" metric alongside the standard "Frame Consistency" metric.
- **Frame Consistency:** Measures the average cosine similarity of CLIP embeddings between consecutive frames, indicating high-level semantic coherence.
- **Motion Consistency:** Designed to overcome limitations of Frame Consistency by assessing how similar two frames are *after accounting for motion*. It estimates optical flow between consecutive frames using RAFT, reverses the motion on the second frame, and then computes the SSIM metric between the first frame and the motion-registered second frame. This metric specifically evaluates consistency of textures and low-level details under motion.

Across a diverse set of physics simulations (Fluid Dragons, Melting Man, Satellite Scan, Revolving Earth, Birds), MOTIONCRAFT demonstrated quantitative improvements (Table 1):
- **Average Frame Consistency:** MOTIONCRAFT achieved an average of 0.9819 (± 0.02) compared to T2V0's 0.9658 (± 0.01). While both methods show high frame consistency, MOTIONCRAFT exhibits a slight edge.
- **Average Motion Consistency:** MOTIONCRAFT significantly outperformed T2V0, with an average of 0.8655 (± 0.12) compared to T2V0's 0.6740 (± 0.23). The higher standard deviation for T2V0 indicates greater variability in its ability to maintain motion consistency.
 - Notable improvements were observed in scenarios requiring complex content generation or precise motion, such as "Fluid Dragons" (0.9637 for MotionCraft vs. 0.6846 for T2V0) and "Rigid Body Satellite Scan" (0.9219 for MotionCraft vs. 0.2852 for T2V0). This indicates MOTIONCRAFT's effectiveness in preserving content details while implementing prescribed motion.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

The qualitative results are presented through various scenarios demonstrating different physical dynamics:

- **Rigid Body Flows (Satellite Scan, Revolving Earth):**
 - **Satellite Scan (Fig. 4):** MOTIONCRAFT coherently scrolls down an initial satellite image of a city, generating plausible new city portions from the top while maintaining the consistency of existing structures like a river. T2V0 struggled with temporal consistency, even for large elements.
 - **Revolving Earth (Fig. 5):** When simulating Earth's rotation, MOTIONCRAFT autonomously generated new continents in correct positions as they appeared from the side, even without specific text prompt references. T2V0 failed to consistently rotate the Earth and create new, coherent content.

- **Fluid Dynamics (Melting Man, Dragon Fire, Pouring Drink, Evaporating Man):**
 - **Dragon Fire (Fig. 6):** MOTIONCRAFT produced a consistent scene with realistic fire breath animations, including coherent global scene illumination changes and realistic occlusions due to smoke. T2V0 showed increasing artifacts, color shifts, and inconsistent object appearances (e.g., a dragon changing or disappearing).
 - **Melting Man (Fig. 1):** The generated video realistically depicted a melting statue, including phenomena like parts bouncing on the ground before the fluid settled.
 - **Pouring Drink (Fig. 18 in Appendix C):** Comparing MOTIONCRAFT to applying flow in pixel space, the latent space warping prevented artifacts like deformations and pixel stretching, resulting in a more realistic animation of liquid pouring.

- **Multi-agent Systems (Bird Flock, Fig. 7):**
 - MOTIONCRAFT generated realistic flocking motion for birds, even when starting from an image with a single bird and cloning its latent patch for multiple agents. T2V0 exhibited inconsistent numbers of birds and lacked coherent flock motion.

- **Additional Results (Fig. 10):**
 - Demonstrated generation of videos like a tree growing with coherent shadow evolution, indicating the model's ability to handle subtle, non-explicitly flowed changes.
 - Showcased generalizability by applying MOTIONCRAFT to SDXL, producing high-resolution videos with enhanced detail.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirmed the importance of key components:
- **Multiple Cross-Frame Attention (MCFA) (Fig. 8, Appendix B.1):** Attending to both the initial and previous frames was found to be optimal. Without MCFA, frames were implausible. Attending only to the first frame reduced overall motion (e.g., perpetually showing Africa in the Earth example), while attending only to the previous frame led to reduced color consistency over longer sequences.
- **Spatial Noise Map Weighting (Spatial-η) (Fig. 9, Appendix B.2):** This mechanism was crucial for generating novel plausible content. By enabling DDPM sampling in regions where flow was undefined or discontinuous, it allowed the model to correctly synthesize new scene elements. Without Spatial-η (i.e., $\eta=0$ everywhere), the model struggled to generate new content, leading to corrupted videos.
- **Partial Inversion Process (Appendix B.3):** The DDIM inversion of previous frames was essential for bringing textures and details into subsequent frames. Without it, generated videos suffered from corruption.

In summary, the findings indicate that MOTIONCRAFT successfully generates realistic and temporally consistent videos with prescribed physical motions, outperforming baseline zero-shot methods, particularly in maintaining motion-aware content consistency. The unique architecture, leveraging latent space warping, MCFA, and Spatial-η, appears critical for these results.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MOTIONCRAFT presents a notable advancement in the field of video generation, particularly for scenarios requiring explicit control over physical motion. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Enhanced Control and Plausibility:** The most significant contribution is the ability to generate videos with finely prescribed, physically plausible motion dynamics. Unlike text-to-video models that offer only semantic control, MOTIONCRAFT allows users to define precise physical behaviors (e.g., fluid flow, rigid body rotations, multi-agent interactions) through physics simulations. This addresses a critical limitation in current generative models where complex, explainable physical motion is difficult to achieve or control.

2. **Zero-Shot Efficiency:** By operating in a zero-shot manner, MOTIONCRAFT eliminates the need for extensive training or finetuning of diffusion models for video tasks. This significantly reduces computational costs and data requirements, making high-quality video generation more accessible. It effectively re-purposes existing, powerful image diffusion models (like Stable Diffusion) for video tasks, thereby maximizing the utility of pre-trained resources.

3. **Decoupling Content and Motion:** The method's approach of decoupling the learning of content from the learning of motion dynamics is impactful. By relying on physics simulators for motion and the image diffusion model for content synthesis, it creates a more modular and potentially more robust system. This architecture could lead to more parsimonious models that do not need to implicitly learn complex physical laws from vast datasets, as that knowledge is provided externally.

4. **Novel Content Generation with Coherence:** The application of optical flow warping in the *latent space*, combined with mechanisms like Multiple Cross-Frame Attention (MCFA) and Spatial Noise Map Weighting (Spatial-η), enables the model to not only move existing content but also to synthesize *novel* and *coherent* content that enters the scene or adapts to dynamic changes (e.g., new continents appearing during Earth's rotation, smoke obscuring objects). This is a crucial capability that distinguishes it from simple pixel-space warping.

5. **Generalizability:** The demonstrated application of MOTIONCRAFT with different diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion 1.5 and SDXL) suggests a degree of generalizability across various image generation backbones and resolutions, indicating its potential for adaptation to future, more advanced image models.

#### 5.1. Potential Impact

- **Scientific Visualization:** One direct and positive impact highlighted by the authors is its utility for scientists. Researchers in fields like physics, engineering, or biology could use MOTIONCRAFT to visualize their simulations (e.g., fluid dynamics, molecular interactions, astronomical phenomena) with AI-generated realistic imagery, making complex processes more understandable and visually engaging.
- **Animation and Special Effects:** The fine-grained control over motion makes MOTIONCRAFT valuable for animators and visual effects artists. It could streamline the creation of complex, physically accurate movements and environmental effects in movies, games, or virtual reality, potentially reducing manual labor and rendering times.
- **Content Creation:** General content creators could leverage this tool to generate dynamic visuals with specific motions that are difficult to achieve through traditional methods or generic prompt-based generation.
- **Further Research:** The work opens avenues for future research, particularly in developing generative models for optical flows that are conditioned on prompts and physics, or establishing closed feedback-loops between image generators and physics simulators for enhanced physical fidelity.

#### 5.2. Broader Ethical Considerations

As noted by the authors, synthetic video generation is a powerful technology with potential for misuse, such as creating deepfakes or misinformation. The paper states that MOTIONCRAFT does not introduce new restrictions or relax existing ones beyond its base text-to-image model, implying it inherits similar ethical considerations. However, the ability to precisely control motion could make generated fake videos more convincing. On the positive side, by not requiring new model training, MOTIONCRAFT aligns with environmental considerations by avoiding the significant energy consumption associated with large-scale model development.
