# Optimistic Task Inference for Behavior Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Optimistic Task Inference for BFMs (OpTI-BFM) introduces an online framework for Behavior Foundation Models to infer reward functions solely through environment interaction. This method efficiently recovers near-optimal policies within a few episodes, significantly reducing the need for extensive pre-labeled data and demonstrating adaptability to changing reward functions.

## Problem

- Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs) traditionally require substantial pre-existing datasets of labeled (state, reward) pairs for accurate task inference.
- Generating reward labels, especially for complex tasks or raw sensor inputs like pixels, is often prohibitively expensive or practically infeasible.
- Access to the full pre-training dataset, which could be used for sampling inference data, might be limited or proprietary during BFM deployment.

## Method

- **OpTI-BFM** proposes an online, episodic framework where the agent actively collects (state, reward) data during deployment to estimate the true task embedding.
- The algorithm maintains a regularized least-squares estimate of the reward function and constructs a confidence ellipsoid in the task embedding space.
- It selects policies by maximizing an Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) objective, optimistically picking the most promising task embedding within its current belief set to balance exploration and exploitation.

## Results

- OpTI-BFM achieved near-Oracle performance within approximately 5 episodes (5,000 environment steps) across Deepmind Control Suite tasks, outperforming existing baselines.
- The framework successfully adapted to non-stationary reward functions by applying a decay rate to older observations in the least-squares estimator, tracking changing objectives.
- Computational overhead was minimal (approx. 5 times slower than policy execution, operating at **280 Hz**), and a variant could achieve comparable performance while requiring an order of magnitude fewer explicit reward labels.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Online task inference for BFMs can be modeled as a linear contextual bandit problem by leveraging the approximate linearity of expected discounted returns with Universal Successor Features.
> - Actively collecting reward-level feedback through environment interaction provides more informative data for task identification compared to passive data collection.
> - The UCB approach effectively balances the trade-off between reducing uncertainty about the reward function and maximizing the agent's immediate performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of zero-shot reinforcement learning (RL), a paradigm where agents can retrieve high-performing policies for various reward functions specified at test-time, often without prior exposure to that specific reward. Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs) represent a scaling approach within zero-shot RL, frequently relying on Universal Successor Features (USFs). USFs provide explicit state representations where the expected discounted sum of features along a policy's trajectory can be estimated offline. This enables zero-shot policy evaluation and improvement by representing reward functions as linear combinations of these features, with the coefficients forming "task embeddings" (z).

The standard process of task inference in BFMs, which involves mapping a reward function to an approximately optimal policy, traditionally requires a pre-existing dataset of labeled (state, reward) pairs. This necessitates either access to a functional form of rewards or significant manual labeling efforts. This paper addresses this limitation by proposing an *online* task inference framework, where the agent actively collects data through environment interaction during deployment to estimate the task embedding.

This approach contrasts with existing methods like those focusing on fast adaptation or fine-tuning of already initialized policies, such as LoLA (Sikchi et al., 2025), by aiming to identify a strong policy purely through environment interaction without relying on pre-existing labeled data. The methodology draws a direct connection to established theoretical frameworks in linear contextual bandits, a well-studied area of bandit literature that deals with online optimization of linear functions under uncertainty. This integration of practical BFM applications with rigorous theoretical foundations represents a notable contribution. The paper also relates to broader reward learning problems, particularly active strategies for query selection, but distinguishes itself by specifically targeting BFMs and leveraging their inherent structure.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for efficient task inference in Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs) by exclusively leveraging interaction with the environment at test-time. This aims to alleviate the significant data requirements typically associated with deploying BFMs.

The motivation for this objective stems from several limitations observed in current BFM deployment paradigms:
- **High Data Demand for Task Inference:** While BFMs are efficient in terms of computational resources for retrieving policies once a task is known, they commonly necessitate a substantial "inference dataset" of labeled (state, reward) pairs. This dataset is used to determine the task embedding `z_r` that parameterizes the optimal policy for a given reward function `r(s) = z_r^T ϕ(s)`.
- **Practical Infeasibility of Labeling:** In realistic scenarios, generating reward labels for every state in a dataset can be prohibitively expensive or practically impossible. For instance, when BFMs are trained on raw pixel inputs, evaluating success or assigning a reward from a single image often requires human supervision or a precise, manually defined reward function, both of which incur significant costs.
- **Data Access Constraints:** The pre-training dataset, from which inference data might be sampled, could be unavailable or proprietary, further limiting the feasibility of standard task inference.

To overcome these challenges, the authors propose an alternative online framework where an agent actively collects a minimal amount of data during deployment. This approach aims to eliminate the need for storing large pre-training datasets and, more importantly, to drastically reduce the number of reward labels required for task identification. The specific goal is to enable BFMs to quickly and accurately identify an unseen reward function and retrieve a near-optimal policy with minimal interaction and computational overhead, thereby broadening the applicability of BFMs beyond simulated environments where reward functions are easily accessible.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is Optimistic Task Inference for BFMs (OpTI-BFM), an algorithm designed to perform task inference by actively collecting data and updating beliefs about the reward function in an online, episodic setting.

**Online Task Inference Setting:**
The agent is assumed to have access to a pre-trained USF-based BFM, which includes parameterized policies (π_z), successor feature (SF) estimates (ψ_π_z), and state features (ϕ). Interaction with the environment is episodic, with a defined horizon H. At each step `t` within an episode, the agent selects a task embedding `z_t` from a low-dimensional space Z. An action `a_t` is then sampled from the policy `π_z_t(·|s_t)`, leading to a new state `s_t+1` and an observed reward `r_t`. The objective is to minimize the expected regret over `n` episodes, where regret is defined as the difference between the discounted return of an optimal policy (π_z_r) and the policy chosen by the agent (π_z_k). The key insight leveraged is that, for well-trained USFs, the expected discounted return is approximately linear in the successor features, which connects the problem to online optimization of a linear function, a known problem in bandit theory.

**OpTI-BFM Algorithm:**
OpTI-BFM operates by continuously estimating the true task embedding `z_r` based on observed (state, reward) pairs.
1. **Least-Squares Estimation:** The algorithm maintains a regularized least-squares estimate `ˆz_t` of `z_r`. This estimate is derived from the linear relationship `r(s) = z^T ϕ(s)`, where `ϕ(s)` are the state features and `r(s)` is the observed reward. The update rule for `ˆz_t` and the associated precision matrix `V_t` (which includes an `l2`-regularization term) allows for efficient, recursive computation.
2. **Confidence Ellipsoid:** Based on `ˆz_t` and `V_t`, OpTI-BFM constructs a confidence ellipsoid `C_t` in the task embedding space. This ellipsoid is guaranteed to contain the true task embedding `z_r` with high probability, with its size controlled by a parameter `β_t`.
3. **Optimistic Decision Criterion (UCB):** At each step, the agent selects the task embedding `z_t` by maximizing an Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) objective. Specifically, `z_t` is chosen to maximize `max_w∈C_t w^T ψ(s_t, z)`. This means OpTI-BFM picks the "most promising" task embedding within its current belief set that is compatible with observed rewards, thereby balancing exploration (reducing uncertainty) and exploitation (maximizing expected reward).
 - A notable aspect is the use of state features `ϕ` for constructing the confidence set, while successor features `ψ` are used in the acquisition function. The paper formally demonstrates that using `ϕ` for regression leads to tighter estimates compared to using aggregated successor features for returns.

**Theoretical Guarantees:**
The paper provides a regret bound for OpTI-BFM, connecting it to algorithms for linear contextual bandits. Under four key assumptions: (A1) perfect USF estimation, (A2) linear reward functions corrupted by subgaussian noise, (A3) an oracle for objective optimization, and (A4) bounded norms, OpTI-BFM (when updating `z_t` only at the start of each episode) is shown to incur an expected regret of `O(d√n)`, where `d` is the dimension of the task embedding and `n` is the number of episodes. The detailed proof involves showing how reward-level feedback enables tighter confidence sets and applying the elliptical potential lemma. A study on robustness under weaker (misspecified) assumptions (A1', A2') is also provided, leading to regret bounds that include terms proportional to the degree of mismatch.

**Practical Implementation and Variants:**
1. **UCB Optimization:** The UCB objective is reformulated to simplify optimization. In practice, a sampling-based approach ("random shooting") is used, where `n=128` candidates are sampled from the confidence ellipsoid `C_t` and evaluated through forward passes of the BFM's successor feature network.
2. **Efficient Parameter Updates:** `ˆz_t` and `V_t^{-1}` are updated efficiently in `O(d^2)` time using Cholesky factor updates, ensuring low computational overhead per step.
3. **Thompson Sampling (TS) Variant (OpTI-BFM-TS):** This variant leverages the Bayesian interpretation of the least-squares estimator, directly sampling `z_t` from the Gaussian posterior `N(ˆz_t, V_t^{-1})`, thereby avoiding the explicit UCB optimization problem.
4. **Non-stationary Rewards Variant:** For environments with changing reward functions, a decay rate `ρ` is introduced to weight recent observations more heavily in the least-squares estimator, allowing the algorithm to adapt.
5. **Explicit Reward Label Request:** An additional variant explores scenarios where reward labels are costly. It only requests a reward `r_t` if the "D-gap" (log determinant ratio) of the precision matrix exceeds a threshold `κ`, thereby improving labeling efficiency by selectively requesting data points that significantly reduce uncertainty.
6. **Warm-Starting:** OpTI-BFM can be initialized with a small amount of pre-existing labeled data, updating its least-squares estimator and accelerating convergence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of OpTI-BFM and its variants was conducted on established zero-shot reinforcement learning benchmarks, primarily using tasks from the Deepmind Control Suite (DMC) (Walker, Cheetah, Quadruped) and also in a loco-navigation task (Antmaze). The performance was compared against baseline methods including "Random" (random embedding selection), "Oracle" (optimal policy with privileged access to `z_r`), and LoLA (Sikchi etal., 2025).

1. **Online Task Inference Performance:** OpTI-BFM consistently demonstrated rapid learning, recovering near-Oracle performance within approximately 5 episodes (equivalent to 5,000 environment steps) across all tested tasks in DMC. The UCB-based OpTI-BFM generally outperformed its Thompson Sampling (TS) variant in terms of convergence speed, particularly in the Cheetah environment, although the TS variant remained a promising approach due to its simplicity. LoLA, which employs a black-box policy search without leveraging the linear structure of rewards, exhibited slower progress compared to OpTI-BFM.
2. **Informativeness of Actively Collected Data:** An evaluation of the quality of the inferred task embeddings (`z_n`) showed that data collected by actively exploring methods (OpTI-BFM and RND) led to better policies compared to passive data collection. OpTI-BFM and OpTI-BFM-TS were observed to be more data-efficient than RND, which was attributed to their task-awareness during data collection.
3. **Impact of Update Frequency:** The study found that updating the task embedding `z_t` at every step, rather than only at the beginning of each episode (as assumed in the theoretical bound), resulted in faster initial performance improvement. While both approaches eventually converged to similar performance levels over longer durations, per-step updates offered a more reactive adaptation to new information.
4. **Adaptation to Non-stationary Rewards:** OpTI-BFM was evaluated on custom Walker tasks with dynamically changing velocity targets. A direct application of OpTI-BFM (without weighting past data) struggled to adapt to changing reward functions after initial convergence. However, introducing a decay rate (`ρ < 1.0`) for older observations in the least-squares estimator enabled OpTI-BFM to effectively track the varying velocity targets, demonstrating its capacity for adaptation in non-stationary environments.
5. **Warm-Starting with Labeled Data:** When OpTI-BFM was warm-started with a small number of pre-existing labeled states, its initial performance rapidly improved with the increasing size of the initial labeled dataset. This indicates that OpTI-BFM can function as an extension to traditional offline task inference, benefiting from any available initial labels.
6. **Computational Overhead:** The computational cost of OpTI-BFM was measured to be approximately 5 times slower than merely executing a pre-selected policy, and the TS variant was about 4 times slower. This was considered a minimal overhead, maintaining high operational frequencies (280 Hz for OpTI-BFM, 363 Hz for OpTI-BFM-TS). The random shooting optimization strategy for UCB was found to be more computationally efficient than gradient-based optimization for typical task embedding dimensions.
7. **Labeling Efficiency with Explicit Reward Requests:** The variant that conditionally requested reward labels based on an information threshold (D-gap) demonstrated a trade-off between environment interaction and labeling cost. In easier tasks, it achieved comparable performance while requiring an order of magnitude fewer reward labels.
8. **Robustness Analysis:**
 - **Inaccurate Successor Features:** Introducing a systematic, z-dependent bias into the successor features (`ψ`) demonstrated that OpTI-BFM's performance degrades with increasing bias magnitude. However, very significant perturbations (bias magnitude far exceeding the nominal norm of `ψ`) were required to render the algorithm uninformative, suggesting a degree of robustness.
 - **Non-Linear Rewards:** When the true reward function had components orthogonal to the feature space (`ϕ`), OpTI-BFM showed a slightly greater performance reduction compared to the Oracle, particularly in specific tasks. This indicates a dependence on the assumed linearity for both data collection and inference.
 - **Noisy Rewards:** The algorithm's convergence slowed down as the standard deviation of Gaussian noise added to observed rewards increased, aligning with theoretical predictions. Extremely high noise levels (10 times the reward range) significantly impeded convergence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a method, OpTI-BFM, that addresses a critical limitation in the deployment of Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs): the need for extensive labeled datasets for task inference. By demonstrating efficient online task inference through direct environment interaction, this work significantly enhances the practical applicability of BFMs.

The key significance and potential impact include:

- **Enabling Real-World Deployment:** OpTI-BFM mitigates the challenge of costly and time-consuming reward labeling, particularly in complex scenarios like learning from raw pixel data or in robotics, where acquiring ground-truth reward signals is difficult. This makes BFMs more viable for real-world applications beyond simulated environments.
- **Reduced Data Requirements:** The ability to infer tasks in a handful of episodes with minimal compute overhead translates to substantial savings in data collection and annotation efforts, making BFM deployment more cost-effective and scalable.
- **Bridging Theory and Practice:** The work successfully connects the practical utility of scalable BFMs with the rigorous theoretical guarantees of linear bandit algorithms. Providing a regret bound for OpTI-BFM, even under specific assumptions, contributes to a deeper theoretical understanding of online task inference in this context.
- **Adaptability and Robustness:** The demonstrated ability of OpTI-BFM to adapt to non-stationary reward functions (with appropriate hyperparameter tuning) and its relative robustness to moderate levels of noise and model misspecification further underscore its practical utility. The optional mechanism for explicit reward label requests offers a valuable trade-off between interaction cost and labeling expense, relevant for scenarios where labels are a scarce resource.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens several avenues for future exploration. These include extending theoretical guarantees to the empirically stronger per-step update mechanism, further investigating the properties of the BFM's latent task embedding space, and exploring combinations of OpTI-BFM with policy fine-tuning methods for long-term performance optimization. The paper highlights BFMs as a fertile ground for applying theoretically principled approaches, which could have broader implications across various domains in reinforcement learning and artificial intelligence.
