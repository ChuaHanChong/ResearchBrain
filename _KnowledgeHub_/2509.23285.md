# Toward Effective Tool-Integrated Reasoning via Self-Evolved Preference Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Renmin University researchers introduced Tool-Light, a framework that boosts large language model performance in tool-integrated reasoning by employing information entropy analysis and a self-evolved preference learning approach. It demonstrated improved accuracy and efficiency across diverse mathematical and knowledge-intensive tasks.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) often exhibit suboptimal tool usage, characterized by both insufficient and excessive invocation of external tools.
- LLMs frequently struggle with processing low-quality or ambiguous tool call results, leading to unproductive reasoning loops and overthinking.
- Prior tool-integrated reasoning (TIR) methods often focus on specific aspects or individual tools, struggling to generalize effectively across multi-tool scenarios.
- Existing approaches lack a comprehensive optimization strategy addressing both tool underuse and overuse, along with reasoning stability.

## Method

- **Tool-Light** is a multi-stage training pipeline that leverages ==information entropy analysis== to guide dataset construction and a two-stage ==Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)== (DPO) for model alignment.
- An entropy-guided sampling strategy generates diverse reasoning paths, favoring paths with lower entropy to encourage efficiency in tool calls.
- A ==Self-Evolved DPO== training paradigm iteratively refines tool usage preferences, with dynamically adjusted data selection criteria for hard and easy tasks to balance efficiency and necessity.
- The framework is applied to enhance LLM capabilities with both code interpreter and search tools across various reasoning tasks.

## Results

- Tool-Light achieved an average performance of **58.0** across **10** challenging datasets, outperforming all single-TIR and multi-TIR baselines by up to **12.7** points.
- The framework demonstrated superior tool-use effectiveness, scoring **0.44** in efficiency and **0.75** in necessity, indicating a reduction in both tool overuse and underuse.
- Tool-Light produced lower output sequence entropy distributions and shorter average output lengths compared to baselines, suggesting reduced overthinking while maintaining or improving correctness.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the two-loop self-evolved DPO alignment and the specific design of positive and negative example selection criteria for optimal performance and efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Information entropy distribution in reasoning chains correlates with tool usage: paths with fewer tool calls generally exhibit lower overall entropy.
> - Focusing training on challenging examples where an initial model fails without tools is effective for improving tool-integrated reasoning.
> - Iterative DPO, guided by an understanding of entropy and specific criteria for preferred/dispreferred reasoning paths, can dynamically teach models to make both efficient and necessary tool calls.
> - The framework reduces both unnecessary tool calls and unproductive reasoning loops, leading to more stable and concise outputs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of large language models (LLMs) has led to remarkable improvements in their internal reasoning abilities across various domains, from mathematical problem-solving to code generation. However, LLMs still face significant limitations when encountering tasks that require deep factual recall, precise computation, or access to up-to-date external information. This is where **Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR)** emerges as a critical paradigm. TIR enables LLMs to autonomously interact with external tools (e.g., search engines, code interpreters, calculators) during their reasoning process, thereby compensating for inherent knowledge gaps or computational limitations.

Despite its promise, current TIR approaches often grapple with several challenges:
- **Suboptimal Tool Usage:** LLMs may exhibit "incorrect tool calls," characterized by insufficient or excessive invocation of tools. This leads to either incomplete reasoning or unnecessary computational overhead and extended reasoning chains.
- **Overthinking and Analysis Paralysis:** When provided with low-quality or ambiguous tool call results, models can get stuck in unproductive reasoning loops, leading to "overthinking" or "analysis paralysis."
- **Generalization across Multiple Tools:** Many existing methods, particularly those based on reinforcement learning (RL), tend to focus on optimizing the use of individual tools and struggle to generalize effectively to multi-tool scenarios.
- **Lack of Comprehensive Optimization:** Prior work often addresses only specific aspects, such as reducing tool overuse (e.g., Wang et al., 2025a; Qian et al., 2025), neglecting tool underuse or the broader impact of tool call results on the subsequent reasoning flow.

This paper positions itself as a significant contribution to overcoming these limitations by offering a more holistic approach to effective TIR. It departs from the prevailing RL-centric methods by proposing a novel framework based on **preference learning** and an **information entropy perspective**. By analyzing how tool calls affect the information entropy of an LLM's reasoning process, the authors aim to develop a more efficient and accurate strategy for guiding tool usage. This work fits into a growing trend of self-improvement and self-evolution in LLMs, where models learn from their own generated data and iteratively refine their behaviors to adapt to complex, dynamic tasks. The introduction of entropy-guided sampling and a multi-stage self-evolved DPO training pipeline represents a novel direction for improving TIR, moving beyond simple reward functions to a more nuanced understanding of reasoning efficiency and stability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to incentivize LLMs to perform Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR) more efficiently and accurately, while simultaneously stabilizing the overall reasoning process. The authors identify the core problems in current TIR as:
- **Suboptimal Tool Call Patterns:** This includes both *insufficient* tool usage, where necessary tools are not invoked, and *excessive* tool usage, where tools are called unnecessarily, leading to redundancy and inefficiency.
- **Overthinking/Analysis Paralysis:** LLMs often struggle to process low-quality tool outputs effectively, leading to prolonged and unproductive reasoning steps.

To address these challenges, the paper is motivated by two key questions:
1. **From the training perspective:** Can the effectiveness of tool calls in TIR tasks be improved through post-training methods, encompassing both algorithmic advancements and data construction strategies? This question targets the need for better learning mechanisms to guide tool use.
2. **From the inference perspective:** How can the observed entropy distribution characteristics of the model's TIR process be leveraged to guide the construction of high-quality training data? This highlights the novel insight derived from their preliminary entropy analysis.

Ultimately, the goal is to develop a framework that not only reduces redundant tool calls but also ensures the invocation of necessary tools, minimizes overthinking, and prevents analysis paralysis, thereby achieving a more "effective" TIR that encompasses both efficiency and accuracy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **Tool-Light**, is a multi-stage training pipeline designed to optimize LLM reasoning and tool-call effectiveness. It comprises two main components: Dataset Construction and a Two-Stage TIR Training Paradigm.

#### 3.1. Preliminary Exploration: Information Entropy Analysis

The authors begin by exploring the relationship between TIR paths and information entropy distribution. They calculate the information entropy `H(i)` for each token position `i` in the reasoning chain. Their key findings are:
- Upon receiving a tool call result, the model's output information entropy initially rises, fluctuates, and then sharply drops before the next tool call.
- Reasoning chains with fewer tool calls tend to have a lower overall entropy distribution compared to those with more tool calls, especially as reasoning progresses.
These insights form the basis for their entropy-guided sampling strategy.

#### 3.2. Dataset Construction

The data construction process aims to create high-quality positive and negative pairs for preference learning.
1. **Source Data Construction (`D_source`):**
 - Start with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) data (`D_sft`) to train an initial instruct model (`M_sft`).
 - `M_sft` performs inference on `D_sft` *without* external tools.
 - Only samples where `M_sft` produces *incorrect* answers are retained, forming `D_source`. This focuses training on challenging examples where TIR is most needed.
2. **Sampling Strategy Design (`D_dpo`):** `M_sft` then performs TIR inference on `D_source`, generating multiple reasoning paths for each question using two strategies:
 - **Vanilla TIR Sampling:** `M_sft` generates multiple paths for each sample, forming `D_1_dpo`. This provides diverse trajectories.
 - **Entropy-Guided Sampling:**
 - A "main" reasoning chain (`C_main`) is generated.
 - Information entropy is calculated for subsequences within each step (e.g., first 10-50 tokens).
 - Branching occurs from the top-k highest-entropy positions, guiding the model to generate alternative continuations. This strategy aims to explore diverse paths more efficiently, potentially reducing computational complexity.
 - `D_1_dpo` and `D_2_dpo` are mixed in a specific ratio (found to be optimal at 13:7 based on ablation) to form `D_dpo_1` for subsequent training.

#### 3.3. Two-Stage TIR Training Paradigm

Tool-Light employs a two-stage training approach to progressively improve TIR effectiveness.

1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - The base model is initially fine-tuned using `D_sft` to acquire fundamental TIR capabilities. This creates `M_sft`.

2. **Self-Evolved Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** `M_sft` is further fine-tuned using a DPO loss function, which maximizes the relative probability of generating preferred (`y_w`) over dispreferred (`y_l`) responses. This stage is divided into two parts:

 - **Pre-Aligned DPO Training:**
 - **Data Selection Criteria (Cri_1):** From the sampled `D_dpo_1`, trajectories are categorized as correct (F1 score = 1) or incorrect (F1 score = 0).
 - **Hard Set:** Samples with ≤ 50% correct trajectories (or ≤ 40% for vanilla sampling).
 - **Easy Set:** Samples with ≥ 50% correct trajectories (or ≥ 70% for vanilla sampling).
 - **Positive Example (y_w):** The correct trajectory with the *minimal number of tool calls and lowest entropy*. If no correct trajectory exists, the SFT trajectory from `D_source` is used. This encourages efficiency.
 - **Negative Example (y_l):** An incorrect trajectory with *more tool calls* than the positive example. This penalizes inefficient incorrect reasoning.
 - The hard and easy sets are balanced (2:1 ratio). This stage aims to reduce *unnecessary* tool calls and excessive reasoning. Training this yields `M_dpo1`.

 - **Self-Evolved DPO Alignment:**
 - `M_dpo1` is used to *resample* from `D_dpo_1`, generating new trajectories.
 - **New Data Selection Criteria (Cri_2):** The classification into hard/easy sets is updated based on `M_dpo1`'s performance: hard samples now have *less than half* correct trajectories compared to incorrect ones (easy set criteria remain similar).
 - **Positive Example (y_w) in Easy Set:** Correct trajectory with fewer tool calls and lowest entropy. (Maintains efficiency focus for problems the model now handles well).
 - **Negative Example (y_l) in Easy Set:** Incorrect trajectory with the *most* tool calls. (Reinforces avoidance of highly inefficient incorrect paths).
 - **Positive Example (y_w) in Hard Set:** Correct trajectory with the *longest* reasoning chain. (Encourages comprehensive reasoning, even with more tool calls, for difficult problems where tools might be necessary).
 - **Negative Example (y_l) in Hard Set:** Incorrect trajectory with the *shortest* reasoning chain. (Discourages truncated incorrect reasoning for hard problems).
 - This stage involves *multiple iterative loops* of sampling and training, where the model continuously generates better training data based on its evolving proficiency. This enables the model to learn necessary tool calls while retaining efficiency. This process yields the final model `M_dpo2`.

#### 3.4. Tool Design

The framework explores two mainstream tools: a **code interpreter** (receives code, returns compilation result/error) and a **search tool** (receives query, retrieves relevant content from local knowledge base or web).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on 10 challenging reasoning tasks, categorized into mathematical-reasoning (AIME24, AIME25, AMC23, MATH, MATH500, GSM8K) and knowledge-intensive tasks (HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, MuSiQue, Bamboogle). The backbone LLMs used were Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama3.1-8B-Instruct.

#### 4.1. Metrics

- **Performance:** Evaluated by LLM-as-Judge (Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct) for mathematical tasks and F1 score for knowledge-intensive tasks.
- **Efficiency:** `Effi = (1/n) * sum(M_i / T_i)`, where `M_i` is performance and `T_i` is the number of tool calls for sample `i`. Higher values indicate less tool overuse.
- **Necessity:** `Nece = M * (1/n) * sum(N_i_in - N_i_co)`, where `N_i_in` is count of incorrect methods with *more* tool calls, and `N_i_co` is count of correct methods with *fewer* tool calls. Higher values indicate better necessary tool usage (less underuse). `M` denotes Min-Max Scaling.

#### 4.2. Main Results (Table 1 & 3)

- **Superior Performance:** Tool-Light (Qwen) achieved an average performance of 58.0 across all 10 datasets, outperforming all single-TIR baselines (e.g., Search-R1: 45.6, ToRL: 50.4) and multi-TIR baselines (e.g., Prompting-Based: 35.3, ReCall: 43.2, Tool-Star: 56.6). This demonstrates significant improvements in overall task completion accuracy.
- **Generalization Advantage:** Unlike single-TIR models that show strengths in specific task types (e.g., Search-R1 in knowledge-intensive, ToRL in mathematical), Tool-Light exhibits strong performance across both mathematical-reasoning and knowledge-intensive tasks, highlighting the effectiveness of its multi-TIR training.
- **Effectiveness of DPO Training:** The multi-round DPO training within Tool-Light (Pre-Aligned and Self-Evolved) consistently boosted performance over the initial SFT model and prompting-based methods. The gradual improvement across SFT, Pre-Aligned DPO, and Self-Evolved DPO stages (Table 3) validates the pipeline design.

#### 4.3. Quantitative Analysis (Figure 4)

- **Tool-Use Effectiveness:** Tool-Light achieved the best scores in both **Efficiency (0.44)** and **Necessity (0.75)** metrics compared to baselines like Tool-Star (0.39, 0.73), ReCall (0.32, 0.61), and Search-R1 (0.28, 0.58). This quantitatively confirms that Tool-Light effectively reduces both tool overuse and underuse.
- **Reduced Overthinking:** Tool-Light also produced shorter average output sequence lengths compared to Tool-Star, indicating less "overthinking" while maintaining or improving correctness.

#### 4.4. Entropy Distribution Analysis (Figure 5)

- Analysis of output sequences' entropy distribution showed that Tool-Light results in **lower entropy distributions** compared to other TIR models like Search-R1 and ReCall. This finding supports the hypothesis that the entropy-guided sampling and DPO criteria (which prefer low-entropy paths for efficiency) successfully mitigate excessive reasoning.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies (Table 2)

- **Impact of DPO Loop Number:** Two loops in the Self-Evolved DPO Alignment stage yielded optimal performance (58.0), efficiency (0.44), and necessity (0.75). Increasing loops beyond two led to diminishing returns or even performance degradation, suggesting potential overfitting to the evolving training data distribution.
- **Impact of Sampling Criteria:** The specific design of positive and negative example selection criteria was crucial. Random selection of positive or negative examples significantly dropped performance (e.g., -4.4% for random positive selection, -4.1% for random negative selection), and also negatively impacted efficiency and necessity. This underscores the importance of carefully distinguishing between preferred and dispreferred reasoning paths based on tool usage and entropy. The ratio of mixed sampling strategies (vanilla vs. entropy-guided) had a lesser, but still notable, impact.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Tool-Light framework represents a significant advancement in the field of Tool-Integrated Reasoning for LLMs, offering several key contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Pioneering Entropy-Guided TIR:** This work is a pioneering exploration of TIR from the perspective of information entropy. The initial analysis revealing the link between tool calls, entropy fluctuations, and reasoning efficiency provides a novel theoretical foundation. This insight offers a new lens through which to understand and optimize LLM reasoning processes, potentially inspiring further research into information-theoretic approaches for controlling model behavior.

2. **Comprehensive Tool-Use Optimization:** Unlike previous methods that often focus on single tools or only mitigate tool overuse, Tool-Light addresses the full spectrum of "incorrect tool calls"—including both excessive and insufficient tool usage, as well as overthinking. By introducing explicit metrics for Efficiency and Necessity and demonstrating superior performance on both, the paper establishes a new benchmark for comprehensive tool-use effectiveness.

3. **Innovative Self-Evolved Preference Learning:** The proposed two-stage training paradigm, incorporating Supervised Fine-Tuning followed by a sophisticated Self-Evolved Direct Preference Optimization, is highly innovative. The dynamic adjustment of data complexity and the iterative self-evolution mechanism allow the model to learn necessary tool calls while maintaining efficiency. This preference learning approach, guided by entropy, offers a compelling alternative or complement to traditional reinforcement learning methods, which can be complex to implement and sensitive to reward function design.

4. **Enhanced Generalization and Stability:** Tool-Light demonstrates strong generalization capabilities across a diverse set of mathematical and knowledge-intensive tasks, outperforming specialized single-tool models. The framework also contributes to more stable reasoning by producing lower-entropy outputs, suggesting a reduction in internal uncertainty and overthinking—a critical aspect for deploying LLMs in real-world applications.

5. **Practical Guidance for Future Research:** The quantitative analyses, including ablation studies on DPO loops and sampling criteria, provide practical insights for future development in TIR. They highlight the importance of careful data construction, the benefits of iterative self-improvement, and the optimal balance between different training objectives. Researchers can leverage these findings to design more robust and efficient training pipelines for LLM agents.

In summary, Tool-Light pushes the boundaries of effective TIR by integrating information theory, advanced data sampling, and preference learning into a cohesive, self-evolving framework. Its success in improving accuracy, efficiency, and stability of multi-tool reasoning opens new avenues for developing more capable and reliable LLM agents that can effectively navigate complex tasks by strategically leveraging external resources.
