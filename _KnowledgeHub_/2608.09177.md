# Intuitive Directional Sense Presentation to the Torso Using McKibben-Based Surface Haptic Sensation in Immersive Space

> [!summary] Summary
> A wearable fabric actuator with 12 McKibben artificial muscles deforms clothing over the torso to produce surface haptic sensations, forward and backward bending plus left and right twisting, that steer a user's gaze in an immersive mixed-reality space without visual cues. A Stevens-power-law fit maps air pressure to gaze angle, and error-increasing-only pressure feedback guides gaze faster than synthesized speech or arm-mounted haptics.

## Problem

- Visual gaze-guidance overlays in immersive spaces force users to watch added cues closely, interfering with the manipulation task they are supposed to support.
- Existing wearable haptics target hands, fingers, face or neck, ignoring torso motion that dominates large gaze shifts and limiting the information a small area conveys.
- Vibrotactile torso vests need prior instruction to decode cue meaning and are built for two-dimensional map navigation, so they transfer poorly to three-dimensional gaze guidance.

## Method

- A wearable fabric actuator embeds 12 ==McKibben artificial muscles== in clothing; pressurizing distinct muscle groups yields forward bend, backward bend, left twist or right twist of the torso.
- Per-user calibration fits a ==Stevens power law==, pressure equals a times angle to the b plus c, over ten pressure-angle samples per direction using ==Levenberg-Marquardt least squares==.
- An ==asymmetric air-pressure adjustment law== adds gain-scaled feedback on the pressure error only while gaze drifts away from the target, so decreasing deformation is never mistaken for arrival.

## Results

- With **15** subjects, all four guidance conditions placed the target within the **30-degree** central visual field, but synthesized speech beat every haptic condition on angular error (**p < 0.001**).
- In the **40-second** search task with **50** distractors, the full method found the most targets, about **10.7** per trial versus **8.4** for speech, and won for **10** of **15** subjects (**p < 0.05**).
- Ablating the pressure adjustment or moving muscles to the arms removed the speed advantage; standard deviations were **3.0**, **3.3**, **4.0** and **1.4** targets across the four conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Haptic guidance can be fast without being accurate: speech conveyed direction more precisely, yet surface haptics let users act on it sooner.
> - Changing stimulus intensity matters more than absolute intensity, since touch perception is relative; feedback only on growing error makes the resisting cue legible.
> - Per-user sensitivity calibration cannot compensate for garment fit; one-size wearables cap benefit for some users, and saturation at a safety pressure limit silently disables adaptation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges in providing effective directional guidance within immersive spaces, a field that has seen increasing demand across various applications, including medical education, surgical planning, and teleoperation. Immersive environments, while rich in visual information, can paradoxically lead to information overload, causing users to overlook critical details or directions, especially in unfamiliar or micro-scale settings. This necessitates non-visual guidance methods.

Existing approaches to guidance in immersive spaces include:
- **Visual Guidance:** While present, these methods often require constant visual attention, which can interfere with the user's primary tasks within the immersive environment.
- **Haptic Sensation:** Various haptic devices have been developed to convey information without visual cues.
 - **Stationary Haptic Devices:** These devices, focusing on friction, hardness, or roughness, are limited by their fixed locations and restricted presentation space.
 - **Wearable Haptic Devices:** Developed to overcome spatial limitations, many wearable devices primarily target hands and fingers. While enhancing the realism of interaction, they offer limited haptic information due to their restricted application area.
 - **Haptic Feedback to Other Body Parts:** To provide more comprehensive haptic information, systems have extended feedback to areas such as arms, feet, neck, head, face, and torso.
 - **Face and Neck Haptics:** These can provide gaze guidance but often do not account for torso movements, which are significant for large gaze shifts.
 - **Torso Haptics (e.g., vibrotactile):** Used for navigation and guidance, these systems frequently require users to learn specific meanings of vibrotactile patterns, making them less intuitive for new users. Additionally, many are optimized for two-dimensional navigation, which poses challenges for three-dimensional gaze guidance.

This research distinguishes itself by focusing on McKibben-based surface haptic sensations delivered to the torso via wearable fabric actuators. This approach aims to provide intuitive, whole-body kinesthetic perception across a wide area. By leveraging the torso's role in large gaze movements, the proposed system seeks to address the limitations of existing haptic guidance methods, particularly their lack of intuitiveness for 3D gaze guidance and their disregard for significant torso movements. The work aims to offer a method for 3D gaze guidance that is both intuitive and does not require users to divert visual attention from their primary tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent challenges faced by users in immersive environments. While these spaces offer extensive visual data, the sheer volume of information can be detrimental, causing users to miss crucial details or become disoriented, particularly in complex or unfamiliar scenarios such as microscopic environments. Current visual guidance systems, which modify or add visual cues, often necessitate active observation by the user, thereby interrupting their primary tasks and potentially diminishing the immersive experience. Consequently, there is a clear demand for guidance mechanisms that operate independently of visual input.

This study specifically focuses on haptic sensation as an alternative to visual information for directional guidance. The research identifies that large gaze movements are inherently linked to head and torso movements, with torso posture being a particularly influential factor. The central objective is to indirectly guide a user's gaze by inducing specific torso posture changes through haptic sensations, aiming to position desired objects within the user's central field of view (defined as a 30-degree radius from the viewpoint center), where object identification is optimal.

The key objectives of this study are:
- **Development of a Novel Air Pressure Adjustment Method:** To create a new method for controlling the air pressure in McKibben-based artificial muscles integrated into a wearable fabric actuator. This method is designed to provide surface haptic sensations across a broad area of the torso, facilitating whole-body motion guidance.
- **Implementation of Directional Sense Presentation:** To enable the system to provide intuitive directional cues to users in an immersive space without requiring visual information. The system is designed to provide four kinesthetic perceptions: forward bending, backward bending, left twist, and right twist, corresponding to up, down, left, and right gaze guidance, respectively.
- **Evaluation of System Effectiveness:** To quantitatively assess the proposed system's accuracy in directional presentation and the speed of haptic perception through controlled subject experiments and statistical data analysis.
- **Demonstration of Applicability:** To validate the practical utility of the proposed system in a real-world application, specifically by demonstrating its integration into a mixed-reality micromanipulation system for teleoperation, where precise gaze guidance is critical.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a system designed to provide intuitive directional guidance through McKibben-based surface haptic sensations to the torso in immersive environments.

**System Configuration:**
The proposed system integrates several key components:
- **Wearable Fabric Actuator:** This core component consists of clothing embedded with 12 McKibben artificial muscles. These muscles are arranged to induce four distinct torso movements: forward bending, backward bending, left twist, and right twist, by selective contraction. The intensity of the surface haptic sensation is directly correlated with the applied air pressure.
- **Mixed-Reality Device:** A Microsoft Hololens2 is used, equipped with gaze-tracking capabilities to monitor the user's gaze direction in real-time.
- **Computer System:** A Windows 10 Pro PC processes gaze direction data, calculates necessary air pressure commands, and manages the overall system.
- **Air Pressure Control System:** An air compressor (ACP-39SLA, TAKGI Corporation) supplies air, which is regulated by an air pressure controller comprising programmable logic controllers (Industrial Shields) and electric regulators (CRCB-0135W/0136W, KOGANEI Corporation).

**Algorithm for Physical Directional Sense Presentation:**

**A. Derivation of Equation between Air Pressure and Gaze Angle:**
To establish a precise relationship between haptic sensation and gaze direction, the study developed an equation linking air pressure to gaze angle. Gaze angles are categorized into horizontal (left/right) and vertical (up/down) planes. The system is designed to induce specific torso movements (e.g., left twist for left gaze guidance, backward bend for upward gaze guidance).

The relationship between applied air pressure ($P_k$) and the resulting gaze angle ($\theta_k$) for each of the four directions (k=1: left twist, k=2: right twist, k=3: forward bend, k=4: backward bend) is modeled using Stevens' power law, given by:
$P_k = a_k \theta_k^{b_k} + c_k$
where $a_k$, $b_k$, and $c_k$ are coefficients determined through a fitting procedure.

**Algorithm 1: Approximate Equation Fitting:**
1. **Data Collection:** For each of the four directions (k=1 to 4), 10 discrete air pressure values ($P_{kn}$) are applied incrementally to the artificial muscles, starting from zero up to a maximum of 375 kPa.
2. **Gaze Angle Recording:** For each applied pressure, subjects are instructed to adjust their torso posture and gaze direction in response to the haptic sensation. The resulting gaze angle ($\theta_{kn}$) between the current gaze vector and an initial gaze vector is recorded.
3. **Coefficient Estimation:** The collected pressure-angle pairs ($P_{kn}, \theta_{kn}$) are then used to estimate the coefficients ($a_k, b_k, c_k$) for each direction using the Levenberg-Marquardt method, which minimizes the squared error between the model's prediction and the measured pressure values.

**B. Air Pressure Adjustment Method for Surface Haptic Sensations:**
The system calculates a target gaze angle ($\theta_{t_k}$) based on the user's current gaze and the position of the target object. It then uses the derived approximate equation (from Section IV-A) to determine an initial air pressure. A feedback control algorithm adjusts this pressure dynamically:
$P_{t_k} = P_{t_k} + G (f_k(\theta_{t_k}) - f_k(\theta_k))$
This adjustment occurs only when the user's gaze is moving *away* from the target direction (i.e., when the error is increasing and $\theta_{t_k} > 0$). If the user's gaze is approaching the target, the air pressure is maintained ($P_{t_k}$). If the target angle is reached or passed ($\theta_{t_k} \leq 0$), the pressure is set to zero. This selective feedback is implemented to prevent users from misinterpreting a decrease in haptic sensation as reaching the target. The adjustment gain $G$ was set to 0.05 in experiments.

**Experimental Evaluation:**
The system's performance was evaluated through two subject experiments with 15 healthy participants (21-25 years old, BMI 17-26).
1. **Directional Sense Presentation Accuracy (Experiment V):**
 - **Conditions:** Four conditions were tested:
 1. Surface haptic sensation (proposed method) *with* air pressure adjustment.
 2. Surface haptic sensation *without* air pressure adjustment.
 3. Simple haptic sensation (McKibben muscles attached to arms) *with* air pressure adjustment.
 4. Synthesized speech guidance (outputting target direction and angle once).
 - **Procedure:** Subjects were presented with a random target gaze angle (within 30 deg vertical, 60 deg horizontal). They adjusted their gaze according to the guidance and pressed a keyboard when they believed their gaze matched the target.
 - **Metrics:** Horizontal and vertical gaze errors.
2. **Speed of Haptic Perception (Experiment VI):**
 - **Conditions:** The same four conditions as the accuracy experiment were used.
 - **Procedure:** Subjects aimed to identify as many target objects as possible within 40 seconds in a mixed-reality space containing 50 dummy objects. Guidance was provided to bring the target into the central visual field. An object was considered identified if the gaze error was within 5 degrees.
 - **Metrics:** Number of identified target objects.

**Demonstration in Immersive Micromanipulation System (Experiment VII):**
The proposed system was integrated into a previously developed mixed-reality micromanipulation system. This demonstration aimed to confirm the system's ability to smoothly guide an operator to virtual objects (e.g., pipettes, microbeads) within a complex manipulation task, thereby showcasing its applicability in teleoperation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments yielded several findings regarding the accuracy and speed of the proposed directional sense presentation system.

**V. Directional Sense Presentation Accuracy:**
- **Central Vision Field Coverage:** For all four experimental conditions, the mean and median horizontal and vertical errors were consistently below 30 degrees. This indicated that all tested guidance methods, including the proposed surface haptic sensation system, were capable of guiding the user's gaze towards the central vision field, where object recognition is optimal.
- **Comparison with Synthesized Speech:** Condition 4 (synthesized speech guidance) resulted in significantly smaller horizontal and vertical errors compared to Conditions 1, 2, and 3 (all haptic guidance methods) (Friedman test with Dunn-Bonferroni correction: p < 0.001). This suggests that speech guidance provided more precise directional information in this evaluation.
- **Haptic Method Comparison:** No statistically significant differences were observed in horizontal and vertical errors among the three haptic guidance conditions (Conditions 1, 2, and 3). This indicates that the proposed air pressure adjustment method and the location of haptic sensation (torso vs. arm) did not significantly affect the accuracy of directional guidance in this specific accuracy assessment.

**VI. Speed of Haptic Perception:**
- **Overall Performance:** Condition 1, which employed the proposed method (surface haptic sensation with air pressure adjustment), demonstrated the highest mean number of identified target objects among all four conditions. Specifically, 10 out of 15 subjects achieved their highest mean in Condition 1.
- **Statistical Significance:** Multiple comparison analysis (Friedman test with Dunn-Bonferroni correction) revealed that Condition 1 had a significantly higher number of identified target objects than Conditions 2, 3, and 4 (p < 0.05). This finding indicates that the proposed method enabled the fastest gaze direction guidance among the tested approaches.
- **Impact of Adjustment Algorithm:** The results suggest that the air pressure adjustment algorithm significantly improved the speed of directional sense presentation when utilizing surface haptic sensation.
- **Comparison with Simple Haptics:** The proposed method (Condition 1) exhibited a significantly higher speed of directional guidance than simple haptic sensation (Condition 3). However, there was no significant difference in speed between surface haptic sensation without adjustment (Condition 2) and simple haptic sensation with adjustment (Condition 3). This implies the effectiveness of the air pressure adjustment method primarily for surface haptic sensations.
- **Comparison with Synthesized Speech:** Despite its higher accuracy in the first experiment, synthesized speech guidance (Condition 4) resulted in a lower mean number of identified target objects compared to the proposed haptic method (Condition 1). The standard deviation for Condition 4 (1.4) was lower than for Condition 1 (3.0), Condition 2 (3.3), and Condition 3 (4.0), indicating less variability in performance with speech. The lower speed for speech guidance is attributed to the time required to process linguistic information into directional cues.

**VII. Demonstration in Immersive Micromanipulation System:**
- The demonstration successfully integrated the proposed directional sense presentation system into a mixed-reality-based immersive micromanipulation system.
- The system effectively guided the operator's gaze to virtual holding pipettes, microbeads, and injection pipettes during a simulated micromanipulation task. This confirmed the practical applicability of the proposed system in an actual immersive manipulation environment, demonstrating its ability to support tasks where operators may lose sight of critical objects.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a directional sense presentation system for immersive environments that utilizes McKibben-based surface haptic sensation applied to the torso, along with a novel air pressure adjustment method. The findings contribute to the field of human-computer interaction and wearable haptics by offering a non-visual, intuitive method for guiding user attention in complex virtual or mixed-reality spaces.

**Significance:**
- **Enhanced Intuition in Haptic Guidance:** The system leverages McKibben artificial muscles to induce intuitive torso movements (forward bending, backward bending, left/right twisting). This kinesthetic feedback is designed to be directly perceivable, unlike vibrotactile systems that often require pre-learning specific patterns, making the system more accessible for first-time users in 3D guidance tasks.
- **Effective Gaze Alignment:** The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed system, alongside other tested methods, can guide a user's gaze to within the central vision field (30-degree radius). This is critical for tasks requiring precise object identification and interaction.
- **Superior Perception Speed:** A key finding is the significantly higher speed of directional guidance achieved by the proposed method (surface haptic with adjustment) compared to both simpler haptic methods and even synthesized speech. This efficiency is attributed to the continuous feedback of surface haptic sensation, which, when combined with the air pressure adjustment algorithm, provides immediate cues for redirection, particularly when the user deviates from the target. The discussion suggests that the resistance felt during incorrect movements, enhanced by the adjustment algorithm, contributes to this rapid recognition.
- **Addressing Information Overload:** By providing directional cues without relying on visual information, the system mitigates the problem of visual information overload in immersive spaces, allowing users to maintain focus on their primary visual tasks.
- **Applicability in Complex Domains:** The successful demonstration in a mixed-reality micromanipulation system highlights the system's potential in demanding applications such as teleoperation, surgical guidance, and intricate assembly tasks, where precision and non-distracting guidance are crucial.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Improved User Experience and Efficiency:** The ability to intuitively and rapidly guide user gaze non-visually can dramatically enhance the user experience in various immersive applications. This could lead to faster task completion times and reduced cognitive load, particularly in professional fields that rely on VR/AR technologies.
- **Advancements in Teleoperation:** For remote operation of complex machinery, robotics, or medical instruments, the system could provide operators with essential directional awareness, reducing the likelihood of disorientation and increasing operational accuracy, as demonstrated in the micromanipulation scenario.
- **Foundation for Future Haptic Systems:** The novel air pressure adjustment method and the emphasis on whole-body kinesthetic feedback to the torso could serve as a foundation for developing more sophisticated and personalized haptic interfaces.
- **Broader Adaptability:** The concept of intuitive surface haptic guidance to the torso could be adapted for other immersive systems, navigation aids, training simulations, and accessibility tools, where spatial awareness is paramount.

**Future Research Directions:**
The authors identify several areas for future work, including the development of fabric actuators in various sizes to better accommodate individual body shapes, customizing the adjustment gain for each user to maximize performance, and extending the guidance algorithm to track and guide users towards moving targets. Furthermore, investigating the correlation between user familiarity with the wearable fabric actuator and the efficiency of directional guidance, and exploring motion guidance in other parts of the body, are also suggested.
