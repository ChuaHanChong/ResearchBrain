# ResT: Reshaping Token-Level Policy Gradients for Tool-Use Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> ResT introduces an entropy-aware mechanism to reshape token-level policy gradients for tool-use large language models, dynamically reweighting structured tool calls versus reasoning tokens during reinforcement learning. This approach improves training stability and achieves state-of-the-art performance on tool-use benchmarks, enabling smaller models to surpass larger commercial LLMs on specific tasks.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning for tool-use LLMs suffers from high policy-gradient variance due to sparse, multi-turn outcome rewards, hindering efficient credit assignment.
- Uniform reward application across all tokens in tool-use tasks dilutes the RL signal, especially for structured elements versus free-form reasoning tokens.
- Training multi-turn tool-use agents is computationally intensive and inefficient compared to other LLM training paradigms.

## Method

- Factorizing multi-turn tool use into single-turn steps, providing dense, ==process-level supervision== at each interaction.
- Proposing **ResT**, an entropy-aware mechanism that reshapes token-level policy gradients by reweighting tokens based on their region-level average entropy.
- Incorporating a curriculum learning strategy to dynamically adjust reweighting factors, prioritizing structural correctness early and reasoning tokens later in training.

## Results

- ResT achieved state-of-the-art results on tool-use benchmarks (BFCL and API-Bank), improving performance over baselines by up to **8.76%**.
- A **4B** Qwen3 model fine-tuned with ResT surpassed GPT-4o by **0.38%** in overall multi-turn tool-use tasks on BFCL, and by larger margins on specific sub-tasks.
- Training with ResT led to lower and less volatile policy entropy, indicating greater stability, and maintained longer, more complete reasoning sequences during learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Policy gradient variance is inversely related to token-level policy entropy, meaning low-entropy, structured tokens offer more stable gradient signals.
> - Reshaping token-level policy gradients with entropy-aware reweighting and a curriculum strategy stabilizes training by focusing on reward-critical tokens.
> - Adaptively shifting the learning focus, from structural correctness to semantic reasoning and parameter accuracy, optimizes the learning progression for tool-use LLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) has progressed beyond mere text generation, enabling them to function as goal-directed agents capable of interacting with and invoking external tools. This capability is critical for a wide range of domain-specific applications, from deep search to travel assistance. Reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a principled framework for optimizing these emergent tool-use policies in LLMs.

However, the prevailing RL paradigm for tool-use LLMs predominantly relies on sparse outcome rewards, typically assigned only after a multi-turn sequence of tool calls. This approach presents two primary challenges:
1. **Reward Noise and Weak Incentives**: Many practical tool-use scenarios admit multiple valid outputs, making outcome-only rewards noisy. This leads to high-variance gradients and provides insufficient incentives for the model to develop robust reasoning capabilities, even when augmented with LLM-as-a-judge or learned reward models.
2. **System Inefficiency**: Multi-turn tool-use tasks are computationally intensive due to per-sample interactions, concurrency control, and variable horizons, resulting in lower throughput and higher infrastructure costs compared to supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or single-turn RL.

Prior work in RL for LLMs has explored various techniques, including Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), and its variants like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), to enhance reasoning abilities. For tool-use agents specifically, research has investigated prompt engineering, SFT on curated datasets, and specialized architectures. RL formulations have also been applied, with methods like ToolRL, GIGPO, PVPO, and Agent Lightning aiming to improve multi-step tool use. ToolRL, for instance, validated the use of rule-based matching scores by decomposing multi-turn calls into single-turn tasks for more efficient RL training.

Despite these advances, a significant limitation persists: prior RL methods often overlook the unique characteristics of tool-use tasks at the token level. Rule-based rewards tend to concentrate on structured elements like format tags, tool names, and key parameters, while tokens related to general reasoning contribute less, particularly during early training stages. Uniformly applying rewards to all tokens dilutes the RL signal, exacerbating policy-gradient variance, especially for critic-free methods such as GRPO that lack token-level critics. This work directly addresses this gap by proposing a token-level policy gradient reshaping mechanism that accounts for the differential importance and entropy of various token types in tool-use scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the identified limitations of existing reinforcement learning approaches for optimizing tool-use in large language models. The central question guiding this work is whether a tool-use agent can be trained more effectively by providing specific, low-variance rewards at each turn, rather than relying solely on sparse outcome rewards.

The specific challenges that motivated this study include:
- **High Policy-Gradient Variance**: The use of sparse, coarse-grained outcome rewards in multi-turn tool-use tasks leads to high variance in policy gradients, which impedes efficient credit assignment and slows down learning. This is particularly problematic in complex tasks where the path to a correct solution involves multiple steps and varied token types.
- **Inefficient Training**: The noise and instability introduced by high-variance gradients contribute to inefficient training processes, requiring more samples and computational resources to achieve desired performance levels.
- **Lack of Granular Reward Signals**: Existing rule-based reward mechanisms, while an improvement over outcome-only rewards, often treat all tokens uniformly. This overlooks the fact that different token categories—such as structured tool calls (names, parameters, format) versus free-form reasoning (chain-of-thought)—have distinct roles and contribute differently to task success, particularly during different phases of training.
- **Suboptimal Learning Progression**: Without a mechanism to prioritize different aspects of tool use, models may not learn in an optimal sequence, such as first mastering structural correctness, then parameter accuracy, and finally complex reasoning.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of this paper are:
1. **Establish a Theoretical Foundation**: To theoretically and empirically demonstrate the relationship between token-level policy entropy and the stability of policy-gradient updates. This involves showing that lower average entropy correlates with reduced variance in gradients and identifying that structured, low-entropy tokens are primary determinants of task rewards.
2. **Propose an Entropy-Aware Gradient Reshaping Mechanism**: To introduce Reshaped Token-level policy gradients (ResT), an algorithm that reweights token-level policy gradients based on their region-level average entropy. This aims to provide dense and effective reward signals by emphasizing reward-critical tokens.
3. **Incorporate Curriculum Learning**: To integrate a lightweight curriculum learning strategy that adaptively adjusts token reweighting factors based on training progress. This scheme is designed to progressively upweight reasoning tokens as training proceeds, enabling a smooth shift from focusing on structural correctness and format compliance to semantic reasoning and parameter accuracy.
4. **Enhance Training Stability and Convergence**: To improve the stability of convergence in multi-turn tool-use tasks by mitigating policy-gradient variance.
5. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance**: To empirically validate the effectiveness and efficiency of ResT by achieving superior performance on established tool-use benchmarks compared to existing state-of-the-art methods and commercial LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Reshaped Token-level policy gradients (ResT), is designed to enhance the efficiency and stability of reinforcement learning for tool-use LLMs. It achieves this by factorizing multi-turn tool use into single-turn steps and introducing an entropy-aware mechanism to reshape policy gradients.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Motivation

ResT's design is grounded in a theoretical analysis linking policy entropy to training stability:
- **Policy Gradient Variance Decomposition (Lemma 1)**: This lemma establishes that the variance of the mini-batch policy gradient estimator is inversely proportional to the batch size and directly proportional to the variance of individual trajectory-level gradients.
- **Second-Order Moment and Entropy Connection (Lemma 2)**: This lemma shows that the variance of a single-trajectory gradient estimator depends on the squared advantage function and the norm of the per-step score (s_t), where s_t is related to the difference between the one-hot encoding of the sampled token and its probability distribution. Crucially, the expected squared norm of s_t is bounded by `1 - e^(-H_t)`, where `H_t` is the Shannon entropy of the token distribution at timestep `t`. This establishes that lower token-level entropy corresponds to a reduced variance contribution. This insight suggests that structured, low-entropy tokens (e.g., tool names, parameters) inherently have a more stable gradient signal.
- **Entropy-Aware Reweighting for Variance Reduction (Theorem 1 & 2)**: Based on this, ResT introduces token-level reweighting factors `˜w_t` for the policy gradient. Theorem 1 upper-bounds the variance of this reweighted estimator, showing it is minimized when `˜w_t` is inversely proportional to `β_t = E[||J_t||^2_F (1 - e^(-H_t))]`. This indicates that tokens with higher variance contributions (often higher entropy) should be down-weighted. Practically, `β_t` is approximated using entropy-only statistics (e.g., `1/(1 - e^(-H_avg))` or `1/H_avg`) within specific token regions. This theoretical foundation provides a principled basis for down-weighting high-entropy, open-ended tokens (like chain-of-thought) to improve training efficiency and stability.

#### 3.2. Algorithm Design

ResT integrates these theoretical insights into a practical algorithm:
- **Reward Score (Section 3.2.1)**: Following prior work, ResT employs a rule-based reward mechanism, decomposing the total reward into a format matching score and a tool-calling correctness score.
 - **Format Score (`S_i,format`)**: A binary score (0 or 1) indicating whether all required output fields are complete and in the correct order.
 - **Tool-Calling Correctness Score (`S_i,acc`)**: A normalized score combining Jaccard similarity for tool names (`r_name`), Jaccard similarity for parameter names (`r_para`), and exact matches for parameter values (`r_value`).
 - **Final Dynamic Scaling Reward (`R_i,final`)**: Calculated as a weighted sum of `S_i,acc` and `S_i,format`, dynamically scaled by `(1 - ν_bar)`, where `ν_bar` represents the training progress. This dynamic scaling implies a gradual shift in reward emphasis during training.

- **Reshaping Token-level Policy Gradients (Section 3.2.2)**:
 - **Advantage Calculation**: For each sampled response, an advantage function `ˆA_i` is computed by standardizing `R_i,final` within a mini-batch.
 - **Token Partitioning**: The generated response sequence is tokenized and partitioned into four distinct regions: format tags (`˜w_t,fmt`), tool names (`˜w_t,name`), tool parameters (`˜w_t,para`), and chain-of-thought (`˜w_t,thk`).
 - **Entropy-Based Initialization**: The reweighting factors `˜w_t` for each token are initialized using the entropy-based rule derived from the theoretical motivation (e.g., inversely proportional to average entropy in its region).
 - **Curriculum Learning**: ResT incorporates a dynamic curriculum that adjusts these weights based on the training progress `ν`:
 - **Tool Names**: Consistently assigned a high weight (`w_max`) due to their critical importance.
 - **Format Tags**: Weights are annealed (decreased) over training using `max(w_min, ˜w_t,fmt - α_f * ν)`. This prioritizes structural correctness early on.
 - **Tool Parameters & Chain-of-Thought**: Weights are gradually increased using `min(w_max, ˜w_t,para + α_p * ν)` and `min(w_max, ˜w_t,thk + α_t * ν)`. This shifts focus to semantic accuracy and reasoning as the model becomes structurally competent.
 - **Normalization**: The clipped and curriculum-adjusted weights `ˆw_t` are then normalized by their mean across the sequence to obtain `ω_t`. This ensures a redistribution of learning signals within each sample.
 - **Optimization Objective**: The final optimization objective `L_ResT(θ)` is a PPO-style clipped surrogate objective. It incorporates the `ω_t` reweighting factor for each token, allowing for differentiated gradient updates based on their importance and entropy.

#### 3.3. Training Details

- **Data Curation**: A 4k mixed corpus was curated from ToolACE (invocation decision), Hammer (robust grounding), and XLAM (compositional tasks) to train for invocation timing, robust grounding, and compositional planning.
- **Multi-turn to Single-step Decomposition**: Multi-turn dialogues were decomposed into single-step instances, where each step is conditioned on the full preceding conversation. This provides denser, more fine-grained, process-level supervision at each step, akin to the SWiRL framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of ResT involved comparisons against various baselines and commercial models on two established benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Baselines**: The method was compared against a Base Model (instruction-tuned), Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), TSFT (SFT with up-weighted loss on tool-call tokens), RSFT (SFT with up-weighted loss on reasoning tokens), GRPO (an RL-only method), and SFT+GRPO (SFT initialization followed by GRPO).
- **Benchmarks**:
 - **Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard (BFCL)**: Assesses multi-step tool use, including various complexities (missing functions, missing parameters, long context).
 - **API-Bank**: A three-tiered suite evaluating tool invocation in multi-turn dialogues, categorized by difficulty levels.
- **Base LLMs**: Open-source models from the Qwen3 family (1.7B, 4B-2507, 8B, 14B) and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct were fine-tuned. Comparisons were also made against commercial models such as GPT-4o, GPT-4.5-Preview, Grok-3-beta, DeepSeek-V3, DeepSeek-R1, and Gemini-2.5-Pro.

#### 4.2. Performance Results

- **State-of-the-Art Performance**: ResT fine-tuning consistently achieved state-of-the-art results across both the BFCL and API-Bank benchmarks when applied to the Qwen3 model family.
- **Improvements over Baselines**:
 - On BFCL, ResT demonstrated performance gains of up to 8.76% over the GRPO baseline.
 - On API-Bank, ResT showed improvements of up to 3.02% over GRPO under identical data budgets.
- **Comparison with Commercial LLMs**: The Qwen3-4B-2507 model fine-tuned with ResT notably surpassed GPT-4o by 0.38% in overall multi-turn tool-use tasks on BFCL. More specifically, it outperformed GPT-4o by 1.50% on multi-turn base tasks and by 4.11% on single-turn tool-use tasks.
- **Generalization**: The ResT-trained model exhibited robust generalization capabilities, performing comparably to baseline models on single-turn tasks while showing superior performance on real-time updated tasks (Table 6).
- **Model Agnostic Applicability**: The effectiveness of ResT was also verified when fine-tuned on the Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct model (Table 4, Table 5), indicating that the benefits of the approach are not confined to a specific model architecture or family.

#### 4.3. Training Dynamics Analysis

- **Policy Entropy (Figure 3)**: During training, ResT's policy entropy converged to approximately 0.02, which was lower and exhibited less volatility compared to GRPO's convergence at around 0.04. This indicates that ResT leads to a more deterministic and stable policy.
- **Reward Performance**: Despite the lower entropy, both ResT and GRPO achieved similar final mean reward values, suggesting ResT maintains performance while increasing stability.
- **Response Length**: ResT maintained longer average response lengths (mean > 300 tokens) throughout training, whereas GRPO's average response length collapsed to approximately 125 tokens. This suggests that ResT fosters more complete and complex reasoning sequences without sacrificing reward.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

Ablation studies conducted on various base models (Table 3) confirmed the necessity of each component of ResT for achieving peak performance:
- **Dynamic Rewards**: Disabling the dynamic reward scaling component resulted in a significant accuracy reduction, up to 6.54% on Qwen3-8B.
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Policy Gradients**: Removing the gradient updates for chain-of-thought tokens led to a decline of up to 4.36% in accuracy on Qwen3-8B.
- **Curriculum Learning**: Training without the curriculum-based adaptive weighting of token regions resulted in an accuracy reduction of up to 4.86% on Qwen3-8B.

These results indicate that the dynamic reward scaling, the emphasis on reasoning tokens (CoT), and the progressive curriculum learning mechanism are complementary and contribute to ResT's robustness and overall performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of large language models for tool use, particularly in the domain of reinforcement learning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Theoretical Foundation for RL in Tool Use**: The work establishes a theoretical and empirical link between token-level policy entropy and the stability of policy gradient updates. This insight—demonstrating that lower entropy corresponds to reduced variance and that structured, low-entropy tokens are primary determinants of rewards—provides a principled understanding for optimizing tool-use LLMs. This theoretical grounding can inform future research in designing more stable and efficient RL algorithms for complex generative tasks.

2. **Methodological Innovation for Robust Training**: The proposed ResT algorithm introduces an entropy-aware, token-level gradient reshaping mechanism combined with a curriculum learning approach. This method directly addresses the critical challenges of high policy-gradient variance and inefficient credit assignment inherent in existing RL paradigms for tool use. By dynamically reweighting different token categories (structured tool calls vs. reasoning tokens) and progressively shifting the learning focus, ResT offers a robust and stable training framework.

3. **Enhanced Performance and Efficiency**: ResT achieves state-of-the-art results on prominent tool-use benchmarks (BFCL and API-Bank), outperforming prior methods by substantial margins. The ability of a 4B base LLM fine-tuned with ResT to surpass larger, proprietary models like GPT-4o on specific multi-turn and single-turn tasks indicates a significant leap in performance and practical efficiency. This suggests that focused algorithmic improvements can unlock advanced capabilities in smaller, more accessible models.

4. **Advancement Towards Agentic AI**: By fostering a smoother transition from structural correctness to semantic reasoning, ResT contributes to the development of more capable and "agentic" LLMs. The observed emergent proactivity and metacognition, alongside improved efficiency and reduced hallucinations, are indicative of progress towards foundational agentic intelligence in LLMs. Such models are better equipped to handle complex, real-world tasks requiring multi-step planning and dynamic tool orchestration.

5. **Broad Applicability and Reproducibility**: The demonstration of ResT's effectiveness across different base LLM sizes (Qwen3 family and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct) suggests its model-agnostic nature. This broad applicability increases the potential for wider adoption and impact within the research community and industry. The detailed methodology and theoretical proofs also contribute to the reproducibility and further development of this approach.

6. **Guidance for Future Research**: The paper opens several avenues for future work, including the integration of verifiable structural rewards with judge-based semantic scores, learning schedules for entropy-aware mixing, and calibrating LLM-as-a-judge with uncertainty estimates. These directions indicate a clear path for building upon the successes of ResT to create even more sophisticated and reliable tool-using LLM agents.
