# ResT: Reshaping Token-Level Policy Gradients for Tool-Use Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> ResT introduces an entropy-aware mechanism to reshape token-level policy gradients for tool-use large language models, dynamically reweighting structured tool calls versus reasoning tokens during reinforcement learning. This approach improves training stability and achieves state-of-the-art performance on tool-use benchmarks, enabling smaller models to surpass larger commercial LLMs on specific tasks.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning for tool-use LLMs suffers from high policy-gradient variance due to sparse, multi-turn outcome rewards, hindering efficient credit assignment.
- Uniform reward application across all tokens in tool-use tasks dilutes the RL signal, especially for structured elements versus free-form reasoning tokens.
- Training multi-turn tool-use agents is computationally intensive and inefficient compared to other LLM training paradigms.

## Method

- Factorizing multi-turn tool use into single-turn steps, providing dense, ==process-level supervision== at each interaction.
- Proposing **ResT**, an entropy-aware mechanism that reshapes token-level policy gradients by reweighting tokens based on their region-level average entropy.
- Incorporating a curriculum learning strategy to dynamically adjust reweighting factors, prioritizing structural correctness early and reasoning tokens later in training.

## Results

- ResT achieved state-of-the-art results on tool-use benchmarks (BFCL and API-Bank), improving performance over baselines by up to **8.76%**.
- A **4B** Qwen3 model fine-tuned with ResT surpassed GPT-4o by **0.38%** in overall multi-turn tool-use tasks on BFCL, and by larger margins on specific sub-tasks.
- Training with ResT led to lower and less volatile policy entropy, indicating greater stability, and maintained longer, more complete reasoning sequences during learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Policy gradient variance is inversely related to token-level policy entropy, meaning low-entropy, structured tokens offer more stable gradient signals.
> - Reshaping token-level policy gradients with entropy-aware reweighting and a curriculum strategy stabilizes training by focusing on reward-critical tokens.
> - Adaptively shifting the learning focus, from structural correctness to semantic reasoning and parameter accuracy, optimizes the learning progression for tool-use LLMs.
