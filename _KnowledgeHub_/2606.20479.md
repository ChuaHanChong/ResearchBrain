# GroundControl: Anticipating Navigation Failures in Vision-Language Agents via Trajectory-Consistent Uncertainty Estimates

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UIC developed GroundControl, a framework that anticipates navigation failures in vision-language agents by estimating trajectory-consistent uncertainty based on goal-directed distance dynamics. It achieves near-optimal performance in identifying failing and inefficient navigation episodes across various models and challenging scenarios.

## Problem

- Vision-language navigation (VLN) agents lack mechanisms to anticipate emergent failures (e.g., oscillation, stagnation, detours) during execution, hindering real-world deployment.
- Existing uncertainty estimation methods, often based on instantaneous signals like action entropy, do not effectively capture trajectory-level consistency or overall goal-directed progress.
- Current evaluation protocols for embodied agents primarily assess task outcome post-episode termination, offering limited insight into the agent's ability to identify potential failures during ongoing execution.

## Method

- Introduces "**GroundControl**," a lightweight, interpretable uncertainty estimator that models the distance-to-goal evolution using a ==constant-velocity Kalman filter==.
- Integrates Kalman filter innovation statistics and posterior covariance with complementary trajectory features (progress, monotonicity, path efficiency, oscillatory behavior) to form a comprehensive episode-level uncertainty score.
- Proposes "Selective Risk–Coverage Navigation (SRCN)," a formal protocol to evaluate episode-level uncertainty ranking quality using risk–coverage curves, AURC, and E-AURC, independently of the navigation policy.

## Results

- GroundControl consistently achieved the lowest or tied-lowest AURC_SR and E-AURC_SR across all five EB-Navigation splits and three VLM backbones (GPT-4o, GPT-5-mini, Gemini-**1.5**-Flash), indicating near-optimal failure detection.
- The framework demonstrated superior performance in SPL-based selective risk (AURC_SPL and E-AURC_SPL), effectively identifying inefficient navigation trajectories in addition to outright failures.
- Uncertainty score distributions generated by GroundControl showed clear separation between successful and failing episodes, enabling reliable identification of deteriorating executions, unlike baselines that exhibited substantial overlap.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Trajectory-level consistency, particularly the dynamics of distance-to-goal and deviations from expected progress, provides a robust signal for anticipating navigation failures and inefficiencies, outperforming instantaneous confidence measures.
> - A combination of model-based dynamics (Kalman filter) and geometric/behavioral trajectory features yields a comprehensive and interpretable uncertainty estimate that aligns with both categorical success/failure and continuous efficiency metrics.
> - A dedicated selective prediction framework (SRCN) is necessary to rigorously evaluate an uncertainty estimator's ability to rank episodes by true loss, providing a standardized metric for assessing its practical utility in abstention strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language navigation (VLN) agents have shown progress in benchmark tasks such as Room-to-Room (R2R) and Habitat-based environments. These agents combine language understanding with visual grounding and discrete motion policies, often leveraging large pretrained vision-language models (VLMs) to achieve competitive performance in metrics like Success Rate and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL). However, current evaluation protocols primarily assess task outcome post-episode termination, offering limited insight into the agent's ability to anticipate or identify potential failures during ongoing execution.

The broader research landscape for embodied agents includes efforts to address uncertainty in sequential decision-making. Existing approaches typically rely on instantaneous signals, such as predictive entropy over action distributions, logit margins, or heuristics from reasoning traces. While these measures can capture ambiguity in individual decisions or provide state-wise prediction accuracy, they often do not reflect the consistency of goal-directed dynamics over an extended trajectory. Consequently, an agent might exhibit high confidence in its immediate actions even while its overall trajectory is leading to a failure state, such as oscillation, stagnation, or inefficient detours.

Previous work on failure prediction for embodied agents has explored auxiliary classifiers, trajectory statistics (e.g., episode length), or heuristic signals like invalid-action rates. These methods can be architecture-specific or implicitly rely on dataset characteristics. In contrast, this paper proposes a more general, model-agnostic approach that focuses on geometric observables, specifically the distance-to-goal signal, as a core indicator of navigation progress dynamics.

Furthermore, the concept of selective prediction and risk-coverage analysis, which allows models to abstain from high-uncertainty inputs to reduce risk, is widely adopted in classification tasks. However, its application to embodied navigation, where uncertainty must be aggregated over temporally correlated trajectories rather than individual states, has been less developed. This research adapts and formalizes a selective evaluation framework tailored for episode-level uncertainty in navigation.

The "GroundControl" framework addresses these gaps by proposing a trajectory-level uncertainty estimator that models the temporal evolution of goal-directed progress. It aims to move beyond instantaneous confidence signals to detect emergent failure dynamics, thus contributing to the reliability and interpretability of VLN systems during deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a recognized gap in the reliability and introspection capabilities of current vision-language navigation (VLN) agents. While these agents achieve competitive average success on benchmark tasks, their real-world deployment is hindered by the lack of mechanisms to anticipate emergent failures during execution. Failures in navigation often manifest not as abrupt, unpredictable events, but as gradual trajectory-level breakdowns such as sustained oscillation, prolonged stagnation, or inefficient detours that lead to excessive path length. These behaviors are reflected in the temporal evolution of the agent's distance-to-goal and its action trajectory.

Existing uncertainty proxies, such as predictive entropy over action distributions or token-level confidence, are considered insufficient for this context. These measures typically capture instantaneous decision ambiguity but do not provide an integrated signal of whether the overall trajectory remains consistent with successful goal-directed progress. An agent might consistently make individually "confident" decisions that collectively lead to a sub-optimal or failing trajectory. Therefore, there is a need for uncertainty signals that reflect the trajectory-level consistency of goal-directed dynamics.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Formalize Trajectory-Level Consistency for Uncertainty Estimation:** To establish a conceptual framework where uncertainty in embodied navigation is understood as a statistical deviation from expected, goal-directed distance dynamics. This involves defining what constitutes "nominal" goal-directed progress and identifying systematic violations of this structure as quantitative evidence of deviation.

2. **Develop a Trajectory-Consistent Uncertainty Estimator (GroundControl):** To design and implement "GroundControl," a lightweight and interpretable estimator. This estimator aims to detect statistically significant deviations from nominal goal-directed motion by modeling the distance-to-goal evolution using a constant-velocity Kalman filter. It further integrates innovation statistics and posterior covariance growth with complementary trajectory features that capture aspects like progress, monotonicity, path efficiency, and oscillatory behavior. The goal is for the resulting uncertainty score to reflect geometric and temporal inconsistency across an entire episode, rather than just local prediction dispersion.

3. **Introduce a Robust Evaluation Protocol for Episode-Level Uncertainty (SRCN):** To formalize "Selective Risk–Coverage Navigation (SRCN)," a protocol designed to evaluate the quality of episode-level uncertainty signals independently of raw task success. This protocol measures the effectiveness of an uncertainty score in ranking navigation episodes by failure or inefficiency using risk–coverage curves and summary metrics like Area Under the Risk–Coverage curve (AURC) and Excess-AURC (E-AURC). This aims to isolate the ranking quality of uncertainty estimators without modifying the underlying navigation policy.

4. **Demonstrate the Efficacy of Trajectory-Consistent Uncertainty:** To experimentally validate that the proposed GroundControl framework, by modeling deviation from goal-directed dynamics, provides a more interpretable and robust signal for anticipating navigation failures compared to conventional entropy-based, conformal, and heuristic baselines across various navigation scenarios and VLM backbones.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on defining and evaluating trajectory-consistent uncertainty through a novel estimator, GroundControl, and a dedicated evaluation protocol, SRCN.

**A. Task Setup and Standard Navigation Metrics**
The research considers standard vision-language navigation (VLN) episodes, where an agent, given an initial pose and a natural language instruction, navigates to a target. At each timestep, the agent receives an egocentric observation and selects a discrete action from a fixed set (e.g., forward, turn left).
Episode success (Succ_i ∈ {0, 1}) is determined by reaching the target within a 1-meter threshold. Standard metrics like Success Rate and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL_i) are used, which evaluate task completion and efficiency only upon episode termination. This work introduces complementary measures for uncertainty assessment. For each episode, an uncertainty estimator produces a scalar score U_i, with lower values indicating higher confidence. This abstraction allows for evaluating diverse uncertainty estimators within a unified framework.

**B. Selective Risk–Coverage Navigation (SRCN)**
SRCN is formalized as an evaluation protocol for episode-level uncertainty ranking. It quantifies how effectively uncertainty scores {U_i} order episodes by their true loss (failure or inefficiency) without altering the navigation policy.
- **Coverage (Cov(τ)):** The fraction of episodes retained when only those with uncertainty U_i ≤ τ are considered.
- **Selective Risk (Risk_SR(τ) and Risk_SPL(τ)):**
 - For success-based evaluation, loss is ℓ_SR_i = 1 − Succ_i. Risk_SR(τ) is the empirical failure rate among retained episodes.
 - For SPL-based evaluation, loss is ℓ_SPL_i = 1 − SPL_i. Risk_SPL(τ) penalizes both failures and inefficient successful trajectories among retained episodes.
- **Risk–Coverage Curve and AURC:** Varying τ generates a risk–coverage curve. The Area Under the Risk–Coverage curve (AURC) summarizes the trade-off, with lower AURC indicating better ranking quality.
- **Excess-AURC (E-AURC):** Normalizes AURC by subtracting the oracle lower bound (AURC*), obtained by sorting episodes by true loss. E-AURC = 0 signifies perfect ordering.
- **Calibration:** Expected Calibration Error (ECE) is also reported when calibrated failure probabilities are available, though less central to GroundControl itself.

**C. GroundControl: Uncertainty via Distance Dynamics**
GroundControl estimates trajectory-consistent uncertainty by modeling the distance-to-goal (d_t) evolution and detecting deviations from expected goal-directed progress.
- **Constant-Velocity Kalman Filter (KF):** A linear-Gaussian Kalman filter is used to model the scalar distance trajectory. The state is defined as x_t = [d_t, d_dot_t]^T (distance and its instantaneous rate of change). The filter uses constant-velocity dynamics to predict nominal, smooth, decreasing distance. Standard Kalman prediction and update equations yield an innovation ν_t (discrepancy between observed and predicted distance) and its covariance S_t. The normalized innovation squared (ν_t^2 / S_t) serves as a statistical consistency test.
- **Per-Step Uncertainty Score (H_t):** This score aggregates three indicators of trajectory inconsistency:
 1. **Posterior Belief Dispersion:** `log det(Σ_t|t + εI)` from the Kalman filter, measuring uncertainty in local distance dynamics.
 2. **Action-Level Instability:** `H(α_t)`, the Bernoulli entropy of the policy's confidence (softmax probability) in the executed action, indicating indecision.
 3. **Statistical Deviation:** `ν_t^2 / S_t`, the normalized innovation squared from the Kalman filter, indicating deviation from nominal distance dynamics.
 Fixed weights (λ1=1.0, λ2=0.5, λ3=0.8) are used for these terms.
- **Multi-Signal Episode Uncertainty (U_i):**
 - A Kalman-based score (˜s_skf_i) is derived by taking the maximum H_t over the episode and applying a sigmoid function. This captures peak dynamical inconsistency.
 - Four complementary trajectory-level features, each normalized to and indicating deviation:
 1. **Normalized Progress (f_prog_i):** Measures insufficient net reduction in distance.
 2. **Monotonicity (f_mono_i):** Penalizes frequent increases in distance.
 3. **Path Efficiency (f_eff_i):** Detects inefficient detours relative to displacement.
 4. **Oscillatory Behavior (f_osc_i):** Captures repeated action reversals.
 - The final episode-level uncertainty score U_i is a fixed linear fusion of ˜s_skf_i and these four features, using predefined weights (w_skf=0.30, w_prog=0.25, w_mono=0.20, w_eff=0.15, w_osc=0.10).

**D. Baselines**
Seven representative baselines are compared, spanning conformal, entropy-based, trajectory-based, and heuristic signals:
- **Conformal (ACI):** Online adaptive conformal inference using step-wise nonconformity based on distance residuals.
- **Predictive Entropy:** Normalized Shannon entropy of the episode's action histogram.
- **Self-Consistency:** Plan instability measured by the mean Jaccard similarity of consecutive executable plans extracted from VLM reasoning.
- **Invalid-action rate:** The fraction of steps where the environment rejects the agent's executed action.
- **Random:** Uniformly distributed random scores, serving as a lower bound for ranking performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GroundControl and baselines was conducted on the EB-Navigation benchmark, comprising five distinct splits (base, common sense, complex instruction, visual appearance, and long horizon), each with 60 episodes, totaling 300 episodes. The navigation policies were fixed and based on three VLM backbones: GPT-4o, GPT-5-mini, and Gemini-1.5-Flash. All uncertainty metrics were evaluated post-hoc on pre-recorded trajectories.

**A. Standard Navigation Performance (Table I)**
The baseline navigation performance, measured by Success Rate and SPL, varied across models and splits. For GPT-4o, success rates were above 53% on four splits, but significantly lower (16.7%) on the "long horizon" split, indicating increased task difficulty due to compounding errors over longer execution chains. GPT-5-mini achieved higher overall success but similarly struggled with the "long horizon" split. This particular split served as a rigorous test for uncertainty ranking due to its inherent difficulty.

**B. SRCN Summary Metrics (Table II and Table III)**
- **Success-Based Selective Risk (Table II):** GroundControl consistently achieved the lowest or tied-lowest AURC_SR and E-AURC_SR across all five EB-Navigation splits and for all three VLM backbones. Its excess risk (E-AURC_SR) was 0.00 on four splits and 0.01 on the "long horizon" split, indicating a near-optimal ordering of failure episodes. In contrast, baselines such as Entropy, Conformal, and Self-Consistency exhibited substantially higher selective risk, suggesting weaker differentiation between successful and failing trajectories. The performance advantage of GroundControl was particularly notable on the "long horizon" split, where baselines often approached random ordering, while GroundControl maintained near-oracle performance. This suggests that trajectory-level geometric progress dynamics provide a more robust signal for detecting navigation failures over extended temporal horizons compared to instantaneous action dispersion.

- **SPL-Based Selective Risk (Table III):** For SPL-based selective risk, which accounts for navigation inefficiency in addition to outright failures, GroundControl again achieved the lowest or near-lowest AURC_SPL and E-AURC_SPL across most splits and models. For GPT-4o, GroundControl demonstrated the best average performance across all splits, with an average AURC_SPL of 0.29 and E-AURC_SPL of 0.01. Similar trends were observed for GPT-5-mini and Gemini-1.5-Flash, indicating strong cross-model generalization. While entropy occasionally showed competitive performance on some GPT-5-mini splits, its efficacy diminished on more challenging scenarios like "long horizon" tasks. Conformal, Self-Consistency, Invalid-action, and Random baselines generally yielded significantly higher AURC and E-AURC values, suggesting a weaker ability to rank trajectories by navigation efficiency.

**C. Risk–Coverage Curves and Diagnostic Plots (Figures 2, 3, 4, 5)**
- **Distribution of Uncertainty (Figure 2):** GroundControl consistently produced a clear separation in the uncertainty score distributions. Failing episodes were shifted towards higher U_i values with limited overlap with successful episodes, particularly evident on the "long horizon" split. This separation implies that deteriorating executions can be identified from trajectory evolution, enabling potential runtime intervention. Baselines like Entropy and Conformal showed substantial overlap between success and failure distributions, indicating poor discriminative power.
- **Success-Based Risk–Coverage Curves (Figure 3):** GroundControl's risk–coverage curve closely tracked the oracle boundary. This indicates that failing trajectories are reliably assigned higher uncertainty and would be removed early if the system were to abstain from high-risk episodes. Baselines exhibited elevated risk at intermediate coverage levels, reflecting their weaker capacity to isolate failing executions.
- **SPL-Based Risk–Coverage Curves (Figure 4):** Under SPL-based loss, GroundControl maintained low selective risk across various coverage levels. This demonstrates its sensitivity to gradual degradations in navigation efficiency, such as wandering or oscillatory motion, which often precede outright failures.
- **Uncertainty vs. SPL (Figure 5):** The proposed estimator exhibited a strong negative correlation between U_i and SPL_i. Low-efficiency or failing episodes consistently mapped to higher uncertainty, while efficient trajectories concentrated at low U_i values. This alignment suggests that GroundControl effectively captures both categorical (success/failure) and continuous (efficiency) aspects of navigation quality. Baselines, in contrast, displayed substantially weaker monotonic correlation.

In summary, the results indicate that GroundControl's trajectory-consistent uncertainty estimates provide a robust and interpretable signal for identifying navigation failures and inefficiencies, significantly outperforming existing baselines across various models and challenging navigation scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "GroundControl" framework and its accompanying evaluation protocol, SRCN, present several contributions that could influence the development and deployment of vision-language navigation (VLN) agents and embodied AI systems.

One primary significance is the shift in focus for uncertainty estimation from instantaneous action-level ambiguity to **trajectory-level consistency of goal-directed dynamics.** This approach acknowledges that critical failures in embodied navigation often manifest as gradual deviations from an optimal path rather than single missteps. By providing a signal that anticipates these emergent failure dynamics—such as oscillation, stagnation, or inefficient detours—GroundControl offers a mechanism for more proactive and reliable agent behavior.

The introduction of **GroundControl as a lightweight, interpretable, and trajectory-consistent uncertainty estimator** addresses a practical need for runtime introspection. Its design, which combines a constant-velocity Kalman filter for distance-to-goal dynamics with complementary geometric and behavioral trajectory features, provides a multifaceted assessment of execution quality. This interpretability is crucial for developers and users to understand *why* an agent is exhibiting high uncertainty, thereby facilitating debugging, recovery strategies, or informed human intervention. The reported near-oracle ordering for failure detection and strong performance in identifying inefficient trajectories suggests a robust capability that could enhance the safety and efficiency of robotic systems in complex, unstructured environments.

Furthermore, the formalization of **Selective Risk–Coverage Navigation (SRCN)** contributes a standardized and rigorous method for evaluating episode-level uncertainty in embodied agents. By detaching uncertainty evaluation from direct policy modification and focusing on ranking quality through risk–coverage curves and E-AURC, SRCN enables fair and comprehensive comparisons across diverse uncertainty estimation techniques. This protocol can serve as a valuable tool for researchers to benchmark new uncertainty methods, fostering advancements in the field.

The potential impact of this work is broad. In **robotics and autonomous systems**, anticipating failures during execution is critical for safety and mission success. GroundControl could enable robots to detect when they are straying from their intended course or becoming inefficient, allowing for early recovery actions, replanning, or signaling for human assistance. This could lead to more robust navigation systems in applications such as automated warehousing, autonomous delivery, or exploration in unknown territories.

Beyond immediate applications, this research encourages a **re-evaluation of how agent performance is measured and managed.** By highlighting the limitations of purely post-hoc success metrics and instantaneous uncertainty proxies, it advocates for a more holistic view of agent reliability that incorporates continuous monitoring of trajectory integrity. This paradigm shift could influence future research directions in embodied AI, driving the development of agents that are not only capable of achieving tasks but also aware of their own operational state and potential for failure.
