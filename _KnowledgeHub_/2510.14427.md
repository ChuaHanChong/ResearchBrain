# Deep Compositional Phase Diffusion for Long Motion Sequence Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called Deep Compositional Phase Diffusion generates extended human motion sequences that maintain semantic alignment and smooth transitions by modeling motion in a periodic latent frequency domain. The method outperforms baselines on realism and text alignment metrics across compositional generation, long-term sequencing, and motion inbetweening tasks.

## Problem

- Current models struggle to maintain continuity and naturalness at transition boundaries when concatenating multiple semantically defined motion clips, leading to abrupt artifacts.
- Existing phase-based motion models often rely on fixed-length convolution schemes, which are unstable for variable-length motions and can lose critical semantic and transitional information.
- Generating long motion sequences composed of hundreds or thousands of segments poses scalability challenges, as sequential generation methods can accumulate errors and parallel methods struggle with global coherence.

## Method

- The **Compositional Phase Diffusion** framework simultaneously generates multiple ==motion clips== from sequential semantic instructions, ensuring smooth transitions by operating within a latent motion frequency domain.
- An Action-Centric Periodic Autoencoder (ACT-PAE) encodes variable-length motion segments into a unified, periodic latent space, capturing intrinsic phase dynamics without fixed-window slicing.
- Semantic Phase Diffusion (SPDM) and Transitional Phase Diffusion (TPDM) modules denoise phase parameters to align motions with text conditions and adjacent segments, respectively, combining predictions via a Phase Mixing Equation.

## Results

- Achieved the best overall FID (1.807) and MMD (4.711) scores on compositional motion pair generation, demonstrating superior realism and text alignment compared to baseline methods.
- Showed competitive performance in long-term motion generation on an extended sequence (302,298 frames, 168 minutes), obtaining the best overall FID (**0.909**), indicating improved transition-aware motion quality.
- Outperformed baselines across all evaluated metrics (L2-Vel, L2-Rot6D, NPSS, RMS-Jerk) in both unconditional and conditional motion inbetweening tasks, generating smoother and more realistic intermediate motions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing variable-length motion segments in a unified, periodic latent phase space allows for holistic processing, preserving complete semantic and transition-aware information.
> - Explicitly modeling and integrating motion phase information throughout a diffusion process effectively addresses both semantic alignment and kinematic continuity at segment boundaries.
> - The parallel application of semantic and transitional guidance through distinct diffusion modules, combined with a phase mixing mechanism, enables the generation of long-term, coherent motion sequences with global consistency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human motion generation, particularly using deep learning, has demonstrated progress in synthesizing semantically aligned motion sequences. These advancements have implications for virtual humanoid animation and robotics applications. Recent models, such as MDM, MLD, and MotionDiffuse, are capable of generating variable-length motion segments based on singular semantic prompts, often in a text-to-motion paradigm.

However, a recurring challenge in this domain arises when these models are tasked with generating long, composite motion sequences that involve multiple semantic instructions. Current approaches often face difficulties in maintaining continuity and naturalness at the transition boundaries between individual semantically defined motion clips. This can result in abrupt artifacts, dislocated poses, or a lack of smooth kinematic flow, which diminishes the realism of the overall sequence.

The emergence of datasets like BABEL-TEACH, which provides annotations for temporally connected motion segment pairs, has driven efforts towards addressing long-term compositional generation. Methods such as priorMDM have attempted to leverage learned transition knowledge to synthesize bridging segments that smooth out pose discrepancies between independently generated clips. Nevertheless, these existing techniques frequently overlook the intrinsic kinematic properties of each motion segment during the transition process. This can lead to undesirable outcomes such as over-smoothing, where distinct motion characteristics are lost, or abrupt stops that disrupt the flow, failing to capture the dynamic essence of a natural transition.

This work positions itself to address this specific limitation by introducing a framework that explicitly models and integrates motion phase information throughout the generation process. By operating in a latent motion frequency domain and progressively incorporating semantic and transitional guidance, it aims to produce composite motion sequences that not only adhere to semantic instructions but also maintain continuous and natural dynamics across segment boundaries, thereby improving upon the quality and realism of long-term and compositional motion generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust framework capable of generating high-quality composite motion sequences that exhibit both semantic alignment with input conditions and continuous, smooth transitions between different motion segments.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges observed in existing motion generation models when applied to complex, multi-segment scenarios:

- **Preserving Motion Continuity at Transition Boundaries:** Current models frequently struggle to maintain the natural flow and dynamics of motion when concatenating multiple semantically generated clips. This often leads to "awkward transitions and abrupt artifacts" that compromise the overall realism and visual plausibility of the animation. The authors are motivated to address this issue by incorporating explicit mechanisms for managing transitional dynamics.
- **Effective Handling of Variable-Length Motions:** Many existing phase-based motion models rely on fixed-length convolution schemes, which can be unstable when encoding variable-length motion segments. This necessitates arbitrary slicing or padding, potentially losing critical semantic and transitional information. A key objective is to process variable-length motions holistically to preserve comprehensive phase information.
- **Scalability for Long-Term Motion Generation:** Generating extended motion sequences composed of hundreds or thousands of segments poses significant challenges for models primarily designed for shorter clips. Existing sequential generation methods can accumulate errors, while parallel methods often struggle with global coherence. The research aims to develop a scalable framework that can efficiently generate long sequences while maintaining global consistency.
- **Facilitating Motion Inbetweening Tasks:** Motion inbetweening, the process of generating intermediate frames or segments to bridge two existing motions, is a related but distinct task where smooth, context-aware transitions are crucial. The authors aim for their framework to be adaptable to this application, either unconditionally or conditionally with semantic guidance.
- **Enhancing Realism and Application Potential:** By overcoming the aforementioned limitations, the work seeks to unlock the full potential of deep learning-based human motion generation for practical applications in virtual reality, character animation in gaming and film, and the development of more sophisticated humanoid robotics. The goal is to move beyond generating isolated, semantically distinct actions to creating coherent, extended narratives of movement.

To achieve these objectives, the authors propose leveraging a latent motion frequency domain and designing specific modules that progressively integrate semantic and phase details from adjacent motion clips into a diffusion process, thereby ensuring both accurate semantic representation and smooth kinematic transitions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is centered on the **Compositional Phase Diffusion** framework, which is designed to simultaneously generate multiple motion clips from sequential semantic instructions while ensuring smooth transitions. The framework operates within a latent motion frequency domain established by an autoencoder and utilizes diffusion modules guided by semantic and transitional information.

The framework consists of three main components:

1. **Action-Centric Periodic Autoencoder (ACT-PAE):**
 - **Purpose:** To encode variable-length motion segments into a unified, periodic latent space, capturing their intrinsic phase dynamics.
 - **Mechanism:** ACT-PAE is built upon a transformer-based motion autoencoder architecture. Unlike traditional Periodic Autoencoders (PAEs) that use convolution and FFT, ACT-PAE's encoder directly processes an input motion $X \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times E}$ (N frames, E features) using a transformer to predict four phase parameters: Frequency (F), Amplitude (A), Offset (B), and Phase Shift (S). These parameters, $P = [F, A, B, S]$, are then parameterized into a periodic signal $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times Q}$ using the equation: $Q = A \sin(F \cdot (T - S)) + B$. Here, T represents the time difference of each frame relative to the motion segment's center. The decoder then reconstructs the motion $\hat{X}$ from Q.
 - **Key Innovations:** ACT-PAE handles variable-length motion input, eliminating the need for fixed-window motion slicing and thus preserving complete semantic and transition-aware phase information. Adjustments include a "Composite Positional Embedding" (Comp-PE) that provides positional signals relative to the start, middle, and end of the motion, and a "mixed linear parameterization" (mixT) for the time window T, combining normalized progression and actual time duration, to better accommodate variable lengths and transitional segments.
 - **Training:** The entire ACT-PAE is trained using an L2 loss to reconstruct the input motion.

2. **Semantic Phase Diffusion Module (SPDM):**
 - **Purpose:** To denoise phase parameters, aligning the corresponding decoded motion segment with a given semantic condition.
 - **Mechanism:** In text-to-motion scenarios, SPDM uses a pre-trained CLIP-ViT-B/32 text encoder to obtain an embedding vector $C_p$ from the input text condition. This embedding guides the denoising process of the phase parameters $P_k^p$ (noise at step k for preceding motion) for the semantically conditioned motion $X_p$. SPDM utilizes both param-level tokens (F, A, B, S) and frame-level tokens (the periodic signal Q) for input. A self-attention transformer processes these tokens to predict semantically denoised parameters $P_0^{pc}$ (clean prediction).
 - **Training:** SPDM is trained as an $\epsilon$-model using an $\ell_1$ loss to predict the diffusion noise based on semantically annotated motion segments and their text conditions from the BABEL-TEACH dataset.

3. **Transitional Phase Diffusion Module (TPDM):**
 - **Purpose:** To denoise phase parameters, ensuring the resulting decoded motions are transitionally aligned with adjacent motions.
 - **Mechanism:** TPDM leverages clean phase parameters from adjacent motion segments (e.g., $P_0^p$ from the preceding motion) to denoise the phase parameters of the transitioning motion $P_k^t$. Similar to SPDM, it uses both param-level and frame-level tokens. A cross-attention transformer processes the current motion's noisy phase parameters and the adjacent motion's clean phase parameters to predict phase noise $P_0^{tf}$ (forward-conditioned clean prediction). Two variants, TPDM_f (forward, for preceding motion) and TPDM_b (backward, for succeeding motion), ensure dynamic coherence in both temporal directions.
 - **Training:** TPDM is also trained as an $\epsilon$-model using an $\ell_1$ loss to predict diffusion noise, based on transitional segment pairs derived from the BABEL-TEACH dataset, conditioning on neighboring clean phase information.

**Application Scenarios:**

- **Compositional Motion Pair Generation:** The framework simultaneously generates two connected semantic motion segments ($X_p$, $X_s$) and an intermediate transition segment ($X_t$). During the denoising process, SPDMs denoise $X_p$ and $X_s$ based on text, while TPDMs denoise $X_p$, $X_t$, and $X_s$ using phase information from adjacent segments (e.g., $X_t$ is denoised by $X_p$ and $X_s$). Predictions from SPDM and TPDM are combined via a **Phase Mixing Equation** ($P_0 = r \frac{P_0^f + P_0^b}{2} + (1-r)P_0^c$), where 'r' is a time-dependent blending factor. The DDIMScheduler estimates clean and next-step phase latents. Final motion segments are linearly blended.
- **Motion Inbetweening:** For bridging two input motions ($X_p$, $X_s$), the framework generates an inbetweening motion ($X_i$) and two transitional motions ($X_{t1}$, $X_{t2}$). Input motions' phase parameters ($P_0^p$, $P_0^s$) are fixed. TPDMs guide the generation of $X_i$, $X_{t1}$, $X_{t2}$. An optional SPDM can be used for conditional inbetweening. The framework supports parallel generation of multiple segments.
- **Long-term Motion Generation:** The framework is scaled by applying the [TPDM_f, SPDM, TPDM_b] triplet to each segment in parallel. The bidirectional TPDM ensures phase information propagates globally, enabling mutual phase dynamics adjustment and preserving long-term motion integrity over hundreds or thousands of segments.

The training of SPDM and TPDMs follows traditional diffusion frameworks, using an $\ell_1$ loss to predict the noise $\epsilon$ and the DDIM scheduler for inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents experimental evaluations across three key motion generation tasks: compositional motion pair generation, long-term motion generation, and motion inbetweening. The BABEL-TEACH dataset, annotated with subsequence pairs, was used for training and evaluation. Motion data was processed into the HumanML3D format, augmented with 6D root rotation for comprehensive representation.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
- **Motion Realism:** Fréchet Inception Distance (FID)
- **Text Alignment:** Multimodal Distance (MMD)
- **Inbetweening Specific:** L2 loss for joint velocity (L2-Vel), L2 loss for 6D rotation (L2-Rot6D), Normalized Phase Shift Similarity (NPSS) for Transition Realism; Root Mean Squared Jerk (RMS-Jerk) for Transition Smoothness.
Metrics were reported for Semantic (Smt.), Transition (Trn.), and Overall segments.

#### 4.1. Compositional Motion Pair Generation

- **Performance (Table 1):** The proposed framework demonstrated competitive performance, achieving the best overall FID (1.807) and MMD (4.711) scores compared to baselines like MDM-30, MLD-30, TEACH, PCMDM, and priorMDM.
- **Motion Realism and Smoothness (Figure 5):** Qualitative results illustrated that the framework generated fluid walking motion smoothly transitioning into a sit-down action. In contrast, priorMDM exhibited an unrealistic, sudden turn, and TEACH displayed foot skating artifacts.
- **SPDM Effectiveness:** The superior performance on Smt. FID and Smt. MMD compared to MDM-30 indicated the effectiveness of the SPDM module in standalone text-to-motion generation scenarios, despite being part of a compositional framework.

#### 4.2. Long-term Motion Generation

- **Performance (Table 2):** When evaluated on a single extended text sequence comprising 3,164 texts (302,298 frames, 168 minutes), the proposed method achieved competitive performance in both overall FID (0.909) and overall MMD (4.849). It demonstrated the best overall FID among the methods tested.
- **Transition Quality:** The results highlighted the superiority of the phase modeling approach for transition-aware motion generation compared to methods that model transitions directly in the raw motion space.
- **Limitations of Baselines:** TEACH continued to exhibit poor transition generation, reflected in a high Trn. FID. priorMDM and PCMDM struggled with generating realistic semantic segments aligned with text input. MDM and MLD, which use blending for transitions, showed similar performance to compositional pair generation, indicating limited ability to learn complex transitions.

#### 4.3. Motion Inbetweening Performance Evaluation

- **Unconditional Motion Inbetweening (UMIB) (Table 3, Figure 6):**
 - The framework showed superior performance across all evaluated metrics (L2-Vel, L2-Rot6D, NPSS for realism; RMS-Jerk for smoothness) for various inbetweening lengths (60, 120, 180 frames).
 - Qualitative results depicted smooth and realistic inbetweening motions.
 - In comparison, priorMDM often produced "hyperactive" or random motion unrelated to adjacent segments, and RSMT failed to connect succeeding motions, underscoring the benefits of the proposed method's approach to variable-length motion.

- **Conditional Motion Inbetweening (CMIB) (Table 4, Figure 7 - Appendix B):**
 - The framework excelled at producing natural inbetweening motion while adapting to textual conditions.
 - It outperformed CMB and priorMDM in terms of motion realism (Smt. FID) and text alignment (MMD), demonstrating its ability to generate smooth inbetweening motion that aligns with a given text prompt (e.g., "bend arms up"). Baselines showed hyperactivity or misalignment with the text.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and User Studies

- **Module Effectiveness:** Ablation studies indicated that the integration of frame-level tokens within SPDM and TPDM enhanced their performance in denoising param-level tokens.
- **Hyperparameter Optimization:** The phase mixing parameter setting $r = (\frac{k}{K})^3$ was identified as optimal for semantic conditional scenarios, while $r=1$ was optimal for unconditional scenarios.
- **User Perception:** User studies corroborated the quantitative findings, with the proposed approach receiving the highest scores for motion realism and smoothness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for compositional motion generation that addresses key limitations in the field, specifically concerning the creation of long, semantically coherent motion sequences with kinematically smooth transitions. The work's significance lies in its methodological contributions and demonstrated performance, holding potential for various applications.

**Technological Advancements and Contributions:**
- The introduction of the **Compositional Phase Diffusion** framework offers a scalable and efficient solution for diverse motion generation tasks, including compositional generation, long-term sequencing, and motion inbetweening. Its capacity to process multiple variable-length motion segments in parallel represents a notable advancement in managing complex motion sequences.
- The development of the **Action-Centric Periodic Autoencoder (ACT-PAE)**, **Semantic Phase Diffusion Module (SPDM)**, and **Transitional Phase Diffusion Module (TPDM)** represents a novel approach. By operating within a unified phase latent space, these modules effectively capture and manage both semantic information and transitional dynamics, enabling a more integrated and consistent motion generation process than prior methods.
- The framework's ability to maintain continuity of motion dynamics at transition boundaries and ensure semantic alignment, validated through quantitative metrics (FID, MMD, L2-Vel, L2-Rot6D, NPSS, RMS-Jerk) and qualitative observations, directly addresses a persistent challenge in human motion synthesis.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Virtual Humanoid Animation:** The ability to generate long, compositionally rich, and smoothly transitioning animations could significantly enhance the realism and efficiency of character animation workflows in film, video games, and virtual reality environments. This could reduce manual labor and accelerate content creation.
- **Robotics:** For humanoid robotics, generating physically plausible and smooth motion sequences is crucial for natural interaction and task execution. This framework could contribute to developing more sophisticated motion planning and control systems.
- **Deep Learning and Signal Processing:** The exploration and application of phase latent spaces within a diffusion framework contribute to broader advancements in deep learning methodologies, particularly in the domain of sequential data generation and signal processing for complex, multi-modal data.

**Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge areas for further exploration. These include investigating more advanced diffusion techniques (such as score-based or potential-based diffusion) beyond the basic phase mixing approach used. Additionally, the incorporation of learnable parameters or adaptive mechanisms for the phase mixing process is identified as a potential avenue for enhancing results, although it is recognized to involve increased data modeling complexity and architectural challenges. These future directions indicate a trajectory towards refining the framework's core diffusion and mixing mechanisms.

**Societal Impact:**
The research states that its exploration and application of phase latent spaces contribute to the advancement of deep learning by offering new methodologies for signal processing and multimedia generation. It suggests no negative societal impact, focusing on technological improvement rather than sensitive or privacy-implicating datasets.
