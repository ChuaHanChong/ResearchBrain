# RT-Affordance: Affordances are Versatile Intermediate Representations for Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RT-Affordance introduces end-effector pose affordances as an intermediate representation to guide robot policies, enhancing manipulation precision and data efficiency for learning new tasks on novel objects and scenarios. The method achieved 68% success on novel object grasping and 70% on complex manipulation tasks, utilizing a hierarchical model trained with robot, web, and efficiently collected annotated image data.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often struggle with precise, fine-grained manipulation, as language instructions are underspecified and goal images can be over-specified.
- Prior intermediate policy representations like trajectory sketches or keypoints lack crucial details such as end-effector orientation, limiting their effectiveness for complex manipulation tasks.
- Learning new robot tasks is resource-intensive, requiring extensive and costly robot teleoperation to collect sufficient task-specific trajectory data.

## Method

- A hierarchical model composed of an affordance-conditioned policy that executes actions based on language, observations, and an affordance plan (sequence of end-effector poses at critical task stages).
- An affordance prediction model that automatically infers the affordance plan from an initial scene image and language instruction, enabling seamless user interaction.
- A training strategy leveraging existing robot trajectory data, large-scale web datasets for general visual-linguistic knowledge, and 'cheap-to-collect' in-domain images post-hoc annotated with affordance plans to reduce teleoperation costs.

## Results

- RT-Affordance achieved **68%** success in grasping diverse unseen household objects, significantly outperforming language-conditioned RT-2 (**28%**) and goal-conditioned RT-2 (**24%**).
- On complex, unseen manipulation tasks (e.g., placing objects, articulated object manipulation), RT-Affordance demonstrated robust performance with a **70%** success rate, compared to RT-2's **3%**.
- The affordance prediction model showed good robustness to out-of-distribution factors, maintaining **77%** success for novel camera views and only a **3%** average drop for novel backgrounds, with a larger but still contained drop for novel object instances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Affordances, defined as precise end-effector poses, offer an optimal balance between the abstractness of language and the over-specificity of goal images, providing actionable guidance for fine-grained manipulation.
> - Visually projecting affordance poses directly onto the observation image effectively integrates this spatial and orientational guidance into vision-language models, enhancing their control capabilities.
> - Combining large web datasets with a small set of cheaply-collected, manually annotated in-domain images for affordance prediction drastically improves data efficiency and generalization for learning novel manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation has seen a transformative shift with the advent of large pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, such as RT-1, RT-2, and PaLM-E. These models, leveraging the vast knowledge embedded in vision-language models (VLMs) and large-scale robot datasets, hold the promise of unprecedented generalization capabilities across novel objects, scenes, and tasks. However, despite their impressive potential, current VLA models are not yet robust or reliable enough for widespread deployment outside highly controlled laboratory environments.

A primary challenge lies in the nature of how humans interface with these policies. While language is intuitive, linguistic instructions often suffer from underspecification, failing to provide the precise "how-to" details necessary for complex manipulation tasks (e.g., "pick up the dustpan" might not specify grasping the handle). Conversely, goal images, which offer rich spatial context about the desired final state, are often high-dimensional and can lead to "over-specification" issues, making learning difficult and providing them cumbersome for human users at test time.

To mitigate these shortcomings, researchers have explored various "intermediate policy representations" that aim to provide more effective guidance. These include trajectory sketches, goal sketches, or keypoints, which offer abstract spatial plans. While these representations provide more information than language alone, they often lack crucial details about the robot's end-effector orientation or specific manipulation strategies required for successful interaction (e.g., how the gripper should be oriented when picking a clothes hanger).

"RT-Affordance" positions itself precisely within this debate by proposing affordances as a novel and versatile intermediate representation. It aims to strike an optimal balance between the abstractness of language and the over-specificity of goal images or simple spatial keypoints. By defining affordances as the pose (position and orientation) of the robot's end effector at critical task stages, this work provides expressive yet lightweight abstractions. This approach directly addresses the limitations of prior intermediate representations, offering precise spatial and orientational guidance without the burden of high-dimensional inputs. Furthermore, it leverages the power of large VLA models (PaLM-E 2) but enhances their data efficiency and generalization through a hierarchical design and a unique data augmentation strategy, allowing it to bridge knowledge more effectively from heterogeneous data sources, including vast web datasets and limited robot trajectories. This research, therefore, represents a significant step towards developing more robust, generalizable, and user-friendly robot manipulation systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives of the RT-Affordance research are threefold:

1. **To identify and implement an intermediate policy representation that is expressive yet compact:** The authors sought a representation capable of describing a broad spectrum of manipulation tasks with sufficient detail to guide a robot policy, yet simple enough to be easily specified and efficiently learned. This addresses the limitations of existing representations that are either too abstract (language) or too detailed and cumbersome (goal images).
2. **To effectively bridge knowledge from external datasets and facilitate generalization:** A major bottleneck in robot learning is the cost and scalability of collecting real-world robot trajectory data. The research aims to develop a method that can better leverage readily available, large-scale web datasets (e.g., vision-language data) alongside robot-specific data, thereby enhancing the generalization capabilities of robot policies to novel objects, scenes, and tasks.
3. **To enable efficient learning of novel tasks without costly additional robot teleoperation:** The authors are motivated to reduce the immense resource investment typically required to teach robots new skills. They propose a strategy for learning new tasks by collecting "cheap-to-collect" in-domain data, significantly reducing the reliance on expensive and time-consuming robot teleoperation.

The primary motivation stems from the observed shortcomings of current VLA models. While these models can understand novel semantic concepts and objects, they often fail at precise, fine-grained manipulation, as demonstrated by their struggles with grasping specific parts of complex objects or performing multi-stage tasks reliably. The authors hypothesize that conditioning policies on "affordances"—the precise end-effector poses at key interaction points—can provide the missing spatial and orientational guidance necessary for robust manipulation, while maintaining ease of specification and learning efficiency. This approach seeks to unlock the full potential of large pre-trained models by providing them with a more actionable and interpretable form of task instruction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RT-Affordance (RT-A) proposes a hierarchical model for robot manipulation, integrating affordances as a crucial intermediate representation. The methodology is structured into two main components: an affordance-conditioned policy and an affordance prediction model.

**A. Affordance-Conditioned Policies**
The core of RT-A is a policy, $\pi(a|l, o, q)$, that generates robot actions ($a$) conditioned on a language instruction ($l$), the current observation image ($o$), and an affordance plan ($q$). An affordance plan is defined as a sequence of robot end-effector poses (position and orientation) at critical stages of the task. These "key timesteps" are automatically extracted from robot proprioception data whenever the gripper state changes (e.g., from open to close, or vice versa) or at the final timestep of a trajectory. This ensures that affordances inherently capture object-centric interactions such as grasping, pushing, or releasing.

To incorporate affordances into the policy's input, the robot hand's outline at the specified affordance poses is visually projected onto the current observation image, $\psi(o, q)$. This visual overlay provides explicit, pixel-level spatial guidance directly within the robot's visual input, with unique colors indicating temporal order. This method is advantageous as it integrates naturally with vision-language models (VLMs) that excel at processing visual information. If camera intrinsic/extrinsic parameters are unavailable, affordances can be represented as tokenized text values.

The affordance-conditioned policy is trained using behavioral cloning on a dataset of robot trajectories, $D$. Crucially, it is also co-trained on large web datasets, similar to the RT-2 approach, allowing it to leverage broad visual-linguistic knowledge.

**B. Learning to Predict Affordances**
While humans can manually provide affordance plans (e.g., by marking poses on an image UI), RT-A also develops an affordance prediction model, $\phi(q|l, o)$, which automatically predicts the affordance plan given the initial scene image ($o$) and task language instruction ($l$). This module ensures a seamless experience for the end-user, requiring only a natural language command.

The affordance prediction model is trained on:
1. **Robot trajectory data ($D$):** Hindsight affordance labels (extracted poses) from existing robot demonstrations.
2. **Web datasets:** Large-scale vision-language datasets, which help the model learn semantic and spatial reasoning. For pixel-level predictions, this can leverage data from object detection tasks.
3. **Cheap-to-collect in-domain images ($D_{aug}$):** This is a critical innovation for scalability. The authors collect a modest number of new images with corresponding task labels ($o_i, l_i$) specific to novel tasks. Instead of expensive robot teleoperation, these images are *post-hoc annotated* with affordance plans. This annotation can be done manually via a UI or semi-automatically with tools like VLMs or grasp planners. This process is significantly faster and cheaper than collecting full robot trajectories, enabling efficient learning of new tasks.

**C. Model Inference**
During inference, the user provides an initial image ($o_{init}$) and a natural language task instruction ($l$). The affordance prediction model $\phi$ then infers the affordance plan $q$. This plan is visually projected onto the current observation image $o$ (i.e., $\psi(o, q)$). Finally, the affordance-conditioned policy $\pi$ uses this combined input ($l, \psi(o, q)$) to execute the task. The system can optionally replan affordances at specified intervals to adapt to dynamic or unexpected situations.

The underlying architecture for both the affordance prediction and policy modules is based on PaLM-E 2, using its 1-billion parameter variant, which underscores the leverage of powerful pre-trained VLA backbones.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to answer three key questions: the general utility of affordance-conditioned policies, their efficiency in learning novel tasks, and the robustness of affordance prediction to distribution shifts.

**A. Experiment Implementation**
The experimental setup used an RT-1 robot manipulator with a single head-mounted camera. Training data included the RT-1 dataset, the MOO dataset, additional dexterous task trajectories, and the same web datasets used for RT-2. The core VLA model was PaLM-E 2 (1-billion parameter variant). A dedicated VLA was trained for affordance prediction using hindsight affordances from robot data, web data, and approximately 750 "cheap-to-collect" in-domain images manually annotated with affordance labels (collected in ~1 hour, annotated in ~2 hours).

**B. Learning to Grasp Novel Objects Efficiently (Table I)**
RT-Affordance was evaluated on grasping a diverse set of *unseen* household objects (dustpans, kettles, pots, boxes, headphones) amidst distractors.
- **Baselines:** Language-conditioned RT-2 achieved only 28% success, often identifying the correct object but failing to grasp it precisely (e.g., grasping the dustpan's body instead of its handle). Goal-conditioned RT-2 (GC-RT-2, using a larger 24-billion PaLM 2 backbone) fared similarly, with 24% success, struggling with precise end-effector rotation despite explicit goal images.
- **RT-A (Oracle Affordances):** When provided with manually-specified "oracle" affordance poses, RT-A achieved a significantly higher 76% success rate, demonstrating the inherent effectiveness of affordances in guiding precise manipulation.
- **RT-A (Full Model):** The full hierarchical RT-A model, with automatically predicted affordances, achieved 68% success, remarkably close to the oracle performance. This indicates the strong performance of the affordance prediction module.

**C. Beyond Object Picking (Table II)**
The applicability of RT-A was extended to more complex, *unseen* manipulation tasks like placing objects into receptacles ("place apple into pot") and articulated object manipulation ("close the cubby").
- **Baseline:** RT-2 performed poorly, achieving only 3% success, exhibiting failures such as unreliable grasping, freezing, or incorrect placement.
- **RT-A (Full Model):** RT-A achieved a robust 70% success rate. These results highlight that affordances serve as a flexible task specification capable of describing a broad range of manipulation skills. Crucially, learning these novel tasks with RT-A required only a handful of annotated images for the affordance prediction model, contrasting sharply with the dozens or hundreds of costly robot demonstrations typically needed for fine-tuning language- or goal-conditioned policies.

**D. Robustness to Out-of-Distribution Factors (Figure 4, Figure 5)**
An analysis of the affordance prediction model's robustness to distribution shifts was performed via human evaluation on grasping tasks.
- **In-Distribution:** The model achieved 77% success in predicting correct affordances.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD):** Performance degraded gracefully across various OOD settings:
 - **Novel Camera Views:** Nearly identical performance at 77%.
 - **Novel Backgrounds:** Only a 3% average drop.
 - **Novel Object Instances:** The largest drop in performance, particularly for kettles and boxes, but still within 10% of in-distribution performance on average. This demonstrates good, albeit not perfect, generalization of the affordance prediction model.

**E. Ablation Study (Table III)**
An ablation study on the affordance prediction model examined the impact of different data sources on in-distribution grasping tasks.
- **Removing Augmented Data ("No aug data"):** Performance dropped significantly from 77% to 24%. This clearly demonstrates the critical role of the cheap-to-collect, in-domain annotated images in boosting task-specific performance.
- **Removing Web Datasets ("No web data"):** Performance plummeted further to 11%. This confirms that large web datasets are indispensable for training robust, general-purpose vision-language models capable of underlying spatial and semantic reasoning.

Overall, the findings strongly support that affordances are a powerful intermediate representation, significantly enhancing robot policy performance and data efficiency on novel manipulation tasks, largely due to a robust affordance prediction model trained with a strategic mixture of robot, web, and cheaply-acquired in-domain data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RT-Affordance work presents a significant advancement in the field of robot manipulation, addressing fundamental challenges related to policy generalization, data efficiency, and user interaction. Its key contributions and potential impacts are multifaceted:

Firstly, the introduction of **affordances as a versatile intermediate representation** fills a critical gap between overly abstract language instructions and overly specific, high-dimensional visual goals. By capturing the precise pose (position and orientation) of the robot's end effector at key task stages, affordances provide targeted, actionable guidance that is both expressive enough for complex tasks and lightweight enough for efficient learning and user specification. This balances the trade-offs of existing policy interfaces, leading to substantially improved task success rates (over 50% improvement compared to state-of-the-art baselines on novel tasks).

Secondly, the methodology introduces a novel and highly effective approach to **data-efficient learning of novel tasks**. The ability to train a performant affordance prediction model using a modest dataset of "cheap-to-collect" in-domain images, manually annotated post-hoc, drastically reduces the reliance on expensive and time-consuming robot teleoperation. This is a crucial step towards making robot learning more scalable and accessible, as it allows researchers and practitioners to teach new skills to robots with significantly less effort and resources than traditional methods. This efficiency is further bolstered by effectively bridging knowledge from vast, uncurated web datasets with robot-specific data.

Thirdly, the demonstrated **robustness of the affordance prediction model** to various out-of-distribution factors (novel camera views, backgrounds, and even some novel object instances) is a strong indicator of its potential for real-world deployment. This implies that robots guided by RT-Affordance could perform reliably in varied environments beyond their training distribution.

The potential impact of RT-Affordance is substantial. It could lead to the development of more **deployable and general-purpose robotic systems** capable of adapting to new tasks and environments with minimal human intervention. For human-robot interaction, affordances offer a more intuitive and effective way for users to specify desired robot behaviors, bridging the semantic gap between human intent and robot action. Furthermore, this work provides a blueprint for leveraging the strengths of large pre-trained VLA models while mitigating their inherent limitations in fine-grained control and data hunger.

Despite its strengths, the paper acknowledges a limitation: the policy did not exhibit generalization to completely novel motions or skills, a challenge also observed in other VLA works. Future work is poised to explore this by investigating the complementary strengths of different policy representations and combining them into a unified model, as well as developing adaptive affordance re-planning mechanisms to handle unforeseen scenarios during task execution. By continuing to refine these intermediate representations and learning strategies, RT-Affordance paves the way for a future where robots can learn and execute a broader range of complex tasks with greater autonomy and efficiency.
