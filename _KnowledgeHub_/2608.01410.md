# GenTrack: Physical Alignment for Robot-Native Motion Generation and Zero-Shot Humanoid Tracking

> [!summary] Summary
> The GenTrack framework from Zhejiang University and Peking University introduces an online co-training method for robot-native motion generation and zero-shot humanoid tracking on the Unitree G1. It achieves mutual improvement by using a lagged tracker to provide execution-grounded feedback to the motion generator and a dynamically generated curriculum to train the tracker, improving zero-shot tracking success rates by up to 5.0% on some out-of-distribution datasets and reducing generated motion key-body position error by 20.7%.

## Problem

- Current zero-shot humanoid tracking methods rely on extensive, costly, and resource-intensive embodied motion corpora, especially for physical robots.
- Motions retargeted from human data often have an "executability gap," appearing kinematically plausible but failing dynamically on a robot due to mismatches in contacts, joint continuity, or self-collisions.
- Existing approaches for connecting motion generation and control typically follow one-way pipelines, preventing dynamic, mutual adaptation and limiting overall performance improvements.

## Method

- **GenTrack** implements an online, bidirectional ==co-training== framework that couples a robot-native motion generator and a humanoid tracker in a continuous sample-execute-update loop.
- A frozen, "lagged" version of the tracker from the preceding round provides stable, execution-grounded feedback to the motion generator, guiding it to produce physically executable motions via a ==FlowGRPO== objective.
- The tracker is optimized using a ==dynamic curriculum== that combines a fixed public reference pool with an increasing number of structurally valid, on-policy generated motions, with an equal number of transitions drawn from both sources.

## Results

- GenTrack improved zero-shot tracking success rates for humanoid controllers: up to **5.0%** absolute increase on LAFAN1 with the SONIC backbone and **1.4%** on Wild-G1 with the ProtoMotions backbone.
- The framework reduced the key-body position error of generated motions by **20.7%** (from **0.410m** to **0.325m**), demonstrating improved physical executability without compromising diversity or semantic quality.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the mutual adaptation loop, the lagged execution feedback, and the constraints on generator drift are all essential for achieving GenTrack's overall performance benefits.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Online, mutual adaptation between a motion generator and a tracker enables both components to continuously improve, with the generator providing an evolving curriculum and the tracker offering precise physical feedback.
> - Using a lagged version of the tracker as a reward judge for the generator is crucial for stable training, preventing self-reinforcing biases that could arise from using the current trainee tracker.
> - Maintaining semantic fidelity and diversity in generated motions alongside physical executability requires safeguards like KL penalties toward an initial generator and periodic supervised rehearsal.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses challenges in developing general-purpose humanoid controllers capable of executing diverse, previously unseen reference motions, a task known as zero-shot tracking. Current approaches to zero-shot humanoid tracking typically rely on extensive embodied motion corpora. Acquiring these corpora is costly and resource-intensive, particularly for physical robots, due to the slow, expensive, and safety-constrained nature of hardware rollouts. While human motion data is abundant, its application to robots necessitates a retargeting process.

Existing retargeting pipelines, despite advancements in kinematic correspondence, often result in a "gap" between kinematically plausible human motion and actual robot executability. This gap manifests as residual mismatches in contacts, joint continuity, self-collision avoidance, or rapid transitions, which can lead to tracking failures under closed-loop dynamics. Physics-aware refinement methods exist to mitigate these issues but are typically separate post-processing steps.

A complementary approach involves text-to-motion generators, which offer scalable supervision by synthesizing diverse references. However, if these generators are trained on human motion or merely retargeted data, they perpetuate the same executability gap. Sampling more motions from such generators expands the *proxy* distribution but does not inherently improve compatibility with robot physics.

Prior research has explored connections between motion generation and control. These include using generated motions for downstream physics policies, employing frozen generative priors to supervise policy training, or utilizing fixed trackers to provide physical feedback for generator post-training. However, these established methodologies generally follow a one-way pipeline, meaning either the generated corpus or the reward tracker remains static. This limitation prevents mutual adaptation; improvements in one component do not dynamically inform and enhance the other. A fixed generated corpus can become "stale" as the tracker improves its capabilities, while a static reward model for the generator might not recognize newly executable motions or failure modes.

GenTrack aims to integrate motion generation and tracking into a bidirectional, online co-training framework. This framework seeks to address the coupled challenges of acquiring scalable embodied supervision and bridging the executability gap in retargeted references by enabling dynamic, mutual adaptation between a motion generator and a humanoid tracker.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a framework that significantly advances zero-shot humanoid control by improving both the physical fidelity of robot motion generation and the generalization capabilities of humanoid trackers, without requiring additional physical data collection. This overarching goal is motivated by and broken down into several specific objectives:

1. **Reduce the Cost and Improve the Breadth of Embodied Supervision:** Traditional methods for scaling zero-shot humanoid trackers rely on vast embodied motion corpora, which are expensive, time-consuming, and often safety-constrained to collect directly from physical robots. While human motion capture offers scale, it introduces the challenge of robot compatibility. The objective is to leverage text-to-motion generators to synthesize diverse, robot-specific references, thereby expanding the training data for trackers beyond what is available from physical demonstrations or human-derived retargeted motion. This aims to provide a scalable and cost-effective source of supervision.

2. **Bridge the Kinematic Plausibility and Robot Executability Gap:** Motions derived from human data, even after retargeting to a robot's kinematics, often exhibit a "gap" in physical executability. These motions may appear plausible kinematically but can lead to dynamic instabilities, contact failures, joint limit violations, or self-collisions when executed by a physics-driven robot controller. The motivation is to create a feedback mechanism where actual robot execution provides a learning signal to the motion generator. This signal should guide the generator to produce motions that are not only kinematically correct but also dynamically feasible and robustly executable by the robot.

3. **Enable Co-evolution of Motion Generator and Humanoid Tracker:** Existing approaches typically involve one-way information flow, such as a frozen generator providing data to a tracker, or a fixed tracker evaluating a generator. This limits the potential for synergy. The paper's motivation is to establish an online, closed-loop interaction where the motion generator and the tracker mutually inform and improve each other. As the tracker improves its ability to execute motions, it provides richer, more nuanced physical feedback to the generator, allowing the generator to produce even more complex and executable motions. Concurrently, the generator provides an evolving curriculum of increasingly compatible motions, pushing the boundaries of the tracker's zero-shot generalization capabilities. This dynamic co-adaptation is intended to overcome the limitations of static reference pools and fixed reward models.

4. **Preserve Semantic Alignment and Motion Diversity:** While improving physical executability is crucial, it should not come at the expense of the generator's ability to produce semantically aligned and diverse motions from textual prompts. A key motivation is to ensure that the physical alignment process guides the generator towards robust, executable motions while maintaining its capacity to interpret and generate a broad range of actions, styles, and compositions as specified by text. This involves incorporating mechanisms to prevent the generator from collapsing to overly simple or conservative motions that are easy to execute but lack expressiveness.

By addressing these objectives, GenTrack aims to provide a framework that facilitates the development of more capable and versatile humanoid robots through a self-improving cycle of motion generation and control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GenTrack introduces an online generator–tracker framework designed to facilitate mutual improvement between a robot-native motion generator and a humanoid tracker. The framework operates on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot with a deterministic 38D G1 parameterization for motion representation.

**Problem Formulation:**
- **Motion Generation:** A conditional motion generator, $G_\theta(y, z)$, models a distribution $p_\theta(q|y)$ over robot motion sequences $q$ given a condition $y$ (text prompt $c$ in this work) and stochastic input $z$.
- **Humanoid Motion Tracking:** A tracker $\pi_\phi$ maps the robot's current state $s_t$ and a reference command $g_t(q)$ to an action $a_t$. The robot dynamics produce a trajectory $\tau = E(\pi_\phi, q)$. The tracker aims to maximize tracking return $J_{track}(\phi; q)$.
- **Robot-Native Parameterization:** Each motion frame is 38-dimensional (3D root channel, 6D pelvis rotation, 29 actuated joints). Planar position and heading are canonicalized per-clip, with planar motion stored as displacement and height as absolute. GMD retargeting is used once offline to supply initial paired data for the generator, but no retargeter runs within the online loop.

**Framework Overview (Fig. 1, 3):**
GenTrack couples a pretrained motion generator ($G_{\theta_0}$) and an existing humanoid tracker ($\pi_{\phi_0}$) in a bidirectional online training loop. Each round involves a sample–execute–update cycle:

1. **Motion Synthesis:** The current generator $G_\theta$ samples diverse robot-space references ($D_{gen}$) from a fixed pool of training prompts ($P_{train}$). Only structurally valid references (finite, well-formed, not scene-dependent) are retained.

2. **Execution-Grounded Feedback for Generator Alignment:**
 - **Execution Scoring:** The tracker from the *preceding round* ($\bar{\pi}^{(r)}$) is frozen and used to execute each newly generated reference in a closed-loop simulation. This "lagged" tracker acts as a stable reward judge. The current trainee tracker has zero reward weight to prevent self-reinforcing biases.
 - **Execution Score Calculation:** A scalar execution score $S_{exec}$ is computed for each rollout, reflecting:
 - $(1 - c)$: Rollout incompletion fraction.
 - $[e_j]^2$: Maximum joint error (radians), clamped at 2.
 - $[e_t/0.5]^2$: Mean root-trajectory error (meters), clamped at 2.
 - $0.5[e_d/0.5]^2$: Root-displacement error (meters), clamped at 2.
 - $2I_{fall}$: Binary indicator for a fall.
 - **Generator Reward:** The negative of this execution score, $R(q; \bar{\pi}^{(r)}) = -S_{exec}$, provides a group-relative reward for the generator. Rewards are normalized within each prompt group.
 - **Generator Optimization:** The generator is updated using a FlowGRPO (Flow-Guided Reinforcement Learning with Policy Optimization) objective. Sampled trajectories are replayed for multiple clipped policy-ratio updates.
 - **Drift Constraints:** To prevent generator collapse or forgetting:
 - A **KL penalty** is applied towards the frozen initial generator $G_{\theta_0}$.
 - **Periodic supervised flow-matching updates** are performed on the original text–motion pairs used for the generator's pre-training (rehearsal).

3. **Generated-Reference Curriculum for Tracker Optimization:**
 - **Reference Pool Expansion:** Structurally valid on-policy generations from the current generator are accumulated into $D_{gen-valid}$.
 - **Tracker Training Data:** The tracker is trained on a mixture of a fixed "seed" public reference pool ($D_{base}$, consisting of AMASS/LAFAN motions) and the accumulated $D_{gen-valid}$.
 - **Transition Balancing:** Crucially, during tracker updates, an equal number of transitions are drawn from $D_{base}$ and $D_{gen-valid}$. This strategy prevents the growing pool of generated motions from overwhelming or altering the source mixture proportions.
 - **Tracker Optimization:** The tracker $\pi_\phi$ is updated using its native objective (e.g., AMP/PPO for ProtoMotions, or its specified objective for SONIC).
 - The newly updated tracker then serves as the lagged reward judge ($\bar{\pi}^{(r+1)}$) for the next round's generator optimization.

**Training Schedule:**
The GenTrack framework orchestrates an alternation between these two branches:
1. Sample $K$ candidates per training prompt, filter invalid/scene-dependent references.
2. Score survivors with the frozen preceding-round tracker, apply FlowGRPO with KL and rehearsal anchors.
3. Accumulate structurally valid generations, update the tracker with equal public/generated transition counts using its native objective.
4. Export the updated tracker for the next round's reward judging.

This online co-training allows the generated motion pool and its execution-grounded reward signal to co-adapt, enabling continuous improvement of both the generator's physical fidelity and the tracker's zero-shot coverage.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GenTrack focused on whether online generated references extend zero-shot tracker coverage beyond static replay and whether closed-loop tracker feedback improves generator physical fidelity while preserving semantic quality. Experiments were conducted on the Unitree G1 robot, using ProtoMotions and SONIC as tracker backbones.

**Tracker Post-Training Results (Table 1):**
- **ProtoMotions Backbone:** GenTrack demonstrated an ability to recover performance on AMASS-test and achieved the highest success rate on the out-of-distribution Wild-G1 split (47.3%) compared to other post-training methods (45.9% initial). It also reduced the Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE) from 142.2 mm to 139.3 mm. Static replay methods (G$_0$ replay, G$_{final}$ replay) showed some gains but did not consistently improve across all splits and metrics as GenTrack did, indicating the benefit of the online co-training.
- **SONIC Backbone:** GenTrack yielded broader and more consistent accuracy gains. It increased success rates across all three zero-shot tracking splits: LAFAN1 (90.0% vs. 85.0% initial), AMASS-test (79.7% vs. 79.0% initial), and Wild-G1 (48.0% vs. 47.2% initial). Concurrently, it reduced MPJPE from 126.2 mm to 124.1 mm and global position error (E$_g$) from 814.2 mm to 807.2 mm, while maintaining velocity fidelity (RootVelErr, MPJVE). Comparisons against frozen-G$_0$ replay showed improvements in success across all splits and reductions in MPJPE and E$_g$. The "G$_{final}$ replay" control, using the final generated pool offline, did not reproduce the online GenTrack's performance across metrics, suggesting that the dynamic, online adaptation is crucial.

**Generator Results (Table 2):**
- GenTrack (SONIC) demonstrated substantial physical improvement for the generator. It reduced the key-body position error (E$_{key}$) from 0.410 m (G$_0$) to 0.325 m. This improvement surpassed that achieved by "Tracker-filtered SFT," which showed a higher nominal success rate (96.97%) but suffered from degraded semantic metrics (e.g., lower R@1/2/3, higher FID), indicating a collapse towards simpler, easily executable motions aligned with a static tracker's biases.
- GenTrack (SONIC) also improved semantic quality and distributional preservation, increasing TMR-G1 R@1/2 scores (0.783/0.890) and lowering FID to 0.020, while maintaining diversity. The ProtoMotions variant showed similar semantic gains. These results indicate that GenTrack aligns the generator towards robot-compatible motions without sacrificing motion diversity or semantic fidelity.

**Ablation Study (Table 5, 6, and Supplementary Section D.1):**
- **One-way controls:** Confirmed that static replay or one-way filtering (e.g., "Tracker-filtered SFT") did not replicate the full benefits of mutual adaptation. For instance, "Generator-only" training improved physical metrics for the generator, but the "GenTrack (SONIC)" branch achieved further gains, supporting the contribution of bidirectional adaptation.
- **Execution Feedback and Objective:** Controls experimenting with different reward signals and generator objectives revealed that the dense, lagged execution reward combined with FlowGRPO was more effective. For example, "Success only" feedback achieved better results than no reward but was still inferior to the dense score. Using the "current trainee as sole judge" for the generator led to degraded performance, underscoring the importance of a lagged, stable reward judge. Reward-weighted SFT and Diffusion-DPO also performed below the full GenTrack loop on a combination of metrics, supporting the specific choices of GenTrack's design.
- **Generator Retention:** Ablations demonstrated that both the KL penalty towards the frozen initial generator and periodic supervised rehearsal (flow-matching updates) were essential for maintaining the balance between physical executability and semantic/distributional quality. Removing these anchors led to increased FID and decreased diversity.

**Qualitative Analysis (Figure 4):**
- Visualizations illustrated that GenTrack-aligned generators produced motions that were successfully executed by a fixed SONIC tracker, whereas outputs from the initial generator failed due to artifacts.
- The GenTrack-trained tracker demonstrated improved generalization, successfully completing challenging phases of held-out Wild-G1 references where initial or static-replay trackers failed.

**Diagnostic Analysis (Figure 2):**
- Analysis of the reference-execution gap confirmed a systematic difference between retargeted references and executed motions. Shared PCA showed broad overlap but with systematic local shifts, while a classifier achieved high discriminative power. This gap was localized to metrics like body jerk, foot skating, root height, and foot clearance, justifying the use of closed-loop execution for evaluation rather than assuming kinematic plausibility equates to executability.

In summary, the results indicate that GenTrack's online co-training strategy consistently improves both the physical fidelity of generated motions and the zero-shot coverage and accuracy of humanoid trackers, particularly on out-of-distribution references, by effectively bridging the executability gap through mutual adaptation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GenTrack framework presents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robot control, particularly for zero-shot motion tracking and robot-native motion generation. Its significance stems from addressing two critical bottlenecks in current research: the high cost of extending embodied supervision and the persistent executability gap of retargeted human motion data.

**Key Contributions and Impact:**

- **Scalable Zero-Shot Humanoid Control:** By enabling a motion generator to synthesize diverse, increasingly robot-executable references, GenTrack offers a scalable method to expand the supervision for zero-shot humanoid trackers. This reduces the dependency on expensive and labor-intensive physical robot demonstrations, allowing for broader and more robust generalization to unseen motions. The demonstrated improvements in zero-shot coverage across challenging out-of-distribution datasets highlight this potential.
- **Improved Robot-Compatible Motion Generation:** The framework demonstrates that closed-loop physical feedback from a tracker can effectively align a language-conditioned motion generator. This alignment guides the generator to produce motions that are not merely kinematically plausible but also dynamically robust and physically executable by the target robot. This is achieved without sacrificing the semantic fidelity, diversity, or difficulty of the generated motions, which is a crucial distinction from one-way filtering approaches that can lead to degenerate or overly conservative motion distributions.
- **Mutual Adaptation Paradigm:** GenTrack establishes a paradigm of online, mutual adaptation where the motion generator and the tracker co-evolve. The generator provides an increasingly challenging and relevant curriculum for the tracker, while the tracker's improved execution capabilities offer refined and targeted physical feedback to the generator. This bidirectional learning loop enables continuous improvement for both components, surpassing the limitations of static reference pools or fixed reward models.
- **Generalizability Across Backbones:** The successful application and observed benefits across two distinct tracker backbones (ProtoMotions and SONIC) suggest that the GenTrack framework is not limited to a specific controller architecture. This indicates the broad applicability of the online co-training strategy to various humanoid control systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays a foundation for future investigations into self-improving robot learning systems. It opens avenues for exploring how robots can autonomously generate their own training data, guided by physical interaction, to learn more complex and agile skills. This could extend to other robot morphologies, more intricate tasks involving manipulation, or integration with higher-level planning.
- **Enhanced Real-World Deployment Potential:** By training generators to produce motions that are inherently more compatible with robot physics, GenTrack could facilitate more effective sim-to-real transfer. Robots learning from such physically aligned synthetic data might exhibit greater robustness and agility in real-world deployment, reducing the need for extensive fine-tuning on hardware.

In conclusion, GenTrack offers a practical and principled solution to a fundamental problem in robot learning, paving the way for more capable, adaptable, and autonomous humanoid robots through an innovative framework of coupled, online generation and tracking.
