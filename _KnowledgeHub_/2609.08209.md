# Monkey See, Can Monkey Do? A Benchmark for Evaluating Robot Skill Learning by Observation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboReel is a unified benchmark for learning-from-observation, pairing seven hours of real-world human demonstration video across ten tabletop manipulation tasks with digital-twin simulation environments and scripted robot trajectories. Four test suites probe distractor robustness, long-horizon competence, and calibrated keypoint use. Across seven evaluated methods, video-conditioned VLA policies perform best, yet long-horizon and low-tolerance tasks remain below 25% success.

## Problem

- Existing learning-from-observation methods adopt incompatible assumptions, hardware and environment setups, so no apples-to-apples lateral comparison can establish which approach actually advances the field.
- Robot datasets and simulator benchmarks pair language with trajectories but omit real human demonstration video as input, so observational replication of a watched skill goes unmeasured.
- The closest resources fall short: one offers paired human video without simulated evaluation, another offers evaluation environments but only robot play video, not human demonstrations.

## Method

- **RoboReel** collects 2,000 calibrated human demonstrations over ten tasks from four synchronized RGB-D views, three exocentric plus one helmet-mounted egocentric, with 3D hand and object pose annotation.
- A paired ManiSkill ==digital twin== reconstructs 18 rigid objects as SAM3D meshes, imports PartNet-Mobility articulated assets, ==ArUco-calibrates== camera poses, and synthesizes Franka trajectories via GraspGen plus mplib.
- Four suites (==No Distraction==, ==With Distraction==, ==Evaluation Distraction== for test-time shift, ==Pose Defined== for keypoint methods) plus new ==video-conditioned VLA== baselines using uniform or ==keyframe frame sampling==.

## Results

- Video-conditioned VLA variants dominate every suite, reaching **100%** on Push Cube and Close Drawer without distractors, while implicit baselines UniSkill and Vid2Robot rarely exceed **30%**.
- Explicit language pipelines underperform implicit ones on all tasks; keyframe sampling beats uniform sampling on **7 of 10** tasks under evaluation-time distraction shift.
- Long-horizon tasks such as Stack Cups and Toys in Basket stay under **25%** for all models; keypoint policy reaches **95%** on Push Cube but **2%** on Bowl in Plate.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Trajectory pretraining beats language grounding: video-conditioned action models outperform video-to-instruction pipelines, suggesting language extraction discards temporal detail that policies actually need.
> - Keyframe selection is cheap robustness: weighting task-relevant frames pays off mainly under domain shift, where uniform redundant frames act as noise.
> - Keypoint supervision transfers gross motion but not grasping; pose alone cannot specify grasp configuration, and its distractor robustness comes from discarding image context.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is actively pursuing the capability of Learning from Observation (LfO), which mirrors how humans and animals acquire skills through social learning. This paradigm offers a potential solution to the data scarcity and sample inefficiency often encountered in robotics. Recent advancements have demonstrated the potential for robots to learn manipulation skills from human videos. However, evaluating progress in this area has been challenging due to a lack of standardization. Existing LfO methods often operate under diverse assumptions, utilize varied hardware, and are tested in disparate environmental setups, which hinders direct and meaningful comparisons between different approaches.

The broader research landscape includes several categories of related work:
- **Human Action Video Datasets in Computer Vision:** Datasets such as UCF101, ActivityNet, Howto100M, and Ego-Exo4D focus on understanding human actions from videos, sometimes with detailed semantic annotations. However, these datasets are primarily designed for vision and language model development and typically lack the paired robot environments or action spaces necessary for training and evaluating embodied robotic agents.
- **Robotics Datasets, Benchmarks, and Simulators:** Benchmarks like RoboCerebra, LIBERO, RoboCasa, VLABench, and MimicGen provide extensive robot data, often with language conditioning and simulated environments. While valuable for robot learning, they generally do not include human video demonstrations as an input modality for LfO tasks.
- **Closer LfO-specific Datasets:**
 - **RH20T** provides paired robot trajectories and human demonstration videos but lacks simulated evaluation environments, making systematic lateral comparisons difficult.
 - **MimicDroid** offers paired robot demonstration videos and evaluation environments but omits real-world human demonstration inputs.
 - **Human2Robot** includes real-world human and robot videos but lacks a calibrated, paired simulation environment for consistent evaluation across methods.

This paper identifies a critical gap: the absence of a unified benchmark that integrates real-world human demonstration videos with calibrated, paired simulation environments and robot trajectories for training and evaluation. Specifically, the authors highlight two fundamental challenges for standardized LfO evaluation: ensuring visual fidelity between real and simulated scenes and providing a portable, calibrated environment for robust robot performance testing. RoboReel is introduced to address this specific need for a standardized evaluation platform.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the challenges in systematically assessing progress within the Learning from Observation (LfO) field for robotics. Despite promising results in enabling robots to learn manipulation skills from human videos, the diverse methodological assumptions, hardware configurations, and environmental setups employed by various research groups impede direct comparisons and the identification of robust advancements. This heterogeneity makes it difficult to establish a reliable state-of-the-art or draw conclusive findings about the efficacy of different LfO approaches. The authors argue that this lack of reproducibility prevents a comprehensive understanding of what constitutes genuine progress in the field.

Furthermore, the authors emphasize the inherent value of LfO. Beyond its biological inspiration from human and animal social learning, LfO offers a practical strategy for data scaling in robotics, a domain often characterized by sample inefficiency and limited data availability. To fully harness this potential, a standardized and universally accessible evaluation framework is deemed essential. Two specific, fundamental challenges for standardized LfO evaluation are identified: achieving high visual fidelity between real-world observations and simulated environments, and ensuring the availability of a portable and calibrated environment for consistent robot performance assessment.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the research are detailed as follows:

- **Introduce RoboReel:** To establish a novel, unified benchmark specifically designed for evaluating models that learn policies from human videos.
- **Provide Comprehensive Data and Environments:** To bundle real-world human demonstration videos, corresponding simulated robot trajectories, and integrated evaluation environments across ten distinct manipulation tasks. The benchmark aims to be the first unified platform offering such paired training data and evaluation setups from real-world human demonstrations.
- **Develop Multi-faceted Test Suites:** To design and implement four distinct test suites that enable evaluation of LfO models across multiple critical performance axes. These include assessing robustness to visual distractors, evaluating capability in long-horizon tasks, and understanding the utility of explicit pose information.
- **Conduct Extensive Algorithmic Evaluation:** To cover and evaluate LfO models from different architectural categories and study the effectiveness of various representation choices. This includes benchmarking over seven state-of-the-art algorithms, including the authors' Vision-Language-Action (VLA) based variants.
- **Analyze Algorithmic Performance Gaps:** To present a detailed analysis of the performance characteristics of different LfO algorithms, identifying existing limitations and challenging areas, such as long-horizon tasks and tasks requiring low manipulation tolerances.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce **RoboReel**, a benchmark designed to standardize the evaluation of robot skill learning from human observations. The methodology encompasses benchmark creation, task curation, data collection, simulation environment development, and a systematic experimental evaluation of various LfO models.

**Benchmark Creation:**
RoboReel is built upon the principle of providing a unified platform with paired real-world human demonstrations, simulated robot trajectories, and evaluation environments. A key aspect is the use of generative methods to reconstruct accurate 3D meshes of real-world objects, enabling the creation of a calibrated real-world demonstration setup matched with a high-fidelity simulation environment.

**Test Suites:** Four distinct evaluation suites are developed to assess LfO models under varied conditions:
1. **No Distraction Suite (ND):** Models are trained using human videos and robot trajectories collected in environments without visual distractors. Evaluation is also performed in clean environments without distractions.
2. **With Distraction Suite (WD):** Models are trained on human videos and robot trajectories collected in environments that include randomly sampled, non-task-related visual distractors. Evaluation is performed in environments *with* these distractors.
3. **Evaluation Distraction Suite (ED):** This suite tests robustness to domain shift. Models are trained on human videos and robot trajectories from *clean* environments (no distractors). However, evaluation is conducted in environments *with* visual distractors present in both the human demonstrations and the simulation.
4. **Pose Defined Suite (PD):** This suite investigates the utility of explicit keypoint annotations. Human demonstration videos are annotated with 3D keypoints, and this calibrated data is used for training. Evaluation is performed in a simulation environment with a common 3D action space, assessing how keypoint annotations benefit LfO.

**Task Curation:**
Ten manipulation tasks are designed to challenge models across varying axes: object type, task horizon (short vs. long), and manipulation precision (high vs. low tolerance). Examples include "push cube," "close drawer," "empty basket," "press toaster button," and "stack three cups." The focus is on goal-based tasks to manage complexity in soft-body simulations and task validation.

**Human Demonstration Collection:**
A calibrated tabletop environment was established in a lab. It featured 18 rigid-body objects, each with a corresponding digital twin. Human demonstrations were recorded from four synchronized camera views: a helmet-mounted egocentric view (RealSense D435) and three exocentric views (front, left, right) using RealSense L515 cameras. Both RGB and depth images were captured. A total of 2,000 human demonstration videos (100 per task, under clean and distracted conditions) were collected, accumulating over seven hours of data.

**Simulation Environment and Paired Data Generation:**
- **Platform:** The simulated test bench is built on ManiSkill, leveraging its integration with PartNet-Mobility, GPU-accelerated rendering, and a gym-compatible interface.
- **Robot:** A 7 DoF Franka Panda arm, commonly used in robot learning research, is employed.
- **Object Reconstruction:** Rigid objects from the real world are reconstructed as digital twins using SAM3D-objects, ensuring high fidelity in color, shape, and physical geometry for visual consistency. Articulated objects (e.g., drawer, toaster) are sourced from the PartNet-Mobility dataset.
- **Camera Calibration:** ArUco markers were used in the real-world setup to establish precise camera-to-table coordinate relationships. This configuration was replicated in ManiSkill to ensure visual consistency between real and simulated environments.
- **Robot Trajectory Generation:** Success conditions for tasks were manually defined. For pick-and-place tasks, GraspGen generated candidate grasps, which were then ranked. mplib was used to synthesize collision-free robot trajectories through a five-phase process (pre-grasp, grasp, transport, pre-place, drop). For articulated objects, waypoints guided the end-effector. All generated robot skills were labeled successful upon meeting predefined goal conditions.

**Baselines and Experimental Setup:** The authors categorized and evaluated several state-of-the-art LfO models based on their representation choices for human demonstration videos:
1. **Explicit Language Representation:** Includes SeeDo (a VLM planner) and a VLM-based variant of π0.5 (using Qwen3-VL-4B for language commands). A baseline π0.5 trained with expert language annotations was also used for comparison.
2. **Implicit Latent Representation:** Includes UniSkill and Vid2Robot. The authors also augmented π0.5 to accept human video inputs, encoding frames using either uniform sampling or a BPP-inspired keyframe selection method.
3. **Keypoint-based Learning:** PointPolicy was used as the baseline, which directly performs imitation learning from human video keypoints.

Each model was trained twice (once for the no-distraction training set and once for the with-distraction set). Evaluation involved three runs, each using a different set of random seeds for environment instantiation. Success rates were computed from 20 rollouts per run (60 total rollouts per cell), and means and standard errors were reported. Pre-recorded evaluation banks were utilized to ensure identical starting states and distractor configurations across different model evaluations, enhancing reproducibility.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Learning from Observation (LfO) models on the RoboReel benchmark yielded several findings regarding the efficacy and limitations of current approaches.

**Overall Performance Trends:**
- **Dominance of Video-Conditioned π0.5:** Across all ten tasks and the diverse test suites, the video-conditioned π0.5 variants, which utilize implicit task representations, consistently achieved the strongest overall performance. This is attributed to the large-scale pretraining of the underlying Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which aids in establishing correspondence between human video features and robot actions.
- **Implicit vs. Explicit Representations:** Implicit methods consistently outperformed explicit language-based representation methods. This finding suggests that despite the internet-scale pretraining of VLMs, the direct conditioning on robot trajectory data within VLA models provides a significant advantage for visual-temporal reasoning and action generation compared to relying solely on language instructions extracted from videos.

**Performance Across Test Suites:**

- **No Distraction (ND) Suite:**
 - Video-conditioned π0.5 variants significantly outperformed other baselines.
 - The performance gap was particularly pronounced in long-horizon tasks such as "Empty Basket," "Stack Cups," "Toys in Basket," and "Toy in Drawer."
 - SeeDo, an explicit VLM planner, exhibited limited performance on multi-step pick-and-place tasks.

- **With Distraction (WD) Suite:**
 - The video-conditioned π0.5 variants maintained their lead.
 - However, most models from both explicit and implicit families experienced a performance drop compared to the ND suite, indicating that the presence of distractors introduces challenges even when models are trained in distracted environments.

- **Evaluation Distraction (ED) Suite:**
 - This suite, designed to assess robustness to observational domain shift, proved to be the most challenging, resulting in generally lower success rates across all models.
 - The video-conditioned π0.5 variant employing *keyframe-based* sampling outperformed its *uniform sampling* counterpart in 7 out of 10 tasks. This suggests that focusing on task-relevant moments (keyframes) makes policies more robust to unseen visual clutter introduced at evaluation time, as uniform sampling might include noisy or uninformative frames that hinder generalization.

- **Pose Defined (PD) Suite:**
 - PointPolicy, a keypoint-based method, achieved strong results on tasks like "Push Cube" and "Close Drawer," which involve less precise, non-prehensile manipulations.
 - It demonstrated significant robustness to distractions, with comparable performance in both distracted and non-distracted environments.
 - However, its performance dropped substantially on tasks requiring precise grasping, such as "Pick Cube," "Open Lid," and "Bowl in Plate." This limitation is attributed to the inherent difficulty in deriving accurate grasp configurations solely from keypoints and a detected precision floor of 2-3 cm, which leads to grasp failures on smaller or more complex objects.

**Analysis of Research Questions (Key Limitations Identified):**

- **Effect of Task Length:** Task horizon exhibited a consistent negative correlation with success rates across all model families. Long-horizon tasks, requiring the correct execution of multiple sequential sub-goals, remained a critical bottleneck, with even the strongest baselines achieving success rates below 25%.
- **Low Tolerance Manipulation:** Tasks requiring high precision, such as "Press Toaster" (small target area) and "Stack Cups" (precise placement, long-horizon), proved difficult. Performance for "Press Toaster" degraded consistently from ND to WD and further to ED, suggesting visual clutter significantly impacts the localization of precise interaction points. "Stack Cups" showed uniformly low performance across all suites and models, indicating a compounding difficulty from both precision and long-horizon requirements.
- **Fundamental Limitations of Current LfO:** The analysis highlighted consistent gaps:
 1. **Long-horizon tasks:** Compounding errors across sequential sub-goals remain a significant challenge.
 2. **Precise manipulation:** Especially difficult in cluttered environments, where visual encoders struggle with localization.
 3. Despite the strong foundation provided by large-scale VLA pretraining, these two categories of tasks largely remain unsolved by current evaluated methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of RoboReel represents a significant contribution to the field of robot learning from observation (LfO) by addressing a critical need for standardization. Its significance and potential impact can be elaborated as follows:

- **Enabling Systematic and Reproducible Evaluation:** RoboReel directly tackles the long-standing challenge of comparing diverse LfO methods. By providing a unified benchmark with paired real-world human demonstration videos, simulated robot trajectories, and calibrated evaluation environments, it establishes a shared protocol. This standardization allows for "apples-to-apples" comparisons, which is crucial for identifying genuine advances, quantifying progress, and fostering reproducibility within the research community. This capability was previously hindered by varied assumptions, hardware, and environmental setups.

- **Comprehensive Assessment of LfO Capabilities:** The benchmark's four distinct test suites (No Distraction, With Distraction, Evaluation Distraction, and Pose Defined) allow for a multi-faceted and rigorous evaluation of LfO models. These suites systematically probe critical aspects of robot learning, including robustness to visual distractors, generalization to unseen clutter (domain shift), and the effectiveness of explicit pose information. This comprehensive evaluation framework ensures that models are tested across a broad spectrum of real-world challenges.

- **Identification of Current LfO Limitations:** The detailed evaluation of over seven state-of-the-art LfO algorithms on RoboReel has provided valuable insights into the current limitations of the field. The consistent struggles with long-horizon tasks and precise manipulation in cluttered environments, even for the most advanced VLA-based models, clearly delineate future research directions. These findings serve as a roadmap for researchers, highlighting specific areas where fundamental breakthroughs are still required.

- **Accelerating Research and Development:** By providing a readily available and standardized dataset with diverse tasks and evaluation environments, RoboReel is poised to accelerate research in LfO. Researchers can spend less time on dataset creation and environment calibration, and more time on developing and testing novel algorithms. The benchmark's release is expected to lower the barrier to entry for new groups and facilitate broader participation in LfO research.

- **Guiding Model Development:** The comparative analysis of different model families (explicit, implicit, keypoint-based representations) and the insights into their performance characteristics—such as the superior robustness of keyframe-based sampling in domain shift scenarios or the challenges of keypoint methods for precise grasping—offer concrete guidance for the design and improvement of future LfO architectures. It emphasizes the importance of robust visual-temporal reasoning and effective handling of visual clutter.

- **Laying Foundation for Future Generalist Robots:** Improved LfO capabilities, facilitated by benchmarks like RoboReel, are foundational for the development of more adaptable and generalist robots. Robots that can reliably learn complex skills directly from human observation could significantly reduce the need for extensive manual programming or costly robot demonstrations, thereby expanding the applicability of robotics in diverse real-world settings. The stated plan for continuous development of RoboReel, including increasing data scale and task variety, indicates a long-term commitment to supporting this vision.
