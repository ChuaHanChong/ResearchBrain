# OGPO: Sample Efficient Full-Finetuning of Generative Control Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> Off-policy Generative Policy Optimization (OGPO) is an algorithm developed by researchers from Carnegie Mellon and collaborating institutions for sample-efficient full-policy finetuning of Generative Control Policies (GCPs). This approach, which decouples costly environment interactions from computationally cheap policy updates, consistently achieves high success rates across diverse robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Policies derived from imitation learning often exhibit brittleness and lack robustness when deployed in environments with minor variations, leading to low real-world success rates.
- Existing finetuning methods for Generative Control Policies (GCPs) present a trade-off: sample-efficient approaches offer limited expressiveness, while methods for maximal improvement are highly sample-inefficient.
- Directly backpropagating gradients through the entire generative process of GCPs or through noisy Q-functions can lead to unstable or inaccurate updates, particularly in contact-rich manipulation tasks.

## Method

- **OGPO** introduces a ==bi-level MDP== framework that decouples off-policy critic learning for expensive environment interactions from on-policy policy extraction for computationally cheap action generation.
- It employs off-policy ==Temporal Difference== (TD)-learning to train Q-functions for the environment dynamics and applies ==zeroth-order Proximal Policy Optimization== (**PPO**)-style updates for the inner denoising MDP, using learned Q-functions as terminal rewards.
- The **OGPO**+ variants incorporate enhancements such as behavior cloning regularization from successful trajectories, conservative advantages with ensemble critics, and χ²-regularization for pixel-based observations to mitigate critic over-exploitation and improve stability.

## Results

- OGPO achieved high success rates and demonstrated substantial sample efficiency improvements, requiring approximately 10 times fewer environment steps than on-policy baselines like DPPO on ROBOMIMIC tasks.
- The method successfully finetuned poorly-initialized behavior cloning policies to near full task success without requiring additional expert data in the online replay buffer, showcasing novel capability in real-world deployment scenarios.
- Ablation studies confirmed that OGPO's zeroth-order PPO extraction method was more stable and effective than direct backpropagation or other policy extraction variants (e.g., AWR/FPO-style), particularly on complex robotic tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the optimization of computationally cheap generative action processes from expensive environment interactions enables both sample-efficient and expressive full-policy finetuning of GCPs.
> - Using zeroth-order PPO-style policy extraction over the denoising MDP provides a stable and effective way to update generative models, avoiding the instability associated with direct backpropagation through complex generative chains.
> - Regularization techniques like behavior cloning from successful trajectories and conservative advantages are critical for grounding policy learning, mitigating initial performance dips, and robustifying updates against imperfect critic estimates, particularly in sparse-reward settings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work operates within the field of robot learning, specifically focusing on the challenges associated with finetuning pre-trained robotic policies using reinforcement learning (RL). The broader context involves the increasing adoption of Generative Control Policies (GCPs), such as diffusion and flow models, as expressive parameterizations for robot control due to their ability to model complex action distributions.

Prior research in robot learning often employs imitation learning via behavior cloning (BC) to teach robots skills from human demonstrations. While BC policies can acquire a wide range of behaviors, they frequently exhibit fragility to minor environmental variations or task changes, leading to suboptimal performance in real-world deployment. A natural progression has been to enhance the robustness of these pre-trained policies through autonomous improvement via RL, using self-collected experience.

However, existing methodologies for finetuning GCPs present a tradeoff. Approaches prioritizing sample efficiency, such as steering the initial generation noise or learning residual corrections, often involve partial finetuning of the GCP. These methods learn quickly when the base policy already offers sufficient coverage of optimal actions but demonstrate limited capacity for exploration or significant policy shifts. Conversely, methods aimed at maximizing final task performance, typically employing on-policy policy gradient updates, can drive aggressive policy improvement but at the cost of substantial sample inefficiency. This inefficiency is a significant barrier in robotics, where real-world environment interactions are costly.

"Off-policy Generative Policy Optimization" (OGPO) positions itself as a method designed to circumvent this tradeoff. It aims to provide both sample-efficient and expressive full-policy finetuning of GCPs, thereby combining the benefits of efficient data reuse with comprehensive policy adaptation. The work builds upon the conceptualization of GCP optimization as a bi-level Markov Decision Process (MDP), which includes a nested inner denoising MDP for action generation and an outer environment dynamics MDP for executed actions, a concept explored in previous works such as Ren et al. and Black et al.. By addressing the sample inefficiency of on-policy bi-level MDP optimization, OGPO seeks to advance the state of robot learning towards more robust and autonomously adaptable systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a sample-efficient algorithm, Off-policy Generative Policy Optimization (OGPO), capable of finetuning Generative Control Policies (GCPs) with expressive, full-policy updates. This algorithm is designed to overcome limitations observed in current robot learning paradigms, particularly in the context of improving pre-trained policies.

The motivation for OGPO stems from several challenges in modern robot manipulation:

1. **Brittleness of Imitation Learning Policies:** Policies acquired through behavior cloning (BC) from human demonstrations, while enabling initial skill learning, are typically sensitive to subtle changes in tasks and environments. These policies rarely achieve high success rates in diverse deployment scenarios, requiring considerable additional manual data collection to improve robustness. The research aims to enable autonomous bolstering of pre-trained imitation learning policies without extensive human intervention.

2. **Tradeoffs in Existing GCP Finetuning Methods:** Current approaches to finetuning GCPs via reinforcement learning face a dilemma:
 - **Sample-Efficient but Limited:** Methods that focus on sample efficiency often achieve this by performing partial finetuning of the GCP, such as merely steering the initial generation noise or learning residual corrections. While these methods learn quickly when the base policy has strong coverage of optimal actions and good initialization, they struggle with exploring novel behaviors or making substantial corrections when the base policy is suboptimal.
 - **Expressive but Sample-Inefficient:** Conversely, methods designed to elicit maximum final task performance, which often rely on on-policy policy gradient updates, drive aggressive policy improvement across the entire generative process. However, this comes at a significant cost in sample efficiency, rendering them impractical for real-world robotics where collecting environment interactions is expensive.

3. **Leveraging Sample-Cost Asymmetry:** A core motivation for OGPO is to exploit the inherent sample-cost asymmetry in robotic tasks. Generating action trajectories through a GCP's denoising Markov Decision Process (MDP) is a purely computational process, therefore cheap. In contrast, collecting interaction data from the real environment MDP is expensive. OGPO is motivated to leverage this difference to achieve efficient and expressive policy updates.

To address these motivations, OGPO aims to:
- **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** Deliver high performance on complex manipulation tasks, including those involving multi-task settings, high-precision insertion, and dexterous control.
- **Finetune Poorly-Initialized Policies:** Enable the finetuning of BC policies, even those with poor initialization, to near full task-success, crucially without requiring expert data in the online replay buffer and with minimal task-specific hyperparameter tuning. This represents a fundamental new capability for autonomous robot learning.
- **Systematic Empirical Investigation:** Conduct a thorough empirical study to identify the key mechanisms, stabilizing factors, and failure modes that govern successful off-policy full-policy improvement of GCPs.
- **Introduce Practical Stabilization Techniques:** Develop and integrate practical methods, such as success-buffer regularization, two-sided conservative advantages, and Q-variance reduction, to mitigate critic over-exploitation, which can destabilize learning, especially in diverse observation spaces like pixel-based inputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on Off-policy Generative Policy Optimization (OGPO), an algorithm designed for sample-efficient and expressive finetuning of Generative Control Policies (GCPs). The core approach involves decoupling the optimization of environment dynamics from the internal generative process of the policy.

**Overall Framework: Off-Policy Generative Policy Optimization (OGPO)**
OGPO integrates off-policy critic learning with on-policy policy optimization for GCPs. It leverages a bi-level MDP formulation, similar to prior work, where GCP optimization is understood as having an inner denoising MDP (action generation) and an outer environment dynamics MDP (executed actions). The key innovation is to sever the direct connection between these two levels. Instead of directly embedding the denoising MDP into the environment MDP, OGPO uses a learned Q-function from the environment as a terminal reward for the denoising MDP. This allows the internal GCP optimization to proceed computationally without requiring costly environment interactions for each denoising step.

**Components of OGPO:**

1. **Off-Policy Critic Learning:**
 - An experience replay buffer, `D_roll`, stores transitions `(s_t, a_t, s_{t+1}, r_t, d_t)` collected from environment interactions.
 - An ensemble of `M` critic networks, `Q_φ_i`, is trained in parallel to estimate the expected cumulative return.
 - The critic networks are updated using a **Temporal Difference (TD) loss** (Eq. 2.3), which enforces the Bellman consistency equation. This enables strong data reuse, contributing to sample efficiency.
 - Action chunking is employed, treating a sequence of actions `a_{t:t+h-1}` as a single action `a_t` for `M_ENV` (environment MDP) dynamics, with corresponding adjustments to rewards and Q-function backups (Appendix A.1). `Q_targ` (the target Q-value) is computed as an average over the target critic networks to mitigate overestimation bias.

2. **On-Policy PPO for Off-Policy Policy Extraction:**
 - Instead of backpropagating gradients directly through the Q-function and the multi-step generative process, which can lead to unstable gradients, OGPO employs a **zero-order policy gradient method** inspired by PPO.
 - The learned `Q_targ(s_t, a_{t,0})` (where `a_{t,0}` is the final action generated by the GCP) is utilized as a terminal reward for the inner denoising MDP. This allows the policy to maximize estimated future returns without direct environment interaction.
 - The PPO objective (Eq. 3.2) is applied over the computationally generated denoising trajectory `a_{t,K:0}`. The objective involves an Annealed Importance Sampling (AIS) ratio `ω_θ` (ratio of current policy to a target policy) and an advantage `Â = Q_targ(s_t, a_{t,0}) - V̂`.
 - **Multiple Denoising-Trajectory Sampling:** A key aspect is the ability to generate `N_group` denoising trajectories in parallel for each state `s_t` sampled from the replay buffer. This computational parallelization allows for a more robust estimation of the value baseline `V̂` via Monte Carlo approximation and reduces variance in policy gradients by averaging across trajectories (Eq. 3.3). This is analogous to "group-relative policy optimization" in large language models.

3. **Debiasing Noise Injection for Flow Policies:**
 - For flow-based GCPs, deterministic inference steps must be made stochastic to ensure non-singular likelihoods (necessary for the importance sampling ratio `ω_θ`) and facilitate exploration. This is achieved by adding Gaussian noise at each flow step (Eq. 3.4).
 - To prevent this noise from altering the marginal action distributions, OGPO incorporates a **score-based drift correction** (Eq. A.3), a principled method from stochastic interpolants [Albergo et al., 2023]. This correction ensures that the SDE-augmented flow policy maintains the original marginal distributions. A tapering noise schedule `σ_τ = σ_init * sqrt(1-τ)` is used to ensure numerical stability at the final denoising step.

**OGPO+ (Improved Variant for Mitigating Critic Over-exploitation):**
The paper identifies that vanilla OGPO can sometimes over-exploit imperfectly learned critics, leading to suboptimal performance, particularly in high-precision or pixel-based tasks. To address this, OGPO+ introduces practical stabilization mechanisms:

1. **Behavior Cloning (BC) Regularization on Successful Trajectories (Success Buffer):**
 - A separate `D_succ` buffer is maintained, containing only transitions from episodes that successfully completed the task.
 - A BC loss `L_BC(θ)` (Eq. 4.2) is added as a regularization term to the total policy loss (Eq. 4.3): `L_Total(θ) = L_PPO(θ) + λ_BC * L_BC(θ)`. This term biases the policy towards replicating empirically successful actions, effectively grounding the policy's exploration by maintaining coverage over known good behaviors.
 - **(Optional) Best-of-N Inference:** For inference, `N` candidate actions are sampled, and the one with the highest `Q_targ` value is selected (Eq. 4.4). This acts as a verifier of action quality but is noted to provide marginal gains in performance during training.

2. **Conservative Advantages (CA) (OGPO+CA):**
 - This modification addresses the common "dip" in performance during the transition from offline pre-training to online RL.
 - The advantage `Â` in the policy update is calculated conservatively (Eq. 4.5). It is non-zero only if all `M` critic ensemble members agree on the sign of the advantage, in which case it takes the smallest magnitude. This robustifies policy updates by ensuring they occur only along directions of strong critic consensus, mitigating the impact of noisy or inaccurate Q-value estimates.
 - **(Optional) Q-Variance Reduction:** To further improve critic accuracy, especially in pixel-based settings, the TD target for the critic can be averaged over `N_vr` actions sampled from the reference actor (Eq. 4.1).

**Experimental Setup:**
The evaluation involved a range of challenging robotic manipulation tasks:
- **ROBOMIMIC:** SQUARE (medium-horizon insertion), TOOLHANG (long-horizon multi-step insertion), TRANSPORT (bi-manual long-horizon transfer) – covering state-based and image-based observations.
- **FRANKA-KITCHEN:** Multi-task sequential manipulation tasks (KITCHEN-COMPLETE, KITCHEN-MIXED, KITCHEN-PARTIAL) with varying data quality.
- **Adroit Hand:** Dexterous manipulation tasks (DOOR-V1, HAMMER-V1, PEN-V1, RELOCATE-V1) with high-dimensional action spaces.
- **LIBERO:** Vision-based, language-conditioned manipulation tasks.
Policies are initialized from a behavior cloning (BC) checkpoint, typically clipped to around 50% success, and then finetuned online. Crucially, online RL proceeds *without access to expert data in the replay buffer*, simulating challenging real-world deployment scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates that OGPO, particularly its enhanced variants OGPO+ and OGPO+CA, substantially improves the sample efficiency and final performance of Generative Control Policies (GCPs) across a diverse set of robotic manipulation tasks.

**Overall Performance and Sample Efficiency:**
- OGPO achieves high success rates on challenging manipulation tasks, including multi-task settings, high-precision insertion, and dexterous control.
- A key finding is its **superior sample efficiency**, outperforming on-policy methods like DPPO by approximately an order of magnitude (e.g., 10x faster convergence on ROBOMIMIC tasks, Figure 12).
- OGPO+ demonstrates the capacity to fine-tune poorly-initialized behavior cloning policies (starting around 50% success) to near full task mastery, notably **without requiring any expert data in the online replay buffer**. This is identified as a novel capability.
- The method exhibits robust performance with minimal task-specific hyperparameter tuning, suggesting a degree of generality.

**Comparison with Baselines:**
OGPO's performance is contextualized by comparisons with alternative off-policy and on-policy finetuning strategies:

- **Expressivity (Full-Policy vs. Partial Finetuning):**
 - **Steering (DSRL) and Residual Learning (EXPO):** These methods, which partially finetune GCPs (by steering initial noise or learning residual corrections, respectively), are found to be less effective than OGPO. They struggle when the base policy's action coverage is poor (e.g., KITCHEN tasks) or on high-precision tasks (e.g., ADROIT tasks), where they exhibit suboptimal performance or instability (Figures 9, 10, 11). A combined Steering + Residual (S/R) baseline showed limited improvement and instability (Figure 24).
- **Policy Extraction Methods:**
 - **Direct Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT):** The paper empirically shows that directly backpropagating Q-gradients through the entire generative process (denoising chain) of GCPs leads to unstable gradients and catastrophic training failures (Figure 16), validating OGPO's approach of using PPO-style, zero-order optimization for policy extraction.
 - **Behavior Cloning / Self-Distillation (QC):** QC, which uses Q-functions for Best-of-N action selection and behavior cloning, generally plateaus at lower performance, requires more hyperparameter tuning, and exhibits worse sample efficiency (Figures 9, 10, 11). This is attributed to its inability to adequately expand the support of the GCP action distribution for sufficient exploration.
 - **Alternative Policy Gradient Formulations (AWR-FM, AW-OGPO, OFPO++):** A comparison with Advantage-Weighted Regression (AWR) variants and FPO-style methods (OFPO++) for policy extraction reveals that OGPO's PPO-style updates are generally more effective and stable (Figure 17). Pure advantage-weighting of the flow loss (AWR-FM) notably failed across tasks. OGPO's non-exponentiated advantage weighting simplifies hyperparameter tuning, allowing for consistent parameters across tasks.

**Impact of OGPO+ Enhancements (Mitigating Critic Over-exploitation):**
The research identifies and addresses limitations of vanilla OGPO related to critic over-exploitation:

- **Vanilla OGPO Over-exploitation:** Without specific regularization, vanilla OGPO can over-optimize the critic, leading to a "success-speed tradeoff" where task length decreases rapidly, but success rates may plateau or oscillate (Figure 4a, 4d). This issue is more pronounced in pixel-based settings (Figure 5).
- **Success Buffer Regularization (OGPO+):** Incorporating a behavior cloning loss on transitions from successful episodes (stored in a "success buffer") is shown to be a critical modification. It consistently improves sample efficiency and asymptotic performance by anchoring the policy to known successful behaviors (Figure 4b, 4e, Figure 6).
- **Conservative Advantages (OGPO+CA):** This technique significantly mitigates the "offline-to-online performance dip." By requiring all critic ensemble members to agree on the sign of the advantage for an update to occur, it robustifies policy updates against noisy Q-value estimates. OGPO+CA also leads to more accurate and calibrated Q-value estimations, where critic values align more consistently with ground-truth Monte-Carlo returns across training stages (Figure 7).
- **Stable Training on Images (OGPO+CA):** OGPO+CA is crucial for stabilizing policy improvement in challenging high-dimensional, image-based environments, where vanilla OGPO+ and other baselines often fail to converge (Figure 8).

**Exploration Dynamics and Mode Preservation:**
A surprising finding is that OGPO, despite aggressively exploiting the critic for high success rates and task efficiency, also **generates highly diverse trajectories**.

- **Trajectory Diversity:** On the PushT task (Figure 14), OGPO learns faster execution and preserves trajectory-level multimodality, expanding the action manifold beyond the initial BC distribution. Penalizing action magnitudes leads to smaller steps and pruning of modes, demonstrating direct critic exploitation.
- **Orthogonal Variance Preservation:** OGPO selectively preserves action variance. In states demanding high precision, it aggressively reduces variance. However, in states where multiple near-optimal actions are possible, it preserves variance along directions *orthogonal* to the critic gradient (`∇_a Q(s, a)`) (Figure 15). Perturbations in these orthogonal directions have negligible first-order effects on critic values, allowing OGPO to maintain diversity without compromising task success. This mechanism is hypothesized to preserve trajectory-level multimodality, particularly at "decision points" where trajectories might branch (Figure 13).
- The ability of OGPO to perform full-policy finetuning across all steps of the generative process is considered a key factor in its capacity to preserve and strategically allocate variance.

**Optimality of PPO-style Updates:**
The study concludes that PPO-style updates are optimal for policy extraction within OGPO. They provide the most aggressive and stable critic exploitation and operate effectively with consistent hyperparameters across tasks. Negative-advantage gradients are found to be important for "sharpening" optimal modes and mitigating suboptimal action distributions learned during pretraining.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces OGPO as a significant contribution to the field of robot learning, particularly in the domain of finetuning generative control policies (GCPs). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancement in Sample-Efficient Robotic Reinforcement Learning:** OGPO provides a method that simultaneously achieves high sample efficiency and expressive full-policy updates for GCPs. This addresses a long-standing challenge in robot learning, bridging the gap between data-efficient partial finetuning and expressive but sample-inefficient full finetuning. By effectively reusing environment data while performing comprehensive policy updates computationally, OGPO makes advanced robotic skill acquisition more practical.

- **Enabling Robust Robot Deployment with Minimal Human Data:** A key impact is OGPO's demonstrated ability to fine-tune poorly-initialized behavior cloning policies to near full task success without requiring any additional expert data in the online replay buffer. This capability significantly reduces the reliance on extensive human demonstrations during robot deployment in novel or varied environments, paving the way for more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems. Robots can autonomously refine their skills based on self-collected experience, even when initial human data is scarce or imperfect.

- **Insights into Generative Policy Optimization Dynamics:** The paper provides a systematic empirical study that elucidates the mechanisms governing successful off-policy full-policy improvement. Findings regarding selective variance preservation, the role of conservative advantages, and the effectiveness of PPO-style updates offer valuable insights into how generative models can be robustly optimized within an RL framework. The observation that full-policy finetuning can enhance action diversity even when purely maximizing reward is particularly noteworthy, suggesting new avenues for understanding exploration in generative RL.

- **Practical Framework for Practitioners:** The detailed methodology, along with practical stabilization tricks (like success-buffer regularization and conservative advantages), and explicit hyperparameters, provides a comprehensive guide for researchers and engineers implementing and deploying OGPO. The algorithm’s ability to operate with consistent hyperparameters across diverse tasks enhances its practical utility.

- **Potential for Broader Applicability:** While demonstrated in robotics, the underlying principles of decoupling costly environment interactions from cheap internal generative process optimization, coupled with robust policy extraction, could extend to other domains that leverage iterative generative models for sequential decision-making, such as complex system control or even aspects of large language model interaction and refinement.

- **Addressing Critical Stability Issues:** The introduction of OGPO+ and OGPO+CA specifically addresses challenges like critic over-exploitation and the offline-to-online performance dip, which are common hurdles in modern RL. The effectiveness of these interventions, particularly in high-dimensional (e.g., pixel-based) settings, underscores the robustness and generalizability of the proposed approach.

In summary, OGPO represents a step toward more capable and autonomous robotic systems by offering a performant, sample-efficient, and stable method for adapting complex generative control policies.
