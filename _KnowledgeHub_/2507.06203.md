# A Survey on Latent Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> This comprehensive survey from a large multi-institutional collaboration examines "Latent Reasoning" in Large Language Models, an emerging paradigm that performs multi-step inference entirely within the model's high-bandwidth continuous hidden states to overcome the limitations of natural language-based explicit reasoning. It highlights the significant bandwidth advantage of latent representations (approximately 2700x higher) and provides a unified taxonomy of current methodologies.

## Problem

- Large Language Models primarily rely on explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, which verbalizes intermediate steps in natural language.
- This reliance on discrete tokens limits the model's expressive bandwidth compared to its continuous hidden states, which are significantly richer.
- Forcing LLMs to verbalize every reasoning step introduces computational redundancy, as much of their complex processing occurs internally within hidden states.

## Method

- Defines and systematically surveys =="Latent Reasoning" or "Latent Chain-of-Thought (Latent CoT)"== as a paradigm for multi-step inference within an LLM's continuous hidden state.
- Presents a comprehensive taxonomy that categorizes existing latent reasoning approaches based on how information is propagated, including ==vertical and horizontal recurrence==, mechanistic interpretability, and infinite-depth reasoning.
- Unifies disparate research efforts under a coherent conceptual framework to foster a clearer understanding of latent reasoning and chart future research directions.

## Results

- Latent representations offer an approximately **2700 times** higher bandwidth compared to explicit tokens, enabling richer internal expressivity.
- The survey consolidates latent reasoning into two main paradigms: Vertical Recurrence (e.g., architectural loops like Universal Transformer, or training-induced recurrence like Coconut) and Horizontal Recurrence (e.g., Linear-State models like Mamba-2, or Gradient-State models like TTT).
- Analysis of mechanistic interpretability shows that LLM layers exhibit specialized roles, with intermediate layers central to latent Chain-of-Thought, and highlights advanced paradigms for infinite-depth reasoning through spatial (text diffusion) and temporal (optimization-based) approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Latent reasoning, operating within high-bandwidth continuous hidden states, offers a significant improvement over the low-bandwidth discrete tokens used in explicit reasoning.
> - Latent reasoning methodologies can be categorized into vertical recurrence (iterative refinement of activations) and horizontal recurrence (evolution of hidden states across sequences).
> - Mechanistic interpretability reveals that different LLM layers specialize in distinct aspects of latent reasoning, with intermediate layers performing core computational steps.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has witnessed remarkable progress in reasoning capabilities, often attributed to the adoption of explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. In this widely used paradigm, LLMs verbalize intermediate steps in natural language before arriving at a final answer, which has significantly improved both interpretability and accuracy in complex tasks. Prominent models like Qwen3, DeepSeek-R1, and Gemini 2.5 series extensively leverage this principle.

However, the authors identify a critical bottleneck in explicit CoT: its reliance on natural language. This dependence limits the model's expressive bandwidth, as discrete tokens (approximately 15 bits each) inherently constrain the richness and fluidity of internal thought processes. Furthermore, forcing LLMs to verbalize every step can introduce redundant computation, as a significant portion of their processing occurs within continuous hidden states, not in the token space.

"M-A-P: A Survey on Latent Reasoning" directly addresses this fundamental limitation by proposing and systematically surveying "Latent Reasoning" or "Latent Chain-of-Thought (Latent CoT)." This emerging paradigm aims to perform multi-step inference entirely within the model’s continuous hidden state, eliminating the need for token-level supervision or verbalization of intermediate steps. Figure 1 of the paper powerfully illustrates the scale of this difference, showing a ~2.7 × 10³-fold bandwidth gap between explicit reasoning (discrete tokens) and latent reasoning (full 2560-dimensional FP16 hidden states).

This survey positions itself as a foundational effort to unify and advance the research landscape of latent reasoning. It acknowledges the success of explicit CoT but argues for the untapped potential of continuous, internal reasoning. By exploring methodologies that leverage the LLM's latent space, the work seeks to push beyond the performance and expressivity ceilings imposed by language-based reasoning, charting a new frontier in LLM cognition. It is a timely and critical contribution, as the field grapples with scaling LLMs to even more complex tasks and achieving higher levels of efficiency and coherence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey is to overcome the inherent limitations of explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in Large Language Models (LLMs). While explicit CoT has significantly enhanced LLM reasoning capabilities by externalizing intermediate steps in natural language, it fundamentally constrains the model's expressive bandwidth and introduces computational redundancy. The authors argue that LLMs inherently conduct the majority of their complex processing within their continuous latent space, making natural language a suboptimal medium for all internal "thought" processes. The reliance on discrete tokens (approximately 15 bits per token) is seen as a bottleneck compared to the much richer hidden state representations (approximately 40,960 bits per FP16 hidden state).

Against this backdrop, the key objective of this survey is to provide a comprehensive and structured overview of the nascent field of "Latent Reasoning" or "Latent Chain-of-Thought (Latent CoT)." Specifically, the authors aim to:

- **Examine the foundational role of neural network layers:** Highlight how hierarchical representations within these layers serve as the computational substrate for reasoning.
- **Explore diverse latent reasoning methodologies:** Categorize and analyze different approaches, including activation-based recurrence (vertical expansion of computational depth), hidden state propagation (horizontal expansion of sequential capacity), and various fine-tuning strategies that compress or internalize explicit reasoning traces.
- **Discuss advanced paradigms for infinite-depth reasoning:** Delve into cutting-edge concepts like masked diffusion models and optimization-based perspectives, which enable globally consistent and reversible reasoning processes.
- **Clarify the conceptual landscape:** Bring together disparate research efforts under a unified framework to foster a clearer understanding of latent reasoning.
- **Chart future directions for research:** Identify promising avenues for further exploration at the frontier of LLM cognition, moving towards more powerful and flexible AI systems.

In essence, the survey seeks to systematically map out this unexplored territory, demonstrating how latent reasoning can unlock greater expressivity and higher performance ceilings by enabling models to "think" in ways that are not constrained by human language.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey paper, "M-A-P: A Survey on Latent Reasoning" primarily employs a systematic literature review and a novel categorization methodology rather than presenting empirical experiments. The core approach involves:

- **Defining Latent Reasoning:** The paper begins by establishing a general mathematical framework for understanding various Latent CoT approaches, emphasizing continuous representations and hidden states. It contrasts this with explicit CoT, highlighting the significantly higher bandwidth of latent representations.
- **Taxonomic Organization:** The central methodological contribution is a comprehensive taxonomy (illustrated in Figure 2) that organizes existing research into distinct paradigms based on how information is propagated through the model:
 - **Preliminary: Latent Chain-of-Thought (§2):** Sets the stage with a general framework, connections to explicit CoT, and an initial look at latent reasoning in diffusion models.
 - **Latent Reasoning (§3):** This is the core categorization, differentiating between:
 - **Vertical Recurrent (Activation-based Methods):** Focuses on expanding computational *depth*. This includes methods that use architectural recurrence (e.g., Loop/Universal Transformer, CoTFormer) and those that induce recurrence through training (e.g., Coconut, CODI, use of filler/pause tokens).
 - **Horizontal Recurrent (Hidden State-based Methods):** Focuses on increasing *sequential capacity* by evolving hidden states. This covers Linear-State recurrence (e.g., Mamba-2, RWKV-6) and Gradient-State recurrence (e.g., TTT, Titans), which treat hidden states as online-learning parameters. It also discusses training-induced hidden-state conversion (e.g., SUPRA, MOHAWK) to adapt Transformers to RNN/SSM structures.
 - **Mechanistic Interpretability (§4):** Investigates the underlying "how" of latent reasoning within neural networks. It explores whether layer stacks reflect latent CoT, the specialized roles of shallow, intermediate, and deep layers, inter-layer information flow, and the theoretical concept of Turing completeness in Transformer architectures.
 - **Towards Infinite-depth Reasoning (§5):** Discusses advanced paradigms aimed at enabling unbounded computational steps for solution refinement. This section distinguishes between:
 - **Spatial Infinite Reasoning (Text Diffusion Models):** These models operate on entire output sequences in parallel, allowing global planning and iterative self-correction (categorized into Masked, Embedding-based, and Hybrid AR-Diffusion Models).
 - **Temporal Infinite Reasoning (Optimization-Based Perspective):** This views hidden state updates as iterative optimization steps, where longer sequences equate to greater reasoning depth (e.g., Infini-attention, Test-Time Training).
- **Conceptual Unification:** Throughout the survey, the authors strive to draw conceptual links between seemingly disparate approaches, such as relating hidden-state evolution to online optimization or connecting different forms of recurrence.
- **Associated Resources:** The survey is accompanied by a GitHub repository ("LatentCoT-Horizon") that collects papers and repositories, demonstrating a commitment to supporting the community's engagement with this emerging field.

By meticulously structuring the vast and fragmented body of literature, the authors provide a coherent framework for understanding the diverse facets of latent reasoning, identifying common principles and future research trajectories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey systematically synthesizes the current state of latent reasoning, presenting several key findings and conceptual advancements:

- **Fundamental Shift from Explicit to Latent Reasoning:** The core insight is that explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT), while effective, is constrained by the low bandwidth of natural language tokens. Latent CoT, operating in the continuous hidden state, offers a significantly higher bandwidth (~2700x), enabling richer expressivity and potentially more efficient, non-linguistic reasoning pathways.

- **Two Primary Latent Reasoning Paradigms:**
 - **Vertical Recurrence (Activation-based Methods):** These methods deepen the computational graph by iteratively refining activations. This can be achieved through:
 - **Architectural loops:** Models like Universal Transformer and CoTFormer explicitly re-apply the same layers multiple times, effectively increasing depth dynamically. There's a trend towards modular "Pre/Loop/Coda" structures and simpler dynamic stopping mechanisms.
 - **Training-induced recurrence:** Standard Transformers can be trained to exhibit recurrent behavior by feeding back hidden states (e.g., Coconut, CODI), compressing reasoning steps into discrete tokens (e.g., CCOT, gist tokens), or strategically inserting tokens (e.g., filler, pause, planning tokens) to provide more computational steps. These demonstrate that recurrence is not solely an architectural property but can emerge from training.
 - **Horizontal Recurrence (Hidden State-based Methods):** These methods expand reasoning along the temporal dimension by maintaining a compressed, fixed-size hidden state across long sequences, analogous to RNNs.
 - **Linear-State Recurrence:** Models like Mamba-2, GLA, and RWKV-6 use matrix-valued hidden states with update and decay rules, unifying diverse linear attention models under a general associative recurrent framework.
 - **Gradient-State Recurrence:** This more advanced perspective views hidden state updates as steps of an online optimization algorithm (e.g., TTT, Titans, ATLAS), where the hidden matrix acts as a "fast-adapting" parameter. This conceptually unifies temporal and depth-based recurrence, as processing longer sequences becomes mathematically equivalent to more optimization iterations, thus yielding greater reasoning depth.
 - **Training-induced Hidden-State Conversion:** Fine-tuning or distillation techniques (e.g., SUPRA, MOHAWK, LoLCATs, Liger) can convert quadratic self-attention Transformers into sub-quadratic RNN/SSM models, inheriting constant-memory inference while largely preserving performance.

- **Mechanistic Interpretability of Latent CoT in Layers:**
 - Layer depth directly correlates with latent reasoning capabilities; insufficient layers limit the ability to perform multi-step reasoning.
 - **Layer Specialization:** Different layers perform distinct roles: shallow layers handle basic processing, syntax, and factual recall; intermediate layers form the "core" of latent CoT, containing specialized reasoning sub-circuits, exhibiting powerful representations, and causally influencing reasoning outcomes; deep layers focus on output refinement and decision-making, though some studies note potential degradation issues.
 - **Information Flow:** Attention mechanisms are crucial for transmitting and integrating computational information across layers, analogous to a "Chain-of-Embedding."
 - **Turing Completeness:** Transformers are theoretically Turing complete under certain conditions, and prompting with explicit CoT can also render them Turing complete. This implies that general computation can be achieved either through architectural modifications or through interactive paradigms, unifying implicit (layer-by-layer) and explicit (token-by-token) computational expansion.

- **Towards Infinite-Depth Reasoning:** The survey highlights the pursuit of unbounded "thinking time" to refine solutions.
 - **Spatial Infinite Reasoning (Text Diffusion Models):** Unlike autoregressive models, diffusion models begin with a masked or noisy full output and iteratively denoise it in parallel. This allows for global planning, bidirectional context, and iterative self-correction across the entire sequence. Categories include Masked Diffusion Models, Embedding-based Diffusion Models, and Hybrid AR-Diffusion Models.
 - **Temporal Infinite Reasoning (Optimization-Based):** This perspective suggests that each token processed by models with gradient-state recurrence performs an additional optimization step, implicitly deepening the "reasoning layer." This allows for effectively infinite depth proportional to sequence length, while maintaining near-linear wall-clock cost through techniques like chunk-wise parallelism.

- **Challenges and Future Directions:** The survey notes the current lack of direct empirical comparisons between the varied latent reasoning models due to diverse training conditions and benchmarks. This highlights a need for a unified evaluation framework to assess their relative strengths, paving the way for more rigorous research.

In summary, the survey not only consolidates scattered research but also provides a coherent theoretical framework for understanding how LLMs can "think" more efficiently and powerfully in their internal continuous representations, opening new avenues for advancing AI cognition.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The survey "M-A-P: A Survey on Latent Reasoning" holds significant implications for the future of Large Language Models (LLMs) and artificial intelligence, offering a paradigm shift from current explicit reasoning strategies.

- **Unlocking New Performance Ceilings:** By moving reasoning into the continuous, high-bandwidth latent space, this work suggests a path to overcome the limitations imposed by discrete linguistic tokens. This could unlock substantially higher expressivity and lead to more powerful and accurate reasoning capabilities, especially for complex, multi-step tasks that current LLMs struggle with or perform inefficiently.

- **Enhancing Efficiency and Scalability:** Latent reasoning methods inherently bypass the latency associated with generating explicit intermediate tokens. This leads to faster inference times and more efficient computation, which is critical for deploying LLMs in real-time applications and scaling them to even larger models and more demanding workloads. The constant-memory properties of certain horizontal recurrence methods (like SSMs) further enable processing of arbitrarily long contexts, a major bottleneck for traditional Transformers.

- **Deeper Understanding of LLM Cognition:** The extensive section on mechanistic interpretability is crucial. By systematically analyzing how layers specialize and how information flows internally, the survey provides a foundational understanding of *how* LLMs implicitly reason. This moves beyond treating LLMs as black boxes and offers insights into their internal computational mechanisms, which is vital for building more reliable, controllable, and transparent AI systems. The concept of "layer-based hidden CoT" fundamentally changes how we view Transformer depth.

- **Guiding Future Research and Development:** The comprehensive taxonomy and the detailed discussion of current methodologies (vertical/horizontal recurrence, diffusion models, optimization-based perspectives) provide a clear roadmap for researchers. It unifies fragmented ideas, clarifies the conceptual landscape, and highlights promising areas for future investigation, such as hybrid architectures, advanced training strategies, and further exploration of infinite-depth reasoning.

- **Bridging Theory and Practice:** The paper connects theoretical concepts like Turing completeness to practical architectural designs and training methodologies. This synergistic approach will likely inspire new model architectures and training paradigms that explicitly leverage the principles of latent reasoning. The idea that "depth emerges from optimization over time" offers a powerful unifying view for designing next-generation LLMs.

In essence, this survey is not merely a summary of existing work; it is a vision statement for how LLMs can evolve to "think" more like sophisticated, non-linguistic computational systems. By enabling higher-bandwidth, more efficient, and deeply integrated internal reasoning, latent reasoning has the potential to fundamentally transform LLM capabilities, leading to more intelligent, robust, and scalable AI.
