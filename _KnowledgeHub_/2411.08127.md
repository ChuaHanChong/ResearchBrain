# TIPO: Text to Image with Text Presampling for Prompt Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> TIPO provides an efficient framework for automatically refining text-to-image prompts by aligning user inputs with the statistical properties of T2I model training data. It generates richer, distribution-consistent prompts, leading to improved image quality, reduced artifacts, and enhanced user experience across diverse T2I models.

## Problem

- Generating high-quality images from Text-to-Image (T2I) models often requires complex, detailed prompts, demanding significant artistic expertise from users.
- Existing prompt optimization methods, such as direct LLM application, database fine-tuning, or reinforcement learning, face challenges like misalignment, high computational cost, and limited generalization across different T2I architectures.
- The need for precise control over T2I outputs creates a barrier to broader adoption due to the difficulty of prompt engineering.

## Method

- **TIPO** (==Text-to-Image Prompt Optimization==) is introduced as a computationally efficient and scalable method to reformulate user input into prompts that align with the text distributions of T2I model training data.
- The approach uses a structured prompt schema and a multi-task pre-sampling algorithm, executed by a lightweight LLaMA architecture (200M parameters).
- A curated 30M-pair, 40B-token caption corpus, combining tag-based and natural language data, is used for pre-training and fine-tuning the model.

## Results

- TIPO achieved superior distribution alignment (FDD score of **0.2282**) and lower AI corrupt scores in in-domain tag-based prompt optimization, outperforming baselines like GPT-4o-mini.
- In human preference evaluations, TIPO-optimized images secured the highest overall win rate at **51.3%** across metrics like prompt adherence and aesthetic appeal.
- The framework demonstrated efficiency gains, reducing per-prompt latency by up to **29.4%** and significantly lowering training costs compared to RL-based optimization methods, while maintaining broad compatibility with diverse T2I models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimal prompts for Text-to-Image models inherently align with the large-scale text distributions (including aesthetic fine-tuning data) on which these models were trained.
> - A multi-task pre-sampling mechanism, rather than plain text completion, can effectively expand simple user inputs into structured, informative, and edit-friendly prompts while maintaining original intent.
> - Lightweight, pre-trained language models can achieve robust prompt optimization across various T2I architectures without the high computational overhead of reinforcement learning or large general-purpose LLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of Text-to-Image (T2I) generative models has enabled the creation of diverse visual content from textual prompts. However, generating high-quality images often necessitates crafting detailed and complex prompts, which can be challenging for users lacking artistic expertise or deep understanding of T2I model prompt sensitivities. This has led to an increasing focus on prompt optimization, a field dedicated to automatically expanding and refining user-provided input into more effective prompts.

Existing approaches to prompt optimization generally fall into several categories. Some methods leverage Large Language Models (LLMs) to rewrite prompts in a zero-shot manner. These approaches can struggle with misalignment, as LLMs are trained on general natural language rather than the specific structured prompts often favored by T2I models. Another strategy involves fine-tuning LLMs on user-collected prompt databases, which can be limited by the inconsistency and varying quality of user-generated data. Reinforcement Learning (RL) techniques have also been employed, where aesthetic scores of generated images guide LLM training for prompt optimization. While effective for specific T2I models, RL-based methods are computationally intensive and may not generalize well across different T2I architectures. Iterative refinement using Vision-Language Models (VLMs) is another recent trend, where VLMs evaluate prompt-image pairs and suggest revisions, but this still faces potential distribution mismatch with T2I training data.

TIPO distinguishes itself by proposing that optimal prompts should inherently align with the large-scale text distributions underlying T2I models' training data, including those emphasized during aesthetic fine-tuning. This work moves prompt optimization into the domain of large-scale multi-task pre-training, aiming for computational efficiency and broad compatibility across T2I models, in contrast to resource-intensive or model-specific approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and scalable method for automatic prompt refinement in text-to-image (T2I) generation, which the authors term TIPO (Text-to-Image Prompt Optimization).

The motivation for TIPO stems from several challenges in the current T2I landscape:

1. **Complexity of Prompt Engineering**: Modern T2I models, particularly those aesthetically fine-tuned, often require lengthy, detailed, and nuanced text descriptions—ranging from formatted tags to complex paragraphs—to achieve precise control over generative visual content. This complexity forces users into iterative trial-and-error, requiring significant expertise.
2. **Accessibility Barriers**: High-quality T2I artwork is largely accessible to users with considerable artistic knowledge or specialized prompt engineering skills, creating a barrier for broader adoption.
3. **Limitations of Existing Optimization Methods**:
 - **Direct LLM Application**: Relying on general-purpose LLMs for prompt rewriting often leads to misalignment with the structured, specialized prompts used by T2I models, necessitating further manual refinement.
 - **Prompt Databases**: Methods that train LLMs on user-collected prompt databases are constrained by the inconsistent quality and expertise of contributors, leading to suboptimal or inconsistent outputs.
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: While RL-based methods optimize prompts effectively for specific T2I models using aesthetic scores as rewards, they are computationally expensive and lack generalization to a wider variety of models.
 - **Iterative VLM Refinement**: Although iterative refinement can improve quality, a persistent distribution mismatch between VLMs (used for evaluation) and T2I training data limits effectiveness.
4. **Hypothesis of Distribution Alignment**: The core motivation for TIPO is the hypothesis that "good prompts should align with the large-scale text distributions of T2I models’ training." By aligning user prompts with these intrinsic distributions, T2I models can better interpret intent and leverage learned priors, resulting in stronger text alignment and overall image quality.

Therefore, TIPO aims to overcome these limitations by providing a computationally efficient, scalable, and generalizable framework for prompt optimization that expands simple user inputs into richer, distribution-aligned, and edit-friendly prompts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TIPO's methodology centers on reformulating user input into prompts that align with the high-quality text distributions used in T2I model training. This is achieved through a structured prompt schema and a multi-task pre-sampling algorithm.

#### 3.1. Text Set Preparation

The foundation of TIPO is a curated 30M-pair, 40B-token caption corpus, filtered and balanced for compatibility with leading T2I models and aesthetic quality. This corpus combines:
- **Danbooru2023**: A large-scale dataset of anime-style images with detailed, comma-separated tag-based captions.
- **GBC10M**: 10 million images from CC12M, annotated using Graph-Based Captioning (GBC), where only root node captions are utilized.
- **Coyo HD 11M**: 11.4 million high-resolution images with synthetic captions generated by LLaVA-Next-8B, post-processed for conciseness.
The raw captions from these datasets are categorized into "tag-based" and "natural language (NL)-based" formats, represented as a unified text set $T = \{t_1, t_2..., t_n\}$. To mitigate excessively lengthy or information-overloaded prompts, a simpler subset is constructed by removing elements from the original captions.

#### 3.2. Formatted Prompt Construction

Prompts are structured with common metadata (e.g., `<Category>: <Content>` for style, aspect ratio, quality, year) to provide strong guidance to T2I models. The system generates both simple (incomplete) and complete prompts for each image to train an auto-regressive language model to expand the simple versions.

- **Tag-based Prompts**: Given a complete set of tags $T$, the tags are randomly shuffled. A simpler tag set $T_s$ is created by randomly selecting $m < n$ tags, ensuring $T_s$ is a prefix of the concatenated full tags $p_o = \text{concat}(T)$. The simple prompt is $p_s = \text{concat}(T_s)$.
- **NL-based Prompts**: For an ordered sequence of sentences $S = [\text{sentence}_1..., \text{sentence}_n]$, a simple subsequence $S_s$ is derived by preserving the first sentence and randomly dropping others, while maintaining original order. The simple prompt is $p_s = \text{concat}(S_s)$, and the original $p_o = \text{concat}(S_s, S)$.

#### 3.3. Text Pre-sampling

TIPO employs a flexible pre-sampling mechanism to reformulate user input, balancing preservation of original intent with distribution-consistent expansion. Instead of plain text completion or full rewriting, TIPO appends a structured, informative, and edit-friendly segment to the original input. This process is decomposed into three subtasks and executed as either basic (single transformation) or composite (chained transformations) tasks:

- **Basic Tasks**:
 - `tag to long`: Generates an NL prompt from tags.
 - `long to tag`: Extends a tag sequence from an NL prompt.
 - `short to tag`: Extends a tag sequence from a short prompt.
 - `short to long`: Generates a refined, detailed NL prompt from a user-provided NL prompt.
- **Composite Tasks**: Chains two basic tasks, such as `short to tag to long` (short NL/tags to refined NL prompt).

During training, tasks are randomly selected to enhance model generalization. For combined inputs (e.g., tags $T_s$ and short NL $S_s$), TIPO processes each type separately and progressively aggregates the outputs:
1. `short to tag`: Uses $T_s$ to generate a detailed tag sequence $T_d$.
2. `tag to long`: Uses $T_d$ as metadata to produce a refined short NL prompt $S_s$.
3. `short to tag to long`: With $T_d$ and $S_s$ in metadata, generates a comprehensive long NL prompt $S_d$.
4. Finally, TIPO aggregates $T_d$, $S_d$, and any additional metadata into a context-rich prompt $p_d$.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Architecture**: TIPO utilizes the LLaMA architecture, with a 200M-parameter model used for primary experiments, and 100M and 500M variants for analysis.
- **Tokenizer**: A vocabulary of 32,000 LLaMA2 tokens plus 13 special tokens for tasks, length control, and placeholders.
- **Length Control**: Unique length tags (`<very_short>`, `<short>`, `<long>`, `<very_long>`) enforce specific constraints on tag counts and NL sentence lengths (e.g., `<long>` specifies 36-52 tags and 4-8 sentences).
- **Random Augmentation**: Techniques include random removal/repositioning of metadata tags, shuffling of content tag order, length-based truncation, and selective sentence removal for NL prompts to enhance input diversity.
- **Optimizer**: AdamW with $\beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.98$, and weight decay of 0.01.
- **Learning Schedule**: Cosine annealing learning rate scheduler.
- **Training**: Multi-stage training (pretrain and finetune) across Danbooru2023, GBC10M, and CoyoHD11M datasets. The 200M model was trained for 150 hours (pretrain) and 270 hours (finetune) on 4 NVIDIA RTX3090 GPUs.
- **Inference**: A hybrid stochastic decoding strategy combining nucleus sampling (top-p = 0.95) and top-k = 60 filtering. The pipeline is designed to handle various input types (tags, NL, or both) and combines different tasks (e.g., `short to tag` followed by `tag to long`).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate TIPO's effectiveness across various metrics and T2I models, both in-domain and out-of-domain.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Metrics

The performance was assessed using:
- **FDD (Fréchet DINO Distance)**: Measures fidelity by comparing DINOv2 features, aligning with human perception. Lower is better.
- **Aesthetic Score**: Quantifies visual appeal, composition, and artistic merit via Aesthetic Predictor V2.5. Higher is better.
- **AI Corrupt Score**: Detects technical flaws and visual artifacts. Lower is better.
- **Vendi Score**: Quantifies image diversity using von Neumann entropy from DinoV2 embeddings. Higher is generally better when accompanied by quality.
- **Human Preference**: Pairwise comparison win rates and ELO ratings based on prompt adherence, image quality, aesthetic appeal, and overall preference.
- **CLIPScore**: Measures semantic consistency between optimized prompts and generated images. Higher is better.
- **Prompt Distribution Alignment**: Fréchet Distance (FD) and Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) of text embeddings using T5-XXL and jina-embeddings-v3 encoders. Lower is better.
- **Efficiency**: Prompt generation latency (seconds per prompt) and speedup.

#### 4.2. Experimental Results

- **In-domain Tag-based Prompt Optimization**:
 - TIPO achieved the best FDD score (0.2282) by a substantial margin over GPT-4o-mini (0.5414) on scenery tags, indicating superior distribution alignment.
 - It also showed the lowest AI Corrupt Score (0.0805) and competitive Aesthetic Score (6.2571). MagicPrompt and Promptist underperformed in Aesthetic and AI Corrupt scores.
- **In-domain NL-based Prompt Optimization (Short & Truncated Long)**:
 - TIPO consistently achieved either the best or second-best Aesthetic and AI Corrupt Scores on short and truncated long NL prompts, indicating effective prompt enrichment without introducing noise.
 - While all methods showed some compromise in fidelity (FDD) and diversity (Vendi) compared to original prompts, TIPO remained competitive, maintaining semantic deviation through progressive refinement.
- **Out-of-domain Performance (SD-3.5-Large)**:
 - TIPO enhanced the diversity (Vendi Score of 21.571 vs. GPT-4o-mini's 8.9718) of images generated by SD-3.5-Large, even though SD-3.5-Large's training data differs from TIPO's.
 - While GPT-generated prompts yielded lower AI Corrupt Scores, TIPO demonstrated competitive aesthetic quality while increasing diversity.
- **Compatibility with other T2I Models**:
 - TIPO generally improved image quality and alignment across models with undisclosed training data (FLUX.1-dev, Omnigen2, Lumina-2, HiDream-I1, Gemini-2.0-flash-image). For instance, Lumina-2 and HiDream-I1 showed higher aesthetic scores and lower corruption rates post-TIPO optimization.
 - TIPO even yielded measurable improvements over Gemini-2.0-flash-image's integrated optimization, underscoring its practical value.
- **Efficiency**:
 - TIPO demonstrated significant runtime efficiency, reducing per-prompt latency by up to 29.4% compared to baselines like Promptist and PromptExtend. The 200M model generated prompts in approximately 1.03 seconds.
 - Training cost analysis showed TIPO achieves the lowest cost per 1k prompts (0.056 GPU-hours/1k prompts) compared to RL-based methods like PAE (0.20) and Promptist (0.70), due to its larger training corpus and independence from T2I rollouts.
- **Human Preference Evaluation**:
 - Across 1,400+ pairwise comparisons from 221 volunteers, TIPO achieved the highest overall win rate at 51.3% (52.5% in out-of-domain scenarios), significantly outperforming competitors.
 - ELO ratings consistently ranked TIPO highest across prompt adherence, image quality, aesthetic, and overall criteria.
- **Prompt Distribution Alignment**:
 - TIPO consistently exhibited better embedding-space alignment (lower FD and MMD) with T2I training corpora (COYO and Danbooru2023) across both Jina and T5 text encoders and different prompt types (NL-short, NL-trunc, Tag-based). This supports the hypothesis that TIPO's gains stem from closer alignment with T2I training text distributions.
- **Prompt–Image Alignment**:
 - TIPO achieved the strongest CLIPScore for tag-based prompts. While MagicPrompt had higher scores for NL-based prompts, this was attributed to its stylistic bias from its specific community-sourced training corpus, which TIPO did not share, yet TIPO remained competitive.
- **Ablation Study (Appendix H)**:
 - Experiments confirmed that TIPO-enhanced prompts consistently lowered AI corruption artifacts and generally boosted aesthetic scores, while maintaining computational efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TIPO represents a notable contribution to the field of text-to-image generation by introducing an efficient and effective framework for automatic prompt optimization. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

1. **Enhanced Image Quality and Coherence**: By aligning user prompts with the intrinsic textual distributions of T2I model training datasets, TIPO facilitates stronger text alignment, reduced visual artifacts, and improved overall image quality, coherence, and detail.
2. **Computational Efficiency and Scalability**: Unlike resource-intensive methods based on large language models (LLMs) or reinforcement learning (RL), TIPO employs a lightweight pre-trained model and a multi-task pre-sampling pipeline. This design provides computational efficiency (up to 29.4% runtime improvement) and scalability, making automated prompt engineering more practical for real-world applications. Its training cost per prompt is significantly lower than RL-based methods.
3. **Broad Compatibility and Generalization**: TIPO is designed to be compatible with a broad spectrum of T2I models, including those with undisclosed training data. Its ability to refine both tag-based and natural language inputs into unified, distribution-consistent forms enhances its generalization capabilities, making it adaptable to diverse T2I architectures.
4. **Improved User Experience and Accessibility**: TIPO lowers the barrier to entry for high-quality T2I generation by automating the complex task of prompt engineering. Users can start with simple inputs, and TIPO expands them into detailed, effective prompts, democratizing access to advanced T2I capabilities and enabling more effective creative workflows.
5. **Strong Human Preference**: Extensive human evaluation studies consistently show that TIPO-optimized images receive higher preference rates compared to those generated with other prompt optimization methods or original prompts, indicating a strong alignment with user expectations and aesthetic judgments.
6. **Principled Approach to Prompt Engineering**: The core insight of TIPO—that optimal prompts align with the statistical properties of T2I training data—offers a principled direction for future research in prompt engineering, moving beyond heuristic or brute-force methods.

Despite its current capabilities, the authors acknowledge several avenues for future exploration, including improving generalization to out-of-distribution user inputs and T2I models, model-specific adaptation, personalization, integration of image-feedback-aware refinement, and further scaling of the underlying language model. The release of trained models and source code promotes reproducibility and aims to inspire further advancements in efficient, scalable, and robust generative frameworks.
