# OpenBench: A New Benchmark and Baseline for Semantic Navigation in Smart Logistics

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces OpenBench, a new benchmark for outdoor semantic navigation in last-mile delivery, and the Openstreetmap-enhanced oPen-air sEmantic Navigation (OPEN) system. The OPEN system combines OpenStreetMap data with Large Language Models and Vision-Language Models to achieve robust, scalable navigation, outperforming learning-based baselines in both simulated and real-world environments while significantly reducing map storage requirements.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic navigation methods are resource-intensive, requiring high-precision maps that hinder large-scale and flexible deployment in dynamic outdoor environments.
- Existing learning-based navigation approaches often suffer from limited generalization capabilities and require extensive training data, struggling with the reality gap in diverse, long-term outdoor tasks.
- Current semantic navigation benchmarks primarily focus on indoor environments, failing to capture the interactive and long-term operational demands crucial for outdoor last-mile delivery.

## Method

- ==The OPEN== system integrates LLMs for natural language instruction interpretation and task planning, VLMs for global localization and dynamic map updates, and classic algorithms for local planning and control.
- It utilizes OpenStreetMap (OSM) as a lightweight, flexible, and scalable map representation, eliminating the need for pre-constructed, high-precision semantic maps.
- A new benchmark, **OpenBench**, is introduced with tailored metrics (SRTP, LSR, LSPL) to evaluate interactive, continuous, and long-term last-mile delivery tasks in residential outdoor environments.

## Results

- The OPEN system achieved **100%** success rate in small and medium simulated environments (compared to 20-**40%** for baselines) and maintained strong long-term success rates (e.g., **75.58%** LSR in medium environment).
- GPT-4O-mini demonstrated a **100%** Success Rate of Task Planning (SRTP), accurately parsing delivery instructions, outperforming other LLMs.
- OpenStreetMap-based map representation required significantly less storage, approximately **1%** of a downsampled point cloud map and **0.01%** of a topological map, while OPEN successfully completed real-world delivery sequences where learning-based baselines failed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hybrid navigation approach, combining the semantic understanding of foundation models with the scalability of OpenStreetMap and the robustness of classic algorithms, can effectively address real-world last-mile delivery challenges.
> - Powerful LLMs like GPT-4O-mini can achieve near-perfect task planning from natural language instructions, significantly enhancing robot interaction and autonomy.
> - Dynamic map updates, facilitated by VLMs recognizing new elements, continuously improve navigation efficiency and system adaptability over sequential delivery tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous robotic navigation is rapidly advancing, driven by increasing demands in areas like smart logistics. This paper positions itself at the intersection of traditional robotics, modern deep learning, and practical real-world deployment challenges.

Historically, robotic navigation has relied heavily on **traditional methods** such as Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM), path planning, and control algorithms. These approaches often require high-precision, pre-constructed maps, which are resource-intensive to create and maintain, thus hindering large-scale and flexible deployment, especially in dynamic outdoor environments like residential areas. The paper explicitly points out that such methods "are resource-intensive and hinder large-scale deployment."

More recently, **learning-based approaches**, particularly those leveraging reinforcement learning or end-to-end vision-based navigation (e.g., NoMaD, ViNT mentioned in the paper), have gained traction. While promising, these methods often suffer from limited generalization capabilities, require extensive training data, and can struggle with the "reality gap" between simulated and real-world performance. They are often tailored for short-range or indoor navigation and lack the robustness needed for diverse, long-term outdoor tasks. The paper highlights that these approaches "often struggle with generalization in real-world scenarios and require extensive training data."

The advent of **foundation models**, specifically Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), has opened new avenues for semantic navigation. These models possess powerful capabilities for understanding natural language instructions and interpreting visual information semantically. Existing research in this area (e.g., VLMaps, Vision-Language Frontier Maps, LM-Nav) has primarily focused on **indoor environments** or conceptual semantic understanding, often overlooking the practical challenges of outdoor, urban last-mile delivery. Dhruv et al. is noted as one of the few addressing outdoor semantic navigation, but the landscape for dedicated benchmarks remains sparse.

This work critically identifies a significant gap: the inadequacy of existing benchmarks for evaluating **large-scale outdoor semantic navigation in smart logistics**, particularly for last-mile delivery in residential areas. Current benchmarks predominantly focus on indoor settings and do not sufficiently capture the "interactiveness and the long-term operational demands of real-world last-mile delivery."

Therefore, this paper makes a crucial contribution by:
1. **Bridging the gap** between traditional robust navigation techniques and the powerful semantic understanding of foundation models. It proposes a hybrid approach that leverages the strengths of both.
2. **Addressing the outdoor, last-mile delivery challenge** specifically, which is distinct from general indoor navigation or abstract semantic understanding.
3. **Introducing a novel benchmark (OpenBench)** and a baseline system (OPEN) tailored to these specific real-world operational demands, thereby fostering further research and development in this vital application area.

By combining the scalability and flexibility of OpenStreetMap (OSM) with the semantic intelligence of LLMs and VLMs, the authors aim to overcome the limitations of both traditional and purely learning-based methods, offering a solution that is both "scalable" and "easy to deploy." This positions the work as a practical and timely advancement in embodied AI and robotic logistics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation for this research stems from the rapidly increasing demand for efficient and autonomous last-mile delivery in smart logistics. Autonomous robots offer a compelling solution to enhance efficiency, reduce costs, improve customer experience, and minimize reliance on manual labor. However, significant challenges impede their widespread deployment.

The key objectives and motivations are multifold:

1. **Overcome Limitations of Traditional Navigation Methods:** Traditional approaches that rely on high-precision semantic maps are resource-intensive, requiring extensive pre-mapping efforts. This cost and labor intensity significantly hinder large-scale deployment and adaptability to diverse, dynamic environments. The paper aims to eliminate this dependency.
2. **Address Generalization Issues in Learning-Based Approaches:** Recent learning-based navigation methods, while promising, often struggle with generalization across varying real-world scenarios and demand vast amounts of training data. This limits their practical applicability in open-world settings. The authors are motivated to develop a system with superior generalization capabilities.
3. **Enable Scalable, Interactive, and Easy-to-Deploy Outdoor Navigation:** The core motivation is to create a robotic navigation system that is:
 - **Scalable:** Capable of operating in large and diverse outdoor environments without prohibitive upfront costs or mapping efforts.
 - **Interactive:** Able to comprehend natural language instructions, mimicking human-like understanding for delivery tasks.
 - **Easy to Deploy:** Requiring minimal setup and relying on readily available, open-source data.
4. **Introduce an OpenStreetMap-enhanced oPen-air sEmantic Navigation (OPEN) System:** This is the proposed solution designed to integrate foundation models with classic algorithms. Specific objectives for the OPEN system include:
 - Utilizing **OpenStreetMap (OSM)** as a lightweight and flexible map representation to eliminate pre-mapping needs.
 - Employing **Large Language Models (LLMs)** for comprehensive understanding and analysis of natural language delivery instructions.
 - Incorporating **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)** for robust global localization (even without reliable GPS), dynamic map updates, and fine-grained tasks like house number recognition.
 - Ensuring navigation stability and efficiency through the synergy of these components.
5. **Develop a New Benchmark for Last-Mile Delivery:** The authors identify a critical void in existing semantic navigation benchmarks, which are primarily focused on indoor environments and fail to capture the nuances and long-term operational demands of outdoor last-mile delivery in residential areas. A key objective is to:
 - Create a benchmark specifically tailored for outdoor navigation in residential settings.
 - Simulate real-world conditions for robots to navigate from a starting point to a customer's front door using only navigation maps.
 - Propose new evaluation metrics that account for interactivity, task comprehension, and sustained performance over continuous, sequential delivery tasks.
6. **Provide a Baseline Implementation and Publicly Available Resources:** The paper aims to present the OPEN system as a robust baseline for this new benchmark and to make the code and benchmark publicly available, thereby fostering collaborative research and accelerating advancements in the field.

In essence, the authors are driven to advance the state-of-the-art in autonomous last-mile delivery by proposing a practical, scalable, and intelligent navigation system, validated by a purpose-built, realistic benchmark.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a novel **Openstreetmap-enhanced oPen-air sEmantic Navigation (OPEN) system** and introduce **OpenBench**, a new benchmark specifically for last-mile delivery. The methodology combines classic robotic navigation algorithms with cutting-edge foundation models, anchored by the use of OpenStreetMap (OSM) as a dynamic and lightweight map representation.

#### 3.1. OPEN System Overview

The OPEN system is designed for autonomous last-mile delivery, starting from a natural language delivery request and guiding a robot to a customer's front door. It comprises several interconnected modules:

- **A. LLM-based Task Planning:** This module processes free-form, multilingual natural language delivery instructions into structured robotic tasks.
 - **Address Resolution:** Uses an initial LLM prompt to extract hierarchical sub-addresses from instructions, followed by a secondary prompt for verification to mitigate LLM "hallucinations."
 - **Task Optimization:** Another LLM prompt optimizes the sequence of tasks by grouping geographically proximate deliveries and modeling tasks across regions as a classical scheduling problem.
 - **Location Query:** Queries OSM using hierarchical sub-addresses. If the lowest-level address is found, the robot navigates directly. If not, the robot navigates to the lowest known address and then enters an exploration mode for the missing details.
- **B. Waypoints Generation:** Based on the task sequence, the system generates waypoints for either navigation or exploration.
 - **Navigation Mode:** For known addresses in OSM, it converts OSM road network data into a hierarchical graph. The Multi-Level Dijkstra (MLD) algorithm is used for efficient global routing between geographic coordinates.
 - **Exploration Mode:** When OSM lacks fine-grained details (e.g., specific building entrances), the robot retrieves the target building's geometry from OSM, calculates an inflated concave hull as a search area, and uniformly samples waypoints within it. At each waypoint, a VLM (Vision-Language Model) analyzes captured images via an RGB camera to identify the target entrance (e.g., house number recognition).
- **C. VLM-based Global Localization and Map Update:** This module addresses odometry drift and unreliable GPS, aligning the robot's pose with OSM coordinates and supporting dynamic map updates.
 - **Global Localization:** MobileSAM segments objects in camera images. CLIP encodes these segmented images and OSM element types (e.g., buildings, roads) into a shared embedding space, assigning semantic labels based on probability. Point clouds are projected onto images for semantic information, then retained points are projected onto a Bird’s Eye View (BEV) plane. Finally, 2D registration with OSM-derived geometry provides the robot's global pose. This process leverages CLIP's zero-shot generalization for open-world environments.
 - **Map Update:** Similar to global localization, but focuses on adding newly detected elements (e.g., house number plates) to OSM, enhancing map detail for future deliveries.
- **D. Local Localization and Planning:**
 - **Local Localization:** Uses FastLIO2 for precise LiDAR-based local pose estimation. A factor graph integrates local state estimations as odometry factors and global estimations from the VLM as prior factors, optimizing the map-to-odometry transformation to maintain bounded localization error.
 - **Path Planning:** Converts OSM into a costmap for A\* pathfinding, followed by the Timed Elastic Band (TEB) algorithm to generate smooth, dynamically feasible trajectories for real-time robot control.

#### 3.2. Last-Mile Delivery Benchmark (OpenBench)

To evaluate systems like OPEN, a new benchmark is proposed, focusing on the interactivity, ease of deployment, and long-term reliability required for last-mile delivery.

- **Task Definition:** The robot must interpret natural language instructions and navigate autonomously from a starting point to a customer’s front door, relying solely on publicly accessible OSM data (no pre-constructed maps).
- **Simulation Environment:** Built on the Gazebo platform, it includes three distinct world models (small, medium, large) with varying environmental complexities. Buildings are labeled with house numbers, and corresponding OSM data is generated.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** A comprehensive set of metrics is introduced:
 - **Success Rate of Task Planning (SRTP):** Quantifies the precision of LLM-based task planning, reflecting instruction comprehension.
 - **Success Rate (SR) and Success Weighted by Path Length (SPL):** Standard metrics for task completion and path efficiency in individual tasks.
 - **Long-term Success Rate (LSR) and Long-term Success Weighted by Path Length (LSPL):** Novel metrics for sequential delivery tasks, extending SR and SPL by incorporating an exponential decay weighting factor (c\_i) that prioritizes earlier tasks. This accounts for the cascading impact of early failures on subsequent tasks in a continuous operational setting, crucial for real-world last-mile delivery.

The approach combines robust, publicly available map data (OSM) with the semantic understanding capabilities of modern AI models (LLMs, VLMs) and established robotics algorithms (Dijkstra, A\*, TEB, FastLIO2, factor graphs) to create a scalable, intelligent, and deployable navigation solution for complex outdoor tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted in both simulated Gazebo environments and a real-world campus setting, using a differential-drive robot equipped with a camera and LiDAR. The proposed OPEN system was evaluated against learning-based navigation systems, NoMaD and ViNT, which use goal images and topological maps.

#### 4.1. Simulation Environment Results

1. **Success Rate of Task Planning (SRTP):**
 - GPT-4O-mini achieved a perfect SRTP of 1.0 (100%), demonstrating its superior ability to accurately parse delivery instructions into structured tasks compared to other LLMs like Gemini-1.5-pro (0.27), Qwen-turbo (0.5), WenXinYiYan (0.6), GPT-3.5-turbo (0.9), and Claude-3.5 (0.97). This highlights the critical role of robust LLMs in the initial task comprehension phase.

2. **Navigation Performance (SR, SPL, LSR, LSPL):**
 - **Superiority of OPEN:** OPEN significantly outperformed NoMaD and ViNT across all simulation environments (small, medium, large).
 - In the **small environment**, OPEN achieved 100% SR, compared to 40% for NoMaD and ViNT. OPEN also showed a higher SPL (35.93%) and, importantly, maintained 100% LSR and 61.31% LSPL for continuous tasks.
 - In the **medium environment**, OPEN maintained 100% SR (compared to 20% for baselines) and 32.24% SPL. For continuous tasks, it achieved 75.58% LSR and 12.92% LSPL.
 - In the **large environment**, OPEN still managed 60% SR (baselines achieved 0%) and 31.12% SPL. Its long-term performance was 83.98% LSR and 47.97% LSPL.
 - **Generalization and Robustness:** NoMaD and ViNT struggled with generalization, often encountering collisions and failing, especially in more complex environments. Their multi-task capabilities (LSR/LSPL) were not evaluated due to their limitations in continuous operation. OPEN's strong performance, even in larger and more complex environments, validates its approach of leveraging OSM guidance and VLM capabilities.
 - **Impact of Sequential Tasks:** The decrease in successful tasks (from individual SR to sequential LSR) for OPEN in medium and large environments indicates that sequential execution introduces cumulative challenges. However, the higher weighting of earlier tasks in the LSR/LSPL metrics allowed the system to achieve strong overall long-term scores, particularly in the large environment, demonstrating its sustained reliability.

3. **Influence of Map Update:**
 - The map update mechanism (incorporating new elements like door locations into OSM) significantly improved navigation efficiency. SPL increased by 30.61%, 6.31%, and 47.87% for three different destinations when map updates were enabled. This confirms the value of dynamic map enrichment for enhancing future delivery efficiency.

4. **Map Storage Efficiency:**
 - OPEN's use of OSM for map representation proved highly efficient. The storage space for OSM was approximately 1% of a downsampled point cloud map and 0.01% of a topological map (used by NoMaD). For instance, in the large environment, OSM data was 37.1kB, compared to 10.2MB for a point cloud map and 720.15MB for a topological map. This is a critical finding for practical, scalable deployment on resource-constrained robots.

#### 4.2. Real-World Environment Results

- **Robustness in Reality:** Experiments on a real-world campus (Beijing University of Chemical Technology) involved sequential deliveries to two buildings.
 - Both ViNT and NoMaD failed, encountering collisions while navigating to the first building, highlighting their limitations in real-world outdoor generalization.
 - The proposed OPEN system successfully completed the entire delivery sequence. Its performance closely aligned with a human-operated remote control baseline, which achieved an SPL of 96.1%.
- This real-world validation underscores OPEN's practicality and robustness, demonstrating its ability to handle complex navigation tasks in unstructured outdoor settings where other learning-based methods faltered.

In summary, the results consistently demonstrate that the OPEN system, with its hybrid approach of combining foundation models with OSM and classic algorithms, offers significant improvements in navigation efficiency, reliability, and especially long-term operational capability for last-mile delivery. The new OpenBench benchmark effectively highlights these advantages, particularly in terms of generalization and resource efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents significant contributions to the fields of robotics, artificial intelligence, and logistics, with a clear potential for substantial real-world impact.

1. **Enabling Scalable and Deployable Last-Mile Delivery:** The most immediate and profound impact lies in providing a truly scalable and easily deployable solution for autonomous last-mile delivery. By leveraging OpenStreetMap (OSM)—a freely available, crowdsourced global map—the OPEN system entirely bypasses the resource-intensive and time-consuming process of creating high-precision, proprietary maps. This eliminates a major barrier to the widespread adoption of delivery robots in residential and urban areas. Robots can be deployed "off-the-shelf" in new locations with minimal setup, dramatically reducing operational costs and time.

2. **Advancing Semantic Navigation with Foundation Models:** The work effectively demonstrates the power of integrating cutting-edge Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with classic navigation algorithms. LLMs enable natural language instruction comprehension, making robots more interactive and user-friendly. VLMs enhance global localization, support dynamic map updates by recognizing new elements (like house numbers), and provide robust perception even in GPS-denied environments. This hybrid approach combines the semantic intelligence of foundation models with the reliability and explainability of traditional methods, setting a new paradigm for intelligent robotic systems.

3. **Introducing a Crucial Benchmark for Outdoor Robotics:** The **OpenBench** benchmark is a critical contribution to the research community. By focusing specifically on interactive, long-term, outdoor last-mile delivery in residential areas, it addresses a significant gap in existing evaluation frameworks. The proposed metrics (SRTP, LSR, LSPL) are innovative, comprehensively assessing not only task success and path efficiency but also the robot's ability to plan from natural language and maintain performance over continuous, sequential tasks—a vital aspect for practical logistics operations. This benchmark will stimulate future research, provide a standardized comparison tool, and accelerate the development of more robust and intelligent outdoor navigation systems.

4. **Enhanced Map Maintenance and Adaptability:** The system's ability to dynamically update OSM with newly detected information (e.g., house numbers) means the maps continuously improve over time without manual intervention. This self-enriching map capability improves long-term navigational performance and adaptability to dynamic real-world scenarios, making the system more resilient and efficient with each delivery.

5. **Resource Efficiency:** The demonstration of OSM's extreme storage efficiency compared to point cloud or topological maps is highly significant for deploying robots with limited on-board computational and storage resources. This lightweight map representation further contributes to the system's scalability and cost-effectiveness.

6. **Real-World Validation and Open-Source Contribution:** The successful validation in a real-world campus environment, where other learning-based methods failed, provides strong evidence for the practical robustness of the OPEN system. Furthermore, making the code and benchmark publicly available is a commendable contribution that will foster collaboration, reproducibility, and rapid progress within the robotics and AI communities.

In conclusion, "OpenBench" and the "OPEN" system represent a major step forward in creating practical, intelligent, and scalable autonomous delivery robots. By addressing fundamental challenges in mapping, navigation, and semantic understanding through an innovative hybrid approach and providing a tailored benchmark, this work is poised to significantly impact the future of smart logistics and embodied AI.
