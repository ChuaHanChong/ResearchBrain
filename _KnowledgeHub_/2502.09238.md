# OpenBench: A New Benchmark and Baseline for Semantic Navigation in Smart Logistics

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces OpenBench, a new benchmark for outdoor semantic navigation in last-mile delivery, and the Openstreetmap-enhanced oPen-air sEmantic Navigation (OPEN) system. The OPEN system combines OpenStreetMap data with Large Language Models and Vision-Language Models to achieve robust, scalable navigation, outperforming learning-based baselines in both simulated and real-world environments while significantly reducing map storage requirements.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic navigation methods are resource-intensive, requiring high-precision maps that hinder large-scale and flexible deployment in dynamic outdoor environments.
- Existing learning-based navigation approaches often suffer from limited generalization capabilities and require extensive training data, struggling with the reality gap in diverse, long-term outdoor tasks.
- Current semantic navigation benchmarks primarily focus on indoor environments, failing to capture the interactive and long-term operational demands crucial for outdoor last-mile delivery.

## Method

- ==The OPEN== system integrates LLMs for natural language instruction interpretation and task planning, VLMs for global localization and dynamic map updates, and classic algorithms for local planning and control.
- It utilizes OpenStreetMap (OSM) as a lightweight, flexible, and scalable map representation, eliminating the need for pre-constructed, high-precision semantic maps.
- A new benchmark, **OpenBench**, is introduced with tailored metrics (SRTP, LSR, LSPL) to evaluate interactive, continuous, and long-term last-mile delivery tasks in residential outdoor environments.

## Results

- The OPEN system achieved **100%** success rate in small and medium simulated environments (compared to 20-**40%** for baselines) and maintained strong long-term success rates (e.g., **75.58%** LSR in medium environment).
- GPT-4O-mini demonstrated a **100%** Success Rate of Task Planning (SRTP), accurately parsing delivery instructions, outperforming other LLMs.
- OpenStreetMap-based map representation required significantly less storage, approximately **1%** of a downsampled point cloud map and **0.01%** of a topological map, while OPEN successfully completed real-world delivery sequences where learning-based baselines failed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hybrid navigation approach, combining the semantic understanding of foundation models with the scalability of OpenStreetMap and the robustness of classic algorithms, can effectively address real-world last-mile delivery challenges.
> - Powerful LLMs like GPT-4O-mini can achieve near-perfect task planning from natural language instructions, significantly enhancing robot interaction and autonomy.
> - Dynamic map updates, facilitated by VLMs recognizing new elements, continuously improve navigation efficiency and system adaptability over sequential delivery tasks.
