# ScoRe-Flow: Complete Distributional Control via Score-Based Reinforcement Learning for Flow Matching

> [!summary] Summary
> ScoRe-Flow introduces a novel reinforcement learning framework that enables effective fine-tuning of Flow Matching policies for robotic control by integrating score-based drift modulation with learned variance prediction. The method provides decoupled control over stochastic transitions, leading to improved performance, faster convergence (2.4x over prior flow-based methods), and enhanced stability across diverse continuous control tasks.

## Problem

- Flow Matching policies trained purely via imitation learning are limited by demonstration data quality and cannot surpass suboptimal behaviors.
- Deterministic nature of Flow Matching's ODE dynamics results in ill-defined action likelihoods, preventing direct application of policy gradient methods for RL fine-tuning.
- Existing RL fine-tuning approaches for Flow Matching, which rely on noise injection, lack directional guidance, leading to inefficient exploration, especially when strong priors exist.

## Method

- Derived a closed-form expression for the ==score function== directly from the pre-trained velocity field of linear Flow Matching paths, enabling ==drift modulation== without additional auxiliary networks.
- Proposed **ScoRe-Flow**, a stochastic differential equation (SDE) that combines this score-based drift modulation with an independently learned ==variance predictor==.
- This design provides decoupled control over the mean (directional guidance via score) and variance (exploration magnitude) of stochastic action generation during RL fine-tuning.

## Results

- Achieved the highest average episode reward on D4RL locomotion tasks, including **5100**±**47** on Humanoid-v3, outperforming DPPO and ReinFlow baselines.
- Demonstrated **2.4x** faster convergence than ReinFlow using the same number of integration steps and an average **22x** speedup in wall-clock time compared to diffusion-based DPPO.
- Consistently outperformed baselines on Robomimic visual manipulation tasks (**92.5**% average success rate) and achieved the highest average task completion rate of **3.60** on the Franka Kitchen multi-task benchmark, showing robustness across diverse environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The score function inherently points towards regions of higher probability density, offering a geometrically informed mechanism to guide exploration.
> - For linear flow paths, the score function can be computed directly from the pre-trained velocity field, integrating drift modulation efficiently without significant computational overhead.
> - Decoupled control over the mean and variance in stochastic transitions is crucial for achieving stable training, efficient exploration, and superior asymptotic performance in RL fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper is situated within the field of robotic control, specifically focusing on the development and fine-tuning of generative policies. Generative models, such as diffusion models and Flow Matching (FM), have gained prominence in robotic policy learning due to their capacity to model complex, multimodal action distributions, which is beneficial for tasks exhibiting high variability in demonstrations.

Diffusion-based policies, while effective, often necessitate numerous iterative denoising steps, which can impede their application in real-time control and long-horizon decision-making scenarios. Flow Matching offers an alternative, ODE-based formulation for action generation. It learns deterministic velocity fields to transform noise samples into actions along structured trajectories, typically enabling significantly faster sampling with fewer integration steps (4 to 10 times fewer) compared to diffusion models, while maintaining comparable expressiveness. This efficiency has led to the adoption of FM as a core component in recent large-scale robotic action experts and vision-language-action models.

However, a fundamental limitation of most FM policies is their reliance on imitation learning (IL). This dependence means they inherit biases and performance ceilings from the demonstration data, often struggling with covariate shift and an inability to surpass suboptimal behaviors present in the training demonstrations. To overcome these constraints, reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning is considered essential for achieving super-human or expert-level performance.

The integration of RL with deterministic FM policies presents a challenge: their ODE dynamics result in transitions that approximate Dirac measures, rendering action likelihoods ill-defined. This characteristic precludes the direct application of common policy gradient methods that require tractable log-likelihoods. Existing approaches to enable RL fine-tuning for FM policies involve converting these deterministic flows into stochastic differential equations (SDEs) by injecting learnable noise. A representative method, ReinFlow, learns an auxiliary network to predict the magnitude of injected noise at each integration step. While these noise-only control methods facilitate exploration and stabilize fine-tuning, they primarily perturb the position of actions without modulating the underlying dynamics or drift of the flow. This limits their efficiency, particularly when initial demonstrations provide strong priors. This research aims to address this gap by introducing learnable drift modulation to provide directional guidance during exploration, complementing existing noise-based approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of ScoRe-Flow are as follows:

1. **Overcoming Limitations of Imitation Learning in FM Policies:** The paper recognizes that Flow Matching (FM) policies, when trained solely via imitation learning (IL), are inherently restricted by the quality and scope of the demonstration data. They cannot exceed the performance of suboptimal behaviors or adapt to new situations not covered in the demonstrations. Reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning is identified as a crucial step to move beyond these limitations and achieve higher performance.

2. **Enabling Effective RL Fine-tuning for Deterministic Flow Policies:** A significant challenge for RL fine-tuning of FM policies is their deterministic nature, which models action generation as an ordinary differential equation (ODE). This leads to ill-defined action likelihoods, making direct application of policy gradient methods (which rely on computing gradients with respect to log-likelihoods) impractical.

3. **Addressing the Insufficiency of Noise-Only Exploration:** Existing methods that enable RL fine-tuning for FM policies, such as ReinFlow, convert the deterministic ODEs into stochastic differential equations (SDEs) by injecting learnable noise. While this allows for exploration and likelihood computation, the noise primarily perturbs the action's position at each step without modulating the underlying velocity field (drift) of the flow. This "noise-only control" is considered less efficient, especially in scenarios where demonstrations already provide a strong prior, as it lacks a mechanism for directional guidance during exploration towards high-probability or high-reward regions.

4. **Introducing Geometrically Guided Drift Modulation:** The core motivation of ScoRe-Flow is to introduce learnable drift modulation as a complementary mechanism to noise injection. The authors observe that the score function (the gradient of the log-density of intermediate marginals) inherently points toward regions of higher probability density. By modulating the drift of the SDE with this score function, exploration can be steered along geometrically favorable paths, improving stability and potentially accelerating convergence.

5. **Leveraging Score-Velocity Duality for Computational Efficiency:** The paper is motivated by the insight that for linear flow paths, a closed-form expression for the score function can be derived directly from the pre-trained velocity field. This avoids the need for auxiliary networks to predict the score, making the integration of score-based drift control computationally efficient with negligible overhead.

6. **Achieving Decoupled Mean and Variance Control:** A key objective is to provide decoupled control over the mean (directional guidance) and variance (exploration magnitude) of stochastic transitions. This decoupling offers greater flexibility than methods that rigidly couple drift and diffusion (as in some standard score-based SDEs) or only control variance (as in noise-only methods). This enhanced control aims to allow for adaptive adjustment of exploration based on the learning stage and environmental feedback.

In summary, the key objective is to propose ScoRe-Flow, a novel RL fine-tuning framework for Flow Matching policies that combines score-based drift modulation with learned variance prediction. This aims to achieve more stable, efficient, and higher-performing online fine-tuning by providing geometrically informed exploration, overcoming the limitations of current deterministic and noise-only stochastic flow-based RL approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ScoRe-Flow introduces a method for reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning of Flow Matching (FM) policies that enables decoupled control over the mean and variance of stochastic transitions during action generation. The methodology is primarily divided into two components: the derivation of a closed-form score from the velocity field and the design of the ScoRe-Flow SDE for decoupled control.

#### 3.1. Closed-Form Score from Velocity

The paper addresses the challenge of incorporating policy gradients into deterministic FM dynamics by converting them into a stochastic differential equation (SDE). A general SDE is formulated as `da_t = μ(a_t, t)dt + σ(a_t, t)dW_t`, where `μ` is the drift and `σ` is the diffusion. While existing methods like ReinFlow typically set `μ` to the pre-trained velocity field `v_θ` and learn only `σ`, ScoRe-Flow aims to also modulate `μ`.

To achieve drift modulation without training additional networks, the method leverages the score function, defined as `s_t(a) = ∇_a log ρ_t(a)`, where `ρ_t(a)` is the marginal density of the flow at time `t`. This score function inherently points toward regions of higher probability density.

For linear FM paths, defined by `a_t = (1 − t)a_0 + ta_1` where `a_0 ∼ N(0, I)` and `a_1 ∼ p_data`, the paper derives a closed-form expression for the score in terms of the velocity field `v_θ(t, a, s)`:
`s_t(a) = (t v_θ(t, a, s) − a) / (1 − t)`.
This score-velocity duality is cited as being established in stochastic interpolant literature. The significance of this derivation is that the score can be computed directly from the pre-trained velocity field `v_θ` with minimal computational overhead and without needing additional learnable parameters.

A practical consideration for numerical stability is that the score magnitude diverges as `O((1 − t)^-1)` as `t → 1`. To counteract this, the coefficient multiplying the score in the drift term, denoted `α_scaled_ψ(t)`, is designed to decay as `O(1 − t)`, ensuring the score-modulated drift remains bounded. This is enforced by `α_scaled_ψ(t) = (1 − t) · α_ψ(t)`.

#### 3.2. ScoRe-Flow: Decoupled Mean and Variance Control

ScoRe-Flow extends beyond noise-only control by combining the score-weighted drift modulation with learned variance prediction. This allows for independent control over the first two moments (mean and variance) of each transition kernel.

The method introduces two learnable components:
1. **Score Scheduler (`α_scaled_ψ`):** This component modulates the strength of the score-based drift correction. It is implemented as a lightweight MLP that takes only the scalar time step `t` as input, with its output `α_ψ(t)` then scaled by `(1 − t)` for stability. This scheduler adapts the influence of the directional guidance provided by the score.
2. **Variance Predictor (`σ_ϕ`):** This network independently determines the magnitude of stochastic exploration. It is an MLP that takes the current action `a_t`, time step `t`, and observation `s` (or its encoded representation) as input. Its output `σ_ϕ` is mapped to a bounded range `[σ_min, σ_max]` to ensure stable noise levels.

The resulting SDE for ScoRe-Flow takes the form:
`da_t = [v_θ(t, a_t, s) + α_scaled_ψ(t) · s_t(a_t)]dt + σ_ϕ(t, a_t, s) dW_t`.
Here, `v_θ` is the base FM velocity field, initialized from pre-training and jointly optimized during fine-tuning. `s_t` is the closed-form score.

This decomposition allows the policy to use the score-modulated drift (`α_scaled_ψ · s_t`) for geometrically meaningful guidance towards higher-probability regions, acting as a corrective force when trajectories deviate. Simultaneously, the variance term `σ_ϕ` controls the exploration magnitude independently. This decoupling provides flexibility, allowing the policy to, for example, maintain strong directional correction while reducing random noise as training progresses.

For practical implementation, the SDE is discretized using the Euler-Maruyama scheme with `K` integration steps. The discrete update rule is:
`a_{k+1} = a_k + [v_θ^k + (α_scaled_ψ)^k · s^k]∆t + σ_ϕ^k √∆t ϵ_k`,
where `ϵ_k ∼ N(0, I)`.

Each transition in this discrete formulation follows a Gaussian distribution, enabling the closed-form computation of the trajectory log-likelihood:
`log π(a_1 | s) = Σ_{k=0}^{K-1} log N(a_{k+1}; μ_k, (σ_ϕ^k)^2 ∆t I)`.
This tractable likelihood allows for direct application of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) to jointly optimize the parameters of the velocity field (`v_θ`), score scheduler (`ψ`), and variance predictor (`ϕ`). The score scheduler's initial weights are set to ensure small initial `α` values for training stability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of ScoRe-Flow was evaluated across various continuous control benchmarks, including D4RL locomotion tasks, Robomimic visual manipulation tasks, and the Franka Kitchen multi-task environment.

#### 4.1. D4RL Locomotion Tasks

ScoRe-Flow achieved the highest average episode reward across all D4RL tasks (Hopper-v2, Walker2d-v2, Ant-v0, Humanoid-v3) compared to baselines such as DPPO, ReinFlow-R, ReinFlow-S, and a Score-SDE ablation. Specifically, on the Humanoid-v3 task, ScoRe-Flow attained a reward of 5100±47, surpassing ReinFlow-R (5041) and DPPO (5001).

In terms of efficiency, ScoRe-Flow demonstrated a 2.4× faster convergence rate than ReinFlow (the flow-based state-of-the-art) when using the same number of denoising steps (K=4). The paper also reported an average 22× speedup in wall-clock time compared to diffusion-based DPPO (which requires 50-100 denoising steps), attributing this broader advantage to the inherent efficiency of flow-based sampling. This faster convergence is posited to stem from the score-based drift modulation, which provides per-step geometric guidance, particularly beneficial in early learning stages when the base velocity field `v_θ` is not fully calibrated.

#### 4.2. Robomimic Visual Manipulation Tasks

On visual manipulation tasks from Robomimic (PickPlaceCan, Square, Transport), ScoRe-Flow consistently outperformed all baselines in success rates. It achieved a 98.3% success rate on PickPlaceCan and maintained the highest average success rate of 92.5% across the suite, exceeding the best baseline by 5.4%. These results indicate that the score-based mean control effectively guides exploration even in high-dimensional image observation spaces, leading to more precise and reliable manipulation.

#### 4.3. Franka Kitchen Multi-Task Benchmark

In the challenging Franka Kitchen multi-task environment, which features sparse rewards and sequential subtasks, ScoRe-Flow exhibited substantial improvements over baselines. It achieved the highest average task completion rate of 3.60 (out of 4). Notably, ScoRe-Flow attained a perfect score of 4.0 on the Kitchen-Complete task and demonstrated improved performance on the Partial dataset, achieving 3.8 completed subtasks compared to ReinFlow's 3.4. These findings support the effectiveness of ScoRe-Flow's decoupled mean-variance control in mastering complex, long-horizon behaviors.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Analysis

The paper included several ablation studies to investigate the contribution of individual components:
- **Score-SDE (Ablation Method):** This variant uses only score-based mean control with a fixed, coupled diffusion coefficient (i.e., `σ(t) = √(2λ(t))`, where `λ(t)` also controls drift strength). While Score-SDE sometimes showed faster *initial* convergence, it generally yielded *lower asymptotic performance* and was highly sensitive to the manually specified noise variance (`σ`). This confirmed that the *decoupled* and *learned* variance prediction in ScoRe-Flow is critical for achieving optimal asymptotic performance and eliminating the need for task-specific manual tuning.
- **Training Stability:** ScoRe-Flow demonstrated improved training stability compared to ReinFlow (e.g., on Kitchen-Complete-v0). ReinFlow exhibited higher variance and less stable convergence, whereas ScoRe-Flow maintained steady improvement. This stability is attributed to the geometric guidance provided by score-based mean control, which prevents the policy from drifting into low-probability regions.
- **Impact of Score Term (α=0):** An ablation where the score correction was removed (`α_ψ = 0`) but `v_θ` and learned variance were retained showed slower early convergence and lower asymptotic performance compared to full ScoRe-Flow. This indicates that the score term provides an independent and beneficial contribution to the learning process.
- **Fixed Full-Strength Score (α=1):** Setting `α_ψ = 1` (disabling the learned scheduler) severely degraded performance. This highlights the necessity of the learned `α_ψ(t)` and its `(1-t)` time-decay for numerical stability and effective modulation of the score, preventing it from overwhelming the optimization signal, especially near `t=1`.
- **Coupled Learned Score-SDE:** An attempt to learn a coupled drift-variance schedule with a single MLP performed worse than even fixed-`σ` Score-SDE. This further reinforces the finding that decoupled control over drift and variance is essential for robust performance.
- **Score Scheduler Analysis:** Analysis of the learned `α_ψ(t)` revealed that the scheduler applies stronger score correction during early denoising steps (small `t`) where `v_θ` predictions are less reliable, and gradually reduces its influence towards `t=1`, consistent with the required `(1-t)` boundary decay. The overall magnitude of `α_ψ` also decreased as `v_θ` improved through RL fine-tuning, demonstrating adaptive behavior.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ScoRe-Flow represents an advancement in the field of robotic control, particularly in the realm of fine-tuning generative policies. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced RL Fine-tuning for Flow Matching Policies:** The method offers a more effective strategy for fine-tuning Flow Matching (FM) policies with reinforcement learning (RL). By overcoming the limitations of deterministic flows and purely noise-based exploration, ScoRe-Flow enables FM policies to achieve performance beyond initial imitation learning benchmarks. This is crucial for unlocking the full potential of FM as a backbone for advanced robotic systems.

2. **Improved Performance and Efficiency:** ScoRe-Flow consistently demonstrates superior performance, achieving higher rewards and success rates across a diverse set of locomotion and manipulation tasks, including those with sparse rewards and visual observations. Simultaneously, it significantly accelerates convergence compared to existing flow-based RL methods. This dual benefit of better performance and faster training can reduce the computational resources and time required to develop and deploy high-performing robotic policies.

3. **Decoupled Distributional Control:** The core innovation of providing decoupled control over the mean (through score-based drift modulation) and variance (through learned variance prediction) of stochastic transitions offers a more flexible and robust exploration mechanism. This allows the policy to adaptively steer exploration based on geometric guidance towards high-probability regions while independently adjusting the magnitude of stochasticity. This fine-grained control is a key factor in the method's improved stability and learning efficiency.

4. **Computational Advantages:** Leveraging the closed-form score expression derived from the velocity field means that drift modulation requires no additional auxiliary networks and incurs negligible computational overhead. This preserves the inherent speed advantage of Flow Matching policies over diffusion models, making ScoRe-Flow a practical solution for real-time robotic applications that demand fast inference.

5. **Broader Applicability and Future Research:** While primarily demonstrated in robotic control, the principles of score-based drift modulation combined with learned variance prediction could potentially be applied to other domains where generative models are used for sequential decision-making. The identified limitation regarding the reliance on linear flow paths and Gaussian source distributions for the closed-form score provides a clear direction for future research, potentially exploring approximate or learned score estimation for more generalized stochastic interpolants.

In essence, ScoRe-Flow offers a pathway towards developing more capable, efficient, and robust generative policies for robotic agents, contributing to the broader development of embodied AI systems that can learn and adapt effectively in complex environments.
