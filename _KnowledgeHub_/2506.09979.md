# Locomotion on Constrained Footholds via Layered Architectures and Model Predictive Control

> [!summary] Summary
> A layered control architecture enables legged robots to perform stable and optimal real-time locomotion on constrained footholds by combining gradient-free sampling for discrete decisions with gradient-based Model Predictive Control for continuous motion. The approach demonstrated superior reliability and optimality compared to heuristic methods in simulations and achieved smooth traversal on hardware.

## Problem

- Real-time control of legged robots for dynamic locomotion on complex terrain faces challenges due to the high-dimensional, nonlinear, and hybrid (discrete contact decisions, continuous motion) nature of the systems.
- Existing solutions either incur high computational costs (full hybrid optimization, contact-implicit MPC) or sacrifice optimality and generalizability by relying on heuristics for discrete decisions (fixed-contact MPC).
- Simultaneously optimizing both discrete contact choices (like foot placement) and continuous body movements in real-time is a significant unsolved challenge.

## Method

- A layered control architecture decouples discrete variable selection (e.g., foothold choices) from continuous control.
- Gradient-free weighted random sampling selects potential contact surfaces (polytopes) based on kinematic limits and a nominal trajectory.
- Multiple fixed-mode, full-order nonlinear ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) problems are run in parallel, each optimized with a different sampled contact sequence, and the lowest-cost solution is executed.

## Results

- The sampling-based approach reduced quadruped locomotion failures over stepping stones from **80%** (heuristic) to **20%** in simulation, while achieving a lower average cost (**0.8946** vs. **1.0963**).
- Hardware validation on a Unitree Go2 quadruped demonstrated robust and smooth traversal of physical stepping stones, indicating real-world applicability and tolerance to disturbances.
- Simulation results showed a Unitree G1 humanoid robot performing natural locomotion over complex terrain, utilizing full-body dynamics in real-time, with MPC update rates consistently below 10 ms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Online optimization of hybrid systems (discrete contact decisions and continuous motion) can be achieved in real-time by strategically layering gradient-free sampling for discrete variables with gradient-based Model Predictive Control for continuous dynamics.
> - Decoupling the planning of discrete contact sequences from continuous trajectory optimization allows for the efficient use of different, specialized solvers, improving both computational speed and solution quality.
> - Replacing static heuristics with sampling-based discrete decision-making in real-time significantly improves the optimality and reliability of locomotion over complex terrain, reducing failure rates.
