# MisoDICE: Multi-Agent Imitation from Unlabeled Mixed-Quality Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> MisoDICE introduces a two-stage framework for offline multi-agent imitation learning, enabling policy learning from unlabeled, mixed-quality demonstrations by first using LLMs and preference learning to label trajectories, then applying a novel DICE-based algorithm. This approach achieved superior returns on SMACv1 and SMACv2 benchmarks, outperforming existing baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional multi-agent imitation learning (MAIL) relies on costly, hand-labeled expert demonstrations, which are scarce in real-world scenarios.
- Real-world demonstration datasets often contain mixed-quality trajectories, making it difficult to distinguish expert from suboptimal behaviors without explicit reward functions.
- The combinatorial explosion of joint state-action spaces makes direct application of single-agent imitation learning methods intractable in multi-agent environments.

## Method

- A multi-step data labeling pipeline uses LLMs for initial ==preference generation==, refined by an offline preference-based MARL algorithm (O-MAPL) to rank and partition trajectories into expert and suboptimal sets.
- **MisoDICE**, a novel multi-agent imitation learning algorithm, extends the single-agent DICE framework to multi-agent settings through value factorization and linear mixing networks.
- A decentralized weighted Behavior Cloning approach is used for policy extraction, ensuring consistency with the global policy optimization and enabling decentralized execution.

## Results

- MisoDICE consistently achieved the highest average returns across most SMACv1 and SMACv2 maps, outperforming various Behavioral Cloning, OMAPL, and individual DICE baselines.
- Performance benefits from more capable LLMs (GPT-4o over GPT-4o-mini) in the preference labeling stage, indicating the importance of high-quality initial labels.
- Ablation studies showed that selecting a moderate number of top-K expert trajectories (e.g., around K=200 for SMACv2) and an optimal value for the suboptimal data influence hyperparameter (α=**0.05**) maximizes performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining LLMs with preference-based learning effectively infers trajectory quality from unlabeled, mixed-quality multi-agent demonstrations, providing a scalable labeling solution.
> - Employing linear mixing networks for global value and discriminator functions maintains the convexity of the multi-agent DICE objective, ensuring stable and efficient training.
> - Balancing the influence of explicitly identified expert data and the broader mixed-quality dataset through a hyperparameter (α) is critical for robust imitation learning.
