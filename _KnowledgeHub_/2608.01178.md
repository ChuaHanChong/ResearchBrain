# DynActiveGS: Active Gaussian Splatting for Dynamic Scene Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> DynActiveGS, developed by researchers at Tsinghua University and Huawei Technologies, introduces an active 3D reconstruction framework for dynamic environments that uses a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation. The system minimizes motion-corrupted observations and guides exploration through dynamic-aware uncertainty modeling, yielding improved geometric accuracy and rendering quality over static-scene baselines in simulated dynamic indoor scenes.

## Problem

- Current active 3D reconstruction methods primarily assume static environments, failing to perform robustly in real-world dynamic scenes.
- Moving objects introduce motion-corrupted observations, significantly degrading the quality of 3D Gaussian Splatting representations.
- Existing active exploration strategies and uncertainty estimations are misled by dynamic elements, resulting in inefficient or unreliable viewpoint planning.

## Method

- A closed-loop active reconstruction system, **DynActiveGS**, is proposed, which iteratively improves a 3D Gaussian scene representation in dynamic environments.
- A ==dynamic-aware Gaussian reconstruction== module estimates per-pixel motion uncertainty to weight observations, minimizing the impact of dynamic objects during map optimization.
- The system employs a ==hierarchical dynamic-aware viewpoint selection==, distinguishing structural uncertainty from motion uncertainty, and a ==dynamic-constrained path planner== to guide the agent through stable and informative routes.

## Results

- DynActiveGS consistently surpassed state-of-the-art active reconstruction baselines across geometric and rendering metrics on dynamic indoor datasets (Social-MP3D, Dynamic Gibson) in the Habitat simulator.
- It achieved improved geometric accuracy, for example, an average Accuracy of **2.86 cm** (vs. 4.34 cm for ActiveGAMER) and a Completion Ratio of **94.08%** (vs. 90.17% for ActiveGAMER) on Social-MP3D.
- The system delivered higher rendering quality, with an average PSNR of **23.04** (vs. 21.48 for ActiveGAMER) and LPIPS of **0.279** (vs. 0.410) on Social-MP3D, effectively suppressing motion artifacts present in baseline reconstructions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling and differentiating between structural uncertainty and motion-induced uncertainty is crucial for effective active reconstruction in dynamic environments.
> - Applying uncertainty-weighted loss during 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization effectively mitigates the negative impact of motion-corrupted observations, leading to more accurate scene representations.
> - Integrating dynamic risk assessment into both next-best-view planning and path generation significantly improves exploration efficiency and reconstruction robustness in scenes with moving objects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "DynActiveGS" addresses a challenge at the intersection of 3D scene reconstruction, active perception, and real-time scene representation, particularly in dynamic environments. Traditional 3D reconstruction methods, such as Structure-from-Motion (SfM) and early Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) systems, primarily operate under the assumption of static environments. While robust, these methods are often computationally intensive and struggle with moving objects, which introduce visual inconsistencies and violate static scene assumptions.

The field of active 3D reconstruction extends passive perception by enabling an agent to autonomously determine optimal viewpoints for data acquisition, aiming to build high-fidelity scene representations efficiently. Early approaches in active reconstruction often relied on geometric uncertainty or information gain for next-best-view (NBV) planning. More recently, the emergence of neural scene representations, such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and especially 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), has provided new avenues for explicit, differentiable, and efficient scene modeling. These representations offer high-quality rendering and incremental optimization capabilities, inspiring active reconstruction methods that leverage their advantages. However, existing active reconstruction frameworks based on NeRFs or 3DGS, like ActiveGAMER, ActiveGS, and ActiveSplat, have predominantly been designed for static environments. In these approaches, dynamic elements are either ignored or treated as noise, leading to degraded reconstruction quality and unreliable planning in real-world scenarios.

Concurrently, research in dynamic scene reconstruction has sought to address the challenges posed by moving objects. Traditional SLAM methods employ motion segmentation and robust optimization, while newer neural and Gaussian-based approaches focus on modeling or filtering dynamic content to improve robustness. For instance, dynamic 3DGS and Gaussian-based SLAM systems have been developed for passive mapping and dynamic object filtering. However, these systems primarily focus on passively tracking and mapping dynamic scenes without an active component to guide data acquisition. They do not intrinsically provide mechanisms for an embodied agent to intelligently navigate and collect informative observations in environments with moving elements.

DynActiveGS bridges this gap by integrating dynamic scene modeling with active viewpoint planning within a unified 3D Gaussian Splatting framework. It extends active 3D reconstruction to dynamic settings by explicitly accounting for motion-induced uncertainty during both scene representation optimization and active exploration strategies, thereby aiming for more robust and efficient scene acquisition in complex, non-static environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust active 3D reconstruction framework that can operate effectively in dynamic environments. This framework, named DynActiveGS, aims to incrementally reconstruct a 3D Gaussian scene representation while mitigating the adverse effects of motion-corrupted observations and actively guiding an agent's exploration strategy.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations in existing active reconstruction approaches:

- **Static Environment Assumption:** Current active 3D reconstruction methods, including those leveraging 3D Gaussian Splatting, are largely developed under the assumption that the environment is static. This fundamental assumption is violated in many real-world scenarios, which commonly feature dynamic objects such as pedestrians, vehicles, or articulated agents.
- **Degradation by Motion-Corrupted Observations:** Dynamic objects introduce unreliable observations (e.g., motion blur, occlusions) that can significantly degrade the quality of 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization. Such corrupted data can lead to distorted scene geometry, inaccurate color representation, and overall lower reconstruction fidelity.
- **Misleading Uncertainty Estimation and Viewpoint Planning:** Existing methods' uncertainty estimation mechanisms are typically not designed to differentiate between truly unknown static regions and regions temporarily obscured or distorted by dynamic elements. Consequently, motion-corrupted observations can lead to incorrect uncertainty assessments, which in turn can mislead next-best-view planning, causing the agent to explore areas that are unstable or uninformative due for dynamic reasons rather than structural incompleteness.
- **Lack of Active Exploration in Dynamic 3DGS:** While some dynamic 3DGS and Gaussian-based SLAM methods exist, they primarily focus on passive mapping and filtering dynamic objects. They do not provide a framework for an embodied agent to actively decide where to move or what to observe to improve the scene reconstruction systematically in dynamic settings.
- **Need for Robust Embodied Perception:** To enable intelligent agents (e.g., robots) to operate autonomously and effectively in human-centric or complex real-world environments, they require the ability to robustly perceive and reconstruct their surroundings even when dynamic elements are present. This necessitates a closed-loop system that integrates perception, uncertainty estimation, and motion planning in a dynamic-aware manner.

By addressing these motivations, DynActiveGS seeks to establish a unified perception–planning–reconstruction pipeline for robust active reconstruction, moving beyond static scene limitations towards more practical applications in dynamic real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DynActiveGS operates as a closed-loop active reconstruction system, iteratively improving a 3D Gaussian scene representation in dynamic environments. At each timestep, an embodied agent acquires an RGB-D observation and an estimated camera pose. The system then updates its Gaussian representation, quantifies uncertainty, selects an informative next viewpoint, and plans a dynamically stable path towards it.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)

The scene is represented as a collection of anisotropic 3D Gaussian primitives, each defined by its mean ($\boldsymbol{\mu}$), color ($\mathbf{c}$), opacity ($\eta$), scale ($\mathbf{S}$), and rotation ($\mathbf{R}$). The covariance matrix ($\mathbf{\Sigma}$) is derived from the scale and rotation. Scene rendering is performed via alpha compositing, where the color of each pixel is determined by a weighted sum of colors of Gaussians projected onto it, modulated by their opacities and densities. This representation is fully differentiable and supports efficient online optimization.

#### 3.2. Dynamic-aware Gaussian Reconstruction

This module focuses on updating the 3D Gaussian map by addressing the challenges posed by dynamic objects.

- **Frame-level Uncertainty Prediction:** For each incoming RGB frame ($\mathbf{I}_t$), a pre-trained DINOv3 feature extractor ($\mathcal{F}$) extracts dense visual features ($\mathbf{F}_t$). These features are then fed into a lightweight uncertainty prediction network ($\mathcal{P}$) to estimate a per-pixel uncertainty map ($\boldsymbol{\beta}_t$). High uncertainty values indicate pixels affected by dynamic motion or transient occlusions. This uncertainty predictor is trained using appearance and depth consistency objectives.
- **Uncertainty-weighted Gaussian Mapping:** The current 3D Gaussian map ($\mathcal{G}_t$) is optimized using an uncertainty-weighted rendering loss. This loss combines pixel-wise $\ell_1$ and SSIM losses for color, $\ell_1$ loss for depth, and an isotropic Gaussian regularization term. The predicted per-pixel uncertainty map ($\boldsymbol{\beta}_t$) serves as a weighting factor, diminishing the contribution of unreliable (high uncertainty) observations to the map optimization. This strategy biases the optimizer towards stable static structures and suppresses dynamic disturbances.
- **Map-level Structural and Motion Uncertainty:** To inform active planning, two distinct map-level uncertainty fields are maintained over a discrete 3D query set ($\Omega$):
 - **Structural Uncertainty ($\mathbf{U}_s$):** This field quantifies regions with insufficient observation or reconstruction mismatch. It is calculated based on the effective observation count for each 3D point and an aggregated rendering residual, penalizing under-observed areas and poorly explained geometry.
 - **Motion Uncertainty ($\mathbf{U}_m$):** This field accumulates the frame-level uncertainty ($\boldsymbol{\beta}_t$) into 3D. For each query location, its projection into the current frame's uncertainty map is aggregated over time using an exponential moving average. This distinguishes areas where future observations are likely to be unreliable due to dynamic disturbances from static regions requiring more structural detail.

#### 3.3. Dynamic-aware Viewpoint Selection

Given the map-level uncertainty fields, the system selects viewpoints that are both informative (high structural gain) and dynamically reliable (low motion risk). A hierarchical planning strategy is employed on a Voronoi graph ($\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$).

- **Dynamic-aware Subregion Partition:** The Voronoi graph is partitioned into subregions using agglomerative clustering. The pairwise distance between nodes incorporates Euclidean distance, graph travel distance, and accumulated motion risk along connecting paths. This creates subregions, including a current local subregion for the agent.
- **Local-Global Viewpoint Selection:**
 - **Local Score:** For candidate nodes within the agent's current local subregion, a local score is computed. This score prioritizes structural utility (sum of $\mathbf{U}_s$ in visible areas), penalizes motion risk (sum of $\mathbf{U}_m$ in visible areas), and accounts for travel cost. Soft visibility scores are used to estimate what is observable from a candidate viewpoint.
 - **Global Score:** When the local region is sufficiently explored or becomes unstable, the planner switches to global selection. For candidate subregions, a global score is calculated, similarly incorporating aggregated structural utility and motion risk, while penalizing inter-region travel cost and repeatedly visited regions. The subregion with the highest score is chosen, and a specific viewpoint within it is selected as the target.

#### 3.4. Dynamic-constrained Path Planning

Once a target viewpoint is selected, a feasible path is computed that considers both geometric distance and dynamic risk.

- **Dynamic-constrained Traversal Cost:** For each edge in the Voronoi graph, a dynamic-constrained traversal cost is defined. This cost is a sum of the geometric edge length and the average motion uncertainty along that edge ($\overline{\mathbf{U}}_m(\mathbf{e})$). This encourages the robot to choose paths that are not only geometrically short but also dynamically stable.
- **Temporal Robustness:** The motion uncertainty used in path planning is accumulated over time using an exponential moving average. This ensures that transient dynamic disturbances do not overly influence long-term planning, while persistently dynamic regions are consistently down-weighted, leading to more temporally robust path choices.

The integration of these modules forms a unified perception-planning-reconstruction loop, allowing the robot to continuously update its map, assess uncertainties, and plan its movements to efficiently and robustly reconstruct dynamic scenes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of DynActiveGS was evaluated through extensive experiments conducted in the Habitat simulator, using dynamic indoor scenes.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Simulator and Dynamic Scenes:** Experiments utilized the Habitat simulator, incorporating runtime human dynamics. Two datasets were used:
 - **Social-MP3D:** A public benchmark augmenting Matterport3D scenes with dynamic humans.
 - **Dynamic Gibson Protocol:** A custom protocol developed by the authors for Gibson scenes, simulating virtual pedestrians using ORCA and UPL++ crowd dynamics. Dynamic humans were not part of the static ground-truth meshes used for evaluation.
- **Baselines:** DynActiveGS was compared against state-of-the-art active reconstruction methods, including 3DGS-based approaches (ActiveGAMER, ActiveGS, ActiveSplat) and neural active mapping methods (NARUTO). Dynamic passive mapping baselines were also considered.
- **Metrics:**
 - **Geometric Metrics:** Accuracy (Acc), Completion (Com), and Completion Ratio (C.R.), all with a 5 cm threshold. These quantify the robustness of static scene reconstruction amidst dynamic disturbances by comparing extracted point clouds from Gaussian maps against static ground-truth meshes.
 - **Rendering Metrics:** PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS for RGB novel view synthesis, and Depth L1 distance for depth rendering. Evaluations were performed on predefined novel-view trajectories against static ground-truth renderings.
- **Implementation Details:** A ground mobile robot with a fixed camera height was simulated. The agent performed discrete navigation actions and acquired one posed RGB-D observation per step. All methods were evaluated under an equal observation budget of 1000 exploration steps, ensuring identical input image counts.

#### 4.2. Comparison Results

- **Results on Social-MP3D:** On the Social-MP3D dataset (Table 1), DynActiveGS demonstrated consistent improvements across all metrics. For instance, it achieved an average Accuracy of 2.86 cm (lower is better) compared to 4.34 cm for ActiveGAMER, and an average Completion Ratio of 94.08% (higher is better) compared to 90.17% for ActiveGAMER. In terms of rendering, DynActiveGS yielded an average PSNR of 23.04 (higher is better) against ActiveGAMER's 21.48, and an LPIPS of 0.279 (lower is better) compared to 0.410. The results indicated that static-scene baselines suffered degradation in complex layouts with frequent human occlusions, whereas DynActiveGS maintained stable reconstruction quality.
- **Results on Dynamic Gibson:** On the Dynamic Gibson dataset (Table 2), DynActiveGS again consistently outperformed all baselines. It achieved an average Accuracy of 2.52 cm and a Completion Ratio of 94.82%, both superior to ActiveGAMER's 3.98 cm and 91.40%, respectively. Rendering quality metrics also showed significant gains, with an average PSNR of 24.34 and LPIPS of 0.288, improving upon ActiveGAMER's 22.24 PSNR and 0.397 LPIPS. These results suggest that the proposed motion-aware uncertainty modeling generalizes effectively, filtering motion-contaminated observations and avoiding duplicated structures common in static-scene baselines.
- **Qualitative Comparison:** Visual comparisons (Figure 3 and Figure 4) corroborated the quantitative findings. Static baselines exhibited duplicated structures and motion streaks in areas with human movement. DynActiveGS effectively suppressed these artifacts, preserving consistent geometry and texture fidelity, which authors attribute to risk-aware planning under dynamic disturbances.
- **Reconstruction Progress over Exploration:** Analysis of reconstruction progress over exploration steps (Figure 5) showed that DynActiveGS achieved the fastest improvement in both PSNR and completion ratio on the Social-MP3D HxpK scene, maintaining higher performance throughout the exploration process compared to baselines.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

Ablation studies were conducted to validate the contribution of key components:

- **Core Module Ablation (Table 3):** On the Dynamic Gibson Denm scene, progressively enabling uncertainty-weighted Gaussian mapping, motion-aware viewpoint selection, and dynamic-constrained planning demonstrated consistent gains. The uncertainty-weighted mapping improved reconstruction robustness, motion-aware viewpoint selection enhanced observation quality, and dynamic-constrained planning improved exploration efficiency. The full model achieved the best overall performance.
- **Motion-aware Viewpoint Selection Ablation (Table 4):** Comparing random sampling, structural-gain-only selection, structural gain with travel cost, and the full motion-aware formulation on Dynamic Gibson Ribe scene revealed the importance of explicitly modeling motion uncertainty. Structural-gain-only selection was insufficient in dynamic scenes, as it could lead to planning in high-gain but unreliable regions. The full motion-aware approach significantly improved accuracy and rendering fidelity.
- **Dynamic-constrained Planning Ablation (Table 5):** On Social-MP3D YmJk, evaluating a static hierarchical planner, a planner with dynamic edge cost, and the full dynamic-constrained planner showed that hierarchical planning improved long-horizon exploration. Introducing dynamic edge cost reduced unstable routes, and temporal accumulation of motion uncertainty yielded the best final performance, indicating that persistent motion patterns are more informative for planning than instantaneous disturbances.
- **Stage-wise Rendering Analysis (Figure 6):** Visualizations showed that initial online results contained strong dynamic artifacts, which were substantially reduced after uncertainty-guided online optimization, and further refined by offline processing, demonstrating the effectiveness of the learned uncertainty map in highlighting motion-corrupted regions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DynActiveGS represents a step towards robust active 3D reconstruction in dynamic environments, an area where existing methods, largely designed for static scenes, encounter significant limitations. The primary significance of this work lies in its ability to explicitly model and leverage uncertainty to address the challenges posed by moving objects during both scene representation and active exploration.

The decomposition of uncertainty into structural and motion-induced components is a key contribution, enabling the system to intelligently distinguish between regions requiring more observation for structural completeness and areas that are dynamically unreliable. This distinction allows for more informed decision-making in next-best-view planning, preventing the agent from wasting resources on corrupted observations or navigating through high-risk, unstable areas.

The integration of uncertainty-weighted Gaussian optimization ensures that motion-corrupted observations contribute less to the scene representation, leading to more accurate and artifact-free 3D models. Concurrently, dynamic-aware viewpoint selection and motion-constrained path planning guide the agent through dynamically stable and informative routes, enhancing exploration efficiency and reconstruction quality over time.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Robotics and Autonomous Navigation:** The framework could significantly improve the capabilities of autonomous robots operating in human-populated or dynamic real-world settings (e.g., smart factories, public spaces, domestic environments). Robots could build more reliable environmental maps, enabling safer navigation and more effective interaction.
- **High-Fidelity 3D Reconstruction:** By mitigating the impact of dynamic elements, DynActiveGS could lead to higher fidelity 3D models of complex, real-world scenes, which is valuable for applications in virtual reality, augmented reality, urban planning, and digital twin creation.
- **Embodied AI Research:** The approach provides a more robust foundation for embodied AI agents, enabling them to learn and operate more effectively in environments that closely mimic real-world unpredictability.
- **Extension to 4D Scene Representations:** The authors suggest future work on extending this to 4D scene representations, which could lead to not just static scene reconstruction but also dynamic scene understanding and predictive modeling of motion patterns. This could have profound implications for human-robot collaboration and advanced simulation.

The work's evaluation in simulation, with controllable human dynamics on standard datasets, provides a reproducible benchmark for future research in dynamic active reconstruction. While current limitations include reliance on simulation and unoptimized performance for large-scale multi-floor environments, the proposed methodology establishes a foundation for future deployment on real robotic platforms and further advancements in online motion prediction and 4D scene modeling.
