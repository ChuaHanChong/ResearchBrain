# ObjectFolder 2.0: A Multisensory Object Dataset for Sim2Real Transfer

> [!summary] Summary
> ObjectFolder 2.0 is a dataset of 1,000 household objects virtualized as implicit neural representations called Object Files, each encoding visual, acoustic, and tactile data through three sub-networks. Improved simulation pipelines and a grid of tiny MLPs make rendering real-time and higher fidelity, and models trained purely on these virtual objects transfer to real counterparts for scale estimation, contact localization, and shape reconstruction.

## Problem

- Prior 3D object datasets model geometry or 2D appearance only, ignoring acoustic and tactile properties, so they cannot support multisensory object-centric learning.
- The earlier 100-object multisensory dataset was small, slow to render, and its audio and touch simulation quality was too coarse for real-world generalization.
- Earlier work evaluated virtualized objects only in simulation, leaving unproven whether models trained on simulated multisensory data transfer to real physical objects.

## Method

- **Object File** ==implicit representation== splits into **VisionNet**, **AudioNet**, **TouchNet**, mapping extrinsic parameters (viewpoint, lighting, impact force, gel pose) to continuous sensory signals at arbitrary resolution.
- ==KiloOSF== **VisionNet** subdivides each object into a grid of thousands of ==tiny MLPs== trained by ==distillation== from an ==object-centric scattering function== teacher, with ==empty-space skipping== and ==early ray termination==.
- **AudioNet** predicts only ==location-dependent modal gains== from ==FEM tetrahedral modal analysis==, then analytically sums ==damped sinusoids==; **TouchNet** maps an 8D coordinate to deformation maps rendered through ==Taxim==.

## Results

- Rendering one observation across all three modalities drops from **4.129s** to **0.111s**, with vision **60 times faster** (**3.699s** to **0.062s**) than the prior dataset version.
- Rendering quality improves on every modality: vision PSNR **35.7** to **36.3**, touch PSNR **27.9** to **31.6** (SSIM **0.64** to **0.78**), audio STFT distance **4.94** to **0.19**.
- Real-object scale error falls from **7.41/6.85/4.92 cm** to **5.08/4.68/3.51 cm** for vision/audio/touch; fusing audio with touch and vision with touch gives the best real contact localization and reconstruction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Factorizing a signal into learned location-dependent parts plus analytic physics beats predicting the full output, improving both fidelity and speed for simulated sensing.
> - Touch showed the smallest sim-to-real gap of the three modalities, and modality fusion consistently outperformed any single sensor on real objects.
> - Sim-to-real realism hinges on calibrating simulation to the specific physical sensor; assumptions of rigid bodies and homogeneous material bound what transfers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The research presented in "OBJECTFOLDER 2.0" is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: multisensory learning, implicit neural representations, large-scale dataset creation, and Sim2Real transfer in robotics and computer vision.

Historically, object datasets have often focused on single modalities, predominantly vision, and primarily in 2D (e.g., ImageNet, MS COCO) or purely on geometry in 3D (e.g., ModelNet, ShapeNet). While these datasets have been instrumental in advancing object recognition and 3D shape understanding, they fall short when it comes to modeling the full spectrum of physical object properties essential for real-world interaction, especially in robotics. Prior attempts to model real-world objects in 3D (e.g., BigBIRD, YCB) were often limited in scale or fidelity, and recent large-scale efforts like ABO (Amazon Berkeley Objects) primarily focus on the visual modality.

The immediate predecessor to this work, OBJECTFOLDER 1.0, represented a pioneering step by introducing a dataset of 100 virtualized objects with implicit visual, acoustic, and tactile representations. This initial work demonstrated the potential of multisensory object-centric learning using neural representations. However, as acknowledged by the authors, OBJECTFOLDER 1.0 suffered from limitations in scale, rendering speed, and the quality of simulated sensory data, which hampered its generalization to real-world scenarios.

OBJECTFOLDER 2.0 addresses these limitations head-on, positioning itself as a critical advancement in several ways:
- **Multisensory Learning:** It aligns with the growing trend in AI to integrate multiple sensory modalities (vision, audio, touch) to achieve more robust and human-like perception. Researchers are increasingly exploring how different senses complement each other for tasks like object identification, localization, and manipulation. OBJECTFOLDER 2.0 provides a much-needed, high-quality testbed for such explorations.
- **Implicit Neural Representations (INRs):** The work heavily leverages INRs, a paradigm that has gained significant traction with the success of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) for scene representation. By encoding object properties (shape, texture, material) as continuous functions parameterized by neural networks, INRs offer advantages in memory efficiency, arbitrary resolution rendering, and continuous representation, which are particularly beneficial for complex multisensory data. OBJECTFOLDER 2.0 pushes the boundaries of INRs by applying them to a large scale of diverse objects and optimizing their rendering speed.
- **Large-Scale Data for Deep Learning:** Deep learning models thrive on vast amounts of data. OBJECTFOLDER 2.0 contributes a dataset that is an order of magnitude larger than its predecessor, providing the scale necessary for training powerful, generalizable multisensory models.
- **Sim2Real Transfer:** A core challenge in robotics is the gap between models trained in simulation and their performance in the real world. By focusing explicitly on validating Sim2Real transfer, OBJECTFOLDER 2.0 provides a benchmark that directly addresses this critical problem, offering a pathway for developing AI systems that can learn in virtual environments and operate effectively in physical ones.

In essence, OBJECTFOLDER 2.0 is not just a dataset; it's a significant research infrastructure that pushes the state-of-the-art in multisensory object modeling, enabling the next generation of AI and robotic systems that can robustly perceive and interact with the physical world.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the "OBJECTFOLDER 2.0" research is to **develop a large-scale, realistic, and multisensory 3D object dataset, represented through implicit neural representations, that facilitates effective Sim2Real transfer for object-centric learning.** The authors are motivated by the inherent limitations of prior work in modeling real-world objects, which often falls short in terms of realism, multisensory integration, scale, and utility for practical applications in robotics and computer vision.

Specifically, the key motivations and objectives can be broken down as follows:

1. **Overcome Unrealistic Object Modeling:** Current object models in datasets are often simplistic. Computer vision datasets largely focus on 2D images, lacking 3D geometry and physical properties. 3D CAD datasets concentrate on geometry without realistic textures. Crucially, most prior work focuses on a single modality, typically vision, ignoring the rich information provided by other senses like touch and sound. The authors aim to create object models that capture a full spectrum of physical properties (3D shapes, appearance, material types) across multiple sensory modalities.

2. **Address Limitations of OBJECTFOLDER 1.0:** While OBJECTFOLDER 1.0 was a foundational step towards multisensory implicit object representations, it had significant drawbacks. The authors explicitly state their motivation to significantly enhance its successor by addressing three core issues:
 - **Scale:** OBJECTFOLDER 1.0 contained only 100 objects, which is insufficient for training generalizable deep learning models. A key objective is to increase the dataset size by an order of magnitude.
 - **Rendering Speed:** The rendering process in OBJECTFOLDER 1.0 was slow, making it impractical for applications requiring on-the-fly data generation, such as reinforcement learning or real-time robotics. The goal is to achieve orders of magnitude faster rendering times.
 - **Data Quality:** The multisensory data generated by OBJECTFOLDER 1.0 had limited quality, potentially hindering models' ability to generalize to real-world data. Improving the realism and fidelity of visual, acoustic, and tactile data is a critical objective.

3. **Enable Sim2Real Transfer:** A core motivation is the belief that learning from highly realistic virtual objects can directly transfer to real-world tasks. The authors explicitly aim to demonstrate that models trained purely on their virtualized objects can successfully perform challenging tasks on their physical counterparts. This objective highlights the practical utility and real-world relevance of the dataset.

4. **Leverage Implicit Neural Representations:** The authors are motivated by the advantages of implicit neural representations (INRs) – continuous functions that map extrinsic parameters to sensory signals. They seek to design an improved INR network, called "Object File," that can more realistically and flexibly encode object-centric multisensory data, making it memory-efficient and capable of arbitrary resolution sampling.

5. **Serve as a New Testbed and Path for Research:** Ultimately, the authors aim for OBJECTFOLDER 2.0 to become a standard benchmark and a rich repository for the computer vision and robotics communities. It is intended to offer a new path for research in areas such as multisensory learning with vision, audio, and touch; robot grasping of diverse real objects; and applications that require on-the-fly multisensory data, thereby accelerating progress in these fields.

By tackling these motivations, OBJECTFOLDER 2.0 positions itself as a crucial resource for developing more robust, perceptive, and interactive AI systems that can seamlessly operate between virtual and physical environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology for creating OBJECTFOLDER 2.0 involves a multi-stage process: first, curating a large and diverse collection of 3D object models; second, developing advanced physics-based simulation pipelines for generating high-fidelity multisensory data (vision, audio, touch); and third, encoding this data into efficient and realistic implicit neural representations (INRs) to form an "Object File" for each object. Finally, the utility of the dataset is rigorously validated through Sim2Real transfer experiments on various robotic tasks.

#### 3.1. Dataset Construction

The dataset comprises **1,000 3D objects**, a tenfold increase over OBJECTFOLDER 1.0. These objects are sourced from:
- **OBJECTFOLDER 1.0 (100 objects):** High-quality 3D models from 3D Model Haven, YCB, and Google Scanned Objects.
- **ABO Dataset (855 objects):** A large repository of real-world 3D household objects from Amazon.com product listings, including metadata like category, material, color, and dimensions. These were filtered for material type homogeneity (ceramic, glass, wood, plastic, iron, polycarbonate, steel) to ensure accurate acoustic simulation.
- **Google Scanned Objects (45 objects):** Additional polycarbonate objects to diversify material types.
The selected objects represent common household items across various categories and materials, providing a diverse base for multisensory learning.

#### 3.2. Improved Multisensory Simulation and Implicit Representations

Each object in OBJECTFOLDER 2.0 is encoded as an **Object File**, an implicit neural representation network containing three specialized sub-networks: VisionNet, AudioNet, and TouchNet. This approach allows continuous function parameterization, memory efficiency, and arbitrary resolution sampling.

##### 3.2.1. VisionNet (KiloOSF)

- **Background:** Builds upon Object-centric Neural Scattering Function (OSF) and principles of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) for volume rendering. The OSF predicts volume density and scattered light fraction at a 3D location and light conditions.
- **Improvements (KiloOSF):** To overcome the slow rendering speed of traditional NeRF/OSF (due to many forward passes), KiloOSF (inspired by KiloNeRF) subdivides the object's axis-aligned bounding box (AABB) into a uniform grid. Each grid cell is represented by a small, independent MLP. During rendering, only the relevant tiny MLPs are queried.
- **Training:** A "training with distillation" strategy is used: an ordinary OSF model is first trained for each object (teacher), and then its knowledge is distilled into the KiloOSF model (student). Techniques like empty space skipping and early ray termination further enhance efficiency.
- **Result:** 60 times faster visual rendering than OBJECTFOLDER 1.0 with improved quality.

##### 3.2.2. AudioNet

- **Background:** Utilizes physics-based 3D modal sound synthesis via linear modal analysis. This method models object vibration as a sum of damped sinusoidal waves, where frequencies and damping coefficients are intrinsic to the object, and gains are specific to contact force and location.
- **Improvements:**
 - **Meshing:** Instead of volumetric hexahedron meshes (as in 1.0), higher-order tetrahedral meshes are used for Finite Element Methods (FEM) with Abaqus. This allows for more accurate capture of finer features, surface curvature, and precise elastic deformations, leading to more realistic acoustic properties.
 - **MLP Prediction:** Unlike OBJECTFOLDER 1.0, which directly predicted complex audio spectrograms (fixed resolution/length), AudioNet predicts only the **location-dependent gains** of each mode. The intrinsic frequencies and damping coefficients are obtained from modal analysis. This targeted prediction significantly improves audio quality and accelerates inference.
- **Result:** Real-time audio rendering with significantly improved quality (e.g., much lower STFT and ENV distances to ground truth).

##### 3.2.3. TouchNet

- **Background:** Simulates tactile readings from a GelSight tactile sensor, which measures contact surface geometry. The goal is flexible, fast, and realistic tactile rendering.
- **Improvements:**
 - **Two-Stage Simulation:**
 1. **Deformation Map Simulation:** Simulates the contact deformation map (local shape at contact point) using Pyrender and OpenGL with GPU acceleration, achieving 700 fps. This is flexible enough to handle varied contact locations, orientations (±15°), and pressing depths (0.5-2 mm).
 2. **Tactile Image Rendering:** The deformation maps are then converted into realistic RGB tactile images using Taxim, an example-based GelSight simulation framework calibrated with a real sensor.
 - **MLP Design:** TouchNet encodes an 8D function (3D location, 3D unit contact orientation, gel penetration depth, and 2D spatial location in the deformation map) to output per-pixel deformation map values.
- **Result:** Enables rendering of tactile outputs with varied interaction parameters and produces realistic tactile optical outputs suitable for Sim2Real transfer (smaller Sim2Real gap compared to 1.0).

#### 3.3. Sim2Real Object Transfer Evaluation

The authors validate OBJECTFOLDER 2.0 by training models on the virtualized data and testing their generalization capabilities on 13 real-world counterparts. Real-world sensory data (impact sounds with a microphone, GelSight tactile readings) was collected for these objects.

- **Object Scale Estimation:** ResNet-18 models predict object scale from single RGB images, impact sound spectrograms, or a recurrent neural network (RNN) processing 10 consecutive tactile readings.
- **Tactile-Audio Contact Localization:** Particle filtering is used to localize contact points. Features are extracted from tactile readings (FCRN network for depth prediction) and impact sounds (MFCC features).
- **Visuo-Tactile Shape Reconstruction:** Point Completion Network (PCN) is used. For touch, 32 tactile readings are converted to a sparse point cloud. For vision, a global feature from a ResNet-18 on a single image is used. A two-stream network combines vision and touch.

This comprehensive methodology ensures that OBJECTFOLDER 2.0 is not only large and high-quality but also directly applicable and validated for real-world robotic and vision tasks, making it a valuable resource for future research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The core findings of the "OBJECTFOLDER 2.0" paper demonstrate significant advancements over prior work, particularly OBJECTFOLDER 1.0, in terms of dataset scale, rendering efficiency, sensory data quality, and most importantly, the ability to facilitate robust Sim2Real transfer for multisensory learning tasks.

#### 4.1. Dataset Scale and Rendering Performance

- **Scale:** OBJECTFOLDER 2.0 successfully curates **1,000 diverse 3D objects**, representing a tenfold increase in size compared to OBJECTFOLDER 1.0 (100 objects). This expanded scale provides a richer and more varied foundation for training generalizable models.
- **Rendering Speed:** The introduction of optimized implicit neural representation designs dramatically improves rendering speed across all modalities:
 - **VisionNet (KiloOSF):** Achieves **60 times faster visual rendering** (0.062s per sample) compared to OBJECTFOLDER 1.0 (3.699s), making visual data generation near real-time.
 - **AudioNet:** Renders audio **12 times faster** (0.035s per sample) than OBJECTFOLDER 1.0 (0.420s).
 - **TouchNet:** Renders tactile data in **0.014s per sample**, showing improved efficiency.
 - **Overall:** The total rendering time for one multisensory observation sample is reduced from 4.129s in OBJECTFOLDER 1.0 to just **0.111s** in OBJECTFOLDER 2.0, enabling on-the-fly data generation.

#### 4.2. Multisensory Data Quality

- **Qualitative Improvement:** Visual comparisons (e.g., Figure 4) clearly show that OBJECTFOLDER 2.0 renders significantly more realistic and artifact-free visual, acoustic, and tactile data than its predecessor, closely matching ground-truth simulations and real-world observations.
- **Quantitative Improvement (Table 2):**
 - **Vision:** Achieves higher PSNR (36.3 vs. 35.7) and SSIM (0.98 vs. 0.97), indicating better visual fidelity.
 - **Audio:** Demonstrates a drastic reduction in STFT Distance (0.19e-5 vs. 4.94e-5) and ENV Distance (1.29e-4 vs. 7.65e-4), signifying a substantial improvement in acoustic realism by more accurately capturing mode signals and envelopes.
 - **Touch:** Shows improved PSNR (31.6 vs. 27.9) and SSIM (0.78 vs. 0.64), indicating higher quality and realism in tactile image rendering, particularly with the more advanced GelSight simulation and its ability to handle varied contact conditions.

#### 4.3. Sim2Real Transfer Validation

The most crucial finding is the successful transfer of models trained on OBJECTFOLDER 2.0's virtual data to real-world objects across three challenging tasks:

- **Object Scale Estimation (Table 3):**
 - Models trained on OBJECTFOLDER 2.0 exhibit significantly smaller errors when predicting the scale of real-world objects across all modalities compared to models trained on OBJECTFOLDER 1.0.
 - For example, visual scale estimation error on real objects dropped from 7.41 cm (1.0) to 5.08 cm (2.0); audio error from 6.85 cm to 4.68 cm; and touch error from 4.92 cm to **3.51 cm**.
 - Touch-based estimation shows the smallest Sim2Real gap, highlighting the realism and utility of the new TouchNet.

- **Tactile-Audio Contact Localization (Table 4, Figure 6):**
 - Models trained on OBJECTFOLDER 2.0 achieve superior real-world contact localization accuracy.
 - **Combining audio and touch modalities consistently yields the best Sim2Real performance**, reducing localization error. For instance, on the pitcher object, combined modalities achieved an error of 0.59 cm in real-world, significantly better than audio-only (1.79 cm) or touch-only (1.26 cm).
 - Touch provides much more accurate localization than audio when used independently.
 - The particle filter approach successfully converges to the ground-truth contact locations in real-world scenarios, demonstrating the practical applicability of the multisensory data.

- **Visuo-Tactile Shape Reconstruction (Table 5, Figure 7):**
 - Models trained on OBJECTFOLDER 2.0 successfully reconstruct the shapes of real-world objects from vision, touch, or a combination of both.
 - **Combining vision and touch leads to the best Sim2Real transfer performance**, achieving the lowest Chamfer-L1 distances to ground-truth meshes. For example, on the square tray, combined vision+touch achieved a real-world error of 0.25 cm, outperforming vision-only (0.32 cm) and touch-only (0.56 cm).
 - Qualitative results show accurate capture of object shapes in both simulation and real-world contexts, demonstrating the complementary nature of visual (global context) and tactile (precise local geometry) information.

In summary, the main findings confirm that OBJECTFOLDER 2.0 successfully delivers on its promises of scale, speed, quality, and crucial Sim2Real transferability. The enhanced multisensory implicit neural representations are instrumental in achieving these superior results, paving the way for more effective learning in robotics and computer vision.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

OBJECTFOLDER 2.0 represents a significant contribution to the fields of computer vision and robotics, offering a novel and powerful resource that addresses long-standing challenges in multisensory perception and Sim2Real transfer. Its potential impact is broad and transformative.

#### 5.1. Establishing a New Multisensory Benchmark

The creation of OBJECTFOLDER 2.0, with its 1,000 implicitly represented objects and high-fidelity multisensory data, establishes a new, large-scale, and realistic benchmark. This dataset provides a standardized testbed for researchers to develop and evaluate algorithms for:
- **Multisensory Learning:** It enables the exploration of how vision, audio, and touch can be integrated for more robust object perception, recognition, and manipulation. Researchers can develop models that leverage the complementary strengths of each modality.
- **Object-Centric Representation Learning:** The implicit neural representations offer a continuous and high-resolution way to model object properties, pushing the boundaries of how objects are represented and learned in AI systems.

#### 5.2. Bridging the Sim2Real Gap

The most critical aspect of OBJECTFOLDER 2.0 is its demonstrated success in facilitating Sim2Real transfer. By showing that models trained on virtual objects can generalize to real-world counterparts across challenging tasks like scale estimation, contact localization, and shape reconstruction, the dataset provides a credible pathway for:
- **Accelerated Robotic Development:** Robots can be trained extensively and safely in diverse simulated environments using OBJECTFOLDER 2.0, significantly reducing the need for costly, time-consuming, and potentially damaging real-world data collection and experimentation. This can speed up the development and deployment of intelligent robots.
- **Robust Perception Systems:** The ability to transfer learning from simulation to reality means AI systems can learn more generalizable and robust perception models, leading to better performance in unpredictable real-world scenarios.

#### 5.3. Increased Accessibility and Democratization of Research

Collecting real-world multisensory data is notoriously expensive, time-consuming, and technically challenging, often requiring specialized hardware and expertise. OBJECTFOLDER 2.0 democratizes access to such data by providing virtualized, high-quality alternatives. This means:
- **Lower Barrier to Entry:** Researchers, particularly those in institutions with limited resources or in regions facing logistical challenges (e.g., international shipping for specific objects), can now readily access diverse multisensory object data.
- **Focus on Algorithm Development:** The availability of this dataset allows researchers to concentrate more on developing innovative learning algorithms rather than expending significant effort on data acquisition.

#### 5.4. Enabling New Applications in Robotics and Computer Vision

The dataset is poised to enable a new generation of applications:
- **Advanced Robot Manipulation:** Robots can learn finer grasping strategies, identify objects by touch and sound, and adapt to object properties in real-time, leading to more dexterous and intelligent manipulation capabilities.
- **On-Policy Reinforcement Learning:** The fast rendering speed of OBJECTFOLDER 2.0 makes it suitable for on-the-fly data generation, which is crucial for on-policy reinforcement learning where agents continuously interact with their environment and generate new data.
- **Human-Robot Interaction:** More realistic multisensory feedback can lead to more intuitive and effective human-robot interaction interfaces.

#### 5.5. Broader Societal and Environmental Impact

The authors highlight that the computationally cheaper rendering of multisensory data compared to initial simulations could be "more environmentally friendly" by reducing the energy footprint associated with data generation. Furthermore, by making advanced robotic research more accessible, it can accelerate the development of AI solutions for various societal needs.

#### 5.6. Acknowledged Limitations and Future Work

Despite its significant contributions, the authors responsibly acknowledge several limitations, which also define exciting avenues for future research:
- **Object Rigidity and Homogeneity:** The dataset currently focuses on rigid-body objects with approximately homogeneous material properties. Real-world objects are often non-rigid, multi-part, and composed of diverse materials.
- **Environmental Complexity:** The current modeling simplifies environmental factors like diverse lighting, noise conditions, and reverberation effects.
Addressing these limitations would further enhance Sim2Real transfer and lead to even more realistic and comprehensive multisensory object models, building directly upon the strong foundation laid by OBJECTFOLDER 2.0.

In conclusion, OBJECTFOLDER 2.0 is not merely an updated dataset; it is a meticulously engineered research platform that pushes the boundaries of multisensory perception and Sim2Real transfer, promising to be an indispensable resource for advancing computer vision and robotics in the coming years.
