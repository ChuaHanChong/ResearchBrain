# Physics-Grounded Motion Forecasting via Equation Discovery for Trajectory-Guided Image-to-Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> A neuro-symbolic framework merges equation discovery with trajectory-guided video generation, enabling the creation of future video sequences where object motion adheres to learned physical laws. The method, using retrieval-based symbolic regression (ReSR), improves both equation discovery efficiency and the physical consistency and visual quality of generated videos, outperforming data-driven baselines.

## Problem

- Existing video generation models frequently fail to accurately replicate real-world physical dynamics in object motion, often learning only statistical correlations.
- Current trajectory-guided video generation methods rely on inputs like text prompts or manually drawn trajectories, which do not inherently guarantee adherence to underlying physical laws.
- Traditional symbolic regression (SR) algorithms, crucial for discovering physical equations, often suffer from slow convergence due to random initialization in their search process.

## Method

- The framework extracts object motion trajectories from an input video using a ==tracking model== like ==CoTracker==, focusing on points with high temporal variance.
- ==Retrieval-based Symbolic Regression== (**ReSR**) discovers governing physical equations by pre-training with a curated equation bank and initializing its search using a similarity metric (N-DTW) against extracted trajectories.
- Physics-aligned future trajectories, predicted by the discovered ==symbolic equations==, are then used to guide existing trajectory-guided image-to-video (I2V) models in an inference-only manner to synthesize physically consistent future video sequences.

## Results

- **ReSR** consistently outperformed baseline symbolic regression methods in equation discovery, showing faster convergence and achieving lower **MSE** in trajectory fitting and higher **TED** for symbolic similarity.
- Trajectory-guided video generation models, especially when utilizing **ReSR-predicted trajectories**, demonstrated improved visual quality (lower FVD/FID) and physics alignment (higher Motion Smoothness, lower Trajectory Error) compared to trajectory-free and manually guided baselines.
- Human evaluations confirmed a preference for videos generated using ReSR-guided trajectories, which achieved physical consistency comparable to videos generated with ground-truth trajectories and surpassed manually guided counterparts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly embedding physics knowledge via discovered symbolic equations significantly enhances the physical plausibility and consistency of object motion within generated videos.
> - A retrieval-based pre-training mechanism for symbolic regression effectively improves search efficiency, convergence speed, and accuracy by providing informed initial candidates from an equation bank.
> - While trajectory guidance improves global motion consistency, current data-driven generative models may still struggle with fine-grained physical details, indicating a need for stronger physical inductive biases beyond high-level conditioning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation has made significant progress, primarily driven by diffusion-based and autoregressive models, which have achieved considerable visual realism. These advancements have enabled the synthesis of visually compelling video sequences. A related development is trajectory-guided video generation, which incorporates explicit motion trajectories to control object movements within generated videos, aiming for more precise and intended dynamics.

However, a notable limitation of existing video generation models, including those that are trajectory-guided, is their frequent failure to accurately replicate real-world physical dynamics. This deficiency stems from their reliance on learning statistical correlations within large datasets rather than explicitly capturing the underlying physical laws that govern object motion. Current trajectory-guided methods often utilize text prompts, manually drawn trajectories, or statistically derived paths, none of which inherently ensure adherence to physical principles.

In parallel, symbolic regression (SR) represents a computational approach for automatically discovering mathematical equations from observational data. This method is distinct from traditional regression because it searches for both the structure and parameters of an equation, offering interpretability and robustness to out-of-distribution data. Scientists have historically formulated physical laws into symbolic equations to forecast object movements reliably, a process that typically does not require extensive training data.

This research aims to bridge the gap between visually realistic video generation and physically accurate motion. It integrates the strengths of symbolic regression, which can derive interpretable and physically consistent equations from observed motion data, with the capabilities of modern trajectory-guided image-to-video (I2V) models. By leveraging discovered physical equations to forecast future object trajectories, the work seeks to provide a mechanism for generating videos that are not only visually plausible but also adhere to the fundamental laws of physics. This approach represents a departure from purely data-driven methods by incorporating a neuro-symbolic component to instill physical consistency in generated content.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this study is to establish a novel framework for physics-grounded motion forecasting, specifically for trajectory-guided video generation. This framework aims to enable the creation of future video sequences where object motion is consistent with real-world physical dynamics, rather than merely statistically plausible.

The motivation for this work arises from several observed limitations in current video generation technologies:
- **Lack of Physical Alignment:** Existing diffusion-based and autoregressive video generation models, despite their progress in visual realism, often fail to accurately represent real-world physical dynamics in object motion. They tend to learn statistical correlations, which may not generalize to true physical mechanisms.
- **Unreliable Motion Forecasting:** Current trajectory-guided video generation methods typically rely on text prompts, manually drawn trajectories, or statistically inferred paths. These inputs do not inherently guarantee adherence to underlying physical laws, leading to potentially unrealistic or inconsistent object movements in generated videos.
- **Demand for Interpretable Dynamics:** In contrast to purely data-driven models, physicists understand object dynamics by formulating physical laws into symbolic equations. Such equations offer interpretability and reliable forecasting capabilities that are robust to shifts in data distributions. The authors are motivated to investigate whether AI methods can discover such physics equations directly from video clips.
- **Data Efficiency for Equation Discovery:** Traditional scientific discovery of physical laws often requires limited observational data for formulating symbolic equations, unlike the extensive data requirements and scaling laws common in contemporary deep learning models. A key motivation is to determine if AI methods can identify physics equations from a small number of video clips.
- **Enhancing Symbolic Regression:** Evolutionary algorithm (EA)-based symbolic regression methods, while powerful, can suffer from slow convergence due to random initialization. The authors are motivated to improve the efficiency and accuracy of SR by incorporating a mechanism for better initial search points.
- **Modular and Inference-Only Integration:** The goal is to integrate physics knowledge into video generation without requiring extensive retraining or fine-tuning of existing video generation models, enabling a modular and inference-only framework.

To address these motivations, the authors chose to conduct experiments in a controlled laboratory environment focusing on Classical Mechanics (e.g., spring-mass systems, pendulums, projectile motion). This setting allows for:
1. Direct evaluation of discovered equations against known ground-truth analytical expressions.
2. Gaining insights into fundamental object motion, which can be extended to more complex scenarios.
3. Relevance to a wide range of applications including physics simulation, scientific visualization, and physics education.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates symbolic regression (SR) with trajectory-guided image-to-video (I2V) generation models. The approach operates in an inference-only manner and comprises three main steps: extraction of object motion trajectories, discovery of governing equations via retrieval-based symbolic regression (ReSR), and subsequent trajectory-guided video generation.

#### 3.1. Task Formulation and Notations

Given an input video $V_i$ depicting an object's initial motion, the framework aims to generate a future video $V_o$. This involves:
1. Extracting motion trajectories $P = \{p_1, p_2..., p_n\}$ from $V_i$, where each $p_i = [p_1, p_2..., p_T]$ is a time series of object positions $(x_t, y_t)$.
2. Learning a pair of functions $f_{x_i}(t)$ and $f_{y_i}(t)$ for each trajectory $p_i$, such that $x_t = f_{x_i}(t)$ and $y_t = f_{y_i}(t)$.
3. Forecasting future trajectories $p_i = f_i(t)$ for $t \in \{T+1..., T+K\}$ using the learned equations.
4. Utilizing these predicted, physics-aligned trajectories to guide trajectory-based video generation models to synthesize $V_o$.

#### 3.2. Extraction of Object Motion Trajectory

To obtain motion data, the framework uses CoTracker, a state-of-the-art point tracking model, to extract object trajectories from the input video $V_i$.
- **Query Point Selection:** An automated approach is used where query points are uniformly sampled on a 2D $M \times M$ grid across the first frame. These points are then tracked throughout the video.
- **Trajectory Filtering:** After tracking, the temporal variance of each trajectory is computed. The top-k trajectories with the highest positional variance over time are retained. This strategy assumes that target objects in physics-driven videos exhibit the most motion, thereby filtering out static background noise and focusing on relevant object dynamics. All tracking is performed in the original image coordinate system.

#### 3.3. Symbolic Regression with Pre-training (ReSR)

This is the core component for discovering physical laws. Instead of initializing the SR search randomly, ReSR uses a retrieval-based pre-training mechanism.

#### 3.3.1. Construction of Equation Bank

A curated equation bank is established, containing a diverse set of equations from classical and empirical physics, serving as priors for motion dynamics. The bank includes equations from:
- **Feynman Equation Dataset:** Equations from "The Feynman Lectures on Physics" are adapted by substituting time-dependent variables with 't' and time-independent variables with constant values (e.g., 10) to preserve functional structure.
- **Nguyen Dataset:** Common empirical formulas from SR benchmarks are similarly adapted.
- **Manually Augmented Physics Equations:** Additional well-known motion dynamics, such as (damped) harmonic oscillators and projectile motions, are included to ensure broad coverage.
All equations are stored as symbolic expressions in Julia syntax.

#### 3.3.2. Retrieval-based Pre-training Mechanism

The pre-training mechanism retrieves candidate equations from the bank based on their similarity to the extracted object trajectories.
- **Similarity Metric:** Normalized Dynamic Time Warping (N-DTW) is employed. DTW is chosen for its ability to handle temporal misalignments like phase shifts and local time warping.
- **Normalization (N-DTW):** To overcome standard DTW's limitations with spatial offsets and scale variations, trajectories are rescaled to match the coordinate range of each equation-generated trajectory before DTW computation. This normalization ensures the comparison focuses on shape similarity.
 - $\bar{x}_t = (\hat{x}_{\text{max}} - \hat{x}_{\text{min}}) \cdot \frac{x_t - x_{\text{min}}}{x_{\text{max}} - x_{\text{min}}} + \hat{x}_{\text{min}}$
 - $\bar{y}_t = (\hat{y}_{\text{max}} - \hat{y}_{\text{min}}) \cdot \frac{y_t - y_{\text{min}}}{y_{\text{max}} - y_{\text{min}}} + \hat{y}_{\text{min}}$
- **Retrieval Process:** N-DTW scores are computed independently and in parallel for each equation in the bank. The top-k equations with the lowest N-DTW distances are selected as initial candidates.

#### 3.3.3. Initialization of ReSR

The SR algorithm is initialized using a hybrid strategy:
- An initialization weight hyperparameter $\alpha \in$ determines the proportion of the initial population seeded with retrieved equations.
- The remaining population members are randomly generated. This balances exploitation (using physics-aligned priors) and exploration (maintaining diversity).
- If the number of top-k retrieved equations is less than required by $\alpha$, equations are duplicated to fill the remaining slots, ensuring the initial population predominantly contains relevant equations.
This initialization occurs once at the beginning of the SR run. The implementation is based on a modified SymbolicRegression.jl framework.

#### 3.4. Trajectory-Guided Video Forecasting

To generate future videos consistent with the discovered motion dynamics, the framework integrates with existing trajectory-guided I2V models (e.g., SG-I2V, Tora, MotionCtrl).
- **Input to I2V Models:** The final frame of the observed input video $V_i$ serves as the starting image. The future trajectories, predicted by the equations learned from ReSR, are formatted as sequences of $(x, y)$ coordinates and sampled at intervals matching the I2V model requirements.
- **Modularity:** This integration is modular and model-agnostic, meaning it can be applied to any trajectory-guided I2V model without requiring retraining or fine-tuning of the generative model itself. This allows for the synthesis of future video sequences that are both visually plausible and physically governed by the inferred dynamics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research involved two sets of experiments: evaluating the performance of ReSR in discovering motion equations and assessing motion forecasting within trajectory-guided video generation.

#### 4.1. Evaluation of Symbolic Regression

**Datasets:** Trajectories were extracted from videos of classical physics systems, including spring-mass, damped spring-mass, two-body, and projectile motion (systems with ground-truth equations), as well as single pendulum, double pendulum, and fluid motion (systems without closed-form analytical solutions). Ten videos with varying initial states were used for each system. CoTracker extracted 10x10 grid query points, with the top 5 trajectories (by temporal variance) used for SR. Each trajectory was split 80%/10%/10% for training, validation, and evaluation, respectively, to assess generalization to future states.

**Metrics:**
- **Symbolic Similarity (with GT equations):** Normalized Tree Edit Distance (TED) measured the structural similarity between discovered and ground-truth equations. Higher TED values indicate better similarity.
- **Trajectory Error (all systems):** Mean Squared Error (MSE) quantified the deviation between trajectories generated by learned equations and actual observed trajectories. Lower MSE indicates better fit.
- **Convergence:** MSE at each iteration was tracked to compare convergence speeds.

**Baselines:** APO, gplearn, uDSR, KAN, PySR (ablation of ReSR without retrieval), and LaSR were used for comparison.

**Key Results (Table 2, Figure 2, Figure 3):**
- **Overall Performance:** ReSR consistently outperformed all baselines in both symbolic similarity (TED) and trajectory error (MSE) across all classical physics systems. This included systems without analytical solutions, where equation discovery is more challenging.
- **Ablation Study (ReSR with varying $\alpha$):** Performance improved as the initialization weight hyperparameter $\alpha$ (proportion of retrieved equations) increased, peaking at $\alpha=0.75$. This indicates that a hybrid initialization strategy balancing exploitation of priors and exploration through random sampling is beneficial. Setting $\alpha=1.0$ (exclusive reliance on retrieved equations) showed a slight performance degradation for some systems, suggesting the importance of diverse exploration.
- **Convergence Behavior:** ReSR demonstrated faster convergence and achieved lower MSE earlier in the search process compared to baselines (Figure 2). Baselines like PySR and LaSR, while reducing training error, often showed higher validation MSE, indicating potential overfitting and weaker generalization to future motion.
- **Qualitative Evaluation:** ReSR produced trajectories that closely aligned with observed and ground-truth trajectories, even for non-linear systems like the single pendulum (Figure 3). Baselines frequently yielded distorted or phase-shifted trajectories.

#### 4.2. Evaluation of Video Generation

**Datasets:** The same physical systems were used. Initial images were sourced from synthetic physics simulators and real-world videos. Equations learned by ReSR or other SR methods from the training segment (first 80%) were used to forecast trajectories into the test segment (final 10%).

**Metrics:**
- **Visual Quality:** Fréchet Video Distance (FVD), Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) measured the difference between generated and ground-truth videos. DINO Subject Consistency (SubC) assessed consistency between the initial image and generated video content. Lower FVD/FID and higher SubC are desirable.
- **Physics Alignment:** Motion Smoothness (AMT) quantified temporal coherence. Trajectory Error (TrajErr) measured the deviation between the input trajectory and the actual object trajectory extracted from the generated video. Higher Smoothness and lower TrajErr are desirable.
- **Human Evaluation:** Pairwise comparisons were conducted by three annotators, judging visual quality and physics alignment for selected top-performing models (CogVideoX1.5, Tora, Kling, and a physics simulator).

**Video Generation Models:**
- **Trajectory-free I2V:** SVD, CogVideoX1.5, Cosmos, HunyuanVideo, ID.
- **Trajectory-guided I2V:** DragAnything, MotionCtrl, SG-I2V, Tora, Kling (commercial model).
- **Reference:** A physics simulator was used for synthetic data as an upper-bound performance reference.

**Key Results (Table 3, Table 4, Figure 4):**
- **Trajectory-Guided vs. Trajectory-Free:** Models guided by predicted trajectories consistently outperformed trajectory-free baselines in both visual quality and physics alignment (Table 3).
- **Performance of Trajectory-Guided Models:** Kling demonstrated the strongest overall performance among the trajectory-guided I2V models. However, a significant performance gap remained between Kling and the physics simulator, particularly for physics alignment metrics. Synthetic data generally yielded better performance than real-world initial frames, attributed to background noise and system complexity.
- **ReSR Trajectory Effectiveness:** Kling guided by ReSR-predicted trajectories outperformed Kling using manually drawn or LaSR-derived trajectories, confirming ReSR's ability to provide more accurate physical dynamics. Furthermore, Kling guided by ReSR achieved performance comparable to Kling using ground-truth future trajectories (extracted by CoTracker), indicating the high fidelity of ReSR's predictions.
- **Human Evaluation:** Human annotators preferred trajectory-guided models over trajectory-free baselines. Kling with ReSR-guided trajectories was preferred over its manually guided counterpart. The physics simulator was rated as the most physically accurate in the synthetic setting, highlighting the limitations of current data-driven generative models in explicitly capturing physical causality.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** Trajectory-guided models showed improved global motion consistency compared to erratic motions from trajectory-free models (Figure 4). Despite this, even strong models like Kling still struggled with fine-grained physical details (e.g., spring deformation), suggesting a lack of physical inductive biases beyond high-level motion conditioning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a novel neuro-symbolic framework that significantly advances the capabilities of video generation by embedding physically accurate motion forecasting. The integration of equation discovery into the video generation pipeline addresses a critical limitation of existing models, which often produce visually realistic but physically inconsistent animations.

The key contributions and their significance include:
- **Physics-Grounded Video Generation:** The framework enables the generation of future video sequences where object motion is explicitly governed by learned physical laws. This provides a method to create videos that are not only visually plausible but also adhere to real-world dynamics, moving beyond mere statistical correlations.
- **Enhanced Symbolic Regression (ReSR):** The introduction of a retrieval-based pre-training mechanism for symbolic regression (ReSR) improves the efficiency, convergence speed, and accuracy of equation discovery. This enhancement makes SR more practical for real-world applications by providing a more informed starting point for the search process.
- **Interpretability and Robustness:** By discovering explicit symbolic equations, the framework offers interpretable representations of physical laws from observational video data. These equations are more robust to out-of-distribution scenarios compared to purely data-driven models, which can struggle when encountering novel conditions.
- **Modular and Inference-Only Design:** The approach allows existing trajectory-guided I2V models to generate physics-consistent videos without requiring retraining or fine-tuning. This modularity makes the framework flexible and readily applicable to a wide range of current and future generative models.
- **Bridging Data-Driven and Physics-Based Approaches:** The work highlights the limitations of current data-driven video generation models in capturing physical causality, even when provided with high-level trajectory guidance. By combining the realism and flexibility of generative models with the interpretability and fidelity of physics-based equations, it paves the way for a hybrid approach that leverages the strengths of both paradigms.

The potential impacts of this research are broad:
- **Scientific Discovery and Education:** The ability to automatically discover physical laws from video data can accelerate scientific discovery by providing interpretable models. It also has significant implications for physics education, enabling the creation of accurate and interactive visualizations of physical phenomena.
- **World Simulations and Robotics:** Generating physically consistent simulations is crucial for training and testing intelligent agents in robotics. This framework can contribute to developing more realistic and reliable world models for reinforcement learning and robotic control.
- **Computer Graphics and Content Creation:** For applications requiring high physical fidelity, such as special effects, animation, or architectural visualization, this method offers a way to generate more convincing and accurate motion.
- **Advancement of Neuro-Symbolic AI:** The work represents a step forward in neuro-symbolic AI, demonstrating the benefits of integrating neural network capabilities (for video processing and generation) with symbolic reasoning (for equation discovery) to achieve tasks that are challenging for either paradigm alone.
