# TOPReward: Token Probabilities as Hidden Zero-Shot Rewards for Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> TOPReward introduces a method to extract zero-shot task progress rewards from video Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by leveraging the probability of an affirmative answer token rather than requiring direct numerical generation. This approach enables strong performance with open-source VLMs, achieving high value-order correlation and outperforming prior methods in real-world manipulation tasks and success detection, while also enhancing policy learning.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) in robotics is hampered by the 'sparse reward problem,' requiring labor-intensive, hand-crafted reward functions that lack scalability and generalization across tasks.
- Existing training-free VLM-based reward models struggle with open-source VLMs when asked to generate explicit numerical progress values, limiting their broad applicability for zero-shot progress estimation.
- A 'representation bottleneck' prevents effective utilization of open-source VLMs' underlying video understanding for progress estimation, as their numerical text generation is often inconsistent.

## Method

- TOPReward bypasses numerical text generation by prompting the VLM with a binary question about task completion and extracting the log probability of the 'True' token as the reward signal.
- The method evaluates progress on uniformly spaced video trajectory prefixes, then normalizes these log probabilities to derive a continuous, dense progress score within each episode.
- A new benchmark, ManiRewardBench, was developed to rigorously evaluate progress estimation across over 130 real-world manipulation tasks, four robot platforms, and including stage-aware annotations and failure trajectories.

## Results

- TOPReward with Qwen3-VL-8B achieved 0.857 Value-Order Correlation (VOC) on the OXE dataset, a +0.663 improvement over GVL's 0.194, and maintained high VOC (0.942–0.954) on the ManiRewardBench across diverse robot platforms.
- The method demonstrates superior success detection capabilities on failed trajectories, yielding 0.654 ROC-AUC with Qwen3-VL-8B, outperforming GVL's near-random 0.519.
- Integrating TOPReward's derived progress signals into Advantage-Weighted Behavior Cloning (AWR) consistently improved policy performance over a standard Behavior Cloning baseline across six real-world manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Open-source Vision-Language Models possess substantial latent knowledge for understanding robotic task progress, which can be robustly accessed by querying their internal probabilistic beliefs rather than explicit numerical outputs.
> - The observed limitations of open-source VLMs in generating numerical progress scores are primarily a 'representation bottleneck' in text generation, not a lack of underlying temporal or task understanding.
> - Prompt formatting, such as the use of chat templates, can significantly interfere with the direct extraction of internal token probabilities, highlighting the sensitivity of logit-based VLM querying.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is rapidly evolving with the integration of large Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which have shown impressive capabilities in language-conditioned manipulation. However, a significant bottleneck for deploying these models in real-world scenarios, particularly when leveraging Reinforcement Learning (RL), is the "sparse reward problem" and low sample efficiency. Traditional RL often requires hand-crafted, task-specific reward functions, which are labor-intensive, difficult to scale, and prone to breaking under minor environmental or task variations.

Existing solutions in the broader landscape can be categorized:
- **Learned Reward Models (Task-Specific Fine-tuning):** Many approaches involve fine-tuning VLMs or training specialized networks on curated robot datasets. Examples include RoboDopamine and RoboReward, which train on extensive manipulation data or human-provided labels. While these show promise, they often require domain-specific training data, lack generalization across different robot embodiments or environments, and can necessitate additional demonstrations for new tasks.
- **Embedding-Based Methods:** Approaches like VIP, LIV, and R3M learn visual representations to capture progress but also typically require task-specific fine-tuning and offer limited language grounding.
- **VQA-style Success Detectors:** Methods like SuccessVQA reframe reward as a binary classification (task succeeded/failed). However, the resulting signal is often too coarse for dense reward shaping needed for effective RL.
- **Code-Generation Strategies:** Eureka synthesizes reward functions using LLMs but relies on simulation ground truth.
- **Training-Free Value Estimation with VLMs:** This is the most directly relevant category. Generative Value Learning (GVL) represents the state-of-the-art in training-free progress estimation. GVL prompts a VLM to directly output numerical progress scores (0-1) for shuffled trajectory frames, using its semantic understanding to order them. Critically, the paper highlights that GVL performs well only on proprietary VLMs (like Gemini, GPT-4) and often collapses on open-source alternatives, leading to the perception that open-source VLMs are not "robotics-ready" for progress estimation.

TOPReward enters this landscape by directly challenging the latter assumption. It addresses the fundamental limitation of existing training-free VLM-based reward models: their reliance on explicit numerical text generation. The authors hypothesize that the failure of open-source VLMs in GVL-like settings is not due to a lack of underlying video understanding but rather a "representation bottleneck" stemming from inconsistent instruction-following and numerical misrepresentation in text generation. By circumventing explicit text generation and instead probing the VLM's internal probabilistic "belief," TOPReward aims to unlock the zero-shot reward modeling capabilities of open-source VLMs, thereby democratizing access to powerful and generalizable reward signals.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of TOPReward is to develop a novel, probabilistically grounded temporal value function that can leverage the latent world knowledge of pre-trained video Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to estimate robotic task progress in a **zero-shot** manner. This means it should function effectively without any additional training or fine-tuning on task-specific robot data.

The core motivations behind this objective are:
- **Addressing the Reward Bottleneck in Real-World RL:** The scarcity of dense, fine-grained reward signals is a major impediment to the widespread application of RL in robotics. Generalizable process reward models are crucial for providing the necessary feedback.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing VLM-based Reward Models:** Previous training-free methods like GVL, which prompt VLMs to output numerical progress values, have shown poor performance on open-source VLMs. The authors hypothesize that this is not due to a lack of temporal understanding in these models, but rather a **representation bottleneck** associated with the textual output, specifically the models' difficulties with consistent instruction-following and accurate numerical generation.
- **Harnessing Latent VLM Knowledge:** Pre-trained VLMs contain vast amounts of world knowledge learned from web-scale data. The motivation is to find a robust way to extract this implicit understanding of task progress, even from open-source models, which have previously been deemed unsuitable for this task.
- **Enabling Scalable and Generalizable Robotics:** By providing a zero-shot, generalizable progress estimator, TOPReward aims to facilitate scalable robot learning across diverse tasks and embodiments, reducing the reliance on costly, task-specific data collection and human annotation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TOPReward's methodology revolves around a fundamental shift in how Vision-Language Models are queried for progress estimation. Instead of asking the VLM to generate a numerical progress value (e.g., "75% complete"), it asks a binary question about task completion and extracts the probability associated with an affirmative answer token.

#### 3.1. Token Probability as the Reward Signal

The core idea is to bypass autoregressive text generation for numerical outputs.
- **Prompt Formulation:** Given an instruction `x` and a video trajectory prefix `τ_1:t`, a prompt `u` is constructed to ground the judgment in the video:
 " `<|video|> The above video shows a robot manipulation trajectory that completes the following task: {INSTRUCTION}. Decide whether the above statement is True or not. The answer is: {a}`"
- **Probability Extraction:** TOPReward then computes the log probability `log p_θ(a | c(τ_1:t, u))` of the answer token `a="True"` given the video-conditioned textual context. This leverages the VLM's internal belief distribution over tokens. The "True" token was specifically chosen for its single-token nature and its observed largest absolute difference in mean token probability between successful and failed episodes.
- **Zero-Shot Nature:** This approach requires zero additional training or fine-tuning, as it directly uses the pre-trained VLM's internal logits.

#### 3.2. Progress Estimation from Trajectory Prefixes

- **Prefix Sampling:** To obtain a continuous temporal progress curve, the log probability is evaluated on a set of `K` uniformly spaced video trajectory prefixes (`τ_1:t_k`). This results in `K` model forwards, generating a sequence of rewards `{r_t_k}`.
- **Normalization:** Since log probabilities range from `(-∞, 0]`, min-max normalization is applied within each episode to map these raw rewards to a standardized progress score `s_t_k` in the range ``.
- **Dense Rewards for Downstream Use:** For applications requiring a per-step reward, such as reward-aligned behavior cloning, a progress increment `Δ_t_k` is derived from the change in normalized progress scores (`exp(s_t_k - s_t_k-1)`), scaled and clipped.

#### 3.3. ManiRewardBench: A New Benchmark

To rigorously evaluate progress estimation methods, the authors introduce ManiRewardBench.
- **Scope and Diversity:** The benchmark comprises over 130 unique real-world manipulation tasks across four distinct robot platforms (Franka, SO-100/101, single-arm YAM, and bimanual YAM).
- **Stage-Aware Annotation:** Episodes are manually labeled and segmented into predefined, ordered subtasks with start/end time annotations. This allows for fine-grained evaluation of progress understanding beyond coarse success/failure.
- **Failure Trajectories:** It includes a dataset of 23 tasks with 156 episodes containing both successful and failed attempts, specifically designed to test success detection capabilities.
- **Controlled Access:** To ensure evaluation integrity and prevent data leakage, the underlying dataset is restricted and accessible only through a controlled evaluation protocol.

#### 3.4. VLM Backbones

The method is evaluated on three video-language models: Qwen3-VL-8B, Molmo2 (both open-source), and Gemini-2.5-Pro (proprietary). Qwen3-VL and Molmo2 are chosen for their strong video understanding capabilities and reproducibility, while Gemini-2.5-Pro serves as a proprietary baseline allowing logit access.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

TOPReward demonstrates significant advancements in zero-shot progress estimation for robotics, particularly with open-source VLMs.

#### 4.1. Large-Scale Real-World Evaluation (Progress Estimation)

- **Metrics:** Value-Order Correlation (VOC), a Spearman's rank correlation between chronological order and predicted values, is used.
- **Open X-Embodiment (OXE) Dataset:** TOPReward substantially outperforms GVL on open-source models. With Qwen3-VL-8B, TOPReward achieves 0.857 VOC, a +0.663 improvement over GVL's 0.194. With Molmo2-8B, TOPReward reaches 0.417 VOC, a +0.433 improvement over GVL's -0.016. On the proprietary Gemini-2.5-Pro, GVL performs better (0.541 VOC) than TOPReward (0.433 VOC), an anomaly later explained by the chat template ablation.
- **ManiRewardBench:** On this new benchmark (113 tasks, 497 episodes across 4 platforms), TOPReward achieves remarkably consistent high performance with Qwen3-VL-8B (mean VOC of 0.942–0.954 across datasets). GVL, in contrast, struggles with near-zero or negative VOC on Molmo2-8B and shows inconsistent performance (0.164–0.544) on other models.
- **Qualitative Results:** Progress traces show TOPReward producing smooth, monotonically increasing signals that accurately track stage-aware ground-truth task completion, even capturing plateaus during intermediate subtask completions. Gemini-GVL, where available, exhibits noisier and non-monotonic predictions.

#### 4.2. Success Detection

- **VOC Failure Mode:** The paper identifies a critical limitation of VOC: it measures rank correlation, not absolute completion. A trajectory that makes early progress and then plateaus (a failure) can still achieve a high VOC if the predictions are well-ordered, preventing it from distinguishing incomplete but ordered failures from full successes.
- **Results:** TOPReward, which directly measures the probability of instruction satisfaction, naturally assigns lower scores to failed trajectories. On the failure trajectory split of ManiRewardBench, TOPReward with Qwen3-VL-8B achieves 0.654 ROC-AUC for success detection, significantly outperforming GVL's near-random 0.519. On Gemini, both methods perform comparably (0.826 for TOPReward vs. 0.823 for GVL), suggesting the VOC failure mode is more pronounced when the VLM already struggles with calibrated progress estimation.

#### 4.3. Real-World Advantage-Weighted Behavior Cloning (AWR)

- **Application:** TOPReward's progress signal is used to compute advantage weights for AWR, fine-tuning a base policy pretrained on the SO-100 dataset.
- **Results:** AWR with TOPReward-derived advantages consistently outperforms a standard Behavior Cloning (BC) baseline across six real-world manipulation tasks. For example, on "Place doll in box," TOP-AWR achieved 10 out of 10 successes, while BC reached only 7.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study (Effect of Chat Templates)

- **Hypothesis:** The relatively lower performance of TOPReward on Gemini-2.5-Pro was hypothesized to be due to the API enforcing a chat template.
- **Results:** Wrapping TOPReward's prompt with a chat template significantly degraded VOC scores for both Qwen3-VL-8B (nearly 50% drop) and Molmo2-8B (20% drop). This confirms that the logit-based formulation is sensitive to prompt formatting and that chat templates can interfere with the direct extraction of internal probabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TOPReward represents a significant step forward in making generalizable, zero-shot reward modeling accessible and effective for real-world robotics. Its core contribution is demonstrating that the inherent video understanding capabilities of even open-source VLMs can be robustly leveraged for task progress estimation, provided the "representation bottleneck" of numerical text generation is circumvented.

**Key Significance:**
- **Democratization of Zero-Shot Reward Modeling:** By enabling strong performance with open-source VLMs like Qwen3-VL-8B, TOPReward reduces the reliance on expensive proprietary models (like Gemini, GPT-4), making advanced robot learning more accessible to a broader research community and practitioners.
- **Scalability and Data Efficiency:** The zero-shot nature eliminates the need for extensive task-specific data collection or fine-tuning for reward model training, which is a major bottleneck in real-world RL. This significantly improves scalability for deploying robots on novel tasks.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** TOPReward's high VOC and consistent performance across diverse tasks and robot platforms (as shown in ManiRewardBench and OXE) highlight its strong generalization capabilities, addressing a critical challenge in robotics.
- **Versatile Downstream Applications:** The demonstrated utility in success detection (where VOC-based methods fail for open-source models) and in improving policy learning through advantage-weighted behavior cloning illustrates TOPReward's versatility as a foundational tool for various robotics applications. This can lead to more robust policy evaluation, data curation (e.g., filtering suboptimal trajectories), and more effective robot learning.
- **Deeper Understanding of VLMs:** The work offers insights into the internal workings of VLMs, suggesting that their limitations in numerical output generation do not necessarily reflect a lack of underlying conceptual understanding. By probing internal logits, researchers can better access and utilize the "latent world knowledge" embedded within these powerful models.

**Potential Impact:**
TOPReward has the potential to accelerate the development and deployment of generalist robot policies. By providing a reliable, automated, and scalable reward signal, it can unlock more efficient reinforcement learning, lead to robots that learn faster and generalize better, and reduce the human effort required for task specification and data annotation. This could ultimately contribute to more autonomous and capable robots in various domains, from manufacturing and logistics to assistive robotics and exploration.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by authors):**
- **Visual Perception Limitations:** TOPReward's performance is bounded by the underlying VLM's visual perception capabilities. Tasks requiring extremely fine-grained spatial reasoning or subtle visual cues may still lead to noisy progress estimates.
- **Normalization Scope:** The per-episode min-max normalization prevents direct comparison of absolute progress values across different trajectories without additional calibration. However, the success detection experiments show that the raw probability of the entire trajectory can be used for quality comparison.
- **VLM Backbone Dependency:** While TOPReward makes current open-source VLMs effective, its ultimate performance will still improve with advancements in the video understanding capabilities of future VLM backbones.
