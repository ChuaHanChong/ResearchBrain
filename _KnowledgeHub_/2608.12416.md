# RoboSynChallenge: Mastering Real-World Dexterity via Generalizing Synthesized Manipulation Skills

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboSynChallenge is a competition benchmark for generalizable bimanual manipulation that pairs large-scale generative simulation data with standardized real-world evaluation. Trials are procedurally synthesized in EmbodiChain, optionally co-trained with a small teleoperated real dataset, then policies are deployed on dual-arm AgileX Piper hardware across ten tasks at three difficulty levels. Baseline results show simulation-trained policies match or beat real-data-trained ones.

## Problem

- Real-world robot benchmarks depend on costly, slow physical interaction data tied to specific hardware, so they cannot scale like internet-scale vision or language corpora.
- Simulation scales data cheaply but dynamics, kinematics, and sensing mismatches leave a Sim2Real gap that degrades real-world deployment performance.
- Prior benchmarks use fixed offline datasets in static single-modality settings, mostly single-arm, so they cannot measure generalization to unseen objects and environments.

## Method

- ==EmbodiChain== generative simulation pipeline: ==generative asset synthesis== plus ==gradient-based layout composition==, ==reachability-aware trajectory sampling==, ==closed-loop error-recovery relabeling==, and ==Online Data Streaming== visual augmentation.
- ==Sim-and-real co-training protocol==: 1,000 randomized synthetic trials per task over lighting, materials, geometry, table, background, and camera intrinsics/extrinsics, mixed with teleoperated real trajectories.
- ==Real-world evaluation harness== on dual 6-DoF AgileX Piper arms spanning ten entry/mid/high-level tasks, scored by success rate, inference time, and action steps under held-out out-of-distribution conditions.

## Results

- Simulation-only training matches or exceeds real-only training on average success: pi0.5 reaches **38.50%** with sim data versus **33.00%** with real, pi0 **22.00%** versus **22.50%**.
- Motus, the world-action-model baseline, hits the best per-task scores on Click Bell (**14/20** real, **13/20** sim) but is slowest, averaging roughly **130s** versus **80-90s** for the VLA baselines.
- High-precision tasks stay unsolved: every baseline scores **0/20** on Item Assembly and exhausts the **1000**-step action budget, while Sample Loading and Pipette peak at **4/20**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Carefully randomized synthetic data can reach parity with teleoperated real data for policy training, shifting the bottleneck from data quantity to evaluation rigor.
> - Success rate alone hides cost: action-step counts and inference latency expose whether a policy is recovering from errors or silently burning its budget.
> - Coarse pick-and-place is largely saturated, but fine-grained multi-stage assembly and tool use remain a hard floor where current VLA and WAM policies score zero.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalizable robotic manipulation policies is a central challenge within the field of embodied artificial intelligence (EAI). Data-driven learning methods have emerged as a primary approach for developing such policies, with advancements seen in architectures like Transformers, diffusion models, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) frameworks, and World Action Models (WAMs). These models aim to enable end-to-end learning that maps multimodal observations and language instructions to continuous robot actions.

However, progress in this area faces limitations primarily due to data scarcity and the restricted diversity of real-world datasets. Unlike fields such as computer vision or natural language processing, which benefit from vast internet-scale datasets, real-world robotic benchmarks rely on data collected through physical interaction, a process that is costly, time-consuming, and specific to particular hardware configurations. While simulation offers a scalable solution for data generation, the inherent discrepancies in dynamics, kinematics, and sensing between simulated and real environments lead to a "Sim2Real gap." This gap often results in degraded performance when policies trained in simulation are deployed in the real world.

Furthermore, many existing robotic benchmarks are categorized as either purely simulation-based or purely real-world. These benchmarks frequently rely on fixed datasets and static, controlled environments, which may not adequately assess the generalization capabilities required for generalist policies to operate with unseen objects and diverse environmental conditions.

RoboSynChallenge addresses these limitations by providing a unified benchmark that specifically targets the evaluation and advancement of generalizability for manipulation policies. It seeks to bridge the Sim2Real gap by integrating large-scale synthetic data generation with standardized real-world robotic evaluation. The work fits into the landscape by proposing a method to quantify how effectively simulated data can enhance policy generalizability in scenarios where real-world data is limited, thereby contributing to the development of broadly capable, data-efficient, and adaptable manipulation systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the RoboSynChallenge competition is to introduce a unified benchmark designed to evaluate and advance the generalizability of robotic manipulation policies across a spectrum of tasks, environments, and difficulty levels. A central motivation behind this objective is to quantify the effectiveness of simulated data in improving the generalizability of manipulation policies, particularly when real-world data is scarce.

The specific motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

- **Alleviation of Real-World Data Scarcity**: Real-world robotic data collection is inherently costly, slow, and hardware-dependent. To overcome this limitation, the challenge integrates large-scale synthetic data generation. This aims to expand available training datasets and reduce the reliance on expensive real-world interactions for policy learning.
- **Bridging the Sim2Real Gap**: Mismatches between simulation and reality often hinder the deployment of policies trained in simulated environments. RoboSynChallenge is motivated by the need to systematically study and mitigate this Sim2Real gap. Participants are encouraged to leverage synthesized state-action trials, with the understanding that final assessments will be conducted exclusively on unseen real-world manipulation environments. This setup directly evaluates the transferability of skills learned in simulation to physical reality.
- **Evaluation of Generalizable Policies**: Existing benchmarks often rely on static datasets and controlled environments, which may not adequately test a policy's ability to generalize to novel conditions. The challenge aims to foster the development of generalist policies that can adapt to unseen objects, varied environments, and different levels of task difficulty. The evaluation protocol incorporates diverse real-world variations (e.g., background, lighting, object instances, distractors, spatial generalization) to rigorously assess this capability.
- **Establishment of a Standardized Benchmark**: RoboSynChallenge seeks to create a standardized real-world benchmark that covers the entire pipeline of dexterous manipulation policy development. This includes large-scale data generation, policy learning utilizing synthesized data, and rigorous evaluation within physical environments.
- **Enhancement of Reproducibility**: The competition aims to improve the reproducibility of robotic control policies by providing standardized data synthesis protocols and evaluation frameworks. This standardization is intended to ensure consistent benchmarking and enable fair comparisons among different methods in real-world settings.
- **Provision of Open-Source Tools and Baselines**: To facilitate community engagement and research, the challenge commits to providing publicly available baseline models, data synthesis tools, and training pipelines. This open-source approach is intended to foster transparent evaluation, reproducible experimentation, and collaborative progress in robotics research.
- **Advancement Towards General Robotic Intelligence**: By coupling scalable simulation-based training with rigorous real-world validation, RoboSynChallenge aims to provide a foundational platform for developing truly generalizable manipulation policies. This approach is intended to enable systematic study of scalable policy generalization, thereby contributing to the broader vision of general robotic intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RoboSynChallenge employs a comprehensive pipeline designed to facilitate policy development using synthetic data and evaluate its generalization performance in real-world settings. The methodology integrates data generation, policy learning, and a rigorous real-world evaluation protocol.

#### 3.1. Competition Pipeline

The challenge's workflow is structured into several stages:
1. **Data Synthesis**: Large-scale manipulation trials are generated within a simulated environment.
2. **Data Filtering**: This stage is implied to refine the synthesized data for quality.
3. **Policy Learning**: Participants train manipulation policies using the synthesized data, potentially combined with a smaller amount of static real-world data provided. Models and algorithms for policy learning include World Action Models (WAM), Vision-Language-Action (VLA), Diffusion Policy (DP), and Action Chunking Transformer (ACT), utilizing methods like Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Imitation Learning.
4. **Real-World Deployment**: Trained policies are deployed on physical robot platforms.
5. **Evaluation**: Final assessments are conducted exclusively on unseen real-world manipulation environments.

#### 3.2. Data Collection and Generation

The dataset for RoboSynChallenge is composed of both large-scale synthesized data and a smaller subset of real-world data.

- **Synthesized Data Collection Protocol**:
 - Data is primarily generated using the EmbodiChain generative simulation framework.
 - This framework produces embodied interaction trajectories, multi-modal sensory streams (RGB-D, proprioceptive signals, physics metadata), and structured annotations for task semantics, contact events, and success outcomes.
 - The generation process involves systematic perturbations across various parameters to enhance diversity and robustness:
 - **Lighting**: Intensity, position, and color variations.
 - **Object Attributes**: Initial position, position range, orientation, material (base color), and size scaling.
 - **Table Properties**: Height variation, random texture probability, and base color range.
 - **Background Color**: Variation in background plate color.
 - **Camera Parameters**: Randomization of both intrinsic (focal length) and extrinsic (position, rotation) camera parameters.
 - **Robot Configuration**: End-effector position and joint configuration randomization.
 - **Distractors**: Common task-irrelevant items (e.g., bowls, cups, toys) are introduced for scene complexity.
 - For each task, 1,000 manipulation trials are generated with randomized variations.
 - The complete data collection pipeline is open-source.
 - Participants can also leverage the provided realistic data to guide video generation models or scene augmentation techniques for further data synthesis.

- **Real-World Data Collection Protocol**:
 - A smaller subset of data is collected through teleoperation to provide real-world correspondence and mitigate Sim2Real transfer difficulty.
 - Each manipulation task is executed under five distinct experimental conditions, four positional variations, and three orientation settings, totaling 60 samples per task.
 - Experimental conditions introduce variability in background texture (Table 3), illumination, and scene complexity (e.g., distractors).
- **Confidential Ground Truth and Leakage Prevention**: Evaluation measures real-world performance directly without requiring access to testing data or ground-truth labels for verification. Training data consists solely of successful trajectories in the learning environment. Test environments are held-out and out-of-distribution relative to training observations to quantify generalizability and prevent data leakage.
- **Data Release**: Training and evaluation datasets are made available to registered participants through online platforms (e.g., Hugging Face) under a Creative Commons Attribution–NonCommercial–ShareAlike 4.0 (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) license.

#### 3.3. Hardware and Tasks

- **Hardware**: The evaluation utilizes a dual-arm platform consisting of two AgileX Piper manipulators, each with 6-DoF. This platform is equipped with cameras and configurable lighting, and backup workstations are provided for scalability and reliability.
- **Manipulation Tasks**: Ten diverse bimanual manipulation tasks are defined, performed solely based on visual observations and robot proprioceptive feedback. These tasks are categorized into three difficulty levels (Figure 3, Table 5):
 - **Entry-level**: Short-horizon, low-contact complexity (e.g., Click Bell, Dual-Arm Water Pouring, Table Rearrangement, Basket Pick-and-Place).
 - **Mid-level**: Compound, sequential interactions requiring moderate coordination and adaptive control (e.g., Items Hand-Over, Drawer Open and Place, Mixer Operating).
 - **High-level**: Fine-grained manipulation demanding high precision, multi-stage sequencing, and dynamic adaptation (e.g., Item Assembly, Manipulate Pipette, Sample Loading).
 - The competition includes both in-distribution testing (with respect to real-world training data) for development and out-of-distribution testing as a held-out benchmark for generalization assessment.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Protocol and Metrics

- **Evaluation Protocol**: Policies are assessed in the real world under systematic variations:
 - **Background**: Three table textures (wood, blue fabric, yellow grid).
 - **Lighting**: Three distinct light positions with varying illumination colors.
 - **Object Variation**: Both seen and unseen object instances within the same task category.
 - **Distractor Presence**: Task-irrelevant objects at three difficulty levels (2, 4, and 8 distractors).
 - **Spatial Generalization**: Evaluation on unseen positions within a predefined 3x3 grid.
 - Each task is evaluated under a canonical base configuration, followed by varying one factor at a time. Success rates are reported across multiple object positions.
- **Evaluation Metrics**:
 - **Success Rate (SR)**: Percentage of task completions according to predefined criteria.
 - **Inference Time**: Time taken by the policy to generate actionable commands (measured on an NVIDIA A800 GPU).
 - **Action Steps**: Number of control steps required to complete a task. Higher counts may indicate latency, errors, or incomplete task execution.

#### 3.5. Baselines and Provided Resources

- **Baselines**: Five representative policy families are provided:
 - Action Chunking Transformer (ACT)
 - Diffusion Policy
 - π0 (VLA model)
 - π0.5 (VLA model building on π0)
 - Motus (unified latent action world model)
- **Resources**: A starting kit, including baseline code, data-loading/preprocessing tools, sample simulated data, and a data-generation pipeline, is released through a dedicated GitHub repository. A competition website and tutorial are also provided.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents experimental results for three baseline models (π0, π0.5, and Motus) across ten diverse manipulation tasks. These models were trained using either simulation-only data (`(sim)`) or real-only data (`(real)`) and subsequently evaluated in real-world environments. The evaluation focused on Success Rate (SR), Action Steps, and Inference Time.

#### 4.1. General Performance Trends

The empirical results indicate that the performance metrics of models trained on the provided simulation data are comparable to, and in several scenarios, outperform those trained on limited real-world data when both are deployed in the real world. This observation suggests that the synthesized data produced by the challenge's framework possesses a quality sufficient for training robust manipulation policies that transfer to physical environments.

#### 4.2. Task-Specific and Model-Specific Analysis

- **π0 Baseline**:
 - The average success rate for π0 trained on simulation data was 22.00%, while for real-world data, it was 22.50%. This indicates very similar overall performance.
 - On specific tasks, π0 (sim) achieved higher success rates in "Click Bell" (8/20 vs 5/20), "Basket Pick-and-Place" (5/20 vs 6/20 - real slightly better), and "Mixer Operating" (3/20 vs 1/20).
 - Conversely, π0 (real) showed slightly better performance on "Table Rearrangement" (8/20 vs 7/20) and "Sample Loading" (3/20 vs 2/20).
 - Action Steps and Inference Time generally varied, but no consistent pattern clearly favored one training data source over the other for this model across all metrics.

- **π0.5 Baseline**:
 - The average success rate for π0.5 trained on simulation data was 38.50%, notably higher than the 33.00% achieved by the real-data-trained counterpart. This suggests that the synthetic data provided a stronger basis for training this particular VLA model.
 - π0.5 (sim) consistently performed better or comparably across several tasks, including "Click Bell" (10/20 vs 6/20), "Items Hand-Over and Place" (7/20 vs 5/20), "Drawer Open and Place" (11/20 vs 12/20 - real slightly better), and "Mixer Operating" (4/20 vs 3/20).
 - Action steps were often lower for π0.5 (sim) in successful runs, indicating potentially more efficient task completion. Inference times were generally similar for both training data sources for this model.

- **Motus Baseline**:
 - The average success rate for Motus trained on simulation data was 31.50%, which also surpassed the 27.50% from real-data training.
 - Motus (sim) demonstrated stronger performance in "Click Bell" (13/20 vs 14/20 - real slightly better), "Items Hand-Over and Place" (10/20 vs 12/20 - real slightly better), "Dual-Arm Water Pouring" (8/20 vs 6/20), and "Drawer Open and Place" (10/20 vs 11/20 - real slightly better).
 - However, Motus (real) outperformed Motus (sim) significantly on "Table Rearrangement" (3/20 vs 4/20 - sim slightly better), "Mixer Operating" (0/20 vs 2/20), "Manipulate Pipette" (0/20 vs 4/20 - sim slightly better), and "Sample Loading" (0/20 vs 2/20 - sim slightly better). This indicates varied performance depending on the task, possibly suggesting sensitivity to domain shifts for this model type.
 - Motus models generally exhibited higher inference times compared to π0 and π0.5, irrespective of the training data source, which might be attributable to their architectural complexity.

#### 4.3. Challenges in High-Level Tasks

A consistent finding across all baselines, regardless of training data source, was the low success rate on high-level tasks. "Item Assembly" consistently yielded 0/20 successes for all evaluated models. "Manipulate Pipette" and "Sample Loading" also showed very low success rates (typically 0-4/20). This suggests that these tasks, which require high precision, multi-stage sequencing, and dynamic adaptation, remain challenging for current generalist policies and represent significant areas for future research.

In summary, the baseline results demonstrate that the synthesized data provided within the RoboSynChallenge framework can produce policies that achieve comparable, and sometimes superior, real-world performance compared to training with limited real-world data. This validates the challenge's foundational approach to leveraging simulation for generalizable robotic control, while also highlighting the inherent difficulty of complex manipulation tasks for existing methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RoboSynChallenge introduces a standardized framework that addresses several critical gaps in the field of generalizable robotic manipulation, with potential impacts across research and application domains.

#### 5.1. Addressing the Sim2Real Gap and Data Scarcity

The challenge directly confronts the persistent Sim2Real gap and the scarcity of diverse real-world robotic data. By providing a scalable, generative data synthesis pipeline coupled with rigorous real-world validation, it offers a platform to systematically study and quantify how effectively simulated data can improve policy generalizability. This is significant because it aims to reduce the heavy reliance on costly and time-consuming real-world data collection, potentially accelerating research and development in data-efficient robot learning.

#### 5.2. Fostering Generalizable Robotic Dexterity

Unlike many existing benchmarks that operate within single modalities (Sim2Sim or Real2Real) or rely on fixed datasets, RoboSynChallenge focuses on benchmarking Sim2Real transferability for policies designed to generalize across varied tasks, environments, and difficulty levels. The inclusion of diverse tasks (entry-level to high-level), unseen object instances, and various environmental perturbations (background, lighting, distractors, spatial shifts) encourages the development of truly robust and adaptable manipulation systems. This focus on generalization beyond static, controlled conditions is a step towards robots that can operate in unstructured real-world settings.

#### 5.3. Enhancing Reproducibility and Collaboration

The provision of open-source tools, baseline models, data synthesis protocols, and a standardized evaluation framework is intended to foster reproducibility and fair comparison within the robotics community. This infrastructure lowers the barrier to entry for researchers, allowing them to build upon existing methods and benchmark new approaches against a common set of criteria. Such transparency and standardization can accelerate collaborative progress in the field.

#### 5.4. Advancing Embodied AI Research

By integrating large-scale synthetic data generation with real-world validation, RoboSynChallenge creates a unique testbed for embodied AI research. It encourages investigations into advanced learning algorithms and model architectures (e.g., VLA, WAMs, diffusion policies) that can effectively leverage multimodal data and translate learned skills from simulation to physical reality. The baseline results, demonstrating that simulation-trained policies can achieve comparable or superior real-world performance, provide empirical support for this paradigm.

#### 5.5. Broader Applications and Future Directions

The advancements fostered by RoboSynChallenge have potential implications for various application scenarios, including manufacturing, healthcare, and household robotics, where adaptable and dexterous manipulation is crucial. The insights gained from the competition can guide the development of future robot learning systems that are more autonomous, instruction-following, and capable of operating in dynamic, open-world environments. The identified challenges in high-level manipulation tasks also highlight areas requiring significant future research.
