# NoMaD: Goal Masked Diffusion Policies for Navigation and Exploration

> [!summary] Summary
> NoMaD, developed by researchers at UC Berkeley, introduces a unified diffusion policy for autonomous robots that performs both task-oriented navigation and task-agnostic exploration within a single model. This approach achieves a 98% success rate in exploration with 0.2 collisions per episode and matches specialized baselines for goal-conditioned navigation, all while being 15x more computationally efficient than prior methods.

## Problem

- Autonomous robots traditionally use separate, specialized systems for goal-directed navigation and task-agnostic exploration, leading to increased complexity and inefficiency.
- Existing policy representations often struggle to capture the multimodal nature of navigation decisions, resulting in averaged or suboptimal behaviors.
- High computational requirements of previous learning-based navigation methods limit their practical deployment on resource-constrained onboard robot hardware.

## Method

- **NoMaD** extends the ==Visual Navigation Transformer (ViNT)== architecture with a conditional diffusion model decoder to generate action sequences.
- It introduces a 'goal masking' mechanism within the Transformer's attention, using a binary mask to switch between attending to goal information for navigation and ignoring it for exploration.
- A conditional diffusion model, implemented as a 1D U-Net, iteratively denoises action sequences over 10 steps, enabling the generation of multimodal and temporally coherent action distributions.

## Results

- Achieved a **98%** success rate in undirected exploration tasks with only **0.2** collisions per episode, outperforming the previous best method, Subgoal Diffusion (**77%** success, **1.7** collisions).
- Matched the performance of specialized baselines for goal-conditioned navigation while maintaining a unified architecture.
- Reduced model size by **15x** compared to previous methods (19 million parameters vs. 335 million for Subgoal Diffusion), enabling deployment on resource-constrained onboard computers like the NVIDIA Jetson Orin.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single attention-based goal masking mechanism within a Transformer effectively unifies goal-conditioned navigation and task-agnostic exploration within one policy.
> - Conditional diffusion models are well-suited for robotic control by naturally representing and generating multimodal action distributions for navigation tasks.
> - Learning both navigation modes jointly does not compromise performance in either domain and leads to substantial computational efficiency gains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Autonomous robots operating in unknown environments face a fundamental challenge: they must seamlessly switch between exploring new areas to discover goals and navigating directly to specific targets once identified. Traditional approaches handle these behaviors with separate, specialized systems, creating complexity and inefficiency. NoMaD (Goal Masked Diffusion Policies) addresses this limitation by introducing a unified policy that can perform both task-oriented navigation and task-agnostic exploration within a single model.

Developed by researchers at UC Berkeley, NoMaD represents a significant advancement in learning-based robotics by demonstrating that a single diffusion policy can achieve state-of-the-art performance in both navigation modes while being 15x more computationally efficient than previous methods.

### 2. The Challenge of Unified Navigation and Exploration

Robotic navigation in unfamiliar environments requires two distinct yet complementary capabilities. Goal-directed navigation involves reaching a specific visual target when its location is known, requiring precise path planning and obstacle avoidance. Task-agnostic exploration, conversely, involves systematically searching novel environments to discover new regions or locate goals without prior knowledge of their positions.

Historically, these behaviors have been implemented as separate systems. A typical autonomous robot might use one model for exploration and another for goal-reaching, with complex handoff mechanisms between them. This separation creates several problems: increased system complexity, higher computational requirements, and potential inconsistencies in learned behaviors.

The multimodal nature of navigation decisions compounds this challenge. At any given moment, particularly at intersections or open spaces, multiple valid actions may exist. Traditional policy representations often struggle to capture these multimodal action distributions, leading to averaged or suboptimal behaviors that may result in collisions or inefficient paths.

### 3. NoMaD Architecture

NoMaD builds upon the Visual Navigation Transformer (ViNT) architecture, extending it with a diffusion model decoder to create a unified policy capable of both navigation modes. The system processes RGB observations from the robot's onboard camera along with optional goal images to generate appropriate action sequences.

The architecture consists of three main components working in concert. The visual encoding stage uses EfficientNet-B0 encoders to process individual observation images, converting them into feature representations. A goal fusion encoder then tokenizes these inputs, creating a structured representation that can be processed by the attention mechanism.

The Transformer backbone, containing 4 layers with 4 attention heads and 5 million parameters, processes these tokens to generate a context vector. This context vector encodes the robot's current state relative to its environment and, when available, the specified goal. The attention mechanism is where the goal masking innovation takes place, allowing the same architecture to operate in fundamentally different modes.

### 4. Goal Masking Mechanism

The goal masking mechanism represents the core innovation enabling NoMaD's unified behavior. A binary goal mask controls whether the policy attends to goal information during action generation. When the mask is set to 0 (unmasked), the Transformer can attend to goal tokens, enabling goal-conditioned navigation behavior. When set to 1 (masked), goal tokens are blocked from the attention computation, forcing the policy into exploration mode.

This elegant solution allows the same model parameters to learn both behaviors simultaneously. During training, the goal mask is randomly sampled with 50% probability, ensuring the policy learns both modes equally. At deployment time, the mask is set deterministically based on whether a goal image is available and the desired behavior.

The attention-based masking is more sophisticated than simple input dropout. By operating at the attention level, the mechanism preserves the model's ability to process visual observations while selectively ignoring goal information. This approach maintains the full representational capacity of the model while enabling clean behavioral switching.

### 5. Diffusion Models for Action Generation

Rather than directly predicting actions, NoMaD employs a conditional diffusion model to generate action sequences. This choice is motivated by the multimodal nature of navigation decisions, where multiple valid actions may exist at any given state.

The diffusion process begins with a sequence of actions sampled from Gaussian noise. A noise prediction network, implemented as a 1D conditional U-Net with 15 convolutional layers, iteratively denoises this sequence over 10 steps. The network is conditioned on the context vector from the Transformer, which may or may not include goal information depending on the mask setting.

This approach offers several advantages over traditional action prediction methods. Diffusion models naturally represent multimodal distributions, assigning appropriate probabilities to multiple valid actions while suppressing dangerous or suboptimal choices. The iterative refinement process allows the model to generate smooth, coherent action sequences that account for temporal dependencies.

The mathematical formulation involves predicting noise $\epsilon_\theta$ at each denoising step:

$$a_{k-1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_k}}(a_k - \frac{1-\alpha_k}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_k}}\epsilon_\theta(a_k, k, c_t))$$

where $\alpha_k$ and $\bar{\alpha}_k$ are noise schedule parameters, and $c_t$ is the context vector from the Transformer.

### 6. Training and Implementation

NoMaD is trained end-to-end on large-scale, heterogeneous real-world datasets comprising over 100 hours of robot trajectories. The training data comes from the GNM and SACSoN datasets, collected across diverse indoor and outdoor environments using multiple ground robots.

The training objective combines two components: a mean squared error loss for the diffusion model comparing predicted noise to actual noise, and an auxiliary loss for temporal distance prediction. The model is optimized using AdamW with a learning rate of $10^{-4}$, cosine scheduling, and warmup.

The training procedure alternates between goal-conditioned and exploration examples based on the randomly sampled goal mask. This balanced training ensures the model learns shared representations while maintaining the ability to specialize behavior based on the masking condition.

For integration with higher-level planning, NoMaD interfaces with a topological mapping system. This hybrid approach combines the reactive capabilities of the learned policy with classical planning for long-horizon navigation in large environments.

### 7. Experimental Results

NoMaD's evaluation across six distinct indoor and outdoor environments demonstrates significant improvements over existing methods. In undirected exploration tasks, NoMaD achieves a 98% success rate with only 0.2 collisions per episode, substantially outperforming the previous best method, Subgoal Diffusion, which achieved 77% success with 1.7 collisions.

For goal-conditioned navigation, NoMaD matches the performance of specialized baselines while maintaining the unified architecture. This demonstrates that learning both behaviors jointly does not compromise performance in either domain.

The computational efficiency gains are particularly striking. With 19 million parameters compared to 335 million for Subgoal Diffusion, NoMaD enables deployment on resource-constrained onboard computers like the NVIDIA Jetson Orin. This 15x reduction in model size makes practical real-world deployment feasible without sacrificing performance.

Ablation studies confirm the importance of architectural choices. The attention-based goal masking mechanism proves superior to alternatives like early/late fusion or dropout-based approaches. The choice of visual encoder also significantly impacts performance, with the EfficientNet-B0-based ViNT encoder outperforming both CNN and Vision Transformer alternatives.

### 8. Significance and Impact

NoMaD represents a significant advancement in autonomous robotics by demonstrating that unified policies can match or exceed the performance of specialized systems while offering substantial practical advantages. The work validates the potential of diffusion models for robotic control, marking the first successful deployment of goal-conditioned action diffusion on physical robots.

The computational efficiency improvements address a critical barrier to real-world deployment. By reducing model size by 15x while maintaining superior performance, NoMaD makes advanced navigation capabilities accessible to a broader range of robotic platforms. This efficiency gain is crucial for practical applications where robots must operate on limited onboard computational resources.

The unified architecture simplifies system design and deployment. Rather than maintaining separate models for exploration and navigation with complex coordination mechanisms, roboticists can deploy a single policy that seamlessly transitions between behaviors. This simplification reduces engineering complexity and potential failure modes while improving overall system reliability.

The work also advances the broader field of learning-based robotics by demonstrating effective techniques for multimodal action generation and behavioral conditioning. The attention-based goal masking mechanism provides a general framework for creating policies that can operate in multiple modes, with potential applications beyond navigation.

Future research directions enabled by NoMaD include extending goal specification beyond visual images to natural language or spatial coordinates, integrating more sophisticated exploration strategies based on semantic understanding, and scaling to even larger and more diverse training datasets. The success of the unified approach suggests that similar benefits might be achieved in other robotic domains where multiple related behaviors are traditionally handled by separate systems.

NoMaD's contribution lies in its elegant integration of modern deep learning techniques—Transformers and diffusion models—with a clever conditioning mechanism that enables behavioral flexibility without sacrificing performance or efficiency. This work represents a meaningful step toward more capable, practical, and deployable autonomous robots that can operate effectively in complex, real-world environments.
