# NoMaD: Goal Masked Diffusion Policies for Navigation and Exploration

> [!summary] Summary
> NoMaD, developed by researchers at UC Berkeley, introduces a unified diffusion policy for autonomous robots that performs both task-oriented navigation and task-agnostic exploration within a single model. This approach achieves a 98% success rate in exploration with 0.2 collisions per episode and matches specialized baselines for goal-conditioned navigation, all while being 15x more computationally efficient than prior methods.

## Problem

- Autonomous robots traditionally use separate, specialized systems for goal-directed navigation and task-agnostic exploration, leading to increased complexity and inefficiency.
- Existing policy representations often struggle to capture the multimodal nature of navigation decisions, resulting in averaged or suboptimal behaviors.
- High computational requirements of previous learning-based navigation methods limit their practical deployment on resource-constrained onboard robot hardware.

## Method

- **NoMaD** extends the ==Visual Navigation Transformer (ViNT)== architecture with a conditional diffusion model decoder to generate action sequences.
- It introduces a 'goal masking' mechanism within the Transformer's attention, using a binary mask to switch between attending to goal information for navigation and ignoring it for exploration.
- A conditional diffusion model, implemented as a 1D U-Net, iteratively denoises action sequences over 10 steps, enabling the generation of multimodal and temporally coherent action distributions.

## Results

- Achieved a **98%** success rate in undirected exploration tasks with only **0.2** collisions per episode, outperforming the previous best method, Subgoal Diffusion (**77%** success, **1.7** collisions).
- Matched the performance of specialized baselines for goal-conditioned navigation while maintaining a unified architecture.
- Reduced model size by **15x** compared to previous methods (19 million parameters vs. 335 million for Subgoal Diffusion), enabling deployment on resource-constrained onboard computers like the NVIDIA Jetson Orin.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single attention-based goal masking mechanism within a Transformer effectively unifies goal-conditioned navigation and task-agnostic exploration within one policy.
> - Conditional diffusion models are well-suited for robotic control by naturally representing and generating multimodal action distributions for navigation tasks.
> - Learning both navigation modes jointly does not compromise performance in either domain and leads to substantial computational efficiency gains.
