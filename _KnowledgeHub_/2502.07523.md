# Scaling Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning with Batch and Weight Normalization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from TU Darmstadt, University of Freiburg, and University of Oxford developed an enhanced off-policy reinforcement learning algorithm, CrossQ + WN, that integrates Batch Normalization with Weight Normalization. This approach enables reliable scaling to higher Update-to-Data ratios, achieving competitive sample efficiency on challenging DeepMind Control Suite and MyoSuite tasks without requiring complex algorithmic interventions.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) often suffers from high sample inefficiency, limiting its practical application, especially in robotics, due to extensive data requirements.
- Existing model-free RL methods aiming for high sample efficiency (e.g., using high Update-to-Data ratios) frequently introduce instability, computational overhead, or rely on complex heuristics like network resets or large ensembles.
- The CrossQ algorithm, while effective at low Update-to-Data (UTD) ratios, became brittle and unstable when scaled to higher UTDs on more complex continuous control benchmarks like DeepMind Control Suite and MyoSuite.

## Method

- The authors integrate ==Weight Normalization== (WN) into the CrossQ architecture, specifically applying it to the first two linear layers of the critic network to regulate weight norms.
- This method re-introduces target networks while maintaining CrossQ's key mechanism of a joined forward pass through both critic and target critic to compute shared batch statistics for Batch Normalization (BN).
- Weight decay is applied to unbounded parameters (e.g., the final output layer) to further manage norm growth and ensure training stability.

## Results

- CrossQ + WN reliably scales its performance with increasing Update-to-Data (UTD) ratios, demonstrating improved or stable performance on challenging tasks where vanilla CrossQ often failed or deteriorated.
- The method achieves competitive or superior sample efficiency compared to state-of-the-art baselines like BRO (UTD=10) and SIMBA (UTD=8) on DeepMind Control Suite and MyoSuite tasks, notably using significantly fewer network parameters (e.g., ~600k vs ~**5M** for BRO).
- CrossQ + WN reaches strong performance without requiring common complex interventions such as periodic network parameter resets or auxiliary exploration policies, highlighting its algorithmic simplicity and robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The instability of vanilla CrossQ at high UTDs stems from rapidly growing L2 norms of the critic network parameters, leading to a decreased effective learning rate and loss of network plasticity.
> - Weight Normalization effectively counteracts these growing weight norms, maintaining a more stable effective learning rate without altering the critic's output due to the scale-invariance provided by Batch Normalization.
> - Combining Batch Normalization and Weight Normalization creates a synergistic effect that stabilizes training dynamics at high UTDs, allowing for consistent sample efficiency improvements without complex interventions.
