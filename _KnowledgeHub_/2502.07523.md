# Scaling Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning with Batch and Weight Normalization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from TU Darmstadt, University of Freiburg, and University of Oxford developed an enhanced off-policy reinforcement learning algorithm, CrossQ + WN, that integrates Batch Normalization with Weight Normalization. This approach enables reliable scaling to higher Update-to-Data ratios, achieving competitive sample efficiency on challenging DeepMind Control Suite and MyoSuite tasks without requiring complex algorithmic interventions.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) often suffers from high sample inefficiency, limiting its practical application, especially in robotics, due to extensive data requirements.
- Existing model-free RL methods aiming for high sample efficiency (e.g., using high Update-to-Data ratios) frequently introduce instability, computational overhead, or rely on complex heuristics like network resets or large ensembles.
- The CrossQ algorithm, while effective at low Update-to-Data (UTD) ratios, became brittle and unstable when scaled to higher UTDs on more complex continuous control benchmarks like DeepMind Control Suite and MyoSuite.

## Method

- The authors integrate ==Weight Normalization== (WN) into the CrossQ architecture, specifically applying it to the first two linear layers of the critic network to regulate weight norms.
- This method re-introduces target networks while maintaining CrossQ's key mechanism of a joined forward pass through both critic and target critic to compute shared batch statistics for Batch Normalization (BN).
- Weight decay is applied to unbounded parameters (e.g., the final output layer) to further manage norm growth and ensure training stability.

## Results

- CrossQ + WN reliably scales its performance with increasing Update-to-Data (UTD) ratios, demonstrating improved or stable performance on challenging tasks where vanilla CrossQ often failed or deteriorated.
- The method achieves competitive or superior sample efficiency compared to state-of-the-art baselines like BRO (UTD=10) and SIMBA (UTD=8) on DeepMind Control Suite and MyoSuite tasks, notably using significantly fewer network parameters (e.g., ~600k vs ~**5M** for BRO).
- CrossQ + WN reaches strong performance without requiring common complex interventions such as periodic network parameter resets or auxiliary exploration policies, highlighting its algorithmic simplicity and robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The instability of vanilla CrossQ at high UTDs stems from rapidly growing L2 norms of the critic network parameters, leading to a decreased effective learning rate and loss of network plasticity.
> - Weight Normalization effectively counteracts these growing weight norms, maintaining a more stable effective learning rate without altering the critic's output due to the scale-invariance provided by Batch Normalization.
> - Combining Batch Normalization and Weight Normalization creates a synergistic effect that stabilizes training dynamics at high UTDs, allowing for consistent sample efficiency improvements without complex interventions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a fundamental and critical challenge in Reinforcement Learning (RL): **sample efficiency**. While RL has achieved remarkable breakthroughs, the need for vast amounts of interaction data makes real-world applications (especially in robotics) time-consuming, costly, and often impractical due to physical or safety constraints. This paper positions itself squarely within the model-free RL paradigm, which aims to maximize learning from available data without explicitly learning a model of the environment dynamics.

Within model-free RL, several strategies have been explored to improve sample efficiency:
- **Increasing the Update-to-Data (UTD) ratio:** This involves performing more gradient updates per collected environment sample. However, high UTD ratios can lead to challenges such as overfitting, known as "primacy bias" (Nikishin et al., 2022), often necessitating drastic interventions like periodic network resets (Nauman et al., 2024; D'Oro et al., 2022) or using ensembles of Q-functions (Chen et al., 2021). These solutions often introduce computational overhead or abrupt performance drops.
- **Architectural modifications:** This includes designing specific network structures or integrating normalization techniques. Layer Normalization (LN) has been widely adopted (Hiraoka et al., 2021; Lee et al., 2025; Lee et al., 2025), while Batch Normalization (BN) was historically thought to be problematic in RL due to distribution mismatch issues, until CrossQ (Bhatt et al., 2024) successfully demonstrated its efficacy by carefully handling these mismatches.

The paper highlights a "central tension" in these approaches: balancing sample efficiency with computational complexity (wall-clock time). Many existing solutions for high UTDs, such as model-based rollouts, auxiliary exploration policies, or larger models/ensembles, significantly increase computation time. CrossQ stood out by achieving state-of-the-art sample efficiency at a low UTD of 1, primarily through careful use of BN, without a significant impact on wall-clock time.

This work directly extends CrossQ by investigating its scaling behavior to *higher* UTD ratios. It fills a gap by demonstrating that while CrossQ excels at low UTDs, it becomes brittle on more complex tasks (DeepMind Control Suite, MyoSuite) when attempting to leverage higher UTDs for even greater sample efficiency. The proposed solution, integrating Weight Normalization (WN), addresses this brittleness without resorting to the complex or drastic interventions common in other high-UTD methods, such as full parameter resets (BRO, SR-SAC) or large ensembles (REDQ), thus maintaining simplicity and computational efficiency as core tenets. It contrasts directly with concurrent work like SIMBA (Lee et al., 2025), which uses LN, and BRO (Nauman et al., 2024), which relies on network resets and auxiliary policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is clearly articulated in the abstract and introduction: **to extend the sample efficiency benefits of CrossQ and Batch Normalization (BN) to the high Update-to-Data (UTD) training regime.**

The motivation for this objective stems from a critical observation and a subsequent hypothesis:
1. **Observation:** The original CrossQ algorithm, despite its success at low UTDs on MuJoCo, proves to be brittle and fails to scale reliably with higher UTD ratios on more complex and higher-dimensional tasks found in the DeepMind Control Suite (DMC) and MyoSuite benchmarks. This instability manifests in large confidence intervals and an inability to consistently learn performant policies.
2. **Underlying Problem Identification:** The authors identify that with increasing UTD ratios, vanilla CrossQ exhibits rapidly growing network parameter norms in its critic network. This phenomenon is known to lead to a "loss of plasticity" – where networks become resistant to updates and can prematurely converge (Elsayed et al., 2024). Furthermore, growing network weights in models with normalization layers can decrease the "effective learning rate" (ELR), hindering optimization (Van Hasselt et al., 2019; Lyle et al., 2024).
3. **Hypothesis:** The authors hypothesize that the training instabilities observed in CrossQ with higher UTDs are linked to these issues of growing weight norms and a declining effective learning rate. They propose that regularization techniques known to maintain plasticity and stable ELRs, particularly Weight Normalization (WN), could be beneficial for achieving more sample-efficient RL at higher UTDs.

Therefore, the key motivation is to find a simple yet robust mechanism to stabilize CrossQ's training dynamics at higher UTDs, thereby maximizing its sample efficiency potential without introducing algorithmic complexity like auxiliary policies, large ensembles, or disruptive interventions like periodic network resets. The goal is to create a more scalable and reliable model-free RL algorithm suitable for challenging real-world control tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this work revolves around enhancing the existing CrossQ framework with Weight Normalization (WN).

**Building upon CrossQ:**
The paper starts by acknowledging CrossQ's foundational contribution: the successful integration of Batch Normalization (BN) into actor-critic architectures in RL. CrossQ addressed the distribution mismatch problem inherent in using BN in Q-networks by evaluating both current-state and next-state Q-values within a single forward pass, forcing BN layers to compute shared statistics over both distributions. This approach eliminated the need for separate target Q-networks, which typically stabilize training but slow down learning.

**Problem with CrossQ at High UTDs:**
The authors empirically demonstrate that vanilla CrossQ's performance degrades and becomes unstable when scaling to higher UTD ratios on complex environments like DMC and MyoSuite. They trace this issue to a significant increase in the L2 norms of the critic network's dense layers, which in turn leads to a decrease in the effective learning rate (ELR) and an increase in "dead neurons," indicative of a loss of plasticity.

**Proposed Solution: Integration of Weight Normalization (WN):**
Inspired by prior work on normalization and effective learning rates (Van Laarhoven, 2017; Lyle et al., 2024), the authors propose integrating Weight Normalization (Salimans and Kingma, 2016) into the CrossQ architecture.
- **Theoretical Justification:** The paper leverages two key theorems by Van Laarhoven (2017):
 - **Theorem 1 (Scale Invariance):** Functions normalized with BN are scale-invariant with respect to their parameters (i.e., scaling weights and biases by `c` does not change the output). This is crucial because it means WN can regulate weight norms without affecting the critic's output.
 - **Theorem 2 (Inverse Proportional Gradients):** For such scale-invariant functions, the gradient scales inversely proportionally to the scaling factor of its parameters.
 These theorems imply that by keeping weight norms constant (via WN), the effective learning rate can be maintained stable, counteracting the observed phenomenon of decreasing ELR due to growing weight norms.
- **Implementation:** WN is applied to the first two linear layers of the critic network, effectively projecting their weights onto the unit ball after each gradient step (constraining their L2 norm). For remaining unbounded parameters (specifically, the final dense output layer), a small amount of weight decay is applied (AdamW with a decay of 1e-2, otherwise vanilla Adam for normalized layers).
- **Re-integration of Target Networks:** While original CrossQ removed target networks, this work found that for some environments (especially DMC), re-integrating them helped stabilize training. To ensure compatibility with BN, the authors maintain the critical CrossQ mechanism: a joined forward pass through both the critic and target critic networks, evaluating them in training mode to compute shared batch statistics. Polyak averaging is used for target network updates (τ = 0.005).

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Benchmarks:** DeepMind Control Suite (15 tasks) and MyoSuite (10 tasks), known for their complexity and high-dimensional state spaces.
- **Metrics:** Interquartile Mean (IQM) and 90% stratified bootstrap confidence intervals (Agarwal et al., 2021) over 10 random seeds.
- **Baselines:** BRO (Nauman et al., 2024), SIMBA (Lee et al., 2025), TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al., 2024), SR-SAC (D'Oro et al., 2022), and vanilla CrossQ (Bhatt et al., 2024).
- **Computational Resources:** Experiments were run on RTX 3090 and A5000 GPUs, leveraging `jax.vmap` for parallel execution of seeds.

The methodological approach is characterized by a theoretically grounded intervention (WN for stable ELR) applied to a proven architecture (CrossQ with BN) to address empirically observed training instabilities, with careful consideration of implementation details to maintain stability and simplicity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across 25 challenging continuous control tasks on the DeepMind Control Suite (DMC) and MyoSuite benchmarks yield several key findings:

1. **Vanilla CrossQ Fails to Scale Reliably:** Without Weight Normalization (WN), vanilla CrossQ struggles to benefit from higher Update-to-Data (UTD) ratios, particularly on complex tasks like DMC dog and humanoid environments. Its performance remains similar or deteriorates, exhibiting large confidence intervals, and it often fails to reliably converge with increased UTD (e.g., CrossQ UTD=5). This empirically validates the initial problem statement.

2. **Weight Normalization Stabilizes Training and Enables Scaling:** The integration of WN into the CrossQ framework (CrossQ + WN) effectively counteracts the observed rapid growth of critic network parameter norms, maintaining a more consistent effective learning rate (ELR). This leads to significantly improved training stability and reliability.

3. **Superior Sample Efficiency and Performance with Higher UTDs:**
 - CrossQ + WN reliably scales with increasing UTD ratios (Figure 4), demonstrating that as computational resources (UTD) are increased, performance either improves or remains stable.
 - At UTD=5, CrossQ + WN achieves competitive or superior performance compared to state-of-the-art baselines like BRO (which uses UTD=10 and requires parameter resets) and SIMBA (UTD=8), especially on the most complex dog and humanoid tasks (Figure 1).
 - CrossQ + WN UTD=5 uses significantly fewer network parameters (~600k) compared to BRO (~5M), indicating a more parameter-efficient solution.
 - The method also outperforms SR-SAC (UTD=32), which relies on network resets (Figure 6, Appendix).

4. **No Need for Drastic Interventions:** Crucially, CrossQ + WN achieves its strong performance and scalability *without* requiring drastic interventions such as periodic network parameter resets or additional exploration policies, which are common in other high-UTD methods (e.g., BRO, SR-SAC). An ablation study confirms that while resets offer a marginal improvement on very hard tasks, CrossQ + WN scales stably without them.

5. **Importance of WN and Target Networks:**
 - **Weight Normalization:** Ablation studies show that removing WN results in the most significant drop in performance across all ablations, particularly on MyoSuite tasks, often leading to a complete failure to learn. This strongly supports the hypothesis that WN is crucial for stabilizing training at higher UTDs.
 - **Target Networks:** While original CrossQ aimed to eliminate target networks, their re-integration in CrossQ + WN significantly improves performance, especially on DMC tasks. This suggests that while BN offers stability, for certain complex environments and higher UTDs, the additional stability provided by target networks remains beneficial.
 - **L2 Regularization vs. WN:** Soft L2 regularization, when ablated, consistently underperforms WN across tasks and regularization scales. This indicates that the hard constraint on weight norms provided by WN is more effective and easier to tune than a soft penalty, which would require task-specific scheduling.

In summary, the main findings confirm that by intelligently combining Batch Normalization with Weight Normalization, CrossQ can overcome its scaling limitations with higher UTDs, achieving robust and state-of-the-art sample efficiency on a diverse set of complex continuous control tasks, all while maintaining algorithmic simplicity and avoiding computationally expensive or disruptive training heuristics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers a significant advancement in the field of Reinforcement Learning, particularly for real-world applications where sample efficiency is paramount.

### 6. Enhanced Sample Efficiency and Scalability

The most direct impact is the provision of a model-free RL algorithm (CrossQ + WN) that reliably scales its sample efficiency with increasing computational budget (higher UTD ratios). This is critical for practical applications like robotics, where data collection is expensive and time-consuming. By achieving competitive or superior performance to existing state-of-the-art methods on complex benchmarks, the work pushes the frontier of what is possible with model-free RL in terms of learning speed.

### 7. Algorithmic Simplicity and Robustness

A key strength of the proposed approach is its simplicity. Unlike many high-UTD methods that rely on drastic interventions (e.g., periodic network resets, auxiliary exploration policies, large Q-function ensembles), CrossQ + WN achieves its performance gains through a theoretically grounded modification to network normalization. This makes the algorithm easier to implement, tune, and more robust across different environments, as demonstrated by its stable performance without task-specific parameter resets. The significantly lower parameter count compared to some baselines (e.g., BRO) also hints at greater computational efficiency per update step.

### 8. Deeper Understanding of Normalization in RL

The paper contributes to a more nuanced understanding of how different normalization techniques interact within deep RL architectures. It validates the critical insights of CrossQ regarding BN and extends them by demonstrating the synergistic power of combining BN with WN. This work highlights that carefully chosen architectural components, particularly those influencing stability and effective learning rates, can have profound impacts on learning dynamics and overall performance. This could inspire further research into the interplay of various normalization and regularization techniques in RL.

### 9. Bridging Theory and Practice for Robotics

Given the authors' strong background in robotics (especially Jan Peters' group), this work has immediate implications for real-world robotic control. The ability to learn complex policies like humanoid locomotion with high sample efficiency and robustness, without extensive hyperparameter tuning or retraining, is a significant step towards deploying RL agents in more dynamic and diverse physical environments. The previous success of CrossQ in real-world quadruped locomotion within minutes underscores this potential.

### 10. Pathway for Future Research

The work opens several avenues for future exploration. The finding that target networks, while theoretically made redundant by CrossQ's BN approach, can still offer additional stability in certain complex settings, warrants further investigation into their precise role and necessity. Additionally, extending CrossQ + WN to discrete action spaces, vision-based tasks, or combining it with other promising RL techniques (e.g., efficient exploration strategies) could lead to even broader applicability and performance gains.

In conclusion, "Scaling Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning with Batch and Weight Normalization" provides a robust, efficient, and conceptually elegant solution to a critical problem in RL. By stabilizing and scaling an already potent algorithm (CrossQ), it lowers the barriers to applying deep reinforcement learning in complex, data-hungry real-world scenarios, promising a tangible impact on fields like robotics and autonomous systems.
