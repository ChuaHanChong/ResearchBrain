# X-OP: Cross-Morphology Whole-Body Teleoperation via MPC Retargeting

> [!summary] Summary
> X-OP, a hierarchical teleoperation framework, enables cross-morphology whole-body robot control using a single XR device by employing an MPC-based motion retargeter. This system improves task success rates, reduces power consumption, and enhances collision avoidance on both humanoid and mobile manipulator platforms compared to direct mapping methods, while offering real-time customizability.

## Problem

- Existing whole-body teleoperation systems using single XR devices face challenges with full-body tracking, often require robot-specific retraining, and struggle with out-of-distribution commands.
- Many motion retargeting techniques prioritize geometric similarity over the dynamic feasibility of the robot, often resulting in unstable or physically unrealizable trajectories.
- Offline training of end-to-end reinforcement learning policies for teleoperation precludes real-time adjustments to robot behavior.

## Method

- A hierarchical teleoperation framework (**X-OP**) uses a single XR device to provide high-level commands to a robot's existing low-level controller.
- An online ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) retargeter continuously optimizes robot actions to align with operator intent while ensuring the dynamic feasibility and safety of the resulting motions.
- A state synchronization mechanism is employed to continuously align the simulator's internal state with real-world robot measurements, enhancing robustness.

## Results

- The X-OP framework achieved **100%** success rates in various loco-manipulation tasks for a humanoid robot in simulation, reducing completion times and power consumption compared to direct mapping baselines.
- For a mobile manipulator, the system successfully prevented collisions, achieving **100%** task success rates with zero collisions, significantly outperforming direct mapping which had high collision rates (**90%**).
- The MPC-based approach enabled real-time adjustment of teleoperation behavior, allowing customization of motion aggressiveness and stability, which enhanced task performance like non-prehensile transport.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling high-level motion retargeting from the robot's low-level controller enables "plug-and-play" generalization across different robot morphologies without specific retraining.
> - Incorporating dynamic feasibility via MPC during motion retargeting is crucial for generating precise, stable, and safe whole-body robot movements.
> - Real-time state synchronization between the physical robot and its simulator is vital for the robust performance of online MPC in complex, contact-rich tasks.
