# X-OP: Cross-Morphology Whole-Body Teleoperation via MPC Retargeting

> [!summary] Summary
> X-OP, a hierarchical teleoperation framework, enables cross-morphology whole-body robot control using a single XR device by employing an MPC-based motion retargeter. This system improves task success rates, reduces power consumption, and enhances collision avoidance on both humanoid and mobile manipulator platforms compared to direct mapping methods, while offering real-time customizability.

## Problem

- Existing whole-body teleoperation systems using single XR devices face challenges with full-body tracking, often require robot-specific retraining, and struggle with out-of-distribution commands.
- Many motion retargeting techniques prioritize geometric similarity over the dynamic feasibility of the robot, often resulting in unstable or physically unrealizable trajectories.
- Offline training of end-to-end reinforcement learning policies for teleoperation precludes real-time adjustments to robot behavior.

## Method

- A hierarchical teleoperation framework (**X-OP**) uses a single XR device to provide high-level commands to a robot's existing low-level controller.
- An online ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) retargeter continuously optimizes robot actions to align with operator intent while ensuring the dynamic feasibility and safety of the resulting motions.
- A state synchronization mechanism is employed to continuously align the simulator's internal state with real-world robot measurements, enhancing robustness.

## Results

- The X-OP framework achieved **100%** success rates in various loco-manipulation tasks for a humanoid robot in simulation, reducing completion times and power consumption compared to direct mapping baselines.
- For a mobile manipulator, the system successfully prevented collisions, achieving **100%** task success rates with zero collisions, significantly outperforming direct mapping which had high collision rates (**90%**).
- The MPC-based approach enabled real-time adjustment of teleoperation behavior, allowing customization of motion aggressiveness and stability, which enhanced task performance like non-prehensile transport.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling high-level motion retargeting from the robot's low-level controller enables "plug-and-play" generalization across different robot morphologies without specific retraining.
> - Incorporating dynamic feasibility via MPC during motion retargeting is crucial for generating precise, stable, and safe whole-body robot movements.
> - Real-time state synchronization between the physical robot and its simulator is vital for the robust performance of online MPC in complex, contact-rich tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Whole-body teleoperation is identified as a critical capability for scaling robot data collection in loco-manipulation tasks, which involve coordinated locomotion and manipulation. The broader research landscape for teleoperation has historically relied on two primary categories of systems: exoskeleton suits and multi-camera setups. Exoskeleton suits offer direct control but are characterized by their bulkiness, high cost, and the need for a large operator workspace, thereby limiting scalability for data collection. Multi-camera systems, such as OptiTrack or VICON, provide precise tracking but require intricate setup, careful calibration, and restrict operator movement to a defined field of view. These methods are noted for imposing considerable complexity, cost, and environmental constraints.

More recent advancements have explored the use of single Extended Reality (XR) devices, typically headsets equipped with cameras and inertial sensors, for spatial tracking. These devices offer improved portability and simplified setup, making them suitable for diverse environments. However, a single XR headset cannot directly track lower-body movements, posing a significant challenge for whole-body teleoperation. To compensate, these contemporary approaches often employ end-to-end reinforcement learning (RL) policies. These policies are trained to enable a robot to track end-effector poses from the XR device (e.g., headset, wrist poses) while coordinating lower-body movements for balance and locomotion.

Despite these advancements, existing single-XR-device methods face several limitations. They typically require robot-specific retraining of RL policies, which hinders generalization across different robot morphologies. Furthermore, these policies may exhibit failures when encountering out-of-distribution (OOD) commands, leading to imprecise and unstable control. A notable deficiency in many motion retargeting techniques used in conjunction with these methods is their focus on geometric similarity (joint angles, end-effector positions) without adequately considering the dynamic feasibility of the resulting motions for the robot. This can lead to physically unrealizable trajectories for the underlying RL policies. Additionally, the offline training paradigm of these RL approaches precludes real-time adjustments to teleoperation behavior.

This research addresses these identified gaps by proposing a hierarchical teleoperation framework. It aims to overcome the need for robot-specific retraining and enhance robustness to OOD commands by decoupling the motion retargeting process from the low-level control loop. By incorporating dynamic feasibility into the retargeting process and allowing real-time behavioral adjustments, this work seeks to provide a more generalizable, precise, and safe teleoperation solution compared to prior methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a hierarchical whole-body teleoperation framework that utilizes a single Extended Reality (XR) device and can generalize across diverse robot morphologies without requiring robot-specific retraining of low-level policies. This objective is motivated by the recognized need for scalable and efficient data collection systems for training advanced robot loco-manipulation capabilities, particularly for the development of foundation models.

Several key motivations drive this research:
- **Addressing limitations of existing teleoperation systems:** Current systems, whether based on exoskeleton suits or multi-camera setups, are noted for their high cost, complexity, and restrictive environmental requirements. While single XR devices offer a more portable and less intrusive alternative, they struggle with full whole-body control due to the inability to directly track foot motions and often lead to issues with dynamic feasibility and out-of-distribution failures when relying on end-to-end reinforcement learning.
- **Improving generalization and reducing retraining effort:** Traditional methods often necessitate retraining or significant adaptation for each new robot morphology, which is labor-intensive and time-consuming. The authors aim to create a "plug-and-play" framework that leverages existing, off-the-shelf low-level controllers, thereby eliminating the need for retraining specific policies for teleoperation across different robots.
- **Ensuring dynamic feasibility and safety:** A significant limitation of many existing motion retargeting methods is their focus on geometric matching without considering the robot's dynamics. This can result in unstable or physically impossible motions. A core motivation is to integrate dynamic feasibility directly into the retargeting process to produce commands that are both aligned with operator intent and safely executable by the robot.
- **Enhancing precision and stability:** Prior single-XR-device methods are observed to suffer from drift and instability, making precise control challenging. The authors are motivated to develop a system that maintains reliable positioning and prevents unintended movements, thereby improving the overall precision of teleoperation.
- **Enabling real-time customization:** End-to-end learning policies are typically trained offline, which restricts the ability to adjust teleoperation behavior in real time based on task requirements or operator preferences. This research aims to provide a flexible framework where parameters and constraints can be modified online to customize robot responses.
- **Leveraging advanced control techniques:** By employing Model Predictive Control (MPC), the framework seeks to optimize actions online, considering future dynamics and constraints, which offers a robust approach to managing complex robot behaviors in real-time.

In essence, the research seeks to overcome the trade-offs between ease of use, generalizability, dynamic safety, and precision in whole-body teleoperation, paving the way for more efficient and adaptable robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named X-OP, is a hierarchical whole-body teleoperation system driven by a single Extended Reality (XR) device. It is designed to generalize across diverse robot morphologies without requiring specific retraining of low-level policies. The core of the methodology lies in an MPC-based motion retargeter.

**A. Hierarchical Framework Overview:**
The framework operates as a high-level controller that maps operator motions, captured by an XR device, to the command space of a robot's existing low-level policy. This low-level policy, which can be either learning-based (e.g., RL policies like FALCON) or a traditional model-based controller (e.g., differential drive), then generates motor torques to execute the commands. This decoupling allows the framework to be "plug-and-play" with various controllers and robot platforms.

**B. MPC-based Retargeter (Problem Formulation):**
Teleoperation is framed as a goal-reaching problem where the operator provides continuous time-varying goals, denoted as G(t). Unlike direct mapping approaches, the method solves a Model Predictive Control (MPC) optimization problem online. The objective is to minimize a cost function over a prediction horizon H, balancing alignment with operator motion and the closed-loop system's dynamic feasibility and safety.

The optimization problem (Eq. 1) is defined as:
```
min_{a_{0},...,a_{H-1}} sum_{k=0}^{H-1} l(x_k, a_k, G(t)) + V_f(x_H, G(t))
s.t. τ_k = π(x_k, a_k, h_k), k = 0,..., H-1
 x_{k+1} = g(x_k, τ_k), k = 0,..., H-1
 a_k ∈ A, k = 0,..., H-1
 x_0 = x(t)
 h_0 = h(t)
```
Where:
- `a_k`: High-level action generated by MPC at time step k.
- `x_k`: Robot state (base position, orientation, joint positions, velocities).
- `G(t)`: Teleoperation goal specified by the operator (e.g., headset, wrist poses).
- `l(·)`: Stage cost function.
- `V_f(·)`: Terminal cost function.
- `π(·)`: Low-level policy that maps state, high-level action, and hidden parameter (`h_k`, e.g., state-action history for RL) to joint torques (`τ_k`).
- `g(·)`: Simulator dynamics, specifically MuJoCo, which computes the next state. MuJoCo is chosen for its high fidelity and efficient parallel simulation capabilities, facilitating zero-shot transfer to hardware.
- `A`: Admissible action set for high-level commands.
- `x_0`, `h_0`: Initial robot state and hidden parameters synchronized from the real world.

The optimization problem is solved using Kernel Interpolation Model Predictive Path Integral (KMPPI) control, a sampling-based MPC algorithm that incorporates kernel smoothing for temporally correlated action sequences.

**C. State Synchronization:**
Crucial for robust online execution, especially for legged robots sensitive to contact, is synchronizing the simulator's internal state (x and h) with real-world measurements at each MPC step. This addresses measurement noise and the sensitivity of rollout predictions to robot-ground contact.

The state estimation problem is formulated as a nonlinear least-squares optimization (Eq. 2) to determine accurate initial conditions for the MuJoCo simulation:
```
min_{p,θ,q} 1/2 ||p - p̃||^2_{W_p} + 1/2 ||θ - θ̃||^2_{W_θ} + 1/2 ||q - q̃||^2_{W_q}
 - sum_{i∈C} 1/2 ||h^pos_i(p,θ,q)||^2_{W^pos_c} + sum_{i∈C} 1/2 ||h^ori_i(p,θ,q)||^2_{W^ori_c}
s.t. q_min ≤ q ≤ q_max
```
Where:
- `p̃`, `θ̃`, `q̃`: Noisy measurements from SLAM and motor encoders.
- `W_p`, `W_θ`, `W_q`: Weight matrices reflecting measurement confidence.
- `C`: Set of active contact points.
- `h^pos_i(·)`, `h^ori_i(·)`: Contact position and orientation residuals, treated as soft constraints.
- `q_min`, `q_max`: Joint angle limits.

Specific formulations for humanoid (enforcing stance foot position and flat ground contact) and wheeled robot (enforcing wheel ground contact and horizontal axle) are provided. This optimization is solved using Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) with the DAQP solver via Mink.

**D. Design of Cost Function:**
The running cost `l(x_k, a_k, G(t))` comprises four components (Eq. 3), designed for generality across platforms:
1. **Goal Tracking Cost (`l_track`):** Penalizes deviations of the robot's base position and orientation from the desired references (`p_d`, `θ_d`) derived from the teleoperation goal `G(t)`.
2. **Contact Point Cost (`l_stance`):** Encourages stability by penalizing velocities of active contact points, primarily for legged robots during stance phases.
3. **Collision Avoidance Cost (`l_col`):** Utilizes exponential barrier functions to repel the robot from obstacles, with terms for proximity and velocity-dependent repulsion.
4. **Control Energy Cost (`l_ctrl`):** Penalizes the squared magnitude of high-level actions to promote energy-efficient motions.

A terminal cost `V_f(x_H, G(t))` is also included, which is a scaled version of the tracking cost applied at the end of the planning horizon to encourage convergence to the desired goal.

**E. Input Device and Signals:**
The Apple Vision Pro (AVP) serves as the XR input device, providing accurate hand/finger tracking and global headset pose estimation via Visual-Inertial SLAM. The framework uses headset pose (T_head), left wrist pose (T_lw), and right wrist pose (T_rw). The retargeter allows operators to select any subset of these targets to control specific body parts. A double-pinch gesture (ς) is used to toggle between stationary and mobile modes, compensating for the AVP's inability to directly detect foot motions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated the proposed X-OP framework through extensive experiments conducted in both simulation and real-world environments, utilizing two distinct robotic platforms: the Unitree G1 humanoid and the Rainbow RB-Y1 dual-arm mobile manipulator. The experiments focused on dual-point touch and box pick-and-place tasks, with an additional non-prehensile transport task for the mobile manipulator to demonstrate flexibility.

**A. Teleoperation on Humanoid (Unitree G1)**
- **Setup:** Used the FALCON RL policy as the low-level controller. The retargeter optimized 7-dimensional commands (waist height, waist angles, base linear velocity, base yaw rate) from the headset pose (T_head), while arm joints were controlled via inverse kinematics (IK) from wrist poses (T_lw, T_rw).
- **Baselines:** Direct mapping methods combined with either AMO or FALCON policies, and an ablation of the proposed method without state synchronization.
- **Results (Table II):**
 - **Direct mapping limitations:** These methods exhibited significant drift, making precise repositioning difficult and leading to prolonged completion times. They also amplified unintentional noise from the operator's head, causing unstable squatting. Jerky movements and imprecise velocity tracking resulted in lower pick-and-place success rates (e.g., 1/10 in simulation, 5/10 in real-world for FALCON) and increased motor power consumption, leading to overheating in real-world trials.
 - **Proposed Method (Ours):** Achieved consistent performance across all metrics. It demonstrated drift-free repositioning and precise tracking of desired height and waist angles. For dual-point touch in simulation, our method achieved 10/10 success rate with a completion time of 26.58 ± 6.60s and average balance power of 0.14 ± 0.06W, outperforming direct mapping with AMO (69.62 ± 38.36s, 0.17 ± 0.07W) and FALCON (38.30 ± 16.42s, 0.24 ± 0.26W). For box pick-and-place in simulation, it achieved 10/10 pick-up and 10/10 pick-and-place success rates, with a completion time of 30.75 ± 7.99s and power consumption of 0.17 ± 0.05W, significantly better than baselines. Real-world results showed similar trends, with our method achieving 10/10 dual-point touch and 9/10 pick-and-place success rates, with reduced completion times and power consumption.
 - **Impact of State Synchronization:** The ablation study ("Ours w/o state sync") showed degraded performance, particularly for precision tasks like dual-point touch (CT of 52.66 ± 31.44s vs 26.58 ± 6.60s with state sync), and increased power consumption. This indicated the critical role of accurate foot-ground contact estimation and precise future predictions provided by state synchronization for reliable operation.

**B. Teleoperation on Mobile Manipulator (Rainbow RB-Y1)**
- **Setup:** Used a differential drive controller for the low-level policy. Two variants of the retargeter were tested: Version 1 (V1) where T_head controlled 8-dim actions (velocity, yaw rate, torso joints) and arms via IK, and Version 2 (V2) where T_lw controlled 13 whole-body joints (left arm + torso), with optional upright constraint for non-prehensile transport.
- **Baseline:** Direct mapping with the differential drive controller.
- **Results (Table III):**
 - **Direct mapping limitations:** The baseline suffered from frequent collisions due to the operator's limited field of view from the AVP and lack of obstacle awareness. This was particularly evident in pick-and-place tasks in confined environments (9/10 collision rate in simulation). Its pick-and-place success rate was low (1/10 in simulation and real-world).
 - **Proposed Method (Ours):** The MPC-based retargeter's collision avoidance term effectively prevented obstacles. It achieved 10/10 success rates and 0/10 collision rates for dual-point touch and box pick-and-place in both simulation and real-world settings. In simulation, the collision avoidance behavior sometimes led to slightly longer completion times as operators adapted to repulsive forces near walls. Real-world failures were attributed to unstable grasps rather than navigation or collision issues.

**C. Flexibility and Customization (Non-prehensile Transport)**
- **Setup:** Mobile manipulator performing non-prehensile transport in the real world, using V2 of the retargeter.
- **Results (Table IV):**
 - **Control Energy Weight (`w_a`):** Adjusting `w_a` allowed tuning motion characteristics. A higher `w_a` (0.5) yielded smoother (lower jerk: 769.99 ± 11.02 m/s³), more conservative motion but with longer completion times (40.75 ± 1.06s) and increased collision risk due to slow response. A lower `w_a` (0.02) resulted in aggressive, responsive motion (higher jerk: 2144.55 ± 87.80 m/s³) with shorter completion times (24.50 ± 3.50s) but higher risk of dropping the toy.
 - **Alignment Targets and Constraints:** The ability to change alignment targets and cost functions online was demonstrated. Using 1-point alignment with an upright constraint on the end-effector significantly improved the delivery success rate (10/10 vs. 1/10 for 3-point alignment without constraint), alongside smaller tilt angles (0.03 ± 0.03 rad) and lower jerk, indicating more stable object transport.

Overall, the findings indicate that the X-OP framework provides a robust, precise, and safer teleoperation experience compared to direct mapping methods, with the added benefit of real-time customizability and cross-morphology generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The X-OP framework represents a notable advancement in whole-body robot teleoperation, offering a scalable, flexible, and morphology-agnostic solution. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

- **Enhanced Generalizability and Reduced Retraining Burden:** A core contribution is the framework's ability to generalize across diverse robot morphologies (demonstrated on a humanoid and a mobile manipulator) by leveraging existing low-level policies without requiring robot-specific retraining. This "plug-and-play" capability significantly reduces the engineering effort and cost associated with deploying teleoperation systems on new platforms, accelerating research and development in robotics.
- **Improved Precision and Safety through Dynamic Feasibility:** By integrating an MPC-based retargeter that explicitly optimizes for both operator intent alignment and the robot's dynamic feasibility, the framework addresses a critical limitation of previous geometric-focused retargeting methods. This leads to more precise control, prevents physically unrealizable motions, and enhances safety by mitigating risks such as falls in humanoids or collisions in mobile manipulators. The demonstrated reduction in power consumption and collision rates validates this benefit.
- **Robustness in Real-World Conditions:** The introduction of a state synchronization method is crucial for ensuring reliable online execution. By properly resetting the simulator state at each MPC step, the framework effectively handles noisy real-world sensor measurements and the sensitivity of robot-ground contact. This robustness is essential for practical deployment in dynamic and uncertain environments.
- **Real-time Behavioral Customization:** The MPC-based approach allows for online adjustment of cost function parameters and constraints, providing operators with the flexibility to fine-tune teleoperation behavior based on task requirements or personal preferences (e.g., motion aggressiveness, specific stability constraints). This adaptability can lead to more efficient task completion and a more intuitive user experience.
- **Facilitating Scalable Data Collection for Robot Learning:** As highlighted by the authors, efficient and scalable robot data collection is vital for training foundation models in loco-manipulation tasks. X-OP provides a tool that can reliably gather high-quality whole-body robot motion data across various platforms, thereby potentially accelerating progress in robot learning and the development of more capable autonomous systems.
- **Broader Applicability:** The framework's compatibility with arbitrary low-level controllers (both learning-based and model-based) and diverse robot platforms makes it widely applicable across research and industrial settings. This broadens the accessibility of advanced teleoperation capabilities to a wider range of robotic systems.

In summary, X-OP's emphasis on generalizability, dynamic safety, precision, and customizability positions it as a foundational step towards more practical and deployable whole-body teleoperation systems, with significant implications for both fundamental robotics research and real-world robotic applications. Future work on distilling the MPC policy for faster execution and integrating force sensing could further extend its capabilities.
