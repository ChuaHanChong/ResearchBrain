# Multi-Turn On-Policy Distillation with Prefix Replay

> [!summary] Summary
> Microsoft Research and University of Amsterdam developed ReOPD (Replayed-Prefix On-Policy Distillation), an efficient offline framework for training large language model agents in multi-turn interactive tasks. The method reduced training time per step by at least 4x and eliminated environment interactions, while matching or improving performance over online on-policy distillation, particularly for tasks with larger teacher-student capability gaps.

## Problem

- High computational cost of fully online multi-turn On-Policy Distillation (OPD) for LLM agents, requiring fresh environment rollouts and teacher queries for every training update.
- The "prefix trap," a two-sided distribution shift in multi-turn learning where replaying pre-collected teacher trajectories leads to a mismatch between student's likely interactions and teacher's reliable supervision.
- Lack of a scalable and reusable method for training a single student agent across multiple heterogeneous interactive environments efficiently.

## Method

- **ReOPD** leverages a fixed pool of offline teacher trajectories, replaying ==teacher-recorded prefixes== up to step 't' to avoid live environment interaction during student training.
- At step 't', the student's own generated action tokens receive dense, ==on-policy supervision== from the teacher's conditional probability, maintaining student relevance for the immediate action.
- A ==reliability-aware step-decaying schedule== (ω(t; κ) = κ^t) is applied, prioritizing distillation from earlier, lower-shift prefix steps to balance student occupancy relevance and teacher reliability.

## Results

- ReOPD achieved at least a **4x** speedup per training step and utilized **zero** tool calls during student training, significantly reducing costs compared to online OPD.
- For mathematical reasoning tasks, ReOPD consistently improved average accuracy over online OPD, achieving gains of **1.4 to 2.7 percentage points** across various teacher-student model scales.
- A single Qwen3-4B student trained with ReOPD jointly on both mathematical reasoning and search environments performed on par with single-environment online OPD in both domains, demonstrating scalability and reusability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The challenge in multi-turn distillation can be formally decomposed into a 'student interaction-occupancy mismatch' and a 'teacher reliability mismatch', requiring a balance between student-on-policy data and reliable teacher supervision.
> - An optimal interaction-prefix distribution is a 'geometric bridge' that mediates between the student's and teacher's interaction occupancies, with the balance shifting towards teacher reliability as trajectory depth increases.
> - A simple, empirically validated step-decaying schedule effectively approximates this reliability-aware prefix distribution by assigning higher weights to earlier turns, which inherently exhibit less distribution shift.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of large language model (LLM) agent development, specifically focusing on improving the efficiency and effectiveness of knowledge distillation techniques for models engaged in multi-turn interactive tasks. Recent advancements in LLMs demonstrate their ability to act as agents, interacting with environments and tools (e.g., code execution, search engines) over multiple steps. Reinforcement learning (RL), particularly on-policy methods like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Generalized Policy Optimization (GRPO), has been instrumental in training LLMs for complex reasoning and tool use. However, RL typically provides sparse reward signals, leading to sample inefficiency, especially for smaller models.

Knowledge distillation offers an alternative by enabling a smaller student model to imitate the dense output distribution of a larger, more capable teacher model. Traditional distillation is often off-policy, meaning the student learns from trajectories generated by the teacher. This can lead to "exposure bias" or "covariate shift," where the student encounters prefixes during inference that it never saw during training, causing compounding errors. On-policy distillation (OPD) addresses this by training the student on prefixes sampled from its own interactions, combining the relevance of on-policy data with the dense supervision of a teacher.

The current research extends OPD to multi-turn agentic tasks. While single-turn OPD has been explored, applying it to multi-turn scenarios online is computationally expensive. Each training update would require the student to perform new rollouts in the environment and query the teacher for supervision at every step of these interactions, incurring significant environment-interaction and teacher-inference costs. This paper proposes a method that seeks to retain the benefits of on-policy distillation for multi-turn agents while mitigating the high costs associated with online environment interaction. It addresses the challenges of distribution shift that arise when moving from fully online, student-driven interactions to an offline, replayed prefix setting.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and scalable alternative to fully online multi-turn On-Policy Distillation (OPD) for LLM agents operating in interactive environments. The motivation stems from several limitations of existing approaches:

1. **High Cost of Online Multi-Turn OPD:** In agentic tasks, LLMs interact with environments over multiple turns, interleaving actions and observations. If OPD is implemented fully online, every training update necessitates the student model to perform fresh rollouts through the environment. Concurrently, the teacher model must be queried at each visited history to provide dense, per-step supervision. This process generates substantial costs related to environment interaction (e.g., tool calls, API usage) and teacher inference, making online multi-turn OPD resource-intensive and slow.

2. **Addressing the "Prefix Trap":** When attempting to make distillation more efficient by reusing pre-collected teacher trajectories (an "off-environment" approach), a new challenge, termed the "prefix trap," emerges. This trap involves a two-sided distribution shift:
 - **Student Occupancy Shift:** To be relevant, the student should ideally be trained on histories it is likely to encounter itself (student-on-policy). However, if training uses pre-recorded teacher trajectories, these prefixes reflect the teacher's behavior, not necessarily the student's, especially if the student deviates from the teacher's path.
 - **Teacher Reliability Shift:** While making histories more student-on-policy improves relevance to the student, it can lead to querying the teacher on interaction histories where its target distribution might be unreliable. The teacher's optimal behavior might be defined for its own interaction patterns, and its conditional action probabilities might not constitute a trustworthy improvement signal when presented with novel histories generated by a student that has diverged significantly.
 This two-sided shift means that neither fully student-on-policy rollouts nor purely teacher-forced trajectories are uniformly optimal. The objective is to design a prefix distribution that balances these two competing factors.

3. **Enhancing Scalability and Reusability:** The researchers aim to transform expensive agent-environment interaction into a reusable offline resource. This would allow for scalable distillation across various tools, tasks, and environments. For instance, if a single student needs to learn from multiple heterogeneous environments, an online OPD approach would require deploying all environments simultaneously, further escalating operational complexity and cost. An offline approach, however, could collect teacher trajectories separately for each environment and merge them into a unified pool for student training.

In summary, the key objectives are to develop an OPD method that is:
- **Efficient:** Significantly reduces per-step training time and eliminates environment interactions during student training.
- **Effective:** Preserves or improves the accuracy benefits of online OPD by addressing distribution shifts appropriately.
- **Scalable:** Facilitates training a single student across multiple heterogeneous environments using a unified offline resource.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Replayed-Prefix On-Policy Distillation (ReOPD), addresses the challenges of multi-turn OPD by leveraging a fixed pool of offline teacher trajectories. The methodology comprises a theoretical framework for understanding distribution shifts and a practical algorithm designed to implement a reliability-aware prefix distribution.

**Problem Formulation and Analysis:**
The paper begins by formulating multi-turn OPD within an interactive environment.
- **Ideal Interactive Improvement Objective:** An ideal objective, R⋆, evaluates an updated student policy (πθ) on histories (H_t) that the current student (πθ_old) would actually encounter under environment interaction. This objective uses an ideal improvement target (q⋆_t) for each step and history, reflecting student relevance.
- **Practical Objective (Offline Reuse):** In practice, online interaction for R⋆ is prohibitively costly. ReOPD replaces online student rollouts with off-environment training from a pre-collected pool of teacher trajectories. This means the collected histories (P_t) approximate the teacher's interaction occupancy (d_t^T), rather than the student's (d_t^θ_old). The challenge is to approximate R⋆ using only samples from d_t^T.
- **Two-Sided Decomposition of Mismatch:** The paper formally quantifies the difference between the ideal objective R⋆ and a practical replayed objective Lρ. Proposition 1 demonstrates that this gap can be bounded by two terms:
 1. **Student Interaction-Occupancy Mismatch:** Measured by the Total Variation (TV) distance between the student's actual occupancy (d_t^θ_old) and the effective training history distribution (ρ_t). This term is minimized when ρ_t closely matches d_t^θ_old.
 2. **Teacher Reliability Mismatch:** Measured by the expected teacher reliability error (ϵθ_T,t), which is the difference between the ideal target loss and the teacher's distribution loss, averaged over ρ_t. This term is minimized when the teacher is queried on histories where its output is a reliable proxy for the ideal target.
 This decomposition highlights the "prefix trap": fully student-on-policy distillation (zeroing the occupancy term) can lead to querying the teacher on unreliable histories, while strictly teacher-forced histories (zeroing the reliability term in theory) might not be relevant to the student's actual behavior.
- **Reliability-Aware Interaction-Prefix Distribution:** The analysis motivates selecting an effective history distribution (ρ⋆_t) that balances these two mismatches. This is framed as minimizing a combination of KL divergences to the student's occupancy and the teacher's occupancy. The solution is a "geometric bridge" distribution, ρ⋆_t ∝ (d_t^θ_old)^γ_t * (d_t^T)^(1-γ_t), where γ_t controls the balance between student relevance (γ_t=1) and teacher support (γ_t=0). The paper predicts that γ_t should decrease with trajectory depth as teacher reliability on student-induced histories diminishes.

**ReOPD Algorithm:**
ReOPD implements this principle using two main ingredients:

1. **Teacher-Forced Prefix, Student Action, Teacher Supervision:**
 - For any supervised step `t`, the interaction history (O1, A1..., Ot) is replayed exactly from a pre-collected teacher trajectory. This means all prior actions (A<t) and observations (O≤t) are the teacher's recorded ones. No environment interaction is performed during training.
 - At step `t`, the student policy generates its own action (A_t) autoregressively.
 - The teacher's recorded conditional probability (π_T(· | x, h_t, a_<j_t)) provides the distillation target for each token within the student's generated action. The token contexts (a_<j_t) for this supervision are on-policy (generated by the student), maintaining student relevance for the immediate action.
 This combination allows for on-policy supervision at the evaluated step while completely avoiding costly online environment rollouts by anchoring to teacher-generated prefixes.

2. **Reliability-Aware Step Schedule:**
 - The "geometric bridge" analysis suggests that the importance weight for a prefix (proportional to (d_t^θ_old / d_t^T)^γ_t) can be approximated by a product of student-to-teacher likelihood ratios over the prefix (br_t). Empirically, log(br_t) correlates negatively with the step index, indicating that this ratio typically decreases with depth.
 - ReOPD replaces the exact, high-variance likelihood ratio with a simplified, stable **step-decaying schedule**: ω(t; κ) = κ^t, where κ ∈ (0, 1] is a single steepness parameter.
 - This schedule assigns higher weight to earlier, lower-shift steps, and lower weight to later, higher-shift steps, where the teacher-forced prefix is a less reliable surrogate for student-generated histories.
 - The parameter κ can be interpreted as inversely related to the student-to-teacher gap and the bridge exponent γ_t. A larger gap or a stronger preference for teacher reliability would lead to a smaller κ (steeper decay).
 - **Implementation:** In experiments, this schedule is applied by sampling positions for training in proportion to ω(t; κ). This means early steps are sampled more frequently for distillation. The per-step loss is the token-level KL divergence between the student's and teacher's distributions accumulated over the student-sampled action tokens.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Models:** Qwen3-family models (e.g., Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507) are used for both teacher and student, allowing for variation in teacher-student capability gaps.
- **Training Stages:** Models undergo a "cold start" (SFT on teacher trajectories) to learn basic tool use and formatting. Teacher models are then trained with GRPO (on-policy RL) to achieve higher performance. The student models are initialized from the cold start version and then distilled. The teacher's RL rollouts are directly reused as the offline prefix pool for ReOPD, incurring no additional data collection cost.
- **Environments:** Two agentic tasks are used:
 - **Mathematical reasoning:** Requires Python tool use, evaluated on six competition benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, AMC23, Minerva Math, OlympiadBench, MATH500).
 - **Search:** Requires retrieval tool use, evaluated on seven QA benchmarks (NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA, HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, MuSiQue, Bamboogle).
- **Baselines:** Untrained (Base), Cold Start, SFT (off-policy distillation on teacher trajectories), OPD (fully online student-on-policy distillation), and GRPO (teacher's own RL result for reference).
- **Multi-Environment Setting:** A single student is trained jointly on data from both math and search environments, demonstrating ReOPD's scalability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that ReOPD effectively balances efficiency and performance in multi-turn on-policy distillation, aligning with the theoretical predictions regarding distribution shifts.

**Single Environment Distillation:**
- **Mathematical Reasoning (Teacher-Reliability Dominance):** For mathematical reasoning tasks, ReOPD consistently improved student accuracy over online OPD across various teacher scales.
 - With a Qwen3-4B teacher and Qwen3-4B student, ReOPD improved the average accuracy from 55.1% (OPD) to 57.2%.
 - With a Qwen3-8B teacher and Qwen3-4B student, accuracy rose from 51.0% (OPD) to 53.7%.
 - With a Qwen3-30B-A3B teacher and Qwen3-4B student, accuracy increased from 51.1% (OPD) to 52.5%.
 - For a stronger Qwen3-8B student under a Qwen3-30B-A3B teacher, ReOPD showed a slight improvement from 56.5% (OPD) to 56.8%.
 These gains were more pronounced when the teacher-student capability gap was wider, supporting the hypothesis that ReOPD's teacher-anchored replay addresses situations where the teacher's target might be less reliable on student-generated histories.
- **Search (Student-Occupancy Dominance):** For search tasks, ReOPD demonstrated performance comparable to online OPD.
 - With a Qwen3-4B teacher, OPD achieved 40.6% average accuracy, while ReOPD achieved 40.5%.
 - With a Qwen3-8B teacher, OPD obtained 39.1%, and ReOPD obtained 39.0%.
 This outcome is consistent with the predicted "student-occupancy shift" regime, where the teacher likely remains reliable even on student-induced histories, meaning the benefits of teacher-anchored replay are less pronounced as the occupancies of student and teacher are already similar.

**Multiple Environments Distillation:**
- A single Qwen3-4B student trained jointly on both math and search environments achieved performance on par with single-environment online OPD in both domains. This indicates that ReOPD's replay-based training pipeline can effectively combine heterogeneous environments. For math, the average accuracy for OPD was 55.2%, and ReOPD was 55.3%. For search, both OPD and ReOPD achieved an average accuracy of 41.0%.

**Efficiency:**
- ReOPD significantly reduced training time per step and eliminated tool calls during student training.
- It was observed to be at least **4 times faster** per training step than online OPD.
- ReOPD utilized **zero tool calls** during student training by replaying teacher-recorded prefixes. This contrasts with OPD, which requires fresh environment rollouts and tool calls for every student update.

**Analysis and Discussion:**
- **Validation of Step-Level Schedule:** Experiments directly supported the effectiveness of the step-decay schedule. Favoring early, low-shift steps (either by sampling chunks or varying the decay steepness κ) consistently improved distillation performance. This corroborates that the gain stems from reliability-aware prefix selection rather than merely increased data.
- **Two Regimes in Practice:** The results from math and search tasks confirmed the existence of two distinct regimes. In the math tasks with larger teacher-student gaps, the "teacher-reliability shift" dominated, leading to ReOPD's performance gains. In search tasks, where the teacher remained reliable on student-induced histories, the "student-occupancy shift" was less critical, and ReOPD matched OPD. These observations align with the theoretical decomposition.
- **Prefix Source: Reliability, Not Raw Capability:** An experiment showed that prefixes drawn from the teacher's own model yielded the best student performance. Substituting a larger or stronger model as the prefix generator degraded the distilled student. This suggests that the reliability of the teacher's conditional action distribution on its *own* interaction histories is more crucial than the standalone capability of the prefix generator, providing direct empirical support for the "teacher reliability shift" component of the prefix trap.
- **RL-Collected Pool vs. Stationary Teacher Pool:** Comparing performance using the teacher's mixed-policy RL rollouts (free by-product) versus a stationary pool from the final teacher showed nearly identical student performance. This indicates that the conveniently available RL rollouts are an effective and cost-efficient source of prefixes for ReOPD.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Replayed-Prefix On-Policy Distillation (ReOPD), offering a significant contribution to the development and scalability of LLM agents for multi-turn interactive tasks. Its significance and potential impact include:

- **Cost Reduction and Scalability for Agentic LLMs:** ReOPD transforms the computationally expensive process of multi-turn online on-policy distillation into an efficient, off-environment alternative. By leveraging pre-collected teacher trajectories and eliminating live environment interactions and tool calls during student training, ReOPD dramatically reduces training costs and time. This enables the training of more capable student agents for complex tasks without the prohibitive resource requirements of traditional on-policy methods, thereby broadening access to advanced agentic LLM capabilities.
- **Addressing Distribution Shift in Multi-Turn Learning:** The paper's theoretical framework, which identifies and decomposes the "prefix trap" into student occupancy and teacher reliability shifts, provides a nuanced understanding of distribution shift in multi-turn interactive learning. This conceptualization offers a basis for designing more robust distillation and reinforcement learning algorithms. ReOPD's solution, a reliability-aware prefix distribution implemented via a step-decaying schedule, demonstrates a practical way to navigate this two-sided shift.
- **Unified Training for Heterogeneous Environments:** The ability of ReOPD to train a single student agent jointly across multiple, heterogeneous environments (e.g., mathematical reasoning and search) from a merged offline data pool represents an operational advantage. It streamlines the training pipeline for general-purpose agents without requiring simultaneous deployment and online interaction with diverse tools and environments, facilitating the creation of more versatile LLM agents.
- **Efficient Knowledge Transfer:** ReOPD achieves or improves upon the accuracy levels of online OPD, particularly in regimes where teacher reliability is a key constraint (e.g., large teacher-student capability gaps). This indicates that the method effectively transfers dense knowledge from a strong teacher to a student model, maintaining the benefits of on-policy supervision (student relevance for actions) while bypassing the associated high costs.
- **Reusability of RL Training Data:** The finding that the teacher's RL-collected rollouts can be directly reused for ReOPD without dedicated data collection highlights a synergistic relationship between RL and distillation. This makes the offline prefix pool a "free by-product" of teacher training, further enhancing the efficiency and practical applicability of the method.

While the analysis assumes access to pre-collected teacher trajectories and the reliability surrogate is a first-order approximation, ReOPD establishes a foundation for more scalable and principled approaches to training LLM agents in complex, interactive settings. Future work may explore more dynamic or learned reliability estimates and adaptive schedules.
