# ProRL: Effective Reinforcement Learning for Proactive Recommendation via Rectified Policy Gradient Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> ProRL, a reinforcement learning framework from Fudan University, rectifies policy gradient estimation deficiencies to discover item sequences that effectively guide user preferences towards a target item while maintaining high user engagement. It achieved superior performance in guidance effectiveness and path feasibility across various datasets, demonstrating generalizable guidance strategies. ProRL's analytical rectification of gradient issues allows stable and efficient learning, overcoming limitations of existing proactive recommendation methods.

## Problem

- Recommender systems traditionally focus on reflecting existing user preferences, lacking mechanisms to proactively guide users towards new content or specific target items.
- Current proactive recommendation approaches (heuristics, LLMs, supervised learning) suffer from limitations such as suboptimal global paths, high computational costs, or inability to explore beyond historical data.
- Standard policy gradient methods fail in proactive recommendation due to a "length shortcut" (biasing towards overlong paths) and "high gradient variance," leading to ineffective and unstable learning.

## Method

- **ProRL** formalizes proactive recommendation as a ==reinforcement learning== task, optimizing a composite reward function that balances short-term user acceptance and long-term guidance effectiveness.
- It introduces Stepwise Reward Centering (SRC) to mitigate the "length shortcut" by normalizing step-level rewards, ensuring the policy optimizes for path quality rather than indiscriminate path extension.
- Position-Specific Advantage Estimation (PSAE) is proposed to reduce high gradient variance by weighting each step's gradient with future rewards and utilizing a position-specific baseline, facilitating stable learning without requiring an auxiliary critic model.

## Results

- ProRL consistently outperformed state-of-the-art baselines on MovieLens-1M, Steam, and Amazon-Book datasets, achieving superior guidance effectiveness (e.g., 728.18 IoR on MovieLens-1M) and high path feasibility (e.g., 0.8543 CTR on MovieLens-1M).
- The framework demonstrated generalizability by maintaining statistically significant performance improvements in guidance metrics even when evaluated with unseen user simulators, suggesting robust learning of guidance principles.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of both SRC and PSAE, with their removal leading to severe performance drops or unstable training, and ProRL achieved the lowest gradient variance (approximately **5%** of REINFORCE's variance) during training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Standard policy gradient estimation in proactive recommendation is prone to a "length shortcut" (leading to overlong, low-quality paths) and "high gradient variance," preventing effective policy learning.
> - Rectifying these specific policy gradient deficiencies through SRC and PSAE is critical for developing stable and effective reinforcement learning policies that can discover high-quality guidance paths.
> - Reinforcement learning, when properly trained, primarily acts as a "probabilistic rectifier," identifying and amplifying high-quality paths that exist in the low-probability tail of a pre-trained model's latent distribution.
