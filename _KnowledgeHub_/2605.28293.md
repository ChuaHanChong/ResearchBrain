# ProRL: Effective Reinforcement Learning for Proactive Recommendation via Rectified Policy Gradient Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> ProRL, a reinforcement learning framework from Fudan University, rectifies policy gradient estimation deficiencies to discover item sequences that effectively guide user preferences towards a target item while maintaining high user engagement. It achieved superior performance in guidance effectiveness and path feasibility across various datasets, demonstrating generalizable guidance strategies. ProRL's analytical rectification of gradient issues allows stable and efficient learning, overcoming limitations of existing proactive recommendation methods.

## Problem

- Recommender systems traditionally focus on reflecting existing user preferences, lacking mechanisms to proactively guide users towards new content or specific target items.
- Current proactive recommendation approaches (heuristics, LLMs, supervised learning) suffer from limitations such as suboptimal global paths, high computational costs, or inability to explore beyond historical data.
- Standard policy gradient methods fail in proactive recommendation due to a "length shortcut" (biasing towards overlong paths) and "high gradient variance," leading to ineffective and unstable learning.

## Method

- **ProRL** formalizes proactive recommendation as a ==reinforcement learning== task, optimizing a composite reward function that balances short-term user acceptance and long-term guidance effectiveness.
- It introduces Stepwise Reward Centering (SRC) to mitigate the "length shortcut" by normalizing step-level rewards, ensuring the policy optimizes for path quality rather than indiscriminate path extension.
- Position-Specific Advantage Estimation (PSAE) is proposed to reduce high gradient variance by weighting each step's gradient with future rewards and utilizing a position-specific baseline, facilitating stable learning without requiring an auxiliary critic model.

## Results

- ProRL consistently outperformed state-of-the-art baselines on MovieLens-1M, Steam, and Amazon-Book datasets, achieving superior guidance effectiveness (e.g., 728.18 IoR on MovieLens-1M) and high path feasibility (e.g., 0.8543 CTR on MovieLens-1M).
- The framework demonstrated generalizability by maintaining statistically significant performance improvements in guidance metrics even when evaluated with unseen user simulators, suggesting robust learning of guidance principles.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of both SRC and PSAE, with their removal leading to severe performance drops or unstable training, and ProRL achieved the lowest gradient variance (approximately **5%** of REINFORCE's variance) during training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Standard policy gradient estimation in proactive recommendation is prone to a "length shortcut" (leading to overlong, low-quality paths) and "high gradient variance," preventing effective policy learning.
> - Rectifying these specific policy gradient deficiencies through SRC and PSAE is critical for developing stable and effective reinforcement learning policies that can discover high-quality guidance paths.
> - Reinforcement learning, when properly trained, primarily acts as a "probabilistic rectifier," identifying and amplifying high-quality paths that exist in the low-probability tail of a pre-trained model's latent distribution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recommender systems traditionally focus on reflecting existing user preferences by suggesting items similar to past interactions. However, platform objectives often extend beyond this, aiming to introduce users to new content, products, or genres—a task referred to as proactive recommendation. Proactive Recommender Systems (PRSs) endeavor to gradually shift user preferences towards a specific target item through a carefully constructed sequence of intermediate recommendations, maintaining user engagement throughout the process.

Existing approaches to proactive recommendation exhibit various limitations. Heuristic methods rely on predefined rules and greedy local searches, which often result in suboptimal global paths. Large Language Model (LLM)-based methods offer sophisticated path planning capabilities but incur significant computational and deployment costs, limiting their practical applicability. Supervised learning methods, typically utilizing sequence-to-sequence models trained on historical interaction sequences, are lightweight for deployment but are inherently limited to imitating past data. This reliance on historical patterns restricts their ability to discover novel, high-quality guidance paths that deviate from the training distribution.

This research addresses these limitations by leveraging Reinforcement Learning (RL) within a lightweight transformer framework. RL provides a principled approach to sequential decision-making, allowing optimization of cumulative rewards that balance both short-term acceptance and long-term guidance effectiveness. However, the authors identify specific deficiencies when applying standard policy gradient RL to PRSs, particularly related to gradient estimation. This work positions itself by rectifying these identified policy gradient deficiencies to enable effective, exploration-driven discovery of proactive recommendation paths, moving beyond the imitation paradigm of supervised methods while maintaining computational efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective reinforcement learning framework for proactive recommendation that can construct paths of intermediate items to guide user preference shifts toward a target item. This framework aims to overcome the limitations of existing methods, particularly the inability of supervised methods to explore beyond historical data and the inefficiency of heuristic or LLM-based approaches.

The motivation for this work stems from two core challenges in proactive recommendation:
1. **Dual Objectives of Path Quality**: A proactive recommendation path must simultaneously satisfy two critical objectives:
 - **Path Feasibility**: Each intermediate item in the path must have a high probability of user acceptance to maintain engagement. If users reject intermediate recommendations, the guidance path is disrupted.
 - **Guidance Effectiveness**: The complete path must significantly increase the user's eventual interest in and acceptance probability of the target item. This represents the long-term goal of the proactive system.
 These objectives are often in tension; locally optimal choices for feasibility may not lead to globally effective guidance. RL is naturally suited for optimizing such joint, sequential objectives by maximizing a composite reward function.

2. **Deficiencies in Standard Policy Gradient Estimation for PRSs**: Initial empirical studies by the authors revealed that a naive application of policy gradient RL to PRSs results in systematic failures, specifically the generation of "nearly identical overlong paths" that lack diversity and effectiveness. The authors trace this failure to two specific deficiencies in standard policy gradient estimation:
 - **Length Shortcut**: The path-level rewards in PRS naturally decompose into step-level rewards, which are observed to have a positive mean at each step. This structural property implies that longer paths, on average, yield higher cumulative rewards. In standard policy gradient estimation, variations in sampled path lengths cause the length dimension to dominate the gradient signal. This biases the model towards indiscriminately extending paths to their maximum allowed length, rather than exploring paths of varying lengths based on actual quality. The paper mathematically formalizes this with "Theorem 2.1 (Length Collapse Rate)", showing that policy gradients systematically reduce stopping probability when step-level rewards have a positive mean, leading to path lengths converging to the maximum.
 - **High Gradient Variance**: Standard policy gradient estimation weights each step's gradient contribution by the entire path-level reward. This uniform weighting ignores the temporal dependency where an action at step *t* only influences rewards from step *t* onward. Including rewards from prior steps (r_1..., r_{t-1}) introduces irrelevant noise, leading to high variance in the gradient estimates. High variance hinders stable and efficient learning, making it difficult for the policy to discern the true impact of individual actions on path quality.

The key motivation is, therefore, to rectify these two policy gradient deficiencies to enable a stable and effective RL training process for proactive recommendation, allowing the system to discover high-quality, user-specific guidance paths that optimize both feasibility and effectiveness without collapsing into pathological behaviors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ProRL framework proposes two novel mechanisms to rectify the identified policy gradient deficiencies: Stepwise Reward Centering (SRC) and Position-Specific Advantage Estimation (PSAE). The overall approach integrates these mechanisms within a reinforcement learning framework, built upon a pretrained sequence-to-sequence model and a user simulator for reward computation.

#### 3.1. Basic RL Framework

The proactive recommendation task is formalized as learning a policy $\pi_\theta(\cdot | S_u, i_T)$ that generates a high-reward path $L_u = (i_1, \ldots, i_L)$, given a user's interaction history $S_u$ and a target item $i_T$.
- **User Simulator**: A pre-trained recommender model (e.g., SASRec) acts as a user simulator to estimate the probability $P(i | S)$ of a user accepting item $i$ given their current preference sequence $S$. This allows for reward computation without online feedback.
- **Reward Function**: Path quality is quantified using a weighted sum of three metrics:
 - **Increment of Interest (IoI)**: Measures the increase in $\log P(i_T | S_u \oplus L_u)$ compared to $\log P(i_T | S_u)$.
 - **Increment of Rank (IoR)**: Measures the improvement in the ranking position of $i_T$ after appending $L_u$.
 - **Click-Through Rate (CTR)**: Measures the average acceptance probability of intermediate items in $L_u$.
 The total path reward is $R_{path} = \alpha \cdot \text{IoI} + \beta \cdot \text{IoR} + \gamma \cdot \text{CTR}$.
- **Policy Optimization**: The policy $\pi_\theta$ is initialized with a supervised pre-trained model $\pi_0$ and updated via policy gradient ascent on the objective $J(\theta) = E_{L_u \sim \pi_\theta}[R_{path}] - \lambda \cdot D_{KL}(\pi_\theta || \pi_0)$. The KL term serves as a regularization to prevent large policy shifts.

#### 3.2. Semantic ID Implementation and Pretraining

- **Semantic IDs**: To handle a large item space and enable semantic generalization, each item is represented as a sequence of discrete semantic tokens (e.g., 4 tokens). A Residual Quantized VAE (RQ-VAE) is trained on item profiles (generated by LLMs like GPT-4) to map dense item embeddings into these compact token sequences. The policy then autoregressively generates these tokens.
- **Smooth-Guided Data Construction**: To train $\pi_0$, historical interaction logs are refined into "Smooth-Guided Paths" using a "Feasibility Oracle." This oracle (either knowledge graph-based or LLM-based) ensures that each step in a historical sequence represents a semantically coherent and feasible transition. This distillation process provides high-quality expert demonstrations for supervised pretraining, focusing on goal-oriented planning rather than passive user drift.
- **Pretraining**: A lightweight encoder-decoder Transformer (T5-based) is pre-trained on these semantic ID sequences, effectively learning a strong semantic prior and initial path planning capabilities.

#### 3.3. Rectified Policy Gradient Estimation (ProRL): Stepwise Reward Centering (SRC)

- **Problem Addressed**: The "length shortcut" deficiency, where positive mean step-level rewards bias the policy towards generating overlong paths.
- **Mechanism**: SRC modifies the step-level reward $r_t$ by subtracting a global expected step reward $\bar{r}$.
 $\tilde{r}_t = r_t - \bar{r}$, where $\bar{r} = E_{\pi}[r_*]$ (global expected step reward).
- **Effect**: By construction, $E_{\pi}[\tilde{r}_t] = 0$. This ensures that the expected cumulative reward $E[\sum_{t=1}^L \tilde{r}_t]$ becomes independent of path length $L$. Consequently, the policy cannot improve rewards by simply extending paths, forcing it to explore paths based on actual quality.
- **Multi-Objective Extension**: For multiple reward components, SRC is extended to normalization: $\tilde{r}_t = \sum_{i=1}^K w_i \cdot \frac{r_t^{(i)} - \mu^{(i)}}{\sigma^{(i)}}$, where $\mu^{(i)}$ and $\sigma^{(i)}$ are the mean and standard deviation of each reward component $i$, estimated from rollouts during a warm-up epoch and then frozen. This centers and rescales components for balanced multi-objective optimization.

#### 3.3.2. Position-Specific Advantage Estimation (PSAE)

- **Problem Addressed**: The "high gradient variance" deficiency, where standard estimation uses the entire path reward to weight each step, introducing noise.
- **Mechanism**: PSAE refines the gradient estimation in two ways:
 1. **Reward-to-Go**: Instead of using the full path reward $R^{(i,j)}$, each step $t$ is weighted by its "reward-to-go" $G_t^{(i,j)} = \sum_{\ell=t}^{L^{(i,j)}} r_\ell^{(i,j)}$, which includes only future rewards affected by the action at step $t$. This removes irrelevant noise from past rewards.
 2. **Position-Specific Baseline**: To further reduce variance, PSAE subtracts a position-specific baseline from the reward-to-go. The baseline $\bar{G}_{i,t}$ is computed as the average reward-to-go at step $t$ across all paths generated from the same input $i$ that reach step $t$. The advantage $\hat{A}_t^{(i,j)} = G_t^{(i,j)} - \bar{G}_{i,t}$.
- **Effect**: The rectified policy gradient estimator becomes: $\hat{g}_{rect} = \frac{1}{nm} \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^m \left[ \sum_{t=1}^{L^{(i,j)}} \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta^{(i,j,t)} \cdot \hat{A}_t^{(i,j)} \right]$. This provides an unbiased, lower-variance gradient estimate that precisely targets path quality by adapting to the expected future return at each position. This approach avoids training an auxiliary critic model, which adds complexity and potential for instability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on three real-world datasets: MovieLens-1M, Steam, and Amazon-Book, using SASRec as the primary user simulator/evaluator.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **Superiority**: ProRL consistently outperformed state-of-the-art baselines across all datasets and evaluation metrics (CTR, Coherence, IoI, IoR).
 - **Guidance Effectiveness (IoI, IoR)**: ProRL achieved the highest scores, indicating its ability to effectively shift user preferences towards target items. For instance, on MovieLens-1M, ProRL's IoR was 728.18 compared to LLM-IPP's 662.52 and T-PRA's 355.16. On Amazon-Book, it reached 1383.41, significantly higher than all baselines.
 - **Path Feasibility (CTR)**: ProRL also demonstrated high CTR, maintaining user engagement with intermediate items. On MovieLens-1M, its CTR was 0.8543.
 - **Coherence**: Notably, ProRL achieved the highest coherence, even though this metric was not part of the reward function. This suggests that ProRL learns to generate genuinely high-quality, semantically consistent paths rather than merely overfitting to the reward signal.

#### 4.2. Cross-Evaluator Analysis

- **Generalizability**: To assess if ProRL overfits the SASRec evaluator, the policy learned with SASRec was evaluated using unseen evaluators (GRU4Rec, BERT4Rec, LightSANs).
- **Consistent Gains**: ProRL demonstrated consistent and statistically significant performance improvements on IoI and IoR, even with unseen evaluators. This indicates that ProRL learns generalizable principles of guidance that transfer across different user behavior models, rather than reward hacking or overfitting to a specific simulator.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies: Rectification Modules (SRC and PSAE)

- **Impact of SRC**: Removing Stepwise Reward Centering (w/o SRC) led to an unusually high CTR but severe drops in guidance metrics (IoI, IoR). This confirms that without SRC, the model over-optimizes short-term click probability due to the positive mean of CTR-based rewards, leading to the "length shortcut" and neglecting guidance effectiveness.
- **Impact of PSAE**: Removing Position-Specific Advantage Estimation (w/o PSAE) resulted in lower performance across all metrics compared to the full ProRL model. This validates that PSAE's variance reduction is crucial for effective optimization.

#### 4.3.2. Multi-Reward Design

- **Necessity**: Ablating individual reward components (CTR, IoI, IoR) generally led to performance degradation, particularly in the corresponding metric, but often across all metrics. This validates the necessity of the multi-objective design, suggesting that the reward components are mutually reinforcing and collectively essential for effective policy learning.

#### 4.3.3. Gradient Estimators

- **Comparison**: ProRL was compared against REINFORCE (RF), reward-to-go (RTG), GRPO (path-level baseline), and A2C (actor-critic).
- **Performance and Stability**: ProRL achieved the best overall performance, with steady improvements and the highest guidance metrics. RF and GRPO exhibited failure modes (collapsing to minimal or maximal path lengths). RTG showed moderate length stability but lower guidance effectiveness. A2C's performance was unstable, with increasing variance.
- **Variance Reduction**: ProRL achieved the lowest gradient variance (approximately 5% of RF's variance), which correlated with its stable, moderate path lengths (around 3-4 steps). The analytic baseline of PSAE was noted to adapt naturally without the drift observed in A2C's learned critic.

#### 4.4. Quantitative Analysis of Training Stages

- **Pretraining vs. RL**: The pre-trained model achieved high CTR (path feasibility) but limited guidance effectiveness. The RL stage, using rectified policy gradients, significantly improved guidance effectiveness while maintaining feasibility, demonstrating its role in breaking performance bottlenecks beyond imitation.
- **Latent Capacity**: Rollout@K analysis on the *fixed* pretrained model showed that its latent capacity for high IoI/IoR was remarkably high when sampling multiple paths. This indicates that the RL stage primarily functions as a "probabilistic rectifier," identifying and amplifying high-quality paths present in the low-probability tail of the pretrained distribution, rather than imparting entirely new capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ProRL framework offers several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Rectified Policy Gradient for Sequential Recommendation**: The identification and rectification of the "length shortcut" and "high gradient variance" deficiencies represent a foundational contribution to applying reinforcement learning effectively in sequential recommendation tasks. These issues are likely to extend beyond proactive recommendation to other sequence generation problems in real-world systems.
2. **Enhanced Proactive Recommendation Performance**: ProRL's demonstrated superiority across diverse datasets and metrics indicates a substantial advancement in the field of proactive recommendation. Its ability to generate coherent, feasible, and highly effective guidance paths could improve user experience by smoothly introducing new content while respecting user preferences.
3. **Generalizability and Robustness**: The cross-evaluator analysis and robustness studies confirm that ProRL learns generalizable guidance strategies, not just patterns specific to a single simulator or fixed intervention ratios. This suggests that ProRL can be effectively deployed in dynamic, real-world recommender systems with varying user models and guidance objectives.
4. **Efficiency and Scalability**: By building upon a lightweight transformer architecture and using analytical baselines instead of learned critics, ProRL maintains computational efficiency. The semantic ID approach further reduces the action space and facilitates semantic generalization, making the framework potentially scalable for industrial deployment where LLM-based solutions are often too costly.
5. **Understanding of RL Training Dynamics**: The detailed analysis of policy gradient deficiencies, path length collapse, and the role of pretraining provides valuable insights into the challenges and solutions for training RL agents in complex combinatorial spaces. The finding that RL acts as a "probabilistic rectifier" for existing latent capabilities is particularly insightful.

**Potential Impact**: ProRL has the potential to enable recommender platforms to more strategically guide user behavior, fostering exploration of new content, promoting specific product lines, or achieving broader platform objectives. This could lead to increased content consumption, diversified user interests, and potentially new revenue streams for platforms. The authors' impact statement acknowledges the importance of aligning such proactive strategies with user utility and ethical standards to ensure a positive user experience, highlighting the need for responsible deployment.
