# Real-Time Motion-Controllable Autoregressive Video Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> AR-Drag is an autoregressive video diffusion model that achieves real-time, motion-controllable image-to-video generation by integrating reinforcement learning. It demonstrates superior visual quality (FID 28.98, FVD 187.49) and precise motion control (Motion Consistency 4.37) with significantly reduced latency (0.44 seconds per first frame) compared to current bidirectional and autoregressive baselines, even with a compact 1.3 billion parameter model.

## Problem

- High latency in existing motion-controllable video diffusion models, predominantly bidirectional, prevents real-time interactive control.
- Autoregressive video diffusion models often suffer from quality degradation, motion artifacts due to error accumulation, and limited control signals for Image-to-Video (I2V) tasks.
- Integrating reinforcement learning (RL) into AR video generation is challenged by the need to preserve the Markov property, manage computational expense over long decision horizons, and lack of suitable reward models.

## Method

- A base I2V model is fine-tuned for ==motion control==, then distilled into a few-step autoregressive student model using causal attention and distribution matching distillation.
- The Self-Rollout training strategy maintains a key-value memory cache of fully denoised past frames as causal context, ensuring the Markov property essential for stable RL training.
- Reinforcement Learning (GRPO framework) is applied to enhance the AR VDM, utilizing a composite reward function (perceptual quality and motion alignment) and selective stochastic sampling for efficient exploration over long Markov chains.

## Results

- AR-Drag achieved a latency of **0.44** seconds per first frame, significantly outperforming bidirectional models (e.g., Tora at **176.51s**, MagicMotion at **1426.37s**) and an AR baseline (**0.95s**).
- The model demonstrated superior visual quality (FID 28.98, FVD 187.49) and motion control (Motion Smoothness **0.9948**, Motion Consistency 4.37) compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the Reinforcement Learning post-training and the Self-Rollout mechanism are essential for enhancing visual quality, motion controllability, and mitigating severe artifacts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High visual fidelity and precise motion control are attainable with autoregressive video diffusion models when robustly distilled and enhanced with reinforcement learning.
> - The Self-Rollout mechanism is crucial for resolving exposure bias and maintaining the Markov property in autoregressive diffusion model training, enabling stable and effective RL integration.
> - Reinforcement learning, particularly with a tailored composite reward and selective stochastic sampling, can effectively improve generalization and control alignment in generative video models with long sequential decision processes.
