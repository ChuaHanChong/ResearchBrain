# RoboFoundry: System-as-Policy Evolution for Self-Learning Embodied Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboFoundry is an embodied agent framework that treats a frozen foundation model's entire supporting system as the policy and evolves it through filesystem edits. An inner loop runs tasks and logs traces. An outer loop attributes each failure to one of six capabilities, repairs the context or skill surface, and promotes edits that transfer to held-out traces. It improves GPT-5.5 by 27.8% relative on EmbodiedBench and transfers across robots.

## Problem

- Existing embodied agents optimize one predefined component, such as memory, harness, skills, or code, so gains stay component-local and never expose or fix bottlenecks elsewhere in the stack.
- Most systems stay fixed after deployment, so the same weakness recurs across episodes; execution experience feeds evaluation or manual debugging rather than persistent, validated system changes.
- Self-evolving agents and code-as-policy repair rarely separate task reasoning from robot-specific execution, so learned improvements stay bound to one embodiment or one foundation-model backbone.

## Method

- **System-as-Policy** ==inner-outer loop==: a frozen model inspects and edits its supporting system through ==filesystem operations==, split into task-specific and general components, with rollouts producing traces for revision.
- Two evolvable surfaces behind a ==shared semantic interface==: a ==context system== (SAVE/RETRIEVE/UTILIZE over filesystem memory) and ==hierarchical skills== (atomic, compositions, recovery tree), with swappable ==embodiment bindings== for execution.
- ==Trace-grounded diagnosis== and promotion: failures are attributed to six capabilities (perceive, reason, plan, save, retrieve, utilize), repaired and validated in place, then abstracted into general edits promoted only if ==held-out capability transfer== is non-negative.

## Results

- EmbodiedBench: lifts GPT-5.5 from **56.9%** to **72.7%** average and Qwen3.7-Plus from **60.0%** to **70.3%**; full system beats task-edit-only Lite by **11.2%** relative on Qwen3.7-Plus.
- RoboMemArena: **53.5%** TSR and **72.8%** CSR versus **38.5%**/**55.2%** for PrediMem; applied to standalone π0.5, average TSR rises from **26.8%** to **56.2%**.
- LIBERO-PRO: **91.9%** average across **six** perturbation settings versus **87.8%** for Harness VLA and roughly **18%** for CaP-Agent0; real nesting-doll task reaches **70%** versus **30%** for π0.5.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Evolving the scaffold around a frozen model can close much of the gap between open and frontier backbones, so system design rivals model scale as a capability lever.
> - Gating self-modification on held-out validation turns unconstrained self-rewriting into controlled learning; promoting only edits that transfer across tasks adds clear gains over task-local repair alone.
> - Bigger context windows alone do not consistently help, and privileged object poses push success near 100%, suggesting perception, not reasoning, often bottlenecks agentic manipulation systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A robot must place smaller nesting dolls inside larger ones, in order. Its early failures involve both precise insertion and losing track of completed subtasks. RoboFoundry does more than ask the model to try again. It updates the support system around the model, adding alignment checks and progress tracking, so a later attempt can resume from completed work. The authors report 70% success versus 30% for standalone π0.5. This result comes from 10 trials per task, including trials used for online evolution.

Real-world evolution and transfer. In panel (a), nesting-doll failures prompt alignment and progress-tracking edits, with completion shown at Trial 3.

The central idea is to improve the system around a frozen foundation model and leave the model's weights unchanged. A robot's effective policy also depends on what context it sees, what experience it retains, how it executes decisions, and how it recovers when actions fail. The authors frame their advance as moving from code-as-policy to system-as-policy evolution.

### 2. How the system changes

The supporting system has two complementary surfaces. The **context surface** manages active context and persistent file-system memory. It decides what to save, retrieve, and use. The **skill surface** organizes atomic actions into compositions and recovery structures, so the system can replan from a valid state rather than repeat a failed action. A shared semantic interface separates task decisions from robot-specific execution bindings. It is designed to let one semantic decision be grounded through different embodiments.

Evolution proceeds in steps:

1. Execution produces traces of observations, states, actions, outcomes, and feedback.
1. The system attributes a failure or inefficiency to one of six capabilities: perceive, reason, plan, save, retrieve, or utilize.
1. It edits the relevant surface.
1. A new execution checks whether the task-level repair works.

If the repair works, RoboFoundry uses traces from other tasks with the same capability label to evaluate a proposed general rule. It promotes that rule only if the held-out check is not worse than the recorded successful repairs. The two checks answer different questions: fixing the original task is not by itself proof of transfer. The result is controlled evolution, not unconstrained self-rewriting.

Overview of RoboFoundry. Traces feed capability diagnosis and targeted edits, followed by held-in repair and held-out retention checks. General context and skill rules supply reusable support to task-level systems.

### 3. What the comparisons establish

EmbodiedBench contains 1,128 tasks in four suites: EB-ALFRED, EB-Habitat, EB-Navigation, and EB-Manipulation. Its reported average is the arithmetic mean of the four suite success rates, not a task-weighted average. With GPT-5.5, RoboFoundry scores **72.7% versus 56.9%** for the unmodified model, a reported **15.8 percentage-point** increase (27.8% relative). The evidence supports gains across five tested backbones.

These are online-evolution results. All variants share the task stream and interaction budget and start evolving from the first scored episode. Every evolution trajectory is included in the reported score.

The most direct test of the mechanism compares full RoboFoundry with RoboFoundry-Lite. Lite keeps task-level edits but removes general evolution. On Qwen3.7-Plus, the overall averages are **70.3% versus 63.2%**, and the authors report an **11.2% relative gain** for the full system. This supports the importance of the added general-scope evolution within this evaluation setup. It does not establish that general evolution alone explains all gains.

EmbodiedBench comparison. Entries are percentages. Gains beside RoboFoundry variants are percentage-point differences from the corresponding foundation model.

### 4. Contribution and limits

RoboFoundry turns interaction experience into persistent, validated changes to a frozen model's support system. Because semantic decisions are kept separate from embodiment-specific execution, a validated support rule need not be tied to one robot's low-level controls. The main boundary is the evaluation itself. The benchmark gains are online-evolution results, not scores solely on untouched held-out episodes. Held-out promotion checks related traces that share a diagnosed capability, and the real-world examples cover particular robots and tasks.
