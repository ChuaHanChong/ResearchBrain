# IGOR: Image-GOal Representations are the Atomic Control Units for Foundation Models in Embodied AI

> [!summary] Summary
> IGOR learns a unified, semantically consistent latent action space shared across humans and robots by compressing visual changes between an image and its goal state via an inverse/forward dynamics model pair. Latent actions label internet-scale video, training a foundation policy and world model, and low-data RT-1 finetuning shows the approach matches or beats scratch-trained policies on SIMPLER Google Robot tasks.

## Problem

- Interaction data for embodied AI foundation models is orders of magnitude scarcer than internet text or video, limiting scale of generalist agent training.
- Prior latent-action recovery methods like Genie and LAPO target 2D platformer games where each latent action maps to one control button, an action space too narrow for real embodiments.
- No existing framework learns a semantically consistent latent action space transferable across human activity video and diverse robot embodiments for knowledge transfer.

## Method

- ==Latent Action Model== pairs an ==Inverse Dynamics Model==, using ==ViT== plus ==Spatio-Temporal transformer== with ==vector quantization==, and a single-frame ==Forward Dynamics Model== to compress goal-reaching visual changes into discrete latent actions.
- **Foundation World Model** extends pretrained Open-Sora's ==rectified-flow transformer==, replacing text conditioning with latent-action tokens via ==cross-attention== and adding coarse FDM predictions as auxiliary conditioning.
- Two-stage **Foundation Policy** plus low-level policy: first predicts latent actions from video and text at scale, then finetunes a low-level ==ST-transformer== to map latent actions to continuous robot actions on task-specific data.

## Results

- Trained on about **2.8M** robot and human trajectories, with RT-1 held out for OOD evaluation, using latent action codebook of **4** tokens size **32** with **128**-dim embeddings.
- On SIMPLER Google Robot tasks finetuned on only **1%** of RT-1 data, IGOR achieves equal or higher success rate than a from-scratch low-level policy baseline across Pick Coke Can, Move Near, and Open/Close Drawer.
- Nearest-neighbor analysis on **15,000** RT-1 image pairs shows normalized action standard deviation stays below **1.0** and shrinks with smaller neighbor count N, confirming latent actions predict real robot movement more than rotation or gripper actions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Compressing image-goal visual change into a discrete latent bottleneck, rather than raw pixels, yields sub-task representations that generalize across embodiments without paired human-robot action labels.
> - Conditioning generation on a single frame rather than full context history forces information through the latent action, preventing the model from bypassing it via temporal shortcuts.
> - Decision-making at a compressed sub-task level, rather than raw continuous action level, may be a more data-efficient path to cross-embodiment transfer than scaling raw action-labeled data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Embodied AI systems, such as robots, face a significant challenge: they need vast amounts of interaction data to develop effective foundation models, yet available robot interaction datasets are orders of magnitude smaller than those used for training large language or vision models.

The IGOR (Image-GOal Representations) framework addresses this challenge by leveraging internet-scale video data to learn a unified, semantically consistent action space that can be shared across humans and various robots. This approach enables knowledge transfer from human activity videos to robots, facilitating the development of more generalizable foundation models for embodied AI.

### 2. The Data Scarcity Challenge

Foundation models have revolutionized AI in domains like natural language processing and computer vision by training on massive datasets. However, in embodied AI, existing robot interaction datasets like Open-X Embodiment and DROID are relatively small:

- NLP models: Trained on trillions of tokens
- Vision models: Trained on billions of images
- Robot models: Limited to millions of interaction samples

This data scarcity creates a bottleneck for developing effective foundation models for embodied AI. IGOR tackles this problem by utilizing the vast amounts of unlabeled video data available on the internet, which contains human activities and demonstrations of various tasks.

### 3. IGOR Framework

The core concept of IGOR is to compress visual changes between an initial image and its desired goal state into latent actions. These latent actions serve as a unified representation applicable across different embodiments (humans and robots).

The IGOR framework consists of three main components:

1. **Latent Action Model**: Extracts semantically meaningful latent actions from videos
2. **Foundation World Model**: Predicts future frames based on observations and latent actions
3. **Foundation Policy Model**: Predicts latent actions based on observations and task descriptions

This approach enables the system to leverage 2.8 million robot and human activity video clips to learn a unified latent action space that can be shared across different embodiments.

### 4. Latent Action Model

The Latent Action Model extracts latent actions from video data through two key components:

1. **Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM)**: Predicts the latent action based on the full sequence of observations. It uses:
 - A Vision Transformer (ViT) to extract features for each frame
 - A Spatio-Temporal transformer to compress visual changes into tokens
 - Vector quantization to create a discrete codebook and compress information

2. **Forward Dynamics Model (FDM)**: Reconstructs the goal state based on the initial image and the latent action, encouraging more information to be encoded into the latent action.

The mathematical formulation can be expressed as:

- Inverse Dynamics Model: $a_t = IDM(o_1, o_2..., o_{t+1})$
- Forward Dynamics Model: $\hat{o}_{t+1} = FDM(o_t, a_t)$

This approach allows the system to learn a semantically consistent action space where similar latent actions correspond to similar visual changes, even across different tasks and embodiments.

### 5. Foundation World Model

The Foundation World Model predicts future frames conditioned on past observations and future latent actions. It is based on a continuous-time Rectified Flow model built upon the pre-trained Open-Sora architecture, modified to enable control from observations and actions.

This model learns various possible movements of objects, demonstrating an understanding of physical interactions. When given different latent actions, it can generate different plausible future states, showing the controllability of the latent action space.

### 6. Foundation Policy Model

The Foundation Policy Model predicts latent actions based on raw observation frames and textual descriptions of the task. It is trained in two stages:

1. **Pre-training**: The model learns to predict latent actions based on observations and task descriptions using a large dataset of human and robot activity videos.

2. **Fine-tuning**: A prediction component is added to predict real continuous robot actions based on observations and latent actions, enabling the model to control specific robot embodiments.

The training process can be formalized as:

```python
# Pre-training stage
latent_action = foundation_policy(observation, text_description)

# Fine-tuning stage
robot_action = low_level_policy(observation, latent_action)
```

This approach allows the Foundation Policy Model to follow diverse language instructions by generating appropriate sequences of latent actions, which can then be translated into specific robot control signals.

### 7. Results and Findings

The research demonstrates several key findings that highlight the effectiveness of the IGOR framework:

1. **Semantic Consistency**: IGOR learns a semantically consistent action space, where similar latent actions correspond to similar visual changes, even across different tasks and embodiments.

2. **Movement Migration**: The system can "migrate" movements from one video to another, even between humans and robots, by extracting latent actions and applying them in different contexts.

3. **Controllability**: The world model demonstrates the ability to predict different plausible futures based on different latent actions, showing an understanding of physical interactions.

4. **Instruction Following**: The foundation policy model can follow diverse language instructions by generating appropriate sequences of latent actions.

5. **Improved Robot Control**: Experiments on Google Robot tasks show that IGOR-based policy training significantly improves performance in low-data regimes.

### 8. Semantic Consistency and Action Migration

One of the most important contributions of IGOR is the semantic consistency of its latent action space. The research demonstrates that similar latent actions correspond to similar visual changes, even across different tasks and embodiments.

This semantic consistency enables "movement migration," where movements can be transferred from one video to another, even between humans and robots. For example, a latent action extracted from a human opening a drawer can be applied to a robot, enabling it to perform a similar action despite the differences in embodiment.

### 9. Robotics Applications

The practical impact of IGOR on robotics is demonstrated through experiments on Google Robot tasks, where IGOR-based policy training significantly improves performance in low-data regimes:

- **Open/Close Drawer**: 20% success rate with IGOR vs. 3% without latent actions
- **Pick Coke Can**: 16% success rate with IGOR vs. 18% without latent actions
- **Move Near**: 18% success rate with IGOR vs. 9% without latent actions

The latent actions are also shown to be predictive of real robot actions, including robot movements, rotations, and gripper actions. As the training data increases, the predictiveness of latent actions for robot control improves, demonstrating the scalability of the approach.

### 10. Limitations and Future Work

While IGOR represents a significant advancement in embodied AI, several limitations and areas for future work remain:

1. **Non-Agent Factors**: The current implementation does not account for visual changes caused by factors other than agent actions, such as environmental dynamics or other agents.

2. **Long-Horizon Planning**: The model's ability to plan over longer horizons and more complex sequences of actions is limited and could be improved in future work.

3. **Cross-Embodiment Transfer**: While IGOR demonstrates some ability to transfer knowledge between humans and robots, more work is needed to enhance this capability, especially for robots with different morphologies.

4. **Real-World Deployment**: The transition from simulation to real-world deployment presents additional challenges that need to be addressed in future research.

### 11. Conclusion

IGOR (Image-GOal Representations) presents a novel approach to addressing the data scarcity challenge in embodied AI by learning a unified latent action space from internet-scale video data. By compressing visual changes into latent actions, IGOR enables knowledge transfer from human activity videos to robots, facilitating the development of more generalizable foundation models for embodied AI.

The framework demonstrates semantic consistency, movement migration, controllability, and improved robot control, making it a promising approach for advancing embodied AI research. While limitations exist, IGOR represents a significant step toward more capable and generalizable robots that can learn from the vast amounts of human activity data available on the internet.

With its ability to bridge the gap between high-level language instructions and low-level robot control, IGOR serves as atomic control units in visual space that can be used for visual trajectory planning and as sub-tasks for robot-specific policies to execute.
