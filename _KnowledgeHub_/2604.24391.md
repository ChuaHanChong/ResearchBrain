# FreqCache: Accelerating Embodied VLN Models with Adaptive Frequency-Guided Token Caching

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel framework, FreqCache, accelerates Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) models by applying adaptive frequency-guided token caching to address limitations of visual-domain methods. It achieved a 1.59x speedup in inference, reducing per-step latency from 637 ms to 401 ms, while maintaining navigation accuracy with an Oracle Success rate of 76.0%.

## Problem

- High computational overhead in Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) models due to repeated, expensive inferences at each timestep, hindering real-time deployment.
- Existing visual-domain token caching methods struggle with viewpoint migration, leading to redundant recomputations and erroneous cache decisions in dynamic environments.
- Current methods fail to robustly identify critical edge information for safe navigation and lack adaptive mechanisms to adjust cache budgets based on temporal scene complexity.

## Method

- **FreqCache** introduces a three-module framework leveraging ==frequency-domain analysis== for adaptive token caching in VLN models.
- It employs ==global amplitude similarity== and ==phase correlation== to establish ==migration-aware token caches==, addressing viewpoint shifts.
- The framework identifies critical edge information via ==high-frequency energy analysis== and dynamically adjusts the token reuse budget using ==spectral entropy== to adapt to scene complexity.

## Results

- FreqCache achieved a **1.59x speedup** in both per-step and episode-level inference latency, reducing average per-step latency from **637 ms to 401 ms**.
- The framework maintained competitive navigation accuracy with **76.0% Oracle Success** and **63.0% Success Rate**, comparable to non-cached baselines.
- An average token reuse ratio of **53.5%** was demonstrated with negligible per-step overhead (**< 2.54 ms**) for its frequency-domain processing modules.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Frequency domain properties, such as amplitude spectrum invariance to translation, provide an intrinsic mechanism to handle viewpoint migration more robustly than visual-domain methods.
> - Orthogonal decoupling of visual information in the frequency domain allows for model-free identification of critical edge features through high-frequency energy analysis.
> - Spectral entropy serves as an intrinsic metric for visual information density, enabling dynamic adjustment of cache budgets based on temporal variations in scene complexity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) models represent a notable advancement in embodied AI, enabling robots to interpret natural language instructions and navigate complex environments by processing first-person visual streams. These models leverage large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to achieve capabilities in commonsense reasoning and spatial understanding, leading to improved generalization in navigation tasks. However, the high computational overhead associated with VLN models during continuous inference—where actions must be predicted at each timestep—constitutes a bottleneck for real-time deployment and practical applications.

The broader research landscape for accelerating VLN models can be categorized into four main areas:
1. **Architecture redesign:** Modifying model structures to mitigate long-horizon overhead (e.g., Uni-NaVid, Efficient-VLN, ETP-R1, StreamVLN).
2. **Model compression:** Reducing computational overhead through techniques like MiniVLN.
3. **Runtime optimization:** Strategies to curb redundant computation during inference (e.g., NAP, KERV, HeiSD). Token caching falls into this category.
4. **Collaborative deployment:** Dynamically adjusting load and overhead in distributed systems (e.g., RAPID, RoboECC).

Among these, token caching has emerged as a promising training-free and plug-and-play runtime optimization technique. It aims to accelerate inference by reusing visual token representations that exhibit similarity across different timesteps, thereby addressing temporal redundancy. Existing token caching methods, such as VLA-Cache, VL-Cache, AirCache, and Strata, typically rely on visual domain methods to identify and select cacheable tokens.

This paper positions itself by identifying critical limitations of these visual-domain-based token caching strategies when applied to dynamic VLN scenarios. Specifically, it highlights issues related to viewpoint migration, unawareness of critical edge information, and the inability to adapt to temporal variations in scene complexity. While some prior work has attempted to address these issues within the visual domain (e.g., VLN-Cache), the authors argue that these efforts are fundamentally constrained by the inherent characteristics of the visual domain.

FreqCache proposes a shift in approach by leveraging the frequency domain. It aligns with a growing trend of incorporating frequency domain methods into model inference optimization, as seen in works like Fourier-VLM (compressing visual tokens via DCT), FlashCache (identifying performance-critical spectral outliers), E-AdaPrune (allocating pruning budgets based on spectral energy), and FAST (compressing action sequences in frequency space). By applying frequency-domain analysis to token caching in VLN, FreqCache seeks to offer a more robust and adaptable solution that overcomes the specific challenges faced by visual-domain methods, thereby enhancing the efficiency and practical applicability of VLN models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the significant computational overhead associated with modern Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) models. Despite their advanced navigation accuracy, the repeated, expensive model inferences required at each timestep for predicting robot actions render them computationally intensive. This substantial cost acts as a core impediment to the real-time deployment and widespread application of VLN systems, particularly in scenarios demanding immediate interaction.

While token caching has been recognized as a promising, training-free strategy to accelerate model inference by reusing stable visual token computations, existing approaches face specific challenges when adapted to the dynamic nature of VLN tasks. The authors identify three key limitations of current visual-domain-based token caching methods:

1. **Viewpoint Migration:** During navigation, a robot's continuous movement causes significant visual translation and perspective distortion, resulting in structural shifts in images. Visual domain methods, which often rely on rigid, position-wise matching, struggle to identify semantically similar but positionally shifted image patches. This leads to the erroneous omission of cacheable tokens and redundant recomputations.
2. **Edge Unawareness:** Critical details such as obstacle edges and door frames are essential for safe navigation and collision avoidance. Visual domain methods, based on global similarity, often fail to perceive these fine-grained yet crucial edge variations. This can lead to the incorrect reuse of tokens associated with dynamic edges, increasing the risk of physical collisions and task failures. Without additional, computationally expensive algorithms, visual methods lack the ability to robustly identify these critical features.
3. **Temporal Variation:** The complexity and information density of navigation scenarios fluctuate over time. Existing token caching methods typically employ static cache budgets, failing to adapt to these temporal dynamics. A fixed budget can lead to inefficient resource allocation—either over-caching in simple environments or under-updating crucial visual cues in complex, information-dense areas, thereby impairing navigation performance.

Given these limitations, the key objective of this work is to develop a novel, efficient, and robust token caching framework for VLN models that can effectively address the aforementioned challenges. The authors propose to achieve this by moving beyond the visual domain and leveraging the inherent properties of the frequency domain. They aim to demonstrate that the frequency domain offers a more suitable foundation for guiding VLN token caching by providing intrinsic mechanisms for:

- Handling viewpoint migration (due to translation invariance of the amplitude spectrum).
- Identifying critical edge information (through orthogonal decoupling of frequency components).
- Adapting to temporal variations in scene complexity (via spectral entropy).

The ultimate goal is to achieve significant inference acceleration and reduce computational overhead in VLN models while maintaining, or even enhancing, navigation reliability and accuracy, thereby facilitating their practical deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The FreqCache framework is designed to overcome the limitations of visual-domain token caching in Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) by integrating frequency-domain analysis. The methodology is structured around three core modules, each addressing a specific challenge identified through detailed analysis of visual-frequency domain discrepancies.

**Core Insights from Visual-Frequency Domain Discrepancies:**

1. **Viewpoint Migration (Insight ①):** The authors analyze how viewpoint migration, leading to spatial shifts in image patches, poses a challenge for visual-domain methods that rely on position-wise matching. In contrast, the frequency domain's Fourier Shift Theorem states that spatial translation results in a linear phase shift, leaving the amplitude spectrum invariant. This property allows the frequency domain to identify patches with structural similarity despite positional changes.
2. **Edge Identification (Insight ②):** Visual-domain methods struggle to isolate and identify critical edge information, often leading to erroneous caching of edge-related tokens. The frequency domain offers orthogonal decoupling of visual information: low frequencies capture global structures, while high frequencies encode fine details like edges. This enables a model-free approach to edge detection by monitoring high-frequency energy.
3. **Temporal Variation (Insight ③):** Raw visual representations lack a direct metric for quantifying scene complexity and temporal variations, often requiring external models or heuristics. The frequency domain naturally captures this complexity through spectral entropy, where simple scenes exhibit low entropy (energy concentrated in low frequencies) and complex scenes show high entropy (energy diffused across high frequencies).

**FreqCache Framework Components:**

Building on these insights, FreqCache comprises three modules:

#### 3.1. Module I: Migration-Aware Token Cache Establishment

This module addresses viewpoint migration by leveraging the properties of the 2D Fast Fourier Transform (FFT).
1. **Global Amplitude Similarity:** Given visual feature maps from consecutive frames (F_t-1, F_t), 2D FFT is applied to obtain their amplitude and phase spectra. The similarity of the global amplitude spectrum (Sim_freq) is calculated. If Sim_freq falls below a preset threshold (τ_mig), indicating a significant scene change (e.g., entering a new room), the entire cache is flushed, and all tokens are recomputed.
2. **Phase Correlation for Spatial Displacement:** If Sim_freq is above the threshold, suggesting stable scene structure, phase correlation is used to estimate the precise spatial displacement (Δi, Δj) between the frames. This involves computing the cross-power spectrum and then applying an Inverse Fourier Transform to locate the peak, which corresponds to the displacement.
3. **Aligned Region Definition:** The estimated displacement (Δi, Δj) is used to spatially align the frames. A binary alignment mask (Mask_align) is created, marking tokens within the aligned region (R_align) as eligible for reuse, while tokens outside this boundary (newly visible areas) are scheduled for recomputation. This mechanism ensures that tokens are only considered for reuse if their corresponding features are spatially aligned across frames, mitigating false cache refreshes.

#### 3.2. Module II: Edge-Information-Related Token Identification and Cache Refresh

This module tackles the edge unawareness problem by identifying critical edge information to prevent incorrect token reuse.
1. **High-Frequency Energy Extraction:** The current frame is divided into patches, and a block-wise 2D Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) is applied to each patch. A high-pass filter is then used to retain only the high-frequency coefficients, from which the high-frequency energy E(i,j) for each patch is calculated.
2. **Adaptive Edge-Awareness Mechanism:** Instead of a fixed threshold, an adaptive mechanism formulates a binary refresh mask (Mask_fresh). A token is marked for mandatory refresh (Mask_fresh(i,j) = 1) if its high-frequency energy E(i,j) exceeds a dynamic threshold derived from the spatial mean (μ_E) and standard deviation (σ_E) of high-frequency energy across all patches in the current frame (E > μ_E + λ * σ_E, where λ is a tunable sensitivity scalar). This ensures that tokens containing critical edge information are not erroneously cached, preserving navigation safety.

#### 3.3. Module III: Temporal Adaptive Cache Budget Determination

This module addresses the issue of static cache budgets by dynamically adjusting the reuse ratio based on scene complexity.
1. **Spectral Entropy as Scene Complexity Proxy:** The authors leverage spectral entropy (Ψ_t) as an intrinsic indicator of visual information density and semantic complexity. The normalized spectral intensity distribution is computed from the amplitude spectrum, and then Ψ_t is calculated.
2. **Dynamic Reuse Budget Allocation:** Spectral entropy is mapped to a dynamic token reuse budget (α_t) using a monotonically decreasing exponential function (α_t = α_min + (α_max - α_min)e^(-Ψ_t)). This function ensures that in structurally simple scenes (low Ψ_t), a higher reuse ratio is permitted, while in visually complex scenes (high Ψ_t), a lower reuse ratio (i.e., higher refresh rate) is enforced. The final cache budget for reuse is K_reuse = floor(α_t * N), where N is the total number of visual tokens.

#### 3.4. Overall Fusion

The framework integrates these three modules. A token is deemed a "candidate reusable token" if it satisfies two conditions: it must be within the inter-frame aligned region (Mask_align = 1) AND it must not contain critical edge information (Mask_fresh = 0). Tokens not meeting these criteria are directly sent to the recomputation queue. From the candidate reusable tokens, the final set for reuse is selected by sorting them in ascending order of their high-frequency energy E(i,j) (prioritizing stable tokens with less edge variation) and picking the top K_reuse tokens, with the final count being K_final = min(K_reuse, K_candidate).

**Implementation Details:**
The framework is implemented on a GPU (NVIDIA A100), utilizing Tensor Cores for the computationally intensive VLN model inference and CUDA Cores for the lighter frequency domain processing (FFT, DCT, entropy calculation) and token selection operations. A three-process parallel dispatch configuration is employed on the GPU, with dedicated processes for VLN reasoning, frequency-domain processing, and token selection. Synchronization pointers ensure that the frequency analysis is completed before the VLN model requires the cache mask, optimizing execution efficiency by overlapping computation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the R2R dataset within the VLN-CE continuous environment simulator, using InternVLA-N1 (a 7B-parameter VLA model), demonstrate the effectiveness of FreqCache in accelerating VLN inference while preserving navigation accuracy.

#### 4.1. Navigation Accuracy (Table 1)

FreqCache exhibits competitive navigation accuracy compared to a baseline without token caching and surpasses existing visual-domain-based methods.
- **Oracle Success (OS):** FreqCache achieves the highest OS at 76.0%, indicating its ability to identify optimal actions more frequently than other methods.
- **Success Rate (SR):** FreqCache maintains a SR of 63.0%, which is comparable to the baseline without caching (64.3%) and VLN-Cache (63.1% for the best configuration).
- **Success weighted by Path Length (SPL):** FreqCache yields an SPL of 57.2%, also comparable to the non-cached baseline (58.5%) and VLN-Cache (57.6%).
- **Comparison:** Visual-domain methods like VLN-Cache often experience accuracy degradation, particularly in SR and SPL, due to their limitations in handling viewpoint migration and edge awareness. The "Naive Freq-based Method" also shows significant degradation (SR 53.4%, SPL 48.5%). FreqCache's ability to maintain high accuracy is attributed to its frequency-domain approach, which robustly handles structural matching and avoids incorrect token reuse of critical edges.

#### 4.2. Inference Efficiency (Table 2)

FreqCache delivers substantial improvements in inference efficiency with minimal overhead.
- **Per-step Latency:** FreqCache reduces the average per-step inference latency from 637 ms (without token cache) to 401 ms.
- **Speedup:** This translates to a 1.59× speedup at both the step and episode levels.
- **Token Reuse:** FreqCache achieves an average actual token reuse ratio of 53.5%. This is higher than VLN-Cache (up to 36%) and significantly higher than the Naive Freq-based Method (15.2%), indicating more effective identification of reusable tokens.
- **Overhead:** The additional computational cost for FreqCache's frequency-domain modules is reported to be less than 2.54 ms per step, which is considered negligible compared to the hundreds of milliseconds saved by the caching mechanism.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (Table 3)

Ablation experiments confirm the contribution of each of FreqCache's three core modules:
- **Without Module I (Migration-Aware Token Cache Establishment):** Removing this module, which handles viewpoint migration, results in a noticeable drop in performance (SR -2.8%, SPL -2.7%) and a significant decrease in speedup (1.05×, down from 1.59×) and token reuse (19.8%). This highlights the necessity of robust alignment in dynamic environments.
- **Without Module II (Edge-Information-Related Token Identification and Cache Refresh):** Disabling this module, responsible for identifying and refreshing edge-related tokens, leads to a substantial decline in navigation accuracy (SR -4.2%, SPL -4.4%). This confirms that preventing the incorrect reuse of tokens associated with critical edges is vital for collision avoidance and task success.
- **Without Module III (Temporal Adaptive Cache Budget Determination):** Replacing the dynamic cache budget with a fixed 30% reuse ratio results in suboptimal efficiency (speedup 1.52×) and a slight accuracy drop (SR -0.7%, SPL -0.9%). This indicates that adaptive budget allocation, guided by spectral entropy, is crucial for maximizing efficiency while maintaining performance across varying scene complexities.

#### 4.4. Hyperparameter Discussion (Figure 8)

Analysis of hyperparameters (migration threshold τ_mig, edge detection sensitivity λ, reuse bounds α_max and α_min) demonstrates trade-offs between navigation accuracy (SPL) and inference latency. Optimal empirical settings are identified (τ_mig = 0.12, λ = 0.25, α_max = 0.5, α_min = 0.08) that balance high token reuse with robust obstacle awareness.

In summary, FreqCache demonstrates that leveraging frequency-domain properties for token caching in VLN models provides a robust and efficient solution. It achieves significant acceleration (1.59× speedup) with minimal overhead, while maintaining or even improving navigation success rates, by effectively addressing the challenges of viewpoint migration, edge awareness, and temporal scene complexity that plague visual-domain methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of FreqCache holds significance for the field of embodied AI, particularly in the domain of Vision-Language Navigation, by offering a practical and effective solution to a critical bottleneck.

#### 5.1. Significance

1. **Addressing Core Limitations:** FreqCache provides a principled approach to overcoming the fundamental limitations of existing visual-domain token caching methods when applied to VLN models. By moving to the frequency domain, it intrinsically addresses viewpoint migration, critical edge identification, and adaptive scene complexity without requiring complex auxiliary models or heuristics in the visual domain.
2. **Training-Free Optimization:** As a training-free and plug-and-play runtime optimization technique, FreqCache offers a deployment-friendly acceleration strategy. This reduces the burden of retraining or fine-tuning large VLN models, making it readily adaptable to various existing architectures.
3. **Enhanced Reliability and Efficiency:** The framework achieves a notable 1.59× speedup in inference, significantly reducing per-step latency, while concurrently maintaining or improving navigation accuracy. This demonstrates that performance gains can be achieved without compromising the reliability and safety crucial for robotic applications.
4. **Leveraging Frequency Domain Properties:** The work highlights the utility of frequency-domain analysis, traditionally used in signal and image processing, for optimizing complex deep learning models in dynamic embodied environments. It provides a detailed theoretical and empirical demonstration of how properties like amplitude invariance, orthogonal decoupling, and spectral entropy can be leveraged for intelligent system design.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

1. **Accelerated Deployment of VLN Models:** The primary impact is enabling the more efficient and widespread deployment of high-performance VLN models in real-world scenarios. This includes applications in robotics for autonomous navigation, assistive technologies, and intelligent agents operating in dynamic environments where real-time decision-making is paramount.
2. **Facilitating Real-time Interaction:** By significantly reducing inference latency, FreqCache can enable VLN systems to operate with lower latencies, leading to more responsive and fluid interactions with the environment and human users. This is critical for applications demanding immediate feedback and adaptation.
3. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays a foundation for future research in several directions:
 - **Advanced Frequency-Domain Techniques:** Exploring more sophisticated frequency-domain analyses or hybrid approaches that combine frequency and spatial domain insights for even greater optimization.
 - **Cross-Modal Optimization:** Applying similar frequency-guided principles to other modalities (e.g., audio, depth) in multimodal AI systems.
 - **Resource-Constrained Environments:** The efficiency gains could be particularly impactful for deploying VLN models on edge devices or platforms with limited computational resources, broadening the accessibility of advanced embodied AI.
4. **Influence on VLM Optimization:** The success of FreqCache suggests that frequency-domain methods could be a valuable tool for optimizing other large Vision-Language Models beyond navigation tasks, particularly those involving sequential visual processing where temporal redundancy is a concern.

In conclusion, FreqCache represents a step forward in making advanced embodied VLN models more computationally efficient and practical for real-world applications, driven by a novel and effective integration of frequency-domain analysis.
