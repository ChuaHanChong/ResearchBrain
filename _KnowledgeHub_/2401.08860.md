# Cross-Level Multi-Instance Distillation for Self-Supervised Fine-Grained Visual Categorization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Wuhan University and collaborators developed Cross-level Multi-instance Distillation (CMD), a self-supervised framework that integrates Multiple Instance Learning and knowledge distillation to enhance fine-grained visual categorization by explicitly learning from discriminative local regions. This approach yielded Top-1 accuracy improvements of over 20% on datasets like CUB and CAR compared to standard self-supervised methods.

## Problem

- Fine-grained Visual Categorization (FGVC) suffers from the scarcity of high-quality, large-scale annotated datasets, as expert labeling is resource-intensive.
- Traditional self-supervised learning (SSL) methods are less effective for FGVC due to a "learning objective inconsistency," where image-level objectives dilute critical fine-grained, region-level details.
- Applying standard SSL to FGVC often leads to ambiguous gradients during training, hindering the learning of discriminative features for subtle differences.

## Method

- The proposed **Cross-level Multi-instance Distillation (CMD)** framework extends the ==DINO== self-distillation paradigm by integrating ==Multiple Instance Learning (MIL)== to model images as bags of patches (instances).
- It employs ==Intra-level Multi-instance Distillation== to align bag representations from corresponding image-level and region-level crops between teacher and student networks.
- ==Inter-level Multi-instance Distillation== is introduced to enforce fine-grained semantic consistency between image-level and region-level bag representations within the same network.

## Results

- CMD achieved Top-1 accuracy gains of up to **10.14%** on CUB and **11.13%** on CAR datasets over LCR, a self-supervised FGVC method, and over **20%** on these datasets compared to general SSL methods like DINO.
- In image retrieval tasks, CMD consistently outperformed compared methods, showing **20.19%** higher Rank-1 accuracy on CUB and **16.95%** higher Rank-1 on NAB than LCR.
- Ablation studies confirmed the positive contributions of Multi-instance Learning, region-level distillation, and inter-level distillation components, with student-side inter-level distillation yielding substantial performance improvements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The primary challenge in applying self-supervised learning to FGVC lies in the incompatibility between general SSL's image-level objectives and FGVC's requirement for region-level discrimination.
> - Integrating MIL principles into a self-supervised knowledge distillation framework enables the model to inherently prioritize and learn from discriminative local patches without explicit fine-grained annotations.
> - Ensuring consistent fine-grained semantic information across different granularities (image-level and region-level crops) and across the teacher-student networks is crucial for building robust FGVC representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Fine-grained Visual Categorization (FGVC) is a computer vision task focused on distinguishing subtle differences between subordinate categories (e.g., specific bird species, car models). A significant challenge in FGVC is the scarcity of high-quality, large-scale annotated datasets, as expert domain knowledge is often required for precise labeling. This annotation process is resource-intensive and time-consuming.

Self-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as a promising paradigm to address data scarcity by learning robust visual representations from abundant unlabeled images. However, recent investigations indicate that traditional SSL methods, which often learn class-agnostic representations, are less effective for FGVC. The core issue identified is a "learning objective inconsistency": most SSL pipelines primarily utilize image-level objectives, minimizing distances between augmented views of an entire image in a coarse-grained feature space. In contrast, FGVC fundamentally relies on identifying and distinguishing subtle details located within specific local regions or "parts" of an image (region-level objectives). When standard SSL methods process an entire image, the information from these critical fine-grained regions can be diluted or overwhelmed by more dominant, less discriminative patterns.

Previous efforts to combine SSL with FGVC have largely fallen into two categories: directly applying image-level contrastive learning objectives or incorporating region-level activation patterns primarily during the inference stage. These approaches, however, do not fundamentally reconcile the learning objective inconsistency at the core of the representation learning process.

Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) is a machine learning paradigm designed for scenarios with weak supervision, where labels are available for "bags" (e.g., images) but not for individual "instances" within them (e.g., image patches). Deep MIL integrates this concept into neural networks. While MIL has been applied in various weakly-supervised tasks, its integration with self-supervised learning, particularly for fine-grained representation learning where no labels (even bag-level) are available, remains less explored.

This research aims to bridge this gap by introducing a novel framework that integrates MIL principles into a self-supervised knowledge distillation paradigm, specifically to address the learning objective inconsistency in self-supervised FGVC. It seeks to enable SSL to inherently focus on discriminative fine-grained regions while simultaneously understanding their relation to the overall image context, without relying on any manual fine-grained annotations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the observed inadequacy of existing self-supervised learning (SSL) methods for Fine-grained Visual Categorization (FGVC). While SSL offers a solution to the annotation bottleneck in FGVC by leveraging unlabeled data, current SSL pipelines are typically designed with image-level learning objectives. This design overlooks the fundamental characteristic of FGVC, which demands attention to subtle, discriminative local regions. The authors identify this as a "learning objective inconsistency" between standard SSL and the requirements of FGVC.

To address this, the research sets out with two specific objectives:

1. **To ensure that key local regions, which determine fine-grained categories, contribute more prominently to the pre-trained class-agnostic representation.**
 - **Motivation:** Fine-grained differences often reside in a few subtle local patterns. For an SSL pipeline to yield a discriminative representation for FGVC, it must effectively highlight and learn from these informative regions, preventing their information from being diluted by less relevant background or coarse-grained features.
 - **Approach:** The authors propose incorporating Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) into the self-supervised framework. In this MIL formulation, each image patch is treated as an "instance," and the entire image (or a larger crop of it) is considered a "bag." Traditionally, MIL requires bag-level annotations. However, in this self-supervised context, no labels are available. Therefore, the MIL framework is integrated with a knowledge distillation paradigm (specifically DINO, "self-distillation with no labels"). This allows the bag representations derived from augmented image crops to guide the learning of instance-level representations, effectively highlighting important patches without explicit supervision.

2. **To align the fine-grained information consistency between region-level and image-level representations.**
 - **Motivation:** Modern SSL approaches, such as EsViT, often employ both region-level and image-level crops to enrich the learned representation. For FGVC, it is crucial that the fine-grained semantic information is consistent across these different granularities of crops. That is, cropped image-region pairs that portray semantically similar fine-grained categories should generate closely embedded representations, while those representing dissimilar categories should be distant. This ensures that local details are interpreted in the context of the whole image and vice-versa.
 - **Approach:** To achieve this alignment, the paper introduces two distinct distillation mechanisms:
 - **Intra-level multi-instance distillation:** This mechanism operates between the teacher and student networks. It distills knowledge by minimizing the distance between corresponding image-level bag representations (from image crops) and region-level bag representations (from region crops) generated by the teacher and student networks. This fosters consistency across network views for similar content at different scales.
 - **Inter-level multi-instance distillation:** This mechanism focuses on internal consistency within a single network (either teacher or student). It distills knowledge by minimizing the distance between image-level and region-level bag representations derived from different crops of the *same* augmented view within that network. This objective ensures that the fine-grained semantics inferred from local parts are coherent with the semantics derived from the broader image context.

By jointly implementing these objectives, the proposed framework aims to comprehensively learn the relationships between informative parts and the overall fine-grained semantics of an image, leading to more discriminative self-supervised representations for FGVC.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is termed Cross-level Multi-instance Distillation (CMD) and extends the Self-distillation with no labels (DINO) paradigm. The framework comprises three principal steps: Multi-instance Modeling (MIM), Intra-level Multi-instance Distillation, and Inter-level Multi-instance Distillation.

#### 3.1. Multi-instance Modeling (MIM)

- **Conceptualization:** An image is treated as a "bag" of instances, where each instance corresponds to an image patch. This addresses the need to highlight key patches for fine-grained semantics.
- **Feature Extraction:** A Swin Transformer backbone acts as the feature extractor for both the teacher and student networks. A linear layer transforms the patch-wise embeddings into a set of instance embeddings.
- **Bag Aggregation:** For each augmented view (image-level crop `I` or region-level crop `R`), instance embeddings are aggregated into a single bag embedding using mean pooling. This aggregation function is mathematically proven to be permutation-invariant, adhering to MIL principles. Specifically, `z = (1/N) * sum(z_x_i)` where `z` is the bag embedding, `z_x_i` are instance embeddings, and `N` is the number of instances. `z_t` and `~z_t` denote region-level and image-level bag embeddings for the teacher, and `z_s` and `~z_s` for the student.

#### 3.2. Intra-level Multi-instance Distillation

This component aims to minimize the discrepancy between the bag representations generated by the teacher and student networks for corresponding crops. It ensures that augmented views representing similar fine-grained categories are drawn closer in the feature space.
- **Image-level Distillation (L_I):** This loss minimizes the difference between the image-level bag representation of the student (`~z_s`) and the teacher (`~z_t`). It is formulated as a summed cross-entropy loss, where the teacher's output is regularized by a moving average of image centers (`C_i`). The teacher's gradient is stopped (`detach()`).
- **Region-level Distillation (L_R):** This loss minimizes the difference between the region-level bag representation of the student (`z_s`) and the teacher (`z_t`). A key aspect here is the alignment of features: for each feature in the student's region embedding, the most similar feature in the teacher's region embedding (based on cosine similarity) is identified. The cross-entropy loss is then computed between these aligned features. This ensures specific informative local patterns are matched across networks.

#### 3.3. Inter-level Multi-instance Distillation

This component promotes semantic consistency between different granularities of crops (image-level and region-level) *within* the same network (teacher or student).
- **Student Net Distillation (L_S):** This loss enforces consistency between all possible pairs of image-level (`~z_s`) and region-level (`z_s`) bag embeddings derived from different crops within the student network. It is calculated using Kullback–Leibler divergence between these paired embeddings.
- **Teacher Net Distillation (L_T):** Similar to `L_S`, this loss ensures consistency between image-level (`~z_t`) and region-level (`z_t`) bag embeddings within the teacher network, also using Kullback–Leibler divergence.

#### 3.4. Overall Loss Function and Training Details

The total learning objective (`L`) is a weighted sum of these distillation losses:
`L = (L_I + L_R)/2 + λ_1(L_S + L_T)`
where `λ_1` is an empirical balancing weight, set to 0.1.

- **Network Update:** The student network parameters are updated via gradient descent on `L`. The teacher network parameters are updated through an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student's weights, with an EMA parameter (`λ`) that follows a cosine schedule from 0.996 to 1.
- **Augmentations:** Two image-level crops (224x224x3) and eight region-level crops (96x96x3) are used for both teacher and student. Each instance for MIL is a 32x32x3 patch.
- **Hyperparameters:** AdamW optimizer, initial learning rate of 5e-4, weight decay of 0.05, trained for 300 epochs. Data augmentations include random/horizontal flipping, color jittering, random-resized cropping, Gaussian blurring, and solarization.

#### 3.5. Theoretical Analysis of Problem Resolution

The paper provides a theoretical discussion on why conventional SSL struggles with FGVC. Standard SSL relies on an image-level objective `L_SSL` that minimizes distance between `x_i` and `x+` while maximizing against `x-`. For FGVC, the discriminative information `x_1_i` (from fine-grained patterns) is a very small fraction of the total feature `x_i`. The gradient `∂L_SSL/∂x_i` involves `∂(x_1_i)/∂x_i`, which is often close to zero for most channels, leading to ambiguous gradients and a less discriminative feature space for FGVC.

In contrast, CMD employs an instance-wise learning objective `L_CMD`. By focusing on instances, the problematic `∂(x_1_i)/∂x_i` term (when considering the entire image feature) is implicitly addressed. The instance-level loss directly operates on `x_1_i`, allowing `∂L_CMD/∂x_i` to be dominated by `∂min Dist(x_1_i, x_1+)/∂x_1_i`. This enables a more effective learning signal for the subtle fine-grained patterns, mitigating the ambiguous gradient problem.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed Cross-level Multi-instance Distillation (CMD) framework was evaluated on four common fine-grained visual categorization (FGVC) datasets: CUB-200-2011 (CUB), Stanford Cars (CAR), FGVC Aircraft (AIR), and NA-BIRDS (NAB). Evaluations were performed using linear probing (Top-1 accuracy) and image retrieval metrics (Rank-1, Rank-5, mAP).

#### 4.1. Comparison with Self-supervised Methods

- **Linear Probing (Table I):** CMD, particularly with the Swin-Tiny backbone, demonstrated substantial performance gains. On CUB and CAR, it achieved an improvement of up to 10.14% and 11.13% respectively, over LCR, a prominent self-supervised FGVC method. Compared to general self-supervised visual representation learning methods (e.g., DINO, MoCo v2, EsViT), CMD showed over 20% accuracy gain on CUB and CAR, and over 12% on AIR. On the large-scale NAB dataset, CMD achieved a 25.60% improvement over the second-best method. Performance consistency was also observed with ResNet-50 and ViT-B-16 backbones.
- **Image Retrieval (Table II):** CMD consistently outperformed compared methods across most metrics. On CUB, it achieved 20.19% higher Rank-1, 13.43% higher Rank-5, and 6.45% higher mAP than LCR. Similar significant gains were observed on AIR (9.34% Rank-1, 6.83% Rank-5, 13.18% mAP) and NAB (16.95% Rank-1, 16.69% Rank-5, 19.27% mAP). While Rank-1 and Rank-5 on CAR were slightly inferior to LCR, CMD's mAP on CAR was 7.34% higher, attributed to the potentially synthetic nature and low contrast of some CAR samples impacting instance-level responses.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Alternative Localization-based Solutions (Table III)

CMD also showed superior performance against self-supervised localization methods, which are considered alternative solutions for learning fine-grained representations. CMD (Swin-Tiny) surpassed the second-best method by 8.61% (CUB), 8.16% (CAR), and 8.31% (AIR) in Top-1 accuracy, and by 24.19% on NAB. Similar trends were observed in image retrieval metrics.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Table IV)

- **Contribution of Components:**
 - Introducing Multi-instance Learning (MIL) yielded initial gains of 4.89% (CUB), 5.12% (CAR), and 3.27% (AIR) over a DINO baseline (`L_V`).
 - The inclusion of region-level crops (`L_R`) further significantly boosted performance, with improvements of 13.37% (CUB) and 9.36% (CAR), indicating the benefit of joint image-level and region-level learning.
 - Inter-level distillation losses (`L_T` for teacher and `L_S` for student) both contributed. `L_S` provided more substantial gains (e.g., 15.73% on CUB) compared to `L_T` (e.g., 3.74% on CUB). T-SNE visualizations (Fig. 4) supported that `L_S` leads to better separation of fine-grained categories.
- **Intra-level vs. Inter-level Distillation:** Both distillation types contributed positively. Intra-level distillation demonstrated slightly higher impact on the AIR dataset (9.17% vs. 4.54% improvement from inter-level), possibly because datasets with fewer fine-grained categories benefit more from discriminative image- and region-level crop relations.

#### 4.4. Impact of Hyperparameters and Augmentations

- **Region-level Crop Number (Fig. 5a, 5b):** Performance peaked with 8 region-level crops, declining with fewer (2, 4) or excessive crops (10, 12).
- **Instance Size (Fig. 5c, 5d):** An instance size of 32x32x3 was optimal. Smaller sizes (e.g., 2x2x3, 4x4x3, 8x8x3) led to performance degradation, attributed to the "Curse of Dimensionality."
- **Label Proportions (Table V):** CMD maintained its performance advantage over competitors even when the proportion of labels for linear classifier training was reduced (50% and 20%), demonstrating robustness in low-data regimes.
- **Data Augmentation (Table VII):** All five standard data augmentation operations (Random & Horizontal Flipping, Color Jittering, Random-resized Cropping, Gaussian Blurring, Solarization) contributed incrementally to the overall performance.
- **Sensitivity of λ_1 (Table VI):** An optimal `λ_1` value of 0.1 was identified, balancing the contributions of intra-level and inter-level distillation.

#### 4.5. Generalization Ability

- **Across Different Backbones (Table VIII):** CMD showed good generalization across various CNN (ResNet-50, ResNet-101) and Vision Transformer (CvT, ViL, Swin-Tiny, Swin-Small) backbones on the AIR dataset.
- **Across Other SSL Paradigms (Table IX):** The cross-level multi-instance formulation (CM) improved cluster-based SSL (Suave). When combined with a contrastive learning paradigm (`CM+L_CL`), it yielded strong results, though the knowledge distillation-based CMD consistently outperformed it. This suggested that knowledge distillation is more effective for self-supervised FGVC than contrastive learning, possibly due to its ability to handle subtle inter-class differences without negative pair confusion and its capacity to learn richer semantic information from teacher soft targets.

#### 4.6. Effectiveness over Fully-supervised FGVC Methods (Table X)

When adapted to a one-stage fully-supervised paradigm (by adding pooling and MLPs with direct category label supervision), CMD with a ResNet-50 backbone achieved competitive or superior performance to existing state-of-the-art fully-supervised FGVC methods, including some using more powerful ViT-B-16 backbones. This indicates the scalability and robustness of the learned representation under full supervision.

#### 4.7. Visualization (Fig. 6, 7, 8)

- **Fine-grained Patterns:** GradCAM visualizations (Fig. 6) demonstrated that CMD effectively highlights key local discriminative patterns, often surpassing DINO, MoCo v2, and EsViT, and achieving activation quality comparable to fully-supervised methods.
- **Activated Instances:** Visualizations confirmed that CMD successfully activated relevant instances in both image-level and region-level crops, corresponding to fine-grained discriminative features (Fig. 7).
- **Failure Cases:** The analysis of failure cases (Fig. 8) indicated that challenges such as severe occlusion, low contrast, and varied viewpoints continue to pose difficulties for the model, which are common issues in many vision tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, Cross-level Multi-instance Distillation (CMD), that demonstrates notable advancements in self-supervised learning for fine-grained visual categorization (FGVC).

**Key Significance:**
- **Addressing the Core Problem:** The work directly addresses the fundamental "learning objective inconsistency" between general self-supervised learning (SSL) and FGVC. By integrating Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) with self-distillation, CMD enables the self-supervised pipeline to inherently focus on subtle, discriminative local regions crucial for FGVC, rather than being confined to coarse, image-level representations.
- **Alleviating Annotation Burden:** One of the most significant impacts is its potential to mitigate the intensive, expert-driven annotation requirements of FGVC. By learning effective representations from unlabeled data, CMD offers a feasible alternative, making FGVC applicable in scenarios where manual annotation is impractical or prohibitively expensive (e.g., in biology for species identification, or industrial quality control).
- **Theoretical Bridging:** The paper bridges a research gap by effectively formulating the MIL paradigm within a label-free self-supervised learning context, particularly for fine-grained feature extraction. The theoretical analysis further explains how the instance-wise learning objective helps resolve the ambiguous gradient problem prevalent in applying conventional SSL to FGVC.
- **Empirical Performance:** CMD consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art SSL methods and self-supervised FGVC approaches across multiple benchmark datasets and evaluation metrics (classification accuracy, image retrieval performance). This indicates its practical effectiveness and robustness.
- **Generalizability and Scalability:** The framework demonstrates strong generalization across diverse backbone architectures (both CNNs and Vision Transformers) and shows adaptability to other SSL paradigms (clustering, contrastive learning). Furthermore, when evaluated under a one-stage fully-supervised setting with additional modules, the representations learned by CMD prove to be highly effective, often surpassing or competing with specialized fully-supervised FGVC methods, indicating its scalability and fundamental strength.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Broader Application of FGVC:** By reducing reliance on labeled data, CMD could enable fine-grained analysis in new domains with abundant unlabeled imagery, such as environmental monitoring, medical imaging, and product quality inspection, where expert annotations are scarce.
- **Foundation for Future SSL Research:** The novel integration of MIL with knowledge distillation for fine-grained learning could serve as a foundational contribution, inspiring further research into multi-granularity representation learning within self-supervised frameworks.
- **Enhanced Robustness:** The ability to learn discriminative features from local parts without explicit labels may lead to representations that are more robust to variations in background, pose, and minor occlusions, which are common challenges in FGVC.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge certain limitations. The current CMD framework assumes that fine-grained patterns reside in key, uncorrupted instances. Its performance may degrade under extreme adverse conditions (e.g., heavy noise, severe occlusion). Additionally, the mean pooling aggregation function for MIL does not differentiate the individual contribution of each instance to the bag representation.

Future research directions proposed include:
1. Improving robustness to challenging scenarios where key instances might be degraded.
2. Developing learning schemes to explicitly weight the individual contribution of each instance to the fine-grained bag representation.
3. Investigating the generalization ability of CMD to semi-supervised and weakly-supervised learning pipelines, further extending its utility in scenarios with limited or imprecise labels.
