# Masked-and-Reordered Self-Supervision for Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces Masked-and-Reordered Self-Supervision for Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (MR-RLVR), a two-stage framework enhancing large language models' mathematical reasoning by integrating self-supervised process-level rewards. It achieves average relative gains of +9.86% Pass@1 and +5.27% Pass@5 over standard RLVR, demonstrating improved performance and data efficiency on challenging mathematical benchmarks.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) suffers from sparse supervision, as it relies only on terminal verification, leading to 'process hallucinations' in intermediate reasoning steps.
- Process supervision, which provides fine-grained step-level feedback, is prohibitively expensive due to the need for extensive human annotation for complex mathematical tasks.
- Traditional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on chain-of-thought data often leads to rote memorization of solution patterns rather than generalizable reasoning strategies.

## Method

- A ==two-stage training framework==: an initial RLVR phase uses process-level rewards from self-supervised tasks, followed by fine-tuning with outcome-only rewards for final answer accuracy.
- Automatic curation of process-aware self-supervised data using two tasks: =='Masked-Then-Fill'== to reconstruct missing elements in reasoning steps and =='Step Reordering'== to recover the correct logical sequence of permuted steps.
- Design of ==process-level rewards== (ranging from 0 to 1) for each self-supervised task, measuring semantic match for masked elements and position-based distance for reordered steps, combined into a single `r_proc` signal.

## Results

- MR-RLVR achieved average relative gains over standard RLVR of **+9.86%** Pass@1, **+5.27%** Pass@5, and **+4.00%** Pass@8 across mathematical benchmarks.
- Less optimized base models (e.g., Qwen2.5-3B) showed substantially larger improvements, with up to **+35.98%** Pass@1 and **+22.44%** Pass@5 on AIME25.
- The method demonstrated better sample efficiency, consistently outperforming standard GRPO even with limited training data (**1k** and **3k** samples), particularly enhancing Pass@5 and Pass@8.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dense, structured process-level signals can be automatically extracted from existing mathematical reasoning trajectories, overcoming the cost and scalability limitations of human-centric process supervision.
> - Integrating these self-supervised process signals into RLVR significantly enriches the learning process by providing fine-grained guidance for intermediate steps, leading to more robust and interpretable reasoning.
> - The two-stage approach effectively combines the benefits of fine-grained process quality with outcome-oriented precision, enabling models to generate structurally sound reasoning while optimizing for accurate final answers.
> - Process-level self-supervision enhances sample efficiency, allowing models to learn more effectively even in low-data regimes and generalize better to complex, unseen problems.
