# Masked-and-Reordered Self-Supervision for Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces Masked-and-Reordered Self-Supervision for Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (MR-RLVR), a two-stage framework enhancing large language models' mathematical reasoning by integrating self-supervised process-level rewards. It achieves average relative gains of +9.86% Pass@1 and +5.27% Pass@5 over standard RLVR, demonstrating improved performance and data efficiency on challenging mathematical benchmarks.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) suffers from sparse supervision, as it relies only on terminal verification, leading to 'process hallucinations' in intermediate reasoning steps.
- Process supervision, which provides fine-grained step-level feedback, is prohibitively expensive due to the need for extensive human annotation for complex mathematical tasks.
- Traditional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on chain-of-thought data often leads to rote memorization of solution patterns rather than generalizable reasoning strategies.

## Method

- A ==two-stage training framework==: an initial RLVR phase uses process-level rewards from self-supervised tasks, followed by fine-tuning with outcome-only rewards for final answer accuracy.
- Automatic curation of process-aware self-supervised data using two tasks: =='Masked-Then-Fill'== to reconstruct missing elements in reasoning steps and =='Step Reordering'== to recover the correct logical sequence of permuted steps.
- Design of ==process-level rewards== (ranging from 0 to 1) for each self-supervised task, measuring semantic match for masked elements and position-based distance for reordered steps, combined into a single `r_proc` signal.

## Results

- MR-RLVR achieved average relative gains over standard RLVR of **+9.86%** Pass@1, **+5.27%** Pass@5, and **+4.00%** Pass@8 across mathematical benchmarks.
- Less optimized base models (e.g., Qwen2.5-3B) showed substantially larger improvements, with up to **+35.98%** Pass@1 and **+22.44%** Pass@5 on AIME25.
- The method demonstrated better sample efficiency, consistently outperforming standard GRPO even with limited training data (**1k** and **3k** samples), particularly enhancing Pass@5 and Pass@8.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dense, structured process-level signals can be automatically extracted from existing mathematical reasoning trajectories, overcoming the cost and scalability limitations of human-centric process supervision.
> - Integrating these self-supervised process signals into RLVR significantly enriches the learning process by providing fine-grained guidance for intermediate steps, leading to more robust and interpretable reasoning.
> - The two-stage approach effectively combines the benefits of fine-grained process quality with outcome-oriented precision, enabling models to generate structurally sound reasoning while optimizing for accurate final answers.
> - Process-level self-supervision enhances sample efficiency, allowing models to learn more effectively even in low-data regimes and generalize better to complex, unseen problems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research paper "MASKED-AND-REORDERED SELF-SUPERVISION FOR REINFORCEMENT LEARNING FROM VERIFIABLE REWARDS" addresses a critical challenge in the advancement of large language models (LLMs) for complex reasoning tasks, particularly in mathematics and science. It operates at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: LLMs, reinforcement learning (RL), and self-supervision.

In recent years, LLMs have demonstrated remarkable progress in mathematical and scientific reasoning, propelled by innovations such as chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting, diverse sampling, and test-time scaling. Reinforcement learning has further amplified these capabilities by enabling models to learn from feedback and optimize for specific outcomes in tasks like mathematical problem-solving, code generation, and program synthesis. A key paradigm within this space is **Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**, exemplified by work from Shao et al. (2024). RLVR leverages programmatic checks on final answers or execution outcomes as scalable and low-cost reward signals, thereby avoiding the expense of human annotation. Systems like DeepSeek-Math and Qwen2.5-Math have showcased substantial gains using this approach.

However, a fundamental limitation of standard RLVR is its reliance solely on *terminal* verification. While effective for final answers, this provides only sparse and weak supervision for the *intermediate reasoning process*. Consequently, RLVR models are susceptible to "process hallucinations"—generating plausible-looking but logically incorrect or redundant steps—which are difficult to detect or correct without direct insight into the reasoning trajectory.

This contrasts with **process supervision** frameworks, such as Process Reward Models (PRMs) (Lightman et al., 2023), which explicitly provide step-level guidance. PRMs enhance stability and interpretability by scoring or classifying individual reasoning steps. However, process supervision is notoriously expensive, requiring large-scale, high-quality human annotations, especially for complex domains like theorem proving where judging intermediate steps is inherently difficult. Moreover, traditional token-level supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on CoT data often leads to rote memorization of solution templates rather than inducing generalizable reasoning strategies.

Recognizing these trade-offs—scalability and low cost (RLVR) versus fine-grained process quality (PRM/SFT)—the research landscape has seen an emerging interest in **self-supervised signals** for reasoning. This line of work seeks to derive dense, intrinsic rewards from existing data without additional human annotation. Examples include using model confidence in reference answers (Yu et al., 2025), next-segment prediction objectives (Li et al., 2025), or action-level similarity (Deng et al., 2025). These methods mitigate reward sparsity but often focus on whole answer consistency or long segments, lacking specialization for the fine-grained logical dependencies and structural elements crucial in mathematical reasoning.

This paper positions itself precisely at this juncture. Inspired by self-supervised techniques from BERT (Devlin et al., 2019) that capture contextual dependencies through "destruction-reconstruction" tasks, MR-RLVR aims to bridge the gap between scalable, outcome-only RLVR and the need for process-level quality. It proposes a novel approach to automatically extract and convert process-level signals from existing mathematical reasoning trajectories into verifiable rewards. By doing so, MR-RLVR seeks to enhance the robustness and transferability of reasoning abilities in LLMs, maintaining the scalability benefits of RLVR while addressing its shortcomings in intermediate step quality, without incurring the high costs of human process annotations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind MR-RLVR stems from the inherent limitations of existing approaches to training LLMs for complex mathematical reasoning. While Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) offers a scalable method by using programmatic checks on final answers, it provides only sparse supervision for the intermediate reasoning process. This sparsity often leads to models generating "process hallucinations"—sequences of seemingly logical but ultimately flawed or irrelevant steps that happen to arrive at a correct final answer, or fail to converge on a correct answer due to a poor reasoning trajectory. Conversely, approaches like process supervision provide fine-grained step-level feedback but demand expensive, large-scale human annotations, limiting their scalability and applicability to diverse, complex mathematical corpora like theorem proving. Furthermore, traditional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on chain-of-thought data can devolve into rote memorization of solution patterns rather than fostering genuine, transferable reasoning strategies.

Given these challenges, the **key objectives** of this research are:

1. **Extract Process-Level Signals Automatically:** To develop a framework that can automatically derive dense, structured process-level signals directly from existing mathematical reasoning trajectories, without relying on additional human annotations or explicit expert action sequences. This aims to overcome the cost and scalability limitations of human-centric process supervision.
2. **Integrate Process Signals into RLVR:** To seamlessly convert these automatically extracted process-level signals into verifiable rewards that are compatible with the RLVR framework, thereby enriching the learning process with fine-grained guidance for intermediate steps.
3. **Enhance RLVR Scalability and Performance:** To leverage these process-aware self-supervised signals to improve RLVR's ability to tackle complex multi-step reasoning tasks, reduce process hallucinations, and ultimately achieve superior performance and scalability in settings where only terminal outcomes are reliably verifiable.
4. **Improve Data Efficiency:** To demonstrate that incorporating such self-supervised signals can lead to more sample-efficient learning, particularly in low-data regimes, making the training process more robust.

In essence, the paper aims to combine the best aspects of verifiable rewards (scalability, low cost) with the benefits of process-level supervision (reasoning quality, reduced hallucinations) by introducing an innovative self-supervision mechanism, thereby creating a more robust and efficient training paradigm for mathematical reasoning LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MR-RLVR (Masked-and-Reordered RLVR) proposes a novel, two-stage training framework to imbue LLMs with enhanced process-level reasoning capabilities, especially when only final outcomes are verifiable. This approach cleverly integrates self-supervised learning into a reinforcement learning pipeline.

#### 3.1. Two-Stage Training Framework

The core of MR-RLVR is a sequential training process designed to first instill robust reasoning structures and then refine them with outcome-oriented precision:

1. **Stage I: Self-Supervised RLVR with Process-Level Rewards:**
 - **Objective:** To train the policy to generate reasoning processes that exhibit coherent local logic and clear step dependencies.
 - **Mechanism:** An initial RLVR phase is conducted using only *process-level rewards* derived from specially designed self-supervised tasks.
 - **Data:** This stage utilizes a diverse corpus of mathematical problems, including both proof-style and computational reasoning tasks.
 - **Output:** A checkpointed model with a strong foundational understanding of reasoning structure.

2. **Stage II: Fine-tuning with Outcome-Only Rewards:**
 - **Objective:** To fine-tune the model to achieve accurate final answers on verifiable tasks.
 - **Mechanism:** The model, initialized from the Stage I checkpoint, is further fine-tuned using RLVR, but this time exclusively with *programmatically verifiable final-answer rewards*.
 - **Data:** This stage focuses solely on computational reasoning problems where outcomes can be deterministically verified.
 - **Benefit:** This approach leverages the dense gradients from process-level signals in Stage I to stabilize exploration and provide a better starting point for the sparser, yet precise, outcome-based supervision in Stage II.

#### 3.2. Process-Aware Self-Supervised Data Curation (Stage I)

To generate the self-supervised training data for Stage I, mathematical reasoning problems and their solutions are collected and processed:

- **Data Sources:** DeepTheorem (for proof-style problems) and DeepMath (for computational reasoning tasks) are used.
- **Refinement:** Reasoning traces, particularly from DeepMath, are refined using GPT-o3-mini to ensure cleanliness, logical flow, and absence of redundant self-verification steps.
- **Task-Specific Annotation:** DeepSeek-R1-0528 is employed to automatically generate annotations for the self-supervised tasks, specifically identifying key formulas/steps for masking and defining step boundaries for reordering.
- **Two Self-Supervised Tasks:**
 1. **Masked-Then-Fill Task:** Key elements (formulas, inference steps, theorem invocations) within the reasoning text are masked with `<formula_masked>` tags. The model's task is to reconstruct these missing components based on the surrounding context.
 2. **Step Reordering Task:** A reasoning trajectory is decomposed into its constituent logical steps, which are then randomly permuted. The model must recover the correct, coherent logical sequence of these steps.

#### 3.3. Process-Level Reward Design for Self-Supervision (Stage I)

For each self-supervised task, a corresponding process-level reward is defined, ranging from 0 to 1:

1. **Masked-Then-Fill Reward (r_mask):**
 - For a given masked sample, `h` masked locations are selected for evaluation.
 - The reward is the mean semantic match score between the model's generated completions (`m_k_hat`) and the ground-truth completions (`m_k_star`).
 - Matching is primarily performed using `MathRuler` for mathematical entity alignment, supplemented by textual similarity for fallback, ensuring `r_mask` is `1/h * sum(Match_entity(m_k_hat, m_k_star))`.

2. **Step Reordering Reward (r_order):**
 - Given the reference step order (`o_true`) and the model's predicted order (`o_pred`) for `n` reasoning steps.
 - A normalized position-based distance `d_pos` is calculated as the fraction of steps whose predicted position differs from their true position (`1/n * sum(I[pos_pred(k) != pos_true(k)])`).
 - The reward is defined as `r_order = 1 - d_pos`, yielding 1 for a perfectly ordered sequence and 0 if all steps are misplaced.

These two rewards are combined into a single process-level reward `r_proc = I_mask * r_mask + I_order * r_order`, where `I_mask` and `I_order` are indicator functions ensuring only one task's reward is used per training instance.

#### 3.4. Stage II: Fine-tuning with Outcome-Only Rewards

- **Initialization:** The model weights are loaded from the best checkpoint of Stage I.
- **Data:** Only computational reasoning problems with programmatically verifiable final answers are used.
- **Reward Function:** A binary reward `r_final(y_star, y)` is assigned: 1 if the model's generated answer `y` is verified as correct against the ground truth `y_star` (using symbolic and numerical checks), and 0 otherwise.
- **Optimization:** The training utilizes the Generalized Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO) objective, which is a common RLVR algorithm, optimizing the policy to maximize this binary outcome reward while maintaining proximity to a reference policy via KL regularization.

This comprehensive methodology allows MR-RLVR to leverage the fine-grained insights derived from self-supervised process tasks, effectively guiding the model towards structurally sound reasoning, which is then honed for final answer accuracy through targeted outcome-level reinforcement learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of MR-RLVR demonstrates its effectiveness in enhancing mathematical reasoning performance, particularly for challenging problems and in data-constrained scenarios. The experiments were conducted on two distinct base models: Qwen2.5-3B-Base and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, using the GRPO objective for reinforcement learning. The models were evaluated on four diverse mathematical benchmarks: AIME24, AIME25, AMC23, and MATH500, with performance measured by Pass@k (k=1, 5, 8) using an unbiased estimator with n=64 samples per problem.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Improvements

MR-RLVR consistently outperforms the standard GRPO baseline across most benchmarks and metrics. Under a fixed sampling and decoding budget, MR-RLVR achieved average relative gains over the original RLVR of:
- **+9.86% Pass@1**
- **+5.27% Pass@5**
- **+4.00% Pass@8**

These significant improvements highlight that incorporating process-aware self-supervised signals can effectively bolster RLVR's scalability and performance in settings where only outcomes are verifiable.

#### 4.2. Model-Specific and Benchmark-Specific Trends

- **Qwen2.5-3B:** This model, having undergone less complex post-training, showed substantially larger improvements. For instance, on AIME25, Qwen2.5-3B saw relative gains of +35.98% in Pass@1, +22.44% in Pass@5, and +16.11% in Pass@8. Similar strong gains were observed on AIME24 Pass@1 (+12.04%) and across all metrics for AMC23 (ranging from +5.29% to +12.98%). This suggests that models with more "room for improvement" from initial pre-training benefit more significantly from MR-RLVR. A minor degradation was noted on AIME24 Pass@5/Pass@8 for Qwen2.5-3B, attributed to sampling variance.
- **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B:** While still showing consistent gains, the magnitude of improvement was relatively smaller (e.g., AIME25: +8.17% Pass@1, +15.77% Pass@5, +20.12% Pass@8). This model, being more optimized prior to this work, might have already captured many reasoning patterns, leading to diminishing marginal returns.
- **MATH500:** Both models showed relatively limited improvements on MATH500 (0.13%-4.06%). This is explained by the benchmark's problems being simpler and models having largely mastered their solution patterns, resulting in high baseline performance and minimal marginal gains from advanced verification techniques.
- **Challenging Benchmarks:** MR-RLVR demonstrated its strongest value on competition-level problems such as AIME and AMC, where reasoning chains are longer and more complex, and initial model performance is lower. This aligns with the method's design principle to tackle high-difficulty problems at the boundary of model competence.

#### 4.3. Data Efficiency Analysis

Experiments with varying training data scales (1k and 3k samples) on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B revealed that MR-RLVR consistently outperforms GRPO, demonstrating better sample efficiency.
- **1k Samples:** MR-RLVR showed significant improvements in Pass@5 and Pass@8 (e.g., AIME24: +9.26% Pass@5, +10.99% Pass@8), even when Pass@1 gains were minimal. This indicates that with limited data, MR-RLVR primarily enhances the model's ability to generate a diverse set of high-quality candidate solutions rather than solely boosting single-sample accuracy.
- **3k Samples:** The advantage persisted, with MR-RLVR maintaining its lead (e.g., AIME24: +10.08% Pass@8 relative gain).
This consistent outperformance under data scarcity suggests that the process-level self-supervision in MR-RLVR provides more informative learning signals compared to standard GRPO, facilitating better generalization with restricted training data.

#### 4.4. Value of MR-RLVR Tasks

The paper provides qualitative analysis of the two self-supervised tasks:

- **Step Reordering Task:** In a case involving the Lebesgue differentiation theorem, the model successfully restored shuffled proof steps and generated explicit logical analyses explaining inter-step dependencies. This process effectively reconstructs and explicates implicit reasoning structures, providing richer training signals for learning complete proof construction.
- **Masked-Then-Fill Task:** In a hexadecimal bitwise operation problem, the model correctly filled two masked formulas but made a subtle error in the third. It coincidentally arrived at the correct numeric outcome for `0x7EFEFEFF + 0x81010100` by using an XOR operation instead of addition, as both yield `0xFFFFFFFF` in this specific case due to non-overlapping bits. This "speculative behavior"—where the model infers the target outcome and reverse-engineers a plausible, but incorrect, operation—highlights a valuable aspect of the task. Such errors can be transformed into error-correction objectives, thereby enhancing the model's self-checking and logical adherence capabilities.

Overall, the findings strongly support the hypothesis that process-level self-supervision, when integrated into an RLVR framework, significantly improves mathematical reasoning performance, especially for complex problems, by providing denser, more structured, and sample-efficient learning signals.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MR-RLVR represents a significant step forward in training large language models for complex, multi-step reasoning tasks, particularly in mathematical domains. Its significance and potential impact can be understood through several key aspects:

#### 5.1. Addressing the Sparse Reward Problem in RLVR

The most immediate impact of MR-RLVR is its effective solution to the sparse reward problem inherent in traditional Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). By introducing dense, process-level self-supervised rewards, the framework provides continuous, fine-grained feedback during the generation of intermediate reasoning steps. This mitigates the challenge of sparse terminal rewards, making the learning process more stable and efficient, especially for long and intricate reasoning chains.

#### 5.2. Enhancing Reasoning Quality and Reducing Hallucinations

Standard RLVR, by optimizing solely for the final answer, can lead to "process hallucinations"—plausible but incorrect or irrelevant intermediate steps. MR-RLVR directly tackles this by explicitly training models to understand and adhere to the logical coherence and step dependencies of a reasoning trajectory. The "Masked-Then-Fill" task encourages precise formula and theorem application, while "Step Reordering" fosters a strong grasp of logical flow. This leads to models generating more robust, interpretable, and logically sound reasoning processes, reducing the incidence of such hallucinations.

#### 5.3. Scalability and Cost-Effectiveness without Human Annotation

A major limitation of process supervision (e.g., Process Reward Models) is the prohibitive cost and scalability challenges associated with human annotation of intermediate steps. MR-RLVR cleverly bypasses this by automatically curating self-supervised tasks and generating process-level rewards from existing mathematical corpora. This breakthrough allows for the benefits of process-level supervision to be integrated into LLM training without incurring additional human annotation costs, making it a highly scalable and cost-effective solution for improving reasoning abilities.

#### 5.4. Improved Sample Efficiency and Generalization

The empirical results demonstrate that MR-RLVR offers better sample efficiency compared to standard RLVR, especially in low-data regimes. The dense process-level signals provide richer training gradients, enabling models to learn more effectively from limited examples. This capability is crucial for specialized domains where large, high-quality, human-annotated datasets are scarce, leading to better generalization and stronger performance on challenging, unseen problems.

#### 5.5. Fostering Deeper Understanding, Not Rote Memorization

By training on destruction-reconstruction objectives, MR-RLVR encourages the model to capture the underlying semantic and structural dependencies within mathematical reasoning. This moves beyond mere rote memorization of solution templates, which often occurs with token-level SFT, towards inducing transferable reasoning patterns. The analysis of tasks, particularly how errors in "Masked-Then-Fill" can reveal speculative behavior, opens avenues for training models to identify and correct logical flaws, thereby improving their self-checking capabilities.

#### 5.6. Broadening Applicability to Other Structured Reasoning Domains

The core principles of MR-RLVR—extracting structure-aware self-supervised signals and integrating them into an RL framework—are highly transferable. The paper explicitly mentions future work extending this to domains such as:
- **Program Synthesis:** Where logical step order and correct code snippets are crucial.
- **Formal Theorem Proving:** A domain rich in logical dependencies and requiring precise symbolic manipulation.
- **Multimodal Reasoning:** Involving images, diagrams, and geometric figures, where visual and textual reasoning steps need coherent integration.
This suggests a potential for MR-RLVR to become a foundational technique for enhancing structured reasoning across various AI applications.

#### 5.7. Complementarity with Existing Techniques

The authors also highlight that MR-RLVR is complementary to other advanced techniques like explicit process reward models and test-time scaling. Integrating these components more tightly could lead to even more reliable and scalable reasoning-focused language models. This paves the way for a more holistic approach to building highly capable AI reasoners.

In conclusion, MR-RLVR offers a principled and practical pathway to overcoming critical limitations in current LLM training paradigms for complex reasoning. By cleverly leveraging self-supervision to extract and integrate process-level signals, it significantly enhances the performance, robustness, and scalability of LLMs, pushing the boundaries of what AI can achieve in mathematical and scientific domains.
