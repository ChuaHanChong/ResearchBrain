# Combined Scaling for Zero-shot Transfer Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Google developed BASIC (Batch, Data and Model SIze Combined Scaling), a model for zero-shot transfer learning that achieves 85.7% top-1 ImageNet accuracy by combining massive scaling of data (6.6 billion image-text pairs), model size (3 billion parameters), and batch size (65,536). This approach yields a 9.3 percentage point improvement in zero-shot ImageNet performance and demonstrates enhanced robustness to distribution shifts.

## Problem

- Existing zero-shot transfer models exhibited a notable performance gap compared to highly specialized supervised models on benchmarks like ImageNet classification.
- Traditional deep learning training faced memory constraints that limited the ability to simultaneously scale model size and batch size, particularly for contrastive learning with billions of parameters.
- The role and theoretical implications of extremely large batch sizes in image-text contrastive learning remained underexplored.

## Method

- Implemented ==BASIC== (Batch, Data and Model SIze Combined Scaling), an approach that simultaneously scales data to **6.6 billion** image-text pairs, model size to **3 billion parameters** (CoAtNet), and batch size to **65,536**.
- Introduced advanced memory management techniques, including ==Pipelining with gradient accumulation== and rematerialization, and a Single-Program Multiple-Data (SPMD) scheme with weight sharding, to enable training at this unprecedented scale.
- Developed a theoretical framework to demonstrate that larger ==contrastive batch sizes== reduce the generalization gap in image-text models.

## Results

- The BASIC-L model achieved **85.7%** top-1 accuracy on ImageNet ILSVRC-2012 in a zero-shot setting, representing a **9.3 percentage point** absolute improvement over prior leading models.
- BASIC models showed enhanced robustness, with a **10.1 percentage point** average accuracy improvement across five diverse robustness benchmarks compared to CLIP and ALIGN.
- Empirical analyses validated that larger contrastive batch sizes (up to **65,536**) consistently lead to higher ImageNet accuracy, demonstrating their critical role in the combined scaling approach.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Comprehensive scaling across data, model, and batch size is a highly effective strategy for substantially improving zero-shot transfer accuracy and enhancing model robustness.
> - Very large contrastive batch sizes are essential for better generalization in image-text models, a finding supported by a theoretical framework indicating a reduced generalization gap.
> - Fine-tuning zero-shot models on target-domain labeled data can, counterintuitively, decrease their robustness to natural distribution shifts, challenging conventional views on generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper is situated at the forefront of several interconnected and rapidly advancing fields in artificial intelligence: multimodal learning, zero-shot transfer learning, and the scaling of deep learning models.

**Multimodal Learning and Zero-shot Transfer:** The work directly builds upon the success of recent groundbreaking multimodal models like **CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training)** by OpenAI (Radford et al., 2021) and **ALIGN (A Large-scale ImaGe-NatioN pretraining)** by Google (Jia et al., 2021). These models demonstrated an unprecedented ability to learn robust, transferable visual representations from vast amounts of weakly-supervised image-text pairs scraped from the internet. A key innovation of CLIP and ALIGN was their "zero-shot transfer learning" capability, allowing them to classify images for unseen categories by simply matching image embeddings to text embeddings of class names, without any task-specific fine-tuning. This marked a significant departure from traditional supervised learning, which requires labeled data for every new task.

**Scaling Laws in Deep Learning:** The paper also fits squarely within the paradigm of "scaling laws," which posits that increasing model size, data size, and computational budget consistently leads to improved performance in deep learning models. This trend has been observed across various domains, from large language models (e.g., GPT-3) to image classification (e.g., EfficientNet, Vision Transformers). This paper explicitly investigates the combined effect of scaling all three dimensions—data, model, and batch size—in the context of image-text contrastive learning, which has not been as thoroughly explored as scaling in single-modality tasks.

**Bridging the Performance Gap:** A critical motivation for this work is to address a known limitation of CLIP and ALIGN: while versatile and robust, their zero-shot performance still lagged behind state-of-the-art supervised or semi-supervised models when abundant labeled data was available for a specific task like ImageNet classification. By demonstrating significantly higher accuracy (85.7% top-1 on ImageNet) without using any ImageNet labels, this work substantially narrows that gap, making zero-shot transfer a more viable and competitive alternative for real-world applications.

**Robustness to Distribution Shifts:** Furthermore, the paper contributes to the growing body of research on model robustness. CLIP and ALIGN showed superior robustness to natural distribution shifts compared to traditional supervised models. BASIC extends this finding, showing that further scaling enhances this inherent robustness, and intriguingly, that fine-tuning on domain-specific labeled data (like ImageNet) can paradoxically *reduce* robustness on out-of-distribution datasets. This observation challenges conventional wisdom and opens new avenues for research into the factors influencing model generalization and robustness.

In essence, this paper represents a significant step forward in the quest for more general-purpose AI systems, demonstrating that the scaling paradigm, when applied comprehensively to multimodal contrastive learning, can yield models with impressive zero-shot transfer capabilities and enhanced robustness, pushing the boundaries of what's achievable without task-specific labeled data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **significantly boost the performance of zero-shot transfer models** by pushing the limits of image-text contrastive learning through a comprehensive scaling strategy. The authors aim to demonstrate that by combining scaling efforts across three critical dimensions—data size, model size, and batch size—they can achieve accuracy and robustness levels that not only surpass previous state-of-the-art zero-shot models like CLIP and ALIGN but also approach the performance of highly specialized supervised models.

Specifically, the key motivations and objectives can be broken down as follows:

- **Bridge the Accuracy Gap:** The most explicit motivation is to address the performance gap between existing zero-shot transfer models (like CLIP and ALIGN, which achieved around 76% on ImageNet) and state-of-the-art supervised or semi-supervised models (which reached over 87% or even 90% with extra data). The authors sought to narrow this gap using only weak supervision from image-text pairs, without requiring any labeled examples from the target dataset (e.g., ImageNet).
- **Enhance Robustness:** Beyond raw accuracy, the paper aims to confirm and further improve the inherent robustness properties observed in CLIP and ALIGN. Zero-shot models exhibit better performance on benchmarks with natural distribution shifts (e.g., ImageNet-{A, R, V2, Sketch}, ObjectNet) compared to their supervised counterparts. This work investigates if combined scaling can amplify this desirable property, making models more reliable in diverse real-world scenarios.
- **Overcome Computational Bottlenecks:** A major practical challenge in scaling deep learning models is the limited memory capacity of accelerators (GPUs and TPUs). A critical objective was to develop and implement efficient computational strategies that enable training models with billions of parameters on datasets with billions of examples, using batch sizes in the tens of thousands, despite these memory constraints. This involves innovating on gradient accumulation and model parallelism techniques tailored for contrastive learning.
- **Provide Theoretical Justification for Large Batch Sizes:** While the benefits of large data and model sizes are generally accepted, the specific role of very large batch sizes in the context of image-text contrastive learning was less understood. The authors aimed to provide a theoretical framework that mathematically explains why larger contrastive batch sizes lead to smaller generalization gaps and, consequently, better performance in image-text models.
- **Validate Combined Scaling as a General Strategy:** The project ultimately aims to provide strong empirical evidence that a holistic, multi-dimensional scaling approach—what they term "combined scaling"—is a highly effective and perhaps necessary strategy for advancing the capabilities of general-purpose AI models, particularly in the domain of multimodal understanding.

Through these objectives, the authors sought to unlock the full potential of weakly-supervised, zero-shot transfer learning, positioning it as a robust and versatile paradigm for future AI applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this work, termed **BASIC (Batch, Data and Model SIze Combined Scaling)**, revolves around scaling up the image-text contrastive learning framework across three key dimensions: data size, model size, and batch size. To achieve this unprecedented scale, the authors developed novel computational strategies and provided theoretical justification for their approach.

#### 3.1. Three Dimensions of Scaling

- **Data Size:** The authors significantly expanded the existing ALIGN dataset (1.7 billion noisy image-text pairs) by collecting an additional 5 billion pairs from the JFT dataset, resulting in a total of **6.6 billion noisy image-text pairs** (4x larger than ALIGN, 16x larger than CLIP). This massive dataset is central to learning robust, general-purpose representations.
- **Model Size:** They adopted **CoAtNet** as the image encoder, an architecture designed for high learning capacity by marrying convolution and attention mechanisms, and scaled it up to **3 billion parameters** (3.75x more weights and 8x more FLOPs than the largest CLIP/ALIGN models). For the text encoder, a simple transformer was used, averaging representations across all steps at the top layer.
- **Batch Size:** The contrastive learning framework was trained with an extremely large batch size of **65,536 examples** per minibatch (2x more than CLIP, 4x more than ALIGN).

#### 3.2. Overcoming Memory Bottlenecks for Large Models and Batches

The combined scaling strategy, especially the massive model and batch sizes, pushes accelerator memory limits. The authors propose two main approaches:

- **Pipelining and Gradient Accumulation (GradAccum) with Rematerialization:**
 - **Challenge:** Standard GradAccum cannot be naively applied to contrastive learning because the contrastive loss (Equation 3) requires the *entire* similarity matrix `A` computed from all `B` examples in a batch, not just a microbatch. Also, storing cumulative gradients for billion-parameter models is memory-prohibitive.
 - **Solution:** They modify GradAccum to first compute the full similarity matrix `A` (discarding intermediate hidden states to save memory). Then, the gradient with respect to `A` is computed and microbatched. For each microbatch, the necessary hidden states are *recomputed* (rematerialization/gradient checkpointing) during the backward pass to propagate gradients to model weights. This ensures exact gradients are computed while keeping peak memory usage proportional to the *microbatch size (M)*, not the global batch size (B).
 - **Gradient Accumulation without Extra Memory:** To avoid allocating memory for a cumulative gradient vector, microbatch gradients are directly accumulated into the optimizer's moment slots (e.g., Adam's `v1` and `v2`). This requires approximating the variance for the second moment (`v2`) using a known identity (Var[c_i] = Var[g]/M).
- **Single-Program Multiple-Data (SPMD) Scheme with Weight Sharding and Rematerialization:**
 - **Challenge:** The GradAccum approach introduces approximation errors (due to moment accumulation) and can be slow, especially with batch normalization layers that behave differently with microbatches vs. global batches.
 - **Solution:** SPMD involves sharding model weights across multiple accelerator cores within a replica, distributing the memory load. The full weight tensor is only reassembled for computation when necessary, then discarded. This provides *exact* gradient computations.
 - **Rematerialization Heuristic:** For SPMD, they selectively rematerialize layers that are fast to recompute but consume significant memory (e.g., activation functions, batch/layer normalization). Layers involving weights (convolution, attention, dense) are mostly kept in memory due to the overhead of cross-core communication for sharded weights.
 - **Comparison:** SPMD offers exact computations and faster training steps compared to Pipelining+GradAccum but requires more manual design for arbitrarily large batch sizes and consumes more peak memory as batch size increases.

#### 3.3. Theoretical Justification for Large Contrastive Batch Sizes

The authors develop a theoretical framework (Theorem 1 and 2) showing that increasing the contrastive batch size `B` leads to a **smaller generalization gap** for image-text models. This gap, representing the difference between expected performance on unseen data and empirical training loss, decreases at a rate of O(1/√m + 1/√B), where `m` is the number of training samples. This formalizes the empirical observation that large batch sizes are crucial for contrastive learning, especially in weakly supervised settings.

#### 3.4. Training Procedure

- **Pretraining and Fine-tuning Scheme:** To further expedite training and potentially improve performance, a hybrid approach is used:
 1. Pretrain the image encoder on a large *labeled* dataset (JFT) using standard softmax classification.
 2. Fix the image encoder weights and train *only* the text encoder using contrastive learning on the image-text dataset (ALIGN+JFT).
 3. Finally, fine-tune *both* image and text encoders together at a small learning rate using the contrastive loss.
- **Data Filtering:** To prevent data contamination, all training instances with a high Structural Similarity Index (SSIM >= 0.5) to any evaluation benchmark image are filtered out.
- **Optimizer:** They use AdaFactorW, a memory-efficient optimizer adapted from AdaFactor and AdamW, further saving memory by storing first gradient moments in bfloat16.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The **BASIC** model, resulting from the combined scaling strategy, achieved significant improvements in zero-shot transfer learning:

- **State-of-the-Art Zero-shot ImageNet Accuracy:** The largest BASIC model (BASIC-L) achieved **85.7% top-1 accuracy** on the ImageNet ILSVRC-2012 validation set without training on any labeled ImageNet examples. This is a substantial improvement, surpassing the best-published similar models (CLIP and ALIGN) by **9.3 percentage points**.
- **Enhanced Robustness:** BASIC models demonstrated significant improvements on robustness benchmarks testing natural distribution shifts. On an average of five test sets (ImageNet-{A,R,V2,Sketch} and ObjectNet), BASIC-L achieved an average of **84.3% top-1 accuracy**. This represents a remarkable **10.1 percentage point average improvement** over CLIP and ALIGN, and crucially, only a small drop from its original ImageNet accuracy, indicating high effective robustness.
- **Outperformance on Diverse Image Classification Benchmarks:** When evaluated on 17 other image classification datasets (e.g., CIFAR, Caltech101, Flowers), BASIC consistently outperformed CLIP models of similar computational budgets on **13 out of 17 benchmarks**. For instance, BASIC-L achieved 97.9% mean per-class recall on Oxford IIIT Pets, setting a new state-of-the-art.
- **Empirical Validation of Large Batch Sizes:** Ablation studies showed that larger batch sizes (up to 32,768 and 65,536) consistently led to higher ImageNet accuracy even when controlling for the total number of examples seen. This confirms the critical role of batch size in contrastive learning, extending observations from previous works like SimCLR and showing that benefits do not saturate at smaller batch sizes when models and datasets are larger.
- **Contributions of Data and Model Scaling:** Both data scaling (expanding ALIGN to ALIGN+JFT) and model scaling (from BASIC-S to BASIC-M/L) independently contributed significant accuracy gains. The combination of pretraining on labeled data followed by contrastive fine-tuning on image-text data yielded the best results.
- **Impact of ImageNet Fine-tuning on Robustness:** A surprising finding was that fine-tuning converged BASIC models on increasing amounts of ImageNet labeled data, while improving ImageNet accuracy, often led to *reduced* accuracy on robustness benchmarks. This suggests that domain-specific labeled training data can sometimes make models less robust to natural distribution shifts.
- **Efficiency of Memory Management Techniques:** Both the Pipelining + GradAccum and SPMD techniques successfully enabled training models at scales previously infeasible due to memory limits. SPMD was found to be faster and provide exact computations, albeit with a higher memory footprint for larger batch sizes and requiring more manual design compared to the more generic Pipelining approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Combined Scaling for Zero-shot Transfer Learning" paper by Pham et al. holds significant implications across several dimensions of AI research and application:

- **Advancing Zero-shot Transfer Learning to Practical Utility:** By achieving 85.7% accuracy on ImageNet without any labeled training data for that specific task, BASIC demonstrates that zero-shot transfer models can now rival, and in some cases even surpass, traditional supervised models in terms of raw performance. This dramatically increases the practical utility of zero-shot learning, potentially enabling rapid deployment of computer vision systems in domains where labeled data is scarce or expensive to acquire. It accelerates the move towards more generalized, "use-anything" foundation models.
- **Redefining Robustness and Generalization:** The superior and enhanced robustness of BASIC models to natural distribution shifts is a critical finding. It strongly suggests that models trained with broad, weakly-supervised image-text data learn more fundamental and generalizable visual concepts than those trained on curated, task-specific labeled datasets. The observation that fine-tuning on ImageNet labels can *decrease* robustness challenges the assumption that more domain-specific data always leads to better generalization, prompting deeper research into what constitutes "true" robustness and how to best achieve it.
- **Validation of Scaling Laws for Multimodal AI:** The paper provides compelling evidence that the scaling laws observed in other deep learning domains (like large language models or single-modality vision) also apply powerfully to multimodal image-text learning. This reinforces the idea that continued scaling of data, model, and computational resources is a primary driver of AI progress, at least for the foreseeable future.
- **Technical Innovations for Large-Scale Training:** The detailed methodologies for overcoming memory bottlenecks through advanced gradient accumulation and SPMD techniques are highly significant. These innovations are not limited to image-text models but can be broadly applied to any large-scale deep learning training, pushing the boundaries of what is computationally feasible on existing hardware. They offer practical blueprints for researchers and engineers tackling similar scaling challenges.
- **Theoretical Contributions to Contrastive Learning:** The theoretical analysis demonstrating the benefits of large contrastive batch sizes fills a crucial gap in understanding why this empirical observation holds true. This provides a stronger foundation for designing and optimizing future contrastive learning algorithms.
- **Reduced Dependence on Labeled Data:** The success of BASIC further reduces the reliance on painstakingly collected, human-annotated datasets. This shift towards leveraging abundant, weakly-labeled internet-scale data makes AI development more scalable, democratic, and potentially faster, impacting a wide range of applications from content moderation to scientific discovery.

**Potential Limitations and Future Directions (as acknowledged by authors):**

- **Computational Cost:** Training models of BASIC's scale is extremely expensive, requiring massive GPU/TPU resources over extended periods. This limits accessibility for smaller research groups and necessitates future work on more efficient training methods.
- **Performance on Underrepresented Domains:** Despite overall strong performance, BASIC models still struggle with highly specialized or out-of-distribution datasets (e.g., medical images like PCam, digits like MNIST) if those domains are not well-represented in their vast but generic training data. This highlights that "general-purpose" does not mean "universal" and suggests avenues for domain adaptation or more targeted data curation.
- **Prompt Engineering Dependency:** The performance of zero-shot transfer relies on effective "prompt engineering," where the textual description of a class significantly influences results. This manual or heuristic process can introduce fragility and bias, underscoring the need for more robust and automated prompt generation or self-calibration methods.
- **Understanding Text Semantics:** The observed discrepancies in performance based on prompt wording (e.g., EuroSAT, MNIST) suggest that the models may not fully "understand" text in a human-like semantic sense but rather learn to match embeddings based on statistical patterns in their training data. This invites further research into truly grounded language understanding in multimodal models.

In conclusion, BASIC represents a landmark achievement in zero-shot transfer learning, demonstrating the immense power of combined scaling to produce highly capable and robust multimodal AI systems. It sets a new benchmark for what can be achieved without task-specific labels and opens new frontiers for both theoretical understanding and practical applications of artificial intelligence.
