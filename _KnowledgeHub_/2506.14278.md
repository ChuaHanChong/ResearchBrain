# Whole-Body Control Framework for Humanoid Robots with Heavy Limbs: A Model-Based Approach

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Chinese University of Hong Kong developed a model-based whole-body control framework for humanoid robots with heavy limbs. This framework achieves stable dynamic walking at speeds up to 1.2 m/s, resists external forces up to 60 N, and navigates uneven terrains by integrating a real-time kino-dynamics planner with a hierarchical optimization scheme that prioritizes limb motion tracking and ensures compliance with full robot dynamics.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots with heavy limbs (40-60% of total mass) exhibit strong base-limb coupling, leading to significant balance and stability challenges during dynamic movements.
- Existing model-based whole-body control methods struggle with real-time computation due to high dimensionality or suffer from a disconnect between high-level planning and low-level control, resulting in unaddressed limb errors and instability.
- Learning-based approaches often lack interpretability and their high-gain feedback controllers can degrade performance due to the nonlinear dynamics of humanoid robots.

## Method

- A two-component ==model-based control framework== integrating a ==kino-dynamics planner (MPC)== operating at 100 Hz and a ==hierarchical optimization problem (Hierarchical Quadratic Programming)== operating at 800 Hz.
- The kino-dynamics planner simplifies system dynamics and constraints to ensure real-time performance while explicitly accounting for the impact of heavy limbs on mass and inertia distribution via ==centroidal momentum==.
- The hierarchical optimization prioritizes ==limb motion tracking== to minimize control errors and ensures explicit compliance with the ==full floating-base robot dynamics== and the planner's policy.

## Results

- The framework enabled stable dynamic walking at speeds up to **1.2 m/s** in simulation and successfully navigated uneven terrain (**5 cm and 10 cm** stages) on a physical Orca I humanoid robot, outperforming comparative methods.
- It demonstrated robust external disturbance rejection, successfully resisting applied forces up to **60 N** in simulation.
- The kino-dynamics planner achieved an average computation time of **6.57-7.89 ms**, and the hierarchical optimization achieved **0.52-0.58 ms**, meeting the real-time operating frequencies of **100 Hz** and **800 Hz** respectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective whole-body control for heavy-limbed humanoids requires explicit modeling of base-limb coupling and ensuring compliance between high-level planning and low-level control execution.
> - Strategic simplification of dynamics and constraint handling (e.g., converting inequalities to penalty costs) in an MPC planner can enable real-time performance without sacrificing critical physical considerations.
> - A hierarchical optimization structure that prioritizes limb motion tracking in the low-level controller is crucial to prevent the amplification of limb control errors and their destabilizing inertial effects on the robot's base.
