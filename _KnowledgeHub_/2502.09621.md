# MME-CoT: Benchmarking Chain-of-Thought in Large Multimodal Models for Reasoning Quality, Robustness, and Efficiency

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from CUHK MMLab, Shanghai AI Laboratory, and ByteDance developed MME-CoT, a benchmark for evaluating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) across six domains. It systematically assesses reasoning quality, robustness, and efficiency, revealing that current LMMs often exhibit "overthinking" on perception tasks and inefficiencies in self-correction mechanisms.

## Problem

- Lack of a systematic and comprehensive benchmark for evaluating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in Large Multimodal Models (LMMs).
- Existing LMM evaluations are outcome-oriented, masking flaws in the intermediate reasoning steps and preventing fine-grained analysis.
- Unclear impact of CoT on LMM performance across different task types (reasoning vs. perception) and the efficiency of the CoT process.

## Method

- Introduced **MME-CoT**, a benchmark with 1,130 questions across six diverse multimodal reasoning domains, categorized into ==reasoning and perception tasks==.
- Curated ==human-verified annotations== of key steps and reference image captions to enable detailed, step-by-step evaluation of ==Chain-of-Thought== processes.
- Established a multi-faceted evaluation suite with novel metrics for ==CoT quality== (Recall, Precision, F1), ==robustness== (Stability, Efficacy), and ==efficiency== (Relevance Rate, Reflection Quality).

## Results

- Reflection-enabled models like Kimi k1.5 (**64.2% F1**) and QVQ-72B (**62.0% F1**) demonstrated superior CoT quality over other models.
- CoT's effect was task- and size-dependent: most LMMs (e.g., InternVL2.5-8B, **-6.8%**) saw reduced perception accuracy, while larger models like Qwen2-VL-72B gained **2.4%** efficacy on reasoning tasks.
- Error analysis revealed "ineffective reflection" as the most common failure (**76.0%**), where models incorrectly adjusted during self-correction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LMM reflection mechanisms, while enhancing CoT quality, suffer from significant inefficiencies with approximately 40% of reflection steps being ineffective.
> - CoT often degrades LMM performance on perception-heavy tasks, indicating a "harmful overthinking" behavior and a lack of adaptive CoT application.
> - Larger LMMs generally benefit more from CoT on complex reasoning tasks, but models generating lengthy CoT sequences frequently include irrelevant information, compromising efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting (Wei et al., 2022) marked a significant breakthrough in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs). By enabling models to articulate intermediate steps, CoT transformed LLMs from mere answer generators to more transparent and capable reasoners, as demonstrated by models like OpenAI's GPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1. This success naturally spurred interest in applying CoT to Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), which extend LLMs' capabilities to process and reason over visual information alongside text.

However, the authors identify a critical gap: while LMMs have shown remarkable proficiency in diverse visual tasks such as image recognition, video understanding, and 3D perception, the impact and efficacy of CoT reasoning in these multimodal contexts remain largely underexplored and unsystematically assessed. Existing efforts to evaluate CoT in LMMs (e.g., Zhang et al., 2024c; Yu et al., 2023) are deemed insufficient in their systematicity and depth, often relying on simplistic metrics that focus solely on the final answer rather than the intricate reasoning process itself. This limitation hinders a comprehensive understanding of how LMMs genuinely reason and where their multimodal CoT capabilities fall short.

MME-CoT directly addresses this gap by proposing the "first comprehensive and specialized benchmark" for evaluating CoT reasoning in LMMs. It distinguishes itself from prior work by:
- **Broader Scope:** Covering a wider array of multimodal reasoning scenarios across six distinct domains.
- **Fine-grained Evaluation:** Moving beyond outcome-oriented metrics to assess the *quality*, *robustness*, and *efficiency* of the intermediate CoT steps.
- **Rigorous Data Curation:** Emphasizing high-quality, human-verified annotations for key reasoning steps and image captions, which is crucial for detailed process-level evaluation.
- **Novel Metrics:** Introducing specific metrics that probe the informativeness, faithfulness, interference, and utility of CoT, including the first investigations into CoT robustness and efficiency in LMMs.

By providing a robust framework and dataset, MME-CoT aims to establish a new standard for benchmarking multimodal reasoning, facilitating deeper insights into LMMs' cognitive processes and guiding future research toward more reliable and efficient multimodal AI systems. It pushes the research frontier by not just asking "if" CoT helps, but "how" and "when" it helps, and at what cost, within the complex multimodal landscape.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce MME-CoT, a comprehensive and specialized benchmark to systematically evaluate the Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning performance of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs). The underlying motivation stems from the recognized success of CoT in Large Language Models (LLMs) and the lack of a thorough, systematic assessment of its impact and characteristics when extended to multimodal contexts.

The authors articulate three critical research questions that MME-CoT is designed to answer, forming the core motivations for their work:

1. **Reasoning Quality (Logical Validity and Faithfulness):** The first motivation is to scrutinize the logical validity and faithfulness of each intermediate CoT step, addressing the limitation of outcome-oriented evaluation paradigms. Existing benchmarks often only judge the final answer, potentially masking scenarios where a model arrives at a correct answer through flawed logic or sheer guesswork. To counter this "illusion of inflated reasoning capabilities," MME-CoT aims to evaluate:
 - **Recall:** Quantifying reasoning informativeness by measuring the proportion of ground-truth solution steps present in the model's response.
 - **Precision:** Assessing faithfulness by evaluating how many of the generated steps are accurate and logically sound.

2. **CoT Robustness (Interference with Perception vs. Enhancement of Reasoning):** The second motivation explores how CoT impacts different types of tasks. While CoT is generally expected to enhance complex reasoning tasks, its effect on perception-heavy tasks (requiring minimal reasoning) is largely unknown. The authors question whether CoT could inadvertently degrade performance on simpler visual perception tasks, implying a "harmful overthinking behavior." MME-CoT investigates this by proposing:
 - **Stability:** Examining if CoT negatively affects performance on direct perception tasks compared to direct prompting.
 - **Efficacy:** Measuring the extent to which CoT enhances performance on complex reasoning tasks.

3. **CoT Efficiency (Relevance and Reflection Quality):** The third motivation focuses on the efficiency of the CoT process, especially given the emergence of models (like o1) that generate excessively long CoT and reflection steps. This raises a crucial trade-off between accuracy and computational cost. MME-CoT seeks to understand if these long processes are optimally balanced by assessing:
 - **Relevance Rate:** Evaluating the proportion of generated content that genuinely contributes to answering the question.
 - **Reflection Quality:** Analyzing whether each self-correction or reflection step effectively drives the reasoning towards correctness.

In essence, the motivation is to move beyond superficial evaluations of LMMs' reasoning by providing a deep, fine-grained analysis of their internal thought processes, shedding light on both the strengths and critical weaknesses of current multimodal CoT implementations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MME-CoT project develops a novel benchmark and evaluation suite designed for a comprehensive assessment of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in Large Multimodal Models (LMMs).

#### 3.1. Dataset Curation (MME-CoT Benchmark)

The MME-CoT dataset is meticulously curated to provide a rich and diverse set of multimodal reasoning scenarios.

- **Data Composition and Categorization:**
 - It comprises 1,130 questions across **six major domains**: math, science, OCR (Optical Character Recognition), logic, space-time, and general scenes. These domains are further divided into 17 subcategories, ensuring broad coverage of CoT-relevant scenarios involving visual input.
 - A key distinction is made between **reasoning tasks** (837 questions, 74.1%) requiring multi-step logical inference and **perception tasks** (293 questions, 25.9%) primarily testing visual recognition with minimal reasoning. This distinction is crucial for evaluating CoT robustness.
 - Questions requiring overly complex domain-specific theorems or specialized knowledge are excluded to maintain focus on the reasoning process itself.
 - The classification into reasoning and perception tasks involves a two-stage approach: initial guidance from LMMs (GPT-4o, Qwen2-VL-7B) comparing CoT vs. direct prompting performance, followed by expert annotator review.

- **Data Annotation and Review:**
 - To facilitate fine-grained CoT evaluation, **key steps annotation** and **reference image captions** are provided for all reasoning questions.
 - Key steps are defined as essential intermediate steps required to reach the correct answer. These are categorized into "inference conclusions" and "image captions."
 - GPT-4o is initially used to generate answer rationales and image captions (given the question and ground-truth answer), which human annotators then refine and verify.
 - For problems with multiple solutions, all possible methods are annotated. All steps are simplified to retain only core conclusions and relevant visual descriptions.

#### 3.2. CoT Evaluation Suite

MME-CoT introduces a rigorous evaluation framework with three novel aspects: Quality, Robustness, and Efficiency. These aspects are assessed using six distinct metrics, often employing GPT-4o for automated judgment based on the curated annotations.

- **CoT Quality Evaluation:** This suite assesses the correctness of the generated CoT process itself, moving beyond final answer accuracy.
 - **Recall:** Measures the informativeness of reasoning. It quantifies the proportion of ground-truth key steps (inference conclusions and image captions) that appear in the model's CoT response. For problems with multiple solution methods, the recall is computed against the most matched method.
 - **Precision:** Measures the faithfulness and accuracy of generated steps. Model responses are first partitioned into logical inference, image caption, and background information steps (using GPT-4o). Precision then evaluates how many of the generated image caption and logical inference steps are correct, either by matching ground truth or being logically valid and faithfully depicting the image.
 - An **F1 score** is computed from Recall and Precision as the overall metric for CoT quality.

- **CoT Robustness Evaluation:** This suite investigates how CoT prompting affects performance across different task types. Two prompting strategies are used: "direct prompt" (DIR) for direct answers and "CoT prompt" (COT) for step-by-step reasoning.
 - **Stability:** Defined as `Acc_P_COT - Acc_P_DIR`. It measures the performance difference on *perception tasks*. A negative score indicates CoT degrades performance on tasks requiring minimal reasoning, suggesting "overthinking."
 - **Efficacy:** Defined as `Acc_R_COT - Acc_R_DIR`. It measures the performance difference on *reasoning tasks*. A positive score indicates CoT successfully enhances reasoning.
 - Both metrics rely on a direct evaluation of the final answer's correctness (accuracy), extracted by GPT-4o mini and compared to ground truth.

- **CoT Efficiency Evaluation:** This suite assesses the conciseness and effectiveness of long CoT processes, especially for models with reflection capabilities.
 - **Relevance Rate:** Measures the proportion of generated content that is pertinent to solving the question. The model's response is partitioned into steps (similar to precision), and GPT-4o determines if each step's content contributes to solving the question. A scaling factor is applied to amplify differences.
 - **Reflection Quality:** Analyzes the effectiveness of self-correction steps. Reflection steps are identified (e.g., by keywords like "Wait," "Alternatively") and judged by GPT-4o for their validity. A valid reflection either correctly identifies previous mistakes or verifies conclusions with new, meaningful insights.

In summary, MME-CoT's methodology combines a diverse and carefully annotated dataset with a sophisticated, multi-faceted evaluation framework that provides an unprecedented depth of insight into LMMs' CoT reasoning abilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The systematic evaluation of state-of-the-art LMMs on MME-CoT yielded several crucial insights into their Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning capabilities.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Key Observations

The experiments reveal a nuanced picture of LMM performance across quality, robustness, and efficiency metrics:

1. **Reflection Greatly Enhances CoT Quality:** Models incorporating a reflection mechanism demonstrate significantly superior CoT quality. Kimi k1.5, a closed-source model with reflection, outperforms GPT-4o and achieves the highest overall F1 score (64.2%), indicating leading quality. Among open-source models, QVQ-72B (62.0% F1), which also features reflection, substantially surpasses its base model (Qwen2-VL-72B, 56.2% F1) and other open-source counterparts. This suggests that the ability to self-correct and verify steps is critical for high-quality reasoning.

2. **Long CoT Does Not Guarantee Key Step Coverage:** Despite high precision in models generating lengthy CoT, their recall scores do not always align with their overall reasoning performance (final answer accuracy). For instance, while Virgo and QVQ might outperform GPT-4o in direct evaluation, they sometimes exhibit lower recall, indicating that they may skip or implicitly infer intermediate steps crucial for a rigorous, stepwise reasoning process. This implies that even a correct answer might not always be reached through a fully explicit and informative chain of thought.

3. **CoT Impairs Performance on Perception Tasks in Most Models:** A surprising and significant finding is that CoT often degrades LMM performance on perception-heavy tasks. Most models exhibit negative "Stability" scores, meaning CoT prompts *reduce* accuracy compared to direct prompts. The most severe case is InternVL2.5-8B, with a 6.8% degradation. This suggests a "harmful overthinking behavior," where attempting a step-by-step process for simple perception tasks leads to errors or inconsistencies, hindering CoT's applicability as a default strategy. Only a few models, like InternVL2.5-MPO, showed modest positive stability.

4. **Larger Models Grasp Reasoning Better:** Models with higher parameter counts generally achieve better "Efficacy" scores, indicating a greater ability for CoT to enhance performance on complex reasoning tasks. For example, Qwen2-VL-7B shows a 4.8% *decrease* with CoT on reasoning tasks, whereas its 72B counterpart shows a 2.4% *improvement*. This pattern across LLaVA-OV, InternVL2.5-MPO, and Qwen2-VL families highlights the importance of scale for effective CoT reasoning.

5. **Long CoT Models are Susceptible to Distraction and Inefficiency:** Models generating longer CoT sequences, especially those with reflection, often exhibit lower "Relevance Rate" scores. They tend to include irrelevant information or exhaustive descriptions of image content, particularly in general scenes, space-time, and OCR tasks, without contributing to the solution. This "distraction" significantly slows down inference without adding value. For instance, QVQ has a relatively low relevance rate compared to its high quality.

6. **Reflection Often Fails to Help Effectively:** Despite its benefits for quality, the "Reflection Quality" metric reveals significant inefficiencies. Both QVQ and Virgo show only around 60% reflection validity, meaning roughly 40% of their reflection steps are ineffective (introducing new errors, being redundant, or incomplete). Even Kimi k1.5 has over 25% invalid reflection steps. This substantial failure rate compromises overall efficiency and suggests that current reflection mechanisms are not always robust or productive.

#### 4.2. Error Analysis on Reflection

A detailed error analysis of 200 model predictions from QVQ identified four distinct types of failures in the reflection process, preventing productive self-correction:

- **Ineffective Reflection (76.0%):** The most common error, where the model makes incorrect adjustments after reflection, failing to correct previous mistakes.
- **Incompleteness (17.3%):** The model proposes new analytical approaches or insights but fails to execute them, stopping at the initial thought.
- **Repetition (4.9%):** The model restates previously generated content or methods without offering new insights, leading to redundant steps.
- **Interference (1.8%):** Critically, the model initially reaches a correct conclusion but then introduces errors during the reflection process, making the answer wrong.

These findings highlight that while reflection is powerful, its implementation in current LMMs is prone to specific failure modes that need to be addressed to improve both reasoning reliability and efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of MME-CoT represents a significant step forward in the rigorous evaluation and understanding of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), particularly concerning their Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning capabilities. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized in several key areas:

1. **Setting a New Standard for LMM Evaluation:** MME-CoT moves beyond simplistic, outcome-oriented metrics that merely assess final answers. By introducing a comprehensive suite evaluating CoT quality, robustness, and efficiency at a fine-grained, step-by-step level, it establishes a more holistic and insightful benchmark. This new standard will encourage researchers to develop LMMs that not only provide correct answers but also demonstrate transparent, logical, and efficient reasoning processes.

2. **Unveiling Critical LMM Weaknesses:** The findings from MME-CoT highlight fundamental limitations in current state-of-the-art LMMs. The discovery of "harmful overthinking" where CoT degrades performance on perception-heavy tasks is a crucial insight. It suggests that current LMMs lack a sophisticated meta-cognition to discern when CoT is beneficial versus detrimental. Similarly, the high rates of irrelevant content and invalid reflection steps reveal significant inefficiencies, particularly in models employing extensive CoT. These identified weaknesses provide clear targets for future research and development.

3. **Guiding Future Research and Development:** MME-CoT's detailed error analyses and metric breakdown offer actionable insights. Future LMM development can focus on:
 - **Improving Reflection Mechanisms:** Reducing "ineffective reflection," addressing "incompleteness," avoiding "repetition," and preventing "interference" will be crucial for enhancing the reliability and efficiency of self-correction.
 - **Developing Adaptive CoT Strategies:** LMMs need to learn when to apply CoT (for reasoning tasks) and when to provide direct answers (for perception tasks) to avoid performance degradation. This calls for more intelligent prompting or internal decision-making mechanisms.
 - **Enhancing Focus and Relevance:** Techniques to train LMMs to generate more concise and question-critical CoT steps, minimizing irrelevant information, will improve efficiency and user experience.
 - **Scaling with Quality:** The observation that larger models tend to grasp reasoning better provides further impetus for continued scaling efforts, but emphasizes that scale must be accompanied by improved CoT generation.

4. **Facilitating Transparency and Interpretability:** By evaluating intermediate steps, MME-CoT implicitly promotes transparency in LMM reasoning. Researchers can better understand *how* models arrive at their conclusions, diagnose errors at a granular level, and ultimately build more interpretable AI systems. This is particularly important for high-stakes applications where understanding the reasoning process is as critical as the final output.

5. **Providing a High-Quality Dataset and Tools:** The meticulously curated dataset, with its human-verified key step annotations and the GPT-4o powered evaluation prompts, offers a valuable resource for the multimodal AI community. It can serve as a foundation for training new LMMs, fine-tuning existing ones, and rigorously comparing different architectural advancements. The project page, `https://mmecot.github.io/`, suggests an open-source spirit, making these resources accessible.

In conclusion, MME-CoT is not just another benchmark; it's a diagnostic tool that pushes the boundaries of LMM evaluation. By shining a light on the intricate dynamics of multimodal CoT, it provides a roadmap for developing the next generation of LMMs that are not only powerful but also reliable, robust, and truly intelligent in their reasoning.
