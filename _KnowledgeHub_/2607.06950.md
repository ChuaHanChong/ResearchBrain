# Residual-Conservative Model Predictive Path Integral Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed Residual-Conservative MPPI (RC-MPPI), an extension to Model Predictive Path Integral control that enhances robustness to model-plant mismatch by adaptively modulating conservatism based on real-time prediction–execution residuals. This approach significantly improved success rates and reduced constraint violations in simulations for both a point-mass system and a planar manipulator, achieving, for instance, a 94% success rate compared to 64% for vanilla MPPI on the point-mass task.

## Problem

- Sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) methods, like MPPI, struggle with model-plant mismatch, where simplified nominal models diverge from true system dynamics.
- Fixed safety mechanisms in standard MPPI do not adapt to varying prediction inaccuracies, leading to insufficient constraint satisfaction and reduced reliability, particularly in low-cost robotic systems.
- Real-world execution variability (e.g., actuator lag, saturation, unmodeled dynamics) on embedded platforms exacerbates model-plant mismatch, making safety guarantees difficult.

## Method

- **RC-MPPI** augments MPPI with an online mechanism that uses the ==prediction–execution residual== as a real-time measure of model-plant mismatch.
- It adaptively modulates conservatism through three coupled mechanisms: ==residual-dependent constraint tightening==, ==amplified safety-cost scaling==, and ==residual-adaptive sampling== (increased temperature and contracted exploration).
- The MPPI temperature is reconceptualized as an ==epistemic parameter== to reflect confidence in rollout cost evaluations, relaxing ==importance weights== when the model is less trustworthy.

## Results

- In simulations on an LTI point-mass system, RC-MPPI increased the success rate from **64%** to **94%** and reduced constraint violations from **4.16** to **0.62** steps per trial.
- For a planar 2R manipulator, RC-MPPI achieved a **96%** success rate compared to **56%** for vanilla MPPI and reduced violation steps from **3.08** to **0.10**, while also improving control efficiency.
- Theoretical analysis establishes probabilistic bounds showing that the combined adaptive mechanisms monotonically reduce the probability of constraint violation as model-plant mismatch increases.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The readily measurable prediction–execution residual effectively quantifies model-plant mismatch online without requiring complex model identification.
> - The MPPI temperature can function as an epistemic parameter; increasing it under high model mismatch reduces the sensitivity of importance weights to unreliable rollout cost evaluations.
> - Residual-dependent constraint tightening, adaptive penalty scaling, and adaptive sampling collaboratively reduce the probability of constraint violation, monotonically improving safety with increasing mismatch.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of model predictive control (MPC) is fundamental for autonomous systems, particularly in handling nonlinear dynamics and complex cost landscapes. Sampling-based MPC methods, such as Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control, leverage Monte Carlo rollouts and importance weighting for real-time nonlinear control without explicit gradient computation. However, a significant challenge arises from *model-plant mismatch*, where the simplified models used by planners diverge from the true system dynamics. Standard MPPI formulations typically assume a fixed nominal model and rely on static constraint penalties or barrier functions that do not adapt to this mismatch. When model-plant mismatch increases, these fixed safety mechanisms may become insufficient, leading to constraint violations and reduced system reliability.

The broader research landscape addresses safety and robustness in MPC through various approaches:
- **Constraint Handling in MPPI**: Baseline MPPI uses soft penalties, barrier functions, or rollout truncation. However, hard rejection of infeasible trajectories can reduce effective sample size and lead to weight degeneracy.
- **Control Barrier Functions (CBFs)**: Recent work integrates CBFs into MPPI, with methods like Shield-MPPI and guaranteed-safe MPPI variants employing composite CBF constructions to enforce safety.
- **Uncertainty Propagation**: Techniques such as unscented MPPI (using sigma-point approximations) and belief-space stochastic MPPI (enforcing approximate chance constraints) explicitly propagate uncertainty to manage risk.
- **Chance-Constrained and Robust MPC**: These enforce probabilistic constraint satisfaction or utilize tube-based tightening to guarantee safety despite disturbances or model inaccuracies.
- **Risk-Sensitive Control**: Penalizes tail events through exponential cost transformations.
- **Learning-Based MPC**: Incorporates model adaptation to improve prediction fidelity while preserving constraint satisfaction.

Residual-Conservative MPPI (RC-MPPI) differentiates itself by introducing a lightweight, online mechanism for modulating conservatism. Instead of performing real-time parameter identification or maintaining a full disturbance belief, RC-MPPI exploits the *prediction–execution residual*—the directly measurable discrepancy between predicted and realized state transitions—as an indicator of model-plant mismatch. A distinguishing feature is its treatment of the MPPI *temperature* parameter not merely as an exploration parameter but as an *epistemic parameter* encoding confidence in rollout cost evaluations. This approach complements existing methods by providing an adaptive safety layer that responds to observed discrepancies, thereby enhancing robustness in dynamic and uncertain environments, particularly for platforms where detailed model learning or complex uncertainty propagation might be computationally prohibitive.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the inherent challenges of deploying control systems on low-cost robotic and embedded platforms. Such systems frequently exhibit real-world execution variability due to factors including actuation lag, actuator saturation, unmodeled inner-loop dynamics, and limited sensing fidelity. High-level planning algorithms often rely on simplified or nominal actuator models, leading to a discrepancy between the predicted system behavior and its actual execution. This *model-plant mismatch* is not a one-time disturbance but rather a persistent and state-dependent issue in receding-horizon control implementations. Critically, this mismatch can compromise the system's ability to satisfy safety constraints, which is a fundamental requirement for reliable operation.

The standard formulations of sampling-based model predictive control methods, such as MPPI, while adept at handling nonlinear dynamics and complex cost landscapes through Monte Carlo rollouts, typically fall short in addressing model-plant mismatch. They employ fixed constraint penalties or barrier functions that do not adapt to the varying degrees of prediction inaccuracy. Consequently, as the model-plant mismatch grows, these static safety mechanisms may become insufficient, increasing the risk of constraint violations.

The key objectives of this research are:
1. **Develop an online conservatism adaptation mechanism**: To enable sampling-based MPC methods to dynamically adjust their safety margins in response to observed model-plant mismatch.
2. **Utilize prediction–execution residual**: To exploit the readily measurable discrepancy between predicted and realized state transitions as a lightweight, real-time signal for quantifying model-plant mismatch. This avoids the computational overhead of real-time parameter identification or maintaining a complex disturbance belief.
3. **Integrate adaptive mechanisms into MPPI**: To embed the residual signal into the sampling-based MPC objective through three coupled adaptive mechanisms: residual-dependent constraint tightening, adaptive safety-cost shaping (amplified penalty scaling), and residual-adaptive temperature relaxation combined with exploration contraction.
4. **Reconceptualize MPPI temperature**: To treat the MPPI temperature not just as a fixed exploration parameter, but as an *epistemic parameter* that reflects the controller's confidence in its rollout cost evaluations. The motivation is that when the model is inaccurate, cost evaluations become unreliable, and a higher temperature can prevent overcommitment to potentially artifactual cost rankings.
5. **Provide theoretical guarantees**: To derive probabilistic bounds on constraint violation and establish that the proposed mechanisms jointly and monotonically reduce violation probability with increasing residual, ensuring improved safety.
6. **Validate experimentally**: To demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed RC-MPPI framework through simulations on representative robotic systems, confirming systematic improvements in safety margin, success rate, and control efficiency compared to vanilla MPPI.

In essence, the work aims to create a more robust and adaptive sampling-based MPC framework that can safely operate in real-world scenarios where precise system models are unavailable or subject to unmodeled dynamics and disturbances.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed **Residual-Conservative MPPI (RC-MPPI)** framework augments vanilla MPPI with three coupled, residual-driven mechanisms designed to tighten safety and reflect reduced confidence in model predictions as execution mismatch increases.

**System Model and Assumptions**:
The system is modeled as a discrete-time process with state $x_k \in \mathbb{R}^{n_x}$ and control $u_k \in \mathbb{R}^{n_u}$. The controller uses a nominal parametric predictor $x_{k+1} = f_\theta(x_k, u_k)$, while the true system evolves as $x_{k+1} = \tilde{f}(x_k, u_k) + w_k$, where $\tilde{f}$ is the unknown true dynamics and $w_k$ is a stochastic process disturbance. Noisy state measurements $y_k = x_k + v_k$ are available. Safety is defined by a constraint function $h(x) \le 0$. The approach relies on four key assumptions:
1. **Local Lipschitz Nominal Dynamics**: The nominal dynamics $f_\theta$ are locally Lipschitz.
2. **Sub-Gaussian Disturbance**: The process disturbance $w_k$ is i.i.d. sub-Gaussian.
3. **Nominal MPPI Competence**: Vanilla MPPI achieves satisfactory constraint satisfaction under zero model-plant mismatch.
4. **Bounded Measurement Noise**: Measurement noise $v_k$ is almost surely bounded.

**Residual Estimation**:
At each time step $k$, after executing $u_{k-1}$ and measuring $y_k$, the one-step prediction residual is computed as $r_k := y_k - f_\theta(y_{k-1}, u_{k-1})$. This vector residual is then converted into a scalar mismatch indicator $s_k := \|W_r r_k\|$ using an invertible weighting matrix $W_r$. A filtered version, $\bar{s}_k = (1-\rho)\bar{s}_{k-1} + \rho s_k$, provides a smoother estimate of the persistent mismatch level.

**Implementable Horizon Prediction Error Bound (Theorem 1)**:
A crucial theoretical component is Theorem 1, which provides a probabilistic bound on the $N$-step terminal prediction error $\|e_{k+N}\| := \|x_{k+N} - \hat{x}_{k+N}\|$. This bound is expressed as $\|e_{k+N}\| \le c_r \bar{s}_k + c_0 + \|\xi_{k+N}\|$. Here, $c_r \bar{s}_k + c_0$ represents the deterministic, residual-dependent error component, and $\|\xi_{k+N}\|$ represents the aggregated sub-Gaussian stochastic disturbance over the horizon. This separation is key, as it allows the RC-MPPI mechanisms to explicitly compensate for the structured bias indicated by $\bar{s}_k$.

**RC-MPPI Algorithm (Overview)**:
The RC-MPPI procedure involves measuring the current state, computing and filtering the residual $\bar{s}_k$, and then translating this signal into three coupled adaptive modulations. These modulations are applied to the MPPI cost function and sampling distribution before performing standard Monte Carlo rollouts, importance weighting, and control updates. When $\bar{s}_k \approx 0$, all modulations vanish, and RC-MPPI reverts to vanilla MPPI behavior.

**Three Coupled Residual-Adaptive Mechanisms**:
1. **Residual-Dependent Constraint Tightening**: The safety constraint $h(x) \le 0$ is tightened by modifying it to $h(x) + m(\bar{s}_k) \le 0$. The tightening margin $m(\bar{s}_k)$ is defined as $L_h(c_r \bar{s}_k + c_0)$, explicitly linking it to the deterministic part of the horizon prediction error bound from Theorem 1. This shrinks the effective safe set as mismatch increases.
2. **Residual-Aware Penalty Scaling**: The safety cost term $\ell_{\text{safe}}(x; \bar{s}_k) = \alpha_k \phi(h(x) + m(\bar{s}_k))$ is scaled by an adaptive penalty coefficient $\alpha_k = \alpha_0(1 + \gamma \bar{s}_k)$. This amplifies the cost incurred by unsafe rollouts when mismatch is high, making the controller more sensitive to constraint violations.
3. **Residual-Adaptive Sampling Modulation**: This involves two aspects:
 - **Exploration Contraction**: The standard deviation of the control perturbations $\epsilon^{(i)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \varsigma_k^2 I)$ is reduced via $\varsigma_k = \text{clip}(\varsigma_0 / (1 + \kappa_\varsigma \bar{s}_k), \varsigma_{\min}, \varsigma_{\max})$. As mismatch grows, rollouts are concentrated more tightly around the mean control sequence, reducing exploration into potentially unreliable regions.
 - **Temperature Relaxation**: The MPPI temperature $\beta_k = \text{clip}(\beta_0(1 + \kappa_\beta \bar{s}_k), \beta_{\min}, \beta_{\max})$ is increased. This softens the importance weights, making the control update less sensitive to individual rollout cost differences when the model is less trustworthy.

**Theoretical Safety Analysis**:
The paper provides a detailed theoretical analysis of these mechanisms:
- **Proposition 1 (Terminal Safety Bound)**: Based on Theorem 1, this proposition quantifies the probability of terminal constraint violation, showing that as the nominal safety margin $d_{\text{safe}}$ exceeds the tightening margin $m(\bar{s}_k)$, the violation probability decreases exponentially.
- **Lemma 1 (Temperature-Averaging Under Mismatch)**: This lemma establishes two properties: (i) the variance of the control update is nonincreasing with $\beta_k$, implying more averaged updates as mismatch grows, and (ii) unsafe rollouts receive asymptotically zero weight because the quadratic growth of the barrier term $\alpha_k \phi(m(\bar{s}_k))$ dominates the linear growth of $\beta_k$, making the ratio $\alpha_k \phi(m(\bar{s}_k))/\beta_k \to \infty$.
- **Lemma 2 (Rollout Concentration)**: It proves that as $\varsigma_k$ decreases (with increasing $\bar{s}_k$), the Monte Carlo rollouts concentrate more tightly around the unperturbed nominal rollout, reducing the probability of large deviations.
- **Proposition 2 (Joint Adaptive Safety Bound)**: This central result combines the effects of all three mechanisms, demonstrating that the joint action monotonically reduces the constraint violation probability as the residual grows.
- **Corollary 2**: This establishes that RC-MPPI achieves at least the constraint satisfaction probability of vanilla MPPI, with strict improvement when model-plant mismatch ($\bar{s}_k > 0$) is present.

**Rollout-Cost Sensitivity Analysis**:
- **Lemma 3 (Bounded Cost Perturbation)**: This lemma bounds the difference between true and nominal trajectory costs by a term proportional to $\bar{s}_k$, indicating how model mismatch directly perturbs cost evaluations.
- **Proposition 3 (Weight Sensitivity Under Rollout-Cost Uncertainty)**: This proposition shows that the 1-norm sensitivity of MPPI importance weights to rollout-cost uncertainty (induced by model mismatch) is bounded by $2C_\Delta \bar{s}_k / \beta$. This provides theoretical justification for increasing $\beta_k$ as $\bar{s}_k$ increases, as it reduces the distorting effect of unreliable cost rankings on the MPPI update.

**Episodic Model Adaptation**:
The framework also considers a two-time-scale architecture where $\bar{s}_k$ drives fast, online adaptation, and on a slower timescale, model parameters $\theta$ can be updated episodically. Lemma 4 states that a descent step on the empirical prediction loss reduces the model error. Theorem 2 proves that if model improvement reduces the expected residual $E[\|r\|]$, then the expected tightening margin $E[m(\bar{s})]$ decreases, allowing conservatism to relax and nominal MPPI behavior to be recovered.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields both theoretical and empirical findings supporting the efficacy of Residual-Conservative MPPI (RC-MPPI).

**Theoretical Findings**:
1. **Horizon Prediction Error Bound (Theorem 1)**: The paper establishes a probabilistic bound on the $N$-step terminal prediction error, demonstrating its dependence on a filtered measure of prediction–execution residual ($\bar{s}_k$) and aggregated sub-Gaussian disturbances ($\xi_{k+N}$). This formalizes the relationship between observed mismatch and future prediction uncertainty.
2. **Joint Reduction of Constraint Violation (Proposition 2, Corollary 2)**: The theoretical analysis demonstrates that the three coupled adaptive mechanisms—residual-dependent constraint tightening, adaptive safety penalty scaling, and residual-adaptive sampling modulation (temperature relaxation and exploration contraction)—work synergistically. Their combined effect is proven to monotonically reduce the constraint violation probability as the measured residual grows. Crucially, RC-MPPI is shown to achieve at least the same constraint satisfaction probability as vanilla MPPI, with a strict improvement whenever model-plant mismatch is non-zero.
3. **Justification for Adaptive Temperature (Proposition 3)**: A key insight is the treatment of MPPI temperature as an epistemic parameter. The rollout-cost uncertainty analysis (Lemma 3, Proposition 3) reveals that model-induced perturbations to MPPI importance weights are bounded proportionally to the residual magnitude ($\bar{s}_k$) and inversely to the temperature ($\beta_k$). This provides a theoretical basis for increasing $\beta_k$ when $\bar{s}_k$ is high, as it reduces overcommitment to cost rankings that are rendered unreliable by model inaccuracies.

**Simulation Results**:
RC-MPPI was evaluated on two distinct systems: an LTI point-mass system and a planar 2R manipulator, both operating under significant model-plant mismatch.

**A. LTI Point-Mass System**:
- **Setup**: A point-mass system moving in 2D with a nominal LTI model, but with the true plant incorporating a severe first-order actuator lag (e.g., $\tau=0.9$s). The task involved navigating to a goal while avoiding a circular obstacle.
- **Performance Comparison (over 50 trials)**:
 - **Success Rate**: Vanilla MPPI achieved a 64% success rate, while RC-MPPI achieved 94%.
 - **Safety Margin**: Vanilla MPPI had a mean minimum clearance of 0.05 m, often resulting in obstacle penetration (a representative trial showed -0.082 m clearance). RC-MPPI improved this to 0.13 m.
 - **Constraint Violations**: Vanilla MPPI averaged 4.16 violation steps per trial, significantly reduced to 0.62 steps for RC-MPPI.
 - **Efficiency Metrics**: RC-MPPI exhibited a modest increase in time-to-goal (232.78 to 249.00 steps) and path length (15.85 to 16.42 m). This increase is consistent with the intended safety–efficiency tradeoff, as the controller adopts safer, slightly longer trajectories to avoid violations.

**B. Planar 2R Manipulator**:
- **Setup**: A 2R manipulator operating under multiple simultaneous mismatch sources: mismatched inertial parameters between nominal and true models, first-order actuator lag, torque saturation, and measurement noise. The goal was to reach a target position while avoiding a circular obstacle with its links.
- **Performance Comparison (over 50 trials)**:
 - **Success Rate**: Vanilla MPPI succeeded in 56% of trials, whereas RC-MPPI achieved a 96% success rate.
 - **Safety Margin**: Vanilla MPPI showed a mean minimum link clearance of approximately -0.00 m (often touching or slightly penetrating the obstacle). RC-MPPI consistently maintained a positive minimum clearance of 0.02 m.
 - **Constraint Violations**: Violation steps decreased substantially from 3.08 for Vanilla MPPI to 0.10 for RC-MPPI.
 - **Efficiency Metrics**: In this more complex scenario, RC-MPPI also demonstrated improved task efficiency. Time-to-goal significantly decreased (100.56 to 26.24 steps), end-effector path length reduced (5.29 to 3.97 m), and control energy decreased (3681.03 to 1490.88). This suggests that by actively avoiding unsafe regions, RC-MPPI leads to more direct and efficient paths, reducing the need for costly recovery maneuvers.

**Overall Simulation Conclusion**:
Across both simulation environments, RC-MPPI consistently demonstrated systematic improvements in safety margin, success rate, and reduction of constraint violation steps compared to vanilla MPPI. These improvements were achieved under significant and varied model-plant mismatch conditions, supporting the effectiveness of the residual-adaptive conservatism mechanisms. The results also suggest that, particularly in complex scenarios, the enhanced safety can indirectly lead to improved task efficiency by guiding the controller toward more robust and direct trajectories.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Residual-Conservative MPPI (RC-MPPI) framework represents a significant contribution to the field of safe and robust control for systems operating under model-plant mismatch. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Safety and Robustness**: The core contribution is the provision of an online, adaptive mechanism to increase safety conservatism in sampling-based MPC methods. By dynamically adjusting constraint tightening, penalty scaling, and sampling characteristics (exploration and temperature) based on real-time prediction–execution residuals, RC-MPPI offers a more robust control strategy for systems operating with imperfect models. This directly addresses a critical vulnerability of traditional MPPI in dynamic and uncertain environments.

2. **Lightweight Adaptation**: Unlike methods that require complex real-time parameter identification, detailed disturbance modeling, or full belief-space propagation, RC-MPPI uses the easily measurable prediction–execution residual as a lightweight indicator of mismatch. This makes the approach computationally efficient and potentially suitable for deployment on low-cost robotic and embedded platforms with limited processing capabilities, where sophisticated model learning or uncertainty quantification might be infeasible.

3. **Theoretical Justification for Adaptive Temperature**: The reinterpretation of the MPPI temperature as an epistemic parameter—encoding confidence in rollout cost evaluations—is a novel conceptual contribution. The theoretical analysis rigorously justifies the strategy of increasing temperature under high mismatch, demonstrating that this reduces the distorting effects of unreliable cost rankings on the importance weights. This provides deeper insight into the role of temperature in information-theoretic control under uncertainty.

4. **Probabilistic Safety Guarantees**: The derivation of probabilistic bounds on constraint violation, demonstrating a monotonic reduction in violation probability with increasing residual, strengthens the theoretical foundation of RC-MPPI. These guarantees are crucial for developing trust in autonomous systems, particularly in safety-critical applications.

5. **Improved Performance in Realistic Scenarios**: The simulation studies on an LTI point-mass system and a planar 2R manipulator, incorporating various sources of model-plant mismatch (actuator lag, saturation, inertial parameter mismatch, measurement noise), empirically validate the theoretical claims. RC-MPPI consistently improved success rates, minimum clearances, and significantly reduced constraint violation steps compared to vanilla MPPI. In some cases, the enhanced safety also led to improved control efficiency, by enabling the controller to avoid costly recovery maneuvers from near-unsafe states.

6. **Broader Applicability**: The framework’s ability to handle systematic model errors (like actuator lag or mismatched parameters) and stochastic disturbances concurrently, without distinguishing their sources directly, makes it broadly applicable to a range of robotic and autonomous systems where perfect models are rarely available.

**Potential Impact**:
The development of RC-MPPI can facilitate the more widespread and safer deployment of sampling-based MPC in real-world applications such as:
- **Autonomous Robotics**: Enabling robots to operate more reliably in unstructured or changing environments where their internal models may not perfectly match reality.
- **Industrial Control**: Enhancing the robustness of control systems in manufacturing and process control, where unmodeled dynamics or sensor degradation can occur.
- **Autonomous Driving**: Contributing to safer navigation and decision-making in complex and unpredictable traffic scenarios.
- **Aerospace Systems**: Providing an additional layer of safety for aerial vehicles operating under varying environmental conditions or actuator degradation.

Future research directions suggested by the authors include hardware validation on physical robotic platforms, integration with learned residual dynamics models (to potentially enhance predictive accuracy further), and extensions to belief-space and multi-agent sampling-based MPC formulations, indicating the potential for further advancement and broader applicability of this adaptive conservatism paradigm.
