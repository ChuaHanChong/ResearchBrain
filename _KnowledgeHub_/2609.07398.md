# OpenWAM: An Open, Modular Exploration Towards Systematic World-Action Model Pretraining

> [!summary] Summary
> OpenWAM is an open, modular stack for World-Action Model pretraining: interchangeable visual encoders, video/action stream backbones and visibility attention masks, one trainer, one policy server, and an eight-benchmark protocol. Controlled studies yield a recipe (capable video backbone, compact latent, dual-system joint self-attention, mutual visibility, synchronized denoising, one-stage ego+robot co-training) realized in OpenWAM-α, pretrained on 518.5M frames, about 6,400 hours.

## Problem

- Existing WAM systems are monolithic: backbone, representation, architecture, information flow, inference procedure and data recipe are tightly coupled, so no single design choice can be attributed.
- Prior work never isolates which cross-modal path creates world-action synergy; nominally joint world and action prediction may still lack an effective route from world features to actions.
- The WAM community lacks the open codebases and pretrained baselines VLA research already has, leaving cross-domain pretraining benefits and their persistence across embodiments unmeasured.

## Method

- **OpenWAM-Infra** factorizes a WAM as C(E,S,M): pluggable visual encoder, stream backbones (video, action, optional VLM), and a ==visibility attention mask==, composing six architectures across single-, dual- and tri-system families.
- A ==joint flow-matching objective== noises the video and action streams at independently sampled timesteps, so any inference path (sync, variance-shift, linear-offset) stays in-distribution; one policy server serves every checkpoint over WebSocket.
- **OpenWAM-α** couples a Wan2.2-TI2V-5B video ==DiT== with a 1B ActionDiT via ==joint self-attention== under ==mutual masking==, an 80-D unified action space, and one-stage ego+robot co-training.

## Results

- Controlled RoboTwin2.0 ablations: dual-system joint self-attention **92.36%** versus single-system vanilla **85.50%**; action-sees-video masking **92.39%** versus isolated **87.41%**; synchronized denoising **93.0%** beats every asynchronous schedule.
- Embodied pretraining lifts out-of-domain success **14.50%** to **26.62%** (**+12.12pp**) while in-domain gains only **+0.68pp**; DINOv3 latents rise **76.42%** to **90.18%** once S-VAE compresses them.
- OpenWAM-α scores **99.3** on LIBERO, sets EBench state of the art at **49.4**, tops real RoboDojo (**37.6** score / **24.4%** SR), averages **82.5%** on six Franka tasks, but lags at **69.2** on LIBERO-Plus.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Latent quality is not reconstructive versus semantic but compactness plus information richness; representation encoders become competitive once dimension-contracted to a DiT-friendly width.
> - Pixel-latent future prediction is inherently fragile under camera and sensor-noise perturbation; only large, viewpoint-diverse pretraining data masks that architectural weakness.
> - Video-latent supervision makes WAMs fit in-distribution better, while action-only VLAs generalize better by avoiding accumulating rollout error; richer data remedies either deficit.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied artificial intelligence systems require both an understanding of the world and the ability to act within it. Modern vision and vision-language models have advanced in learning semantic representations and predicting visual world evolution. World–Action Models (WAMs) build upon these advancements by inheriting generative priors over visual dynamics from video generation pretraining, then adapting this knowledge through embodied experience to produce executable control signals. This contrasts with Vision–Language–Action (VLA) models, which primarily leverage semantic and linguistic knowledge from vision-language pretraining.

A core hypothesis of WAMs is that world prediction and action generation can be learned synergistically. World modeling can provide structured knowledge of states, dynamics, and potential futures to inform action, while action learning can focus the model on changes relevant for control. However, existing WAM systems often exhibit a monolithic design, where components such as the generative backbone, visual representation, model architecture, information flow, inference procedure, and training data are tightly coupled. This coupling complicates the identification of which design choices are effective and why, hindering systematic research and development.

This work addresses the lack of a standardized and modular framework for WAM research, a gap previously filled for VLA models by open ecosystems like OpenVLA, StarVLA, and XPolicyLab. By introducing OpenWAM as an open research stack, the authors aim to transform WAM development from a collection of ad-hoc implementations into a controlled experimental program. This aligns with a broader trend in model design research that treats complex systems as empirical science, decomposing them into controlled variables to test mechanisms, derive recipes, and validate scalability. OpenWAM seeks to provide a principled foundation for understanding and scaling WAM pretraining.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the challenges associated with developing effective World–Action Models (WAMs). While WAMs offer a promising approach by leveraging video generative priors, their complex and often monolithic designs obscure the underlying mechanisms of their performance. This lack of clarity makes it difficult to ascertain which components contribute to knowledge transfer, how world and action learning interact, and whether advantages are sustained across different domains.

The work identifies three central questions that guide its investigation:
1. **What world knowledge should a WAM inherit?** This question focuses on understanding the most effective ways for a WAM to leverage existing knowledge about visual dynamics and representations, particularly from video generation models.
2. **How can we create synergy between world and action learning?** This objective aims to uncover design principles that foster productive interaction between the world modeling and action generation components, rather than merely combining them. It addresses architectural capacity, information flow during training, and inference procedures.
3. **How can this synergy be scaled across domains?** The research seeks to determine how the identified design principles translate to broader contexts, especially when training on diverse, heterogeneous data sources like egocentric human videos and robot trajectories, and how they impact generalization capabilities.

To address these questions, the authors developed OpenWAM, an open research stack designed to enable systematic and controlled experimentation. The motivation is to factorize the WAM design space into modular components, thereby facilitating a scientific approach to understanding and optimizing WAM pretraining. This modularity is intended to reduce the complexity of extending and building upon existing WAM systems, minimize interference between design choices during evaluation, and ultimately distill practical principles for building scalable and generalizable embodied foundation models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of OpenWAM is structured into three main components: OpenWAM-Infra, OpenWAM-Study, and OpenWAM-α.

#### 3.1. OpenWAM-Infra: Modular Infrastructure

OpenWAM-Infra provides a modular and unified framework for WAM development, decoupling model design from training, deployment, and evaluation.

##### 3.1.1. Composable Model

A WAM in OpenWAM-Infra is defined as a composition `C(E, S, M)` of three interchangeable module classes:
- **Visual Encoder (E):** Maps observations (images/videos) into latent sequences. Encoders are typically kept frozen to preserve pretraining benefits. Options include reconstructive encoders (Wan2.2-VAE, FLUX.2-VAE) and representation encoders (DINOv3, V-JEPA 2.1), with an optional S-VAE for latent dimension compression. The Wan2.2-VAE provides 4x temporal compression.
- **Stream Backbones (S):** Processes token streams for world (W), action (A), and optionally understanding (U). Each backbone has `prepare`, `per-layer block step`, and `finalize` stages.
 - **Video Backbone:** Five pretrained video generation models (Wan2.1-VACE-1.3B to Wan2.1-I2V-14B) are supported, providing temporal evolution prediction.
 - **Action Backbone:** Can be a separate ActionDiT or a shared video backbone where action tokens are co-processed.
 - **VLM Backbone (optional):** Currently supports the Qwen3-VL family for semantic understanding.
- **Visibility Attention Mask (M):** Governs information flow between streams during mixed self-attention. It comprises intra-modality blocks (first-frame causal for video, bidirectional for action) and cross-modality blocks defining four modes:
 - **Mutual:** Video and action streams attend to each other.
 - **Action Sees Video:** Action attends to video, video does not attend to action.
 - **Video Sees Action:** Video attends to action, action does not attend to video.
 - **Isolated:** No cross-stream attention.
- **Architectures:** The composition rule `C` assembles modules into six variants across three families:
 - **Single-System:** (Vanilla, MoE) – Action tokens integrated into the video sequence, processed by one transformer with shared parameters (Vanilla) or dedicated feed-forward experts (MoE).
 - **Dual-System:** (Joint Self-Attention, Joint Cross-Attention, IDM) – Independent video and action backbones with separate parameters, communicating via self-attention, cross-attention (with optional detached gradients), or an inverse-dynamics model.
 - **Tri-System:** (Joint Self-Attention) – Extends dual-system with a VLM backbone for understanding.

##### 3.1.2. Training Runtime

All architectures are trained with a unified flow-matching objective. Video and action streams are independently noised to `t_v` and `t_a` respectively. The objective minimizes the difference between predicted and actual velocities for both video latents and action chunks, weighted by `lambda_v`, `lambda_a`, and `w(.)` (timestep weight). Training utilities include DeepSpeed ZeRO, mixed precision, gradient accumulation, gradient clipping, and workflows for pretraining, fine-tuning, and resuming. Checkpoints are self-contained, storing weights, configuration, and normalization statistics.

##### 3.1.3. Deployment Runtime

A single policy server deploys any checkpoint, with orthogonal control over inference mode and denoising schedule.
- **Inference Modes:** Synchronous (server blocks for inference latency) or Asynchronous (prefetches next chunk in background).
- **Denoising Schedules:** Path through the joint noise plane.
 - **Sync:** Both streams denoise in lockstep along the diagonal.
 - **Variance Shift:** Leads one stream to reach clean data earlier.
 - **Linear Offset:** Holds one stream at pure noise until global progress.
- **Acceleration:** Prompt-embedding cache, video-decode skip, `torch.compile` for joint denoising loop, and DiT velocity cache (reusing stable predictions).

##### 3.1.4. Evaluation Protocol

Benchmarks connect as thin clients to the policy server via WebSocket. Observations are canonicalized, proprioceptive states are mapped into a model-side action representation, and predicted actions are denormalized to native physical units.
- **Benchmark Suite:** Eight simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, VLABench, RoboTwin2.0, RoboDojo, RoboCasa365, RoboCasa-GR1, EBench) covering single-arm, bimanual, mobile, and dexterous-hand manipulation. Real-robot platforms use the same interface.
- **Action Space:** Benchmarks can use their native action space, or a unified 80-D action space with fixed slot semantics (e.g., end-effector, gripper, dexterous hand, reserved slots) for cross-embodiment training.

#### 3.2. OpenWAM-Study: Design Principles Investigation

OpenWAM-Study conducts controlled experiments on RoboTwin2.0 (Full and Clean2Random settings for in-domain and out-of-domain evaluation) to derive design principles.

- **Inheriting Upstream World Knowledge (Q1):**
 - **Generative World Priors:** Compared four video backbones (1.3B to 14B parameters).
 - **Visual Representation Priors:** Evaluated reconstructive (Wan2.2-VAE, FLUX.2-VAE) and representation encoders (DINOv3, V-JEPA 2.1), with and without S-VAE for dimension compression.
- **Building Synergy between World and Action Learning (Q2):**
 - **Architectural Capacity:** Compared six architectural variants (Single-System, Dual-System, Tri-System).
 - **Training-Time Information Flow:** Ablated four attention mask modes (Isolated, Video Sees Action, Action Sees Video, Mutual).
 - **Inference-Time Information Flow:** Tested sync, variance-shift, and linear-offset denoising schedules.
- **Consolidating Knowledge Across Domains (Q3):**
 - **Pretraining Data Mixture:** Compared from-scratch, robot-only, ego then robot, and ego + robot co-train strategies on a 600-hour budget of EgoDex (egocentric human video) and RoboCOIN (real-robot data).
 - **Revisiting Information Flow:** Re-evaluated attention masks after embodied pretraining.

#### 3.3. OpenWAM-α: Pretrained Model Instantiation

OpenWAM-α instantiates the distilled design principles at scale.

- **Architecture:** Dual-system joint self-attention, frozen Wan2.2-VAE encoder, pretrained Wan2.2-TI2V-5B video DiT, dedicated 1B-parameter ActionDiT, and mutual visibility mask. Conditioned on current observation, language instruction, and 80-D proprioceptive state.
- **Training:** One-stage co-training on egocentric human and robot data. Unified 80-D action space with validity masking. Joint flow-matching objective (`λ_v = λ_a = 1`), bell-shaped timestep weight, independent timestep sampling (`ρ = 5`).
- **Deployment:** Synchronized denoising (10 steps) with `ρ = 5` warp. Synchronous inference mode. Accelerated with `torch.compile`, velocity cache, prompt-embedding cache, and VAE decode skip.
- **Data:** Pretrained on 518.5M frames (approximately 6,369 hours) from five sources: egocentric human video (30%), real-world robot data (AgiBotWorld-Beta, RoboCOIN, DROID – 40%), and synthetic robot data (InternData-A1 – 30%). Comprehensive vision-level and signal-level data curation applied.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings from the OpenWAM-Study and demonstrated the performance of OpenWAM-α across various benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Findings from OpenWAM-Study

- **Finding 1: Inheriting Upstream World Knowledge.**
 - **Generative World Priors:** Performance of WAMs consistently improved with larger video generation backbones. The Wan2.1-I2V-14B backbone yielded the highest average success rate, with Wan2.2-TI2V-5B (5B parameters) trailing by a marginal 1.40 percentage points while offering better efficiency.
 - **Visual Representation Priors:** Representation encoders (DINOv3, V-JEPA 2.1) could achieve performance comparable to or better than reconstructive encoders when combined with an S-VAE adapter for dimension contraction. The quality of a WAM latent space was found to depend on its compactness and information richness, rather than solely on the encoder category.
- **Finding 2: Building Synergy between World and Action Learning.**
 - **Architectural Capacity:** Performance increased with architectural capacity, from single- to dual- and tri-system architectures. Dual-system joint self-attention was identified as a strong variant, with the tri-system model achieving the highest overall performance.
 - **Training-Time Information Flow:** Explicit world-to-action information flow was found to be necessary for action learning. Masks enabling action to see video (Action Sees Video, Mutual) significantly outperformed those without this flow (Isolated, Video Sees Action) by approximately five percentage points.
 - **Inference-Time Information Flow:** Synchronized denoising consistently outperformed all tested asynchronous schedules, suggesting that explicit priors through denoising schedules do not yield gains in this context.
- **Finding 3: Consolidating Knowledge Across Domains.**
 - **Embodied Pretraining Impact:** Embodied pretraining primarily improved out-of-domain (OOD) generalization, showing modest gains in in-domain performance but substantial gains (e.g., +12.12pp on RoboTwin2.0-Clean2Random OOD) in OOD evaluation.
 - **Data Sources:** Robot-only pretraining resulted in the strongest in-domain performance, while mixed strategies (egocentric + robot data) led to better OOD generalization.
 - **Mixed Data Training:** Sequential training (ego then robot) and one-stage co-training yielded similar performance, with co-training being marginally stronger and removing an extra curriculum transition.
 - **Information Flow with Pretraining:** After embodied pretraining, mutual world-action visibility (where both streams attend to each other) was consistently preferred over one-way visibility, indicating that pretraining enables genuinely bidirectional interaction.

#### 4.2. OpenWAM-α Performance

OpenWAM-α, built on the principles derived from OpenWAM-Study, was evaluated on eight simulation benchmarks and real-robot platforms.

- **Simulation Benchmark Evaluation:**
 - **General Performance:** OpenWAM-α demonstrated excellent performance, positioning itself in the top tier on most single-arm (LIBERO, VLABench), bimanual (RoboTwin2.0-Full), mobile single-arm (RoboCasa365), and dexterous-hand (RoboCasa-GR1) benchmarks. It established a new state-of-the-art on the EBench mobile bimanual benchmark. For OOD generalization benchmarks (RoboTwin2.0-Clean2Random, RoboDojo), OpenWAM-α was the strongest WAM, though some VLA models achieved higher scores.
 - **LIBERO-Plus Anomaly:** Performance on LIBERO-Plus was notably lower, particularly under camera and noise perturbations. This was attributed to a less diverse and smaller volume of single-arm pretraining data in OpenWAM-α's mixture compared to top-performing baselines, and the inherent sensitivity of pixel-level future prediction architectures to visual disturbances.
- **Real-Robot Evaluation:**
 - **Single-Arm (Franka-Research-3):** OpenWAM-α surpassed baselines (LingBot-VA, π0.5) on a majority of tasks and achieved the best average success rate across stacking, pick-and-place, and hanging tasks, indicating general and fine-grained manipulation capabilities.
 - **Bimanual (RoboDojo Real-World):** OpenWAM-α led the leaderboard, maintaining strong and consistent performance across three bimanual embodiments (ARX X5, Piper, Piper X) and a range of generalization, precision, long-horizon, memory, and open tasks.
 - **Dexterous-Hand (Wuji-hand + Tianji-arm):** Despite the platform and its 21-DOF dexterous-hand action space being absent from OpenWAM-α's pretraining mixture, the model adapted effectively, outperforming the π0.5 baseline across all tasks and setups (Stack Toy Tower, Collect Shuttlecocks, Twist off Bottle Cap, Put Away Clothes), demonstrating generalization to entirely unseen embodiments.

#### 4.3. VLA vs. WAM Paradigms

Comparison of VLA and WAM models revealed distinct strengths:

- **In-Distribution Fitting:** WAMs generally exhibited an edge in fitting in-distribution data, attributed to the additional video-latent supervision signal during training.
- **Out-of-Distribution Generalization:** VLAs demonstrated better generalization out-of-distribution. WAMs' long-horizon future prediction, while beneficial for fitting, could lead to heavier error accumulation under distribution shift, a burden not carried by action-only VLAs.
- **Data Impact:** Both paradigms' deficits (VLA's ID fitting, WAM's OOD generalization) could be compensated by sufficiently large and diverse pretraining data, highlighting data as a primary factor in model development.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces OpenWAM, an open research stack designed to enable systematic and controlled experimentation in World–Action Model (WAM) pretraining. By factorizing the WAM design space into modular components with unified training, deployment, and evaluation protocols, OpenWAM-Infra provides a substrate for scientific inquiry, addressing the prior monolithic nature of WAM systems. This modularity is intended to facilitate reproducibility, extensibility, and comparative analysis within the WAM research community.

The OpenWAM-Study component systematically investigated key design questions, distilling a set of evidence-backed principles for WAM development. These principles cover effective inheritance of upstream world knowledge, methods for building synergy between world and action learning, and strategies for consolidating knowledge across diverse domains. The findings, such as the importance of capable generative backbones, compact visual representations, explicit world-to-action information flow during training, synchronized denoising during inference, and the benefits of one-stage co-training with egocentric and robot data, offer a concrete recipe for WAM construction.

The instantiation of these principles in OpenWAM-α resulted in a pretrained WAM that demonstrated consistently strong performance across a range of eight simulation benchmarks and real-robot experiments spanning single-arm, bimanual, and dexterous-hand manipulation. OpenWAM-α not only achieved competitive results, including state-of-the-art performance on some benchmarks, but also exhibited reliable adaptation to novel embodiments not seen during pretraining.

The release of the full OpenWAM stack, including infrastructure, evaluation protocols, pretrained models, and data recipes, is a significant contribution to the field. It aims to democratize WAM research by providing a shared, reproducible foundation for future development, fostering a more scientific understanding of embodied foundation models. The insights gained regarding the complementary strengths of WAMs and VLAs also highlight promising directions for future research, suggesting that integrating the best aspects of both paradigms could further advance the capabilities of end-to-end models. The identified limitations, such as the need for more diverse embodied data and robust visual representations, also outline critical areas for future investigation.
