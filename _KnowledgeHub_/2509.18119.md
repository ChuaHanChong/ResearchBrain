# MobileRL: Online Agentic Reinforcement Learning for Mobile GUI Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> The MOBILERL framework introduces an online agentic reinforcement learning approach for mobile GUI agents, significantly improving performance on interactive mobile benchmarks. It achieves an 80.2% success rate on AndroidWorld and 53.6% on AndroidLab, setting new state-of-the-art results through adaptive learning strategies.

## Problem

- Existing mobile GUI agents, primarily based on supervised fine-tuning or offline imitation learning, suffer from limited behavior coverage and poor error recovery in dynamic environments.
- Reinforcement learning for mobile GUI agents is hindered by sparse and delayed rewards in multi-turn tasks, along with heavy-tailed task difficulty distributions.
- Scalable online RL training for mobile GUI agents faces significant sampling bottlenecks due to the computational cost and resource intensity of interactive mobile simulators.

## Method

- Employs a ==multi-stage training== framework combining Reasoning-Free Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Reasoning SFT for transparent policies, and online reinforcement learning.
- Introduces Difficulty-Adaptive Group Relative Policy Optimization (ADAGRPO), which includes Shortest-Path Reward Adjustment (SPA) for denser rewards, Difficulty-Adaptive Positive Replay (AdaPR) for leveraging valuable trajectories, and Failure Curriculum Filtering (FCF) for efficient task sampling.
- Implements a distributed sampling infrastructure with hundreds of Dockerized Android Virtual Devices to overcome mobile emulation bottlenecks and enable large-scale, reproducible online RL training.

## Results

- MOBILERL-**9B** achieved state-of-the-art success rates of **80.2%** on AndroidWorld and **53.6%** on AndroidLab, with the **7B** parameter version outperforming larger **72B** models by up to **16%** on AndroidWorld.
- Ablation studies confirmed that each stage (Reasoning-Free SFT, Reasoning SFT) and ADAGRPO component (SPA, AdaPR, FCF) incrementally improves performance, with the ADAGRPO stage alone contributing over **10%** to AndroidWorld success rates.
- The framework, particularly with Shortest-Path Reward Adjustment (SPA), demonstrated enhanced step efficiency, leading to shorter and more optimal task completion trajectories across various difficulty levels.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Online agentic reinforcement learning, when specifically adapted to mobile GUI challenges, yields substantial performance improvements over supervised or offline learning paradigms.
> - Strategies such as Shortest-Path Reward Adjustment, Difficulty-Adaptive Positive Replay, and Failure Curriculum Filtering are essential for stable, sample-efficient, and robust training in sparse-reward mobile environments.
> - A multi-stage training approach, starting with reasoning-free and then reasoning-augmented supervised fine-tuning, provides a crucial initialization that mitigates the high cost of exploration in interactive mobile environments.
