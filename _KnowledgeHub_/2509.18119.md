# MobileRL: Online Agentic Reinforcement Learning for Mobile GUI Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> The MOBILERL framework introduces an online agentic reinforcement learning approach for mobile GUI agents, significantly improving performance on interactive mobile benchmarks. It achieves an 80.2% success rate on AndroidWorld and 53.6% on AndroidLab, setting new state-of-the-art results through adaptive learning strategies.

## Problem

- Existing mobile GUI agents, primarily based on supervised fine-tuning or offline imitation learning, suffer from limited behavior coverage and poor error recovery in dynamic environments.
- Reinforcement learning for mobile GUI agents is hindered by sparse and delayed rewards in multi-turn tasks, along with heavy-tailed task difficulty distributions.
- Scalable online RL training for mobile GUI agents faces significant sampling bottlenecks due to the computational cost and resource intensity of interactive mobile simulators.

## Method

- Employs a ==multi-stage training== framework combining Reasoning-Free Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Reasoning SFT for transparent policies, and online reinforcement learning.
- Introduces Difficulty-Adaptive Group Relative Policy Optimization (ADAGRPO), which includes Shortest-Path Reward Adjustment (SPA) for denser rewards, Difficulty-Adaptive Positive Replay (AdaPR) for leveraging valuable trajectories, and Failure Curriculum Filtering (FCF) for efficient task sampling.
- Implements a distributed sampling infrastructure with hundreds of Dockerized Android Virtual Devices to overcome mobile emulation bottlenecks and enable large-scale, reproducible online RL training.

## Results

- MOBILERL-**9B** achieved state-of-the-art success rates of **80.2%** on AndroidWorld and **53.6%** on AndroidLab, with the **7B** parameter version outperforming larger **72B** models by up to **16%** on AndroidWorld.
- Ablation studies confirmed that each stage (Reasoning-Free SFT, Reasoning SFT) and ADAGRPO component (SPA, AdaPR, FCF) incrementally improves performance, with the ADAGRPO stage alone contributing over **10%** to AndroidWorld success rates.
- The framework, particularly with Shortest-Path Reward Adjustment (SPA), demonstrated enhanced step efficiency, leading to shorter and more optimal task completion trajectories across various difficulty levels.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Online agentic reinforcement learning, when specifically adapted to mobile GUI challenges, yields substantial performance improvements over supervised or offline learning paradigms.
> - Strategies such as Shortest-Path Reward Adjustment, Difficulty-Adaptive Positive Replay, and Failure Curriculum Filtering are essential for stable, sample-efficient, and robust training in sparse-reward mobile environments.
> - A multi-stage training approach, starting with reasoning-free and then reasoning-augmented supervised fine-tuning, provides a crucial initialization that mitigates the high cost of exploration in interactive mobile environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of general-purpose graphical user interface (GUI) agents, particularly for mobile environments, has gained significant traction with the advancements in vision-language models (VLMs). These agents hold the promise of enabling zero-shot interaction with digital interfaces, mirroring human-like operation.

Historically, efforts to improve GUI agents have predominantly focused on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or offline imitation learning. These methods rely on static expert demonstrations to teach agents how to perform tasks. While foundational, this approach inherently suffers from limitations such as restricted behavior coverage—meaning agents can only learn actions explicitly shown in demonstrations—and poor error recovery capabilities when encountering novel situations or deviations from learned trajectories.

Reinforcement learning (RL) offers a compelling alternative due to its ability to learn from verifiable rewards in interactive environments, thereby fostering adaptability and robust error recovery. However, applying RL to GUI agents, especially in complex mobile settings, presents several significant challenges:
- **Data Scarcity for Agentic Tasks:** Existing datasets often provide only single-step expert labels, which are insufficient for training or evaluating policies that require multi-step planning and reasoning (agentic tasks).
- **Sparse and Delayed Rewards:** In multi-turn mobile interactions, positive feedback (rewards) is often only available upon successful task completion, making it difficult for an agent to learn effective intermediate actions.
- **Heavy-tailed Task Difficulty:** Real-world mobile tasks exhibit a wide spectrum of difficulty. Some tasks are easily solvable, while others may be persistently intractable for the agent, leading to inefficient resource allocation during training if all tasks are treated equally.
- **Sampling Bottlenecks:** Interactive mobile simulators and emulators are computationally expensive and resource-intensive, making large-scale data sampling for online RL prohibitively slow and difficult to reproduce across different setups.

Early progress has been made in online learning for GUI agents, but efficient scaling of *agentic RL* in *interactive mobile simulators* for complex, multi-turn tasks remains an underexplored area.

MOBILERL directly addresses these critical gaps and challenges. It positions itself as an adaptive online agentic RL framework specifically designed for mobile GUI agents. By tackling the issues of sparse rewards, varied task difficulty, and sampling bottlenecks head-on, MOBILERL advances the field beyond the limitations of purely supervised or offline learning. It provides a more robust and scalable solution for training agents that can learn to navigate and interact with diverse mobile applications in a truly autonomous and efficient manner, pushing the frontier of general-purpose mobile AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the MOBILERL research is to develop an effective and robust reinforcement learning framework for building general-purpose GUI agents capable of operating in complex mobile environments. This core objective is driven by the recognition of significant limitations in existing approaches and a desire to overcome the unique technical challenges inherent in mobile GUI automation.

The key motivations and specific objectives can be summarized as follows:

- **Overcome Limitations of Supervised Learning:** The primary motivation stems from the inherent weaknesses of supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and offline imitation learning, which include limited behavior coverage and poor error recovery. The authors aim to leverage reinforcement learning to enable agents to learn more adaptable and robust policies through interactive experience and verifiable rewards.
- **Address Sparse and Delayed Rewards in Multi-Turn Tasks:** Mobile GUI tasks are often multi-step, with rewards only provided at the final completion. This sparsity makes learning challenging. A key objective is to design a reward mechanism that provides denser, more informative signals to guide the agent towards efficient task completion.
- **Handle Heavy-tailed and Unstable Task Difficulty:** Recognizing that mobile tasks vary greatly in difficulty, the authors aim to develop strategies that adapt the learning process to instance difficulty. This is motivated by the inefficiency of uniform sampling, which wastes computational resources on persistently unsolvable tasks or over-samples easy ones.
- **Mitigate Sampling Bottlenecks in Mobile Emulators:** Deploying and managing numerous concurrent mobile instances for large-scale RL training is resource-intensive and slow. A crucial objective is to develop a scalable and high-throughput sampling infrastructure that can orchestrate many Android virtual devices efficiently, ensuring reproducibility.
- **Improve Complex Instruction Following and Agent Reasoning:** Base vision-language models (VLMs) often struggle with complex, GUI-specific instructions and may produce opaque action sequences. The motivation here is to provide a strong, reasoning-aware policy initializer that reduces costly exploratory trials in the mobile environment and makes the agent's decision-making more transparent.
- **Maximize Task Success and Efficiency:** Ultimately, the framework aims to learn policies that achieve high success rates across diverse mobile applications and tasks, while also minimizing unnecessary interactions (i.e., finding shorter, more efficient completion paths).

In essence, MOBILERL is motivated by the need for a more intelligent, adaptive, and efficient learning paradigm for mobile GUI agents, moving beyond passive imitation to active, self-improving agents capable of robust performance in real-world mobile interactions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MOBILERL framework is meticulously designed to address the aforementioned challenges, employing a multi-stage training process and a novel RL algorithm. It comprises three main components: Reasoning-Free Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Reasoning SFT, and online agentic Reinforcement Learning with Difficulty-Adaptive Group Relative Policy Optimization (ADAGRPO).

#### 3.1. The MOBILERL Framework Stages

1. **Reasoning-Free SFT:** This initial stage serves as a warm-up. Directly starting online RL from a base model is inefficient due to the cost and latency of mobile emulation. Therefore, the agent is fine-tuned on expert demonstration data (from human annotations and the AndroidControl dataset) that provides correct action sequences without explicit intermediate reasoning. This builds a strong foundational policy for basic action execution.

2. **Reasoning SFT:** To further enhance the policy and improve instruction following, this stage introduces intermediate reasoning. Manually collected expert demonstrations often lack detailed rationales. The framework iteratively bootstraps a reasoning-augmented training set by:
 - **Bootstrap sampling:** An instruction model (M) generates diverse candidate reasoning-action pairs; those matching expert actions are retained.
 - **Supervised fine-tuning:** An initial reasoning policy (π_R_0) is trained on this dataset.
 - **Iterative refinement:** The policy proposes new candidates, scores those matching expert actions, and adds the best explanations to the training set for further fine-tuning. This process yields a more structured and transparent policy initialization.

3. **Agentic RL with Difficulty–Adaptive Group Relative Policy Optimization (ADAGRPO):** Building upon the warmed-up policy, this is the core online learning stage. ADAGRPO extends Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which itself is an advancement of PPO, by using an on-the-fly group-relative baseline for advantage calculation, derived from multiple trajectories for the same task. ADAGRPO introduces three key strategies:

 - **Shortest-Path Reward Adjustment (SPA):** In multi-turn mobile tasks, terminal binary rewards are sparse. SPA reshapes these rewards to incentivize shorter, more efficient successful trajectories. Instead of a uniform reward for all successes, SPA assigns higher returns to shorter solutions by re-scaling the reward based on the trajectory length relative to the shortest successful path for that instance. This counteracts bias towards verbose actions and aligns with user preferences for efficiency.
 - $R_{SPA}(s_t, a_t) = r(\tau_i) \cdot (1 - \alpha \frac{T_i - T_{min}}{T_i})$, where $T_i$ is trajectory length, $T_{min}$ is the shortest successful trajectory length, and $\alpha$ controls penalty strength.

 - **Difficulty-Adaptive Positive Replay (AdaPR):** Mobile environments yield rare but highly informative successful yet challenging rollouts. AdaPR strategically retains and reuses these high-value trajectories.
 - **Buffer Construction:** A curated replay buffer stores the top κ trajectories based on their trajectory-level advantage, prioritizing challenging successes.
 - **Mixture Sampling:** Policy updates are performed on mini-batches comprising a mixture of trajectories: a portion (γ) drawn from the replay buffer (focusing on high-advantage samples) and the remaining from fresh on-policy samples. This balances exploiting valuable past experiences with exploring new behaviors.

 - **Failure Curriculum Filtering (FCF):** To prevent computational waste on persistently unsolvable tasks, FCF dynamically filters out such instances. If a task yields all-zero rewards for two consecutive epochs, its sampling probability is reduced for a three-epoch cooldown period, after which it is permanently removed from the sampling pool. This reallocates computational budget to feasible instances, improving sample efficiency and retaining signal from recoverable failures.

#### 3.2. Scalable Sampling Infrastructure

MOBILERL implements a distributed sampling framework based on Verl (Sheng et al., 2024). This infrastructure is crucial for overcoming the sampling bottlenecks of mobile environments:
- **Concurrent Execution:** It orchestrates hundreds of Dockerized Android Virtual Devices (AVDs) across multiple machines, supporting true concurrent execution.
- **High Throughput:** The system can interact with over 1,000 environments simultaneously, providing the necessary scale for online RL training.
- **Reproducibility:** The Dockerized AVDs ensure consistent environment behaviors, which is critical for reproducible research.

#### 3.3. UI Representation and Action Space

- **XML Preprocessing:** The framework processes raw Android XML UI hierarchy data to remove off-screen and redundant structural nodes, simplifying attributes (e.g., keeping only `True` boolean values, retaining only the last component of class names) to create a more compact and semantically relevant UI representation for the VLM.
- **Action Space:** A concise set of atomic GUI operations is defined, including `Tap`, `Swipe`, `Type`, `Long Press`, `Launch`, `Home`, `Back`, `Wait`, and a terminating `Finish` action.

#### 3.4. Reward Model for AndroidLab

For the AndroidLab benchmark, which lacks rule-based rewards for its training data, the authors developed a VLM-based reward model. This model is fine-tuned using binary scores assigned by strong proprietary VLMs to executed traces, providing a learnable reward signal for policy optimization in this environment.

#### 3.5. Additional Implementation Details

- **Pruning Negative Trajectories:** To stabilize training and encourage exploration, trajectories with persistently low advantages are pruned from the RL buffer, preventing detrimental gradients and spreading probability mass over a broader action space.
- **Image Resolution:** For optimal performance, especially with GLM-4.1V-9B-Base, full-resolution images are used, as compression was found to negatively impact accuracy, particularly in detail-oriented tasks.

This comprehensive methodology, integrating staged SFT with an adaptive RL algorithm and a robust distributed sampling infrastructure, forms the backbone of MOBILERL's success in addressing the complex challenges of mobile GUI agent learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of MOBILERL demonstrates its significant advancements in training mobile GUI agents, setting new state-of-the-art benchmarks.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance

- **Overall Success Rates:** MOBILERL significantly outperforms both open-source and proprietary models on two interactive mobile benchmarks: AndroidWorld and AndroidLab.
 - **MOBILERL-9B** (using GLM-4.1V-9B-Base as backbone) achieved an impressive 80.2% success rate on AndroidWorld and 53.6% on AndroidLab. This represents a substantial improvement over previous state-of-the-art results (64.2% on AndroidWorld and 41.2% on AndroidLab).
 - **MOBILERL-7B** (using Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct as backbone) also showed strong performance with 72.0% on AndroidWorld and 42.5% on AndroidLab. Notably, despite being substantially smaller (7B parameters), it outperformed much larger 72B-parameter models like UI-Tars-1.5 and UI-Genie-Agent (e.g., +16% on AndroidWorld).

#### 4.2. Framework Ablation Study (Incremental Gains)

The ablation study confirmed the incremental value of each stage of the MOBILERL framework:
- **Reasoning-Free SFT:** Provided a strong initial performance lift, demonstrating its effectiveness in building a foundational action policy. For Qwen2.5-VL-7B, it yielded a +22.6% success rate on AndroidWorld.
- **Reasoning SFT:** Further improved performance by integrating intermediate reasoning, enhancing instruction following and policy transparency. This stage added +6.6% on AndroidWorld for Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
- **ADAGRPO (RL Stage):** Built upon the strong SFT foundation, the final ADAGRPO stage delivered substantial additional gains, exceeding 10% on AndroidWorld and over 5% on AndroidLab, underscoring the power of online agentic RL when properly adapted.
- **Necessity of Reasoning-Free SFT:** Experiments showed that even with subsequent reasoning stages, the Reasoning-Free SFT consistently improved final performance, affirming its role in establishing a robust initial action foundation.

#### 4.3. ADAGRPO Algorithm Ablation Study

Detailed ablations on the ADAGRPO components (AdaPR, SPA, FCF) using the Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct backbone demonstrated their individual and combined effectiveness:
- **All Components are Crucial:** Removing any component or combination of components led to a drop in performance, both in training trajectory-level rewards and final success rates on the AndroidWorld test set.
- **Failure Curriculum Filtering (FCF):** Was critical for efficient training. Without FCF, early sampling was biased towards overly hard tasks, leading to numerous negatives and a lower reward ceiling. The "w/o FCF" curve suggested that without this, more training steps or a more efficient pipeline would be needed to reach comparable performance.
- **AdaPR and SPA for Stability:** The absence of both AdaPR and SPA resulted in training instability, with performance collapsing after approximately 30 steps, indicating their necessity for robust learning in sparse-reward environments.
- **Effect of AdaPR:** Once the replay buffer was populated (around 7 steps), a clear performance gap emerged between the full MOBILERL and the "w/o AdaPR" variant, highlighting the benefit of replaying challenging successful trajectories.
- **Effect of SPA:** Noticeable gains from SPA appeared later in training (after about 60 steps), suggesting its role in refining trajectories towards shorter, more efficient paths and counteracting biases towards longer action sequences.

#### 4.4. Performance by Task Complexity and Step Efficiency

- **Consistent Gains Across Complexity:** MOBILERL showed consistent performance improvements across all task complexity levels (Complexity 1 to 4+) on AndroidWorld. Critically, larger gains were observed on high-complexity tasks, aligning with AdaPR's design to handle heavy-tailed difficulty distributions. The post-RL pass@1 success rate even exceeded the pre-RL pass@8, indicating a significant boost in single-attempt performance.
- **SPA and Step Efficiency:** While SPA had a smaller impact on overall accuracy in ablations, its effect on step efficiency was significant. MOBILERL with SPA consistently completed tasks in fewer steps across all difficulty levels. When comparing scenarios where both models were correct, MOBILERL with SPA more frequently achieved shorter trajectories, proving its effectiveness in promoting efficient solutions.

#### 4.5. Performance on Offline Dataset (AndroidControl)

- The `MOBILERL-9B w/o RL` version (i.e., after SFT stages) surpassed all previous models on the AndroidControl offline dataset, achieving state-of-the-art results (95.9% Type Grounding SR for High-level tasks). The full RL version largely maintained this strong performance, demonstrating that the online RL stage enhances agentic capabilities without degrading performance on offline, supervised tasks.

In summary, the results unequivocally establish MOBILERL as a leading framework for mobile GUI agents, not only achieving superior success rates on complex interactive benchmarks but also demonstrating the crucial role of its adaptive RL components in enhancing learning stability, efficiency, and robustness across varied task difficulties.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MOBILERL represents a significant leap forward in the development of intelligent agents for mobile graphical user interfaces, offering several key contributions and potential impacts on both research and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Advancing the State-of-the-Art in Mobile GUI Agents

By achieving new state-of-the-art success rates on challenging interactive benchmarks like AndroidWorld and AndroidLab, MOBILERL demonstrates that online agentic reinforcement learning, when carefully designed, can substantially enhance the capabilities of VLM-based GUI agents. This work moves the field beyond the limitations of purely imitation learning or offline RL, which struggle with generalization and error recovery in dynamic environments.

#### 5.2. Robust and Efficient Online Learning Paradigm

The Difficulty-Adaptive Group Relative Policy Optimization (ADAGRPO) algorithm is a core contribution. Its three components—Shortest-Path Reward Adjustment (SPA), Difficulty-Adaptive Positive Replay (AdaPR), and Failure Curriculum Filtering (FCF)—collectively address critical challenges in online RL:
- **SPA** provides denser and more informative reward signals, guiding agents towards efficient and concise solutions, which is vital for user experience.
- **AdaPR** intelligently reuses rare but valuable successful trajectories, dramatically improving sample efficiency in sparse-reward environments.
- **FCF** dynamically prunes persistently unsolvable tasks, preventing wasted computational resources and focusing learning on feasible problems.
These strategies make online RL training more stable, sample-efficient, and effective for complex, multi-turn agentic tasks, setting a new standard for RL approaches in this domain.

#### 5.3. Scalable and Reproducible Training Infrastructure

The development of a distributed sampling framework capable of orchestrating hundreds of Dockerized Android Virtual Devices concurrently is a significant engineering achievement. This infrastructure addresses the long-standing bottleneck of resource-intensive mobile emulation, enabling large-scale online RL training that was previously difficult or impossible to achieve reproducibly. This contribution is crucial for future research in mobile GUI automation, providing a robust platform for experimentation.

#### 5.4. Practical Implications and Real-World Applications

The higher success rates and improved step efficiency demonstrated by MOBILERL have direct practical implications:
- **Enhanced Mobile AI Assistants:** More reliable and capable AI assistants that can perform complex, multi-step tasks across diverse mobile applications, from setting calendar events to ordering products, with greater accuracy and less user intervention.
- **Automated Mobile App Testing:** The framework could significantly accelerate and improve the automation of mobile application testing, allowing for more comprehensive and intelligent exploration of app functionalities.
- **Accessibility Features:** Improved GUI agents could lead to more sophisticated accessibility tools for users with disabilities, enabling them to interact with mobile devices more effectively.

#### 5.5. Open-Source Contribution

By open-sourcing the MOBILERL framework, the authors foster community engagement and accelerate further research and development in mobile GUI agent technology. This enables other researchers to build upon their work, test new ideas, and contribute to the collective advancement of the field.

#### 5.6. Identified Limitations and Future Directions

The authors are transparent about current limitations, which also chart important avenues for future research:
- **Reward Model Bias:** The reliance on a VLM-based reward model for AndroidLab introduces potential biases. Future work aims to develop more general and unbiased verification models that do not depend on proprietary VLMs or rule-based systems.
- **Public-Use App Evaluation:** The current work does not evaluate on out-of-domain public-use applications, which present challenges in reproducibility, login management, and state restoration. This is a critical area for ensuring real-world applicability.
- **XML Dependency:** Some popular applications lack comprehensive XML hierarchy data. Future efforts will explore adapting the models towards purely visual grounding, reducing reliance on XML data for greater generality.
- **Training Resource Consumption:** The high computational demands of Android virtual devices remain a barrier to further scaling. Innovations in more efficient simulation or distributed computing are needed.

In conclusion, MOBILERL not only pushes the empirical performance boundaries for mobile GUI agents but also introduces a principled and practical methodology for online agentic reinforcement learning in this complex domain. Its contributions in algorithm design, scalable infrastructure, and clear demonstration of efficacy lay a strong foundation for the next generation of intelligent mobile AI.
