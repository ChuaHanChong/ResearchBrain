# Hiking in the Wild: A Scalable Perceptive Parkour Framework for Humanoids

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and Shanghai Qi Zhi Institute developed a scalable perceptive parkour framework for humanoid robots, enabling robust traversal of complex, unstructured terrains at speeds up to 2.5 m/s through zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. The framework demonstrated high success rates across diverse environments like stairs, gaps, and high platforms, significantly improving foothold safety and locomotion agility.

## Problem

- Reactive proprioception-only locomotion policies limit humanoid robots from proactively avoiding hazards in complex, unstructured environments.
- Existing perceptive locomotion methods struggle with state estimation drift in mapping-based approaches or lack scalability and robustness in prior end-to-end visual policies.
- Challenges in ensuring safe foothold placement, mitigating reward hacking during training, and achieving reliable zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for dynamic humanoid tasks.

## Method

- A single-stage, end-to-end reinforcement learning policy (PPO) directly mapping raw depth inputs and proprioception to joint actions, avoiding intermediate mapping.
- Utilizing a highly realistic and efficient ego-centric depth simulation with bidirectional depth alignment, extensive noise modeling, and temporal depth aggregation for robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
- Incorporating a Terrain Edge Detector with Volumetric Point Penetration Penalization for safer foothold placement, and a position-based velocity command strategy with Flat Patch Sampling to mitigate reward hacking and promote goal-directed exploration.

## Results

- Achieved robust real-world traversal of diverse and challenging terrains (e.g., stairs, 32 cm platforms, 50 cm gaps, grassy slopes) with the Unitree G1 humanoid robot via zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
- Demonstrated high-speed locomotion agility, reaching a maximum running speed of 2.5 m/s, with high success rates across various terrains (e.g., 100% for large box, ramp, platform up/down, stair up, and gap traversal).
- The edge-aware penalization mechanism visibly improved foothold safety by encouraging feet placement away from terrain edges and generalized to novel terrain topologies without explicit engineering.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct perception-to-action learning, combined with carefully designed depth simulation and noise modeling, enables robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for highly dynamic humanoid locomotion.
> - Implicit, learning-based safety mechanisms, such as edge contact penalization, can effectively guide robots to safer footholds and generalize across diverse, unseen terrain types.
> - Integrating position-based velocity commands and adversarial motion priors is crucial for mitigating reward hacking and teaching natural, agile, and stable parkour behaviors for humanoids in complex environments.
