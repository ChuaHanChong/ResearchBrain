# VISD: Enhancing Video Reasoning via Structured Self-Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> VISD, a structured self-distillation framework, enhances video reasoning in Video Large Language Models by integrating diagnostically meaningful, token-level supervision. It improves spatio-temporal grounding and overall video understanding, leading to a +28.4 point increase in answer accuracy on V-STAR and nearly 2x faster training convergence.

## Problem

- VideoLLMs face challenges in aligning long reasoning trajectories with temporally evolving visual evidence, lacking fine-grained learning signals.
- Existing reinforcement learning rewards are sparse and sequence-level, providing insufficient token-level credit assignment for heterogeneous errors in video tasks.
- Current self-distillation methods offer unstructured or modality-agnostic token-level supervision, limiting diagnostic specificity and potentially conflicting with reinforcement learning.

## Method

- Introduced a ==video-aware judge model== to generate structured, diagnostically meaningful feedback on reasoning aspects like answer correctness, logical consistency, and spatio-temporal grounding.
- Developed a ==direction–magnitude decoupling== mechanism to stably integrate this structured token-level supervision, where sequence-level rewards determine update direction and structured signals modulate update magnitudes.
- Employed ==curriculum scheduling== for the mixing coefficient and an ==EMA-based teacher stabilization== strategy to ensure robust optimization, especially for long video sequences.

## Results

- Achieved the best overall V-STAR scores (mAM/mLGM improved from **34.3/47.5** to **35.1/48.9** over VisionCoach) and improved V-STAR answer accuracy by **+28.4 points** over Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
- Outperformed strong baselines on general video understanding benchmarks, with **+7.4** average points over Qwen2.5-VL-7B and **+1.8** over VisionCoach, especially on long-video reasoning and temporal grounding.
- Demonstrated nearly **2x faster** convergence in optimization steps, reaching stronger performance at approximately **1200 steps** compared to over **2300 steps** for baselines, indicating enhanced sample efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly structuring privileged information with a video-aware judge provides targeted feedback, allowing for more precise token-level credit assignment than generic self-distillation.
> - Decoupling update direction from magnitude prevents conflicts between sparse global rewards and dense structured supervision, leading to stable and efficient policy learning.
> - Combining curriculum scheduling and EMA stabilization for the teacher policy is critical for robustly integrating fine-grained feedback, particularly in long-horizon video reasoning.
