# VISD: Enhancing Video Reasoning via Structured Self-Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> VISD, a structured self-distillation framework, enhances video reasoning in Video Large Language Models by integrating diagnostically meaningful, token-level supervision. It improves spatio-temporal grounding and overall video understanding, leading to a +28.4 point increase in answer accuracy on V-STAR and nearly 2x faster training convergence.

## Problem

- VideoLLMs face challenges in aligning long reasoning trajectories with temporally evolving visual evidence, lacking fine-grained learning signals.
- Existing reinforcement learning rewards are sparse and sequence-level, providing insufficient token-level credit assignment for heterogeneous errors in video tasks.
- Current self-distillation methods offer unstructured or modality-agnostic token-level supervision, limiting diagnostic specificity and potentially conflicting with reinforcement learning.

## Method

- Introduced a ==video-aware judge model== to generate structured, diagnostically meaningful feedback on reasoning aspects like answer correctness, logical consistency, and spatio-temporal grounding.
- Developed a ==direction–magnitude decoupling== mechanism to stably integrate this structured token-level supervision, where sequence-level rewards determine update direction and structured signals modulate update magnitudes.
- Employed ==curriculum scheduling== for the mixing coefficient and an ==EMA-based teacher stabilization== strategy to ensure robust optimization, especially for long video sequences.

## Results

- Achieved the best overall V-STAR scores (mAM/mLGM improved from **34.3/47.5** to **35.1/48.9** over VisionCoach) and improved V-STAR answer accuracy by **+28.4 points** over Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
- Outperformed strong baselines on general video understanding benchmarks, with **+7.4** average points over Qwen2.5-VL-7B and **+1.8** over VisionCoach, especially on long-video reasoning and temporal grounding.
- Demonstrated nearly **2x faster** convergence in optimization steps, reaching stronger performance at approximately **1200 steps** compared to over **2300 steps** for baselines, indicating enhanced sample efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly structuring privileged information with a video-aware judge provides targeted feedback, allowing for more precise token-level credit assignment than generic self-distillation.
> - Decoupling update direction from magnitude prevents conflicts between sparse global rewards and dense structured supervision, leading to stable and efficient policy learning.
> - Combining curriculum scheduling and EMA stabilization for the teacher policy is critical for robustly integrating fine-grained feedback, particularly in long-horizon video reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses fundamental challenges in advancing Video Large Language Models (VideoLLMs) for complex multimodal reasoning. Recent progress in VideoLLMs has enabled models to process dynamic visual content and answer intricate questions. However, training these models for tasks requiring temporally grounded reasoning remains problematic. Unlike static image or text understanding, video reasoning necessitates aligning long reasoning trajectories with continuously evolving visual evidence, where both the correctness of the final answer and the faithfulness of intermediate reasoning steps are critical. This introduces a requirement for fine-grained guidance that can identify specific points of success or failure within a reasoning process.

Current approaches to training VideoLLMs primarily fall into two categories, each with limitations that VISD seeks to overcome:

1. **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** Methods like RLVR provide reliable, task-level supervision derived from ground-truth answers or external verifiers. While effective for establishing optimization directions, these rewards are inherently sparse and operate at the sequence level. Consequently, they often fail to capture the distinct contributions of individual tokens within lengthy reasoning chains, particularly when errors stem from subtle temporal misalignments or inaccurate visual grounding. This coarseness hinders efficient learning, especially in video contexts where a correct final answer might still be supported by flawed internal reasoning.

2. **Self-Distillation (SD) Methods:** An alternative involves using self-distillation to provide dense, token-level supervision. However, existing self-distillation techniques typically treat this additional supervision as an unstructured or modality-agnostic signal. When applied to VideoLLMs, this leads to two issues: first, the supervision lacks diagnostic specificity, making it difficult to differentiate between various types of reasoning errors (e.g., logical inconsistency versus grounding failure); second, the integration of such auxiliary signals with reinforcement learning can be unstable, potentially leading to conflicts or overrides of reward-driven optimization.

VISD positions itself as a framework that integrates the reliability of RLVR's policy update direction with the density of self-distillation, while introducing structure and stability. It aims to bridge the gap by offering diagnostically meaningful, fine-grained, and stable token-level supervision that is specifically tailored to the compositional nature of video reasoning errors, thereby improving both the faithfulness of reasoning and the efficiency of training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the reasoning capabilities of VideoLLMs, particularly for tasks requiring complex spatio-temporal understanding and grounded reasoning. This overarching goal is motivated by several identified limitations in current VideoLLM training paradigms:

1. **Addressing Sparse Sequence-Level Rewards:** A core motivation is the inefficiency caused by sparse, sequence-level rewards in existing reinforcement learning approaches. These rewards, while reliable for indicating overall correctness, do not provide sufficient information for fine-grained credit assignment, making it challenging for models to learn from specific errors within long reasoning trajectories. The goal is to introduce a mechanism that can provide richer, token-level feedback.

2. **Enabling Fine-Grained Credit Assignment:** The authors aim to overcome the lack of precise credit assignment over long, temporally grounded reasoning trajectories. In video understanding, errors can originate from various specific points, such as incorrect temporal references, misidentified objects, or illogical reasoning steps. Traditional methods struggle to pinpoint these specific failures, leading to slow and inefficient learning. The objective is to assign credit (or blame) at the token level, aligning with the granular nature of video evidence.

3. **Introducing Diagnostic Specificity for Video Errors:** Existing self-distillation methods provide dense supervision but lack diagnostic specificity, treating all errors uniformly. A key motivation for VISD is to differentiate between distinct types of reasoning errors in video, such as logical inconsistencies, temporal misalignments, or spatial grounding failures. This requires a mechanism to generate structured feedback that explicitly details the nature and cause of an error.

4. **Ensuring Stable Integration of Dense Supervision with RL:** The research is motivated by the observed instability when integrating dense self-distillation signals with reinforcement learning. Auxiliary signals can conflict with or override primary reward-driven optimization, hindering stable policy learning. Therefore, an objective is to design an integration mechanism that ensures stability while leveraging dense supervision.

5. **Improving Reasoning Faithfulness and Training Efficiency:** Ultimately, the detailed motivation is to develop a framework that not only improves the accuracy of VideoLLM outputs but also enhances the faithfulness of their internal reasoning processes by making them more interpretable and grounded in visual evidence. Concurrently, the approach seeks to improve training efficiency, enabling faster convergence and reducing the computational resources required.

To achieve these objectives, VISD proposes a structured self-distillation framework that leverages diagnostically meaningful privileged information for video reasoning, aiming to provide semantically aligned and fine-grained credit assignment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VISD (Video Reasoning via Structured Self-Distillation) is a framework designed to enhance VideoLLM reasoning by integrating reinforcement learning with structured, diagnostically meaningful token-level supervision. The methodology involves augmenting a standard on-policy training pipeline with a video-aware judge model and a feedback-conditioned teacher policy, utilizing a direction–magnitude decoupling mechanism for stable optimization.

The core components and operational flow of VISD are as follows:

1. **Policy Model (Student) $\pi_{\theta}$:** The training begins with a student policy, $\pi_{\theta}$, which autoregressively generates a reasoning trajectory $y = (y_1, \dots, y_T)$ given a video-question pair $x = (v, q)$. This trajectory can include intermediate reasoning steps, temporal references, object mentions, and the final answer.

2. **Verifier Reward:** A standard verifier computes a scalar reward $R(x, y)$ for each generated trajectory. This reward reflects task-level correctness and, where applicable, grounding quality (e.g., answer accuracy, format correctness, temporal/spatial IoU for answers and reasoning traces). This signal is inherently sparse and sequence-level, providing the primary direction for policy updates.

3. **Structured Privileged Information Generation (Judge Model J):** A central innovation of VISD is the introduction of a video-aware judge model, $J$. For each sampled student trajectory $y$, along with the input $x$, verified answer-side information $a_{\star}$, and available grounding evidence $e$, the judge generates structured feedback $f = J(x, y, a_{\star}, e)$. This feedback is diagnostically meaningful, evaluating multiple dimensions of reasoning quality:
 - **Answer Correctness:** Whether the final answer is correct, partially correct, or wrong.
 - **Reasoning Consistency:** Consistency between the reasoning process and the final answer.
 - **Spatio-Temporal Grounding Quality:** Modality-specific feedback on temporal alignment (e.g., incorrect time windows) and spatial grounding (e.g., mislocalized objects, inconsistent visual evidence).
 This structured feedback serves as privileged information, explicitly detailing the causes of reasoning failures, which is used to condition the teacher policy.

4. **Feedback-Conditioned Teacher Policy $\pi_{\bar{\theta}}$:** A teacher policy, $\pi_{\bar{\theta}}$, is instantiated from the same model architecture as the student but is conditioned on the privileged context $r = (a_{\star}, e, f)$. The teacher then rescores the same student-generated completion $y$ by providing token-level probabilities $P_T(y_t | x, r, y_{<t})$. This conditioning allows the teacher to provide guidance that incorporates both verified ground truth and the diagnostic insights from the judge.

5. **Direction–Magnitude Decoupling Mechanism:** To stably integrate dense supervision with reinforcement learning, VISD adopts a direction–magnitude decoupling paradigm:
 - **Direction (Rollout-Level Advantage):** Rollout-level advantages $A^{(i)}$ are computed from the verifier rewards using group normalization (similar to GRPO). These advantages determine whether an entire sampled trajectory should be reinforced or penalized, thus setting the *direction* of the policy update.
 - **Magnitude (Token-Level Modulation):** The structured privileged information modulates the *magnitude* of token-level updates. For each token $y_t$ in a trajectory, a token-level discrepancy $\Delta_t$ is computed. This discrepancy is based on the log-ratio between the teacher's and student's renormalized probabilities for $y_t$ over a *top-K local support* (which includes the teacher's top-K candidates and the realized token $y_t$).
 - This discrepancy $\Delta_t$ is then converted into a reweighting factor $w_t = \exp(\text{sign}(A) \cdot \Delta_t)$, which is clipped within a bounded range (e.g., $[1-\epsilon_w, 1+\epsilon_w]$). This means if the overall trajectory is good ($A>0$), tokens favored by the teacher are scaled up; if the trajectory is bad ($A<0$), tokens disfavored by the teacher are suppressed more.
 - The final token-level advantage $\hat{A}^{(i)}_t$ is an interpolation between the rollout-level advantage $A^{(i)}$ and the magnitude-modulated advantage, controlled by a mixing coefficient $\lambda$: $\hat{A}^{(i)}_t = A^{(i)} \cdot ((1-\lambda) + \lambda \cdot m^{(i)}_t)$.

6. **Stable Policy Optimization for Long Video Sequences:** VISD incorporates specific strategies to ensure robust optimization:
 - **Curriculum Scheduling:** The mixing coefficient $\lambda$ for the token-level reweighting is gradually annealed from an initial value (e.g., 0.5) to 0 over training steps. This allows the model to leverage structured self-distillation for efficient early learning and then transition to relying primarily on environment rewards for long-term stability.
 - **EMA-based Teacher Stabilization:** The teacher model's parameters $\bar{\theta}$ are maintained as an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student's parameters $\theta$. This stabilizes the teacher signal, reducing high-frequency fluctuations and enhancing robustness during training.
 - **GRPO-style Clipped Surrogate Objective:** The policy is optimized using a clipped surrogate objective, similar to Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) or Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), but with the token-reweighted advantage $\hat{A}^{(i)}_t$. Importantly, VISD does not add a separate reference-model KL penalty or a dedicated distillation loss, preserving the stability benefits of trust-region optimization.

This comprehensive methodology allows VISD to provide fine-grained, semantically aligned credit assignment without compromising the stability of reward-driven optimization, specifically tailored for the complexities of video reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of VISD demonstrates consistent performance improvements and enhanced training efficiency across a range of video reasoning benchmarks.

**1. Overall Performance and Training Efficiency:**
- VISD consistently outperforms strong baselines across diverse benchmarks, showing gains in both answer accuracy and spatio-temporal grounding quality.
- A notable finding is the improved training efficiency: VISD achieves its performance gains with approximately 2x faster convergence in optimization steps compared to baselines such as Open-o3-Video and VisionCoach, which often require more than 2300 optimization steps while VISD reaches stronger performance at around 1200 steps.

**2. Spatio-Temporal Grounding on V-STAR:**
- On the V-STAR benchmark, which assesses spatio-temporal reasoning, VISD improves answer accuracy over Qwen2.5-VL-7B by +28.4 points.
- It obtains the best overall V-STAR scores, improving the aggregate metrics mAM/mLGM over VisionCoach from 34.3/47.5 to 35.1/48.9.
- The improvements are observed not only in final answer correctness but also in the quality of temporal localization and spatial grounding, particularly in scenarios where sparse sequence-level rewards typically provide limited information.

**3. General Video Understanding Benchmarks:**
- Across broader video reasoning benchmarks, including WorldSense, VideoMMMU, LongVideo-Reason-eval (LRR), and TVGBench, VISD demonstrates improved average scores.
- It outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-7B by +7.4 average points and VisionCoach by +1.8 average points.
- The most substantial gains are observed on long-video reasoning and temporal grounding metrics, such as LRR and TVGBench.
- On Video-MME-v2, VISD improves the non-linear score from 8.6 (VisionCoach) to 9.4 and achieves the highest average accuracy. These results indicate that the feedback-conditioned token supervision enhances grounding-oriented behaviors while maintaining or improving general video question-answering capabilities.

**4. Temporal Grounding on Charades-STA:**
- For zero-shot temporal grounding on the Charades-STA benchmark, VISD improves R@0.3, R@0.5, and mIoU over Open-o3-Video and VisionCoach. This indicates an enhanced ability for moment localization, even without task-specific fine-tuning on this particular benchmark.

**5. Ablation Studies:**
- **Structured Feedback:** Ablation studies confirm that structured judge feedback provides additional benefits beyond using only verified answer information. The full VISD configuration, leveraging judge feedback, yields a stronger final reward trajectory and higher answer accuracy, VSTAR scores, and general video scores compared to a no-feedback variant. This indicates that the diagnostic context from the judge is crucial for refining token-level credit assignment.
- **Teacher Update Strategy:** Comparison of teacher update strategies revealed that the EMA-based teacher provides the most stable optimization and leads to the best final reward, answer accuracy, VSTAR overall score, and general-video average. Other strategies, like a current-policy teacher or Sync-10 teacher, showed less stability, with Sync-10 exhibiting a substantially larger final gradient norm.
- **Top-K Support vs. Sampled Token:** The use of a top-K local support for computing teacher-student discrepancy was found to maintain a stronger reward trajectory and better grounding scores (e.g., VSTAR mAM/mLGM, TVGBench) compared to a variant that only compares the teacher and student on the single realized token. This supports the utility of a compact distribution-level comparison.

These results collectively indicate that VISD’s structured self-distillation framework, with its diagnostically meaningful feedback and stable optimization strategies, effectively addresses the challenges of fine-grained credit assignment in video reasoning, leading to improved performance and efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of VISD holds several significances for the field of multimodal AI and has potential impacts across various applications:

1. **Advancement in Video Reasoning Capabilities:** VISD directly addresses a critical limitation in VideoLLMs—the challenge of sparse rewards and coarse credit assignment in complex, long-horizon video reasoning. By enabling diagnostically meaningful, fine-grained, token-level supervision, the framework allows models to learn more effectively from specific reasoning errors related to temporal and spatial evidence. This capability can lead to VideoLLMs that not only provide correct answers but also demonstrate more faithful and interpretable reasoning processes.

2. **Enhanced Training Efficiency:** The demonstrated nearly 2x faster convergence in optimization steps represents a substantial improvement in training efficiency. This can reduce the computational resources, energy consumption, and time required to develop and fine-tune advanced VideoLLMs, making such research and development more accessible and sustainable.

3. **Improved Grounding and Interpretability:** The framework's emphasis on structured feedback for spatio-temporal grounding is crucial. By explicitly evaluating answer correctness, logical consistency, and spatio-temporal alignment, VISD forces models to ground their reasoning more precisely in visual evidence. This can lead to more robust and less "hallucinatory" responses, enhancing the trustworthiness and interpretability of VideoLLM outputs.

4. **Robustness in Complex Scenarios:** The integration of principled optimization strategies, such as curriculum scheduling and EMA-based teacher stabilization, contributes to the robust training of VideoLLMs over long and complex video sequences with heterogeneous reward signals. This stability is important for scaling VideoLLMs to real-world applications where video data is diverse and challenging.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** VISD provides a novel framework for incorporating structured privileged information into reinforcement learning for multimodal tasks. This approach could inspire future research into more sophisticated judge models, richer feedback structures, and advanced credit assignment mechanisms for other grounded reasoning applications, potentially beyond video.

**Potential Impacts:**
- **Video Content Analysis:** Improved video reasoning could enhance automated video content analysis for various purposes, including content moderation, summarization, and indexing.
- **Assistive Technologies:** Applications in assistive video understanding, such as tools for individuals with visual impairments or in educational settings, could benefit from more accurate and grounded explanations of video content.
- **Human-in-the-Loop Systems:** In scenarios where human experts review AI-generated analyses of video (e.g., in medical imaging, surveillance, or sports analytics), VISD's ability to provide interpretable, grounded reasoning could facilitate faster and more reliable decision-making.
- **Autonomous Systems:** More robust video reasoning capabilities could contribute to the perception and decision-making modules of autonomous systems operating in dynamic environments.

**Broader Implications and Limitations:**
The authors acknowledge potential broader impacts, including the misuse of powerful video reasoning models in surveillance or content analysis pipelines. They also highlight that, like other large multimodal models, VISD could inherit biases from pretraining data or produce misleading explanations if its underlying predictions are flawed. The current evaluation is primarily on public benchmarks, suggesting a need for future work to extend VISD to more open-ended, noisy, and complex real-world video tasks, potentially with weaker supervision and less standardized formats. Responsible deployment, dataset governance, and robust evaluation under open-world settings are identified as important future directions.
