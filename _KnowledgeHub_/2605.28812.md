# Beyond Binary: Sim-to-Real Dexterous Manipulation with Physics-Grounded Contact Representation

> [!summary] Summary
> A physics-grounded Center-of-Pressure (CoP) tactile representation enables zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for dexterous manipulation on multi-fingered hands, effectively leveraging rich contact information while maintaining transfer robustness. Policies using CoP achieved a 0.78 success rate in challenging peg-in-hole insertion and a 4.60 seconds time-to-fall in ball balancing, surpassing other tactile input baselines.

## Problem

- The significant "simulation-reality gap" hinders effective sim-to-real transfer for contact-rich dexterous manipulation, particularly when using high-fidelity tactile sensing.
- Existing tactile representations for sim-to-real present a trade-off: simplistic features (e.g., binary contact) are robust but lack crucial detail, while raw tactile signals are information-rich but difficult to accurately simulate and align across hardware.
- The practical challenges and high costs associated with calibrating tactile sensors, especially in estimating unknown taxel orientations, often require ground-truth force measurements.

## Method

- Introduces Center-of-Pressure (CoP) as a physics-grounded tactile representation, summarizing local contact as a 3D force vector and a 3D contact location.
- Develops a differentiable bidirectional mapping between raw taxel forces and the CoP representation, employing a parametric stress-distribution model to account for force spreading.
- Proposes a differentiable dynamics-based calibration method to estimate taxel orientations by minimizing the discrepancy between inferred external wrenches and observed joint torques, without requiring ground-truth force measurements.

## Results

- CoP policies achieved a 0.78 success rate for peg-in-hole insertion across six different peg shapes and an average time-to-fall of 4.60 seconds for ball balancing, outperforming binary contact, raw taxel, and proprioception-only baselines.
- The CoP policy exhibited robust adaptation to out-of-distribution peg pose initializations, showing the least performance reduction among baselines and demonstrating emergent in-hand translation and re-orientation capabilities.
- Analysis of policy latents revealed that for the ball balancing task, object position was well-encoded (RMSE of 0.013m for x), and the latent embeddings implicitly clustered by object mass over time, indicating emergent physical property identification.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A physics-grounded, intermediate tactile representation like CoP can effectively bridge the sim-to-real gap by providing a balance between preserving rich contact information and ensuring robustness for transfer.
> - Policies conditioned on CoP can develop emergent capabilities for adapting to complex contact scenarios, including inferring secondary contacts and re-orienting objects in-hand.
> - Recurrent policies leveraging physics-grounded tactile inputs can implicitly encode and organize their latent states around task-relevant physical properties, such as object mass, without explicit supervision.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly contact-rich dexterous manipulation, faces significant challenges in developing robust and scalable control policies. Traditional approaches involving real-world data collection, such as imitation learning from human demonstrations, are often limited by high costs and substantial human effort. This has led to an increasing interest in sim-to-real reinforcement learning (RL) as a scalable alternative, where policies are trained in simulation and then transferred to physical robots.

However, a major obstacle in sim-to-real transfer is the "simulation-reality gap," which becomes particularly pronounced when dealing with information-dense modalities like tactile sensing. Accurately simulating tactile sensor responses is challenging due to complex, high-dimensional, and often unmodeled physical processes, as well as the variability in sensor designs. Consequently, tactile inputs are frequently simplified or avoided in many sim-to-real applications.

Existing sim-to-real methods attempt to bridge this gap in various ways. Some simplify tactile data into coarse, low-dimensional features (e.g., binary or discretized contact signals), which offer robustness in transfer but sacrifice the detailed information crucial for precise, contact-rich tasks. Others aim to directly model and simulate raw tactile sensor behaviors, often by using tactile encoders to compress raw simulated activations into latent observation vectors. While these methods can capture more complexity, their representations are often sensor-specific, less interpretable, and difficult to align directly with simulator contact quantities. The research landscape thus highlights a trade-off between the richness of tactile data and its transferability from simulation to reality.

This work directly addresses this bottleneck by proposing a physics-grounded, intermediate tactile representation. By focusing on a representation that balances expressiveness with sim-to-real robustness, this research aims to enable the effective utilization of tactile information for complex dexterous manipulation tasks, pushing the boundaries of what sim-to-real RL can achieve in contact-rich scenarios. The paper positions itself as a step towards making tactile sensing a more viable and effective input modality for general and scalable robotic manipulation policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from a critical bottleneck in contact-rich dexterous manipulation: the difficulty of effectively leveraging tactile information in sim-to-real reinforcement learning. While sim-to-real RL offers a scalable approach by allowing policies to be trained in large-scale simulations, the inherent differences between simulation and reality (the "sim-to-real gap") severely hinder the direct use of high-fidelity sensor data, particularly touch.

The paper identifies a fundamental trade-off in existing tactile representations for sim-to-real transfer:
1. **Simplistic Features:** Low-dimensional tactile features (e.g., binary contact) enable robust transfer due to their coarse nature but discard the rich contact information necessary for intricate manipulation tasks.
2. **Raw Signals:** Representations closer to raw tactile signals contain detailed information critical for complex control but are difficult to accurately simulate and align across hardware and software, leading to a larger sim-to-real gap.

Against this background, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

- **Develop a Physics-Grounded Tactile Representation:** The primary objective is to introduce Center-of-Pressure (CoP), a novel tactile representation that is grounded in physical principles. This representation is designed to summarize local tactile information compactly as a 3D contact force vector and a 3D contact location. The motivation here is to provide a "middle ground" that is both compact enough for robust alignment between simulation and hardware and expressive enough to preserve essential force and location details for fine-grained dexterous control.
- **Propose a Differentiable Sensor Calibration Scheme:** To support the CoP representation, the authors aim to develop a method for estimating taxel orientations (a crucial but often unknown parameter for tactile sensors) without requiring ground-truth force measurements. This is motivated by the practical difficulty and cost of traditional sensor calibration methods. The proposed method leverages differentiable dynamics to match inferred external wrenches from tactile readings with observed joint torques under static equilibrium.
- **Evaluate on Challenging Blind Manipulation Tasks:** The work seeks to evaluate the effectiveness of the CoP representation on two "blind" (minimal visual input) and challenging contact-rich tasks: peg-in-hole insertion and ball balancing. The motivation for these specific tasks is to explicitly isolate and test the role of tactile feedback, avoiding reliance on vision or simple periodic motions common in prior evaluations, and requiring policies to infer "secondary contacts" (e.g., between the manipulated object and the environment) through primary hand-object contacts.
- **Demonstrate Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A core objective is to achieve successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of policies trained with CoP, without requiring real-world policy learning or teacher-student distillation. This demonstrates the robustness and alignment of the proposed representation.
- **Benchmark Against Baselines:** The research aims to systematically compare CoP against various baselines, including simplistic binary-contact representations, raw-taxel inputs, and ablations of CoP's components, to quantify its performance advantage.
- **Analyze Emergent Policy Properties:** Finally, the authors aim to investigate whether policies conditioned on physics-grounded representations like CoP can implicitly encode task-relevant physical properties (e.g., object mass) as an emergent byproduct of control, highlighting the potential for more general and scalable manipulation.

In summary, the motivation is to overcome the tactile data bottleneck in sim-to-real RL by providing a robust, information-rich, and physically interpretable contact representation that can scale to complex, precision-demanding dexterous manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on the introduction and validation of Center-of-Pressure (CoP) as a tactile representation for sim-to-real transfer. This involves a multi-pronged approach covering representation definition, mapping from raw sensor data, calibration, sim-real alignment, and experimental evaluation.

#### 3.1. Physics-Aware Contact Representation (CoP)

The proposed CoP representation for a robot fingertip consists of two components:
- A **3D force vector** ($\text{S}\mathbf{f}_{cop} \in \mathbb{R}^3$) representing the total contact force acting on the robot link.
- A **3D Cartesian contact position** ($\text{S}\mathbf{p}_{cop} \in \mathbb{R}^3$) where the force is applied.
Both are expressed in the sensor frame S. In rigid-body simulators like IsaacSim or MuJoCo, similar pairwise contact information (force and position) is typically available. The term CoP here denotes the resultant contact wrench reduced to a single force vector at a centroidal contact point, acting as a compact local contact descriptor.

#### 3.2. Taxel-CoP Mapping

To enable direct sim-to-real transfer, a differentiable bidirectional mapping is established between raw tactile sensor readings and the CoP representation. This assumes tactile sensors similar to XELA uSkin, which consist of an array of $N$ taxels, each providing 3-axis force measurements ($\text{T}_i\mathbf{f}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$) in its local taxel frame $\text{T}_i$.

- **Stress Distribution Model:** Instead of simple summation, a parametric stress-distribution model is proposed. It accounts for force spreading through the compliant silicone layer by decomposing the CoP force vector ($\mathbf{f}_{cop}$) into normal ($\mathbf{f}_n$) and shear ($\mathbf{f}_s$) components. For each taxel, the model defines effective normal ($\mathbf{f}_{i,n}$) and shear ($\mathbf{f}_{i,s}$) forces, considering changes in force direction due to deformation and magnitude decay proportional to distance from the contact point.
- **Forward Mapping Derivation:**
 - $\mathbf{p}_{cop}$ is estimated as the weighted average of active taxel positions, weighted by force magnitudes.
 - The unit surface normal at CoP ($\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{cop}$) is approximated via inverse distance weighting of taxel normals.
 - Effective normal force direction ($\hat{\mathbf{b}}_i$) at each taxel is a blend between the taxel's local normal and the unit vector from CoP to the taxel, weighted by a Gaussian radial function. Its magnitude decays similarly.
 - Effective shear force direction ($\mathbf{f}_{i,s}$) is approximated by projecting $\mathbf{f}_{cop}$ onto the surface tangent plane at $\mathbf{p}_{cop}$. Its magnitude also decays with distance.
 - The relationship between individual taxel forces ($\mathbf{f}_i$) and the CoP force ($\mathbf{f}_{cop}$) is formulated as $\mathbf{f}_i = \mathbf{M}_i \mathbf{f}_{cop}$, where $\mathbf{M}_i$ is a 3x3 matrix incorporating the blended normal direction and shear projection.
- **Solving for CoP Force:** The unknown $\mathbf{f}_{cop}$ is found by aggregating taxel equations into a global linear system and solving a regularized least-squares problem, resulting in a closed-form solution. This model is differentiable and computationally efficient, enabling bidirectional conversion between simulated CoP and hardware taxel readings.

#### 3.3. Sensor Calibration via Differentiable Dynamics

The orientations ($\mathbf{R}_i$) of taxel frames relative to the sensor frame are critical but difficult to obtain. A differentiable dynamics-based calibration method is proposed:

- **Data Collection:** A robot hand with a stiff PD controller applies random contacts to fingertips, maintaining fixed nominal joint positions. Raw taxel forces, joint torques ($\tau$), and joint angles ($q$) are recorded.
- **Rotation Parameterization:** Taxel rotations $\mathbf{R}_i$ are parameterized using the $\text{R}^9+\text{SVD}$ method, which projects a learnable 3x3 matrix to a valid rotation matrix.
- **Optimization:** For each data sample, recorded taxel forces are rotated using current estimates of $\mathbf{R}_i$, then converted to $\text{S}\hat{\mathbf{f}}_{cop}$ and $\text{S}\hat{\mathbf{p}}_{cop}$ via the CoP mapping. These are transformed to the base frame. The position Jacobian ($\text{B}\hat{\mathbf{J}}_{cop}$) at the contact point is computed. Under static equilibrium, the expected joint torques ($\hat{\tau}$) are computed as $\hat{\tau} = -\text{B}\hat{\mathbf{J}}^T_{cop} \text{B}\hat{\mathbf{f}}_{cop}$. An MSE loss between $\hat{\tau}$ and recorded $\tau$ is minimized using gradient-based optimization to update the rotation parameters. This method avoids the need for ground-truth force measurements.

#### 3.4. Sim-Real Alignment

Beyond the CoP representation itself, several aspects are aligned between simulation and reality:

- **Contact Representation:** Only the surface-normal component of CoP is used for both sim and hardware due to unreliable shear estimates in IsaacLab. The taxel-CoP mapping parameters are calibrated using paired sim/hardware rollout data.
- **Actuator Dynamics:** A Bayesian optimization-based system identification approach is used to align simulated actuator parameters (stiffness, damping, friction) with hardware characteristics by comparing robot responses to probing trajectory sequences.
- **Sensor Delay:** Real-world tactile sensor delay (time from contact to policy observation) is measured and explicitly introduced into the simulation during policy training.

#### 3.5. Experiments

- **Hardware:** A 16-DOF Allegro hand equipped with XELA uSkin sensors.
- **Tasks:** Two challenging blind, contact-rich manipulation tasks:
 - **Peg-in-hole insertion:** Hand grasps and inserts a peg with varying head shapes into a fixed hole, requiring inference of secondary contacts.
 - **Ball balancing:** Hand balances a ball on a lightweight plate supported by fingertips, using various balls with different properties.
- **Baselines:** CoP is compared against proprioception-only (base), binary contact, CoP force magnitude, CoP force vector-only, CoP position-only, and raw taxel forces. Human performance is also provided for reference.
- **Policy Architecture:** A recurrent neural network (GRU) is used for the policy to provide temporal context, which was found to be more sample-efficient and performant than MLP networks with stacked history observations.
- **Training:** Policies are trained using asymmetric actor-critic PPO in IsaacLab. The actor receives proprioception and CoP, while the critic receives additional privileged information. Direct sim-to-real transfer is employed, supported by systematic domain randomization of object properties, states, and observation noise/delay.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides a comprehensive evaluation of the CoP representation across two challenging dexterous manipulation tasks, supported by comparative analyses and insights into emergent policy behaviors.

#### 4.1. Peg-in-Hole Insertion

- **Overall Performance:** The CoP representation consistently achieved the highest overall success rate (0.78) across six different peg-in-hole shapes, outperforming all other baselines. Policies conditioned on CoP demonstrated adaptability and persistence, leading to successful insertions even if initial attempts failed.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - Higher-fidelity representations (CoP, `vec`) generally led to more robust policies with higher success rates but often resulted in longer task completion times compared to simpler baselines. This suggests a trade-off between speed and adaptability.
 - Simplified representations (`base`, `bin`) achieved faster initial insertions in favorable yaw orientations but struggled to adapt to contacts or out-of-distribution (OOD) initializations, indicating insufficient tactile information for recovery.
 - Raw taxel forces (`taxel`) performed worse than most other baselines. This finding aligns with previous works that highlight challenges in direct simulation and alignment of high-dimensional, sensor-specific raw tactile signals.
- **Ablation Study:** The `vec` (force vector only) and `pos` (contact position only) baselines showed that both components of the CoP representation are complementary and contribute to overall task performance.
- **Human Performance:** A human expert significantly surpassed the robot policies in both success rate and task completion time, suggesting that humans integrate tactile cues with higher-level cognitive abilities like geometric reasoning and sophisticated exploratory strategies.
- **Robustness to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Initialization:** When presented with OOD peg pose initializations, the CoP policy exhibited the least reduction in success rate. It demonstrated emergent capabilities for in-hand object translation and re-orientation to achieve peg-hole alignment, highlighting its robust adaptation.
- **Robustness to Masked Sensors:** Under conditions where 40% of raw hardware taxel forces were randomly masked, higher-fidelity contact representations (including CoP) experienced larger performance degradation compared to simplified representations. This indicates a sensitivity of dense representations to precise individual taxel forces.

#### 4.2. Ball Balancing

- **Overall Performance:** Policies using CoP (`cop`) and force vector-only (`vec`) successfully learned the ball balancing task, achieving the highest time-to-fall (CoP: 4.60 seconds overall). In contrast, policies conditioned on contact representations lacking explicit numerical force information (`base`, `bin`, `pos`) failed to learn the task. This underscores the crucial role of precise force information for this dynamic and sensitive task.
- **Comparison to Baselines:** Similar to the peg-in-hole task, `taxel` performed worse than `cop` and `vec`, suggesting that the direct use of high-dimensional raw taxel data remains challenging. The comparable performance between `cop` and `vec` suggested that force information might be sufficient for this specific task, with contact position playing a lesser role than in peg-in-hole.
- **Emergent Movement Patterns:** The CoP policy developed distinct emergent movement patterns to center and balance the ball, including aggressive single-step accelerate-decelerate maneuvers and slower two-step centering processes.
- **Human Performance:** The human expert excelled overall but struggled most with a dented "moon ball" (exhibiting nonlinear, unpredictable rolling), whereas the robot policy struggled more with a smoother, faster-rolling hockey ball. This suggests that humans might rely more on predictive extrapolation using linear models, while the robot policy might be more reactive to immediate states.
- **Object State Prediction from Policy Latents:** Linear probing of the CoP policy's 256-dimensional recurrent latent output revealed that it effectively captured the ball's position (RMSE of 0.013m for x, 0.019m for y; $r^2$ scores of 0.76 and 0.62 respectively). However, velocity prediction was noticeably weaker ($r^2$ scores of 0.23 and 0.15), indicating that while the policy tracks ball position, it may not encode motion dynamics as precisely, likely due to noise inherent in contact-based state estimation.
- **Implicit Mass Identification:** Analysis of the policy's latent representations showed that for different ball masses, the latent embeddings naturally reorganized into distinct clusters over time, as evidenced by gradually increasing Silhouette Coefficients. This indicates that conditioning on CoP enables the recurrent policy state to implicitly organize around and infer task-relevant physical properties like object mass, even without explicit supervision.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Center-of-Pressure (CoP) as a physics-aware contact representation, offering a notable contribution to the field of dexterous manipulation, particularly within the sim-to-real reinforcement learning paradigm. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Tactile Sensing:** The primary impact is in effectively addressing a long-standing bottleneck: the difficulty of leveraging information-dense tactile modalities in sim-to-real transfer. By grounding the representation in physical principles, CoP achieves an effective balance between preserving rich contact information (force and location) and maintaining robustness for transfer across simulation and hardware. This allows for the first-time effective use of dense contact information in zero-shot sim-to-real dexterous manipulation policies on multi-fingered hands.
- **Enabling Scalable Learning for Contact-Rich Tasks:** CoP facilitates scalable, autonomous learning in simulation for tasks that require fine-grained physical interactions. By reducing the need for costly and time-consuming real-world data collection, it opens avenues for developing more complex and precise manipulation skills that were previously challenging due to limitations in tactile data handling.
- **Robustness and Adaptability:** The demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on challenging "blind" tasks (peg-in-hole insertion and ball balancing) highlights the robustness of CoP-conditioned policies. Their ability to adapt to out-of-distribution initializations and infer secondary contacts suggests a higher level of dexterity and generalization compared to policies using simpler or raw tactile inputs.
- **Emergent Physical Understanding in Policies:** The finding that learned policies implicitly encode task-relevant physical properties, such as object mass, into their latent representations is significant. This suggests that physically grounded intermediate representations can lead to more interpretable and potentially more generalizable policies, as they organize their internal states around meaningful physical concepts without explicit supervision. This could pave the way for policies that exhibit a deeper understanding of their environment and interactions.
- **Novel Calibration Method:** The proposed sensor calibration scheme based on differentiable dynamics provides a practical method for estimating taxel orientations without relying on ground-truth force measurements. This reduces calibration complexity and cost, making the deployment of tactile sensors more accessible.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a promising direction for future research in several areas:
 - **Integration with Other Learning Paradigms:** Extending CoP as a physically grounded tactile modality for imitation learning and sample-efficient real-world RL could enhance their capabilities.
 - **Broader Applicability:** Applying CoP to a wider range of robotic systems (e.g., arm-hand systems), different tactile sensor types, and full-hand tactile coverage could expand its utility.
 - **Addressing Simulation Limitations:** Identifying and addressing current limitations in contact simulation (e.g., unreliable shear force estimation, incomplete contact reporting) could further improve the fidelity and impact of physics-grounded representations like CoP.

In essence, this research provides a crucial component for advancing dexterous manipulation, particularly for tasks where fine-grained contact understanding is paramount. It demonstrates that a carefully designed, physics-aware tactile representation can unlock the potential of sim-to-real learning for complex robotic skills.
