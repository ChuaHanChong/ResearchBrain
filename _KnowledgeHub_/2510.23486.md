# Learning to Reason Efficiently with Discounted Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Amazon and the University of Alberta developed a discounted reinforcement learning approach to train Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) for efficient reasoning. This method enables LRMs to generate significantly shorter reasoning sequences, reducing mean response length by 13-37%, while consistently maintaining or slightly improving Pass@1 accuracy across various mathematical reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) generate excessively long 'chains of thought,' leading to high computational costs, memory usage, inference latency, and reduced serving throughput.
- A common belief in the field suggests an inherent trade-off where shorter reasoning paths inevitably lead to reduced accuracy.
- Existing methods for LRM efficiency often rely on heuristic length penalties or specific data curation, lacking a robust theoretical foundation for simultaneously optimizing accuracy and conciseness.

## Method

- The work models LRM reasoning as a finite horizon ==discounted Markov Decision== Process (MDP) with a binary correctness reward applied by a verifier.
- It leverages Blackwell optimality, which theoretically guarantees that policies optimal for discount factors sufficiently close to one maximize undiscounted success (accuracy) and, among these, minimize expected trajectory length.
- A practical training recipe is introduced using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), applying discounting only to the extrinsic correctness reward, incorporating KL regularization, and masking non-reasoning tokens from discounting.

## Results

- Mean response length was reduced by 13-**22%** on GSM8K and MATH for Qwen2.5 **7B**-Instruct and Llama 3 **8B**-Instruct models, while Pass@1 accuracy was maintained or slightly increased.
- On advanced math benchmarks (AMC 2023, AIME 2025, MINERVA, OLYMPIAD), Phi-4 and Qwen2.5 **14B**-Instruct achieved 21-**37%** length reductions, with some instances showing notable Pass@1 accuracy improvements (e.g., Phi-4 on AMC 2023 from **51%** to **61%**).
- Experiments varying the discount factor demonstrated that stronger discounting reduces length, but overly aggressive discounting eventually compromises accuracy, confirming the theoretical boundary for optimal conciseness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Blackwell optimal policies inherently achieve maximal accuracy and, among accuracy maximizers, minimal expected trajectory length, challenging the assumption of an unavoidable length-accuracy trade-off within certain policy classes.
> - A 'Blackwell discount factor' (γ_bw < 1) exists, defining a range for the discount factor where optimal policies simultaneously maximize correctness and minimize response length.
> - Empirical findings confirm that with a properly chosen discount factor, LRMs can generate substantially shorter reasoning traces without sacrificing accuracy, and sometimes even improving it.
