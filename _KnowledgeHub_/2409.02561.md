# Vision-Language Navigation with Continual Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at CASIA and UCAS developed Vision-Language Navigation with Continual Learning (VLNCL) and the Dual-loop Scenario Replay (Dual-SR) method, which allows agents to incrementally learn from new environments. Dual-SR demonstrated a 16% increase in Success Rate on unseen environments compared to a base agent, while effectively retaining previously acquired knowledge.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) agents struggle to generalize effectively to novel, unseen 3D environments, experiencing performance degradation.
- Relying on massively expanding training datasets to cover all possible environments is impractical and costly for real-world applications.
- Traditional sequential learning methods for VLN agents suffer from catastrophic forgetting, where new knowledge overwrites previously acquired navigational skills.

## Method

- Introduces the **Vision-Language Navigation with Continual Learning (VLNCL)** paradigm, where agents sequentially learn from distinct task domains.
- Proposes **Dual-loop Scenario Replay (Dual-SR)**, a ==brain-inspired== method using an ==inner loop== for immediate learning and an ==outer loop== for long-term knowledge consolidation.
- Integrates Dual-SR with a ==cross-modal structured transformer== agent that utilizes global, candidate target, history, instruction, and vision tokens for navigation.

## Results

- The Dual-SR method increased the Success Rate (SR) by **16%** and Oracle Success Rate (OSR) by **8%** on unseen environments compared to the base VLN agent.
- Dual-SR significantly mitigated catastrophic forgetting, achieving an average Seen Transfer (ST) of **-6.00** on the Val Seen split, outperforming a fine-tuning baseline's **-10.59**.
- The method demonstrated positive knowledge transfer to new environments with an average Unseen Transfer (UT) of **2.18** on the Val Unseen split, while a fine-tuning baseline showed negative transfer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continual learning provides a framework for VLN agents to adapt to dynamic real-world environments without requiring massive, static datasets.
> - A dual-loop memory replay mechanism, mirroring working and long-term memory consolidation, effectively balances retaining prior knowledge with acquiring new skills.
> - Evaluating continual learning in VLN requires specific metrics like Seen Transfer (for forgetting resistance) and Unseen Transfer (for generalization to new environments).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) is a field within embedded intelligence that focuses on enabling agents to navigate 3D environments by interpreting natural language instructions. Traditional VLN research has primarily concentrated on improving environmental understanding and the accuracy of target decision policies. Early work, such as the introduction of the Room-to-Room (R2R) dataset by Anderson et al. (2018), established foundational benchmarks. Subsequent research has expanded to include diverse environments (e.g., Touchdown for outdoor navigation), conversational tasks (e.g., CVDN), and remote object navigation (e.g., REVERIE). Methodological advancements have involved leveraging CNN-based features, integrating long-term history via multimodal transformers, graph-based modeling of relationships, reinforcement learning, and transformer-based architectures for decision-making.

Despite these developments, a critical challenge in VLN is the generalization of agents to diverse unseen scenes, which is crucial for real-world applications. Existing agents often exhibit a performance degradation when deployed in novel environments due to the limited diversity of training data. The conventional solution of massively expanding datasets to cover all possible environments is both impractical and costly.

This research addresses this generalization limitation by integrating the concept of continual learning (CL) into VLN. Continual learning, initially explored by Thrun (1998), aims to enable agents to acquire new tasks sequentially without experiencing catastrophic forgetting—the phenomenon where learning new information erases previously acquired knowledge. Approaches in CL generally fall into categories such as rehearsal-based (retaining and replaying past data), regularization-based (constraining weight updates to protect prior knowledge), and parameter isolation methods (allocating distinct parameters for different tasks). While CL has been an active area in broader machine learning, its application to VLN is relatively nascent. Recent work by Jeong et al. (2024) explored rehearsal-based methods in VLN to mitigate catastrophic forgetting.

This paper positions itself by proposing the "Vision-Language Navigation with Continual Learning (VLNCL)" paradigm, which goes beyond merely alleviating catastrophic forgetting. It emphasizes the necessity for VLN agents to not only retain prior knowledge but also to continuously improve their generalization ability in unfamiliar environments. By introducing novel experimental setups and evaluation metrics tailored for VLNCL, this work aims to establish a benchmark and advance the field towards more robust and adaptable embodied intelligent agents capable of operating in dynamic real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a VLN agent capable of continually learning from new, unseen environments while effectively retaining knowledge acquired from previously encountered tasks. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

- **Addressing the Generalization Gap in VLN:** Traditional VLN agents often struggle to generalize their navigation capabilities to novel environments not present in their training data. This limitation results in a significant performance drop when agents are deployed in new settings. The authors motivate this work by highlighting that current approaches, which heavily rely on extensive training data, are insufficient for real-world applications where agents must navigate an ever-changing array of environments. The prohibitive cost and impracticality of massively expanding datasets to cover every possible scenario underscore the need for an alternative approach.

- **Enabling Incremental Learning and Adaptation:** Real-world applications of VLN agents require continuous adaptation. Agents must incrementally acquire new skills and knowledge as they encounter new environments or tasks, rather than being trained once on a static dataset. This motivates the adoption of a continual learning framework, which allows agents to learn new information sequentially, enhancing their robustness and effectiveness in dynamic scenarios.

- **Mitigating Catastrophic Forgetting:** A central challenge in sequential learning is catastrophic forgetting, where the acquisition of new knowledge leads to the overwriting or degradation of previously learned information. For a VLN agent to accumulate knowledge over time, it must maintain a balance between stability (retaining old knowledge) and plasticity (learning new knowledge). The research is motivated by the need to develop mechanisms that prevent agents from forgetting earlier navigational experiences and environmental understanding as they are exposed to new task domains.

- **Improving Knowledge Transfer to Unseen Environments:** Beyond simply resisting forgetting, the authors aim for agents to leverage their accumulated knowledge to rapidly adapt and perform effectively in entirely new, unseen environments. This involves facilitating positive knowledge transfer, where prior learning aids the acquisition of new tasks. The paper is motivated by the goal of enhancing the agent's ability to quickly infer and apply relevant navigational strategies in novel settings.

- **Establishing a Benchmark for VLNCL:** Given the nascent state of continual learning applications in VLN, there is a lack of standardized paradigms, experimental setups, and evaluation metrics. The authors are motivated to introduce the VLNCL paradigm, define appropriate metrics (Seen Transfer and Unseen Transfer), and establish a benchmark using existing datasets (like R2R) to facilitate future research and comparison in this emergent area.

- **Brain Memory as an Inspiration:** The research draws inspiration from biological memory replay mechanisms observed in the human brain, where consolidation of long-term memories occurs through replay during rest. This provides a biological motivation for designing an artificial system that can effectively integrate new sensory input with existing knowledge.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces the Vision-Language Navigation with Continual Learning (VLNCL) paradigm and proposes a novel method called Dual-loop Scenario Replay (Dual-SR) integrated with a structured transformer agent.

#### 3.1. Setting of Vision-Language Navigation with Continual Learning

The traditional VLN problem, typically modeled as a Markov Decision Process where an agent navigates based on visual inputs and language instructions, is reformulated for a continual learning setting. The validation dataset (specifically, the R2R dataset in experiments) is partitioned into multiple distinct "task domains" ($td_i$), with each domain representing a specific scene. These task domains are presented to the agent sequentially, simulating an environment where an agent continually encounters new environments while needing to retain prior knowledge. The objective function is reformulated to minimize the expected loss across all sequentially encountered task domains, balancing knowledge retention and generalization.

#### 3.2. Dual-loop Scenario Replay (Dual-SR)

Inspired by the working memory and long-term memory mechanisms in the human brain, the Dual-SR algorithm employs two interconnected weight update loops to manage the trade-off between learning new tasks and retaining old knowledge.

- **Inner Loop (Working Memory Simulation):** This loop focuses on learning new task domain data while preventing immediate forgetting of recent past experiences.
 - When the agent receives a new task batch ($B_t$) from the current task domain, it randomly samples a batch of old tasks ($B_M$) from a multi-scenario memory buffer.
 - These new and old samples are combined to form a mini-batch.
 - The model's weights ($\theta$) are updated based on the loss computed from this combined mini-batch, using a meta-learning-based update rule: $U_k(\theta) = \theta - \alpha \circ \nabla L(\theta)$. Here, $\alpha$ is a learnable parameter vector determining update direction and rate, allowing for adaptive learning. This update mechanism is designed to mimic memory retrieval in working memory.

- **Outer Loop (Long-Term Memory Consolidation):** This loop, performed after the agent has completed learning for an entire task domain, simulates the consolidation of long-term memories and aims to enhance generalization.
 - It leverages a meta-update mechanism inspired by the Reptile algorithm. The Reptile algorithm helps models acquire a generalizable structure from current tasks, enabling rapid adaptation to similar new tasks.
 - The outer loop updates the model's global weights by moving them towards the weights learned during the inner loop for the current task domain, but with a meta-learning rate ($\beta$): $\theta = \theta + \beta \times (\theta' - \theta)$, where $\theta'$ are the weights after the inner loop updates for the current task domain. This step helps balance new and old knowledge and mitigates overfitting that might arise from limited samples within a single task domain.

- **Multi-Scenario Memory Buffer:** A key component of Dual-SR is a memory buffer that stores task indices, organized by environment type (task domains).
 - This buffer allows for targeted replay of experiences from specific task domains.
 - To manage memory efficiently, particularly with many tasks, the buffer updates scenario memory between task domains. When a new task belongs to an already seen task domain, and the task ID is a multiple of a predefined buffer size Z, one of the tasks in that domain within the memory buffer is replaced with the current task. This strategy ensures a diverse and balanced representation of past experiences in the replay process and implicitly prioritizes recent or representative tasks, inspired by working memory principles.

#### 3.3. Structured Transformer VLN Agents with Continual Learning

The Dual-SR algorithm is implemented within a cross-modal structured transformer architecture, which serves as the agent's planner. This architecture processes different token types at each navigation step:
- **Global token ($g_{t-1}$):** Represents the overall context.
- **Candidate target tokens ($C$):** A set of potential long-term navigation targets, encoded using grid coding and positional embeddings. These are refined with visual and instruction cues to predict the destination probability.
- **History tokens ($H$):** Represents the agent's explored path, constructed from panoramic view embeddings, action embeddings, temporal embeddings, and positional embeddings of previously visited locations. A graph structure ($S_t$) with an adjacency matrix ($E$) captures navigability between visited locations.
- **Encoded instruction tokens ($I$):** The natural language instruction, which remains constant.
- **Encoded vision tokens ($V$):** Visual observations from the current viewpoint.

The structured transformer enables the agent to maintain a spatial understanding of the environment and reason about past locations, aiding navigation decisions. The agent's action space includes both local actions (within the current view) and global actions (to previously visited locations). The probability of choosing an action is determined by an MLP acting on the action token and the global token.

The training objective combines several loss components:
- **Imitation Learning (L_IL):** Supervised learning from ground-truth actions.
- **Reinforcement Learning (L_RL):** For exploring and learning from environmental feedback.
- **History Teacher Loss (L_HT):** To ensure actions align with the ground-truth path.
- **Target Prediction Loss (L_T):** For accurately predicting the destination.
The total loss is a weighted sum of these components.

For continual learning inference, the agent processes task-domain-based data streams sequentially. The inner loop updates parameters through continuous memory buffer updates and scenario replays based on the combined loss. Upon completion of a task domain, the outer loop performs its meta-update to balance parameters.

#### 3.4. Experiment Setup and Evaluation Metrics

- **Dataset:** The R2R dataset is used, divided into distinct task domains for Train Seen (55 domains), Val Seen (53 domains), and Val Unseen (8 domains).
- **Comparative Baselines:** Existing VLN agents (e.g., Seq2Seq, SSM, EnvDrop, PREVALENT, VLN⟳BERT), other continual learning methods, and a fine-tuning baseline (training on each task domain independently without explicit forgetting mitigation) are used.
- **Evaluation Metrics for VLNCL:**
 - **Seen Transfer (ST):** Measures forgetting resistance. Calculated as the average performance difference on the *i*-th task domain after training on $T$ domains, compared to training only on the first *i* domains ($i < T$). A higher (less negative) ST indicates better retention of prior knowledge.
 - **Unseen Transfer (UT):** Evaluates knowledge transfer to new environments. Calculated as the average performance difference on unseen tasks after training on $T$ domains, compared to the base agent's performance on those unseen tasks before any continual learning. A higher (more positive) UT indicates better generalization. The unseen task domain set dynamically shrinks as the agent encounters new domains.
- **Implementation Details:** The base model is trained using the Adam optimizer on NVIDIA V100 GPUs for 100,000 iterations. Continual learning is performed for 1,000 iterations per task domain with learnable learning rates.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed Vision-Language Navigation with Continual Learning (VLNCL) paradigm and the Dual-loop Scenario Replay (Dual-SR) algorithm.

#### 4.1. Comparative Performance on Test Unseen Split

- The Dual-SR method, after continual learning, achieved a 16% increase in Success Rate (SR) and an 8% increase in Oracle Success Rate (OSR) compared to the base agent (before continual learning). Specifically, the base agent had an SR of 43% and OSR of 57%, while Dual-SR achieved an SR of 59% and OSR of 65%.
- Compared to several existing VLN agents, Dual-SR demonstrated competitive performance. For instance, Dual-SR's SR of 59% surpassed Seq2Seq (20%), SSM (61%, but with lower SPL), EnvDrop (51%), AuxRN (55%), CCC (51%), PREVALENT (54%), AirBERT (54%), and VLN⟳BERT (53%) on the test unseen split. The Nearest Error (NE) and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) metrics also showed improvements, with NE decreasing by 1.33 and SPL increasing by 14% relative to the base model.
- These results indicate that the continual learning approach, particularly Dual-SR, enhances the agent's task performance and generalization capabilities in unseen environments.

#### 4.2. Resisting Forgetting (Seen Transfer - ST)

- **Quantitative Results:** The Dual-SR method demonstrated superior resistance to catastrophic forgetting compared to a fine-tuning baseline.
 - On the Train Seen split, Dual-SR achieved an average Seen Transfer (ST) of -8.49, which is considerably better than the fine-tune baseline's ST of -15.10.
 - On the Val Seen split, Dual-SR had an ST of -6.00, also outperforming the fine-tune baseline's ST of -10.59.
- **Qualitative Analysis (Success Rate Change):** Visualizations showed that in the Train Seen and Val Seen splits, Dual-SR maintained substantial performance on seen tasks. The success rate drop was approximately 20% after processing a sequence of 50 different scenario tasks, which is presented as an effective mitigation of forgetting over a long sequence of task domains.

#### 4.3. Knowledge Transfer (Unseen Transfer - UT)

- **Quantitative Results:** Dual-SR showed positive knowledge transfer to unseen environments.
 - On the Val Unseen split, Dual-SR achieved an average Unseen Transfer (UT) of 2.18. This contrasts with the fine-tune baseline, which exhibited a negative UT of -8.03, indicating that fine-tuning degraded performance on unseen tasks.
- **Qualitative Analysis (Success Rate Change):** The experiments illustrated that Dual-SR consistently improved performance on unseen tasks in the Val Unseen split as the agent continually learned from new task domains. This demonstrates that the method effectively transfers knowledge from previously learned scenarios to novel ones.

#### 4.4. Tracking Minimum and Maximum Performance

- Tracking the minimum and maximum Success Rates (SR) and Oracle Success Rates (OSR) across all dataset splits (Train Seen, Val Seen, Val Unseen) revealed that Dual-SR maintained higher minimum performance levels compared to the fine-tune baseline. For example, in Val Unseen, Dual-SR's minimum SR was 45.65, significantly higher than fine-tune's 34.43. Similarly, Dual-SR achieved higher maximum SRs (e.g., 65.08 in Val Unseen compared to fine-tune's 52.56).
- The "Init" performance, representing the agent's capability before continual learning, served as a common starting point for comparison.

Overall, the findings indicate that Dual-SR effectively balances the learning of new tasks with the retention of prior knowledge, leading to enhanced generalization to unseen environments and reduced catastrophic forgetting. The work also establishes a benchmark for the VLNCL paradigm.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces the Vision-Language Navigation with Continual Learning (VLNCL) paradigm, which represents a notable advancement in the field of embodied intelligence. Its significance and potential impact stem from several aspects:

- **Addressing a Critical Gap in VLN Generalization:** The work directly confronts the long-standing challenge of poor generalization in Vision-Language Navigation agents when deployed in novel environments. By moving beyond static, one-time training, VLNCL offers a mechanism for agents to adapt continuously, which is essential for real-world applications where environments are dynamic and diverse. This could pave the way for more robust and versatile navigation systems in robotics and virtual agents.

- **Mitigation of Catastrophic Forgetting:** The proposed Dual-loop Scenario Replay (Dual-SR) algorithm provides a novel approach to mitigating catastrophic forgetting, a fundamental problem in continual learning. By effectively balancing stability (retaining old knowledge) and plasticity (learning new knowledge) through its dual-loop mechanism and multi-scenario memory buffer, the research contributes a brain-inspired solution that maintains agent performance over a sequence of diverse tasks.

- **Enhanced Knowledge Transfer:** The demonstrated ability of Dual-SR to achieve positive Unseen Transfer signifies that the agent can effectively leverage previously acquired knowledge to perform better in entirely new environments. This is crucial for practical deployment, as it implies faster adaptation and reduced training requirements in novel scenarios.

- **Establishment of a New Benchmark and Metrics:** By formally introducing the VLNCL paradigm, along with specific evaluation metrics (Seen Transfer and Unseen Transfer) and an experimental setup based on the R2R dataset, this work establishes a new benchmark for continual learning in VLN. This provides a standardized framework for future research, enabling clearer comparisons and accelerating progress in this area.

- **Reduced Resource Dependency:** The continual learning approach offers an alternative to the expensive and often impractical strategy of continuously expanding datasets to cover every possible environmental variation. This could lead to more resource-efficient training and deployment of VLN agents.

- **Inspiration for Future Research:** This work pioneers continual learning in VLN, opening new avenues for research. Future efforts could focus on integrating more sophisticated inference mechanisms, exploring different continual learning strategies within the VLN context, or applying these concepts to more complex, open-ended navigation tasks in real physical robots. The brain-inspired nature of Dual-SR also encourages further exploration of neuroscientific principles for artificial intelligence development.

In summary, this research advances VLN agents closer to real-world applicability by enabling them to learn continuously, adapt to unseen environments, and retain vital information over time, thereby setting a foundation for more intelligent and adaptable embodied AI systems.
