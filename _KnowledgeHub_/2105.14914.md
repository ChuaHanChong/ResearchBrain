# DILIGENT-KIO: A Proprioceptive Base Estimator for Humanoid Robots using Extended Kalman Filtering on Matrix Lie Groups

> [!summary] Summary
> DILIGENT-KIO, developed by researchers at the Italian Institute of Technology, introduces a proprioceptive base estimator for humanoid robots that applies a discrete Extended Kalman Filter on matrix Lie groups. This approach provides geometrically consistent uncertainty representation and demonstrates superior convergence and accurate orientation estimation compared to existing methods, particularly when initialized with large errors.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots require accurate state estimation (position, orientation, velocity of the floating base) using only internal sensors for stable control and dynamic movements.
- Traditional Extended Kalman Filters (EKFs) struggle with the non-Euclidean nature of rotations, leading to linearization errors, poor convergence, and issues like quaternion normalization.
- Existing invariant EKF methods often cannot accommodate rotational constraints imposed by flat foot contacts, which are critical for humanoid locomotion.

## Method

- **DILIGENT-KIO** employs a discrete ==Extended Kalman Filter== operating on ==matrix Lie groups==, using ==Concentrated Gaussian Distributions== to represent uncertainty.
- The system state is represented as a composite matrix Lie group (==SE₂(3) × SE(3)² × T(6)==) capturing base pose, foot poses, and IMU biases, specifically designed to handle flat foot contacts.
- The observation model uses ==forward kinematics== to relate joint encoder measurements to relative poses between feet and the base link, with all filter operations (prediction, update, ==Jacobians==) adapted to the Lie group structure.

## Results

- DILIGENT-KIO demonstrated considerably faster convergence than OCEKF across **25 trials** with random initial errors (**±30°** orientation, **±0.5 m/s** linear velocity).
- In walking experiments, DILIGENT-KIO achieved the lowest rotational Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) of **2.29°**, outperforming Invariant EKF (**3.34°**) and OCEKF (**4.67°**).
- The estimator maintained strong performance during center of mass trajectory tracking experiments with the lowest rotational ATE (**0.59°**) and comparable velocity and position accuracy to other methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing humanoid robot state and observations directly on matrix Lie groups maintains geometric consistency, which is crucial for handling non-Euclidean rotations and rigid body transformations.
> - A carefully constructed composite state representation can explicitly incorporate rotational constraints from flat foot contacts, which was a limitation of previous invariant observer designs.
> - Applying Discrete EKF on matrix Lie groups with Concentrated Gaussian Distributions leads to superior convergence properties, enabling faster recovery from large initialization errors in state estimation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Accurate state estimation is fundamental to autonomous humanoid robot operation, enabling these complex systems to maintain balance, execute dynamic movements, and interact safely with their environment. DILIGENT-KIO (DIscrete LIe Group ExteNded kalman filTer for a contact-aided Kinematic-Inertial Odometry) represents a significant advancement in proprioceptive base estimation for humanoid robots, addressing critical limitations in existing approaches through the application of matrix Lie group theory.

Developed by researchers at the Italian Institute of Technology and collaborating institutions, this work tackles the specific challenges of estimating a humanoid robot's floating base state—its position, orientation, and velocity in 3D space—using only internal sensors. The approach combines inertial measurement unit (IMU) data with joint encoder information through an Extended Kalman Filter framework that operates directly on matrix Lie groups, providing geometrically consistent uncertainty representation and enhanced convergence properties.

### 2. The Challenge of Humanoid State Estimation

Humanoid robots present unique challenges for state estimation that distinguish them from wheeled or tracked vehicles. As "floating-base" systems, humanoids have six additional degrees of freedom representing their base link's position and orientation in space, which cannot be directly measured. This floating base serves as the reference frame for all other robot motions, making its accurate estimation critical for stable control.

Traditional Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) approaches often struggle with the non-Euclidean nature of rotations and rigid body transformations. Conventional methods may suffer from issues like quaternion normalization constraints, linearization errors, and poor convergence when initialized with large errors. These limitations are particularly problematic for humanoid robots, which must recover from disturbances, operate in complex environments, and maintain stability during dynamic movements.

Existing approaches include the Observability Constrained EKF (OCEKF), which handles flat foot contacts but may lack convergence guarantees for large initialization errors, and Invariant EKF methods that provide strong theoretical properties but typically cannot accommodate the rotational constraints imposed by flat foot contacts—a critical requirement for humanoid locomotion.

### 3. Mathematical Framework and Methodology

DILIGENT-KIO employs a discrete Extended Kalman Filter operating on matrix Lie groups, leveraging Concentrated Gaussian Distributions (CGD) to represent uncertainty in a geometrically meaningful way. This mathematical framework provides several advantages over traditional Euclidean-based approaches.

The core innovation lies in representing both the system state and observations as elements of distinct matrix Lie groups. The state evolves on a composite Lie group structure, while observations (forward kinematic measurements) are represented on separate Lie groups. This dual approach maintains geometric consistency throughout the estimation process.

The system dynamics model describes the evolution of the robot's base pose using rigid body kinematics driven by strap-down IMU measurements. The base dynamics incorporate gravity compensation and bias estimation:

```
Ṙ = R[ω̃ - b_g - n_g]×
v̇ = R(a - b_a - n_a) + g
ṗ = v
```

where R represents rotation, ω is angular velocity, a is linear acceleration, b represents biases, n denotes noise terms, and g is gravity.

For feet in contact with the environment, a constant motion model assumes zero velocities with noise to account for potential slippage. IMU biases are modeled as slowly time-varying quantities affected by process noise.

### 4. State Representation and System Design

The state representation in DILIGENT-KIO is carefully constructed as a composite matrix Lie group that captures all quantities requiring estimation. The complete state X belongs to the product manifold SE₂(3) × SE(3)² × T(6), where:

- SE₂(3) represents the base link's position, orientation, and linear velocity
- Two instances of SE(3) capture left and right foot positions and orientations
- T(6) encompasses IMU accelerometer and gyroscope biases

This composite structure is specifically designed to handle flat foot contacts, which require including foot orientations in both the state and observation models. Unlike previous invariant observer designs that assumed point foot contacts, DILIGENT-KIO explicitly incorporates the rotational constraints imposed when feet are in flat contact with surfaces.

The observation model utilizes forward kinematics to relate joint encoder measurements to relative poses between feet and the base link. During single support phases, one foot's relative pose is observed, while double support phases provide observations of both feet's relative poses. The measurement noise is carefully mapped from joint space to the observation manifold using the manipulator Jacobian.

The filter operates through the standard prediction-update cycle, but all operations are adapted to the Lie group structure using exponential maps, logarithm maps, and specialized Jacobian computations that respect the underlying geometric properties.

### 5. Experimental Validation and Results

The authors conducted extensive experimental validation using the iCub humanoid robot, comparing DILIGENT-KIO against established baseline methods including OCEKF, Invariant EKF (InvEKF), and Simple Weighted Averaging (SWA).

The convergence analysis revealed DILIGENT-KIO's superior performance when initialized with large errors. Across 25 trials with random initial orientations (±30° for roll/pitch) and linear velocities (±0.5 m/s), DILIGENT-KIO demonstrated considerably faster convergence than OCEKF toward ground truth measurements. This enhanced convergence is attributed to the more sophisticated uncertainty representation enabled by the Lie group framework.

Performance comparisons using Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) and Relative Pose Error (RPE) metrics showed DILIGENT-KIO achieving the lowest rotational ATE in walking experiments (2.29° vs 3.34° for InvEKF and 4.67° for OCEKF). Velocity estimation performance was comparable to other methods (0.130 m/s vs 0.133 m/s for InvEKF and 0.132 m/s for OCEKF), while position accuracy was competitive with OCEKF.

In center of mass trajectory tracking experiments, DILIGENT-KIO consistently demonstrated strong performance with the lowest rotational ATE (0.59°) and comparable velocity and position accuracy. The results highlight the estimator's ability to maintain accurate tracking during dynamic movements while providing superior orientation estimation.

### 6. Key Contributions and Significance

DILIGENT-KIO makes several significant contributions to humanoid robot state estimation. The primary innovation is the rigorous application of matrix Lie group theory to both state representation and observation modeling, creating a geometrically consistent framework that fully embraces the non-Euclidean nature of rigid body transformations.

The work successfully addresses the flat foot contact limitations of existing invariant observers. Previous methods struggled to incorporate foot rotations without violating underlying group properties, but DILIGENT-KIO overcomes this through careful construction of the composite state space and measurement model. This advancement is crucial for humanoid robots, which rely on flat foot contacts for stable locomotion.

The enhanced convergence properties demonstrated experimentally represent a practical benefit for real-world applications. The estimator's ability to recover quickly from large initialization errors makes it more robust for deployment in challenging environments or recovery scenarios.

From a theoretical perspective, the paper provides a concrete example of applying Discrete EKF on matrix Lie groups with Concentrated Gaussian Distributions to a complex, high-dimensional robotic system. The detailed derivation of system dynamics and observation model Jacobians offers valuable guidance for researchers developing similar geometrically consistent estimators.

### 7. Future Directions and Limitations

While DILIGENT-KIO demonstrates significant improvements in convergence and orientation estimation, the authors acknowledge certain limitations that point toward future research directions. The estimator exhibits some position drift over extended operation periods, though orientation and velocity errors remain comparable to other methods. This suggests that long-term absolute position accuracy remains a challenge for purely proprioceptive approaches.

The authors identify fusion with exteroceptive sensor information (cameras, LiDAR) as a critical next step. Such integration could address the position drift issue while leveraging DILIGENT-KIO's strong proprioceptive foundation for comprehensive SLAM capabilities in humanoid robots.

Additionally, the framework did not explicitly exploit theories of autonomous error propagation or observability-based rules, which could potentially lead to filter inconsistencies. Future theoretical refinement in these areas could further enhance long-term consistency and optimal performance.

The work establishes a strong foundation for more capable and reliable humanoid robots by providing enhanced robustness and convergence in state estimation. As humanoid robots increasingly operate in complex, real-world environments, accurate and reliable proprioceptive state estimation becomes essential for safe and effective autonomous operation. DILIGENT-KIO represents a significant step toward achieving this goal through principled application of geometric methods to robotic state estimation.
