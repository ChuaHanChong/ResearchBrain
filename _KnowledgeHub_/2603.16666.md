# Fast-WAM: Do World Action Models Need Test-time Future Imagination?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and Galaxea AI developed Fast-WAM, a World Action Model (WAM) that achieves competitive robotic task performance with significantly reduced inference latency by leveraging video co-training during learning but skipping explicit future imagination at test time. This approach attains up to 97.6% success rates on LIBERO and RoboTwin benchmarks and over 4x faster inference compared to imagine-then-execute WAMs.

## Problem

- Existing World Action Models (WAMs) often suffer from high test-time latency due to iterative future visual prediction using an "imagine-then-execute" paradigm.
- The source of WAMs' performance benefits (training-time video modeling vs. test-time future imagination) has been unclear, as these factors are typically entangled in current designs.
- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, while generalizable, often lack explicit modeling of physical world dynamics and temporal evolution under actions.

## Method

- **Fast-WAM**, a ==Mixture-of-Transformer== architecture, decouples video co-training during the learning phase from explicit future imagination at inference.
- It employs a ==joint flow matching objective== to train both an action expert and a video backbone on shared initial visual context, with ==structured attention== preventing direct future video leakage into action prediction.
- During inference, the future video branch is entirely removed, enabling a single forward pass for action generation based on learned latent world features, which drastically reduces latency.

## Results

- Fast-WAM achieved competitive success rates on simulation benchmarks (**91.8%** on RoboTwin, **97.6%** on LIBERO) without extensive embodied pretraining, matching or exceeding many state-of-the-art pretrained WAMs.
- Controlled experiments demonstrated that removing the video co-training objective during training resulted in a substantial performance drop (e.g., to **83.8%** on RoboTwin), while Fast-WAM performed comparably to imagine-then-execute variants.
- Fast-WAM exhibited a low inference latency of **190 ms** on a real-world towel-folding task, which is over **4 times faster** than imagine-then-execute variants (e.g., Fast-WAM-IDM at 810 ms), while maintaining strong task performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The video modeling objective applied during training is a more critical factor for WAM performance than the explicit generation of future observations at test time.
> - Robust, physically meaningful world representations can be acquired through training-time video co-training without necessitating iterative future video generation during inference.
> - Decoupling training-time world modeling from inference-time generation can yield competitive performance with significantly improved efficiency for real-time robotic control.
> - The performance drop caused by removing video co-training was consistently larger than the differences between Fast-WAM and imagine-then-execute variants.
