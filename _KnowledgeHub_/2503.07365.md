# MM-Eureka: Exploring the Frontiers of Multimodal Reasoning with Rule-based Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Laboratory, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and The University of Hong Kong developed MM-Eureka, an open-source multimodal model utilizing rule-based reinforcement learning with novel online filtering and two-stage training strategies. This model achieves leading open-source performance in multimodal mathematical and multidisciplinary reasoning, scoring 74.8 on MathVista and 73.4 on WeMath, and is accompanied by the new MMK12 high-quality multimodal mathematical reasoning dataset.

## Problem

- Existing text-based LLM reasoning advancements via reinforcement learning have not effectively translated to multimodal scenarios.
- Prior multimodal reasoning efforts face limitations in task difficulty, small training scales, and instability when applying reinforcement learning techniques.
- There is a scarcity of high-quality, diverse, and human-verified multimodal mathematical reasoning datasets suitable for large-scale RL training.

## Method

- Constructed ==MMK12==, a high-quality and diverse K12-level multimodal mathematical reasoning dataset with human-verified answers and CoT solutions.
- Developed MM-Eureka, a **7B** and **32B** parameter multimodal model built on Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct, enhanced with a ==rule-based reinforcement learning framework==.
- Introduced ==online filtering== and a ==two-stage training strategy== (for the 32B model) to ensure stable and efficient large-scale VLM training with GRPO.

## Results

- MM-Eureka-32B achieved **74.8** on MathVista and **73.4** on WeMath, surpassing most open-source models and several closed-source counterparts.
- The models demonstrated strong generalization, improving performance by up to **11.2%** in out-of-distribution subjects like biology on the MMK12 evaluation set, despite being trained exclusively on math problems.
- MM-Eureka variants consistently ranked as top performers among open-source models on various multimodal mathematical reasoning tasks, narrowing the performance gap with proprietary systems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning primarily enhances reasoning by improving the utilization of a model's existing knowledge rather than acquiring entirely new factual information.
> - Rule-based RL offers superior generalization to out-of-distribution disciplines (e.g., physics, chemistry, biology) for reasoning tasks compared to Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).
> - Online filtering and a two-stage training approach are crucial for stabilizing large-scale VLM training with RL, preventing policy ratio instability and mitigating domain-specific performance degradation.
