# MM-Eureka: Exploring the Frontiers of Multimodal Reasoning with Rule-based Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Laboratory, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and The University of Hong Kong developed MM-Eureka, an open-source multimodal model utilizing rule-based reinforcement learning with novel online filtering and two-stage training strategies. This model achieves leading open-source performance in multimodal mathematical and multidisciplinary reasoning, scoring 74.8 on MathVista and 73.4 on WeMath, and is accompanied by the new MMK12 high-quality multimodal mathematical reasoning dataset.

## Problem

- Existing text-based LLM reasoning advancements via reinforcement learning have not effectively translated to multimodal scenarios.
- Prior multimodal reasoning efforts face limitations in task difficulty, small training scales, and instability when applying reinforcement learning techniques.
- There is a scarcity of high-quality, diverse, and human-verified multimodal mathematical reasoning datasets suitable for large-scale RL training.

## Method

- Constructed ==MMK12==, a high-quality and diverse K12-level multimodal mathematical reasoning dataset with human-verified answers and CoT solutions.
- Developed MM-Eureka, a **7B** and **32B** parameter multimodal model built on Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct, enhanced with a ==rule-based reinforcement learning framework==.
- Introduced ==online filtering== and a ==two-stage training strategy== (for the 32B model) to ensure stable and efficient large-scale VLM training with GRPO.

## Results

- MM-Eureka-32B achieved **74.8** on MathVista and **73.4** on WeMath, surpassing most open-source models and several closed-source counterparts.
- The models demonstrated strong generalization, improving performance by up to **11.2%** in out-of-distribution subjects like biology on the MMK12 evaluation set, despite being trained exclusively on math problems.
- MM-Eureka variants consistently ranked as top performers among open-source models on various multimodal mathematical reasoning tasks, narrowing the performance gap with proprietary systems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning primarily enhances reasoning by improving the utilization of a model's existing knowledge rather than acquiring entirely new factual information.
> - Rule-based RL offers superior generalization to out-of-distribution disciplines (e.g., physics, chemistry, biology) for reasoning tasks compared to Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).
> - Online filtering and a two-stage training approach are crucial for stabilizing large-scale VLM training with RL, preventing policy ratio instability and mitigating domain-specific performance degradation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "MM-EUREKA" is situated at the crucial intersection of Large Language Models (LLMs), Vision-Language Models (VLMs), and Reinforcement Learning (RL), specifically targeting the enhancement of multimodal reasoning capabilities.

**Current State of LLM Reasoning:** Recent breakthroughs, exemplified by models like OpenAI's o1 and DeepSeek-R1, have demonstrated the remarkable power of large-scale reinforcement learning in dramatically improving the reasoning abilities of LLMs in text-based domains, particularly in areas like mathematics and code generation. These models have shown that post-training RL can unlock advanced reasoning skills, sometimes even surpassing models trained with extensive supervised fine-tuning (SFT).

**Challenges in Multimodal Reasoning:** Despite the success in text, transferring these potent RL techniques to multimodal scenarios presents significant challenges. Many real-world reasoning tasks, such as interpreting scientific diagrams, solving geometry problems, or understanding complex visual information, inherently require both visual and textual comprehension. However, existing efforts to apply RL to multimodal reasoning have been constrained by:
- **Limited Task Difficulty:** Previous works often focus on simpler tasks (e.g., basic counting, specific geometric subdomains), failing to demonstrate robust reasoning across diverse, complex problems.
- **Small Training Scale:** The models employed are often of modest size, making it difficult to achieve the "aha moments" or emergent reasoning capabilities seen in larger LLMs.
- **Training Instability:** Achieving stable and long-term RL training in multimodal settings has proven difficult, leading to performance collapse or limited gains.
- **Closed-Source Landscape:** Many of the most powerful multimodal reasoning models (e.g., GPT-4o, Kimi k1.5, Claude3.7 Sonnet, Gemini2-flash, o1) are proprietary, hindering open-source research and development.

**MM-EUREKA's Contribution to the Landscape:** This paper directly addresses these gaps by proposing a comprehensive open-source solution.
- It introduces **MMK12**, a high-quality, diverse, and human-verified multimodal mathematics reasoning dataset, filling a critical void in training data for complex multimodal reasoning.
- It develops **MM-Eureka**, a multimodal model (7B and 32B parameters) that employs novel rule-based RL strategies (online filtering, two-stage training) to achieve *stable* and *effective* training on large-scale VLMs.
- By open-sourcing the entire pipeline—data, code, and models—the authors aim to democratize access to advanced multimodal reasoning capabilities and foster community-driven innovation, pushing the boundaries of what open-source VLMs can achieve. This work significantly advances the open-source community's ability to develop models that can compete with state-of-the-art closed-source systems in complex multimodal reasoning tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind the MM-EUREKA project stems from the observed disparity between the advanced reasoning capabilities of text-based LLMs through large-scale reinforcement learning and the nascent stage of such capabilities in multimodal settings. While LLMs like o1 and DeepSeek-R1 have demonstrated remarkable improvements in mathematical and coding reasoning via RL, these successes have not fully translated to scenarios requiring the integration of visual information. Many real-world problems inherently necessitate multimodal understanding, yet the multimodal community lacks a robust, stable, and open-source framework for achieving sophisticated reasoning.

The authors identify several specific limitations in existing multimodal reasoning efforts:
- **Lack of High-Quality Data:** Current datasets for multimodal mathematical reasoning are either limited in scope (e.g., geometry-only), rely on synthetic data lacking diversity, or suffer from unverified answers, making them suboptimal for training powerful reasoning models.
- **Training Instability and Scale Issues:** Attempts to transfer rule-based RL to multimodal models often face challenges in achieving stable training over extended periods, especially with larger model sizes, leading to suboptimal performance or training collapse.
- **Proprietary Nature of SOTA:** The leading multimodal reasoning models remain closed-source, impeding academic research, reproducibility, and open innovation.

To address these critical gaps, the key objectives of the MM-EUREKA project are:

1. **To construct MMK12, a high-quality and diverse multimodal K12-level mathematical reasoning dataset:** This objective aims to provide a rich and accurate data resource that covers a broad range of mathematical domains (geometry, functions, equations) and includes human-verified answers and detailed solution processes, suitable for advanced RL training.
2. **To develop and implement MM-Eureka, a powerful multimodal model capable of robust multidisciplinary reasoning:** This involves building 7B and 32B parameter variants based on existing strong VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct) and significantly enhancing their reasoning abilities through rule-based reinforcement learning.
3. **To establish a stable and efficient rule-based RL training pipeline for large-scale VLMs:** This objective focuses on innovating training strategies, such as online filtering and a two-stage training approach, to overcome the instability and scaling challenges prevalent in multimodal RL.
4. **To achieve state-of-the-art performance among open-source multimodal models and compete with closed-source counterparts:** The aim is to demonstrate significant performance gains on various multimodal mathematical and multidisciplinary reasoning benchmarks.
5. **To open-source the complete research pipeline (data, code, and models):** This crucial objective is driven by a commitment to fostering community research, enabling others to reproduce results, build upon their work, and accelerate progress in multimodal reasoning.
6. **To gain insights into the nature of knowledge and reasoning in VLMs:** Through experimentation, the authors seek to explore whether knowledge acquisition and reasoning enhancement can be decoupled and how RL compares to other post-training strategies like SFT in terms of generalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MM-EUREKA project employs a sophisticated methodology that integrates dataset creation with advanced reinforcement learning techniques to enhance multimodal reasoning in Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

#### 3.1. Base Models

The research utilizes **Qwen2.5-VL** as the base VLM, specifically its 7B and 32B parameter instruction-tuned variants (Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct-32B). These models are known for their strong general-purpose vision-language capabilities, serving as a solid foundation for further reasoning-focused training.

#### 3.2. Dataset Construction: MMK12

Recognizing the scarcity of high-quality multimodal reasoning data, the authors meticulously constructed **MMK12**, a K12-level (primary and secondary education) multimodal mathematical reasoning dataset.
- **Scope and Diversity:** MMK12 includes over 15,000 problems covering a wide array of mathematical domains (geometry, functions, equations, graphical reasoning, pattern reasoning) and extending to physics, chemistry, and biology for evaluation.
- **Data Collection:** Problems and corresponding answers/solutions were sourced from Chinese mathematics textbooks and examination papers, then translated and refined into English using LLMs.
- **Quality Control:** To ensure data reliability for RL training, only *fill-in-the-blank* problems were retained for the training set (15,616 samples), each accompanied by an image, final answer, and a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) formatted solution. Answers were programmatically verified using `Math-Verify`.
- **Evaluation Set:** A distinct evaluation set of 2,000 *multiple-choice* questions (500 per discipline: math, physics, chemistry, biology) was created, ensuring no overlap with the training data. This setup facilitates reliable and efficient assessment of multidisciplinary reasoning.

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Learning Framework

The core of the methodology is a rule-based Reinforcement Learning approach, adapted from DeepSeek-R1 and implemented within a multimodal input RL framework built on OpenRLHF.
- **RL Algorithm:** **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** (from DeepSeek-R1) is used as the base RL algorithm. GRPO is favored for its efficiency over traditional PPO as it optimizes the policy by leveraging intra-group relative performance, thus eliminating the need for a separate critic model.
- **Reward Function:** A simple, sparse, rule-based reward system is employed to alleviate issues like reward hacking:
 - **Accuracy Reward (r_accuracy ∈ {0, 1}):** Awarded 1 if the model's extracted answer (parsed by `Math-Verify`) matches the reference answer, 0 otherwise.
 - **Format Reward (r_format ∈ {0, 0.5}):** Awarded 0.5 if the response adheres to a specified format (`<think>...</think><answer>...</answer>`), 0 otherwise. This encourages structured and interpretable reasoning.

#### 3.4. Strategies for Training Stability and Efficiency

Two key strategies were introduced to address the challenges of stable, large-scale VLM training:
1. **Online Filtering:** Inspired by PRIME, this strategy dynamically filters out prompts during training for which the model's generated responses are either perfectly correct or completely incorrect (i.e., having zero advantage under GRPO). By focusing on "uncertain" examples, this ensures a continuous flow of meaningful gradients, preventing the model from collapsing or prematurely converging to trivial solutions. This was shown to be crucial for long-term stability.
2. **Two-Stage Training (for MM-Eureka-32B):** To further enhance stability and address domain-specific deficiencies in larger models, a two-stage approach was developed:
 - **Stage 1 (General Reasoning):** GRPO is applied using the MMK12 dataset *without* KL divergence regularization. This stage focuses on developing broad general reasoning capabilities and allows for greater exploration of the policy space.
 - **Stage 2 (Domain-Specific Refinement & Stability):** KL divergence regularization (with a small penalty) is *introduced* into GRPO, and the training data is augmented with domain-specific data, specifically **Geo3k** (a geometry dataset). This stage aims to mitigate performance degradation in specific domains (like geometry) and stabilize training by constraining the magnitude of policy updates, counteracting "ratio instability" which can lead to collapse.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Prompt Template:** A specific prompt template guides the model to first think about the reasoning process (`<think>...</think>`) and then provide the final answer in LaTeX format, wrapped in `\boxed{}` and enclosed in `<answer>...</answer>` tags.
- **Hyperparameters:** Rollout and training batch sizes of 128, 8 rollouts per sample, temperature of 1.0 for response diversity, and a learning rate of 1e-6. Greedy decoding (temperature 0) was used for evaluation.

This comprehensive methodology, combining a high-quality dataset with tailored RL algorithms and stability-enhancing training strategies, enabled the development of MM-Eureka as a robust multimodal reasoning model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The MM-EUREKA project yields significant findings across quantitative performance, qualitative reasoning, and insights into VLM training dynamics.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance

MM-Eureka models, in both 7B and 32B variants, demonstrate remarkable performance gains across various multimodal mathematical and multidisciplinary benchmarks.

- **MM-Eureka-7B:**
 - Achieves **73.0 on MathVista**, outperforming InternVL-78B (72.3) and the reasoning-focused InternVL2.5-38B-MPO (73.8) by 0.7% and 0.2% respectively.
 - Scores **66.1 on WeMath**, approaching InternVL-78B's performance.
 - On the MMK12 evaluation sets, it scores **64.5 (Avg.)**, demonstrating strong multidisciplinary capabilities.

- **MM-Eureka-32B:**
 - Exhibits even stronger performance, scoring **74.8 on MathVista**, surpassing all open-source and several closed-source models (including Claude3.7 Sonnet).
 - Achieves **73.4 on WeMath**, outperforming all open-source models and most closed-source models including Claude3.7 Sonnet (72.6).
 - On the multidisciplinary MMK12 evaluation sets, it scores **72.2 (Avg.)**, significantly exceeding Qwen2.5-VL-72B (70.5) and closed-source Gemini2-Flash (65.2), trailing only slightly behind OpenAI's o1 (73.9) by 1.7 percentage points.

- **Overall Open-Source Leadership:** MM-Eureka variants consistently rank as top performers among open-source models of similar sizes across almost all evaluated multimodal mathematical reasoning tasks.
- **Comparison with Closed-Source Models:** While MM-Eureka-32B outperforms o1 on MathVista and many other closed-source models on WeMath and MMK12, it still shows performance gaps against models like o1 on highly challenging benchmarks such as MathVision and OlympiadBench.
- **Generalization to Multidisciplinary Tasks:** A notable finding is the models' strong generalization. Despite being trained *exclusively on fill-in-the-blank mathematical problems*, MM-Eureka maintains robust instruction-following for *multiple-choice questions* and significantly enhances performance in *physics, chemistry, and biology*. For instance, MM-Eureka-7B showed improvements of 9.8% in chemistry and 11.2% in biology on the MMK12 evaluation set, demonstrating the remarkable generalization capacity of the simple RL strategy.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

Qualitative examples (provided in Appendix A of the original paper) comparing MM-Eureka-32B with its base model (Qwen2.5-VL-32B-Instruct) across mathematics, physics, chemistry, and biology highlight MM-Eureka's enhanced reasoning. The RL-trained model consistently:
- Applies known concepts more effectively.
- Performs multi-step deduction accurately.
- Breaks down problems into logical intermediate steps.
- Synthesizes knowledge to reach correct conclusions.
In contrast, the base model often displays superficial understanding, recalling facts but failing to apply them coherently or making incorrect deductions, demonstrating the clear impact of RL training on reasoning depth.

#### 4.3. Key Findings from Discussion

The authors derive several crucial insights from their development process:
1. **Decoupling of Knowledge and Reasoning:** The study suggests that performance improvements from RL primarily stem from increasing the probability of generating correct answers for problems the model *already possesses some knowledge about*. It's difficult for RL to acquire entirely *new* factual knowledge; instead, it enhances the model's ability to *reason* with its existing knowledge base. For problems where the base model produced zero correct responses out of eight attempts, MM-Eureka showed little improvement in the number of such problems.
2. **Superior Generalization of RL over SFT:** Comparative studies on MMK12 show that rule-based RL significantly outperforms both standard Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Chain-of-Thought SFT (CoT SFT). RL leads to more substantial improvements in core mathematical capabilities (e.g., +12.8 points in math) and, critically, demonstrates much stronger generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) disciplines like physics, chemistry, and biology. SFT/CoT SFT showed negligible gains in these areas.
3. **Effectiveness of Two-Stage Training:** The two-stage training strategy for the 32B model successfully addresses stability issues (preventing sudden collapses caused by policy ratio instability) and mitigates performance declines in specific domains (e.g., geometry, which initially dropped after Stage 1, but recovered significantly after Stage 2 with Geo3k data). The reintroduction of KL divergence in Stage 2 was crucial for stabilizing training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MM-EUREKA project represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal reasoning, with profound implications and potential impacts.

#### 5.1. Advancing Open-Source Multimodal Reasoning

The most direct and impactful contribution is pushing the state-of-the-art for open-source multimodal reasoning models. MM-Eureka-32B's performance, rivaling or surpassing many closed-source counterparts on several benchmarks and being only marginally behind o1 on multidisciplinary K12 reasoning, demonstrates that high-level multimodal reasoning is increasingly attainable within the open-source community. This narrows the gap with proprietary models, fostering greater accessibility and collaboration in AI research.

#### 5.2. Stable and Efficient Large-Scale Multimodal RL

The development and empirical validation of stable rule-based reinforcement learning techniques for large-scale VLMs are crucial. The online filtering strategy and the two-stage training approach provide concrete, actionable methods to address the notorious instability and scaling challenges of RL in multimodal contexts. These strategies are vital for enabling more researchers to successfully apply RL to VLMs, accelerating progress in areas where stable training has been a bottleneck.

#### 5.3. High-Quality Data and Comprehensive Open-Sourcing

The creation of the MMK12 dataset, a diverse, high-quality, and human-verified resource for multimodal mathematical reasoning, addresses a critical data scarcity issue. Coupled with the complete open-sourcing of models, code, and data, this project significantly lowers the barrier to entry for researchers. This open framework will facilitate reproducibility, enable rapid iteration, and encourage a broader range of investigations into multimodal reasoning and large model training.

#### 5.4. Insights into Knowledge and Reasoning Decoupling

The finding that RL primarily enhances reasoning by improving the *utilization* of existing knowledge, rather than by acquiring entirely new knowledge, offers a fundamental insight into the learning mechanisms of large models. This suggests a potential decoupling of knowledge acquisition and reasoning enhancement, opening new avenues for future research into specialized training paradigms where base models focus on knowledge grounding, and subsequent RL fine-tuning focuses on reasoning and problem-solving. Furthermore, the empirical evidence that RL generalizes better than SFT for reasoning tasks, particularly to out-of-distribution domains, underscores its power as a post-training strategy.

#### 5.5. Potential for Broader Applications

The improved multimodal reasoning capabilities developed in MM-Eureka have immediate and far-reaching practical implications:
- **Education Technology:** Automated problem-solving, intelligent tutoring systems, and personalized learning platforms could leverage these models to help students with complex scientific and mathematical problems involving diagrams.
- **Scientific Research:** Assisting scientists in interpreting complex charts, graphs, and experimental data across various disciplines.
- **General AI Assistants:** Enhancing the ability of AI assistants to understand and respond to multimodal queries, making them more capable in real-world scenarios that blend visual and textual information.
- **Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** Potentially improving environmental understanding and decision-making in systems that rely on processing visual data in conjunction with symbolic reasoning.

In conclusion, "MM-EUREKA" not only presents a powerful new open-source multimodal reasoning model but also provides critical methodological advancements and fundamental insights that will significantly shape the future direction of research in large-scale multimodal AI.
