# SEM: Enhancing Spatial Understanding for Robust Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Horizon, Beijing, developed SEM, a diffusion-based framework that creates a unified spatial representation by projecting multi-view image features and robot states into a shared 3D coordinate frame. This approach yields an average success rate of 78.38% on RoboTwin 2.0 tasks and significantly improves 3D and embodiment generalization over baselines in both simulation and real-world manipulation.

## Problem

- Mainstream robot manipulation models often lack explicit 3D reasoning, relying on 2D image backbones that limit understanding of 3D geometry and object relationships.
- Existing methods typically process visual and robot state features independently, leading to inconsistent feature spaces and hindering seamless integration of diverse sensory data.
- Policies struggle with 3D and embodiment generalization, performing poorly with varying camera viewpoints, object arrangements, or when transferring skills to different robot platforms.

## Method

- **SEM** is a ==diffusion-based== framework that establishes a ==unified spatial representation== by projecting multi-view image features and joint-centric robot states into a common 3D robot base frame.
- Introduces a ==Spatial Enhancer== module that integrates camera models and depth information to generate 3D positional embeddings from 2D visual features.
- Employs a ==Robot State Encoder== with forward kinematics and ==joint graph attention== to model robot embodiment structure and provide a joint-centric spatial representation.

## Results

- Achieved **78.38%** average success on **16** RoboTwin 2.0 tasks, outperforming DP3 (**56.25%**) and RDT (**41.25%**).
- Improved 3D spatial generalization with slower success-rate decay under camera-height variation compared to RDT (zero-shot).
- Multi-embodiment training improved UR5 task performance by **+5.5%**; consistently outperformed RDT on 7 real-world tasks with **+25.8%** higher average success rate.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Constructing a unified spatial representation in the robot base frame effectively aligns multi-modal inputs, reducing ambiguity from varying viewpoints and diverse robot embodiments.
> - Explicitly injecting 3D information via a spatial enhancer is crucial for policies to robustly reason about 3D geometry, significantly improving performance.
> - Joint graph attention within the robot state encoder enables effective modeling of robot embodiment structure, promoting generalization across different robot platforms and joint configurations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation has seen rapid advancements, particularly with the adoption of deep learning techniques. Early efforts focused on imitation learning from human demonstrations, evolving into sophisticated action modeling approaches like implicit behavioral cloning (IBC) and diffusion policies (DP). Diffusion Policy, in particular, has emerged as a powerful paradigm for generating high-dimensional action trajectories, demonstrating strong generative capacity.

However, a persistent bottleneck in these mainstream models, including many diffusion-based policies, has been their limited *spatial understanding*. Most rely on 2D image inputs and vision backbones primarily trained for 2D understanding (e.g., object detection, visual grounding). This inherently limits their ability to explicitly reason about 3D geometry, object relationships in a 3D space, and the robot's own embodiment state in a consistent manner. Consequently, these models often struggle with 3D and embodiment generalization, exhibiting fragility when confronted with variations in camera viewpoints, object positions, or different robot morphologies.

Concurrent research has attempted to inject 3D information into manipulation policies. Methods like PerAct and Act3D encode voxelized or point-sampled 3D features to predict end-effector (EE) keyframes. Other approaches, such as RVT and KStarDiffuser, also focus on EE pose prediction, sometimes incorporating graph convolutions over joint trajectories. More recently, the advent of large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has spurred research into generalist action models (e.g., RDT, Pi0, Octo). These VLM-based policies leverage the semantic understanding capabilities of VLMs but still face challenges in real-world performance and spatial generalization, as they often do not explicitly model camera extrinsics or align visual and robot states in a common 3D space. SpatialVLA is a notable related effort that enhances VLMs with 3D position embeddings, though it also primarily outputs EE poses and lacks a unified spatial alignment across cameras and robot states.

SEM positions itself at the intersection of diffusion-based policies, 3D feature learning, and VLM integration, specifically addressing the gap in *consistent spatial understanding* and *robust generalization*. Unlike many prior works that focus on EE pose prediction, SEM directly predicts joint positions, offering finer control for complex tasks. Crucially, it distinguishes itself from VLM-based generalist models by prioritizing network design for improved 3D spatial understanding over merely leveraging a large pretrained VLM. By proposing a framework that explicitly constructs a *unified spatial representation* in a common 3D coordinate frame (the robot base frame), SEM directly tackles the limitations of 2D perceptual bottlenecks and inconsistent feature spaces that plague existing methods. This unified representation, coupled with explicit modeling of camera parameters and robot embodiment structure, represents a significant step towards creating robot manipulation policies that are inherently more robust and generalizable to varying environments and robot platforms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of the SEM framework is to develop robot manipulation policy models with **consistent spatial understanding**, enabling them to robustly reason about 3D geometry, object relations, and the robot's own state within a unified and consistent spatial context.

The motivation for this objective stems from critical limitations identified in existing mainstream robot manipulation models:
1. **Lack of Explicit 3D Reasoning:** Most current models, particularly those based on 2D image inputs, do not perform explicit 3D modeling. Their vision backbones are primarily trained for 2D image understanding, leading to a deficiency in reasoning about the inherent 3D nature of manipulation tasks.
2. **Inconsistent Feature Spaces:** Visual features (from cameras) and robot state features (joint angles) are typically processed independently without proper spatial alignment. This disconnect hinders the policy's ability to seamlessly integrate diverse sensory and proprioceptive information.
3. **Ineffective Modeling of Robot Embodiment:** The varying physical structures of different robots (embodiments) are often not effectively modeled. This leads to ambiguities when training policies on heterogeneous datasets, as the same joint index might correspond to distinct physical motions across robots, severely limiting embodiment generalization.
4. **Limited Generalization:** The aforementioned issues result in policies that lack strong 3D and embodiment generalization capabilities. They perform poorly when camera viewpoints change, objects are rearranged in 3D space, or when transferring skills to new robot platforms or sensor configurations.

To overcome these challenges, SEM aims to construct a **unified spatial representation** that projects multi-view image features and joint-centric robot states into a single, consistent 3D coordinate space, specifically the robot base frame. This unified representation is hypothesized to provide a robust foundation for diffusion-based action generation, leading to enhanced spatial understanding, improved generalization, and superior performance across a wide range of manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SEM (Spatial Enhanced Manipulation) is a diffusion-based policy framework designed to foster robust robot manipulation through enhanced spatial understanding. Its core innovation lies in constructing a unified spatial representation by projecting multi-view image features and joint-centric robot states into a shared 3D coordinate frame (the robot base frame). The overall architecture comprises five key sub-modules: a Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone, a depth encoder, a spatial enhancer, a robot state encoder, and an action decoder.

**A. Unified Spatial Representation:**
The fundamental idea is to align all relevant inputs – visual observations and robot proprioception – into a single, consistent 3D space. This mitigates ambiguities arising from varying camera viewpoints and diverse robot embodiments.

**B. Vision-Language Model (VLM) Backbone:**
SEM is designed to be pluggable with any existing VLM (e.g., GroundingDINO Tiny or Qwen-2.5-VL-3B). The VLM extracts image features (F_I) and text features (F_T), leveraging its strong semantic understanding capabilities. The parameters of the VLM backbone can be frozen during training, reducing the number of trainable parameters in the action generation pipeline.

**C. Spatial Enhancer:**
This module is critical for injecting explicit 3D information into 2D visual features and fusing them with depth data.
- **Camera Model Integration:** It takes multi-view image features, depth features, and camera parameters (intrinsic and extrinsic matrices) as input. For each image feature, multiple depth values are sampled along the camera frustum. Corresponding pixel coordinates are then projected into 3D space using the camera model and converted into high-dimensional position embeddings (E_P) in the robot's base coordinate frame.
- **Depth-Aware Feature Fusion:** A discrete depth distribution is predicted from fused image and depth features. This distribution is used to compute a weighted sum over the 3D point embeddings, yielding a 3D positional embedding (E_ij) for each image feature.
- **Feature Combination:** Finally, the original image feature, depth feature (if available), and the newly generated 3D positional embedding are fused via an MLP to produce "enhanced features with better spatial understanding" (F_3D).
- **Flexibility:** The depth feature input is pluggable; if depth sensors are unavailable, the system can predict the depth distribution directly from image features, similar to monocular depth estimation.

**D. Robot State Encoder:**
This module encodes the current robot state (S) in a spatially aware manner.
- **Enhanced State Representation:** Beyond just joint angles, the 6D pose (3D coordinates and quaternion rotation) of each joint in the robot's base frame is computed via forward kinematics. These poses are concatenated with joint angles to form an enhanced state representation (S'), containing 8 dimensions per joint. This provides more informative physical state data, crucial for different embodiments.
- **Joint-Centric Modeling with Graph Attention:** The enhanced state features are projected into a high-dimensional space and fed into a transformer encoder. The core component is **joint graph attention**, which models the robot's embodiment structure. It uses a joint distance matrix (J) representing the number of links between joints to encode relative positions, facilitating feature propagation among joints. This joint-centric design inherently generalizes to robots with arbitrary numbers of joints.

**E. Spatial-Aware Action Decoder:**
The action decoder is a diffusion transformer that takes all conditioned features (enhanced image, text, and robot state features) to predict future joint trajectories.
- **Diffusion Process:** It samples noise on joint angles, then computes noisy robot states (S'_noise) by obtaining 6D poses via forward kinematics.
- **Joint-Centric Design:** Similar to the state encoder, it employs joint graph attention for interactions among joints.
- **Multi-Modal Conditioning:** It uses three independent cross-attention layers to attend to robot state, image, and text features respectively. Temporal cross-attention operates causally.
- **Structured Prediction and Supervision:** An output head generates the structured prediction (S'_pred) consisting of both joint angles and 6D poses. While only joint angles are used for control, the predicted 6D poses serve as auxiliary supervision, providing stronger constraints and improving positional accuracy.

**F. Training Loss:**
SEM optimizes a composite loss function combining four terms:
- **Joint Position Loss (L_joint):** L1 distance between predicted and ground truth joint angles.
- **Joint Pose Loss (L_pose):** L1 distance between predicted and ground truth 6D joint poses.
- **Forward Kinematics Pose Loss (L_fk_pose):** An additional L1 distance between 6D poses recomputed from *predicted* joint angles (via FK) and ground truth 6D poses. This provides crucial consistency constraints between joint angles and their resulting poses.
- **Depth Loss (L_depth):** Cross-entropy loss on the depth distribution predicted by the spatial enhancer.
This multi-component loss ensures comprehensive supervision across spatial and motion dimensions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SEM was extensively evaluated in both simulation and real-world settings, demonstrating significant improvements over existing baselines, particularly in spatial understanding and generalization.

**A. Simulation Benchmarks (RoboTwin 2.0):**
- **Superior Single-Task Performance:** On 16 representative tasks using the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark with the aloha-agilex embodiment, SEM-QW (using Qwen2.5-VL-3B) achieved an average success rate of 78.38%, outperforming DP3 (56.25%), Pi0 (52.94%), ACT (42.00%), RDT (41.25%), and DP (31.06%). SEM showed exceptional strength in high-precision tasks like 'block ranking rgb' (80.0% vs. 3.0% for DP3, 0.0% for DP).
- **Strong Multi-Task Performance:** In multi-task scenarios, SEM-GD (GroundingDINO Tiny) and SEM-QW achieved 84.5% and 84.62% average success rates, respectively, significantly outperforming the baseline RDT-1B (55.12%). Notably, SEM achieved these results with a considerably lighter action decoder and fewer trainable parameters compared to RDT-1B.

**B. 3D Spatial Generalization Test:**
- **Robustness to Camera Height Variations:** When the height of environment cameras was adjusted (Fig. 4b), SEM consistently yielded higher average success rates than RDT in zero-shot testing. Its normalized success rate exhibited a significantly slower decay rate, indicating better robustness to changes in camera viewpoints. This was further confirmed in a single-task, single-view setting, proving SEM's superior ability to generalize spatially.

**C. Embodiment Generalization Test:**
- **Positive Impact of Multi-Embodiment Training:** Training SEM on a mixed dataset of AgileX-Aloha and Dual-UR5 robots led to a 5.5% performance improvement on the UR5 tasks compared to training solely on UR5 data. In contrast, RDT showed a substantial performance drop of 26.69% in this mixed-data setting, highlighting its sensitivity to multi-embodiment data ambiguity.
- **Enhanced Fine-tuning Efficiency:** Pre-training SEM on AgileX data and then fine-tuning it on the UR5 dataset resulted in significantly faster convergence, demonstrating SEM's ability to leverage knowledge across different robot platforms.

**D. Ablation Studies:**
- **Effectiveness of Spatial Enhancer:** Removing depth features from the spatial enhancer reduced average success by 8.5%, and entirely removing the spatial enhancer led to a 15.72% reduction, confirming its critical role in geometric awareness.
- **Importance of Unified 3D Space:** Replacing the unified base-frame 3D position embedding for images with camera-frame embeddings resulted in a 3.37% performance decrease, validating the benefit of a consistent 3D coordinate system.
- **Contribution of Joint Graph Attention:** Replacing joint graph attention with vanilla attention caused a 4.62% reduction in performance, demonstrating its value in modeling embodiment structure and promoting generalization across joints.

**E. Real-World Experiments:**
- **Consistent Outperformance:** SEM was evaluated on seven real-world tasks (five from RoboTwin, two novel fine-grained and deformable object tasks). Whether pre-trained on simulation data or open-source real-world datasets, SEM consistently and significantly outperformed RDT. For example, when pre-trained on open-source real-world data, SEM achieved an average success rate of 72.6%, a gain of 25.8% over RDT+ (46.8%). This strong real-world validation underscores SEM's practical applicability and robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SEM framework presents a significant advancement in robot manipulation, primarily by addressing the long-standing challenge of achieving robust and generalizable policies through enhanced spatial understanding. Its key contributions and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Bridging the 2D-3D Perception Gap:** By explicitly constructing a unified spatial representation that projects multi-view image features and joint-centric robot states into a shared 3D base frame, SEM effectively bridges the gap between 2D visual perception and 3D physical reasoning. This is crucial for robots operating in complex real-world environments where precise 3D understanding of object locations, relationships, and the robot's own geometry is paramount.

2. **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:**
 - **3D Spatial Generalization:** SEM's ability to reason robustly under varying camera viewpoints (e.g., changes in camera height) is critical for real-world deployment, where sensor configurations might differ or objects may appear from unexpected angles. This reduces the need for extensive data collection under every possible viewing condition.
 - **Embodiment Generalization:** The joint-centric robot state encoder with graph attention, combined with the unified 3D space, allows SEM to generalize across different robot morphologies. This is a crucial step towards training truly generalist robot policies on diverse datasets, enabling skill transfer between robots and accelerating the development of new robotic systems. The ability to benefit from mixed-embodiment data, unlike baselines that suffer performance drops, highlights its potential for large-scale multi-robot learning.

3. **Improved Robustness and Precision:** The comprehensive training loss, including the novel forward kinematics pose loss, provides stronger and more informative supervision. This, coupled with the explicit spatial modeling, leads to more robust and precise manipulation, as evidenced by SEM's superior performance on tasks requiring high accuracy and its strong real-world results, even for challenging deformable object tasks.

4. **Practical Implications for Industrial Robotics:** Given the authors' affiliation with Horizon, an AI chip company, the emphasis on a lightweight action decoder and robust performance in varied settings suggests a focus on deployable, efficient solutions. SEM's design, which can leverage powerful VLMs for semantic understanding while maintaining a smaller, specialized action generation component, aligns with a hierarchical and efficient system architecture suitable for embedded and edge computing platforms. This could accelerate the adoption of advanced AI-driven manipulation in manufacturing, logistics, and service robotics.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** SEM provides a strong foundation for future research directions. The authors suggest refining relative joint distances, integrating more robust depth prediction models, pre-training on larger datasets, and exploring reinforcement learning to further enhance policy robustness. The framework's modularity, particularly the pluggable VLM backbone and depth features, allows for continuous integration of advancements in computer vision and language understanding.

In conclusion, SEM represents a significant step towards creating robot manipulation policies that are not only highly performant but also inherently more intelligent, adaptable, and capable of operating in the messy, unstructured realities of the physical world. By prioritizing explicit spatial understanding and generalization, it addresses fundamental limitations of current approaches and paves the way for more autonomous and versatile robotic systems.
