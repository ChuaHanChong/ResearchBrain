# Learning to Reason: End-to-End Module Networks for Visual Question Answering

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of California, Berkeley and collaborators developed End-to-End Module Networks (N2NMNs), a Visual Question Answering model that learns to dynamically construct specialized neural network architectures for each question. The model achieved 83.7% overall accuracy on the CLEVR dataset, outperforming prior attentional approaches, and delivered 64.9% accuracy on the VQA dataset, offering interpretable reasoning procedures.

## Problem

- Many prior VQA models exploited dataset biases instead of performing explicit compositional reasoning, limiting robustness and interpretability.
- Existing Neural Module Networks (NMNs) relied on external linguistic parsers not optimized for vision-language tasks, leading to brittle and suboptimal network layout predictions.
- Traditional VQA architectures were fixed and lacked the dynamic adaptability needed to address diverse question complexities or provide interpretable reasoning steps.

## Method

- Deployed a collection of ==co-attentive neural modules==, each parameterized by ==soft attention== over question words, to perform specialized sub-tasks.
- Implemented a ==layout policy== using an ==attentional sequence-to-sequence RNN== to dynamically predict question-specific network architectures, represented as sequences in ==Reverse Polish Notation==.
- Developed an end-to-end training framework combining initial ==behavioral cloning== from an expert policy with subsequent ==reinforcement learning== to jointly optimize module parameters and layout prediction.

## Results

- Demonstrated **83.7%** overall accuracy on the CLEVR dataset, achieving nearly a **50%** relative error reduction compared to previous state-of-the-art attentional VQA approaches.
- Attained **64.9%** accuracy on the VQA dataset using ResNet-152 features, which is comparable to the VQA 2016 challenge winner and provides an interpretable reasoning process.
- The two-stage training, particularly the reinforcement learning-based policy search after initial behavioral cloning, consistently improved performance across various question types by discovering more optimal reasoning strategies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning to predict network layouts directly from questions, rather than relying on external linguistic parsers, makes the VQA system more robust and self-contained.
> - Employing soft attention mechanisms for module parameterization enables flexible, context-aware module behavior by dynamically focusing on relevant question words.
> - Combining behavioral cloning with policy gradient reinforcement learning provides an effective strategy for end-to-end training of models that dynamically generate discrete network architectures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

This research is situated within the field of Visual Question Answering (VQA), an area of artificial intelligence that requires models to jointly interpret images and natural language questions to produce appropriate answers.

Prior VQA approaches, particularly those based on conventional deep networks, have demonstrated notable performance but have faced limitations. A significant concern in the VQA community is that many successful models primarily leverage statistical biases present in datasets rather than performing explicit compositional reasoning. Their monolithic network structures also tend to be difficult to interpret, and they apply the same fixed architecture regardless of the input question's complexity or specific reasoning requirements. For instance, some models have shown competitive performance with basic bag-of-words text representations, suggesting limited linguistic compositionality.

The concept of Neural Module Networks (NMNs) was introduced as an advancement to address compositional reasoning. NMNs operate by parsing natural language questions into linguistic sub-structures and dynamically assembling question-specific deep networks from a collection of smaller, specialized modules. However, existing NMN implementations, such as those by Andreas et al., relied on external, general-purpose linguistic parsers. These parsers were not specifically designed for vision-language tasks and often required manual adaptation, leading to limitations in predicting valid or optimal module configurations. Furthermore, these NMNs typically parameterized individual modules using fixed embeddings or hard-coded assignments derived from the parser. Another related approach, compositional modular networks, avoided an external parser but was restricted to a predefined, fixed (subject, relationship, object) structure.

More broadly, the problem of automatically discovering neural network architectures has been explored through various methods, including evolutionary algorithms, Bayesian methods, and reinforcement learning. While some of these learn a fixed architecture, the challenge in VQA lies in adapting the architecture dynamically per input.

The development of new VQA datasets like CLEVR has further underscored the need for models capable of genuine compositional reasoning. CLEVR specifically targets complex, multi-step logical inference, presenting significant challenges to models that rely on statistical shortcuts or non-modular architectures.

This work, End-to-End Module Networks (N2NMNs), advances the NMN paradigm by addressing the dependency on external parsers and the limitations of module parameterization. It aims to integrate the layout prediction process directly into the learning framework, allowing the model to learn to generate instance-specific, modular network architectures and their parameters end-to-end. By doing so, it attempts to provide a more robust, flexible, and interpretable solution for compositional reasoning in VQA.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a Visual Question Answering (VQA) model that can perform explicit compositional reasoning by dynamically constructing specialized neural network architectures for each input question. This objective is motivated by several limitations observed in prior VQA systems and existing modular approaches.

The specific motivations and objectives include:

- **Overcoming limitations of traditional VQA models:** Many VQA models, particularly those with monolithic architectures, have been observed to rely on statistical biases in datasets rather than genuinely understanding and reasoning about both visual and linguistic inputs. This leads to concerns about their true reasoning capabilities and limits their robustness when encountering out-of-distribution questions or images. The authors aim to develop a system that can engage in explicit, step-by-step reasoning.
- **Addressing the "black box" nature of deep learning models:** Monolithic deep networks often lack interpretability, making it challenging to understand how they arrive at a particular answer. By constructing dynamic, modular networks, the authors intend to provide a more transparent and explainable reasoning process, where each module can be conceptually linked to a sub-task.
- **Eliminating reliance on external linguistic parsers in NMNs:** Previous Neural Module Network (NMN) implementations, such as those described in, critically depend on off-the-shelf linguistic parsers to decompose questions into sub-problems and propose network layouts. These parsers are not optimized for vision-language tasks and frequently produce invalid or suboptimal layouts. This dependency introduces brittleness and restricts the possible network configurations to those suggested by the parser. A key objective is to remove this dependency, allowing the model to *learn* to predict suitable network layouts directly from the question, without external linguistic resources at test time.
- **Enabling dynamic and instance-specific network structures:** Questions in VQA are diverse, and the optimal reasoning procedure can vary significantly from one question to another. Traditional fixed-architecture models cannot adapt to this variability. The authors aim to develop a "layout policy" that can dynamically predict a unique computational graph tailored to each specific input question, thereby enhancing efficiency and reasoning accuracy.
- **Improving module parameterization:** Earlier NMNs often used hard-coded textual assignments to parameterize individual modules (e.g., `describe['shape']`). This approach lacks flexibility and might not capture nuanced linguistic context. An objective is to introduce a more flexible and learned parameterization mechanism, specifically soft attention over question words, allowing modules to dynamically focus on relevant parts of the question.
- **Leveraging new diagnostic datasets:** The emergence of datasets like CLEVR, which are specifically designed to test compositional language and elementary visual reasoning, highlights the need for models that can genuinely perform multi-step inference. The paper aims to develop a model that performs robustly on such challenging, reasoning-focused benchmarks.
- **Developing an end-to-end training framework:** Training models that dynamically generate network architectures presents optimization challenges due to the discrete nature of architecture prediction. A core objective is to develop an end-to-end training methodology, combining differentiable components with reinforcement learning techniques, potentially utilizing behavioral cloning from expert demonstrations as an initialization strategy.

In summary, the motivation stems from the perceived limitations of current VQA models in compositional reasoning, interpretability, and flexibility. The objectives focus on creating a self-contained, end-to-end trainable system that can learn to construct and execute dynamic, interpretable, and question-specific neural networks for robust visual question answering.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The proposed End-to-End Module Networks (N2NMNs) model addresses compositional visual reasoning through two core components: a set of co-attentive neural modules and a layout policy that predicts question-specific network structures.

#### 3.1. Attentional Neural Modules

The model utilizes a collection of neural modules, each representing a parameterized function capable of performing specific sub-tasks. A module $m$ is defined as $y = f_m(a_1, a_2, \ldots; x_{vis}, x_{txt}, \theta_m)$, taking zero, one, or multiple image attention maps ($a_i$) as input. It operates using its internal parameters ($\theta_m$) and features extracted from the image ($x_{vis}$) and question ($x_{txt}$). The module's output ($y$) can be either an image attention map or a probability distribution over possible answers.

The set of modules is designed to cover various reasoning operations:
- **Attention-generating modules:** `find` (localizes objects/attributes), `relocate` (transforms attention maps for spatial/relationship inference).
- **Attention-manipulating modules:** `and` (intersection of two attention maps), `or` (union of two attention maps), `filter` (reuses `find` and `and` to refine attention).
- **Answer-generating modules:**
 - Simple inference (based on attention map only): `exist`, `count`.
 - Complex inference (requiring visual appearance): `describe`.
 - Pairwise comparison (based on two attention maps): `eq count`, `more`, `less`.
 - Pairwise comparison (requiring visual features): `compare`.

A key distinction from prior NMN implementations is the method for obtaining textual input ($x_{txt}$) for each module. Instead of hard-coded textual components, N2NMNs employ a soft attention mechanism over question words. For each module $m$, an attention map $\alpha^{(m)}_i$ is predicted over the $T$ question words, and the textual feature $x^{(m)}_{txt}$ is computed as a weighted sum of word embeddings: $x^{(m)}_{txt} = \sum_{i=1}^{T} \alpha^{(m)}_i w_i$, where $w_i$ is the embedding for word $i$. This allows modules to dynamically attend to relevant words in the question.

#### 3.2. Layout Policy with Sequence-to-Sequence RNN

The layout policy is responsible for predicting a suitable reasoning structure (layout $l$) tailored to each input question $q$. This policy outputs a probability distribution $p(l|q)$.
- **Layout Representation:** An arbitrary layout, initially a computational expression (syntax tree) composed of modules, is linearized into a sequence of module tokens using Reverse Polish Notation (post-order traversal). This transforms the problem of predicting a structure into a sequence-to-sequence learning task.
- **Implementation:** An attentional Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) is used:
 - **Question Encoder:** A multi-layer LSTM network encodes the input question $q$ (word embeddings $w_i$) into a sequence of hidden states $[h_1, \ldots, h_T]$.
 - **Layout Decoder:** Another LSTM network acts as a decoder, predicting module tokens sequentially. At each time step $t$, it computes soft attention weights $\alpha_{ti}$ over the encoder's hidden states to form a context vector $c_t$. This context vector, along with the decoder's current hidden state $h_t$, is used to predict the next module token $m^{(t)}$. Importantly, the same attention weights $\alpha_{ti}$ used to predict the module are also utilized to construct its textual input $x^{(t)}_{txt}$ via Equation 1, linking the structural prediction with semantic parameterization.
- **Inference:** At test time, beam search is employed to deterministically find the maximum-probability layout $l$ from $p(l|q)$.

#### 3.3. End-to-End Training

The model is trained end-to-end to jointly optimize the layout policy $p(l|q)$ and the parameters of the neural modules. The overall loss function is $L(\theta) = E_{l \sim p(l|q;\theta)} [\tilde{L}(\theta, l; q, I)]$, where $\tilde{L}$ is the final question answering loss (e.g., softmax loss).
- **Handling Non-Differentiability:** Since the layout $l$ is discrete, the loss function is not fully differentiable. This is addressed using a policy gradient method from reinforcement learning. The gradient $\nabla_\theta L$ is estimated using Monte-Carlo sampling (with $M=1$ in this implementation), incorporating both the gradient of the QA loss and the gradient of the log-probability of the sampled layout. A baseline $b$ (exponential moving average of recent losses) and entropy regularization are used to reduce variance and encourage exploration.
- **Behavioral Cloning for Initialization:** Training from scratch can be challenging. To provide a good initialization, the model undergoes a pre-training stage via "behavioral cloning" from an expert policy $p_e(l|q)$. For datasets like CLEVR, expert layouts ($l_e$) are derived from annotated functional programs using predefined rules. This stage minimizes both the KL-divergence between the learned policy $p$ and the expert policy $p_e$, and the QA loss $\tilde{L}(\theta, l_e; q, I)$ with layouts from the expert. This process is solely for training and the expert policy is not required at test time.
- **Two-Stage Training:**
 1. **Stage 1 (Behavioral Cloning):** Supervised pre-training from the expert policy to obtain initial parameters for the sequence-to-sequence RNN and neural modules.
 2. **Stage 2 (Policy Search):** Further optimization using end-to-end reinforcement learning (Eqn. 5), allowing the model to explore the layout space and improve upon the potentially sub-optimal expert policy.

#### 3.4. Visual Features

For the CLEVR dataset, images are resized and a 15x10 convolutional feature map is extracted using a VGG-16 network pre-trained on ImageNet. Two additional channels encoding spatial coordinates ($x$ and $y$) are appended to the feature map, resulting in a 15x10x514 tensor. For the VQA dataset, LRCN VGG-16 and ResNet-152 features are utilized for comparison with prior work. Question words are embedded into 300-dimensional vectors initialized from scratch.

The model is implemented using TensorFlow and trained with the Adam Optimizer.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research evaluates the End-to-End Module Networks (N2NMNs) across three datasets: SHAPES, CLEVR, and VQA, demonstrating its capabilities in learning and performing compositional reasoning.

#### 4.1. Analysis on the SHAPES Dataset (Table 2)

- On the smaller SHAPES dataset, the model trained with "behavioral cloning from expert" achieved 100% accuracy. This indicates that the N2NMN module implementation, even when guided by an expert layout, performs effectively and surpasses the 90.80% accuracy of the original NMN on this dataset, suggesting improvements in module design (e.g., soft attention).
- "Policy search from scratch," without any expert guidance, achieved 96.19% accuracy, demonstrating the model's ability to learn effective reasoning policies through reinforcement learning alone, even on a small dataset.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on the CLEVR Dataset (Table 3)

The CLEVR dataset is specifically designed to challenge compositional reasoning.
- **Baseline Performance:** "Ours - policy search from scratch" achieved an overall accuracy of 69.0%. This result is higher than most previous VQA methods (e.g., CNN+BoW at 48.4%, CNN+LSTM+SA at 68.5%), indicating that the N2NMN architecture, even without expert supervision, possesses strong reasoning capabilities compared to monolithic models.
- **Impact of Behavioral Cloning:** "Ours - cloning expert" achieved 78.9% overall accuracy. This represents a substantial improvement over previous approaches and the NMN baseline (72.1%) which also used expert layouts. This finding supports the effectiveness of N2NMN's module implementations, particularly the soft attention mechanism for textual parameters, compared to the hard-coded parameters in NMN.
- **Benefits of End-to-End Policy Search:** "Ours - policy search after cloning" achieved 83.7% overall accuracy. This demonstrates a further and consistent improvement across nearly all question types compared to the "cloning expert" stage (e.g., a large improvement in "compare color"). This indicates that the reinforcement learning stage, which allows the model to explore the layout space beyond the expert-provided configurations, can discover more optimal reasoning strategies. The paper highlights an error reduction of nearly 50% relative to state-of-the-art attentional approaches like CNN+LSTM+SA (83.7% vs 68.5%).
- **Interpretability:** Examples illustrate how the model learns interpretable layouts. Figure 5 shows modules like `find`, `relocate`, and `count` acquiring intuitive semantics. Figure 6 demonstrates how the end-to-end optimization can introduce new `filter` modules, correcting previous errors and refining the reasoning process. The soft attention maps also reveal which words each module attends to, providing insights into its function.

#### 4.3. Evaluation on the VQA Dataset (Table 4)

- On the VQA dataset with real images, "ours - cloning expert" (using LRCN VGG-16 features) achieved 61.9% accuracy, outperforming other modular approaches like NMN (57.3%) and D-NMN (57.9%). This indicates that N2NMN's approach to module construction and parameterization is effective even when expert layouts are derived from syntactic parsers.
- With ResNet-152 features, "ours - policy search after cloning" achieved 64.9% accuracy. This result is comparable to, and slightly higher than, MCB (64.7%), which was the VQA 2016 challenge winner. The N2NMN model provides this performance while additionally offering an interpretable underlying reasoning procedure, which MCB does not.

In summary, the main findings include a demonstration of N2NMN's ability to learn effective and interpretable compositional reasoning procedures without direct reliance on external linguistic parsers at test time. The soft attention mechanism for module parameterization proved more effective than hard-coded alternatives. Crucially, the two-stage training approach, starting with behavioral cloning from an expert followed by reinforcement learning-based policy search, enables the model to surpass the performance achievable with expert-derived layouts alone, leading to improved accuracy on challenging VQA benchmarks.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The research on End-to-End Module Networks (N2NMNs) presents several significant contributions to the field of Visual Question Answering (VQA) and the broader domain of neural network architecture learning.

- **Advancement in Compositional Reasoning for VQA:** This work addresses a core challenge in VQA: enabling models to perform explicit, multi-step compositional reasoning rather than relying on statistical correlations. By dynamically assembling neural networks tailored to each question, N2NMNs represent a step towards more robust and generalizable VQA systems, particularly evident in its performance on the CLEVR dataset.
- **Reduced Dependency on External Linguistic Parsers:** A notable impact is the elimination of the need for "brittle off-the-shelf linguistic parsers" at inference time. Previous modular VQA systems were hindered by the limitations and inaccuracies of these parsers, which were not designed for the specific demands of vision-language tasks. N2NMNs learn to predict network layouts directly from the question, making the system more self-contained, robust, and adaptable to novel linguistic structures without manual rule-crafting for parser output.
- **Enhanced Interpretability:** The modular nature of N2NMNs inherently provides a degree of interpretability. By predicting a sequence of semantically distinct modules (e.g., `find`, `relocate`, `count`), the model makes its reasoning process explicit. This contrasts with the "black box" nature of many monolithic deep learning architectures and allows researchers to examine the computational steps taken to arrive at an answer, which could facilitate debugging, error analysis, and building trust in AI systems.
- **Improved Module Parameterization:** The introduction of a soft attention mechanism for parameterizing neural modules represents an improvement over previous hard-coded or fixed embedding approaches. This allows modules to dynamically attend to relevant parts of the question, leading to more flexible and context-aware behavior and contributing to the model's overall performance.
- **Effective Two-Stage Training Strategy:** The proposed two-stage training methodology, combining behavioral cloning from an expert policy (even a sub-optimal one) with subsequent end-to-end reinforcement learning, offers a generalizable paradigm for training complex neural models that involve discrete architectural search. This approach helps to overcome the challenges of training reinforcement learning agents from scratch by providing a strong initialization and then allowing for further optimization and exploration of the solution space.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Achieving significant error reductions (e.g., nearly 50% relative error reduction on CLEVR compared to prior attentional approaches) and competitive performance on established benchmarks like VQA demonstrates the practical efficacy of the N2NMN framework. This provides a new baseline and direction for future research in compositional VQA.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The ability to learn dynamic, specialized network structures for complex tasks could be extended beyond VQA to other domains requiring multi-step reasoning, program synthesis, or adaptive computation. It offers a framework for exploring how higher-level cognitive operations can be learned and executed within deep learning architectures.

In conclusion, this work contributes to the development of more intelligent, interpretable, and adaptable VQA systems. Its methodology provides a foundation for models that genuinely learn to reason by constructing their own computational graphs, moving beyond reliance on external tools or fixed architectures.
