# Visual Jigsaw Post-Training Improves MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> A self-supervised post-training framework, Visual Jigsaw, enhances Multimodal Large Language Models' (MLLMs) intrinsic visual understanding by framing visual ordering as a verifiable task for reinforcement learning. This method, applied across images, videos, and 3D data, yields quantitative improvements such as a +6.00 gain on MMVP for image understanding and +17.11 on DA-2K for 3D depth perception.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models often treat dense visual information as sparse cues for text-based reasoning, leading to undervalued fine-grained visual comprehension.
- Current methods to improve MLLM visual understanding frequently require architectural modifications or new generative components, limiting compatibility with existing understanding-based MLLM designs.
- It remained unclear whether pixel-level visual reconstruction is the most efficient or optimal strategy for enhancing MLLMs' visual comprehension given their primary optimization for textual outputs.

## Method

- The **Visual Jigsaw** framework was developed, posing visual ordering problems (==jigsaw puzzles==) across images, videos, and 3D data, with the MLLM tasked to predict the correct permutation in natural language.
- Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), specifically the GRPO algorithm, was employed with a dense reward function designed to provide partial correctness and format adherence.
- Modality-specific instantiations included image patch reordering, video clip reordering, and depth-sorted 3D point reordering, all implemented without requiring architectural changes to the base MLLM.

## Results

- Image Jigsaw post-training led to consistent improvements on image-centric benchmarks, with gains like +6.00 on MMVP and +6.06 on MMStar (fine-grained understanding) compared to the baseline MLLM.
- Video Jigsaw consistently improved performance across video benchmarks, particularly in temporal reasoning (e.g., +6.15 on AoTBench), showing robustness across different frame settings.
- 3D Jigsaw achieved substantial gains in 3D understanding, notably +17.11 on the DA-**2K** depth estimation benchmark, indicating enhanced perception and reasoning about 3D spatial structures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised visual ordering tasks, such as jigsaw puzzles, are effective for enhancing MLLMs' intrinsic visual understanding across diverse modalities without needing architectural modifications or generative components.
> - Reinforcement Learning (RL) with verifiable rewards promotes better generalization in perceptual tasks for MLLMs compared to Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), which can lead to overfitting on specific datasets.
> - A carefully designed dense reward function, providing partial credit for valid but incomplete permutations, is crucial for enabling learning in challenging ordering tasks where sparse binary feedback might be insufficient.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have witnessed rapid advancements, demonstrating impressive capabilities across a wide spectrum of vision-language tasks. This progress has largely mirrored the successes seen in traditional Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly with the advent of Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). The broader research landscape in MLLMs has, therefore, seen a significant push towards adapting RL-based post-training paradigms to enhance reasoning abilities, especially in complex domains like multimodal mathematical and scientific reasoning.

However, a critical observation made by this paper is that within this dominant paradigm, dense visual information is often treated as mere sparse cues to support text-based reasoning. This approach inadvertently undervalues the intrinsic, fine-grained understanding of the visual signal itself. Existing attempts to address this, such as incorporating visual reconstruction objectives, typically necessitate architectural modifications (e.g., adding generative components) or introducing new learning objectives, making them less compatible with existing understanding-based MLLM architectures. Moreover, it remains an open question whether pixel-level reconstruction is the most efficient or optimal strategy for enhancing MLLMs' visual comprehension.

The paper carefully contextualizes its work by referencing the rich history of self-supervised learning (SSL) in computer vision. While reconstruction-based (e.g., MAE) and discriminative methods (e.g., MoCo, SimCLR) have dominated vision representation learning, simpler "jigsaw-style" pretext tasks (e.g., reordering shuffled image patches or video frames) have existed for years. Historically, these jigsaw tasks, while lightweight and interpretable, were considered weaker for general visual representation learning due to less dense supervision and poorer scalability compared to their more complex counterparts.

"Visual Jigsaw" carves a unique niche by re-evaluating these "jigsaw-style" tasks within the MLLM post-training context. It argues that the very limitations that made jigsaw tasks less mainstream for *traditional vision representation learning* (i.e., less dense supervision than pixel-level reconstruction) make them *ideal* for understanding-based MLLMs. MLLMs are primarily optimized for textual outputs reflecting understanding, not dense visual generation. By formulating visual ordering as a text-based output task within an RLVR framework, Visual Jigsaw offers a lightweight, verifiable, and self-supervised approach that directly enhances intrinsic visual understanding without altering the MLLM's core architecture or output format. This work addresses a significant gap by introducing a vision-centric RL post-training method that complements the predominantly text-centric or task-specific RL approaches in MLLMs, thereby contributing to the development of more perceptually grounded and robust multimodal models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enhance the intrinsic visual understanding and perception capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)**. The authors observe a critical imbalance in the current MLLM landscape: while significant progress has been made in multimodal reasoning, often facilitated by reinforcement learning (RL) post-training, this progress has largely focused on text-centric reasoning, using visual inputs merely as sparse contextual evidence. Consequently, the deep, fine-grained comprehension of visual signals themselves has been undervalued.

This observation motivates several key objectives for the Visual Jigsaw framework:

1. **Strengthen Vision-Centric Understanding:** The core motivation is to develop a method that explicitly trains MLLMs to better understand fine-grained details, spatial relationships, temporal sequences, and 3D structures within visual data.
2. **Avoid Architectural Modification:** Existing approaches that attempt to improve visual understanding (e.g., through visual reconstruction) often require integrating additional visual generative components or altering the MLLM's architecture. A key motivation for Visual Jigsaw is to achieve perceptual enhancement without these intrusive changes, making it seamlessly compatible with current text-only MLLM designs.
3. **Leverage Self-Supervision and RLVR:** The authors aim to derive supervisory signals automatically from the visual data itself, eliminating the need for extensive human annotations. By framing the task as an ordering problem, it naturally aligns with the Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) paradigm, where the ground truth is deterministic and verifiable.
4. **Promote Generalization:** Placing Visual Jigsaw in the post-training phase is motivated by the finding that RL-based post-training, compared to Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), tends to promote stronger generalization. This ensures that the vision-centric skills acquired from the jigsaw tasks can effectively transfer to a broader range of downstream applications.
5. **Demonstrate Generality Across Modalities:** The researchers aim to show that this self-supervised, ordering-based approach is not limited to images but can be universally applied to different visual modalities, specifically demonstrating its effectiveness for images, videos, and 3D data.

In essence, the motivation is to address the "visual shortcomings" of MLLMs by introducing a lightweight, perception-focused post-training mechanism that fundamentally improves how these models process and interpret visual information, without compromising their core text-generation capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is **Visual Jigsaw**, a generic self-supervised post-training framework formulated as a visual ordering problem. The methodology involves partitioning visual inputs into elements, shuffling them, and then tasking the MLLM to reconstruct the original arrangement by producing the correct permutation in natural language. This process is optimized using Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), specifically the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm.

#### 3.1. Visual Jigsaw Formulation

The framework is designed for generality across visual modalities. For any given visual input, *K* jigsaw elements are derived through modality-specific partitioning. These elements are then randomly shuffled, and the MLLM must predict the inverse permutation to restore the original order, outputting a list of indices.

#### 3.2. Reward Design

A crucial aspect of the RLVR approach is the reward function, which is designed to provide dense learning signals:
- **Accuracy Reward:** If the model's predicted permutation (`o`) exactly matches the ground truth (`g`), it receives a reward of 1.
- **Partial Correctness Reward:** For valid but partially correct permutations, the reward is calculated as a fraction of correctly placed indices (`1/K * sum(1[o_i = g_i])`), scaled by a discount factor `γ` (set to 0.2). This encourages progress even when a perfect solution isn't found.
- **Invalid Permutation Penalty:** Any output that is not a valid permutation of size *K* (e.g., duplicate indices) receives a reward of 0.
- **Format Reward:** An additional reward of 0.2 is assigned if the model adheres to a specified output format, including thinking process tags (`<think></think>`) and final answer tags (`<answer></answer>`). Incorrect formatting yields 0 for both format and accuracy rewards.

#### 3.3. Modality-Specific Instantiations

- **Image Jigsaw:**
 - **Partitioning:** An input image is divided into a `m × n` grid of non-overlapping patches (e.g., 3x3, yielding K=9 patches).
 - **Shuffling:** These patches are randomly permuted.
 - **Task:** The MLLM must predict the correct raster-scan order of the shuffled patch indices.
 - **Training Data:** 118K images from the COCO dataset, filtered for minimum side length.

- **Video Jigsaw:**
 - **Partitioning:** A video is segmented uniformly along the temporal axis into *K* non-overlapping clips (e.g., K=6).
 - **Shuffling:** These clips are randomly permuted. To prevent models from relying on simple frame-matching cues at clip boundaries, 5% of frames are trimmed from the beginning and end of each clip.
 - **Task:** The MLLM must predict the correct chronological order of the shuffled clip indices.
 - **Training Data:** 100K videos from the LLaVA-Video dataset.

- **3D Jigsaw (RGB-D based):**
 - **Partitioning:** Instead of volumetric partitioning (which current MLLMs don't typically handle natively), this variant samples *K* points from an RGB-D image, ensuring distinct depth values. These points are initially sorted by depth (nearest to farthest).
 - **Shuffling:** The points are randomly permuted and annotated with their shuffled indices directly on the RGB image view.
 - **Task:** The MLLM must recover the correct depth order (from nearest to farthest) of the annotated points.
 - **Training Data:** 300K samples generated from the ScanNet RGB-D dataset, with constraints to ensure spatial and depth diversity among selected points.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Base MLLM:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct.
- **RL Algorithm:** GRPO, with KL regularization and entropy loss removed (except when fine-tuning reasoning models, where KL constraint is enabled to preserve reasoning).
- **Hyperparameters:** Discount factor `γ`=0.2, global batch size 256 (image) / 128 (video & 3D), learning rate 1e-6, 16 responses sampled per prompt (decoding temperature 1.0).
- **Training Steps:** 1000 steps for image and video jigsaw, 800 for 3D jigsaw.

This comprehensive approach, spanning multiple modalities and a carefully designed RL framework, allows the authors to systematically improve the perceptual grounding of MLLMs without fundamentally altering their architecture or relying on additional generative modules.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that Visual Jigsaw post-training consistently and substantially improves the visual understanding capabilities of MLLMs across images, videos, and 3D data.

#### 4.1. Image Jigsaw

- **Consistent Improvements:** Training with Image Jigsaw leads to notable gains across a diverse set of image-centric benchmarks.
- **Fine-grained Perception:** Significant improvements are observed in tasks requiring detailed object recognition and attribute understanding (e.g., MMVP, MMStar, MMBench, HR-Bench, V*, MME-RealWorld, LISA-Grounding, OVD-Eval). For instance, it achieves +6.00 on MMVP and +6.06 on MMStar (fine-grained) compared to the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline.
- **Monocular Spatial Understanding:** The model shows enhanced ability to infer spatial relationships from single images (VSR, OmniSpatial, DA-2K), with gains like +5.90 on DA-2K.
- **Compositional Visual Understanding:** Performance improves on tasks assessing complex visual compositions (Winoground, SugarCrepe++).
- **Outperformance of Baselines:** Visual Jigsaw outperforms other post-trained MLLMs like ThinkLite-VL, VL-Cogito, and LLaVA-Critic-R1 on most vision-centric benchmarks, highlighting the effectiveness of its vision-centric approach over predominantly reasoning-focused or general perception improvement methods.

#### 4.2. Video Jigsaw

- **Broad Video Understanding:** Video Jigsaw consistently improves performance across a comprehensive suite of video benchmarks, evaluated under different frame settings (16, 32, 64 frames).
- **Temporal Reasoning:** Gains are particularly pronounced on tasks requiring temporal-centric understanding and reasoning about temporal directionality (e.g., AoTBench, with gains of +6.15, +5.52, +5.23 for 16, 32, 64 frames respectively).
- **Cross-Video and Directional Understanding:** Strong improvements on CVBench demonstrate enhanced cross-video understanding, while other benchmarks like Vinoground and TempCompass also show solid gains.
- **Robustness Across Frame Settings:** The improvements are consistent regardless of the number of frames provided, indicating a generalized enhancement of temporal perception.

#### 4.3. 3D Jigsaw

- **Significant 3D Perception Gains:** 3D Jigsaw achieves substantial improvements across all tested 3D understanding benchmarks.
- **Direct Depth Task Improvement:** The largest gain is observed on DA-2K (+17.11), a depth estimation benchmark directly related to the pre-training task, confirming the learning of depth ordering.
- **General 3D Spatial Reasoning:** Consistent improvements on a wide range of other tasks, including those with single-view (3DSRBench, OmniSpatial), multi-view (ViewSpatial, All-Angles), and egocentric video inputs (VSI-Bench), demonstrate that the model's general ability to perceive and reason about 3D spatial structure is strengthened.
- **Limitations of Alternative 3D Jigsaw Designs:** Ablation studies on alternative 3D jigsaw tasks (View-Motion Matching, BEV-Pose Matching) showed that these more complex variants did not yield significant improvements, and often underperformed the simpler depth ordering, suggesting that current base MLLMs might lack the foundational 3D reasoning capability to fully benefit from these sophisticated tasks.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Discussions

- **RL vs. SFT:** Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Visual Jigsaw consistently outperforms Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). While SFT yields moderate gains, it can lead to performance degradation on some benchmarks due to overfitting. RL, in contrast, promotes better generalization, confirming prior findings that "SFT memorizes, RL generalizes."
- **Jigsaw Difficulty:** More challenging jigsaw tasks (e.g., 3x3 patches for images, 6 clips for videos) lead to greater performance gains than easier variants (e.g., 2x2, 4 clips). This indicates that higher difficulty provides a stronger supervisory signal. The partial accuracy reward design is crucial for enabling learning in these difficult settings, where sparse binary feedback would be insufficient.
- **Visual Jigsaw on Reasoning MLLMs:** Applying Image Jigsaw post-training to a reasoning-oriented MLLM (ThinkLite-VL) successfully improves visual perception while *preserving* its reasoning ability, especially when a KL constraint is used during training. This demonstrates that Visual Jigsaw can complement existing reasoning-focused post-training without trade-offs.

Overall, the findings strongly support the hypothesis that self-supervised vision-centric tasks, particularly those leveraging the verifiable nature of ordering problems within an RL framework, are a powerful and effective method for enhancing MLLMs' intrinsic visual understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Visual Jigsaw framework presents a significant contribution to the field of multimodal large language models, addressing a crucial gap in their development and promising several impactful outcomes.

Firstly, its primary significance lies in **redressing the imbalance between visual perception and textual reasoning in MLLMs**. By introducing a dedicated vision-centric post-training paradigm, the work explicitly counteracts the tendency of current MLLMs to treat dense visual information as merely sparse cues for text-based inference. This focus on intrinsic visual understanding—spanning fine-grained perception, spatial reasoning, temporal understanding, and 3D comprehension—is vital for building truly capable and robust multimodal AI systems that can genuinely "see" and interpret the world.

Secondly, the introduction of **Visual Jigsaw as a lightweight, self-supervised, and verifiable task** is highly impactful. It cleverly re-purposes the concept of jigsaw puzzles from traditional computer vision into the MLLM domain, leveraging their text generation capabilities. The fact that it requires **no additional visual generative components or architectural modifications** means it can be seamlessly integrated into existing text-only MLLM architectures. This greatly reduces implementation complexity and computational overhead compared to reconstruction-based methods, making it a highly practical approach for a wide range of MLLMs.

Thirdly, the successful application of **Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)** for enhancing perceptual skills, rather than solely reasoning abilities, broadens the scope and demonstrated power of RL in MLLM development. The carefully designed graded reward function, which provides partial credit for incomplete solutions, is particularly impactful for facilitating learning in challenging ordering tasks where sparse binary rewards might fail.

Fourthly, the **generality and effectiveness of Visual Jigsaw across three distinct visual modalities – images, videos, and 3D data** – highlights its potential as a universal mechanism for perceptual grounding. This multi-modal validation suggests that the underlying principle of learning structural order is fundamental to various forms of visual intelligence. The observed improvements across diverse benchmarks directly translate to MLLMs being more adept at understanding complex visual scenes, tracking temporal events, and inferring spatial relationships, which are critical for real-world applications such as robotics, autonomous driving, medical imaging, and enhanced human-computer interaction.

Finally, this research serves as an **inspiration for future work**. By highlighting the immense potential of perception-focused self- and weakly-supervised tasks, it encourages the research community to explore a broader array of vision-centric pretext tasks. It also demonstrates that complementary post-training strategies – one focusing on visual perception and another on complex reasoning – can collectively lead to more holistic and powerful multimodal models without one necessarily coming at the expense of the other. The ability to enhance perception in reasoning-oriented MLLMs while preserving their reasoning capabilities (as shown in the ablation studies) is a testament to this complementary power. Ultimately, Visual Jigsaw paves the way for MLLMs that are not only skilled at language-based reasoning but also deeply grounded in their understanding of the visual world.
