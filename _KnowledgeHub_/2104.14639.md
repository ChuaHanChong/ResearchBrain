# Keypoint Transformer: Solving Joint Identification in Challenging Hands and Object Interactions for Accurate 3D Pose Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> Keypoint Transformer estimates 3D poses of two interacting hands, and optionally a manipulated object, from one RGB image. It splits joint localization from joint recognition: a U-Net predicts a single-channel keypoint heatmap, self-attention assigns each keypoint an identity, and cross-attention with learned joint queries regresses pose. It reaches state-of-the-art accuracy on InterHand2.6M with roughly half the parameters.

## Problem

- Prior heatmap methods regress one map per joint, forcing localization and recognition simultaneously; ambiguous joint appearance and occlusion corrupt those maps during close two-hand interaction.
- Detecting left and right hands separately then predicting poses independently fails when hands overlap closely, since joints of one hand are occluded by the other.
- Recent fixes add joint-visibility cues, part segmentation, or iterative refinement layers, raising model complexity while still not explicitly modeling relationships among detected joints.

## Method

- Keypoint detection and encoding: a ==U-Net== regresses one single-channel heatmap, ==non-maximum suppression== keeps up to 64 keypoints, each encoded as 224-D decoder features plus 32-D sine spatial encoding.
- Keypoint-joint association: multi-head ==self-attention== layers build context-aware keypoint features supervised by a cross-entropy identity loss over hand, joint index, background, and object classes.
- Pose estimation: one ==cross-attention== layer with learned per-joint queries selects identity-aware keypoints; extra queries predict inter-hand translation, ==MANO== shape, and symmetry-aware object rotation and translation.

## Results

- On InterHand2.6M, MPJPE drops to **12.78 mm** versus **14.22 mm** for InterNet and **14.48 mm** for a DETR-style baseline, with MRRPE improving from **32.57 mm** to **29.63 mm**.
- On HO-3D, joint error falls to **2.57 cm** with AUC **0.54** and object MSSD **7.02**, beating prior hand-object methods; the new H2O-3D set yields **3.09 cm** MPJPE and **7.96 cm** MSSD.
- Ablations: the identity loss improves MPJPE **10 percent** and MRRPE **15 percent**; a ResNet-18 backbone with **28M** parameters still beats the **48M**-parameter InterNet baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Splitting a hard perception task into localize-then-identify lets attention resolve appearance ambiguity that per-instance heatmaps cannot, and needs far fewer parameters.
> - Attending over sparse high-resolution keypoint features beats attending over whole low-resolution feature maps; local features at joint sites carry most of the pose signal.
> - Explicit identity supervision makes the pipeline tolerant to false and missing detections, but without contact or interpenetration modeling predicted poses can stay physically implausible.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The field of 3D hand pose estimation has seen significant advancements, particularly for single-hand scenarios, utilizing both depth maps and single RGB images. RGB-based methods are particularly attractive due to their reduced hardware requirements. While considerable progress has been made, estimating the 3D poses of two hands in close interaction or a hand interacting with an object presents substantial challenges. These challenges arise from the visual similarity between the joints of multiple hands, as well as severe occlusions caused by self-interaction, hand-hand contact, and hand-object obstruction.

Existing approaches to hand pose estimation can broadly be categorized into generative, discriminative, and hybrid methods. Generative methods typically fit a parametric hand model, such as MANO, to observed data but can be prone to tracking drift during complex interactions. Discriminative methods directly predict hand poses from single frames using convolutional neural networks (CNNs), but they often struggle with generalization to novel poses and appearances. Hybrid approaches aim to combine the strengths of both by using discriminative methods to extract visual cues, followed by model fitting.

Previous discriminative methods for interacting hands, such as bottom-up approaches, often predict heatmaps for each joint. However, the inherent visual ambiguity of similar-looking joints and severe occlusions can lead to imprecise heatmap predictions, conflating joint localization with identity recognition. More recent efforts have attempted to mitigate these issues by incorporating joint segmentation, visibility cues, or iterative refinement layers, frequently at the cost of increased model complexity.

In parallel, Transformer architectures have emerged as a prominent development in computer vision, demonstrating success in tasks like object detection (e.g., DETR), image classification, and human pose estimation. While some Transformer-based methods have addressed single-hand or multi-person 2D pose estimation, their direct application to highly ambiguous 3D interacting hand pose estimation has not been fully explored, particularly concerning the distinct challenges posed by hand joints' appearance similarity and severe occlusions.

This work addresses these limitations by proposing a unified discriminative approach, the "Keypoint Transformer," specifically designed for interacting hands and hand-object scenarios. It differentiates itself by explicitly decoupling the tasks of keypoint localization and joint identity recognition. This separation, combined with the global context modeling capabilities of self-attention, aims to provide more robust and accurate pose estimations while maintaining model efficiency. Furthermore, the paper contributes a new, challenging dataset, H2O-3D, to further advance research in two-hand and object interaction.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and accurate method for estimating the 3D poses of two hands in close interaction from a single color image. A secondary objective is to extend this method to accurately estimate the 3D pose of an object being manipulated by one or two hands.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several significant challenges observed in existing 3D hand and hand-object pose estimation techniques:

1. **Ambiguity in Joint Identification:** Current state-of-the-art discriminative methods often attempt to simultaneously localize and identify individual hand joints by regressing per-joint heatmaps. In scenarios involving two hands in close interaction, the visual similarities between joints of different hands (e.g., an index finger tip from the left hand versus the right hand) and even within the same hand (e.g., different finger tips) lead to highly ambiguous heatmaps. This ambiguity results in frequent misidentifications and inaccurate pose estimations, especially under partial occlusions. The paper specifically highlights that previous methods struggle to distinguish between closely interacting joints due to similar appearances and occlusions (Figure 2).

2. **Handling Severe Occlusions:** Close hand-hand and hand-object interactions frequently involve extensive occlusions. Parts of one hand can be completely hidden by the other hand or by the manipulated object. Traditional methods that rely on localized feature extraction or dense heatmap predictions often perform poorly when joints are not clearly visible, necessitating a mechanism to infer joint positions from surrounding context.

3. **Model Complexity vs. Accuracy:** Some recent approaches have sought to improve accuracy in interacting hand scenarios by introducing additional cues, such as joint segmentation or visibility maps, or by employing iterative refinement layers. While these methods may yield improved results, they often lead to an increase in model complexity and computational cost. A motivation for this work is to achieve comparable or superior accuracy with a more efficient and streamlined architecture.

4. **Lack of Comprehensive Datasets for Two-Hand Object Interaction:** While datasets for single-hand and two-hand interactions exist (e.g., HO-3D, InterHand2.6M), there was a perceived gap for challenging, fully annotated 3D datasets capturing two hands actively manipulating an object. Such a dataset is crucial for training and evaluating models that can handle the combined complexities of multi-hand interaction and hand-object occlusion. The creation of the H2O-3D dataset directly addresses this need.

By separating the tasks of keypoint localization and identity recognition—first detecting potential keypoint locations and then using self-attention to associate them with specific joint identities—the authors aim to address the core problem of joint ambiguity and occlusion more effectively, ultimately leading to more robust and accurate 3D pose estimation with potentially lower model complexity.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed "Keypoint Transformer" architecture addresses the challenges of 3D hand and object pose estimation in complex interaction scenarios by decoupling keypoint localization from joint identification. The method operates in three sequential stages, detailed below, and can be extended to include object pose estimation.

#### 3.1. Keypoint Detection and Encoding

The initial stage focuses on detecting potential 2D locations of hand joints.
1. **Detection:** A U-Net architecture is employed to predict a single-channel heatmap from the input RGB image. This heatmap highlights regions likely corresponding to hand joints, without yet assigning specific identities. Non-maximum suppression (NMS) is applied to the heatmap to extract up to 64 discrete 2D keypoint locations. The U-Net is trained by minimizing an L2 loss against a ground truth heatmap, which is generated by applying 2D Gaussian kernels at the actual 2D joint locations.
2. **Encoding:** For each detected 2D keypoint, a 256-dimensional feature vector is constructed. This vector combines two types of encoding:
 - **Appearance Encoding:** Image features are sampled from multiple layers of the U-Net decoder at the normalized 2D keypoint locations using bilinear interpolation. These sampled features are concatenated (forming a 3968-D vector) and then reduced to a 224-D vector via a 3-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP).
 - **Spatial Encoding:** A 32-D sine positional encoding, similar to that used in Transformer architectures, is generated based on the normalized 2D coordinates of the keypoint.
 The appearance and spatial encodings are then concatenated to form the final 256-D keypoint encoding vector, which serves as input to the subsequent stages. The keypoint detector is initially pre-trained before the entire network is fine-tuned end-to-end.

#### 3.2. Keypoint-Joint Association

This stage leverages Transformer self-attention to disambiguate the identities of the detected keypoints.
1. **Self-Attention Module:** The 256-D encoding vectors for all detected keypoints are fed into a multi-layer, multi-head self-attention module. This module processes the keypoint features simultaneously, allowing the network to model relationships and contextual dependencies between all keypoints. This global contextualization helps in resolving ambiguities arising from similar joint appearances and occlusions. The output of this module consists of context-aware feature vectors for each keypoint.
2. **Identity Prediction:** Following the self-attention module, a Feed-Forward Network (FFN), comprising a 2-layer MLP, a linear projection, and a softmax layer, predicts the identity for each keypoint. The possible identities include 42 hand joints (21 per hand: left or right, and a specific joint index) or a 'background' class for false positives.
3. **Loss Function:** A cross-entropy loss, termed `L_KI`, is applied to train the keypoint identity predictions. During training, ground truth identities are assigned to detected keypoints by matching them to the closest reprojected 3D ground truth joint within a small threshold (γ = 3 pixels). Keypoints outside this threshold are assigned to the background class. The `L_KI` loss is applied after each layer of the self-attention module, with shared FFN weights across layers. Keypoints identified as 'background' are subsequently ignored, while multiple keypoints associated with a single joint are all considered in the final pose estimation.

#### 3.3. Pose Estimation from Identity-Aware Keypoints

The final stage uses a cross-attention mechanism to predict the 3D hand poses.
1. **Cross-Attention Module:** A single cross-attention layer is employed. It takes the context-aware keypoint features (output from the self-attention module) and a set of learned joint queries as input. Each learned query `Q_j` is designed to represent a specific joint identity. The cross-attention mechanism dynamically selects and weights the relevant keypoint features that correspond to each queried joint.
2. **Pose Prediction:** The features resulting from the cross-attention are combined with the joint queries (via a residual connection) and then transformed by a 3-layer MLP to map them into the desired pose parameter space.
3. **Additional Parameters:** Beyond joint poses, additional learned queries are used to predict the relative translation between the two hands (`T_L→R`) and the 10-dimensional MANO hand shape parameters (`β`), typically using an L1 loss.

#### 3.4. Hand Pose Representations and Losses

The method supports three different 3D hand pose representations, each with a corresponding loss:
- **3D Representation:** Predicts 20 parent-relative 3D joint vectors per hand, from which root-relative 3D joint locations can be accumulated. Losses include L1 on both joint vectors and root-relative 3D joint locations.
- **2.5D Representation:** Predicts 2D joint locations and root-relative depth differences for each of the 21 joints per hand. Requires camera intrinsics and a predicted root depth (from a RootNet) for 3D reconstruction. Losses include L1 on 2D joint locations and depth differences.
- **MANO Joint Angles (θ):** Predicts 16 3D joint angles per hand and MANO shape parameters, enabling the reconstruction of the full hand mesh. Losses include L1 on both root-relative 3D joint locations (derived from angles) and the joint angles themselves.

#### 3.5. Object Pose Estimation

The Keypoint Transformer can be extended to estimate the 3D pose of an object during hand-object interaction:
1. **Object Keypoint Detection:** An additional prediction head on the U-Net decoder generates a segmentation map for the object. `N_obj = 20` random points are sampled from this segmentation map to serve as 'object keypoints'.
2. **Encoding and Association:** These object keypoints are encoded similarly to hand keypoints (appearance + spatial). They are then processed alongside hand keypoints by the same self-attention module. During the keypoint-joint association stage, an additional 'object' identity is used, associating object keypoints with the object class.
3. **Object Pose Prediction:** Two additional learned queries in the cross-attention module are dedicated to predicting the 3D rotation and translation of the object relative to the right hand. Object rotation is parameterized using a continuous representation.
4. **Loss Function:** A symmetry-aware object corner loss, `L_obj-pose`, is used to train the object pose estimation, accounting for object symmetries.

#### 3.6. End-to-End Training

The entire architecture is trained end-to-end by minimizing a combined loss function:
`L = L_H + L_KI + L_T + L_hand-pose + L_obj-pose`
where `L_H` is the L2 loss for the heatmap, `L_KI` is the cross-entropy for keypoint-joint association, `L_T` is the L1 loss for relative hand translation, `L_hand-pose` is the chosen hand pose loss, and `L_obj-pose` is the object pose loss. The keypoint detector is pre-trained before joint end-to-end training. The U-Net encoder is based on ResNet-50.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the Keypoint Transformer across three challenging datasets: InterHand2.6M, HO-3D, and the newly introduced H2O-3D dataset.

#### 4.1. Performance on InterHand2.6M (Two-Hand Interaction)

On the InterHand2.6M dataset, which focuses on two-hand interaction, the Keypoint Transformer demonstrated improved performance compared to state-of-the-art methods and established baselines, using the 2.5D pose representation.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** The method achieved a Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) of 12.78 mm and a Mean Relative-Root Position Error (MRRPE) of 29.63 mm. This outperformed a CNN-based baseline with self-attention ('CNN+SA') (15.31 mm MPJPE, 33.84 mm MRRPE) by approximately 16% in MPJPE. It also surpassed a DETR-like baseline ('CNN+SA+CA'), which uses features from the entire image, (14.48 mm MPJPE, 32.87 mm MRRPE) by about 12% in MPJPE. These comparisons suggest that explicitly selecting and encoding keypoint features contributes to higher accuracy than using low-resolution features from the entire image.
- **Comparison with State-of-the-Art:** The Keypoint Transformer achieved 10% higher accuracy than InterNet (12.78 mm MPJPE vs. 14.22 mm MPJPE). Furthermore, it reported competitive or superior results against more recent methods like Dong et al. and Fan et al., which employ additional complex cues like joint visibility and segmentation, but often with increased model complexity.

#### 4.2. Performance on HO-3D (Single-Hand Object Interaction)

For the HO-3D dataset, which involves a single hand interacting with an object, the Keypoint Transformer, utilizing the 3D joint representation for hand pose, exhibited notable improvements.
- The method achieved an average joint error (scale-translation aligned) of 2.57 cm and an Area Under the Curve (AUC) of 0.54 for hand pose estimation. For object pose estimation, it recorded a Maximum Symmetry-Aware Surface Distance (MSSD) of 7.02 cm.
- These results significantly outperformed previous methods such as Hampali et al., Hasson et al., and Hasson et al., which reported joint errors ranging from 3.04 cm to 3.69 cm. For instance, the Keypoint Transformer improved hand pose accuracy by approximately 15% compared to. Quantitative comparisons with more recent works in the supplementary material indicate competitive performance, often matching or closely approaching the accuracy of top-performing methods while potentially offering advantages in efficiency.

#### 4.3. Performance on H2O-3D (New Two-Hand Object Interaction Dataset)

The paper introduces H2O-3D, a novel and challenging dataset featuring two hands manipulating objects, complete with 3D annotations. This dataset presents significantly greater occlusions compared to previous datasets.
- On this new dataset, the Keypoint Transformer achieved an MPJPE of 3.09 cm for hand pose estimation and an MRRPE of 8.28 cm for the relative translation between hands. The higher MRRPE compared to InterHand2.6M indicates the increased difficulty in estimating inter-hand translation due to extensive object occlusions.
- For object pose, the method yielded an average MSSD of 7.96 cm across various YCB objects, with object-specific results ranging from 6.5 cm (Mug) to 13.5 cm (Scissors). The performance on this dataset establishes the Keypoint Transformer as a robust baseline for future research in two-hand object interaction.

#### 4.4. Discussion and Ablation Studies

The paper includes several analyses to justify design choices and demonstrate robustness:
- **Cross-Attention Visualization:** Visualizations showed that when joints were visible, cross-attention queries focused on keypoints precisely at the joint locations. For occluded joints, the attention mechanism effectively selected nearby visible keypoints, contributing to a more global, context-aware pose estimation.
- **Robustness to Noise:** The method demonstrated robustness to both added noisy keypoints and missing keypoints. Most noisy keypoints were correctly classified as background. The approach continued to estimate accurate poses even when considering only a subset of detected keypoints or when extraneous keypoints were present.
- **Importance of Keypoint-Joint Association Loss (`L_KI`):** The `L_KI` loss, which makes keypoints "identity-aware," was shown to improve MPJPE by 10% and MRRPE by 15% on interacting hand images, underscoring its contribution to accuracy.
- **Pose Representation Comparison:** Consistent with prior research, 3D and 2.5D joint representations yielded similar accuracy, while the MANO joint angle representation resulted in slightly lower accuracy.
- **Model Efficiency:** The Keypoint Transformer achieved state-of-the-art performance on InterHand2.6M with a ResNet-18 backbone, utilizing approximately half the number of model parameters (28M) compared to InterNet (48M with ResNet-50) while still demonstrating superior accuracy. This highlights the architectural efficiency. The number of self-attention layers (N_SA) positively impacted accuracy, while additional cross-attention layers (N_CA) showed minimal further improvement beyond one layer.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Keypoint Transformer" research contributes to the field of 3D hand and object pose estimation in several significant ways, with potential implications for various applications:

1. **Advancement in Robustness and Accuracy:** The core innovation of explicitly separating keypoint localization from joint identity recognition, leveraging self-attention for contextual disambiguation, demonstrably improves accuracy in highly challenging scenarios involving close two-hand interactions and hand-object occlusions. This addresses a fundamental limitation of previous methods that often conflate these tasks. The achieved state-of-the-art performance on established benchmarks like InterHand2.6M and HO-3D, along with robust results on the new H2O-3D dataset, signifies a notable advancement.

2. **Model Efficiency:** The research shows that the Keypoint Transformer can achieve superior accuracy with a significantly reduced number of model parameters (approximately half compared to a leading baseline on InterHand2.6M). This efficiency is critical for deploying 3D pose estimation systems in real-time applications such as virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), and human-computer interaction, where computational resources may be constrained.

3. **Generalizability to Hand-Object Interaction:** The method's straightforward extension to simultaneously estimate the 3D pose of manipulated objects alongside hands showcases its inherent generalizability. This unified approach simplifies the development of comprehensive interaction analysis systems. The robustness demonstrated against noisy or missing keypoints further underscores its practical utility.

4. **Novel Dataset for Complex Interactions:** The introduction of the H2O-3D dataset, featuring fully and accurately annotated 3D poses of two hands manipulating objects under severe occlusion, is a significant contribution. This dataset fills a critical gap in publicly available resources and provides a challenging benchmark for future research, pushing the boundaries of what current models can achieve in complex interaction understanding.

5. **Broader Applicability:** The underlying principle of combining a keypoint detector with a Transformer architecture that models relationships among these keypoints has potential applicability beyond hand and object pose estimation. The authors suggest its relevance to other articulated object pose prediction tasks, such as general human pose estimation or even object category pose prediction, implying a versatile framework for tasks requiring fine-grained spatial and identity reasoning.

In summary, the Keypoint Transformer represents a robust and efficient discriminative approach for 3D pose estimation in complex interaction scenarios. Its architectural design addresses core challenges in the field, sets new performance benchmarks, and provides a foundational framework with broad potential impact across various computer vision applications.
