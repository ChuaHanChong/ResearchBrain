# Slot-MPC: Goal-Conditioned Model Predictive Control with Object-Centric Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> Slot-MPC, developed by researchers at the University of Bonn, introduces a goal-conditioned Model Predictive Control framework that utilizes object-centric representations for planning from purely visual inputs. The method achieves competitive task success on robotic manipulation tasks, matching Dreamer-v3 on Meta-World tasks and outperforming baselines on robosuite tasks, while significantly reducing planning time by approximately 99% compared to holistic latent space methods.

## Problem

- Existing object-centric reinforcement learning policies are often reactive, limiting their generalization and adaptability to novel situations.
- Traditional Model Predictive Control (MPC) methods, especially sampling-based ones like MPPI or CEM, are computationally expensive due to evaluating numerous candidate trajectories.
- Holistic world models often struggle with long-horizon planning tasks and require extensive state-action coverage in training data for robust generalization.

## Method

- A self-supervised object-centric scene parsing module (SAVi) is used to decompose visual inputs into structured, compact latent slot representations.
- A Conditional Object-Centric Predictor (cOCVP), a transformer-based dynamics model, autoregressively forecasts future object slots conditioned on actions.
- Goal-conditioned planning is achieved via gradient-based ==MPC==, which optimizes action sequences in the differentiable object-centric latent space, warm-started by a behavior cloning policy.

## Results

- Slot-MPC achieved competitive task success rates, matching Dreamer-v3 (**0.64** vs **0.64**, **0.52** vs **0.56**) on Meta-World tasks and outperforming all baselines on robosuite tasks (Stack: **0.42**, Square: **0.22**).
- The framework demonstrated significantly faster planning times, operating at **0.42**-**0.48** seconds per step, which is approximately **99%** faster than DINO-WM (144-145 seconds per step).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical importance of the object-centric world model, the policy network for warm-starting, and gradient-based MPC for effective performance in offline settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object-centric latent representations provide a strong compositional inductive bias, enabling better generalization and more robust long-horizon predictions with limited offline data.
> - Gradient-based Model Predictive Control, when combined with differentiable object-centric world models, offers substantial computational efficiency improvements over sampling-based methods.
> - Warm-starting the MPC optimization with a learned policy prior is crucial for stabilizing gradient-based planning and achieving higher success rates, especially in complex, long-horizon tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in this paper is situated at the intersection of predictive world models, object-centric learning, and model predictive control (MPC) for robotic manipulation. The development of world models, which enable artificial agents to forecast future environment states and plan actions, is a fundamental area in artificial intelligence. Traditional world models often process scenes as unstructured pixel streams. However, human perception tends to parse scenes into discrete, persistent objects, inspiring a growing body of work in object-centric learning. These object-centric approaches have demonstrated benefits such as generalization to novel compositions, transferability to new tasks, and improved sample efficiency in various applications, including model-based reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning.

A common limitation in many existing object-centric learning and reinforcement learning paradigms is their reliance on reactive policies. These policies, once learned, are fixed at inference time, which can restrict their generalization capabilities to novel or previously unseen situations. Furthermore, online RL approaches typically demand extensive environment interactions, leading to high computational and data collection costs.

Model Predictive Control (MPC) offers an alternative approach by enabling online planning through the optimization of actions using a learned dynamics model during inference. This allows for adaptation to changing conditions. Recent advancements, such as TD-MPC2, have explored combining policy networks with MPC to leverage the advantages of both reactive policies and proactive planning. However, planning in these contexts often relies on gradient-free, sampling-based optimization methods like the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) or Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI), which can be computationally intensive due to the need to evaluate numerous candidate trajectories.

This paper addresses these issues by proposing a framework that integrates object-centric representations with gradient-based MPC. It aims to overcome the limitations of reactive policies, enhance generalization, and improve planning efficiency by operating in a structured, lower-dimensional latent space. Concurrently, other works have explored slot-based world models in MPC settings, but often rely on sampling-based methods or do not achieve the performance of holistic world models. The paper also acknowledges challenges in successful application of gradient-based MPC with learned world models, which historically have been limited to low-dimensional domains or struggled with scalability and computational cost. Slot-MPC contributes by providing a formulation for visual goal-directed planning that does not require reward signals during training or planning, using a differentiable object-centric dynamics model for efficient trajectory optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop an object-centric world modeling framework that facilitates goal-conditioned planning through Model Predictive Control (MPC) from purely visual inputs. This overall objective is driven by several specific motivations and aims:

1. **Enhance Generalization beyond Reactive Policies:** Existing object-centric reinforcement learning policies are predominantly reactive, meaning they are fixed after training and exhibit limited adaptability to novel or unencountered situations. The motivation is to move beyond these reactive behaviors by enabling online planning that can dynamically adapt to unforeseen circumstances at inference time.

2. **Improve Planning Efficiency:** Traditional MPC methods, especially those relying on sampling-based optimization (e.g., MPPI, CEM), can be computationally expensive. This is due to the necessity of evaluating hundreds or thousands of candidate action sequences at each decision step. The paper aims to address this by leveraging differentiable world models to enable gradient-based MPC, which directly optimizes action sequences, potentially leading to increased computational efficiency.

3. **Leverage Object-Centric Representations for Structured Reasoning:** Humans perceive the world in terms of distinct objects, allowing for intuitive reasoning about their dynamics and interactions. The motivation is to imbue artificial agents with a similar capability by adopting object-centric representations (slots). These structured representations are hypothesized to provide a strong inductive bias, offering a more compact and controllable latent space for modeling scene dynamics compared to holistic, unstructured pixel-based or patch-based representations. This is particularly relevant for scenarios with limited data or computational resources.

4. **Enable Goal-Conditioned Planning without Explicit Rewards:** Many model-based RL approaches rely on learned reward or value functions during training and planning. This work aims to formulate planning as minimizing the distance between predicted object configurations and a specified goal configuration in the latent object-centric space. This removes the dependency on explicit reward signals during training, allowing for the learning of task-agnostic world models from offline, reward-free data.

5. **Overcome Limitations of Holistic World Models in Long-Horizon Tasks:** While holistic world models can produce visually plausible predictions, they often struggle with long-horizon planning tasks, particularly in low-data regimes. The paper hypothesizes that object-centric models, with their inherent compositional inductive bias, can provide more robust and generalizable dynamics predictions for extended planning horizons, especially when state-action coverage is limited in offline datasets.

In summary, the key objectives are to introduce Slot-MPC as a gradient-based MPC method utilizing object-centric representations for goal-conditioned planning, demonstrate its efficiency and performance advantages over non-object-centric baselines, and illustrate the benefits of structured latent representations for complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Slot-MPC integrates object-centric latent dynamics learning with gradient-based Model Predictive Control (MPC). The framework consists of three main components: a Scene Parsing module, a Conditional Object-Centric Predictor (cOCVP), and an MPC optimizer, optionally guided by a learned policy.

#### 3.1. Object-Centric Latent Dynamics Learning

The foundation of Slot-MPC's representation learning is an object-centric approach based on SAVi (Slot Attention for Video).

- **Scene Parsing Module (SAVi):** This module decomposes an input image $X_t$ into a set of object representations, termed slots $S_t = (s^1_t, \dots, s^{N_S}_t) \in \mathbb{R}^{N_S \times D_S}$. SAVi is a recursive encoder-decoder model. The encoder $E_{SAVi}$ processes an input frame into image features, which are then fed to Slot Attention. Slot Attention refines previous slot representations by performing cross-attention between slots and image features, encouraging distinct slots to represent different entities. The slots are updated iteratively using a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) and a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). The decoder $D_{SAVi}$ reconstructs images from these slots by processing each slot independently with a Spatial Broadcast Decoder to produce object images and masks, which are then combined to synthesize the video frame. SAVi is trained in a self-supervised manner using an image reconstruction loss, minimizing the squared difference between the reconstructed and original images.

- **Conditional Object-Centric Predictor (cOCVP):** Building upon the pretrained SAVi model, cOCVP is a transformer-based latent dynamics model designed to autoregressively predict future object slots. It extends the original OCVP by incorporating action conditioning, allowing it to explicitly account for control inputs. Action vectors $a_t$ are projected into the predictor embedding space and additively combined with slot representations. Given past object slots and corresponding actions, cOCVP predicts the next slot state $\hat{S}_{t+1}$. This process is applied autoregressively to generate future states over a prediction horizon H. Training of cOCVP involves minimizing a combined objective function that includes a future frame prediction loss (comparing reconstructed predicted frames with ground truth) and a joint-embedding alignment loss (aligning predicted slots with slots inferred from ground-truth frames by $E_{SAVi}$). This dual loss aims to ensure accurate visual forecasting and temporal consistency in the latent dynamics.

#### 3.2. Policy Learning

To improve the efficiency and stability of MPC, a policy network $\pi_\theta$ is trained to provide an informed initial action sequence for the MPC optimization.

- **Behavior Cloning:** The policy network is trained via behavior cloning using a small dataset of expert demonstrations. It learns to predict expert actions $a_t$ directly from the structured slot-based latent representations $S_t$ produced by the pretrained SAVi model. The objective is to minimize the squared difference between the policy's predicted action and the expert action. This learned policy is used to "warm-start" the MPC procedure, providing a reasonable starting point for the optimizer.

#### 3.3. Model Predictive Control (MPC)

At inference time, Slot-MPC utilizes MPC in the learned object-centric latent space to optimize action sequences for reaching a goal state.

- **Goal and Current State Encoding:** A goal image $X_G$ is first encoded into latent goal slots $S_G$ using the object-centric encoder. Similarly, the current observation $X_t$ is encoded into initial latent slots $S_t$.

- **Trajectory Optimization:** The learned cOCVP dynamics model autoregressively forecasts future latent slot states $\hat{S}_{t+H}$ over the planning horizon H, conditioned on the initial slots $S_t$ and a candidate action sequence $a_{t:t+H-1}$. The MPC objective $J_{MPC}$ is defined as the squared Euclidean distance between the predicted final slot configuration $\hat{S}_{t+H}$ and the latent goal slots $S_G$.

- **Hungarian Matching:** A critical aspect for object-centric models is the permutation equivariance of slots. To ensure a consistent comparison, Hungarian matching is applied during MPC cost calculation. This algorithm aligns predicted object slots with goal slots by minimizing the pairwise Euclidean distance in latent space before computing the objective.

- **Receding-Horizon Control:** MPC optimizes the action sequence, but only the first action of the optimized sequence is executed in the environment. The process then repeats at the next time step (receding-horizon control), allowing for continuous replanning.

- **MPC Variants:**
 - **MPPI (Sampling-based MPC):** This method iteratively updates parameters of a time-dependent multivariate Gaussian distribution by sampling multiple trajectories, evaluating their costs using $J_{MPC}$, and updating the distribution parameters with importance-weighted empirical estimates. MPPI provides diversity in trajectory exploration.
 - **Gradient-based MPC:** Since the world model is differentiable, actions can be directly optimized by minimizing $J_{MPC}$ via gradient descent. This approach optimizes a single candidate action sequence, initialized by the learned policy, potentially offering higher computational efficiency than sampling-based methods due to direct gradient computation rather than sampling many trajectories. The policy network serves as an efficient guide, stabilizing the gradient-based optimization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Slot-MPC was conducted on four simulated robotic manipulation tasks: Button Press and Lever Pull from Meta-World, and Stack and Square from robosuite.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Setup

- **Datasets:** Two types of offline datasets were used for each environment:
 - Random exploration trajectories: Used for training the object-centric scene parsing model (SAVi) and the structured dynamics model (cOCVP).
 - Expert demonstrations: A smaller set used for training the behavior cloning policy (warm-start for MPC).
- **Observations:** Purely visual observations were used, without proprioceptive states.
- **Baselines:** Slot-MPC was compared against:
 - **Goal-conditioned Behavior Cloning (GC-BC):** Learns a goal-conditioned policy from reward-free offline data.
 - **Dreamer-v3:** A model-based RL method that jointly learns a visual world model and policy from online reward signals (trained with environment interaction).
 - **DINO-WM:** Learns a world model in the latent space of a pretrained DINOv2 encoder using offline, reward-free data, and plans using CEM (sampling-based MPC).
- **Evaluation Protocol:** All methods were evaluated on full episodes, starting from an initial simulator state and given a goal image. Success rates were reported over 50 evaluation episodes. This differs from some baseline evaluation procedures that use short, randomly sampled sub-trajectories.

#### 4.2. Comparison to Baselines

- **Task Success (Table 1):**
 - Slot-MPC demonstrated competitive performance, matching Dreamer-v3 on Meta-World tasks (Button Press: 0.64, Lever Pull: 0.52 vs. Dreamer-v3's 0.64 and 0.56, respectively).
 - On robosuite tasks (Stack, Square), Slot-MPC outperformed all baselines (Stack: 0.42, Square: 0.22, compared to Dreamer-v3's 0.30 and 0.00, and GC-BC's 0.30 and 0.00).
 - DINO-WM consistently failed to achieve task success (0.00%) across all tasks, despite its capacity to generate visually plausible predictions for shorter horizons.
- **Image Reconstruction Quality (Table 2):** DINO-WM showed high PSNR and SSIM, and low LPIPS, indicating good visual fidelity in its predictions. Slot-MPC also achieved comparable image metrics, demonstrating effective visual reconstruction. The failure of DINO-WM on task completion, despite good visual predictions, was attributed to its limitations in long-horizon planning.

- **Hypothesis on DINO-WM's performance:** The paper hypothesizes that holistic latent representations, as used by DINO-WM, demand substantially higher state-action coverage in the training data compared to object-centric representations, which incorporate a compositional inductive bias. This bias in Slot-MPC is suggested to enable stronger zero-shot generalization and mitigate limitations commonly observed in offline-trained policies that struggle with poor generalization from limited data quality or coverage.

#### 4.3. Model Analysis

- **Ablation Studies (Table 3a):**
 - The object-centric world model, the learned policy network for warm-starting, and gradient-based MPC were identified as critical components. Removing any of these led to significant performance drops.
 - The policy prior was particularly beneficial for long-horizon tasks (e.g., robosuite), suggesting its importance in guiding optimization through complex search spaces.
 - Gradient-based MPC was found to be substantially more effective than sampling-based MPPI in the offline setting with limited state-action coverage. MPPI's performance deteriorated significantly, even with a policy prior, potentially due to the dynamics model being queried out-of-distribution. Gradient-based MPC's tendency to stay closer to the nominal policy distribution was considered advantageous in this context.
- **MPC Objective Ablation (Table 3b):**
 - Using the sum of squared errors (SSE) or cosine similarity of aligned slots as the MPC objective yielded the best performance.
 - Objectives based on slot masks (e.g., SSE of aligned slot masks, normalized IoU) performed less effectively, possibly due to biases towards background or larger objects. Direct optimization over slot representations was preferred due to consistency with predictor training.
- **Planning Efficiency (Table 4):**
 - Slot-MPC demonstrated significantly faster planning times per step (0.42-0.48s) compared to DINO-WM (144.37-145.30s).
 - This efficiency gain was attributed to the substantial reduction in latent feature space dimensionality achieved by object-centric slot representations (e.g., 4 slots * 128 dimensions vs. 196 tokens * 384 dimensions for DINO-WM), representing an approximately 99% reduction.
 - Warm-starting MPC with a policy prior also contributed to computational efficiency.
 - Sampling-based MPPI within Slot-MPC was slower (4.22-5.19s) than gradient-based Slot-MPC, reinforcing the efficiency benefits of gradient-based optimization.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results (Appendix E.2, Figures 3-7)

Qualitative visualizations illustrated that Slot-MPC's object-centric models successfully parsed scenes into meaningful object representations, effectively assigning slots to entities like the background, robot arm/gripper, and specific task objects (button, lever, cubes, peg, nut). Visualizations also demonstrated the differences in long-horizon prediction accuracy between DINO-WM and actual environment rollouts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Slot-MPC: Goal-Conditioned Model Predictive Control with Object-Centric Representations" demonstrates a promising direction for developing intelligent agents capable of complex manipulation tasks through visual planning.

#### 5.1. Significance of Contributions

- **Integration of Object-Centric Learning and Gradient-based MPC:** The paper introduces a novel framework that effectively combines the benefits of structured, object-centric latent representations with the efficiency of gradient-based Model Predictive Control. This integration allows for goal-conditioned planning directly from raw visual observations, bypassing the need for explicit reward signals during training or planning.
- **Improved Task Performance and Generalization:** Slot-MPC demonstrates competitive or superior performance on challenging, long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks compared to established baselines, including online model-based RL and offline planning methods. The object-centric representations provide a strong inductive bias that facilitates better generalization, especially in offline settings with limited state-action coverage. This addresses a common limitation of reactive policies and holistic world models.
- **Enhanced Planning Efficiency:** By leveraging a compact object-centric latent space, Slot-MPC achieves significant computational efficiency gains in planning time compared to patch-based or holistic approaches. The use of a differentiable world model enables gradient-based optimization, which is shown to be substantially more efficient than sampling-based MPC methods in the investigated offline context. The policy prior for warm-starting further contributes to this efficiency.
- **Robustness in Low-Data Regimes:** The findings suggest that object-centric models can reason about scene dynamics more directly and controllably, which is particularly beneficial in low-data and low-compute environments. This makes Slot-MPC a viable option for scenarios where extensive online interaction or large, diverse datasets are not feasible.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Advancement in Model-Based Control:** This research contributes to the advancement of model-based control by demonstrating the practical utility of structured latent representations for complex planning problems. It highlights that explicit disentanglement of object-level entities can lead to more interpretable and controllable reasoning over scene dynamics.
- **Foundation for Scalable Robot Learning:** The approach offers a pathway towards more scalable and efficient robot learning from visual observations. By enabling robust planning with task-agnostic world models learned from offline, reward-free data, it reduces the need for expensive online interaction and task-specific reward engineering.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The success of Slot-MPC encourages further exploration into object-centric representations as an inductive bias for decision-making in various domains. Future work could extend this framework to incorporate text conditioning for more flexible goal specification, utilize subgoals for even longer-horizon tasks, and transition to real-world robotic deployments, potentially leveraging more advanced object-centric decomposition models. The demonstrated effectiveness of gradient-based MPC with object-centric world models also provides a new reference point for optimizing action sequences in learned latent spaces.
