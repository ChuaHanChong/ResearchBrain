# Slot-MPC: Goal-Conditioned Model Predictive Control with Object-Centric Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> Slot-MPC, developed by researchers at the University of Bonn, introduces a goal-conditioned Model Predictive Control framework that utilizes object-centric representations for planning from purely visual inputs. The method achieves competitive task success on robotic manipulation tasks, matching Dreamer-v3 on Meta-World tasks and outperforming baselines on robosuite tasks, while significantly reducing planning time by approximately 99% compared to holistic latent space methods.

## Problem

- Existing object-centric reinforcement learning policies are often reactive, limiting their generalization and adaptability to novel situations.
- Traditional Model Predictive Control (MPC) methods, especially sampling-based ones like MPPI or CEM, are computationally expensive due to evaluating numerous candidate trajectories.
- Holistic world models often struggle with long-horizon planning tasks and require extensive state-action coverage in training data for robust generalization.

## Method

- A self-supervised object-centric scene parsing module (SAVi) is used to decompose visual inputs into structured, compact latent slot representations.
- A Conditional Object-Centric Predictor (cOCVP), a transformer-based dynamics model, autoregressively forecasts future object slots conditioned on actions.
- Goal-conditioned planning is achieved via gradient-based ==MPC==, which optimizes action sequences in the differentiable object-centric latent space, warm-started by a behavior cloning policy.

## Results

- Slot-MPC achieved competitive task success rates, matching Dreamer-v3 (**0.64** vs **0.64**, **0.52** vs **0.56**) on Meta-World tasks and outperforming all baselines on robosuite tasks (Stack: **0.42**, Square: **0.22**).
- The framework demonstrated significantly faster planning times, operating at **0.42**-**0.48** seconds per step, which is approximately **99%** faster than DINO-WM (144-145 seconds per step).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical importance of the object-centric world model, the policy network for warm-starting, and gradient-based MPC for effective performance in offline settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object-centric latent representations provide a strong compositional inductive bias, enabling better generalization and more robust long-horizon predictions with limited offline data.
> - Gradient-based Model Predictive Control, when combined with differentiable object-centric world models, offers substantial computational efficiency improvements over sampling-based methods.
> - Warm-starting the MPC optimization with a learned policy prior is crucial for stabilizing gradient-based planning and achieving higher success rates, especially in complex, long-horizon tasks.
