# RLRC: Reinforcement Learning-based Recovery for Compressed Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The RLRC framework from Shanghai Jiao Tong University enables the deployment of large Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models on resource-constrained robotic platforms by significantly reducing memory footprint and improving inference speed. This approach achieves up to an 8.3x memory reduction and a 2.3x speedup, while improving the original VLA's task success rate, especially for out-of-distribution tasks.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models demand substantial computational and memory resources, impeding their real-world deployment on robotic platforms with limited hardware.
- Existing VLA compression methods often compromise performance or fail to provide sufficient efficiency for real-time, edge deployment scenarios.
- High inference latency of current VLA models makes them unsuitable for dynamic robotic tasks requiring real-time responses.

## Method

- RLRC employs a multi-stage pipeline, beginning with aggressive structured pruning (up to 90% parameter reduction) of the VLA's large language model component.
- A two-stage performance recovery process is utilized, combining Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) followed by Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning (RLFT) with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
- An optional final stage of 4-bit post-training quantization is integrated for extreme memory reduction, enabling deployment on highly resource-constrained devices.

## Results

- RLRC achieved an 8.3x memory reduction (from 14.858 GB to 1.772 GB) and an up to 2.3x inference throughput improvement (from 5.9 to 13.5 samples/s).
- The compressed RLRC model (without 4-bit quantization) surpassed the original OpenVLA, demonstrating a 90.62% in-distribution task success rate and a 62.50% out-of-distribution success rate (compared to OpenVLA's 89.06% and 57.81%).
- RL-based fine-tuning improved out-of-distribution generalization by approximately 30% over the original VLA, highlighting its ability to create more robust and adaptable policies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aggressive structured pruning effectively reduces model size for hardware efficiency but necessitates a robust performance recovery mechanism to prevent severe degradation.
> - Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) efficiently restores baseline task performance in pruned VLAs and is critical as a prerequisite for effective Reinforcement Learning optimization.
> - Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning builds upon SFT to significantly enhance both task success rates and out-of-distribution generalization capabilities, surpassing the uncompressed model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in robot learning have led to significant breakthroughs in the accuracy and generalization capabilities of robotic policies. A prominent development in this area is the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which have gained attention since the introduction of RT-2. These models leverage knowledge embedded in large pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to interpret language instructions, perceive visual environments, and generate appropriate actions for robotic manipulation. They exhibit strong generalization abilities, indicating potential for general-purpose robotic control.

However, a significant challenge limiting the practical deployment of VLA models is their computational and memory demands. Derived from large foundation models, VLAs typically have an extensive number of parameters, requiring powerful hardware and incurring high inference latency. This restricts their use on resource-constrained robotic platforms and in real-time, dynamic tasks.

Prior research has attempted to address these limitations through several strategies. One approach involves replacing heavy backbones with more lightweight and efficient architectures. Traditional model compression techniques, such as quantization and pruning, have also been applied to reduce model size and computational overhead. Additionally, methods focusing on refining visual token processing, such as enhanced token selection and adaptive pruning, aim to reduce unnecessary visual details without compromising critical information. While these methods offer some improvements in efficiency, the authors contend that they often do not achieve sufficient acceleration and memory efficiency for deployment in highly resource-constrained environments, and they may not fully account for the specific requirements of VLA models in robotic control.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by focusing on post-training compression and recovery for LLM-centric VLA backbones. It aims to develop a principled pipeline to derive lighter, faster variants of existing capable VLA models while preserving their manipulation abilities. The work specifically addresses the gaps left by previous compression methods, particularly concerning the inherent suitability of VLA for robotic control and the need for significant, hardware-friendly acceleration and memory reduction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the disparity between the advanced capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and their practical deployment limitations. VLA models, built upon large foundation models, demonstrate strong generalization and comprehension abilities for complex robotic manipulation tasks. However, their large parameter counts and high computational requirements result in substantial memory footprints and slow inference speeds. These factors hinder their adoption on resource-constrained robotic platforms, such as edge devices or smaller robots, and prevent their use in real-time or time-sensitive applications that require rapid action generation.

The key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

1. **Systematic Analysis of Compression:** To conduct a systematic empirical study of post-training compression techniques (quantization and structured pruning) specifically applied to LLM-centric VLA backbones. This analysis aims to understand the impact of various compression methods on VLA performance and hardware efficiency, thereby identifying effective strategies and motivating the design of a novel compression pipeline.
2. **Develop an Effective Compression and Recovery Pipeline:** To design and implement a multi-stage pipeline, named RLRC (Reinforcement Learning-based Recovery for Compressed Vision-Language-Action Models), that can substantially reduce VLA model size and inference costs. This pipeline is intended to integrate structured pruning for hardware-friendly compression, followed by performance recovery mechanisms.
3. **Preserve and Recover Task Performance:** A crucial objective is to ensure that compressed VLA models maintain, and ideally recover, their original task execution success rates. The authors observe that aggressive pruning regimes, while yielding significant deployment gains, can introduce substantial performance degradation. Therefore, a core objective is to develop robust recovery strategies capable of mitigating this performance loss.
4. **Enhance Recovery Training Stability:** To address the observed instability and performance drops during the transition from supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to reinforcement learning (RL) in the recovery phase. This involves developing strategies, such as critic warm-up and behavioral cloning (BC) loss regularization, to stabilize the RL training process and ensure effective policy improvement.
5. **Achieve Deployment-Oriented Efficiency:** To deliver VLA variants that are significantly lighter (reduced memory consumption) and faster (increased inference speed/throughput) for on-device deployment, without compromising the original model's manipulation ability. This includes exploring the role of optional 4-bit quantization for extreme memory reduction.
6. **Demonstrate Generalizability and Real-World Applicability:** To validate the proposed pipeline's effectiveness across multiple VLA backbones (e.g., OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, GR00T N1.6) and to demonstrate its performance in real-world robotic manipulation tasks, beyond simulated environments. This aims to establish RLRC as a practical pathway for deploying VLAs.

The motivation is fundamentally practical: to bridge the gap between the theoretical potential of powerful VLA models and their real-world usability on common robotic hardware by making them efficient enough for deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose RLRC (Reinforcement Learning-based Recovery for Compressed Vision-Language-Action Models), a three-stage pipeline designed to compress VLA models while preserving and recovering their task performance. The methodology is informed by an initial empirical study on compression techniques applied to VLA models.

**Empirical Study: Model Compression Applied to VLA**
The research begins with an empirical study on OpenVLA, evaluating the effects of quantization and pruning solely on its LLM component.
- **Quantization:** 8-bit and 4-bit quantization (using BitsAndBytes) showed minimal performance degradation but significant memory reduction. However, 8-bit quantization (LLM.int8) sometimes increased inference latency due to overhead. The study also found that quantization's speedup benefits diminished at higher sparsity ratios due to dequantization overhead.
- **Pruning:** Unstructured pruning (magnitude-based, Wanda) had little performance impact but negligible hardware gains without specialized acceleration. Structured pruning (LLM-Pruner, FLAP) caused greater performance degradation but offered superior acceleration potential by removing entire components (attention heads, neurons). These findings motivated the staged approach of RLRC.

**RLRC Pipeline**
The RLRC pipeline consists of three sequential stages:

**1. Structured Pruning of VLAs**
The first stage involves applying structured pruning to the VLA model, specifically targeting the Large Language Model (LLM) component, which typically accounts for the majority of parameters.
- **Target Selection:** The LLM component is chosen for compression due to its parameter dominance and the hardware efficiency benefits of structured pruning, which produces regular sparsity patterns amenable to modern accelerators.
- **Process:**
 1. **Structural Dependency Graph:** A structural dependency graph is constructed within the LLM to ensure coordinated pruning, preventing inconsistencies.
 2. **Importance Estimation:** The importance of each parameter group is estimated based on its influence on the training objective. The criterion favors groups whose removal minimally disrupts training dynamics. This is approximated using a first-order Taylor expansion of the change in training loss (L) when a parameter group (W_g) is removed, evaluated on the robotic expert datasets used for VLA supervised training. The importance score I_g is defined as:
 $I_g = |\Delta L_g| = |L(W_g = 0) - L(W_g)| \approx \sum_{W \in g} \frac{\partial L}{\partial W} W$
 3. **Group Pruning:** Groups with the lowest importance scores are pruned, assuming they contribute least to task performance. The authors use a 90% pruning ratio in main experiments, retaining the outermost decoder layers unchanged to preserve interface stability. This process structurally transforms attention and MLP layers within the LLM.

**2. Performance Recovery Based on SFT and RL**
Pruning alone creates a performance gap that requires recovery. This stage combines Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL).
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** As the initial recovery step, the pruned model is fine-tuned on the same task data using LoRA adapters and the supervised objective. This adapts the reduced architecture to the downstream task distribution and establishes an initial policy. The authors observe that SFT alone is often insufficient for full recovery, especially with aggressive pruning or subsequent 4-bit quantization.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is applied as the second recovery step to address the remaining performance gap, particularly for out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks.
 - **PPO Objective:** The PPO objective function is used to update the policy. For diffusion-based VLA models, noise is injected via Flow-SDE. A sparse reward system is employed: a constant time penalty per step, with a positive reward only upon task completion.
 - **Actor-Critic Setup:** The pruned VLA serves as the actor, and a lightweight value head attached to the shared transformer backbone acts as the critic.
 - **Addressing SFT-to-RL Transition Instability:** The authors identified a significant performance drop during the transition from SFT to RL due to unstable critic estimates and a distribution mismatch between SFT expert data and early RL rollouts. To mitigate this:
 1. **Critic Warm-up:** The VLA model is frozen, and only the randomly initialized value head is trained initially. This stabilizes advantage estimation before policy updates.
 2. **BC Loss Regularization:** A Behavioral Cloning (BC) loss, based on expert data, is added as a regularizer to the overall loss. This keeps the policy close to expert behavior early in RL training. The regularization weight $\lambda(t)$ linearly decays over time:
 $L_{total} = L_{PPO} + \lambda(t) \cdot L_{BC}$
 These modifications aim to smooth the transition and improve training stability and final policy quality.

**3. Further Quantization of VLAs (Optional)**
The final stage involves optional 4-bit quantization to achieve extreme memory compression.
- **Goal:** To further reduce the model's memory footprint, enabling deployment on highly resource-constrained devices. The nf4 format from the bitsandbytes library is used.
- **Consideration:** This step is labeled "optional" because, while it significantly reduces memory, it can introduce additional dequantization overhead, potentially increasing inference latency, especially in models already heavily compressed by pruning. Thus, it is recommended when memory constraints are critical.

**Experimental Setup**
- **Benchmarks:** LIBERO and ManiSkill3 (PutOnPlateInScene25Main task suite, with in-distribution (IND) and out-of-distribution (OOD) splits).
- **Baselines:** Representative VLAs (OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, GR00T N1.6), native lightweight VLAs (SmolVLA, UniACT, VLA-OS), and VLA acceleration methods (VLA-Cache, EfficientVLA, LightVLA, SP-VLA).
- **Metrics:** Task success rate, memory consumption, inference time per step, and action throughput.
- **Hardware:** NVIDIA RTX 5880 Ada GPU for most simulations.
- **Real-World Experiments:** Flexiv Rizon 4 robot with GRAV gripper, tabletop tasks (pick and place, stacking cubes, lifting peg) in a ManiSkill-like environment, using RealSense D435i cameras. Sim2real transfer was addressed by collecting 100 expert demonstrations for additional fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study's findings are presented in several key areas, including an initial empirical analysis, the overall performance of RLRC, an ablation study, and real-world experiments.

**Empirical Study of Model Compression for VLA:**
- **Quantization Impact:** 4-bit quantization significantly reduced memory (e.g., OpenVLA from 14.858 GB to 4.971 GB) with minimal performance degradation (e.g., 84.7% to 81.0% spatial success for OpenVLA). However, 8-bit quantization could increase inference latency due to dequantization overhead.
- **Pruning Impact:** Unstructured pruning (Magnitude, Wanda) had a small performance impact but negligible hardware acceleration without further mechanisms. Structured pruning (LLM-Pruner, FLAP) led to severe performance degradation (e.g., OpenVLA spatial success from 84.7% to 23.4% with LLM-Pruner at 20% sparsity) but offered substantial inference speedup (e.g., 169 ms to 139 ms). This motivated the need for a recovery pipeline.
- **Combined Effects:** Quantization benefits for inference speed diminished as sparsity increased. For highly sparse models, full-precision versions sometimes outperformed quantized ones in throughput due to dequantization overhead becoming proportionally larger.

**RLRC Overall Performance (Q1):**
RLRC consistently demonstrated competitive performance, often matching or surpassing the original VLA models, while achieving significant efficiency gains:
- **Memory Reduction:** Achieved up to an 8x memory reduction. For instance, OpenVLA-OFT-RLRC reduced memory from 14.977 GB to 4.021 GB (Table II, IV).
- **Inference Speedup:** Delivered up to a 2.3x inference speedup. OpenVLA-OFT-RLRC reduced latency from 149.23 ms to 65.59 ms.
- **Task Success Rate:** Maintained high task success rates. For example, OpenVLA-OFT-RLRC achieved an average LIBERO score of 97.6% (compared to 97.1% for original OpenVLA-OFT) and ManiSkill average of 81.25% (compared to 77.74%).
- **Comparison to Baselines:** RLRC variants generally provided a more balanced trade-off between inference speed, memory footprint, and task performance compared to existing lightweight VLAs or acceleration methods.

**Training Cost of RLRC (Q2):**
- RLRC training incurred additional costs primarily from SFT and RL, requiring approximately 320 GPU hours for OpenVLA-OFT-RLRC, which is higher than training a smaller native model like SmolVLA (80 GPU hours).
- However, this cost was justified by preserving strong priors from larger models, leading to higher success rates and better generalization compared to inherently small models or training-free acceleration methods.

**Applicability and Limitations of RLRC (Q3):**
- **Generalizability:** RLRC was successfully applied across different VLA architectures, including autoregressive (OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT) and diffusion-based (GR00T N1.6) models. Its effectiveness stems from operating primarily on the LLM component, leaving other modules unconstrained.
- **Structural Limitations:** The effectiveness varied with parameter distribution; GR00T N1.6, with a smaller LLM fraction, showed less compression and acceleration gains. RLRC also exhibited suboptimal performance on models like $\pi_0$ and $\pi_{0.5}$ where the LLM tightly shares transformer layers with the action expert, making aggressive LLM pruning disruptive. This indicates RLRC is more suitable for architectures with clear separation between language and action components.

**Ablation Study (Q4):**
- **SFT Importance:** SFT was crucial for initial performance recovery. Directly applying RL to a pruned VLA without SFT yielded no meaningful gains. SFT recovered substantial performance (e.g., LIBERO-Long from 0.0% after pruning to 86.8%).
- **RL Importance:** The RL stage further improved performance beyond SFT, especially for complex and long-horizon tasks (e.g., LIBERO-Long from 86.8% with SFT to 94.8% with SFT+RL).
- **Stabilization Strategies:** Critic warm-up and BC loss regularization were critical for stabilizing the SFT-to-RL transition. Without them, early RL training was unstable, leading to performance drops and less effective optimization.
- **Quantization Trade-offs:** While 4-bit quantization (RLRC-4bit) significantly reduced memory (e.g., 4.02 GB to 1.86 GB), it could increase inference latency (e.g., 65.59 ms to 86.58 ms). This was attributed to dequantization overhead becoming more pronounced in already heavily compressed models.

**Real-World Experiments (Q5):**
- RLRC demonstrated strong performance in real-world manipulation tasks (pick and place, stacking cubes, lifting a peg) after additional fine-tuning with expert demonstrations to mitigate the sim2real gap.
- OpenVLA-OFT-RLRC, for instance, achieved 86.7% success on pick and place, 86.7% on stacking cubes, and 56.7% on lifting a peg, comparable to or even slightly surpassing the original OpenVLA-OFT in some cases, while maintaining substantial memory and latency reductions (e.g., 14.977 GB to 4.021 GB memory, 149.23 ms to 65.59 ms latency for OpenVLA-OFT-RLRC).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic learning by addressing a critical challenge: the deployment of high-capacity Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models on resource-constrained hardware. The proposed RLRC pipeline offers a systematic and empirically validated approach to achieve significant model compression and inference acceleration while maintaining, and in some instances improving upon, the original task performance.

The key significance and potential impact areas include:

1. **Enabling On-Device Deployment:** By achieving up to an 8x memory reduction and a 2.3x inference speedup, RLRC facilitates the deployment of powerful VLA models on edge computing platforms and robots with limited computational resources. This is crucial for expanding the real-world applicability of advanced robotic manipulation capabilities, moving beyond high-performance lab environments.
2. **Practical Compression Pipeline:** RLRC provides a principled, three-stage pipeline (structured pruning, SFT+RL recovery, optional 4-bit quantization) that is directly applicable to existing LLM-centric VLA backbones. This systematic approach offers a clear pathway for practitioners to optimize their models for efficiency without extensive manual tuning or architectural redesign.
3. **Advanced Performance Recovery Strategies:** The research highlights the empirical necessity of combining Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) for comprehensive performance recovery after aggressive pruning. The introduction of critic warm-up and BC loss regularization to stabilize the SFT-to-RL transition is a notable methodological contribution, enhancing the reliability and effectiveness of RL-based policy refinement in this context.
4. **In-depth Empirical Analysis:** The initial systematic study on quantization and structured pruning for VLAs provides valuable insights into their individual and combined effects on performance and hardware metrics. This analysis clarifies trade-offs, such as the diminishing returns of quantization speedup at high sparsity, which can guide future compression efforts.
5. **Cross-Backbone Generalizability:** The validation of RLRC across diverse VLA architectures, including both autoregressive and diffusion-based models (OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, GR00T N1.6), demonstrates its broad applicability. This suggests that the core principles of the pipeline are robust to variations in VLA design, provided certain architectural separations exist.
6. **Real-World Validation:** The inclusion of real-world robot experiments strengthens the practical relevance of RLRC. While acknowledging sim2real challenges, the results indicate that the compressed models retain competitive performance on physical hardware, reinforcing the claim of advancing VLA technologies for real-world scenarios.

In summary, RLRC addresses a critical bottleneck in robot learning by making advanced VLA models more accessible and deployable. Its structured approach and empirical validation lay a foundation for the widespread adoption of general-purpose robotic manipulation systems, fostering further innovation in both efficient model design and practical robot intelligence.
