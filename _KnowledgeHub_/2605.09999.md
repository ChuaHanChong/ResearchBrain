# Muninn: Your Trajectory Diffusion Model But Faster

> [!summary] Summary
> A training-free caching wrapper called Muninn accelerates diffusion-based trajectory planners for real-time robotics by providing quantifiable guarantees on trajectory fidelity, achieving significant latency reductions (e.g., 4.6x on AntMaze) while maintaining task performance and safety.

## Problem

- Diffusion-based trajectory models are computationally expensive due to their iterative denoising process, limiting their use in real-time robotic applications.
- Existing acceleration methods for diffusion models often require costly retraining, modify the original model, or lack explicit guarantees on how approximation errors affect trajectory quality and safety.
- There is a need for a general, training-free solution that can accelerate diverse pre-trained trajectory diffusion architectures while preserving performance and providing quantifiable fidelity bounds.

## Method

- **Muninn** introduces a training-free ==caching wrapper== that leverages an inexpensive internal probe of the trajectory representation and analytically derived sampler sensitivity to manage computational reuse.
- It uses ==split-conformal regression== for offline calibration to provide high-probability upper bounds on per-step reuse errors, ensuring distribution-free finite-sample coverage guarantees.
- At deployment, Muninn employs a ==budgeted reuse rule== that dynamically decides when to recompute or reuse denoiser outputs based on a user-specified trajectory deviation tolerance and calibrated per-step error bounds.

## Results

- Muninn achieved substantial latency reductions (e.g., **4.0x** on HalfCheetah, **4.6x** on AntMaze) across diverse robotic benchmarks and hardware platforms (e.g., ASV, UAV, robotic arm).
- Task performance (e.g., D4RL scores, success rates) and safety metrics (e.g., collision rates) were consistently maintained at or above baselines, with empirical risk violation rates meeting user-specified tolerances.
- The method outperformed other inference-time acceleration baselines and recovered a substantial portion of the speedups from training-time methods without retraining, demonstrating difficulty-adaptive compute allocation and providing a usable runtime certificate for trajectory fidelity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Changes in inexpensive internal representations of a diffusion denoiser serve as a reliable, low-cost probe to estimate the stability of a trajectory and potential reuse error.
> - The analytical form of common diffusion samplers allows for quantifying how errors in predicted noise propagate through subsequent denoising steps, providing pathwise sensitivity coefficients.
> - Split-conformal prediction can be effectively applied to calibrate probabilistic upper bounds on reuse errors, enabling a global guarantee on the final trajectory deviation without distribution assumptions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous robots increasingly utilize diffusion-based trajectory models for motion planning and control. These models are capable of generating complex, multimodal motion sequences and capturing diverse future possibilities in dynamic and uncertain environments. This capability addresses limitations of traditional model-based planning and trajectory optimization methods, which often struggle with multimodal solution sets induced by complex dynamics, human interactions, or multi-goal behaviors. However, a significant challenge for diffusion-based planners is their computational cost. The iterative denoising process, typically involving dozens of steps, renders them prohibitively slow for online planning and real-time control, especially on embedded platforms or in high-rate control loops.

Existing efforts to accelerate diffusion models generally fall into two categories:
- **Model design modifications:** This includes distilling diffusion processes into shallower networks, learning coarse-to-fine samplers, or biasing generative processes with structured priors. While these methods can achieve substantial speedups, they often necessitate retraining the model for each architecture or task, and may sacrifice trajectory quality or generalizability.
- **Adaptive inference and early exiting:** These training-free approaches operate at inference time by monitoring signals during the sampling chain to decide when to stop, skip, or reuse computations. While attractive for their data-efficiency and ability to work with existing architectures, these methods are often calibrated on perceptual metrics for vision tasks, and their approximation errors are not well-understood in terms of trajectory deviation or its impact on closed-loop control.

Muninn addresses this gap by developing a training-free acceleration method specifically for trajectory diffusion models. It aims to achieve real-time performance without modifying or retraining the underlying diffusion model, while also providing guarantees on the fidelity of the accelerated trajectories relative to their full-compute counterparts, and considering the implications for downstream robotic control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to accelerate diffusion-based trajectory planners sufficiently for real-time robot operation. This objective is underpinned by several specific motivations and constraints:
- **Mitigate computational bottleneck:** Diffusion-based planners require numerous iterative denoising steps, leading to high latency that prevents their widespread adoption in real-time robotic applications, particularly on resource-constrained platforms. The core motivation is to overcome this computational burden.
- **Preserve trajectory quality and safety:** Existing acceleration techniques often compromise the quality of generated trajectories or lack explicit guarantees regarding how approximation errors affect plan quality and safety in downstream control. A key motivation is to maintain the rich, multimodal motion generation capabilities of diffusion models without degradation in task performance or an increase in safety violations.
- **Avoid model retraining:** Many acceleration methods necessitate retraining the diffusion model, which is computationally expensive, requires new data or architectural modifications, and ties the acceleration logic to a specific model or task. Muninn is motivated by the desire for a general, training-free solution that can be applied as a wrapper to diverse pre-trained diffusion architectures.
- **Provide quantifiable guarantees on deviation:** A crucial motivation is to establish a clear and quantifiable link between "saved compute" and "lost plan quality." The authors aim to provide explicit bounds on how much an accelerated trajectory can deviate from its full-compute counterpart, enabling users to manage risk with a specified deviation tolerance and risk level.
- **Generalizability across diffusion architectures:** The solution should not be limited to a specific type of diffusion model but should ideally work across various state-space diffusion architectures used for trajectory generation (e.g., offline RL planners, configuration-space motion planners, visuomotor policies).

In summary, the research aims to make powerful diffusion-based trajectory planners practical for real-time robotics by developing a smart caching mechanism that leverages internal signals to selectively reuse computations, thereby accelerating inference without compromising quality or requiring retraining, all while offering quantifiable guarantees on trajectory fidelity.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Muninn is a training-free caching wrapper designed to accelerate trajectory diffusion models. Its methodology is built upon two core insights from diffusion-based trajectory planners: (i) the availability of a low-cost probe of the internal trajectory representation, and (ii) the analytic coefficients describing how denoiser errors propagate through the sampler.

#### 3.1. Core Insights

1. **Internal Trajectory Representation Probe:** Every denoising step involves an inexpensive input-processing stage that transforms the current noisy trajectory and timestep into an intermediate representation. Changes in this representation across timesteps can serve as a cheap signal for the stability of the trajectory.
2. **Analytic Sampler Sensitivity:** The mathematical form of the sampler used to update trajectories from denoiser outputs is known. This allows for an analytical description of how a perturbation in the predicted noise at a given step $t$ affects the state at step $t-1$.

#### 3.2. A. Trajectory Diffusion and Sampler Sensitivity (Section IV-A)

Muninn formalizes the impact of local errors. It considers a paired process where a full-compute chain (denoiser evaluated at every step) and a cached chain (reusing denoiser outputs at selected steps) share the same initial noisy trajectory and sampler noise sequence.
- **Error Variables:** Two types of errors are defined:
 - **Trajectory drift ($\Delta_t$):** The difference between the full-compute trajectory ($\tau^\text{full}_t$) and the cached trajectory ($\tilde{\tau}_t$) at step $t$.
 - **Denoiser mismatch ($e_t$):** The difference between the full-compute denoiser output and the actual noise prediction used by the cached chain at step $t$.
- **Sampler Lipschitzness (Assumption 1):** The core assumption is that the sampler update function ($\Phi_t$) is Lipschitz continuous with respect to changes in the trajectory and the noise prediction. This means there exist constants $K_t$ (scaling trajectory differences) and $L'_t$ (scaling sensitivity to noise-prediction error) that bound the propagation of error. For DDPM/DDIM-style samplers, $K_t$ and $L'_t$ can be derived analytically from their update coefficients.
- **Unrolling the Recursion:** By repeatedly applying the Lipschitz bound from $t=T$ down to $t=1$, and noting that $\Delta_T = 0$, a closed-form bound on the final trajectory deviation $\Delta_0$ is obtained:
 $\|\Delta_0\| \le \sum_{t=1}^T L_t \|e_t\|$, where $L_t := L'_t \prod_{j=1}^{t-1} K_j$ are pathwise sensitivity coefficients. These coefficients quantify how strongly denoiser errors at step $t$ are amplified through subsequent diffusion updates, indicating that early steps can have a larger impact.
- **Metric Compatibility (Assumption 2):** To translate the norm difference to a user-chosen trajectory metric $d(\cdot, \cdot)$, the metric is assumed to be Lipschitz-equivalent to the Frobenius norm, i.e., $d(\tau, \tau') \le \Gamma \|\tau - \tau'\|$ for some constant $\Gamma \ge 1$.
- **Trajectory Deviation Bound:** Combining these, the total trajectory deviation is bounded: $d(\tau^\text{full}_0, \tilde{\tau}_0) \le \sum_{t=1}^T \Gamma L_t \|e_t\|$. Each term $\Gamma L_t \|e_t\|$ is defined as the per-step trajectory cost $c_t(e_t)$.

#### 3.3. B. Probe Features and Per-step Error Scores (Section IV-B)

Since $\|e_t\|$ is unknown at runtime, Muninn introduces a proxy based on internal model signals.
- **Probe Function ($\Psi$):** The denoiser's evaluation is decomposed into a cheap "stem" stage (input processing) and an expensive "core" stage. $\Psi$ is defined to capture the intermediate representation ($F_t$) from the stem stage, satisfying two goals: negligible computation cost compared to a full denoiser pass, and architecture-agnostic implementation (by tapping into common intermediate representations).
- **Per-step Error Score ($s_t$):** A scalar score $s_t \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ is derived from the sequence of probe features $\{F_t\}$. The chosen score, $s_t := \phi(F_t, F_{t+1}, t) = \frac{\|F_t - F_{t+1}\|_1}{\|F_{t+1}\|_1 + \omega}$, intuitively reflects the magnitude of the probe change between consecutive steps. Smaller changes suggest stability and potentially smaller denoiser errors.

#### 3.4. C. Conformal Calibration of Reuse Error (Section IV-C)

Muninn uses offline calibration to predict high-probability upper bounds on reuse error.
- **Calibration Data:** A dataset $S_\text{cal}$ of (score, error) pairs is built by running the full-compute chain alongside a "ghost" reuse chain (which follows a fixed, policy-agnostic reuse rule) on $N$ i.i.d. episodes. For each step $t$ where reuse is permitted, the probe-based score $s_t$ and the actual potential reuse error magnitude $\epsilon_t := \|e_t\|$ are recorded.
- **Split-Conformal Regression:** This technique provides finite-sample coverage guarantees. A regression model $m(s)$ is fit on a training split of $S_\text{cal}$ to capture the typical score-error relationship. Split conformal prediction is then applied on a held-out split to obtain an additive residual quantile $q_{1-\alpha_\text{step}}$. The resulting upper bound $U(s) := \max\{m(s) + q_{1-\alpha_\text{step}}, 0\}$ guarantees that $P(\epsilon_t \le U(s_t)) \ge 1 - \alpha_\text{step}$ for a new test sample, provided calibration and deployment samples are exchangeable. This guarantee is distribution-free.
- **Global Risk:** By defining the upper-bounded per-step cost as $\hat{c}_t(s_t) := \Gamma L_t U_t(s_t)$, Muninn can ensure that with probability at least $1 - \alpha_\text{step}$, $c_t(e_t) \le \hat{c}_t(s_t)$.

#### 3.5. D. Muninn Caching Policy (Section IV-D)

At deployment, Muninn uses the per-step bounds $\hat{c}_t(s_t)$ to make online reuse decisions.
- **Budgeted Reuse Rule (Algorithm 1):** The user specifies a total deviation tolerance $\eta_\text{traj}$ as a budget $B_\text{rem}$. At each diffusion step $t$, Muninn computes $F_t$, then $s_t$, and finally $\hat{c}_t(s_t)$. It reuses a cached denoiser output if $t$ is in the cache-eligible set and $\hat{c}_t(s_t)$ does not exceed $B_\text{rem}$. If reused, $B_\text{rem}$ is reduced by $\hat{c}_t(s_t)$; otherwise, the denoiser is recomputed.
- **Global Risk Guarantee:** By choosing $\alpha_\text{step} := \alpha / |\mathcal{T}_\text{cache}|$, where $|\mathcal{T}_\text{cache}|$ is the number of cache-eligible timesteps, Muninn guarantees that the probability of the final trajectory deviation $d(\tau^\text{full}_0, \tilde{\tau}_0)$ exceeding $\eta_\text{traj}$ is less than or equal to $\alpha$. This relies on a union bound over per-step failure probabilities.

#### 3.6. E. Practical Considerations and Engineering Choices (Section IV-E)

- **Forbidden Regions:** Reuse is disallowed in a short prefix of steps (highly noisy, large sensitivity) and a short suffix (execution-sensitive).
- **Batched Sampling:** An independent remaining budget $B_\text{rem}$ is maintained per trajectory in a batch, with independent reuse decisions.
- **Model Instantiations:** For Transformer denoisers, $\Psi$ uses a prefix of attention/MLP blocks; for MLP denoisers, it uses the penultimate hidden layer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Muninn was evaluated across diverse benchmarks covering offline RL planning (D4RL), configuration-space motion planning, and visuomotor diffusion policies, as well as on hardware deployments. The results consistently demonstrate its effectiveness in accelerating diffusion models while preserving performance and safety.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results: Simulation and Benchmarks (Section V-A)

- **Offline RL / Trajectory Planning (D4RL, Table I):** Across MuJoCo locomotion, goal-reaching navigation, and long-horizon manipulation tasks, Muninn consistently reduced the number of denoiser evaluations and wall-clock latency. For instance, on HalfCheetah, Diffuser saw latency reduced from 580ms to 145ms (4.0x speedup), and on AntMaze, Diffuser reduced latency from 950ms to 207ms (4.6x speedup). Task performance (D4RL normalized score) and safety metrics (e.g., collision rate) were maintained, with empirical risk violation rates ($\hat{p}_\text{viol}$) consistently close to or below the user-specified tolerance $\alpha$.
- **Motion Planning in Configuration Space (Table II):** For a 7-DoF arm in cluttered environments, Muninn yielded latency reductions (e.g., EDMP: 120ms to 92ms for easy scenarios) while preserving success rates and collision percentages.
- **Visuomotor Imitation and Manipulation (Table III):** In tasks like RLBench Reach Target, Meta-World pick-place-v2, and DP3 Pour, Muninn reduced policy inference latency (e.g., Diffusion Policy on Reach Target: 25ms to 15ms) with minimal impact on success rates.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results: Hardware Evaluation (Section V-B)

Hardware deployments on a SeaRobotics Surveyor ASV (2D marine navigation), a Crazyflie quadrotor (3D aerial navigation), and a SO-ARM100 tabletop arm (manipulation) validated Muninn's real-time capabilities.
- **ASV Navigation:** GC-Diffuser+Muninn reduced latency from 70ms to 28ms, maintaining success and collision rates.
- **UAV Navigation:** GC-Diffuser+Muninn reduced latency from 75ms to 30ms, preserving performance.
- **SO-ARM100 Manipulation:** Diffusion Policy+Muninn reduced latency from 40ms to 24ms, maintaining success and collision rates.
The empirical deviation from full-compute trajectories ($E[d]$) and the risk violation probability ($\hat{p}_\text{viol}$) remained low and within specified bounds in hardware settings.

#### 4.3. Key Findings, Analysis, and Ablations (Section V-C)

- **KF#1: Dominance over inference-time acceleration baselines:** Muninn consistently delivered a superior Pareto frontier for task performance vs. latency compared to common inference-time baselines (FewSteps, FixedSkip, ProbeThresh). These baselines, which implicitly or heuristically allocate approximation error, often resulted in lower task success or higher risk violations for comparable speedups, largely because they do not account for sampler-mediated error propagation.
- **KF#2: Closing the gap to training-time accelerations:** Muninn recovered a substantial portion of the latency reduction achievable by training-time acceleration methods (e.g., Distill-1step, SmallNet, LearnedExit) without requiring additional training compute, data, or architectural changes. Furthermore, Muninn demonstrated composability, providing additional speedups when applied on top of already distilled or compact models.
- **KF#3: Difficulty-adaptive compute allocation:** Muninn's decision rule dynamically adapts compute based on the difficulty of the situation. In cluttered or high-risk environments (e.g., tight clearances in arm planning), it automatically performs more denoiser re-evaluations, while aggressively reusing computations in easier, open-space scenarios. This contrasts with fixed acceleration methods that apply uniform compute.
- **KF#4: Usable runtime certificate correlated with safety:** Muninn's internal tracking of the upper-bound proxy for deviation ($\hat{D}$) provides an explicit, online runtime certificate. This allows for post-hoc explainability, attributing risk to specific timesteps, and enables runtime escalation strategies (e.g., temporary full-compute, reduced action magnitude, switching to a safety controller) when the remaining budget is low or $\hat{D}$ is high. The empirical $\hat{D}$ was shown to correlate with actual realized trajectory deviations.

**Ablations:** The ablation studies (Appendix D) confirmed several design choices:
- The deviation budget ($\eta_\text{traj}$) acts as a smooth knob for trading speed and fidelity.
- Split-conformal calibration is essential for reliably achieving target violation probabilities.
- Sensitivity-aware budgeting using $L_t$ significantly improves the speed-safety trade-off.
- Shallow stem probes offer the best balance of predictive power and low overhead.
- Forbidding reuse in early/late diffusion steps further reduces collisions with minor speed loss.
- Calibration is sample-efficient, stabilizing performance after a modest number of rollouts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Muninn represents a notable advancement in making diffusion-based trajectory planners practical for real-time robotic applications. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Enabling Real-time Deployment:** By drastically reducing the computational latency of diffusion models without requiring retraining or sacrificing performance, Muninn directly addresses a critical bottleneck. This allows sophisticated, multimodal trajectory planners to be deployed on resource-constrained platforms or in high-rate control loops, broadening the applicability of diffusion models in areas like autonomous navigation, manipulation, and human-robot interaction.
- **Enhanced Safety and Trustworthiness:** The introduction of a quantifiable, user-controlled fidelity guarantee ($\eta_\text{traj}$, $\alpha$) is a key contribution. This explicit risk budgeting allows engineers to specify how much deviation from the ideal (full-compute) planner is acceptable, providing a layer of safety assurance that is often absent in heuristic acceleration methods. This can contribute to increased trust and reliability in autonomous systems.
- **Generalizability and Ease of Adoption:** Muninn's design as a training-free wrapper that can operate on diverse state-space diffusion architectures makes it highly adaptable. It enables existing pre-trained models to benefit from acceleration without costly and time-consuming retraining, thereby reducing development cycles and computational resources. This broad applicability across different robot types (aerial, ground, marine, manipulators) and diffusion model variants (offline RL, motion planning, visuomotor policies) is a significant advantage.
- **Improved Resource Management:** The adaptive compute allocation, where Muninn spends more computational effort in challenging or uncertain situations and less in simpler ones, leads to more efficient use of resources. This intelligent resource management is particularly valuable in dynamic environments where task difficulty can fluctuate.
- **Diagnostic and Explainable AI:** The online deviation certificate provides an interpretable signal about the fidelity of the generated trajectory. This can serve as a diagnostic tool for understanding when approximations might become critical and can be integrated into higher-level reasoning or safety systems to trigger fallback behaviors or request additional compute, fostering more explainable and robust AI in robotics.

A promising future direction highlighted by the authors is to develop context-aware deviation budgets that dynamically adjust based on environmental clutter, contact-richness, or risk levels, further optimizing compute allocation. Overall, Muninn contributes a robust, principled, and practical method for integrating powerful generative models into the challenging domain of real-time robotic control.
