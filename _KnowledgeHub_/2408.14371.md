# SelEx: Self-Expertise in Fine-Grained Generalized Category Discovery

> [!summary] Summary
> SelEx introduces a Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) method for fine-grained classification by fostering 'self-expertise' through hierarchical representation learning and refined contrastive strategies. The approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on fine-grained datasets, demonstrating 87.4% overall accuracy on CUB-200 and competitive results across other benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) methods often struggle with fine-grained distinctions, leading to fragmented clustering and high false negative rates.
- Traditional contrastive learning, a common foundation for GCD, faces limitations in distinguishing subtly different categories due to its general positive/negative pairing mechanism.
- The challenge lies in simultaneously classifying known categories and discovering novel ones with high accuracy, particularly when subtle visual differences are crucial.

## Method

- ==SelEx== develops =='self-expertise'== for fine-grained GCD using a combination of ==Hierarchical Pseudo-Label Extraction==, Unsupervised Self-Expertise, and Supervised Self-Expertise.
- A ==Hierarchical Semi-Supervised K-means (HSSK)== generates multi-level pseudo-labels to provide hierarchical supervisory signals.
- Unsupervised Self-Expertise (L_USE) refines contrastive learning by emphasizing hierarchical 'hard negatives', while Supervised Self-Expertise (L_SSE) leverages abstract pseudo-labels with ==segmented latent dimensions== to accelerate learning.

## Results

- SelEx consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance on fine-grained datasets including CUB-200 (**87.4%** overall accuracy), FGVC-Aircraft, Stanford-Cars, and Oxford-IIIT Pet, outperforming prior methods.
- The method demonstrates robustness to various conditions, including varying ratios of labeled data, estimated numbers of categories, and adaptability to long-tailed datasets.
- Ablation studies confirm the individual contribution of each component, with HSSK providing the most significant improvement, increasing overall accuracy on CUB-200 from **46.1%** to **62.6%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A theoretical framework based on KL divergence shows that a hierarchical approach reduces the upper bound on estimation error in both supervised and unsupervised contrastive learning, enabling finer granularity.
> - Segmenting the latent embedding dimension across hierarchical levels forces the model to learn progressively finer-grained distinctions, building 'self-expertise' from coarse to fine.
> - Refining negative sample treatment with hierarchical pseudo-labels and label smoothing allows the model to differentiate subtly similar instances more effectively without indiscriminately repelling semantically related samples.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper operates within the active and challenging domain of **Generalized Category Discovery (GCD)**. GCD is a crucial area in machine learning that addresses the limitations of traditional supervised learning, which struggles when confronted with categories not seen during training. In real-world applications, models frequently encounter novel classes, making the ability to simultaneously classify known categories and discover unknown ones highly desirable.

The research landscape for GCD has seen two primary approaches:
1. **Prototype-based methods:** These approaches use a series of reference points (prototypes) to represent categories, e.g..
2. **Local similarity/Pseudo-labeling methods:** These leverage sample similarities to form local clusters or use mean-teacher networks to handle noisy pseudo-labels, e.g..

A common foundation for many GCD techniques is **self-supervised learning (SSL)**, particularly **contrastive learning**. While powerful for learning rich representations from unlabeled data, contrastive learning has documented limitations in **fine-grained classification**. Its core mechanism, which treats augmented versions of a sample as positive pairs and all others as negative, can struggle when distinguishing between subtly different categories. Strong augmentations might make positive samples significantly different, while nuanced visual differences between samples of the same fine-grained category can lead to false negatives and fragmented clustering. Supervised contrastive learning, while improving discrimination among known categories, does not directly address the discovery of *unknown* categories due to the absence of supervisory signals for them.

*SelEx* positions itself by acknowledging these limitations and offering a solution specifically for **fine-grained GCD**. It aims to overcome the shortcomings of traditional contrastive learning by introducing the concept of "self-expertise" and a hierarchical approach. This situates *SelEx* as an advancement in the field, moving beyond generic category discovery to tackle the more intricate problem of discerning subtle differences within novel categories, a challenge that existing methods often "fall short" on. The work also connects to **hierarchical representation learning**, a sub-field that leverages taxonomic structures to enhance feature learning, but *SelEx* introduces a novel way of using negative samples and weak supervision within this hierarchical context for GCD.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of "SelEx" is to develop a novel Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) approach that effectively addresses the simultaneous challenges of **uncovering novel categories** and **accurately classifying known categories**, with a particular emphasis on **fine-grained distinctions**.

The core motivations and specific objectives driving this work are:

- **Addressing limitations of existing GCD methods:** Traditional GCD methods, largely reliant on self-supervision and contrastive learning, often struggle with fine-grained categories. This leads to fragmented clustering and increased false negative rates due to difficulties in distinguishing subtle differences within seemingly similar categories.
- **Introducing "self-expertise":** The paper aims to define and implement a novel concept called 'self-expertise', conceptualized as the model's enhanced ability to generalize across different abstraction levels (like an ornithologist distinguishing species) and recognize subtle differences within categories. This is crucial for fine-grained tasks.
- **Merging discovery with fine-grained classification:** The goal is to develop a unified framework that can both discover unknown categories and perform accurate, fine-grained classification on both known and novel categories, balancing the essential trade-off between these two objectives.
- **Improving contrastive learning for fine-grained GCD:** The authors aim to refine contrastive learning by integrating pseudo-labeling and the 'self-expertise' concept to overcome issues like limited positive samples and poor discrimination of subtle differences.

To achieve these motivations, the authors outline four key contributions:

1. **Develop a hierarchical semi-supervised k-means clustering approach:** This aims to better initialize unknown clusters using known centers and balance distributions to address cluster sparsity, forming the foundation of hierarchical pseudo-labeling.
2. **Propose an unsupervised self-expertise approach:** This strategy focuses on emphasizing "hard negative" samples (those with identical pseudo-labels at each hierarchical level) to sharpen category distinctions.
3. **Introduce a supervised self-expertise strategy:** This utilizes abstract pseudo-labels to generate weaker positive and stronger negative instances, thereby accelerating initial category clustering and enhancing generalization to novel categories.
4. **Provide empirical and theoretical validation:** The paper seeks to demonstrate, both theoretically and empirically, that their proposed approach effectively facilitates generalized category discovery with superior fine-grained abilities compared to existing state-of-the-art techniques.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "SelEx" methodology is built upon three interconnected components designed to foster 'self-expertise' for fine-grained Generalized Category Discovery: **Hierarchical Pseudo-Label Extraction**, **Unsupervised Self-Expertise**, and **Supervised Self-Expertise**. The approach is supported by a theoretical framework based on Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Framework for Self-Expertise

The paper formulates GCD within a Bayesian Network framework, aiming to minimize the KL divergence between the true distribution of category equality and the model's estimated distribution.
- **Supervised Self-Expertise (SSE):** For supervised contrastive learning, where one category variable (c_i) is known, the KL divergence upper bound is shown to be proportional to ln(N/K). The core theoretical insight is that reducing the effective number of categories (K) diminishes this upper bound. This motivates a hierarchical structure where, at each stage, categories are bifurcated, effectively reducing K and thus refining detection granularity. A hierarchical approach, ˆS_K, is shown to reduce the error more effectively (ˆS_K ≤ ˆS_(K/2)).
- **Unsupervised Self-Expertise (USE):** For unsupervised contrastive learning, where both category variables (c_i, c_j) are unknown, the initial upper bound is higher (proportional to K(K+1)/2 * ln(N/K)). To mitigate this, the authors propose restricting the KL divergence to pairs where c_i = c_j (i.e., hard negatives within the same category), which leads to a reduced upper bound similar to the supervised case (K * ln(N/K)). This also benefits from a hierarchical strategy (ˆU_K ≤ ˆU_(K/2)), inspiring the use of hierarchical hard negatives to develop unsupervised self-expertise.

#### 3.2. Hierarchical Pseudo-Label Extraction

This component generates reliable pseudo-labels across multiple abstraction levels for unlabeled data, serving as a supervisory signal.
- **Balanced Semi-Supervised K-means (BSSK):**
 - **Initialization:** K-means centers for known categories are determined from labeled data. For novel categories, an equivalent number of random samples are selected as initial cluster centers. To ensure "purity" and prevent overlap, a specified number of nearest data points (C) are excluded around each initial center, particularly in balanced datasets.
 - **Updating and Balancing:** Unlike traditional K-means, BSSK primarily updates centers for *novel* categories while maintaining known ones. For balanced datasets, it enforces uniform cluster size (C=N/K) using a stable matching algorithm: clusters exceeding size C retain only their C closest samples, releasing others as "cluster-free." These are then assigned to the nearest "sparse" clusters (those with fewer than C assignments). This process guarantees stability and balanced allocation.
 - **Final Assignment:** A traditional Semi-Supervised K-means is run with these refined centers, generating base-level pseudo-labels.
- **Hierarchical Semi-Supervised K-means (HSSK):** Building on BSSK, HSSK iteratively creates higher levels of abstraction. For each subsequent level (k), the prototypes of the (k-1)th level's known categories are clustered into half, and the same partitioning is applied to novel prototypes. Samples are reassigned to these new "hyperlabels." The process continues until the most abstract 'known' vs. 'novel' distinction is reached. This provides a multi-tiered pseudo-label hierarchy.

#### 3.3. Unsupervised Self-Expertise (L_USE)

This strategy refines the learning process by focusing on "hard negatives" within clusters, particularly important in early training stages when pseudo-labels are noisy.
- **Adjusted Target Matrix (Y):** Instead of treating all negative instances uniformly (as in standard contrastive learning where I_ij = 1(i=j)), SelEx recalibrates negativity based on semantic similarity using hierarchical pseudo-labels. The target matrix element y_ij is calculated as a sum across hierarchical levels: `y_ij = Σ_{k=1}^{lg K} 1(c_i^k ≠ c_j^k) / 2^k`. This means samples with identical pseudo-labels at higher hierarchical levels are treated as "harder" negatives (repelled more strongly), forcing finer distinctions. Samples with greater semantic distance across the hierarchy receive lower negativity weights.
- **Label Smoothing (α):** A hyperparameter α controls the mix: `Ŷ = αY + (1-α)I`. A higher α value encourages the model to pay more attention to nuanced details (hard negatives), while a lower α makes it more general.
- **Loss Function:** A binary cross-entropy loss `L_BCE(P, Ŷ)` is used, where P are the contrastive logits derived from the model.

#### 3.4. Supervised Self-Expertise (L_SSE)

This component leverages the hierarchical pseudo-labels as supervisory signals to accelerate learning and enhance generalization.
- **Hierarchical Loss:** The total supervised self-expertise loss (`L_SSE`) is a sum of supervised contrastive losses across hierarchical levels: `L_SSE = 0.5 * Σ_{k=0}^{lg K} L_s^k | D_k/2^k`.
- **Segmented Latent Dimension:** Crucially, for each hierarchy level `k`, only the *first D/(2^k)* segment of the embedding vector (D) is used for differentiating pseudo-labels. This forces the model to learn progressively finer-grained distinctions as it descends the hierarchy (i.e., uses more of the embedding vector). For example, abstract distinctions (like "seen" vs. "unseen" or "feline" vs. "canine") are learned by the initial, coarser segment of the embedding, leaving later segments to specialize in finer distinctions (like "cat" vs. "lion"). For `k=0` (ground truth labels), the full embedding is used.
- **Benefits:** This approach ensures accurate label assignment for known categories and generates informative pseudo-labels for novel ones, promoting faster cluster formation and better generalization.

#### 3.5. Overall Loss Function

The final self-expertise loss function `L_SE` combines the unsupervised and supervised components with a tunable hyperparameter λ:
`L_SE = (1 - λ)L_USE + λL_SSE`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of "SelEx" demonstrates its superior performance across various benchmarks, especially in fine-grained classification tasks, and provides insights into the contributions of its individual components.

#### 4.1. Performance on Fine-Grained Datasets

- **State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Performance:** SelEx consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods on four fine-grained datasets: CUB-200, FGVC-Aircraft, Stanford-Cars, and Oxford-IIIT Pet.
- **High Accuracy across Metrics:** For CUB-200 (DINOv2 backbone), SelEx achieves 87.4% overall, 85.1% known, and 88.5% novel accuracy, marking significant improvements. Similar trends are observed across all fine-grained datasets, with SelEx often being the best or second-best method.
- **Robustness to Small Datasets:** On the Oxford-IIIT Pet dataset, despite its small size and associated overfitting risks, SelEx demonstrates strong performance (95.6% overall, 96.5% known, 95.1% novel with DINOv2), highlighting its robustness.
- **Mechanism for Success:** The authors attribute this success to the model's hierarchical approach to category analysis, which is critical for differentiating closely related categories by paying acute attention to subtle details.

#### 4.2. Performance on Coarse-Grained Datasets

- **Competitive Performance:** SelEx also shows competitive performance on coarse-grained datasets such as CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-100, even though its design prioritizes fine-grained tasks.
- **Enhancement over Baselines:** It notably enhances performance over the traditional non-hierarchical GCD baseline, even in datasets where explicit hierarchical structures might be shallow or absent, indicating the generalizability of its self-expertise mechanisms.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Effect of Each Component:**
 - **Hierarchical Semi-Supervised K-means (HSSK):** This component yields the most significant improvements across both known and novel categories (from 46.1% to 62.6% all accuracy on CUB).
 - **Unsupervised Self-Expertise (L_USE):** Primarily enhances known categories (52.2% known accuracy alone) and, when integrated with HSSK, extends benefits to novel categories.
 - **Supervised Self-Expertise (L_SSE):** Particularly beneficial for novel categories (56.6% novel accuracy alone) and also positively contributes to known categories.
 - **Combination:** The integration of all three components (HSSK, L_USE, L_SSE) yields the most optimal results for both known and novel categories (73.6% all accuracy on CUB).
- **Effect of Hierarchy Levels:** Increasing the number of hierarchical levels generally improves model performance, especially for unknown categories. This is attributed to the increased granularity at finer levels, which allows the model to handle uncertainty and noise in pseudo-labels more effectively.
- **Effect of Smoothing Hyperparameter (α):** A higher α value (which strengthens the focus on hard negatives within a cluster) improves performance on novel categories. This is because it prevents indiscriminate repulsion of semantically similar samples outside the cluster, which is crucial for novel classes lacking ground-truth labels. Conversely, a higher α can slightly decrease performance on known categories. The optimal α depends on dataset granularity (higher α for fine-grained).
- **Effect of Supervised/Unsupervised Mixing Hyperparameter (λ):** Increasing λ (more emphasis on supervised self-expertise) generally enhances performance, particularly for novel categories. This is due to leveraging pseudo-labels for novel category supervision. Beyond λ=0.2, known category performance plateaus or slightly declines, possibly due to pseudo-label noise impacting known categories and reduced unsupervised self-expertise effectiveness.
- **Effect of Number of Frozen Blocks:** Freezing fewer blocks (allowing more fine-tuning) generally leads to better performance, especially for novel categories, by enabling greater model adaptability. Freezing 8 blocks appeared optimal for novel categories, while 10 blocks were better for known categories, indicating a trade-off.
- **Performance with Estimated Number of Categories:** SelEx maintains its superior performance even when the number of categories is estimated (e.g., using Vaze et al.'s method) rather than being precisely known. This demonstrates robustness in real-world scenarios where category counts are uncertain. The hierarchical nature of the model likely helps by allowing it to allocate additional categories to novel classes, mitigating biases towards known categories.
- **Robustness to Long-tailed Datasets:** By simply omitting the cluster size balancing component, SelEx can be applied to long-tailed datasets like Herbarium-19. It achieves respectable performance and shows enhancements over the baseline GCD method, suggesting its self-expertise mechanisms are beneficial even with non-uniform category distributions.
- **Time Complexity and Failure Cases:** The GPU-based K-means variant has a time complexity of O(T*K). Failure cases primarily involve distinguishing known categories with occlusion or reflection, and novel categories suffering from occlusion or camouflage, pointing to areas for future improvement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "SelEx: Self-Expertise in Fine-Grained Generalized Category Discovery" holds significant implications for the field of computer vision and machine learning, particularly in addressing complex, real-world classification challenges.

**1. Novel Concept of "Self-Expertise":** The introduction of "self-expertise" as a guiding principle for Generalized Category Discovery is a novel conceptual contribution. By explicitly modeling a system's ability to generalize across abstraction levels and discern subtle differences, the paper offers a new paradigm for thinking about robust category discovery, especially for fine-grained tasks. This moves beyond simply identifying known vs. unknown to truly understanding hierarchical relationships.

**2. State-of-the-Art Performance in Fine-Grained GCD:** Empirically, SelEx achieves superior performance on challenging fine-grained datasets (CUB-200, FGVC-Aircraft, Stanford-Cars, Oxford-IIIT Pet), outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods. This is a crucial advancement, as fine-grained distinctions are often critical in domains like biology, product classification, and medical imaging, where subtle visual cues differentiate categories.

**3. Robustness and Generalizability:** The method demonstrates robustness across various conditions:
 - **Varying Labeled Data Ratios:** SelEx performs well even with significantly reduced labeled samples for known categories, highlighting its efficiency and reliance on self-supervision.
 - **Estimated Category Counts:** Its strong performance when the number of categories is unknown is highly practical, as real-world scenarios rarely provide this information upfront.
 - **Long-tailed Distributions:** Adaptability to unbalanced, long-tailed datasets (like Herbarium-19) further broadens its potential applicability beyond idealized uniform distributions.
 - **Coarse-grained Datasets:** While optimized for fine-grained, its competitive performance on coarse-grained datasets shows its broader applicability.

**4. Theoretical Underpinnings:** The theoretical framework, derived from KL divergence analysis, provides a solid mathematical justification for the hierarchical approach and the reduction of the upper bound on estimation error. This grounds the empirical successes in a formal understanding of why the method works.

**5. Improved Contrastive Learning:** By proposing a refined contrastive learning strategy that leverages hierarchical pseudo-labels and hard negatives, SelEx addresses known limitations of standard contrastive learning in fine-grained settings. This contribution can potentially influence future developments in self-supervised learning for nuanced visual tasks.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Real-World Applications:** SelEx can significantly impact applications requiring robust classification in dynamic environments where novel categories constantly emerge, such as autonomous driving (recognizing new obstacles), surveillance (identifying new behaviors/objects), e-commerce (new product types), and scientific data analysis (new species or phenomena).
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The 'self-expertise' concept and the hierarchical semi-supervised framework open new avenues for research into how models can learn to reason about categories at different levels of abstraction. This could lead to more human-like cognitive abilities in AI systems.
- **Reduced Labeling Costs:** By effectively discovering novel categories and performing well with limited labeled data, SelEx could help reduce the immense human effort and cost associated with annotating large datasets.
