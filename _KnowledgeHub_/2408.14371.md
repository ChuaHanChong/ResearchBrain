# SelEx: Self-Expertise in Fine-Grained Generalized Category Discovery

> [!summary] Summary
> SelEx introduces a Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) method for fine-grained classification by fostering 'self-expertise' through hierarchical representation learning and refined contrastive strategies. The approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on fine-grained datasets, demonstrating 87.4% overall accuracy on CUB-200 and competitive results across other benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) methods often struggle with fine-grained distinctions, leading to fragmented clustering and high false negative rates.
- Traditional contrastive learning, a common foundation for GCD, faces limitations in distinguishing subtly different categories due to its general positive/negative pairing mechanism.
- The challenge lies in simultaneously classifying known categories and discovering novel ones with high accuracy, particularly when subtle visual differences are crucial.

## Method

- ==SelEx== develops =='self-expertise'== for fine-grained GCD using a combination of ==Hierarchical Pseudo-Label Extraction==, Unsupervised Self-Expertise, and Supervised Self-Expertise.
- A ==Hierarchical Semi-Supervised K-means (HSSK)== generates multi-level pseudo-labels to provide hierarchical supervisory signals.
- Unsupervised Self-Expertise (L_USE) refines contrastive learning by emphasizing hierarchical 'hard negatives', while Supervised Self-Expertise (L_SSE) leverages abstract pseudo-labels with ==segmented latent dimensions== to accelerate learning.

## Results

- SelEx consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance on fine-grained datasets including CUB-200 (**87.4%** overall accuracy), FGVC-Aircraft, Stanford-Cars, and Oxford-IIIT Pet, outperforming prior methods.
- The method demonstrates robustness to various conditions, including varying ratios of labeled data, estimated numbers of categories, and adaptability to long-tailed datasets.
- Ablation studies confirm the individual contribution of each component, with HSSK providing the most significant improvement, increasing overall accuracy on CUB-200 from **46.1%** to **62.6%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A theoretical framework based on KL divergence shows that a hierarchical approach reduces the upper bound on estimation error in both supervised and unsupervised contrastive learning, enabling finer granularity.
> - Segmenting the latent embedding dimension across hierarchical levels forces the model to learn progressively finer-grained distinctions, building 'self-expertise' from coarse to fine.
> - Refining negative sample treatment with hierarchical pseudo-labels and label smoothing allows the model to differentiate subtly similar instances more effectively without indiscriminately repelling semantically related samples.
