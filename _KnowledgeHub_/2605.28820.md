# From Pixels to Words -- Towards Native One-Vision Models at Scale

> [!summary] Summary
> NEO-ov, developed by S-Lab, NTU, and SenseTime Research, introduces a native, encoder-free vision-language model that unifies single-image, multi-image, and video understanding with spatial intelligence through an end-to-end autoregressive architecture. The model establishes new performance benchmarks for native VLMs, matching or surpassing leading modular counterparts on various reasoning and perception tasks, and showing strong spatial intelligence comparable to specialist models.

## Problem

- Modular Vision-Language Models (VLMs) face limitations in flexibility (handling heterogeneous visual inputs), efficiency (fragmented training, costly encoder extension), and scalability (complex balance between modules).
- Existing native VLMs largely focus on single-image understanding or specialized video tasks, often relying on distillation from static encoders, and lack a unified framework for multi-image, video, and spatial intelligence.
- The field lacked a truly unified "one-vision" foundation model with a monolithic backbone that learns directly from pixels and words, enabling deep, early pixel-word interactions.

## Method

- **NEO-ov** adopts a monolithic, encoder-free architecture that processes visual inputs via a lightweight convolutional embedding layer and text via an LLM tokenizer, with both token sequences jointly processed by a decoder-only ==Transformer== backbone.
- It employs a Native Rotary Position Embedding (Native-RoPE) with a T HW-decoupled design for attention heads and a unified spatial-temporal attention mechanism that enables dense bidirectional spatial interactions within each visual unit and causal interactions across units and text.
- A three-stage progressive training curriculum (Pre-Training, Mid-Training, Supervised Fine-Tuning) is utilized, incorporating diverse multimodal data at increasing resolutions and context lengths to scale multimodal understanding.

## Results

- NEO-ov (2B and 8B scales) established new performance benchmarks for native VLMs, consistently surpassing prior native architectures on general VQA, OCR, and reasoning-intensive tasks like MMMU, HallB, and InfoVQA.
- The model demonstrated competitiveness against leading modular VLMs (e.g., InternVL3.5, Qwen3-VL) on several reasoning, perception, multi-image, and video understanding benchmarks, matching or exceeding their performance on various tasks.
- NEO-ov exhibited strong spatial intelligence, achieving performance comparable to or better than dedicated spatial-specialist models and other general-purpose VLMs across a wide range of geometric reasoning and embodied understanding benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A fully native, encoder-free VLM architecture can achieve competitive or superior performance compared to modular VLMs, effectively handling diverse visual inputs and tasks without relying on pre-trained external visual encoders.
> - Deep, early pixel-pixel and pixel-word interactions within a unified backbone, facilitated by Native-RoPE and specialized attention masking, are crucial for robust fine-grained perception and strong spatial intelligence.
> - A progressive, multi-stage training curriculum with varied multimodal data is effective in scaling native VLMs to complex reasoning, high-resolution inputs, and long-context scenarios, demonstrating generalizability across heterogeneous tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has been characterized by two primary architectural paradigms: modular and native. Modular VLMs typically integrate pre-trained image or video encoders with large language models (LLMs) via lightweight adapters. This approach leverages the pre-existing capabilities of specialized encoders for visual representation and LLMs for language understanding and generation. Prominent examples include models like LLaVA, InternVL, and Qwen-VL, which have demonstrated robust performance across various image, multi-image, and video tasks. However, this modular design is noted to impose constraints related to flexibility, efficiency, and scalability. These constraints stem from the need to align distinct visual and language modules, potential fragmentation of pixel-level signals during the encoding process, and limitations in early pixel-word interactions. Visual encoders optimized for high-level semantics may discard fine-grained visual information crucial for spatial intelligence, while the encode-then-project paradigm can hinder deep vision-language integration.

In parallel, native VLMs have emerged as an alternative, aiming to learn directly from pixels and words within a unified, monolithic backbone, eliminating the need for separate pre-trained visual encoders. Early native models like Fuyu and EVE established the feasibility of this approach for single images. Subsequent research in native VLMs has explored techniques such as visual feature distillation, modality-agnostic embeddings, and modality-specific decomposition to improve performance and mitigate vision-linguistic interference. While some native VLMs have extended to video domains, these efforts have often been constrained by distillation from static visual encoders or remained focused on specific modalities, leaving the unification of single-image, multi-image, video understanding, and spatial intelligence within a single native framework largely unexplored. This paper positions itself as an advancement in native VLM research by introducing a model designed to address the aforementioned limitations and achieve a truly unified "one-vision" foundation model at scale.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop NEO-ov, a native vision-language foundation model that overcomes the inherent limitations of conventional modular VLMs and expands the capabilities of existing native VLMs. The motivation for this work is multifaceted:

1. **Address Limitations of Modular VLMs**:
 - **Flexibility**: Modular VLMs struggle to process heterogeneous visual inputs (single images, image sets, videos) uniformly. Image encoders prioritize static representations and lack spatiotemporal reasoning, while video encoders overemphasize temporal dynamics, generalizing poorly to static or interleaved inputs. Both types of encoders limit early pixel-word interactions and unified visual understanding.
 - **Efficiency**: The decoupling of vision and language modules leads to fragmented training and substantial post-alignment overhead. Extending visual encoders to long-duration or high-resolution inputs is computationally expensive, especially as KV caching is not applicable to visual tokens in the same way as language tokens.
 - **Scalability**: The modular design complicates scaling, optimization, and deployment by requiring a delicate balance of capacity between visual encoders and LLMs. This complexity obstructs structural simplicity and deep vision-language integration.

2. **Advance Native VLM Capabilities**:
 - Existing native VLMs, despite demonstrating feasibility, have largely focused on single-image understanding or specialized video tasks. They often rely on distillation from static visual encoders, which might inherit inductive biases that limit performance on fine-grained perception and spatial intelligence.
 - There is an identified gap in unifying single-image, multiple-image, video understanding, and spatial intelligence simultaneously within a truly "one-vision" foundation model using a native architecture.

3. **Introduce a Unified, Monolithic Backbone**:
 - The core motivation is to eliminate pre-trained external encoders, auxiliary adapters, and post-hoc fusion by developing a native foundation model that learns cross-frame and pixel-word correspondence end-to-end directly from raw inputs. This aims to enable fine-grained and unified spatiotemporal modeling to emerge natively within the model.

4. **Demonstrate Competitiveness and Feasibility at Scale**:
 - A key objective is to validate that a native "one-vision" architecture is not only feasible but also competitive with or superior to modular counterparts, particularly in fine-grained visual perception, multi-image reasoning, video comprehension, and spatial intelligence. The researchers aim to narrow the performance gap to modular models.

5. **Provide Architectural Analyses and Training Recipes**:
 - Beyond empirical performance, the paper intends to offer systematic architectural analyses and detailed training procedures to facilitate future research and development in native multimodal modeling.

In essence, the research is driven by the desire to move beyond the limitations of current VLM architectures by proposing a unified, encoder-free approach that can natively handle diverse visual inputs and interactions across space and time, demonstrating its efficacy at scale.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NEO-ov is presented as a native vision-language model designed for unified autoregressive modeling across single-image, multi-image, video understanding, and spatial intelligence. The methodology focuses on a monolithic, encoder-free architecture that integrates visual and textual inputs into a single sequence for end-to-end processing.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture and Input Processing

NEO-ov adopts a unified native vision-language backbone. Visual inputs (images or video frames) and text are encoded into token sequences.
- **Visual Encoding**: Images (I) are processed by a lightweight embedding layer consisting of two convolutional layers with a GELU activation function. The first convolution extracts patches with a stride of 16, and the second aggregates local features with a stride of 2, resulting in one visual token for each 32x32 image region. 2D Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE) are added to these visual tokens.
- **Textual Encoding**: Textual inputs (T) are tokenized using the original LLM tokenizer.
- **Unified Sequence**: Visual tokens are wrapped with `<img>` and `</img>` tags, concatenated with text tokens, and jointly processed by a decoder-only Transformer backbone. The Pre-Buffer and Post-LLM layers are initialized from the NEO model and Qwen3 LLM, respectively.

#### 3.2. Native Rotary Position Embedding (Native-RoPE)

NEO-ov employs an explicit T HW-decoupled design for attention heads and RoPE.
- **Attention Heads**: The original LLM's head dimension is preserved as the temporal component (T), while additional head dimensions are introduced for spatial components (H and W). This allows for both temporal modeling (inherited from the LLM) and dedicated spatial modeling. The correlation between query (Q) and key (K) features for tokens `i` and `j` is defined by summing the dot products of their respective T, H, and W components.
- **RoPE**: Native-RoPE models separate temporal and spatial indices. `idx_i = [t_i, h_i, w_i]`, where `t_i` is the temporal/sequential position, and `h_i`, `w_i` are spatial coordinates. Text tokens only have a temporal index (`h_i = w_i = 0`). Image tokens within the same image share the same temporal index but use `h_i` and `w_i` for spatial positions. Temporal indices are continuous across modalities, while spatial indices are defined independently within each visual unit. The RoPE base frequencies for T, H, and W are set to different values (`1e6`, `1e4`, `1e4` respectively) to cater to their distinct roles.

#### 3.3. Unified Visual Serialization

The model handles diverse visual inputs by serializing them into a unified sequence:
- **Single Image**: A single visual segment is inserted at the `<img>` position.
- **Multi-Image Inputs**: Each `<img>` token in the prompt is replaced by an independent visual segment, following the textual order. Each image can be encoded at an arbitrary resolution, allowing the number of visual tokens to adapt to its spatial size and preserve details at different granularities.
- **Video Inputs**: Videos are represented as temporally ordered sequences of sampled frames. `f` frames are sampled from the raw video, and each frame is serialized as an image unit with a prepended timestamp. A global prefix encoding video duration, frame count, and sampling rate can also be included.

#### 3.4. Unified Spatial-Temporal Attention

The attention mechanism is extended to handle coherent interactions across multiple visual units.
- **Attention Mask**: The attention mask `M_ij` is designed such that tokens `j` can attend to token `i` if `j` is less than or equal to `i` (causal dependency) OR if tokens `i` and `j` belong to the same visual unit (`u_i = u_j > 0`) (bidirectional interaction within a unit).
- **Properties**: This design enables dense bidirectional spatial interactions within each image or frame and causal interactions across different visual units and text. This allows patch-level interactions to emerge directly in early layers, refining cross-image comparison and temporal reasoning from shallow to deep layers.

#### 3.5. Training Procedure

The training of NEO-ov follows a three-stage progressive curriculum:

1. **Pre-Training Stage**:
 - **Objective**: Develop foundational visual perception and align visual representations with the LLM's semantic space.
 - **Data**: Approximately 20 million large-scale image-text pairs from diverse web sources (descriptive captions, OCR-intensive content).
 - **Optimization**: Parameters are restricted to patch embedding layers, pre-buffer layers, and newly introduced QK-related parameters to preserve LLM linguistic priors and ensure stable multimodal adaptation. An autoregressive next-token objective is used.

2. **Mid-Training Stage**:
 - **Objective**: Scale spatial-temporal reasoning and enhance perception over high-resolution visual content.
 - **Data**: Nearly 60 million multimodal samples, covering resolutions from 256x256 to 4096x4096 and videos up to 128 frames.
 - **Optimization**: All model layers are jointly optimized. Context length is progressively extended from 16K to 36K tokens. A unified mixture of text-only, image-text, multi-image, and video-text data (approximate ratio 2:4:1:1) is used for stability and generalization.

3. **Supervised Fine-Tuning Stage (SFT)**:
 - **Objective**: Refine the model using high-quality instruction-tuning data for enhanced multimodal understanding and cross-frame reasoning.
 - **Data**: Approximately 4 million single-image, 1 million multi-image, and 1 million video samples. Covers VQA, OCR, fine-grained perception, temporal reasoning, math analysis, and complex dialogue.
 - **Optimization**: The entire model is optimized end-to-end under next-token prediction objectives, strengthening fine-grained perception, long-context reasoning, and temporal dynamics modeling. Multi-resolution training up to 4096x4096 and videos up to 128 frames are supported.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

NEO-ov is trained on sixteen 8-GPU nodes. The AdamW optimizer is used with cosine learning-rate decay and a 0.01 warmup ratio. Peak learning rates vary across stages. Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-8B serve as language backbones. The pre-buffer module has 12 layers for the 2B model and 6 layers for the 9B model. Native RoPE base frequencies for T, H, and W are fixed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

NEO-ov's performance was evaluated across three main domains: image understanding, multi-image and video understanding, and spatial intelligence, utilizing the VLMEvalKit. The model was tested at both 2B and 8B parameter scales.

#### 4.1. Image Understanding

- **Comparison with Native VLMs**: NEO-ov established a performance benchmark for native VLMs across both 2B and 8B scales. It consistently surpassed prior native architectures, including NEO, EVE series, Mono-InternVL series, OneCAT, Emu3, and SAIL, on a range of general VQA and OCR benchmarks. Notable improvements were observed on reasoning-intensive and hallucination-sensitive benchmarks such as MMMU, HallB, and InfoVQA, indicating that end-to-end native modeling supports strong visual reasoning.
- **Comparison with Modular VLMs**: NEO-ov demonstrated competitiveness against prominent modular VLMs like InternVL3.5 and Qwen3-VL. Despite not using pre-trained visual encoders, NEO-ov matched or exceeded the performance of its modular counterparts on several reasoning and perception benchmarks, including complex reasoning and hallucination suppression. While OCR-intensive tasks remain a challenging area, the native architecture showed progress in narrowing the performance gap.

#### 4.2. Multi-Image and Video Understanding

- **Comparison with Native VLMs**: NEO-ov achieved gains compared to earlier native VLMs such as Fuyu, EVE, and ELVA on video understanding benchmarks like VideoMME, MVBench, and MLVU. These results highlight its temporal reasoning and long-context visual understanding capabilities at both 2B and 8B scales.
- **Comparison with Modular VLMs**: NEO-ov remained competitive with several modular VLMs, including VideoLLaMA3 and InternVL3.5, on benchmarks like BLINK, MUIRBENCH, LVBench, LongVideoBench, and VideoMMMU. This suggests that a unified native backbone can effectively support cross-image reasoning and temporal associations within a single autoregressive framework.

#### 4.3. Spatial Intelligence

- NEO-ov exhibited strong spatial intelligence across various benchmarks covering geometric reasoning, spatial perception, and embodied understanding.
- **Comparison with Spatial-Specialist Models**: As a general-purpose native VLM, NEO-ov achieved performance comparable to or better than dedicated spatial-specialist models such as Cambrian-S, Sensenova-SI, and GeoThinker at both 2B and 8B scales.
- **Comparison with General-Purpose VLMs**: NEO-ov showed advantages over other general VLMs on benchmarks like VSI-Bench, MMSI, Mindcube-tiny, ViewSpatial, SITE, 3DSR, EmbSpatial, SPAR, and Omni-Spatial, indicating its ability to capture fine-grained spatial and geometric representations.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Native Attention vs. Encoder-based Attention**: A comparison between the Pre-Buffer mechanism (native) and conventional visual encoders (randomly initialized) showed that Pre-Buffer consistently achieved competitive or superior performance across VQA, OCR, video understanding, and spatial intelligence tasks. This was particularly evident in OCR and spatial intelligence, where fine-grained visual structure and long-range spatial dependencies are critical. The results suggest that preserving richer intermediate visual context through native pixel-pixel and pixel-word interactions is more effective than relying on compressed representations.
- **Deep Interactions Benefit Spatial Intelligence**: NEO-ov demonstrated substantially larger performance gains from additional spatial intelligence (SI) supervision compared to encoder-based models like InternVL3.5 and Qwen3-VL. This was attributed to NEO-ov's native interaction pattern, where pixel-pixel and pixel-word interactions are formed early in the unified backbone, facilitating richer spatial and cross-modal representations.
- **Performance Improvements Across Stages**: The model's performance consistently improved from Stage 1 to Stage 2 of training for both 2B and 9B variants across all benchmark categories. This suggests that the progressive training regimen effectively strengthens general visual understanding and contributes to robust multimodal capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of NEO-ov represents an advancement in the field of Vision-Language Models by demonstrating the feasibility and competitiveness of a fully native, encoder-free "one-vision" architecture at scale.

#### 5.1. Architectural Paradigm Shift

The work challenges the established modular VLM paradigm by showing that stitching together separate visual encoders and language decoders is not a prerequisite for high performance. NEO-ov's monolithic backbone, which learns cross-frame and pixel-word correspondence end-to-end, suggests that deep vision-language integration can emerge natively within a single model. This could simplify VLM architectures, reducing the complexity associated with aligning disparate modules and managing pre-trained components.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Fine-Grained Perception and Reasoning

By eliminating the compression of visual signals by external encoders, NEO-ov is designed to retain richer pixel-level information. This directly contributes to its strong performance in fine-grained visual perception, spatial reasoning, and complex reasoning tasks, where subtle visual details and precise positional relationships are crucial. Its effectiveness in spatial intelligence benchmarks, often matching or surpassing specialized models, indicates a robust capability in understanding geometric structures and relationships.

#### 5.3. Unified Spatiotemporal Understanding

NEO-ov unifies single-image, multi-image, video understanding, and spatial intelligence within a single framework. This integrated approach, enabled by unified visual serialization and spatial-temporal attention, allows the model to naturally handle diverse visual inputs and temporal dynamics. The ability to process multiple images and video frames cohesively within an autoregressive framework could lead to more versatile multimodal applications.

#### 5.4. Scalability and Generalization of Native Models

The results validate that native architectures are scalable and can achieve competitive performance against leading modular VLMs. The progressive three-stage training recipe, incorporating diverse data and increasing context lengths, is a key contribution to achieving this scalability and generalization across heterogeneous tasks. This opens avenues for developing truly general-purpose multimodal foundation models that are intrinsically multimodal from their foundational design.

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

The paper also outlines limitations that provide clear directions for future research. Bridging the performance gap with top-tier modular systems on certain single-image and video understanding benchmarks, particularly for complex reasoning and fine-grained visual-text alignment, points to the need for larger and higher-quality multimodal training data. Furthermore, improving performance on OCR-intensive and document-centric tasks for native architectures is highlighted, suggesting the necessity of more diverse and specialized OCR-related supervision. Continued scaling in model capacity, data diversity, and long-context training is expected to unlock further reasoning and perception capabilities. The systematic architectural analyses and detailed training recipes provided are intended to facilitate these subsequent efforts in native multimodal modeling.
