# MultiBanana: A Challenging Benchmark for Multi-Reference Text-to-Image Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> MultiBanana is a new benchmark for multi-reference text-to-image generation models, designed to evaluate capabilities with up to 8 heterogeneous references under diverse and challenging conditions. It reveals that current state-of-the-art models exhibit performance degradation with increasing reference counts and struggle with compositional consistency and fidelity when reconciling varied inputs.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks for reference-based image generation typically focus on a limited number of references (1-4), hindering evaluation of models with larger sets of instructions.
- Current task definitions and evaluation frameworks do not adequately capture the complexities inherent in combining diverse and heterogeneous reference images.
- A lack of standardized evaluation under varied conditions, such as domain mismatches, scale differences, or rare concepts, prevents comprehensive assessment of model robustness.

## Method

- **MultiBanana**, a new benchmark, supports evaluation with up to ==8 reference images== and defines various task types, including single, two, and ==multi-reference compositional generation==.
- The benchmark incorporates explicit ==diversity factors==: cross-domain references, scale and viewpoint mismatches, rare concepts, and multilingual textual references.
- A ==four-stage construction pipeline== (Image Collection, Filtering, Category Classification, Task Construction) was used, combining real and synthetic data, filtered for suitability and categorized for task generation.

## Results

- State-of-the-art models showed **decreased performance** as the number of reference images increased, with closed-source models generally outperforming open-source counterparts.
- Tasks incorporating difficult reference combinations (cross-domain, scale/viewpoint mismatches, rare concepts, multilingual text) consistently resulted in **lower MultiBanana scores** across models.
- VLM-based judges (**GPT-5**, **Gemini 2.5**) demonstrated **strong correlations** with human ratings across various metrics, supporting their use as reliable proxies for efficient evaluation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Increased numbers of references lead to a general decline in model performance, with different failure modes observed across model types.
> - Closed-source models tend to prioritize strict adherence to all references, often resulting in degraded visual quality and compositional inconsistencies when constraints increase.
> - Open-source models frequently omit references under high-constraint conditions to maintain overall image coherence, demonstrating a trade-off between fidelity and visual cleanliness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in text-to-image (T2I) generation, particularly those leveraging large-scale multimodal Large Language Model (LLM) backbones, have enhanced models' abilities to follow complex instructions. A notable capability emerging from these developments is multi-reference image generation. This allows users to provide multiple reference images, from which a model can extract and inherit specific appearances of subjects, subsequently re-rendering them within new contexts while aiming to preserve their identity. This shift represents a move beyond single-reference conditioning, offering increased controllability in image generation, a feature with relevance in industrial sectors such as content production, advertising, and fashion design.

Despite these developments, the field has lacked a standardized benchmark capable of comprehensively evaluating how effectively T2I models adhere to instructions involving multiple references under varied and challenging conditions. Existing benchmarks for reference-based image generation typically focus on a limited number of references (often 1 to 4) and feature task definitions that do not adequately capture the complexities inherent in combining heterogeneous reference images. This limitation hinders the assessment of progress in model performance and the identification of weaknesses when processing a larger, more diverse set of references. The authors highlight an analogy to instruction following in LLMs, where performance often degrades as the number of constraints increases. This research addresses this identified gap by introducing a benchmark designed to evaluate multi-reference image generation capabilities in more complex and diverse scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce MultiBanana, a new benchmark specifically designed to assess the capabilities and limitations of text-to-image generation models in multi-reference settings. The motivation stems from several limitations observed in existing benchmarks:

- **Insufficient Coverage of Reference Quantity**: Previous benchmarks generally restrict the number of reference images to a narrow range (typically 1-4), which is inadequate for evaluating how models perform when following instructions with a larger number of references. MultiBanana aims to expand this range significantly, supporting up to 8 reference images.
- **Vague Task Definitions**: Existing evaluation frameworks often employ task definitions that are limited to general axes, such as "what to edit" or "how many references are given." These definitions do not adequately capture the specific challenges associated with combining diverse and heterogeneous reference inputs.
- **Lack of Diverse Reference Conditions**: There is a recognized need to evaluate models under conditions that reflect real-world complexities and potential conflicts among references. MultiBanana introduces several orthogonal axes of difficulty to address this:
 - **Varying Number of References**: Systematically evaluates performance as the number of references increases, up to 8.
 - **Domain Mismatch**: Incorporates references from different visual domains, such as real photographs, anime, computer graphics (CG), or sketches, requiring models to reconcile distinct stylistic attributes.
 - **Scale and Viewpoint Mismatches**: Includes scenarios where the scale or camera perspective of objects differs between reference images and the intended target scene.
 - **Rare Concepts**: Features references depicting uncommon or atypical concepts (e.g., an object with an unusual color or a fictional creature), testing the model's ability to generate novel interpretations.
 - **Multilingual Textual References**: Assesses the model's proficiency in rendering text in various languages (English, Chinese, and Japanese) based on provided references.

By addressing these limitations, the authors aim to establish a more robust and comprehensive evaluation framework. This framework is intended to reveal current model performances, identify common failure modes, and highlight specific areas requiring further improvement. The release of MultiBanana as an open benchmark is intended to foster fair comparison and accelerate research progress in multi-reference image generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The construction of MultiBanana followed a four-stage pipeline: Image Collection, Image Filtering, Category Classification, and Task Construction. The benchmark's design extends previous methodologies by increasing the number of references and incorporating diversity factors intrinsic to the reference set.

**Task Types:**
MultiBanana defines tasks across different reference counts:
- **Single Reference**: This involves a single reference image and a text instruction for image editing, similar to existing benchmarks.
- **Two References (11 task types)**: Tasks include subject addition, subject replacement, background change, color, material, pose, hairstyle, and makeup modification, tone transformation, style transfer, and text correction. These types cover both local and global image manipulations.
- **Multi References (3-8 references, 24 tasks)**: These tasks focus on compositional generation and inter-reference relationships. Four base structures are defined:
 - "X Objects": Composing X objects from the reference set.
 - "X-1 Objects + Background": Placing X-1 objects within the background of another reference image.
 - "X-1 Objects + Local": Applying a local change (specified by a reference) to one of the X-1 objects.
 - "X-1 Objects + Global": Arranging X-1 objects and modifying the overall tone according to another reference.

A central aspect of MultiBanana is the explicit incorporation of **diversity factors**: cross-domain references (e.g., mixing photos and anime), scale and viewpoint differences, rare concepts, and multilingual text rendering.

**Construction Pipeline:**

1. **Image Collection**:
 - **Real Data**: Sourced from the LAION-5B dataset, selecting images with an Aesthetic score above 6.25 and a resolution greater than 512 pixels.
 - **Synthetic Data**: To counteract a distributional bias in real images (e.g., underrepresentation of humans and objects), additional synthetic images were generated using Nano Banana and GPT-Image-1. These synthetic images were designed to provide diverse conditions for humans, animals, objects, and text-containing images across various scenes.

2. **Image Filtering**:
 - Images were filtered to ensure suitability for editing tasks. This involved using YOLOv12 for object detection and SAM2 for semantic segmentation. Images were discarded if the object bounding box area was less than 2% of the image, the segmented region within the bounding box was less than 30%, or the CLIP similarity between the predicted class and region was below 20.
 - Inappropriate or harmful content (e.g., hate, violence, sexual content) was removed through automated filtering using Gemini and subsequent manual inspection, accounting for approximately 3% of the collected images.

3. **Category Classification**:
 - Each reference image was categorized hierarchically to facilitate task construction. Automated multi-level labels were generated using Gemini and verified by human annotators.
 - Six top-level categories were defined: person, object, background, light, tone, and style. These were further subdivided (e.g., 'male,' 'female,' 'hairstyle' under 'person'; 'animal,' 'clothing design' under 'object').

4. **Task Construction**:
 - Editing instructions were generated by Gemini. Manual selection of suitable reference categories and random sampling of images were performed.
 - Gemini was tasked with generating instructions and then evaluating them for potential visual breakdowns (e.g., an object appearing unnaturally suspended). Nonsensical or overly trivial tasks were further filtered by Gemini and human annotators.
 - Difficult reference categories (cross-domain, different scale and viewpoint, rare concept, multilingual) were explicitly assigned based on the content of the sampled images.

**Statistics in MultiBanana:**
The benchmark comprises 3,769 high-quality reference image and instruction sets: 264 single-reference, 907 two-reference, and 2,598 multi-reference tasks. Multi-reference tasks are evenly distributed across 3 to 8 references, with each task type (X Objects, X-1 Objects + Background, etc.) containing at least 70 sets. The difficult reference combinations also include a substantial number of samples, such as 1,063 for cross-domain and 1,357 for different scale and viewpoint tasks.

**Evaluation Setting:**
To assess the quality of generated images, the researchers utilized AI feedback from Vision-Language Models (VLMs), specifically Gemini 2.5 and GPT-5, as a proxy for human evaluation, citing their alignment with human judgments and cost-efficiency. Generated images were evaluated against five criteria on a 10-point scale:
1. **Text-Instruction Alignment**: Measures how accurately the generated image follows the textual instruction.
2. **Reference Consistency**: Assesses how faithfully visual identity, structure, and attributes from reference images are reproduced.
3. **Background-Subject Match**: Evaluates the natural blending and visual coherence between subjects and backgrounds.
4. **Physical Realism**: Determines if the image adheres to basic physical laws and appears plausible.
5. **Visual Quality**: Assesses the overall aesthetic appeal and coherence of the image.

A total weighted score was computed, assigning weights of 3 to Instruction Alignment and Reference Consistency, and 1 to Background-Subject Match, Physical Realism, and Visual Quality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of state-of-the-art closed-source (Nano Banana, GPT-Image-1) and open-source (Qwen-Image-Edit-2509, DreamOmni2, OmniGen2) models on MultiBanana yielded several findings:

**Per-Task Evaluation:**
- Both open-source and closed-source models demonstrated lower performance on background replacement tasks, irrespective of the number of reference images.
- Closed-source models generally exhibited higher performance compared to open-source models, even in simpler single- and two-reference editing scenarios.

**Effect of the Number of References:**
- A general trend of decreasing total scores was observed across all models as the number of reference images increased. However, the nature of this degradation varied between model families.
- **Closed-source models** (e.g., Nano Banana, GPT-Image-1) maintained stronger adherence to reference attributes and prompt instructions, showing a relatively mild decline in Instruction Alignment and Reference Consistency. However, their attempts to strictly satisfy all constraints with an increasing number of subjects often led to degraded overall visual quality, including overcrowded scenes and compositional inconsistencies.
- **Open-source models** (e.g., Qwen-Image-Edit-2509, DreamOmni2) demonstrated weaker adherence to prompts and references. Their Instruction Alignment and Reference Consistency scores were lower, even with fewer references, and deteriorated further with more. These models frequently omitted some reference subjects under high-reference conditions. Conversely, their overall image quality (Background-Subject Match, Physical Realism, Visual Quality) remained more stable, with fewer severe compositional failures observed. This indicates a trade-off between strict reference fidelity and holistic image coherence. Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 showed a notable performance drop when exceeding three references, suggesting a potential training limitation.

**Analysis of Difficult Reference Combinations:**
- Tasks incorporating cross-domain references, different scale and viewpoint references, rare concepts, and multilingual text references consistently resulted in lower MultiBanana scores across most models compared to tasks without these conditions.
 - **Cross-domain diversity**: Led to lower Reference Consistency scores, as models tended to distort individual reference attributes in an attempt to achieve a consistent style.
 - **Scale and viewpoint differences**: Consistently reduced Reference Consistency, indicating difficulty in preserving fine details or adjusting poses for objects at varied scales or perspectives.
 - **Rare concept references**: Resulted in lower Physical Realism scores, as models struggled to flexibly control attributes like size for uncommon subjects, often reproducing them at an inappropriate scale.
 - **Multilingual references**: Caused a general decline across most metrics (except Reference Consistency for some models), primarily due to limited text-rendering capabilities in non-English languages, impacting instruction alignment and visual quality. GPT-Image-1 exhibited a comparatively smaller decline in this area.

**Reliability of VLM Judges:**
- Evaluation of VLM-based judges (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5, and Qwen3-VL) against human ratings on a subset of the benchmark demonstrated strong correlations across various metrics (Pearson r, Spearman r, Cohen's κ). This supports the use of VLMs as reliable and efficient proxies for human evaluation. Qwen3-VL was noted as a reliable open-source alternative.

**Agentic Framework:**
- Experiments with an Iterative Prompt Refinement (IPR) agentic framework showed that GPT-Image-1's performance improved. However, Nano Banana did not show consistent improvements; in some cases, its performance degraded, which the authors attributed to information loss from the initial instruction during refinement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MultiBanana benchmark addresses a critical gap in the evaluation of modern text-to-image generation models. Its main significance and potential impact are:

- **Comprehensive Evaluation Standard**: MultiBanana provides a standardized and open benchmark for multi-reference image generation, moving beyond the limitations of single or limited-reference evaluations. This enables a more thorough assessment of model capabilities in complex scenarios.
- **Identification of Model Limitations**: The benchmark effectively uncovers specific failure modes and challenges that state-of-the-art models currently face. These include difficulties in handling a high number of heterogeneous references, reconciling diverse visual domains, adapting to scale and viewpoint differences, generating rare concepts, and rendering multilingual text.
- **Guidance for Future Research**: By detailing distinct failure patterns—such as closed-source models' tendency towards compositional distortion due to strict adherence versus open-source models' tendency to omit references while maintaining visual cleanliness—MultiBanana provides actionable insights. These insights can guide researchers in developing more robust and controllable T2I models that balance instruction fidelity with overall image coherence.
- **Accelerated Progress**: As an open-source resource, MultiBanana can facilitate fair comparisons among different models and methodologies. This standardized evaluation platform is intended to accelerate research and development in the field of multi-reference image generation.
- **Enhanced Real-World Applications**: The benchmark's focus on complex, heterogeneous reference combinations directly aligns with the demands of industrial applications in content creation, advertising, and design. By pushing models to perform in these challenging settings, MultiBanana contributes to the development of more practical and capable T2I tools for real-world use cases.
