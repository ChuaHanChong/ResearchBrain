# From the Least to the Most: Building a Plug-and-Play Visual Reasoner via Data Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents a method for generating high-quality, multi-step visual reasoning data using a 'least-to-most' synthesis approach, primarily leveraging open-source models. The synthesized data then trains a plug-and-play visual reasoner, which consistently enhances the reasoning capabilities of existing Vision-Language Models across diverse visual question answering benchmarks.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with multi-step visual reasoning due to the scarcity of training data that explicitly outlines sequential reasoning paths (similar to Chain-of-Thought for LLMs).
- VLMs lack the ability to dynamically manipulate or focus on specific visual regions and objects during intermediate reasoning steps, hindering complex visual deduction.
- Existing methods for enhancing VLM reasoning often rely on expensive, proprietary Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) for top-down data generation, leading to issues with cost, reproducibility, and data quality.

## Method

- Introduces a =='Least-to-Most Visual Reasoning' paradigm== where a visual reasoner decomposes complex visual questions into simpler sub-questions and iteratively invokes specialized tools (Grounding, Highlight, OCR, Answer).
- Proposes a =='Least-to-Most Synthesis' pipeline==, a bottom-up data generation method that automatically constructs multi-step visual reasoning data using fine-tuned open-source LLMs.
- Constructs the large-scale ==VIREO dataset== (50K examples, with a 500K version released) and fine-tunes an ==LLaVA-1.5-7B model== on it to serve as a general-purpose, plug-and-play visual reasoner.

## Results

- The Reasoner consistently improved performance for four representative VLMs (BLIP-2, InstructBLIP, LLaVA) across various VQA benchmarks, with absolute gains up to **38.96%** on TallyQA-C.
- A human study confirmed the high quality of the synthesized VIREO dataset, with all **200** sampled examples rated as correct across sub-question fidelity, tool argument accuracy, and main question coherence.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that the removal of any specialized tool (OCR, Highlight, Grounding) negatively impacted performance, affirming the necessity of multi-tool integration for robust visual reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A bottom-up, open-source-reliant data synthesis approach can effectively generate high-quality, multi-step visual reasoning data (VIREO dataset), validated by human evaluation.
> - Complex visual reasoning tasks benefit significantly from the synergistic application of modular, specialized tools (Grounding, Highlight, OCR) integrated into a reasoning framework.
> - A fine-tuned visual reasoner can act as a 'plug-and-play' module, universally enhancing the reasoning capabilities of diverse existing Vision-Language Models without requiring their re-training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), multi-modal reasoning, and tool learning, a rapidly evolving area within artificial intelligence. The paper acknowledges the remarkable success of Large Language Models (LLMs) in NLP and the subsequent surge in Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) and VLMs, which extend LLMs to process visual information.

Current state-of-the-art VLMs have made significant strides in tasks like image captioning and Visual Question Answering (VQA). However, the authors identify a critical bottleneck: their inability to perform robust, multi-step visual reasoning. This limitation stems primarily from two factors:
1. **Data Scarcity:** Training datasets rarely contain explicit multi-step reasoning paths (e.g., question decomposition, intermediate visual operations, and sub-answers), unlike Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data available for LLMs. While some VQA datasets offer rationales, these are typically explanations, not actionable reasoning steps.
2. **Inability to Manipulate Visual Input:** Unlike text-based reasoning where LLMs can operate on textual tokens, VLMs struggle with dynamically manipulating or focusing on specific visual regions based on intermediate reasoning steps.

The broader research landscape has seen various attempts to address reasoning in LLMs, including CoT prompting (Wei et al., 2022b), least-to-most prompting (Zhou et al., 2022), and tool-invoking mechanisms (Schick et al., 2024; Qin et al., 2023b). While these techniques empower LLMs, their application to VLMs, especially regarding dynamic visual interaction and data scarcity, remains less explored. Recent work has begun investigating reasoning in multimodal models (Qi et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2023), often by prompting powerful proprietary LMMs like GPT-4V to generate reasoning steps. However, this "top-down" approach has limitations in terms of cost, reproducibility, and data quality due to the inherent struggles of even powerful models in complex reasoning (Wu et al., 2023).

Another related area is "visual programming" (Gupta and Kembhavi, 2023) or code generation for visual tasks (Surís et al., 2023; Subramanian et al., 2023), where LLMs break down tasks into executable code or subroutines. The authors distinguish their work from these by highlighting that such methods heavily rely on the strong instruction-following capabilities of very large LLMs and can be unstable. In contrast, this paper proposes a method that *fine-tunes* VLMs on a large-scale, synthesized dataset, making the enhanced reasoning capability inherent to the VLM itself, rather than relying on external, potentially brittle, programmatic execution. This approach also allows for the use of smaller, more cost-effective open-source models.

In summary, this work provides a novel solution to a critical gap in VLM capabilities by proposing a *bottom-up data synthesis approach* that generates high-quality, reproducible multi-step visual reasoning data. This synthesized data then enables the creation of a "plug-and-play" visual reasoner that can universally enhance the reasoning abilities of existing VLMs, thus pushing the boundaries of what current VLMs can achieve in complex visual reasoning tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research is to overcome the limitations of current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in performing multi-step reasoning over visual content. Despite their advancements in various vision-language tasks, state-of-the-art VLMs often struggle with questions that require sequential logical deductions, intermediate visual processing, or dynamic interaction with the image.

The core problem identified by the authors is two-fold:
1. **Scarcity of Multi-step Reasoning Data:** Unlike textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data for LLMs, detailed multi-step reasoning paths that interleave visual and language processing are rarely available in existing VLM training datasets. This deprives VLMs of the necessary examples to learn complex reasoning processes.
2. **Lack of Dynamic Visual Manipulation:** Current VLMs are typically not designed to dynamically interact with and manipulate images (e.g., marking specific areas, cropping, or deriving next steps from visual intermediate results) in the way a human might during a reasoning process.

Given these challenges, the **key objectives** of this paper are:

- **Introduce a "Least-to-Most Visual Reasoning" Paradigm:** To provide a structured approach for VLMs to decompose complex visual questions into simpler sub-questions and leverage external tools to resolve them step-by-step. This paradigm aims to emulate human-like problem-solving strategies.
- **Propose a Novel Data Synthesis Approach ("Least-to-Most Synthesis"):** To automatically generate high-quality, multi-step visual reasoning data. This approach is designed to be:
 - **Bottom-up:** Starting from an image and progressively constructing reasoning paths and questions, rather than attempting to prompt a powerful (and often proprietary) VLM to generate them from a question, which can lead to quality issues and high costs.
 - **Reproducible and Cost-Efficient:** Primarily relying on open-sourced models and atomic sub-tasks to ensure the entire synthesis process can be replicated cheaply and consistently.
 - **Quality-Guaranteed:** By breaking down the complex synthesis into simpler, manageable sub-tasks for which open-source models are reliable.
- **Construct a Large-Scale Visual Reasoning Dataset (VIREO):** To create a substantial dataset (50,000 examples, with a larger 500,000 example version released) using the proposed synthesis method, specifically designed to train visual reasoners.
- **Develop a Plug-and-Play Visual Reasoner:** To fine-tune an existing VLM (LLaVA-1.5-7B) on the VIREO dataset, enabling it to function as a visual reasoner that can be "plugged into" and enhance the reasoning capabilities of a wide range of existing VLMs without requiring modifications to the base models themselves.

Ultimately, the motivation is to bridge the gap between current VLM capabilities and the demands of truly complex visual reasoning, making VLMs more versatile, robust, and applicable to real-world tasks requiring multi-step logical inference and visual interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper introduces a two-pronged approach to enhance multi-step visual reasoning in VLMs: first, a "least-to-most visual reasoning" paradigm that formalizes the reasoning process, and second, a "least-to-most synthesis" data generation technique to create the necessary training data for this paradigm.

#### 3.1. Least-to-Most Visual Reasoning Paradigm

This paradigm guides a visual reasoner ($M_R$) to solve complex visual problems by breaking them down into a series of sub-questions and invoking external tools.
- **Reasoning Process:** Given an image ($I$) and a question ($Q$), the visual reasoner $M_R$ iteratively performs steps. At each step $k$, $M_R$ proposes a sub-question $q_k$ and selects a tool $t_k$ from a pre-defined pool based on the current image $I_k$ and previous steps. The chosen tool $t_k$ then produces an answer $r_k$, which might also modify the image to $I_{k+1}$ (e.g., by marking an area). This process continues until $t_k$ refers to the VLM $M$ (Answer tool) to provide the final answer.
- **Tool Pool:** The authors define four specialized tools, chosen to mimic human visual reasoning actions:
 - **Grounding:** Uses GroundingDino to frame a specific area corresponding to a target entity in the image.
 - **Highlight:** Uses GroundingDino to highlight all instances of a target entity in the image.
 - **OCR:** Employs PaddleOCR to recognize textual content within a specified image region.
 - **Answer:** Invokes an off-the-shelf VLM ($M$) to answer a question based on an image (potentially with recognized characters). This is the tool that ultimately provides the final answer to a sub-question or the main question.
- **Plug-and-Play Mechanism:** By varying the VLM ($M$) used for the "Answer" tool, the reasoner can adapt to different existing VLMs, acting as a wrapper that enhances their reasoning capabilities without needing to retrain the core VLM.

#### 3.2. Least-to-Most Synthesis for Data Generation

To address the data scarcity for multi-step visual reasoning, the authors propose a novel "bottom-up" data synthesis pipeline, contrasting it with common "top-down" approaches that rely on expensive and potentially unreliable proprietary LLMs like GPT-4V. The process comprises four main steps:

1. **Entity Recognition:** Initially, Deformable DETR is used to identify and localize various entities within an input image (from COCO2014 dataset). Entities with low confidence scores are discarded.
2. **Node Construction:** Based on the recognized entities, three types of "nodes" are constructed. Each node consists of an image segment and a textual profile describing it. The conversion of visual information to text in profiles is crucial for allowing advanced LLMs to process reasoning steps.
 - **Single-Entity Node:** Focuses on a single recognized entity. Its profile contains fine-grained attributes like Label, Location, Color (using ColorThief), Text (using PaddleOCR), and Size.
 - **Entity-Group Node:** Represents an aggregation of multiple spatially close entities. Its profile is a caption generated by BLIP, describing inter-entity relations.
 - **Whole-Image Node:** Corresponds to the entire image. Its profile is a detailed, comprehensive caption generated by LLaVA.
3. **Reasoning Process Synthesis:** A chain of $M$ nodes ($N_1, \dots, N_M$) is sampled, with rules to ensure logical connections and that the final node $N_M$ is a whole-image node. For each adjacent pair of nodes ($N_m, N_{m+1}$), an LLM (termed "Questioner," fine-tuned LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct) is used to synthesize a sub-question ($q_m$) about $N_m$ conditioned on $N_{m+1}$ and a sampled tool ($t_m$). This process generates the sequence of sub-questions and tool arguments that form the multi-step reasoning path.
4. **Question Synthesis:** A final LLM (termed "Combiner," also fine-tuned LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct) recursively combines the generated sub-questions to form the overarching main question ($Q$) for the entire reasoning process.

Both the "Questioner" and "Combiner" LLMs are fine-tuned using a limited (10k examples each) set of high-quality training data generated by querying GPT-4 with a few seed demonstrations, following a self-instruct pipeline. This approach leverages the generative power of large proprietary models minimally for data generation, focusing instead on fine-tuning open-source LLMs for specific sub-tasks.

#### 3.3. Visual Reasoner Development

Using the "least-to-most synthesis" approach, the authors construct the **VIsual REasOning dataset (VIREO)** with 50,000 examples. An LLaVA-1.5-7B model is then instruction-tuned on VIREO to serve as the "Reasoner." The Reasoner's training objective is to predict the appropriate sub-question ($q_k$) and tool ($t_k$) to invoke, given the current image, the main question, and the history of previous sub-questions.

This methodology emphasizes modularity and leverages the strengths of different AI models (object detectors, OCR, captioners, LLMs) in a systematic pipeline to create high-quality, synthetic multi-step reasoning data, which then trains a general-purpose visual reasoner.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate the proposed method demonstrate significant and consistent improvements across various VLMs and VQA benchmarks.

- **Consistent Performance Enhancement:** The Reasoner consistently improved the performance of all four representative VLMs tested (BLIP-2 2.7B, InstructBLIP 7B, InstructBLIP 13B, and LLaVA 13B) across all four VQA benchmarks (GQA, TextVQA, ST-VQA, TallyQA-S, TallyQA-C). The absolute performance gains ranged from 0.71% (BLIP2 on ST-VQA) to 38.96% (LLaVA on TallyQA-C). This highlights the Reasoner's strong generalization and "plug-and-play" capability.
- **Improved Complex Relational Reasoning (GQA):** The Reasoner brought clear improvements on the GQA dataset, which focuses on diverse inter-entity relations. Notably, stronger VLMs like LLaVA saw more substantial gains (3.63%) compared to weaker ones (1.17%-1.95%), suggesting that the Reasoner allows more capable VLMs to better leverage their potential by simplifying the task of locating target entities.
- **Enhanced Text Understanding in Images (TextVQA, ST-VQA):** The integration of the OCR tool significantly boosted performance on TextVQA and ST-VQA, which require reading text from images. Weaker VLMs showed more pronounced improvements, indicating that the Reasoner effectively addresses a core limitation in their OCR capabilities.
- **Substantial Counting Improvement (TallyQA):** The most significant performance gains were observed on the TallyQA dataset, especially its complex subset (TallyQA-C), with improvements up to 38.96%. This is attributed to the "Highlight" tool, which allows the Reasoner to focus on relevant entities and mitigate interference from irrelevant visual information, leading to more accurate counting.
- **High Quality of Synthesized Data (VIREO):** A human study involving 200 randomly sampled examples from the VIREO dataset confirmed its high quality. Three NLP graduate students consistently rated all examples as correct across three aspects: sub-question fidelity, tool argument accuracy, and main question coherence. This finding strongly validates the effectiveness of the bottom-up, open-source-reliant data synthesis approach.
- **Influence of Data Size:** While larger VIREO dataset sizes led to greater performance gains, the marginal benefit diminished, suggesting that future work might need to explore more diverse data synthesis methods to continue breaking performance ceilings.
- **Ablation Studies on Tools:** Removing any of the integrated tools (OCR, Highlight, Grounding) negatively impacted performance across various tasks. This confirms that complex visual tasks often require a synergistic application of multiple specialized operations, underscoring the value of the Reasoner's modular tool integration.
- **Impact on Broader Visual Tasks:** Experiments on MMMU (multi-discipline knowledge) and POPE (hallucination detection) indicated that the Reasoner has a minimal or negligible impact on these broader tasks. This suggests that the Reasoner primarily enhances multi-step reasoning without harming the VLM's inherent knowledge or increasing hallucinations.
- **Efficacy on Advanced VLMs:** When converting VIREO to an end-to-end format for fine-tuning a more advanced VLM like Qwen-VL, significant improvements were still observed across all datasets without a noticeable increase in inference time. This indicates the general applicability of the reasoning data itself.
- **Error Analysis:** The majority of errors (42%) stemmed from "Inference" (the underlying VLM's inability to provide a correct or relevant answer), followed closely by "Reasoning" errors (39%), primarily due to the Reasoner generating incorrect arguments for tools. "Execution" errors (errors by the specialized tools like GroundingDino or PaddleOCR) were less frequent, highlighting the reliability of these underlying visual tools. This analysis points to critical areas for future VLM development: improving instruction-following and tool-invoking capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of Vision-Language Models, particularly in addressing the critical challenge of multi-step visual reasoning. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Democratization of Complex Visual Reasoning:** By proposing a reproducible and cost-efficient "least-to-most synthesis" method using predominantly open-source models, the paper lowers the barrier for generating high-quality, multi-step visual reasoning data. This contrasts sharply with expensive and often non-reproducible methods relying on proprietary large models, democratizing access to crucial training data for advanced VLM capabilities.
- **Bridging the Gap in VLM Capabilities:** The "plug-and-play" visual reasoner effectively enhances the reasoning abilities of a wide range of *existing* VLMs. This is a practical and impactful contribution, as it allows current VLM users and developers to unlock more complex reasoning without needing to retrain massive foundation models from scratch. It extends the utility and applicability of current VLM architectures.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** The consistent and significant performance improvements across diverse VQA benchmarks (e.g., GQA for relational reasoning, TextVQA/ST-VQA for OCR, TallyQA for counting) highlight the robustness and broad generalization ability of the proposed reasoner. This indicates its potential for real-world applications requiring nuanced visual understanding.
- **Modular and Interpretable Reasoning:** The explicit decomposition of complex questions into sub-questions and the invocation of specialized tools (Grounding, OCR, Highlight, Answer) align with human cognitive processes. This modularity can lead to more interpretable reasoning paths, which is crucial for debugging and building trust in AI systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper opens several avenues for future work:
 - **Data Synthesis:** Exploring more diverse data synthesis approaches to push beyond diminishing returns from current methods.
 - **VLM Tool-Invoking:** Improving the underlying VLMs' capability to invoke tools more accurately and follow complex instructions.
 - **Integration with Advanced VLMs:** Further investigation into how to best integrate explicit reasoning mechanisms with the rapidly evolving, more powerful VLMs, especially those with inherent multi-modal understanding capabilities.
 - **Beyond VQA:** Expanding the application of this multi-step reasoning paradigm to other complex visual tasks beyond standard VQA benchmarks.
- **Practical Resource for the Community:** The release of the code and the VIREO dataset (with a larger 500k version available) is a direct, tangible contribution that will enable other researchers and practitioners to build upon this work, fostering further advancements in visual reasoning.
- **Efficiency and Accessibility:** Achieving competitive performance with relatively smaller, open-source language models (7B or 13B parameters) as the Reasoner makes the approach more computationally efficient and accessible for broader deployment, even for those with limited computational resources.

In conclusion, this research not only presents a robust solution to a pressing challenge in VLM development but also provides a sustainable and accessible framework for fostering future innovation in multi-step visual reasoning, ultimately pushing AI closer to human-like comprehensive understanding of multimodal information.
