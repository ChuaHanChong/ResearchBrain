# Adding simple structure at inference improves Vision-Language Compositionality

> [!summary] Summary
> An inference-time approach enhances the Vision-Language Compositionality of dual-encoder models like CLIP and SigLIP 2 by decomposing images into crops and text into segments, then matching them. This method consistently improves retrieval performance across datasets, achieving gains such as over 22 absolute points on BIVLC and up to 90 points on controlled compositional benchmarks without additional training.

## Problem

- Dual-encoder Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP exhibit "bag-of-words-like" behavior, failing to understand Vision-Language Compositionality.
- These models struggle with attribute-object binding and complex relational understanding in image-text retrieval tasks.
- Current approaches to improve VLM compositionality often involve expensive end-to-end training or are not specifically designed for retrieval.

## Method

- A novel ==inference-time approach== that processes input images by generating ==diverse smaller crops== and texts by segmenting them into hierarchical object-attribute-relation units.
- Utilizes the pretrained dual-encoder VLM to compute ==fine-grained similarities between each image crop and text segment==, creating best matching pairs.
- Aggregates these fine-grained match similarities by averaging them to determine the final image-text alignment score.

## Results

- The inference-time approach consistently and significantly improved retrieval performance for CLIP and SigLIP 2 across all evaluation datasets without additional training.
- SigLIP 2-Giant with the approach achieved over **22 absolute points** improvement in group score on BIVLC and up to **90 absolute points** on controlled BISCOR-CTRL benchmarks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that decomposing images into crops is essential for performance gains, while text-only segmentation provided minimal benefit.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pretrained dual-encoder Vision-Language Models contain latent compositional representations that are not fully leveraged by standard global embedding comparisons.
> - Enhancing compositional reasoning can be achieved through inference-time modifications, challenging the reliance on extensive retraining.
> - Decomposing images into smaller crops is crucial for improving compositional understanding, as confirmed by ablation studies.
