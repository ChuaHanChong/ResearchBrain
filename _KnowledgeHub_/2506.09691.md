# Adding simple structure at inference improves Vision-Language Compositionality

> [!summary] Summary
> An inference-time approach enhances the Vision-Language Compositionality of dual-encoder models like CLIP and SigLIP 2 by decomposing images into crops and text into segments, then matching them. This method consistently improves retrieval performance across datasets, achieving gains such as over 22 absolute points on BIVLC and up to 90 points on controlled compositional benchmarks without additional training.

## Problem

- Dual-encoder Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP exhibit "bag-of-words-like" behavior, failing to understand Vision-Language Compositionality.
- These models struggle with attribute-object binding and complex relational understanding in image-text retrieval tasks.
- Current approaches to improve VLM compositionality often involve expensive end-to-end training or are not specifically designed for retrieval.

## Method

- A novel ==inference-time approach== that processes input images by generating ==diverse smaller crops== and texts by segmenting them into hierarchical object-attribute-relation units.
- Utilizes the pretrained dual-encoder VLM to compute ==fine-grained similarities between each image crop and text segment==, creating best matching pairs.
- Aggregates these fine-grained match similarities by averaging them to determine the final image-text alignment score.

## Results

- The inference-time approach consistently and significantly improved retrieval performance for CLIP and SigLIP 2 across all evaluation datasets without additional training.
- SigLIP 2-Giant with the approach achieved over **22 absolute points** improvement in group score on BIVLC and up to **90 absolute points** on controlled BISCOR-CTRL benchmarks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that decomposing images into crops is essential for performance gains, while text-only segmentation provided minimal benefit.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pretrained dual-encoder Vision-Language Models contain latent compositional representations that are not fully leveraged by standard global embedding comparisons.
> - Enhancing compositional reasoning can be achieved through inference-time modifications, challenging the reliance on extensive retraining.
> - Decomposing images into smaller crops is crucial for improving compositional understanding, as confirmed by ablation studies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical limitation of modern dual-encoder Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP: their struggle with **Vision-Language Compositionality**. Despite achieving impressive performance in general image-text retrieval tasks, these models often exhibit "bag-of-words-like" behavior. This means they can identify individual elements (e.g., "black cat," "white dog") but fail to correctly bind attributes to objects or understand complex relationships when elements are rearranged (e.g., distinguishing "a black cat and a white dog" from "a white cat and a black dog"). This limitation significantly hampers their ability to grasp the nuanced meaning of visual scenes described by text.

The broader research landscape for improving VLM compositionality is largely dominated by two paradigms:
1. **End-to-end training approaches:** These methods typically involve incorporating fine-grained alignments between text and images during training (e.g., X-VLM, PyramidCLIP, FILIP, FineCLIP) or using hard-negative examples in contrastive learning (e.g., NegCLIP, GNM, TripletCLIP, CLIPTROHN-Img). While showing good results on benchmarks, a significant concern with these approaches, as highlighted by Lewis et al., is their potential struggle to generalize to out-of-distribution compositions, questioning whether they truly learn *more compositional* representations or merely adapt to specific training data.
2. **Inference-time approaches:** This area has received less attention, with most existing methods primarily focused on evaluating text-to-image generation rather than improving core VLM compositionality for retrieval. These often involve generating questions conditioned on captions and using VQA systems to score images (e.g., Hu et al., CECE, GECKO, DSG).

This paper uniquely positions itself by exploring **inference-time techniques** to enhance VL compositionality for image-text retrieval. It departs from previous inference-time methods by:
- Generating image crops and aligning them to text segments, rather than relying solely on the entire image.
- Utilizing the dual-encoder VLM itself to compute fine-grained similarities between crops and segments, instead of an external VQA system.

The work builds on the idea that pretrained VLMs might already possess compositional representations that are not fully leveraged by the standard cosine similarity between global image and text embeddings. By proposing a structured inference approach, the authors aim to unlock these latent capabilities without the computational cost and generalization issues associated with retraining large models. It also contributes new bidirectional compositional retrieval datasets (BISCOR-NAT and BISCOR-CTRL) to address the limitations and biases of existing evaluation benchmarks, particularly focusing on challenging SWAP cases.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the recognized weakness of dual-encoder Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in handling **compositionality**. Despite their overall success in image-text retrieval, these models often struggle to correctly interpret descriptions where attributes, objects, or relations are swapped, behaving more like "bag-of-words" systems. This limitation means they fail to truly understand the structural relationships within a scene described by text, leading to performance degradation in complex retrieval scenarios.

The authors are inspired by recent work (e.g., Campbell et al.) suggesting that pretrained VLMs *might already learn compositional representations* to some extent. The hypothesis is that the standard inference mechanism—computing cosine similarity between a single global vector for the image and a single global vector for the text—does not adequately leverage these underlying, potentially compositional, representations.

Therefore, the **key objective** of this paper is to **improve the Vision-Language Compositionality of dual-encoder VLMs by adding simple structure at inference time, without any further training.**

More specifically, the objectives include:
- **Develop a novel inference-time approach** that decomposes both the input image and text into finer-grained components (image crops and text segments, respectively).
- **Establish a matching mechanism** to find the best alignments between these fine-grained visual and textual components using the pretrained VLM's capabilities.
- **Define an aggregation strategy** to combine these fine-grained similarities into a final image-text alignment score.
- **Evaluate the effectiveness** of this approach on established and newly created datasets specifically designed to test VL compositionality, particularly focusing on challenging "SWAP" cases.
- **Conduct ablation studies and in-depth analyses** to understand the contribution of different components (e.g., image crops vs. text segments) and identify areas for future improvement.
- **Publicly release** the code and new datasets to foster further research in this area.

By focusing on inference-time modifications, the authors aim to demonstrate that significant improvements in compositional reasoning can be achieved more efficiently and with better generalization properties than through extensive retraining.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is an **inference-time approach** designed to enhance vision-language compositionality without requiring any additional training of the underlying dual-encoder VLMs. It operates in four distinct steps:

1. **Generate Image Crops:**
 - Given an input image `I`, the method generates a diverse set of smaller image crops `{c_i}`.
 - These crops vary in `scale`, `aspect ratio`, and `stride` (overlap). Specifically, crops of sizes (32, 32), (56, 56), (112, 112), (224, 224), (56, 112), and (112, 56) are used.
 - Two deployment strategies are explored: `grid` (non-overlapping crops of the same size) and `overlap` (stride of `crop_size/2`). This results in 86 crops per image for `grid` and 270 for `overlap`, all resized to the VLM's input size (e.g., 224x224 for CLIP, 256x256 for SigLIP 2).
 - The goal is to capture various relevant visual aspects and localized objects or attributes within the image.

2. **Generate Text Segments:**
 - Given a textual description `T`, the method segments the text into hierarchical object-attribute-relation segments `{t_j}`.
 - Three granularity strategies are investigated:
 - `Fine-grained`: Includes isolated objects, attribute+object combinations, and attribute+object, relation, attribute+object combinations (e.g., "cat", "fluffy cat", "fluffy white cat sleeping on brown rug").
 - `Mid-grained`: Avoids isolated objects, focusing on attribute+object and attribute+object, relation, attribute+object segments.
 - `Coarse-grained`: Similar to mid-grained, but for each object, it includes *all* its attributes in a single text segment.
 - For the controlled dataset (BISCOR-CTRL), ground-truth scene graphs are used to generate perfect text segments. For natural captions (BISCOR-NAT), a large language model (LLM), specifically Gemini 2.0 Flash, is employed to extract segments using specific prompt templates.

3. **Search for Matches between Crops and Text Segments:**
 - For a pretrained dual-encoder VLM with a visual encoder `V_enc` and textual encoder `T_enc`:
 - Each image crop `c_i` is encoded to `v_i = V_enc(c_i)`.
 - Each text segment `t_j` is encoded to `l_j = T_enc(t_j)`.
 - A similarity matrix is computed, where each entry `sim(c_i, t_j)` is the cosine similarity `cos_sim(v_i, l_j)`.
 - For *every text segment* `t_j`, the system selects the image crop `c_k` that exhibits the maximum similarity with `t_j`. This creates a set of best matching pairs `m_j = (c_k, t_j)`.

4. **Compute Image-Text Alignment Score:**
 - The final similarity score `Sim(I, T)` between the entire image `I` and text `T` is calculated as the average of the similarities of all the individual matches: `Sim(I, T) = (1/M) * Σ_j sim(m_j)`.

**Evaluation Setup:**
- **Base Models:** The approach is evaluated using popular dual-encoder VLMs: CLIP (ViT-B/32) and SigLIP 2 (ViT-B/32, giant-opt-ViT/16).
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Bidirectional retrieval metrics are used, following Winoground: image-to-text retrieval accuracy (I2T), text-to-image retrieval accuracy (T2I), and the combined group score (Group), which is the primary metric.
- **Development Datasets:** To guide the design choices, two new datasets, collectively called **BISCOR (Bidirectional Swaps for Compositional Reasoning development)**, were created:
 - `BISCOR-NAT`: 427 high-quality SWAP instances manually curated from TROHN-IMG (natural images and captions).
 - `BISCOR-CTRL`: 4000 instances programmatically generated using CLEVR scenes, specifically designed for controlled evaluation of COLOR, SIZE, MATERIAL, and QUANTITY swaps, with perfect scene graphs for text segment generation.
- **Evaluation Datasets:** The best configurations from development are tested on:
 - BIVLC [Miranda et al., 2024] (359 SWAP instances).
 - Winoground [Thrush et al., 2022] (171 compositional instances identified by Diwan et al.).
 - Test split of BISCOR-CTRL (1000 instances per attribute type).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings:

1. **Consistent Performance Improvement with Inference-Time Structuring:**
 - The proposed inference-time approach consistently and significantly improves the retrieval performance of the evaluated dual-encoder VLMs (CLIP and SigLIP 2) across all evaluation datasets.
 - These improvements are achieved *without any additional training*, demonstrating the potential of inference-time techniques.
 - The gains are particularly notable for the text-to-image (T2I) retrieval direction, indicating that the approach helps to balance the typically weaker performance of base VLMs in this direction. For example, SigLIP 2-Giant on BIVLC saw a group score improvement of over 22 absolute points.
 - On the controlled BISCOR-CTRL dataset, improvements were very strong, ranging from 23 absolute points for CLIP to 50 for SigLIP 2.

2. **Crucial Role of Image Crops:**
 - Ablation experiments showed that dividing images into smaller crops or regions is *essential* for the observed performance gains.
 - When only text segments are used without image decomposition, performance barely improves or even degrades, confirming that fine-grained visual information is critical for compositional understanding.

3. **Strong for Attribute-Object Binding:**
 - The inference-time approach proved especially effective for tasks requiring correct attribute-object binding.
 - In the controlled BISCOR-CTRL dataset, for attributes like COLOR and MATERIAL, the best model (SigLIP 2-Giant with ITA) achieved over 90 group score points, showing improvements of up to 90 absolute points in some cases, significantly outperforming baselines which were often below random.
 - However, SIZE and QUANTITY attributes proved harder, with smaller, though still positive, gains. Manual analysis revealed issues like size information loss in isolated crops and increased complexity in multi-object quantity swaps.

4. **Optimal Configuration and Design Insights:**
 - Development experiments on BISCOR-NAT and BISCOR-CTRL revealed that the `overlap` crop strategy (processing more crops) combined with `coarse-grained` text segments (fewer but semantically richer segments) yielded the best overall group scores.
 - This suggests that capturing more visual details and matching them with descriptive, meaningful text units is beneficial.

5. **Analysis and Areas for Improvement:**
 - **Bias towards Synthetic Images:** An analysis of BIVLC showed that base models exhibited a strong bias towards synthetic images over natural ones (higher similarity scores for synthetic images). While the proposed approach significantly mitigated this bias, it still persisted, suggesting the aggregation function could be further refined.
 - **Quality of LLM-Generated Text Segments:** In failure cases on BIVLC, approximately 59% of LLM-generated text segments were found to be incorrect (e.g., wrong number of segments, missing information, hallucinating). Using higher-quality, manually curated text segments led to further performance gains (e.g., +4.2 absolute points for SigLIP 2-Giant), underscoring the importance of robust text segmentation.
 - **Caption Complexity in Winoground:** The approach showed greater improvements on Winoground instances with more complex, segmentable captions. For very brief captions, the method offered little advantage, reducing to the base VLM's inference. Filtering Winoground to 102 instances with meaningful segments led to substantial gains for SigLIP 2 models (e.g., +13.7 points for SigLIP 2).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research holds significant importance for the field of Vision-Language Models and their practical applications.

**Significance:**
- **Paradigm Shift for Compositionality:** The work successfully demonstrates that significant improvements in vision-language compositionality can be achieved through *inference-time modifications*, rather than solely relying on complex and resource-intensive end-to-end retraining. This challenges the prevailing assumption that compositional failures primarily stem from insufficient training data or model architectures.
- **Unlocking Latent VLM Capabilities:** By showing that pretrained dual-encoder VLMs (like CLIP and SigLIP 2) can exhibit stronger compositional reasoning with a structured inference process, the paper suggests that these models already possess latent compositional representations that are not fully exploited by standard global embedding comparisons. This opens new avenues for leveraging existing powerful VLMs more effectively.
- **Emphasis on Fine-Grained Alignment:** The ablation studies unequivocally highlight the critical role of decomposing images into fine-grained crops. This underscores that compositional understanding is not just about breaking down text, but fundamentally about localizing and associating textual concepts with specific visual regions.
- **New Evaluation Resources:** The introduction and public release of the BISCOR-NAT and BISCOR-CTRL datasets are valuable contributions. These bidirectional compositional retrieval datasets, particularly the controlled BISCOR-CTRL, provide much-needed resources for systematically developing and evaluating new techniques for VL compositionality, addressing shortcomings in existing benchmarks.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Improved Image-Text Retrieval:** Enhancing compositional understanding will directly lead to more accurate and robust image-text retrieval systems, especially for complex, descriptive queries where fine-grained attribute-object binding and relational understanding are crucial. This could benefit applications ranging from e-commerce product search to content moderation and scientific image analysis.
- **Enhanced VLM Applications:** Improved compositionality has ripple effects across various VLM-powered applications. For instance, text-to-image generation evaluation metrics could become more reliable by better assessing whether generated images faithfully represent compositional prompts. Similarly, visual question answering (VQA) and multimodal dialogue systems could benefit from a deeper understanding of visual scene structure.
- **More Efficient Model Development:** If compositionality can be effectively addressed at inference time, it could reduce the pressure to continually train larger, more complex models or collect ever-more diverse and compositionally rich datasets. This could lead to more computationally efficient and sustainable development cycles for VLM-based systems.
- **Future Research Directions:** The detailed analysis, particularly regarding the challenges with LLM-generated text segments and the aggregation function biases, provides clear directions for future research. This includes developing more sophisticated image segmentation and object detection methods, advancing automatic text decomposition techniques, and designing more robust aggregation mechanisms for fine-grained similarities.
- **Broader Implications for AI Understanding:** This work contributes to the broader goal of making AI models more human-like in their reasoning, moving beyond superficial pattern matching towards a deeper, structural understanding of information.

While the current implementation has limitations in terms of computational cost and its focus on English, the foundational insights offered by this work pave the way for a new generation of VLM systems that are not only powerful but also truly "understand" the composition of visual and linguistic information.
