# Remember to be Curious: Episodic Context and Persistent Worlds for 3D Exploration

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for curiosity-driven 3D exploration addresses "amnesiac exploration" by combining a persistent 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) world model with a transformer-based agent maintaining long-term episodic memory. This approach enabled the agent to achieve higher 3D scene completeness and efficiently adapt to downstream tasks using only RGB observations, demonstrating robust generalization.

## Problem

- Curiosity-driven reinforcement learning agents often suffer from "amnesiac exploration," repeatedly revisiting already-explored locations and receiving spurious novelty rewards in complex 3D environments.
- Existing world models lack spatial persistence, and agent memory often fails to maintain sufficient episodic context for long-horizon strategic exploration.
- Traditional active mapping methods typically rely on ground-truth depth observations or SLAM, which can abstract rich semantic information and limit the flexibility of end-to-end learning.

## Method

- A Persistent 3D Forward Model, instantiated as an online 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== (3DGS) model, continuously builds and refines a spatially consistent representation of the environment to provide a stable intrinsic curiosity reward based on prediction error.
- A Long-Context Transformer Agent processes a full sequence of past RGB observations and actions, incorporating a global linear-attention memory module to maintain long-term episodic context for strategic navigation.
- A ==self-supervised== ==reinforcement learning== framework trains the agent using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), with the intrinsic curiosity reward as the sole signal, enabling map-free operation solely on RGB inputs at test time.

## Results

- The proposed agent achieved higher 3D scene completeness and a lower average distance to nearest observed points across various time horizons compared to active-mapping baselines on HM3D and Gibson datasets, using only RGB input at test time.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the persistent 3DGS world model and the agent's long-term episodic memory are crucial for effective exploration, as agents lacking these components exhibited degraded or failed exploratory behaviors.
- The pre-trained exploration policy efficiently fine-tuned to downstream sparse-reward tasks (e.g., apple-picking, image-goal navigation) with few episodes, outperforming agents trained from scratch, and demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization to novel AI-generated worlds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective curiosity-driven exploration in 3D environments fundamentally requires both a world model with spatial persistence and an agent with long-term episodic memory to overcome the "amnesiac exploration" problem.
> - 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) provides a viable and continuously updatable proxy for a persistent world model, generating a reliable novelty signal by rendering predictions from an evolving 3D scene representation.
> - A transformer architecture, augmented with a global linear-attention memory module, enables agents to maintain the necessary long-horizon episodic context from RGB observations, facilitating strategic and far-reaching exploration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Exploration is a prerequisite for agents to learn effective behaviors in environments characterized by sparse rewards and long-horizon tasks, particularly within complex 3D settings. Curiosity-driven reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a strategy to address this by providing intrinsic rewards derived from the mismatch between an agent's predictive model of the world and observed reality. However, the application of this paradigm to complex, photorealistic environments has presented challenges. Existing curiosity-driven methods often suffer from an inability to maintain a stable reward signal, leading agents to become trapped in local loops or to continuously receive novelty rewards for revisiting states that should be considered "seen" but are "forgotten" by the world model—a phenomenon referred to as "amnesiac exploration." This issue stems from a lack of spatial persistence in the world model and insufficient episodic context within the agent's memory.

Concurrently, a distinct branch of research focuses on active mapping and next-best-view (NBV) selection, where agents explicitly build or rely on geometric maps to guide exploration. These methods often require ground-truth depth observations, rely on Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) systems, or explicitly condition policies on geometric abstractions. While providing spatial awareness, such modularity can abstract away rich semantic information, limit the flexibility of end-to-end learning, and restrict generalization to tasks that are purely geometrically definable.

More recently, video-generative models have been explored as data-driven approaches to model world dynamics. While promising for their potential to simulate dynamic worlds, current iterations often exhibit limitations such as spatial forgetting, temporal drift over long horizons, and operating in an open-loop manner, meaning they struggle to continuously update their internal representations based on real-time agent observations.

This work contributes to the landscape by explicitly identifying and addressing the dual challenges of spatial persistence in the world model and episodic context in the agent's memory as critical for effective curiosity-driven exploration in 3D environments. By grounding curiosity in a continuously updated, persistent 3D reconstruction model (3DGS) and pairing it with a transformer-based agent that maintains a long-term episodic history from RGB observations, the authors propose a framework that overcomes the "amnesiac" exploration problem. This approach distinguishes itself from traditional active mapping by being map-free at test time, operating solely on RGB inputs, and maintaining an end-to-end adaptable architecture. Furthermore, it leverages 3DGS as a controlled proxy for a persistent world model, aiming to provide insights into the necessary properties (spatial persistence, closed-loop online updating) that future, more dynamic, generative world models will require to support robust intrinsic motivation. The research thus attempts to bridge gaps in current curiosity-driven RL, active mapping, and world modeling paradigms for robust 3D exploration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable effective curiosity-driven exploration for artificial agents navigating complex, photorealistic 3D environments. The authors identify specific limitations in existing approaches and articulate several detailed goals to address these.

#### 2.1. Key Objectives

1. **Overcome "Amnesiac" Exploration:** A central objective is to resolve the problem where curiosity-driven agents repeatedly revisit already-explored locations and receive spurious novelty rewards for forgotten states. This requires both a persistent understanding of the environment and an agent capable of remembering its past trajectory.
2. **Develop a Spatially Persistent World Model:** The work aims to demonstrate that effective curiosity necessitates a model of the world that is continuously updated and maintains spatial consistency over time. This model should reliably distinguish truly novel regions from previously visited but forgotten ones.
3. **Integrate Episodic Context into Agent Policy:** The research seeks to design an agent architecture that can maintain a long-horizon episodic trajectory history. This internal memory allows the agent to navigate strategically toward novel regions and avoid getting trapped in local loops.
4. **Achieve End-to-End, Map-Free Operation:** A significant goal is to enable the agent to navigate and explore using solely RGB observations at deployment time, without reliance on explicit geometric maps, ground-truth depth sensors, or external localization systems. This design promotes adaptability and generalization.
5. **Facilitate Downstream Task Adaptation:** The framework aims to produce an exploration policy that can be efficiently fine-tuned for a variety of downstream sparse-reward tasks, leveraging its learned exploratory prior rather than requiring training from scratch.
6. **Demonstrate Zero-Shot Generalization:** The authors seek to show that the trained exploration agent can generalize effectively to novel, out-of-distribution scenes, including those generated by AI, without further training.

**Motivation:**
The motivation for this work stems from the inherent challenges of learning in environments that provide infrequent or no explicit external rewards. Drawing inspiration from biological systems, where an inherent drive to explore (curiosity) facilitates learning, the authors seek to imbue artificial agents with a similar capability. Traditional goal-conditioned navigation methods often struggle in real-world scenarios due to the fundamental sparsity of success signals. While curiosity-driven RL offers a compelling alternative by generating intrinsic rewards from prediction error, its scalability to complex, photorealistic 3D environments has been limited by two intertwined issues:
- **Ephemeral World Models:** Existing world models for curiosity often act as statistical priors over lifelong experience rather than maintaining an episodic, spatially persistent record of the environment. This leads to predictive errors spuriously arising in revisited areas, yielding false novelty rewards.
- **Limited Agent Memory:** Without a robust historical context, agents often exhibit "amnesiac" behavior, repeatedly visiting the same locations without progressing to truly novel areas.

The authors hypothesize that by coupling a persistent, continuously updated model of the world with an agent equipped with long-term episodic context, these limitations can be addressed, thereby unlocking the potential of curiosity-driven exploration in diverse and challenging 3D environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on a self-supervised reinforcement learning framework for curiosity-driven exploration in static 3D scenes. The approach consists of two primary, decoupled components: a persistent 3D forward model for generating intrinsic rewards during training, and a long-context transformer-based agent architecture for learning exploratory policies.

**Problem Setup:**
The agent interacts with a 3D environment, taking an action \( a_t \) at each timestep \( t \) and receiving a new observation \( o_{t+1} \). During training, privileged sensory information (denoted \( \tilde{o}_{t+1} \), including camera pose and depth image) can be used, but at test time, the agent operates solely on visual RGB streams.

#### 3.1. Persistent 3D Forward Model (Curiosity Module)

The purpose of this module is to provide a stable intrinsic curiosity reward by quantifying the novelty of incoming observations. This is achieved by comparing a prediction from an internal world model with the actual observation.

- **Model Instantiation:** The forward model is instantiated as an online 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) model. 3DGS is selected for its ability to incorporate streaming observations while maintaining a spatially persistent representation of the environment, crucial for a reliable novelty signal.
- **Training-time Inputs:** The 3DGS model uses privileged training-time data: RGB images (\( I_i \)), corresponding depth images (\( D_i \)), and camera poses (\( p_i \)) up to time \( t \). This stream allows the model to continuously build and refine its 3D representation \( G_t \).
- **3DGS Update Process:** For each observed RGB-D frame, new Gaussians are added per pixel, initialized from their color, depth, and camera pose. At fixed intervals, \( G_t \) is optimized on randomly selected past frames to enhance reconstruction quality, and pruned/densified following established 3DGS techniques.
- **Novel View Prediction:** After the agent executes an action \( a_t \) and moves to a new pose \( p_{t+1} \), the forward model renders a prediction of the incoming observation (\( \hat{I}_{t+1} \)) using the current 3DGS representation \( G_t \) at pose \( p_{t+1} \). Since \( G_t \) only incorporates observations up to time \( t \), any significant discrepancy in \( \hat{I}_{t+1} \) with the actual observation \( I_{t+1} \) indicates new, unexplained information.
- **Curiosity Reward Calculation:** The curiosity reward \( r_{cur,t} \) is derived from the prediction error between the rendered prediction \( \hat{I}_{t+1} \) and the actual observation \( I_{t+1} \). To focus on meaningful novelty and avoid rewarding high-frequency reconstruction errors, the error is computed after low-pass filtering and down-sampling both images. The final reward is binary: a positive reward \( r_{new} \) is given if the filtered error \( e_t \) exceeds a threshold \( \tau \), indicating a sufficiently unexplained view, and a small penalty \( r_{old} \) is applied otherwise.

#### 3.2. Agent Architecture

The agent's role is to seek novelty based on the intrinsic reward signal. It operates purely from a stream of RGB observations and maintains its own internal record of past experiences to guide exploration.

- **Episodic Memory with Transformer:** The agent's policy \( \pi \) is a transformer-based network conditioned on the full sequence of past RGB observations and actions throughout the episode \( \pi(\cdot | o_{1:t}, a_{1:t-1}) \). This architecture provides the agent with the episodic memory necessary for long-horizon planning, such as backtracking to find novel areas.
- **Input Processing:** At each timestep \( t \), the input consists of pairs of RGB observation \( I_i \) and the preceding action \( a_{i-1} \). Actions are geometrically encoded as Plücker-ray images and concatenated channel-wise with the RGB observations.
- **Image Encoder:** Each RGB-action input is compressed into a frame token. This involves fusing patch embeddings with DINOv2 features via a learnable query token through cross-attention.
- **Temporal Attention Layers:** Frame tokens are processed by causal sliding-window temporal self-attention. This design maintains causality (only past and current observations are used) and keeps computation scalable over long episodes by limiting the window size \( W \).
- **Global Linear-Attention Memory Module:** To overcome the limitations of a fixed-size sliding window, selected temporal layers are interleaved with a global linear-attention memory module. This module reads from a running memory state and updates it with the current token, allowing the policy to access information beyond the immediate sliding window and facilitating long-range context propagation.
- **Actor-Critic Heads:** The processed current token is passed through actor and critic heads. The actor head outputs an action distribution \( \pi_\theta(a_t | o_{1:t}, a_{1:t-1}) \), and the critic head provides a value estimate \( V_\theta(o_{1:t}, a_{1:t-1}) \). Actions are sampled from the actor's distribution during both training and testing.

#### 3.3. Training and Regularization

The actor-critic policy is optimized using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). The intrinsic reward \( r_{cur,t} \) is the sole reward signal during training.

- **Action Diversity Regularization:** To prevent the agent from collapsing into repetitive behaviors, particularly in areas requiring traversal through already-seen regions to find new branches, a scheduled random policy injection is employed. At each training step, the executed action is sampled from a mixture of the learned policy and a uniform random distribution over the action space, with a mixing coefficient \( \beta \). This \( \beta \) is gradually annealed from 0.2 to zero over a scheduled window during training. A small entropy regularizer is also included. The PPO update accounts for this mixed behavior distribution in the likelihood ratio.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Simulator and Dataset:** Training is conducted in the Habitat simulator using the HM3D dataset (800 scenes). Evaluation is performed on the HM3D validation set and Gibson dataset for generalization.
- **Agent Dynamics:** A drone agent with a spherical embodiment is simulated, capable of free 3D movement (not restricted to a navmesh). Actions include moving forward (0.25m), looking right (15°), looking left (15°), and pausing. Collisions are handled via ray-tracing.
- **Computational Resources:** Training required 5.5 days on 8x 80GB H100 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental analysis assessed the agent's exploration capabilities, the necessity of its architectural components, its adaptability to downstream tasks, and its generalization to novel environments.

#### 4.1. Indoor Scene Exploration

The agent's exploration capabilities were evaluated on unseen indoor scenes from the HM3D and Gibson datasets, measured by 3D scene completeness (percentage of reachable mesh surface points observed) and average distance to nearest observed point.

- **Superior Performance:** The proposed agent achieved higher 3D completeness at all measured time horizons (256, 512, and 1024 steps) compared to active-mapping baselines such as Active Neural SLAM (ANS-RGB, ANS-depth) and Occupancy Anticipation (OccA-RGB, OccA-RGBD). It also demonstrated a lower average distance from ground truth points to observed points.
- **RGB-only Deployment:** A notable result is that the agent achieved this performance using only RGB input at test time, unlike several baselines that required ground-truth depth observations, limiting their deployment flexibility.
- **Emergent Behaviors:** Qualitatively, the agent exhibited complex emergent exploratory behaviors, including seeking doorways, efficiently traversing corridors, and returning to junctions. It rarely utilized the pause action, suggesting continuous engagement with the environment. The agent also avoided characteristic failure modes of map-based methods, such as getting locally trapped due to erroneous map predictions.

#### 4.2. Memory Ablations

Ablation studies were conducted on the memory capacities of both the forward model and the agent network, specifically on the HM3D dataset.

- **Cruciality of Persistent World Model:** The experiments confirmed that a persistent world model is essential for effective exploration. Initial implementations of ICM without a persistent world memory catastrophically failed, often resulting in the agent spinning in place or becoming stuck. Replacing the forward model with a 3DGS limited to a short-term memory (e.g., the last 64 frames) led to agents that roamed locally but failed to venture far, indicating that long-term spatial persistence in the world model is necessary for a reliable novelty signal.
- **Necessity of Agent Episodic Memory:** Ablations on the agent's memory capacity revealed that both RNN-based agents and transformer agents with limited context windows (e.g., context=1, 4, 16) exhibited degraded exploration performance compared to the full long-context transformer. Furthermore, even an asymmetric memory (where only the critic retained context) outperformed agents with no memory, underscoring the importance of the agent's internal episodic context for learning novelty-seeking policies.

#### 4.3. Task-based Fine-tuning

The agent's adaptability to downstream navigation tasks was evaluated through fine-tuning on apple-picking and image-goal navigation tasks.

- **Efficient Adaptation:** The map-free agent architecture proved capable of adapting to these semantic tasks through fine-tuning with only a few episodes, without requiring architectural modifications.
- **Outperformance Against From-Scratch Baselines:** The fine-tuned exploration agent consistently outperformed agents trained from scratch on the task reward alone, even with the same total number of training episodes. This advantage was more pronounced in sparse-reward settings (e.g., fewer apples per scene), where the baseline struggled (search confined to one room), while the fine-tuned agent maintained consistent performance.
- **Leveraging Exploratory Prior:** For image-goal navigation, a more extreme sparse-reward regime, the fine-tuned agent reliably located target views, whereas the from-scratch baseline often collapsed to undirected roaming. This indicates that the pre-trained exploratory behavior effectively provided a beneficial prior for goal-directed navigation.

#### 4.4. Out-of-distribution Scenes

Generalization capabilities were tested by deploying the agent zero-shot (without further training) to AI-generated worlds from World Labs (Hobbit World and Spaceship).

- **Robust Generalization:** Despite significant differences in aesthetic, representation (3DGS vs. mesh scans for rendering), and underlying rendering pipelines compared to the training data (HM3D), the agent maintained coherent exploratory behaviors. It successfully navigated corridors, discovered new spaces, and demonstrated effective collision avoidance (e.g., only 2-3 collisions over 256 steps in these novel worlds), suggesting robust internal representations of navigable space.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for curiosity-driven exploration that contributes to the field of embodied AI and reinforcement learning in several ways, with implications for future developments.

- **Addressing Foundational Limitations of Curiosity-Driven RL:** The work empirically demonstrates that spatial persistence in the world model and episodic context in the agent's memory are critical components for scaling curiosity-driven exploration to complex 3D environments. By directly tackling the "amnesiac exploration" problem, the research provides a solution that yields more reliable and effective intrinsic motivation signals, thus enabling more robust learning in sparse-reward settings.
- **Advancing End-to-End, Map-Free Navigation:** The development of an agent that operates purely on RGB observations at test time, without explicit geometric maps or depth sensors, represents a step towards more generalizable and adaptable embodied agents. This flexibility contrasts with traditional active mapping approaches that are often tethered to specific geometric representations or input modalities, thereby expanding the applicability of exploratory behaviors to a wider range of semantic tasks.
- **Guidance for Future World Model Development:** By utilizing 3D Gaussian Splatting as a controlled proxy for a persistent world model in static scenes, the research provides concrete insights into the essential properties required for a forward dynamics model to support reliable curiosity: spatial consistency and continuous online updating. This serves as a valuable guide for the development of more sophisticated, dynamic video-generative world models, highlighting the critical architectural and functional requirements necessary for them to overcome current limitations such as spatial forgetting and temporal drift.
- **Enhancing Transfer Learning and Task Adaptation:** The demonstrated ability of the pre-trained exploration policy to efficiently fine-tune for diverse downstream tasks, especially in sparse-reward regimes, underscores the value of general-purpose exploration as a pre-training objective. This can lead to more sample-efficient learning for specific tasks, reducing the need for extensive task-specific data or from-scratch training.
- **Robust Generalization to Novel Environments:** The zero-shot generalization to out-of-distribution and AI-generated worlds indicates that the learned exploration strategy is not merely memorizing training scenes but acquiring a transferable understanding of navigable space and novelty. This robustness is critical for deploying embodied agents in diverse real-world or simulated environments without constant re-training.

The findings open avenues for future research in several directions, particularly in extending the concept of persistent world models to dynamic scenes as generative models mature. The emphasis on closed-loop online observations and continuously updated internal representations offers a blueprint for developing more intelligent and intrinsically motivated agents capable of navigating and learning autonomously in complex, evolving environments.
