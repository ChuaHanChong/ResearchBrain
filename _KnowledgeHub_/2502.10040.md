# Diffusion Trajectory-guided Policy for Long-horizon Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Diffusion Trajectory-guided Policy (DTP) provides explicit 2D trajectory guidance to enhance robot imitation learning for long-horizon manipulation. This approach improves generalization and data efficiency, achieving a 33.9% increase in average sequence length on the CALVIN benchmark compared to GR-1 and doubling the success rate in some real-world tasks over the second-best method.

## Problem

- Compounding errors accumulate over extended action sequences in long-horizon robot tasks, leading to task failures.
- Limited availability of high-quality robot demonstration data hinders generalization, especially in out-of-distribution scenarios.
- A gap exists in effectively translating high-level vision-language instructions into precise robot actions, particularly without intuitive guidance.

## Method

- A two-stage **Diffusion Trajectory-guided Policy (DTP)** framework is introduced, combining a ==Diffusion Trajectory Model (DTM)== with a ==Vision-Language Action (VLA)== policy.
- The DTM learns to generate task-relevant ==2D-particle trajectories== of the end-effector from vision and language inputs using a ==causal diffusion decoding== structure.
- These generated 2D trajectories serve as explicit high-level guidance, conditioned into a ==causal Transformer-based VLA policy== alongside vision, language, and proprioception.

## Results

- DTP improved average sequence length by **33.9%** on the CALVIN benchmark (seen environments) compared to GR-1 and showed superior generalization in unseen environments (Task 5 success rate of **0.497**).
- It demonstrated enhanced data efficiency, outperforming baselines with only **10%** of training data, achieving an average sequence length of **2.55** versus GR-1's **2.00**.
- In real robot experiments, DTP achieved an aggregate success rate of **0.84** across individual tasks, **doubling** the success rate of the second-best method in complex tasks like OpenSideDrawer, and showed an average sequence length of **4.6** in long-horizon tasks compared to GR1's **2.0**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit 2D trajectory guidance, analogous to human intuition for high-level visualization, effectively bridges the gap between vision-language inputs and robot actions, mitigating compounding errors.
> - Generating coarse, task-relevant 2D trajectories using a diffusion model is more data-efficient and computationally less intensive than generating full videos or goal images.
> - The modular, two-stage DTP framework allows for integration as a plug-in with existing Transformer-based VLA models, enhancing their performance without extensive architectural modifications.
