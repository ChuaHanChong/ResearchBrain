# Pixels to Proofs: Probabilistically-Safe Latent World Model Control via Parallel Conformal Robust MPC

> [!summary] Summary
> The SLS² framework provides probabilistically safe robot control directly from pixel observations by integrating learned latent world models with conformal prediction for uncertainty quantification and GPU-accelerated robust Model Predictive Control (MPC). This approach achieves high task success rates and 100% safety in complex visuomotor tasks, including deformable object manipulation, in both simulation and hardware.

## Problem

- Autonomous systems in open-world environments often lack robust safety guarantees because acquiring precise state representations from perception is impractical.
- Traditional safety-critical control methods rely on known state representations and dynamics, which are frequently violated in complex, perception-driven robotic tasks.
- Learned latent world models offer powerful predictive capabilities from raw observations but struggle to provide formal, calibrated safety assurances for the true physical system due to high-dimensional latent states, imperfect dynamics, and complex constraint translation.

## Method

- A Le-WM-style joint-embedding predictive architecture learns compact Markovian latent representations and their dynamics from raw image observations, incorporating multi-step latent prediction and regularization losses.
- ==Conformal Prediction== is used to quantify two types of uncertainty: latent dynamics prediction errors (defining ellipsoidal error sets) and the 'in-domain' latent region, ensuring the planner operates within the training data's support.
- A GPU-accelerated Robust **MPC** framework, based on System Level Synthesis (SLS), synthesizes causal state-feedback policies by linearizing dynamics around a nominal trajectory and integrating CP-calibrated disturbance sets and robustly tightened latent safety constraints for probabilistic guarantees.

## Results

- SLS² consistently achieved higher nominal planning success rates than existing world-model baselines, including **93.75%** for simulated rope manipulation and **51.54%** for challenging contact-rich Push-T tasks.
- In robustly-constrained tasks, the framework demonstrated **100.00%** safety (e.g., avoiding obstacles in rope manipulation, respecting joint limits in Reacher) and high robust task completion rates (e.g., **88.57%** for rope manipulation), significantly outperforming safety-focused baselines.
- The nominal planner was successfully deployed on real hardware for bimanual rope manipulation, showcasing its applicability beyond simulation and demonstrating a clear correlation between latent space error and task space error convergence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining accurate learned latent world models, rigorous uncertainty quantification via conformal prediction, and robust control via System Level Synthesis effectively bridges the gap between learning-based perception and verifiable safety.
> - The use of Markovian latent states, multi-step dynamics losses, and specific regularization (SIGReg, Temporal Straightening) is crucial for training world models that are sufficiently predictive and amenable to gradient-based robust MPC.
> - GPU-accelerated SLS makes robust MPC computationally tractable for high-dimensional latent spaces, enabling real-time probabilistically safe control directly from pixel inputs with formal containment guarantees.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The control of autonomous systems, particularly in open-world environments, necessitates robust safety guarantees. A fundamental challenge in this domain is the acquisition of a precise state representation for planning, control, and verification. In real-world scenarios, especially those involving deformable objects or tasks where the state is implicitly defined through perception, obtaining such a representation is often impractical or requires extensive, manually labeled data.

Traditional safety-critical control methods typically rely on known state representations, well-defined dynamics, and calibrated disturbance bounds. These assumptions are frequently violated in complex, perception-driven robotic tasks, limiting the applicability of classical tools.

Recent advancements in representation learning and world modeling offer an alternative by inferring latent state representations directly from raw observations, such as images. These learned world models can then be used to predict future dynamics in these latent spaces, enabling decision-making and planning without explicitly defined state variables. While promising for tasks where explicit state estimation is challenging, these latent space control policies present significant difficulties for ensuring safety. Latent representations can be high-dimensional, their learned dynamics are inherently imperfect, and physical or observation-space constraints must be accurately translated into the latent domain. Existing approaches to latent safety, such as deep learning for approximating latent safety certificates or various policy filters, often employ heuristic approximations, lack formal guarantees, or are primarily designed for in-distribution recovery, failing to provide sound, calibrated reachability assurances for the true system dynamics.

This work addresses the gap between the predictive capabilities of learned world models and the rigorous safety requirements of autonomous systems. It proposes a framework that leverages the strengths of latent world modeling while introducing formal, probabilistically calibrated safety guarantees. By integrating advanced model learning with robust control techniques, the research aims to enable safe and reliable robot deployment directly from visual observations, without relying on hand-engineered state representations or explicit state estimation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation of this research is to enable reliable and safe robot deployment in open-world scenarios, particularly when the system's state is only inferable from raw observations like images. This is a critical challenge because traditional safety-critical control methods typically assume a well-defined and known state representation, which is often difficult or impossible to obtain in complex real-world tasks involving perception.

The authors identify that recent advancements in world modeling, which learn latent state representations and dynamics directly from observations, offer a promising path for robotic decision-making in such settings. However, a major limitation of these learned models is the difficulty in ensuring that control policies derived from them translate to safe behavior for the true physical system. This difficulty stems from several factors:
- Latent states can be high-dimensional and abstract.
- Learned latent dynamics are approximations and inherently inaccurate.
- Translating explicit safety constraints from the physical or observation space into the latent space is complex.
- Existing latent safety methods often rely on heuristics and lack sound, calibrated reachability guarantees.

To overcome these challenges, the research sets out to achieve two main objectives:

**Problem 1: Learning a Planning- and Verification-Friendly Latent World Model**
The first objective focuses on developing a robust latent world model that is not only effective for prediction and planning but also amenable to rigorous safety verification. This involves:
- **Learning Compact Markovian Latent Representations:** Training a visual encoder (`enc_θ`) to map raw image observations (`o_t`) into a compact latent embedding (`z_t`). These embeddings are then used to construct a Markovian latent state (`s_z_t`) that captures sufficient historical information for accurate prediction.
- **Accurate and Differentiable Latent Dynamics:** Learning a latent dynamics predictor (`f_ϕ`) that maps the current Markovian state and control action to the next state. This model must be accurate under multi-step rollouts, differentiable to support gradient-based optimization, and predictive under actions.
- **Calibrated Uncertainty Quantification:** Critically, the model must quantify its prediction errors (`e_t`). The goal is to construct uncertainty sets (`E(s, u)`) that contain the true prediction error with a specified high probability (1 − δ), using conformal prediction techniques.
- **Learned Latent Safety Classifier:** Developing a latent safety score classifier (`c_ψ`) that can determine whether a given latent state violates task-specific constraints. This classifier must also be conformalized to enable probabilistic constraint satisfaction.

**Problem 2: Reachability-Informed Robust MPC in Latent Space**
The second objective is to integrate the learned, uncertainty-quantified latent world model into a robust control framework. This involves:
- **Optimizing Nominal Trajectories:** Using the learned latent dynamics (`f_ϕ`) to optimize a nominal trajectory (`ζ`) and control sequence (`v`) for goal-reaching.
- **Synthesizing Robust Feedback Policies:** Employing a System Level Synthesis (SLS) based robust Model Predictive Control (MPC) scheme. This scheme synthesizes a causal state-feedback controller (`π`) that stabilizes the true latent dynamics around the nominal trajectory, accounting for prediction errors.
- **Probabilistic Safety and Reachability Guarantees:** Ensuring that the MPC policy generates reachable tubes (`R_s_k`) for the latent states and `R_u_k` for controls such that, with a high probability (at least 1 − δ), the true latent states and controls remain within these tubes and, critically, within the defined safe set (`S_safe`) and in-domain region (`I`) for all steps of the planning horizon. This is achieved by incorporating the CP-calibrated latent dynamics error bounds and learned constraint-checker errors into the SLS framework.

In essence, the work seeks to bridge the gap between high-performance, data-driven perception and prediction capabilities and the imperative for verifiable, robust safety in robotic control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, termed SLS² (Safe Latent Space System Level Synthesis), integrates learned latent world models, conformal prediction for uncertainty quantification, and GPU-accelerated robust Model Predictive Control (MPC) using System Level Synthesis (SLS).

#### 3.1. Training World Models Friendly for Gradient-Based Robust MPC

The initial step involves training an action-conditioned latent world model from an offline dataset of image transitions. This model is based on a Le-WM-style joint-embedding predictive architecture.
- **Visual Encoder:** An encoder (`enc_θ`), implemented as a ViT backbone with an MLP projector, maps raw image observations (`o_t`) to compact latent embeddings (`z_t`). Images are preprocessed by resizing and normalization.
- **Markovian Latent State:** To capture temporal dependencies, a Markovian latent state (`s_z_t`) is constructed by augmenting the current embedding with finite-difference latent features (e.g., `z_t`, `Δz_t`..., `Δ^K z_t`), where `K` is the Markov order.
- **Latent Dynamics Predictor:** An MLP (`f_ϕ`) is trained to predict the next Markovian state (`s_z_(t+1)`) from the current state (`s_z_t`) and action (`a_t`).
- **Training Objectives:** The model is trained using a multi-step latent prediction objective, where `f_ϕ` is rolled out autoregressively over a horizon `H`. Additional losses include:
 - **SIGReg Loss:** Prevents representational collapse, encouraging latent embeddings to follow an approximately Gaussian distribution.
 - **Temporal Straightening Loss:** Encourages alignment between consecutive latent velocity vectors.
- **Latent Obstacle Classifier:** For constrained tasks, a separate MLP classifier (`c_ψ`) is trained using the *frozen* encoder. This classifier maps a latent embedding (`z_i`) to a signed score, where positive scores indicate non-violating states. A balanced dataset of obstacle and non-obstacle observations, labeled with known simulator constraints, is used for training with a hinge loss. The classifier's threshold (`η`) is then calibrated using conformal prediction on a held-out set of violating examples, providing a conservative safety threshold.

#### 3.2. Latent Uncertainty Quantification via Conformal Prediction (CP)

To ensure probabilistic safety, the framework quantifies two types of uncertainty using conformal prediction:
- **Latent Dynamics Prediction Error:** Split conformal prediction is applied to quantify the error of the latent dynamics model. A held-out calibration dataset is used to compute residuals (`e_i`) between true and predicted latent states. An uncertainty model (`Σ_ψ`), parameterized as a neural network, is trained to capture the state-action-dependent error geometry by minimizing a multivariate Gaussian negative log-likelihood loss. The scale of this covariance model is then calibrated using CP on Mahalanobis distance scores (`r_i = e_i^T Σ_ψ^-1 e_i`), yielding a quantile `q`. This defines ellipsoidal one-step error sets `E(s, u)` that contain the true prediction error with high probability.
- **In-Domain Latent Region:** To keep the planner within the support of the training data, an in-domain latent region (`I`) is defined. An ellipsoid is fitted to calibration embeddings, and its threshold (`q_ID`) is calibrated using CP on Mahalanobis distances. This ensures that the planner operates within a region where the learned model is expected to be valid.

#### 3.3. Conformalized Robust MPC via System Level Synthesis (SLS)

The core of the robust control aspect is a GPU-accelerated SLS framework, building on prior work. SLS optimizes over closed-loop system responses, enabling efficient robust MPC for high-dimensional latent spaces.
- **Linearized Dynamics:** The learned latent dynamics are linearized around a nominal trajectory, modeling the system as `Δs_(k+1) = A_k Δs_k + B_k Δu_k + E_k w_k`, where `w_k` are disturbances in a unit ball.
- **Disturbance Sets from CP:** The disturbance matrices `E_k` are defined using the `√q L_ψ(ζ_k, v_k)` terms derived from the CP-calibrated dynamics error sets, propagating the probabilistically bounded errors.
- **SLS Optimization Problem:** The MPC problem jointly optimizes a nominal trajectory (`ζ`), controls (`v`), and closed-loop response matrices (`Φ_s`, `Φ_u`). The objective includes nominal task costs, terminal costs, and a tube-size regularizer.
- **Robust Constraint Satisfaction:**
 - **Safety Constraints:** The learned latent safety constraints (`g_ψ(s) ≤ -η`) are incorporated and *tightened* by reachable tube margins (`τ_i,k`). This ensures robust satisfaction of the safety constraints over the entire predicted reachable state set (`R_s_k`).
 - **In-Domain Constraint:** An additional constraint (`R_s_k ⊆ I`) ensures that the entire reachable tube remains within the conformalized in-domain latent region.
- **Probabilistic Guarantee:** This CP-informed SLS scheme provides a formal probabilistic containment guarantee (Theorem 1), stating that with a probability of at least `1 − δ`, the true latent states and controls remain within their respective reachable tubes throughout the planning horizon, provided the test-time errors are exchangeable with calibration errors.
- **Nominal Planning:** The framework also supports nominal, gradient-based trajectory optimization (similar to iLQR) by simplifying the SLS problem to exclude reachability-related terms and robust constraints.

This comprehensive methodology allows SLS² to generate probabilistically safe motion plans directly from pixel inputs, addressing both the predictive power of learned models and the stringent requirements of safety-critical applications.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated SLS² across four distinct vision-based control tasks: Reacher, OGBench Cube, Push-T, and a bimanual rope manipulation task (simulated and hardware). Comparisons were made against various latent world-model planning baselines for nominal performance and safe latent-space planning baselines for robust performance.

#### 4.1. Nominal Planning Performance

For nominal gradient-based planning (using GPU-SLS simplified to iLQR), SLS² consistently outperformed existing world-model planning baselines (Le-WM, DINO-WM, PLDM).

- **Reacher:** SLS² achieved 83.50% success, significantly higher than Le-WM (17.0%). An ablation study (Table 1) demonstrated the critical role of the Markovian history in the latent state and the multi-step dynamics loss for predictive accuracy. It also showed that gradient-based planning (iLQR) was superior to sampling-based planning (CEM) for exploiting the learned dynamics' local linearity.
- **OGBench Cube:** SLS² achieved 91.5% success, outperforming DINO-WM (69.0%). This highlights its effectiveness in post-grasp transport tasks.
- **Push-T:** Considered the most challenging task due to long-horizon, contact-rich pushing, all methods showed lower performance. Nonetheless, SLS² maintained the highest success rate at 51.54%, with the lowest minimum block-position error, substantially surpassing DINO-WM (2.52%) and PLDM (1.50%). This result suggests the approach's robustness even in complex manipulation scenarios.
- **Rope (Simulated):** For this bimanual deformable object manipulation task, SLS² achieved 93.75% success, significantly better than DINO-WM (25.0%). This demonstrates the method's particular efficacy in complex, deformable object manipulation where small errors accumulate.
- **Rope (Hardware):** The nominal planner was successfully deployed on a real robotic setup for the rope task. It demonstrated the ability to stretch the rope from a start image to a goal image, with plots showing a clear correlation between latent space error and task space error convergence.

#### 4.2. Robustly-Constrained Safe Latent Space MPC

SLS² was evaluated for its ability to provide probabilistically safe control, demonstrating superior safety and robust task completion compared to safety-focused baselines (HJ-filtered, LPB) and non-robust ablations.

- **Rope:** SLS² achieved 100.00% safety (avoiding an ellipsoidal obstacle) and 88.57% robust task completion (safety + goal-reaching). In contrast, HJ-filtered showed 37.14% safety, LPB 57.15%, and Nominal iLQR 54.29%, indicating frequent obstacle collisions. The visualizations (Figure 5, Figure 6, Figure 15) confirmed that SLS² trajectories stayed within the safe region, with reachable tubes effectively guiding the nominal plan.
- **Reacher:** With joint angle constraints, SLS² achieved 100.00% safety and robust success, comparable to the HJ-filtered baseline (100% safety and robust success). Notably, constrained iLQR variants (without robust tubes) also achieved high safety but slightly lower robust success, implying the robust tightenings in SLS² provided a more complete guarantee. The solve times for SLS² were comparable to constrained iLQR, demonstrating limited computational overhead for robust constraints. (Figure 16)
- **OGBench Cube:** To enforce a gripper height threshold, SLS² achieved 94.29% safety and robust success. Baselines like HJ-filtered, LPB, and Nominal iLQR achieved 0% safety, frequently violating the height constraint. (Figure 17) This highlights SLS²'s capability not only for safety but also for altering planning behavior to meet specific constraints (e.g., keeping the cube low).

Overall, the findings indicate that SLS² effectively integrates accurate learned latent world models with rigorous uncertainty quantification and robust MPC. This combination leads to consistently higher task success rates in nominal planning and significantly enhanced probabilistic safety and robust task completion in constrained environments, both in simulation and on real hardware.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents SLS², a framework that addresses a critical challenge in modern robotics: achieving probabilistically safe control directly from pixel observations in complex, real-world environments. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

- **Bridging the Gap between Learning and Safety:** SLS² effectively bridges the gap between the powerful representational and predictive capabilities of learned latent world models and the rigorous safety guarantees required for real-world autonomous systems. It moves beyond heuristic approximations in latent safety by providing formal, calibrated probabilistic assurances.
- **Probabilistic Safety Guarantees from Pixels:** A major contribution is the ability to provide strong probabilistic guarantees on system safety and reachability directly from raw visual inputs, without relying on hand-engineered state estimators or precise system models. This is achieved through the novel integration of conformal prediction (for calibrating latent dynamics and constraint errors) and System Level Synthesis (SLS) robust MPC.
- **Enhanced Performance in Complex Tasks:** The empirical evaluations demonstrate that SLS² not only provides safety but also improves goal-reaching performance across diverse and challenging tasks, including rigid-body manipulation (Cube), multi-joint control (Reacher), contact-rich pushing (Push-T), and deformable object manipulation (Rope). This suggests that incorporating uncertainty-aware planning can lead to more effective and reliable controllers.
- **Scalability for High-Dimensional Latent Spaces:** The use of GPU-accelerated SLS is crucial for making robust MPC computationally tractable in the typically high-dimensional latent spaces learned by world models. This addresses a practical bottleneck that often limits the application of rigorous control methods in deep learning-based robotic systems.
- **Enabling Reliable Robot Deployment:** By providing verifiable safety assurances, SLS² has the potential to accelerate the deployment of autonomous robots in safety-critical applications, such as industrial settings, healthcare, or human-robot interaction, where uncalibrated model errors or constraint violations can have severe consequences. It offers a framework for building confidence in data-driven controllers.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work lays a foundation for future research in several directions. The identified limitation regarding the planner's reliance on Euclidean distance to the latent goal suggests avenues for integrating learned temporal distance metrics or other manifold-aware planning techniques. Furthermore, extending the framework to handle more complex constraint types, online adaptation of uncertainty bounds, or even multi-agent safety could be impactful.

In conclusion, SLS² represents a step towards making robust visuomotor control a more practical and trustworthy reality by combining advanced machine learning for world modeling with principled control theory for safety verification.
