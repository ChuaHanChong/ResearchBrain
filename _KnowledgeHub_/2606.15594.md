# Pixels to Proofs: Probabilistically-Safe Latent World Model Control via Parallel Conformal Robust MPC

> [!summary] Summary
> The SLS² framework provides probabilistically safe robot control directly from pixel observations by integrating learned latent world models with conformal prediction for uncertainty quantification and GPU-accelerated robust Model Predictive Control (MPC). This approach achieves high task success rates and 100% safety in complex visuomotor tasks, including deformable object manipulation, in both simulation and hardware.

## Problem

- Autonomous systems in open-world environments often lack robust safety guarantees because acquiring precise state representations from perception is impractical.
- Traditional safety-critical control methods rely on known state representations and dynamics, which are frequently violated in complex, perception-driven robotic tasks.
- Learned latent world models offer powerful predictive capabilities from raw observations but struggle to provide formal, calibrated safety assurances for the true physical system due to high-dimensional latent states, imperfect dynamics, and complex constraint translation.

## Method

- A Le-WM-style joint-embedding predictive architecture learns compact Markovian latent representations and their dynamics from raw image observations, incorporating multi-step latent prediction and regularization losses.
- ==Conformal Prediction== is used to quantify two types of uncertainty: latent dynamics prediction errors (defining ellipsoidal error sets) and the 'in-domain' latent region, ensuring the planner operates within the training data's support.
- A GPU-accelerated Robust **MPC** framework, based on System Level Synthesis (SLS), synthesizes causal state-feedback policies by linearizing dynamics around a nominal trajectory and integrating CP-calibrated disturbance sets and robustly tightened latent safety constraints for probabilistic guarantees.

## Results

- SLS² consistently achieved higher nominal planning success rates than existing world-model baselines, including **93.75%** for simulated rope manipulation and **51.54%** for challenging contact-rich Push-T tasks.
- In robustly-constrained tasks, the framework demonstrated **100.00%** safety (e.g., avoiding obstacles in rope manipulation, respecting joint limits in Reacher) and high robust task completion rates (e.g., **88.57%** for rope manipulation), significantly outperforming safety-focused baselines.
- The nominal planner was successfully deployed on real hardware for bimanual rope manipulation, showcasing its applicability beyond simulation and demonstrating a clear correlation between latent space error and task space error convergence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining accurate learned latent world models, rigorous uncertainty quantification via conformal prediction, and robust control via System Level Synthesis effectively bridges the gap between learning-based perception and verifiable safety.
> - The use of Markovian latent states, multi-step dynamics losses, and specific regularization (SIGReg, Temporal Straightening) is crucial for training world models that are sufficiently predictive and amenable to gradient-based robust MPC.
> - GPU-accelerated SLS makes robust MPC computationally tractable for high-dimensional latent spaces, enabling real-time probabilistically safe control directly from pixel inputs with formal containment guarantees.
