# SIMPLE: Simulation-Based Policy Learning and Evaluation for Humanoid Loco-manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The USC Physical Superintelligence (PSI) Lab developed SIMPLE, a simulation-based testbed for humanoid loco-manipulation that integrates high-fidelity physics from MuJoCo with photorealistic rendering from Isaac Sim. This framework enables scalable data generation, improving efficiency to 310.3 demonstrations per hour for a pick-and-place task compared to 206.8 for real-robot teleoperation, and facilitates standardized evaluation, demonstrating strong sim-to-real correlation and successful zero-shot transfer for specific tasks.

## Problem

- A lack of standardized, scalable, and reproducible evaluation frameworks for complex whole-body loco-manipulation challenges the development of humanoid foundation models.
- Real-world humanoid robot evaluation is costly, time-consuming, difficult to reproduce, and poses safety risks, while existing simulation benchmarks are fragmented, lacking both accurate contact physics and photorealistic rendering for whole-body tasks.
- Collecting large-scale, high-quality demonstration data for generalist humanoid policies is challenging due to practical difficulties and safety concerns in real-world settings.

## Method

- Developed a ==hybrid simulation architecture== that decouples **MuJoCo** for high-frequency physics (500 Hz) and **Isaac Sim** for photorealistic, ray-traced rendering to combine accurate contact dynamics with visual diversity.
- Curated a large-scale and diverse environment comprising 60 distinct whole-body loco-manipulation tasks, 50 indoor scenes, and over 1,000 varied object assets.
- Provided scalable data collection pipelines through ==automated motion planning== (using CuRobo/BoDex) and a ==low-latency VR teleoperation== interface, along with a standardized evaluation protocol incorporating progressive levels of domain randomization.

## Results

- Benchmarked nine state-of-the-art policies, with Ψ0 consistently demonstrating strong performance across **six** loco-manipulation tasks and **three** domain-randomization levels.
- Sim teleoperation achieved a data collection throughput of **310.3** demonstrations per hour for a pick-and-place task, significantly outperforming real-robot teleoperation (**206.8** demos/hour) and motion planning (**58.9** demos/hour).
- Policies trained exclusively in SIMPLE demonstrated successful zero-shot transfer to physical humanoid robots, achieving real-world success rates of **0.80** for pick & place and **0.80** for handover tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - SIMPLE accurately predicts real-world policy performance, with observed strong correlations, validating its fidelity as a reliable simulation proxy for real-world humanoid evaluation.
> - Simulation-based teleoperation offers significantly higher efficiency for data collection (e.g., 310.3 demonstrations per hour) compared to real-robot methods, enabling rapid generation of large, diverse datasets.
> - Domain randomization during training is crucial for robust policy generalization to out-of-distribution visual conditions, and human teleoperation data captures more diverse and naturalistic motions beneficial for learning complex tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The field of robotics is experiencing rapid advancements in foundation models, which exhibit increasingly generalized behaviors. However, the evaluation of these models, particularly for complex humanoid robots, presents significant challenges. Existing simulation benchmarks, such as RLBench and LIBERO, have primarily focused on tabletop manipulation or wheeled robots, which simplifies the physical interactions and control requirements. These platforms often lack the necessary physics fidelity and environmental diversity to simulate whole-body humanoid loco-manipulation effectively.

Real-world evaluation of humanoid robots, while providing authentic data, is characterized by high costs, time-intensive procedures, and difficulties in reproducibility. This makes conducting fair, side-by-side comparisons of different policies challenging. Recent efforts to automate real-world evaluation, such as AutoEval and RoboArena, address some of these issues but face limitations in scalability and potential biases, especially for dynamic, full-body motions and scenarios where large-scale, in-domain data is scarce.

Simulation offers a scalable and reproducible alternative, but existing simulation environments for humanoids are fragmented. Some platforms, like those leveraging IsaacGym or IsaacLab (e.g., AMO, SONIC), excel in training robust locomotion policies through massively parallel reinforcement learning and often use MuJoCo for superior contact fidelity. However, these environments typically lack diverse object assets, complex indoor scenes, and the photorealistic rendering capabilities required to train and evaluate generalist vision-language-action (VLA) models. Conversely, platforms that provide high-fidelity rendering (e.g., those built on PhysX) may struggle with the accurate, high-frequency contact dynamics essential for stable humanoid locomotion and dexterous manipulation.

SIMPLE addresses this fragmentation by introducing a unified simulation testbed designed to bridge the gap between accurate contact-rich physics and photorealistic visual rendering. It aims to provide a comprehensive, full-stack environment that supports the entire lifecycle of humanoid policy development, from scalable data generation to standardized evaluation, thereby overcoming the limitations of both real-world testing and existing specialized simulation platforms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the development of SIMPLE stems from the recognized constraints in evaluating advanced humanoid foundation models. The authors identify several core problems that their work aims to address:

1. **Evaluation Bottleneck for Humanoid Foundation Models:** Despite rapid progress in developing humanoid foundation models capable of generalist behaviors, their scaling is fundamentally limited by the absence of a standardized, scalable, and reproducible evaluation framework.
2. **Limitations of Real-World Evaluation:** Current real-world testing of humanoid policies is expensive, time-consuming, and notoriously difficult to reproduce. This hinders fair comparisons between different policies, makes iterative development challenging, and increases operational complexity due to dynamic full-body motions, heavy hardware, and safety risks.
3. **Fragmentation of Existing Simulation Benchmarks:** Existing simulation platforms are specialized, focusing either on tabletop manipulation (neglecting locomotion and whole-body balancing) or pure locomotion (lacking diverse object interactions and complex visual scenes). There is no comprehensive benchmark that integrates the complexities of whole-body loco-manipulation with both accurate physics and photorealistic rendering.
4. **Lack of Scalable Data Collection for Humanoids:** Training generalist humanoid policies requires vast amounts of high-quality demonstration data. Collecting such data in the real world is challenging due to the practical difficulties, safety concerns, and human operator fatigue associated with operating complex humanoid robots.

To address these motivations, the authors set forth the following key objectives for SIMPLE:

1. **Develop a Unified Simulation Testbed:** Create a comprehensive, full-stack simulation infrastructure that standardizes the evaluation and training of humanoid policies, encompassing whole-body loco-manipulation.
2. **Integrate High-Fidelity Physics and Photorealistic Rendering:** Combine the accurate contact-rich dynamics of MuJoCo for physics simulation with the photorealistic ray-traced rendering capabilities of Isaac Sim to create a high-fidelity environment that faithfully represents real-world conditions.
3. **Construct a Large-Scale, Diverse Environment:** Design and curate a vast environment featuring 60 diverse whole-body tasks, 50 distinct indoor scenes (from the HSSD dataset), and over 1,000 varied object assets (from GraspNet-1B and Objaverse) to ensure sufficient diversity for robust policy training and evaluation.
4. **Facilitate Scalable Data Collection:** Provide built-in, efficient data generation pipelines, including automated trajectory generation via motion planning and a low-latency VR teleoperation interface, to reduce the engineering overhead and accelerate the collection of high-quality, whole-body demonstration data.
5. **Enable Standardized Policy Integration and Benchmarking:** Offer a unified interface for integrating and benchmarking mainstream humanoid policies, including large vision-language-action (VLA) models and world-action models (WAMs), allowing for systematic comparison across different architectures.
6. **Demonstrate Sim-to-Real Correlation and Transferability:** Validate the fidelity of the simulation by showing a strong correlation between policy performance in SIMPLE and in the real world, and demonstrate the capability for zero-shot transfer of policies trained in simulation to physical humanoid robots.
7. **Open-Source the Framework:** Make the entire codebase publicly available to the community to foster collaborative research and accelerate progress in humanoid robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SIMPLE's methodology is structured around a hybrid simulation architecture, large-scale asset curation, scalable data generation, and a standardized evaluation protocol.

**System Architecture:**
The core of SIMPLE is a dual-simulator architecture that strictly decouples physical simulation from visual rendering.
- **Physics Engine (MuJoCo):** MuJoCo is utilized for all rigid-body dynamics, contact resolution, and robot control. It operates at a high frequency (500 Hz) to ensure robust and accurate physical interactions critical for humanoid locomotion and manipulation.
- **Rendering Engine (Isaac Sim):** Isaac Sim handles photorealistic, ray-traced rendering. At each simulation step, the physical states (joint positions, object poses) from MuJoCo are synchronized to Isaac Sim, which then generates high-quality visual observations. This separation leverages MuJoCo's contact fidelity and Isaac Sim's visual diversity.
- **Control Scheme:** A decoupled whole-body control scheme is implemented. High-level policies (e.g., VLAs) predict kinematic trajectories for the upper body and navigation commands for the base. These commands are then passed to a state-of-the-art lower-body tracking controller (e.g., AMO, SONIC) which operates at a higher frequency to maintain balance and execute locomotion.
- **API Integration:** The entire simulation loop is wrapped in a standard OpenAI Gym interface to ensure compatibility with existing reinforcement learning workflows.

**Large-Scale Asset Curation and Task Design:**
To support diverse learning and evaluation, a comprehensive library of assets was curated:
- **Objects:** Over 1,500 diverse objects were imported from Objaverse and 53 objects from GraspNet-1B.
- **Preprocessing:** All object meshes undergo convex decomposition using CoACD to generate accurate collision geometries for MuJoCo. Concurrently, assets are converted to USD format with high-resolution textures for Isaac Sim rendering.
- **Environments:** 50 complete indoor scenes from the HSSD dataset are integrated, providing varied backdrops for tasks.
- **Tasks:** 60 distinct whole-body loco-manipulation tasks were designed, covering rigid object pick-and-place, non-prehensile interactions, and articulated object manipulation.

**Scalable Data Collection Pipelines:**
SIMPLE provides two built-in pipelines for generating large-scale demonstration data:
- **Automated Motion Planning:** This pipeline uses CuRobo for scalable trajectory generation. Objects are first dropped in MuJoCo to determine stable resting poses. BoDex then synthesizes feasible grasp configurations. A scripted policy decomposes high-level objectives into atomic actions. CuRobo generates kinematic trajectories for dual-arm manipulation, while a scripted lower-body policy coordinates base movements to reach targets.
- **Low-Latency VR Teleoperation:** For complex dexterous manipulation, a human teleoperation pipeline is integrated. During data collection, Isaac Sim rendering is disabled, and MuJoCo's native renderer streams egocentric stereo video to a PICO XR headset. The operator's hand movements are retargeted to the humanoid's upper body via inverse kinematics, while a whole-body tracking policy (e.g., AMO, SONIC) autonomously manages robot balance and locomotion based on joystick inputs, reducing cognitive load. Demonstrations are recorded at 50 Hz and exported to the standardized LeRobot format.

**Offline Rendering and Domain Randomization:**
To bridge the visual sim-to-real gap, trajectories collected through either pipeline are replayed offline in Isaac Sim. During this replay, extensive domain randomization is applied, including:
- Randomization of object instances, initial poses, tabletop textures (using NVIDIA vMaterials), lighting conditions, camera viewpoints, and language instructions.
- This process generates high-quality RGB observations under diverse visual conditions, which are crucial for training robust vision-language-action policies.

**Policy Integration and Evaluation Protocol:**
A standardized protocol is defined for policy evaluation across three progressively difficult levels:
- **Level 0:** Introduction of random distractor objects into the scene.
- **Level 1:** Additional visual randomization through altering object materials and environmental lighting.
- **Level 2:** Spatial randomization, varying the initial positions of target objects and the robot itself.
- **Policy Representation:** The framework uses a 32-dimensional proprioceptive state (14 DoF hands, 14 DoF arms, waist roll-pitch-yaw, and base height) and a 36-dimensional action space (32-dimensional upper-body targets and 4-dimensional locomotion commands).
- **Benchmarking:** SIMPLE natively supports the Ψ0 training stack and integrates mainstream VLA architectures (Ψ0, π0.5, GR00T-N1.6) and World-Action-Models (WAMs) like DreamZero, as well as imitation learning baselines (Diffusion Policy, Action Chunk Transformers). Over 6,000 episodes were rendered to facilitate large-scale benchmarking.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted using SIMPLE yielded several key findings regarding the benchmark's utility, scalability, and fidelity.

**Large-Scale Benchmarking of Humanoid Policies:**
The study benchmarked nine state-of-the-art policies (Ψ0, GR00T N1.6, π0.5, InternVLA, H-RDT, DreamZero, EgoVLA, DP, ACT) across six representative whole-body loco-manipulation tasks and three domain-randomization levels.
- **Performance Trends:** Ψ0 consistently demonstrated strong performance across most tasks, leading the benchmark. DreamZero and π0.5 also showed robust generalization, particularly at Level 2, although they exhibited limitations in tasks requiring highly precise base movements. The relatively smaller ACT model achieved strong overall performance, which the authors attribute to its data efficiency when trained on the less noisy SIMPLE dataset compared to real-world teleoperation data.
- **Sim-to-Real Correlation:** A critical finding was the strong correlation observed between policy performance rankings in SIMPLE and those reported in prior real-world experiments. This suggests that SIMPLE serves as a faithful proxy for evaluating policy effectiveness in real-world scenarios.

**Data Collection Efficiency in the Simulator:**
The efficiency of three data collection paradigms—Motion Planning (MP) in simulation, Real-robot teleoperation, and Sim teleoperation—was compared across three representative tasks.
- **Motion Planning:** MP achieved the lowest throughput due to optimization challenges in high-dimensional spaces, but it operates autonomously without human intervention or hardware resets.
- **Real-Robot Teleoperation:** This method provided higher efficiency than MP and captured more natural whole-body motions.
- **Sim Teleoperation:** This approach was significantly faster than both MP and real-robot teleoperation. For instance, on a whole-body pick-and-place task (T1), Sim teleoperation achieved 310.3 demonstrations per hour, compared to 206.8 for real-robot teleoperation and 58.9 for motion planning. This efficiency gain is attributed to the elimination of physical robot reset overhead, safety constraints, and hardware maintenance. The inherent scalability of simulation further enables infinite scaling of collected trajectories across diverse environments and visual conditions via offline rendering.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Domain Randomization and Data Scaling:** Ablations on Ψ0 fine-tuned for 2,000 steps demonstrated the impact of training data composition. Policies trained solely on Level 0 data performed well in-distribution (0.80 success) but generalized poorly to harder visual conditions (0.50 success at Eval Set 2). Mixing Level 0 and Level 1 training data significantly improved out-of-distribution robustness (0.80 success at Eval Set 2) while maintaining in-distribution performance. This finding supports the necessity of diverse visual conditions during training for robust generalization. Furthermore, increasing the volume of teleoperation data was shown to improve performance across all validation sets.
- **Data Source Quality:** Comparing policies trained on motion-planning-only data versus teleoperation-only data (using Ψ0) across three task families indicated that human teleoperation data generally led to better performance (average 7.56 vs. 5.00 success rate). This is attributed to the more diverse and naturalistic motion profiles captured through human demonstrations, which are beneficial for learning complex tasks.

**Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
The research investigated the transferability of policies trained exclusively on SIMPLE data to the real world.
- **Successful Transfer:** A Ψ0 policy fine-tuned entirely within SIMPLE demonstrated zero-shot generalization to physical humanoid robots for specific loco-manipulation tasks (e.g., Pick & Place: 0.90 success in sim, 0.80 in real; Handover: 1.00 in sim, 0.80 in real) under similar environmental settings. This result indicates that the high-fidelity physics and photorealistic rendering provided by SIMPLE are sufficient to bridge the sim-to-real gap for a subset of loco-manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SIMPLE simulation framework represents a notable contribution to the field of humanoid robotics, with several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Standardization of Humanoid Policy Evaluation:** SIMPLE provides a unified, comprehensive, and reproducible testbed for humanoid loco-manipulation. By defining a clear evaluation protocol and integrating diverse tasks and environments, it establishes a standardized benchmark that can facilitate fair comparisons and accelerate progress in humanoid foundation models. This addresses a critical gap where existing benchmarks were either fragmented or did not adequately cover the complexities of whole-body humanoid control.

2. **Acceleration of Research and Development:** By offering a scalable and reproducible environment, SIMPLE mitigates the inherent challenges of real-world humanoid robot experimentation, such as high costs, time consumption, and difficulties in reproducibility. Researchers can now rapidly iterate on policy designs, conduct extensive experiments, and benchmark models efficiently, thereby accelerating the development cycle for new humanoid control strategies and foundation models.

3. **Enhanced Data Generation Capabilities:** The integrated data collection pipelines—both automated motion planning and low-latency VR teleoperation—significantly reduce the cost and effort associated with acquiring high-quality demonstration data for humanoid robots. This is crucial for training data-hungry foundation models, as it enables the generation of large, diverse datasets without the physical constraints, safety risks, and operational overhead of real-world teleoperation.

4. **Validation of Simulation Fidelity for Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The demonstrated strong correlation between policy performance in SIMPLE and real-world outcomes, alongside successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for certain tasks, validates the fidelity of the hybrid simulation architecture. This suggests that SIMPLE can serve as a reliable platform for developing policies that are directly deployable on physical robots, minimizing the need for extensive real-world fine-tuning. This capability is pivotal for translating research findings into practical robotic applications.

5. **Enabling Generalist Humanoid Robotics:** The large-scale environment, comprising 60 diverse tasks, over 1,000 objects, and 50 indoor scenes, provides a rich testing ground for generalist humanoid policies. By challenging models across a wide spectrum of loco-manipulation abilities, SIMPLE contributes to the development of more robust, adaptive, and broadly capable humanoid robots.

6. **Community Resource and Collaboration:** The commitment to open-sourcing the entire codebase positions SIMPLE as a foundational resource for the global robotics research community. This initiative is expected to foster collaboration, enable broader access to advanced simulation tools, and facilitate further innovation in humanoid robotics.

Despite its contributions, the paper acknowledges certain limitations that indicate future research directions. The computational expense of Isaac Sim's photorealistic ray-tracing pipeline (approximately 4 frames per second per GPU) currently limits large-scale dataset generation throughput. Additionally, the reliance on a rigid-body assumption in the current simulation pipeline restricts the faithful simulation of deformable and soft-body objects (e.g., cloth, rope, food items), which are common in real-world human environments and crucial for more advanced manipulation tasks. Addressing these limitations could further enhance the capabilities and realism of future iterations of the SIMPLE framework.
