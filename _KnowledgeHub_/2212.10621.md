# Full-Body Articulated Human-Object Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University and BIGAI introduced CHAIRS, a comprehensive multi-view RGB-D dataset for full-body articulated human-object interaction (f-AHOI), and developed an interaction-aware model for articulated object pose estimation. The model achieves a mean rotation error of 19.35° and a Chamfer Distance of 72.30 mm, outperforming existing methods by leveraging fine-grained geometric relationships and a learned interaction prior.

## Problem

- A scarcity of comprehensive f-AHOI datasets, as existing 3D HOI datasets often simplify interactions to rigid objects or hand-centric engagement.
- The diverse and complex kinematic structures of articulated objects, which pose challenges for models relying on uniform structure assumptions for accurate reconstruction.
- The intricacies of complex full-body interactions, characterized by heavy occlusions and dense contact points, making traditional pose estimation methods insufficient.

## Method

- Introduced the **CHAIRS** dataset, a multi-view RGB-D collection spanning 17.3 hours of human interactions with 81 sittable objects, including 28 articulated ones with diverse kinematic structures.
- Developed a two-stage interaction-aware model for articulated object pose estimation that combines initial object reconstruction with a subsequent pose optimization.
- Integrated a learned ==interaction prior== using a conditional Variational Auto-Encoder (cVAE) to capture conditional object occupancy given human shape, guiding the pose optimization process implicitly.

## Results

- The proposed model achieved a mean rotation error of **19.35°**, a translation error of **66.23mm**, and a Chamfer Distance (CD) of **72.30mm**, representing a notable improvement over state-of-the-art baselines for articulated object pose estimation.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that removing the interaction prior or contrastive loss resulted in significant performance degradation, with CD increasing from **72.30mm** to **87.90mm** without the prior.
- The CHAIRS dataset enabled a 3D conditional diffusion model to generate more nuanced and natural human poses interacting with articulated objects, showcasing its utility for downstream interaction generation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A learned interaction prior, especially when bolstered by a contrastive loss, is critical for achieving accurate articulated object pose estimation in complex interaction scenarios.
> - Comprehensive full-body interaction datasets like CHAIRS are essential for training models that can generate realistic and nuanced human poses interacting with articulated objects.
> - The quality of human pose estimation directly influences the accuracy of articulated object pose estimation, highlighting the interconnectedness of human and object understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of computer vision and robotics broadly seeks to comprehend human activities, with Human-Object Interaction (HOI) being a central component. Research in HOI has evolved from 2D image-based detection to 3D interaction reconstruction and generation within 3D scenes. Many existing 3D HOI datasets and models, such as GRAB, BEHAVE, and COUCH, primarily focus on interactions with rigid objects or specific body parts, like hands (e.g., ARCTIC), thereby simplifying the complexities of real-world human interactions. While some work addresses Articulated Human-Object Interaction (AHOI) with objects having movable parts (e.g., D3D-HOI, ARCTIC), these efforts often concentrate on hand-object interactions or assume uniform kinematic structures within object categories.

The current work addresses a gap in the literature by focusing on "Full-Body Articulated Human-Object Interaction (f-AHOI)," where entire human bodies interact with a diverse range of articulated objects. This area presents challenges due to the varied kinematic structures of articulated objects and the complex spatial-temporal relationships involving full-body contact and occlusions. Existing methods for articulated object pose estimation often assume consistent kinematic structures, which limits their applicability to the heterogeneous objects encountered in real-world scenarios.

This paper aims to bridge these gaps by introducing a comprehensive dataset specifically designed for f-AHOI and proposing a methodology that can estimate articulated object poses under the complex conditions of full-body interaction, without strong prior assumptions about object kinematics or contact maps. It positions itself to advance the understanding of finer-grained human-object interactions beyond the limitations of previous datasets and models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the complex challenge of Full-Body Articulated Human-Object Interaction (f-AHOI), specifically focusing on the task of object pose estimation within this context. The authors identify three principal challenges that motivate their work:

1. **The Dearth of Comprehensive f-AHOI Datasets:** Previous 3D HOI datasets often simplify real-world interactions by either assuming rigid objects or confining interactions to specific body parts (e.g., hands). This simplification overlooks the common reality of human whole-body engagement with articulated objects like chairs or cabinets, which possess interconnected movable joints. A richer, more intricate dataset is needed to capture these genuine full-body interactions.
2. **The Multifaceted Landscape of Object Kinematic Structures:** Articulated objects exhibit significant diversity in their kinematic frameworks, even within the same category. Existing methodologies frequently rely on assumptions of uniform structures, which limits their applicability to the wide array of real-world objects. The accurate reconstruction of objects with divergent geometries and structures remains a challenge.
3. **The Intricacies of Complex Interactions:** Interactions with articulated objects involve complex spatial and physical relationships, often characterized by heavy occlusions and dense contact points. Traditional pose estimation mechanisms, such as those relying on point cloud template-matching, are deemed insufficient under these conditions. Furthermore, slight inaccuracies in reconstruction can lead to physically implausible interactions, highlighting the need for methods that account for these complexities.

To address these challenges, the authors propose three corresponding solutions:

1. **Introduction of the CHAIRS Dataset:** To overcome the scarcity of f-AHOI datasets, they introduce "Capturing Human and Articulated-object InteRactionS" (CHAIRS), a multi-view RGB-D dataset. CHAIRS captures diverse interactions between 46 participants and 81 sittable objects (28 of which are articulated), providing 3D meshes of both humans and objects across 17.3 hours of recordings.
2. **Diverse Kinematic Structures in CHAIRS:** To navigate kinematic diversity, CHAIRS includes objects ranging from rigid stools to swivel chairs with up to seven movable components, each linked by revolute, prismatic, or composite joints. This heterogeneity aims to support models capable of handling various parts and kinematics.
3. **Innovative Articulated Object Pose Estimation Approach:** To unravel the enigma of complex interactions, the authors propose a novel articulated object pose estimation approach. This method leverages fine-grained interaction relationships by melding a reconstruction model with a learned interaction prior, implemented as a conditional Variational Auto-Encoder (cVAE). This approach aims to reconstruct objects by implicitly understanding interaction patterns, circumventing the need for explicit, manually labeled contact maps. The significance of these relationships is further demonstrated in a downstream task of human pose generation conditioned on articulated objects.

The overall contributions are four-fold: (i) the CHAIRS dataset, (ii) the extension of articulated object pose estimation to f-AHOI, (iii) an object pose estimation approach that is designed to transcend structural restrictions, and (iv) an interaction prior intended to capture the subtleties of fine-grained interactions to facilitate pose estimation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers around the development of the CHAIRS dataset and an interaction-aware model for articulated object pose estimation.

#### 3.1. The CHAIRS Dataset

CHAIRS is designed to provide comprehensive 3D annotations for f-AHOI.
- **Overview:** It comprises 1390 sequences (17.3 hours) of humans interacting with 81 sittable objects (chairs, sofas, stools, benches), of which 28 are articulated. Each object was explored by 6 participants, each performing 3 interaction sequences from a pool of 32 diverse high-level actions (e.g., shifting a stool, reclining on a sofa).
- **Object Diversity:** Objects were selected for stylistic and kinematic diversity. 3D meshes were captured using an iPad Pro and refined manually, then segmented into eight functional parts.
- **Data Collection Setup:**
 - **Camera Setup:** Interactions were captured in a 5m x 4m controlled laboratory area using four calibrated and synchronized Kinect Azure DK cameras for multi-view RGB-D data.
 - **Hybrid MoCap System:** A commercial inertial-optical hybrid MoCap system was used for precise ground-truth poses of humans and objects. This system integrates 5 hybrid trackers (4 optical markers + 1 IMU) and 17 wearable IMUs on participants, alongside hybrid trackers on movable object parts.
 - **Capturing Articulated Object Poses:** Hybrid trackers were affixed to each movable component of an object in its canonical pose. Relative transformations were calculated, and real-time 6D poses of each part were computed. Rigid parts were matched to the object's kinematic structure.
 - **Capturing Human Body Poses:** Participants wore a MoCap suit with 17 IMUs, MoCap gloves, and 5 hybrid trackers on their heads, hands, and feet. The SMPL-X model was used, with shape parameters optimized to align the reconstructed mesh with hybrid tracker positions.
- **Post-processing:**
 - **Data Alignment:** Spatial alignment of Kinect and MoCap data was achieved using a custom ICP algorithm based on plane-to-plane correspondences. Temporal alignment was performed using time-lagged cross-correlation (TLCC) on the height differentials of head and hands.
 - **Penetration Removal:** To address unrealistic contacts and penetrations, an optimization algorithm was applied. This involved computing penetration depths, aggregating maximum penetration depth and direction to human skeleton joints using SMPL-X's linear-blend-skinning weights, calculating a target skeleton, and then using gradient-based optimization to adjust the human model. This reduced average penetration depth from 3.5 cm to 2.6 cm.
 - **Data Quality & Privacy:** Chamfer distances of 2.8 cm for objects and 1.9 cm for humans were reported post-processing. Participant faces were blurred for privacy.

#### 3.2. Articulated Object Pose Estimation Model

The proposed model estimates object pose in f-AHOI by leveraging fine-grained geometric relationships and learned interaction priors. It consists of two stages: object reconstruction and pose initialization, followed by pose optimization.

- **Task Definition:** Given an image *I*, a parameterized human model *H* (SMPL-X), and object meshes *X* with *N* parts, the task is to estimate the object pose *O*, which includes the root 6D pose and 6D poses for each part. Rotations are represented using the orthogonal 6D representation.
- **Model Architecture:** The framework uses an interaction-aware approach (Figure 5).
 - **Object Reconstruction and Pose Initialization:**
 - An off-the-shelf estimator provides initial human pose and shape (SMPL-X parameters).
 - The estimated human shape is voxelized at four resolutions.
 - ResNet-101 extracts image features. These features, combined with the human voxels at each resolution, feed into a 3D decoder.
 - The 3D decoder (three 3DConvT layers, upsampling, two 1x1 3DConv layers) estimates the object occupancy grid *V_O*.
 - An MLP concatenates image features with SMPL-X parameters to regress the object's root 6D pose (*R_0*, *T_0*), used for initialization.
 - Training involves fine-tuning a pre-trained PARE model on CHAIRS for human shape estimation, then fixing its weights and training the reconstruction model with an L1 loss on object voxels.
 - **Interaction Prior:**
 - A conditional Variational Auto-Encoder (cVAE) learns the conditional distribution of object occupancy given the human shape (Figure 6).
 - It uses 3DConvNets for its encoder and decoder. Multi-resolution human voxels condition both components.
 - The latent Gaussian distribution (*μ*, *σ*) for the latent code *z* is estimated by an MLP.
 - The training loss for the prior model includes standard reconstruction and KL divergence losses, a penetration loss (penalizing voxels occupied by both), and a contrastive loss (L_contra). L_contra maximizes the distance of latent variables between original and augmented noisy data (perturbed objects) to guide unreasonable pairs away from the distribution centroid.
 - **Pose Optimization with Interaction Prior:**
 - This stage refines object poses using kinematic insights and the learned interaction prior (Figure 7).
 - The object's CAD model (URDF), estimated SMPL-X parameters, and initial object voxels from the reconstruction model are used.
 - The object model parameters (*R*, *T*, joint parameters *Φ*) are iteratively updated by minimizing a combined objective *J = J_recon + J_z*.
 - *J_recon* measures the distance between the voxelized object model and the estimated object voxels.
 - *J_z* encourages the latent code predicted by the cVAE encoder to have a small norm, regulating proximity to the interaction prior.
 - Optimization is performed via gradient descent. To ensure differentiability for calculating losses involving voxelization, trilinear interpolation is used on affined voxel grids.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents quantitative and qualitative results, including ablation studies and an application demonstrating the utility of CHAIRS.

- **Quantitative Performance:**
 - The proposed model demonstrates substantial improvements in object pose estimation and shape reconstruction compared to existing methods (Table 2).
 - In the *without optimization (w/o opt)* setting (where the object structure is unknown), the model achieves a Chamfer Distance (CD) of 160.2 mm, outperforming LASR (205.2 mm).
 - In the *with optimization (w/ opt)* setting (where object structure is known), the model significantly surpasses baselines such as CHORE, D3D-HOI, ANCSH, and PHOSA across all evaluated metrics. It achieves a mean rotation error of 19.35°, a translation error of 66.23 mm, a CD of 72.30 mm, and an IoU of 21.57%. These metrics represent improvements over the next best baseline (CHORE: 21.82°, 87.58 mm, 95.40 mm, 16.44%, respectively).
 - The model also exhibits lower penetration depths (1.143 mm) and comparable contact values (1.562 mm) compared to other methods (e.g., CHORE: 1.050 mm, 1.742 mm).

- **Qualitative Results:**
 - Visualizations (Figure 8 a-h) show that the model produces plausible and accurate interaction representations even before the optimization process, with optimization refining finer interaction details.
 - The model qualitatively generalizes to "in-the-wild" internet images (Figure 8 i-p, Figure A6), indicating robustness beyond controlled lab settings.

- **Ablation Studies (Table 3):**
 - **Removal of Interaction Prior (`- prior`):** A substantial performance drop was observed in both settings, highlighting the critical role of the interaction prior in accurate object pose estimation. For instance, in the `w/ opt` setting, CD increased from 72.30 mm to 87.90 mm. The prior appears to influence object-human proximity.
 - **Removal of Contrastive Loss (`- contr.`):** The results were analogous to removing the prior, underscoring the importance of the contrastive loss in learning a robust interaction prior. CD increased from 72.30 mm to 87.28 mm.
 - **Removal of Interaction Awareness (`- inter.`):** Eliminating the concatenation of human voxels in the 3DConv layers led to a modest degradation across object reconstruction metrics (CD increased from 72.30 mm to 78.12 mm) but resulted in increased contact values and decreased penetration, suggesting that interaction awareness contributes to bringing the human and object into closer proximity.

- **Human Pose Estimation Quality Ablation (Table 4):**
 - The accuracy of human pose estimation directly correlates with the quality of object pose estimation. Using ground-truth human poses significantly improved object pose estimation (CD 65.50 mm, IoU 23.16%), validating the hypothesis that human pose guidance is beneficial.

- **Failure Cases:**
 - The model encounters difficulties in accurately estimating the orientation of object parts when they exhibit rotation symmetry (e.g., stool bases, round seats), as multiple orientations can lead to geometrically similar outcomes (Figure 9).
 - Performance diminishes in scenarios lacking f-AHOI (e.g., human far from object), as the model cannot leverage interaction cues for pose estimation.

- **Application: Generating Interacting Humans:**
 - As a downstream task, a 3D conditional diffusion model trained on CHAIRS generated more nuanced and natural human poses interacting with articulated objects compared to a model trained on COUCH (a rigid chair dataset) (Figure 10, Figure A3). This demonstrates the value of CHAIRS for learning complex interaction patterns.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work contributes to the field of computer vision and robotics in several ways, with potential implications for various applications:

- **Advancement in HOI Research:** By focusing on Full-Body Articulated Human-Object Interaction (f-AHOI), this research addresses a complex and under-explored area within HOI, moving beyond simplified rigid object or hand-centric interactions.
- **Novel Dataset CHAIRS:** The introduction of CHAIRS is a significant contribution. This extensive, multi-view RGB-D dataset, with its diverse range of articulated sittable objects and detailed 3D meshes of full-body human-object interactions, directly addresses the existing data scarcity for f-AHOI. Its high quality (low Chamfer distances for both human and object reconstructions) and detailed annotations (e.g., kinematic structures) make it a valuable resource for training and evaluating models for fine-grained interaction understanding.
- **Improved Articulated Object Pose Estimation:** The proposed interaction-aware model advances the state-of-the-art in articulated object pose estimation, particularly in challenging scenarios involving heavy occlusion and dense contact. Its ability to leverage implicit geometric relationships and learned interaction priors, rather than relying on explicit contact maps or assumptions of uniform kinematic structures, demonstrates a more flexible and robust approach.
- **Enabling Downstream Applications:** The research demonstrates that the CHAIRS dataset and the proposed methodologies can facilitate various downstream tasks. The application in human pose generation shows that models trained on CHAIRS can learn and generate more realistic and nuanced human interactions with articulated objects, which could benefit areas like virtual reality, animation, and human-robot collaboration. Other potential applications include interaction detection, motion generation, physics-based analysis, and language-guided motion generation.
- **Addressing Kinematic Diversity:** The explicit inclusion of objects with diverse kinematic structures in CHAIRS, and the model's design to handle this heterogeneity, is a step towards more generalizable HOI systems that can operate in varied real-world environments.

In summary, this work provides both a critical dataset and an effective methodology for advancing the understanding and modeling of complex, full-body interactions with articulated objects, which has implications for developing more intelligent artificial agents and systems that interact seamlessly with the human world.
