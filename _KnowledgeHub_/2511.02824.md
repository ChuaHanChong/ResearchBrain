# Kosmos: An AI Scientist for Autonomous Discovery

> [!summary] Summary
> Edison Scientific Inc. developed Kosmos, an AI scientist leveraging a multi-agent architecture and a structured world model to conduct autonomous, data-driven scientific discovery across diverse fields. The system achieves up to 79.4% accuracy in its scientific claims and performs research equivalent to over six months of human effort per run, demonstrating the ability to generate verified insights and novel analytical methods.

## Problem

- Prior AI agents for scientific discovery struggle with maintaining coherence and achieving depth in findings over extended, multi-step tasks.
- Traditional scientific research processes are labor-intensive and slow, involving iterative cycles of literature review, data analysis, and hypothesis generation.
- There is a need for AI systems that can conduct comprehensive, data-driven scientific inquiry across diverse domains without human bias or limited bandwidth.

## Method

- A ==multi-agent== AI system, **Kosmos**, featuring a ==structured world model== that synthesizes and stores information from various specialized agents.
- Utilizes parallel ==Data Analysis Agents== for computational experiments and ==Literature Search Agents== for extensive literature review within iterative discovery cycles.
- Generates scientific reports with ==direct traceability==, linking every statement and figure to its originating code or primary literature to ensure verifiability.

## Results

- Kosmos achieved an overall accuracy of **79.4%** for statements in its scientific reports, reaching **85.5%** for data analysis-based claims.
- Each run performed research equivalent to **6.14 months** of human effort, with collaborators confirming a linear scaling of valuable scientific findings with Kosmos cycles.
- The system generated seven diverse discoveries, including reproducing unpublished work, corroborating preprints, developing novel analytical methods, and identifying clinically relevant mechanisms like entorhinal cortex vulnerability in aging.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A structured world model is essential for AI agents to maintain coherence and enable deep, sustained scientific exploration over hundreds of agent actions.
> - Orchestrating parallel data analysis and literature search agents allows for comprehensive and iterative refinement of scientific understanding.
> - Integrating traceability directly into AI-generated scientific reports, linking claims to specific code or literature, enhances rigor and reproducibility in computational science.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of AI agents for automating scientific discovery is a rapidly advancing frontier, driven by the increasing complexity and volume of data in modern research. This paper situates Kosmos within this landscape by acknowledging significant prior progress while highlighting the inherent limitations of existing systems. The authors explicitly state that previous AI agents, despite their advancements, "remain limited in the number of actions they can take before losing coherence, thus limiting the depth of their findings." This "coherence" challenge—maintaining focus and logical progression over extended, multi-step tasks—is a critical bottleneck that Kosmos aims to address.

The paper contrasts Kosmos with several notable contemporary systems:
- **Robin:** A previous system from many of the same Edison Scientific authors, Robin performed automated literature search and data analysis for therapeutics development. Kosmos represents a significant evolutionary leap, achieving a 9.8x increase in code generation, indicating enhanced capabilities and expanded scope.
- **Sakana’s AI Scientist:** This system autonomously forms hypotheses, conducts computational experiments, and even drafts/reviews manuscripts, but its application is specifically limited to machine learning research. Kosmos, in contrast, targets a wide range of scientific disciplines.
- **Google’s AI co-scientist:** While capable of iterative reasoning and hypothesis generation, this system does not perform or analyze experiments, a crucial component that Kosmos integrates.
- **The Virtual Lab:** Demonstrated success in designing novel nanobodies, but the authors note its lack of exploratory data analysis capabilities and potential domain limitations.

Kosmos carves out a unique niche by tackling the "depth" and "coherence" problem directly through its innovative use of a structured world model. This architectural choice enables it to orchestrate numerous parallel agent activities over extended periods, leading to more profound and sustained scientific investigations. By integrating parallel data analysis, literature search, and hypothesis generation within a coherent framework, and making its reasoning fully traceable, Kosmos pushes the boundaries towards a more comprehensive, autonomous, and general-purpose "AI scientist" that can operate across diverse, data-rich scientific domains. This positions Kosmos as a significant step forward in the quest for fully automated, open-ended scientific discovery, aiming to augment human researchers rather than merely assisting them in isolated tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Kosmos is to **automate data-driven scientific discovery**. This overarching goal is motivated by the inherent challenges and inefficiencies of traditional scientific research, which relies on arduous and iterative cycles of literature review, hypothesis generation, experimental design, data analysis, and result interpretation.

The specific objectives and motivations underpinning Kosmos's development can be broken down as follows:

- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing AI Agents:** The central motivation is to address the acknowledged shortcomings of prior AI agents, which struggle with maintaining coherence and achieving depth in their findings over extended research campaigns. Kosmos aims to rectify this by enabling longer, more complex, and more integrated discovery processes.
- **Enabling Parallel and Iterative Discovery:** The authors sought to create a system that could simultaneously perform various facets of scientific inquiry—data analysis, literature search, and hypothesis generation—and iteratively refine its understanding based on the gathered information. This parallel processing and iterative learning are crucial for accelerating discovery.
- **Achieving Deep and Coherent Exploration:** By introducing a structured world model, a core objective was to allow information sharing and synthesis between multiple specialized agents. This design decision aims to ensure that Kosmos remains focused on its objective and builds a cumulative understanding, leading to deeper, more interconnected findings over hundreds of agent actions.
- **Generating Traceable and Reproducible Scientific Reports:** A key motivation was to instill rigor and transparency in AI-generated science. Kosmos is designed to produce scientific reports where every statement and figure is directly cited with its originating code or primary literature. This ensures that its reasoning is verifiable and reproducible, aligning with fundamental scientific principles.
- **Broadening Domain Applicability:** Unlike some specialized AI scientists, Kosmos was developed to be a general-purpose system applicable across a wide array of scientific disciplines. This objective reflects a vision for an AI tool that can assist researchers regardless of their specific field, provided they have data.
- **Quantifying and Demonstrating Value:** The authors were motivated to empirically measure Kosmos's performance in terms of accuracy, efficiency, and the scientific value of its discoveries. This included assessing the accuracy of its claims, estimating the equivalent human research time it saves, and evaluating the novelty and depth of its findings through expert review.
- **Augmenting Human Scientists:** Ultimately, the motivation is not to replace human scientists but to "augment and accelerate their work." Kosmos is envisioned as a powerful tool that can handle the labor-intensive, iterative tasks of data analysis and literature synthesis, thereby freeing human researchers to focus on higher-level critical thinking, experimental design, and the interpretation of complex, often unexpected, findings.

In essence, Kosmos is driven by the ambition to unlock new frontiers of data-driven discovery by creating an AI scientist that is not only capable of performing complex research tasks but also maintains scientific rigor, transparency, and broad applicability, thereby transforming the pace and nature of scientific inquiry.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Kosmos is presented as an "AI scientist" designed for autonomous data-driven discovery, operating through a sophisticated multi-agent architecture centered around a structured world model. The methodology emphasizes iterative learning, parallel processing, and rigorous traceability.

**Input and Initialization:**
The process begins with a human scientist providing Kosmos with two essential inputs: an **open-ended research objective** and a **dataset**. The nature of the objective can be broad, and the dataset is typically pre-processed, well-formatted, and clearly labeled.

**Core Architecture and Iterative Cycles:**
Kosmos operates in iterative discovery cycles, which can run for up to 12 hours. Within each cycle, it orchestrates multiple parallel operations using specialized agents:
1. **Parallel Task Execution:** Kosmos simultaneously launches several instances (up to ten per cycle) of two general-purpose Edison Scientific agents:
 - **Data Analysis Agent:** This agent is responsible for executing computational experiments and analyzing data. It generates code, primarily in Jupyter notebooks, to perform statistical tests, visualize data, and identify patterns. An average Kosmos run involves 166 data analysis agent rollouts, executing approximately 42,000 lines of code.
 - **Literature Search Agent:** This agent performs extensive literature reviews, reading full-length scientific papers to gather background context, validate emerging hypotheses, and contextualize findings. An average run includes 36 literature review agent rollouts, reading around 1,500 papers.
2. **Structured World Model:** This is the central innovation. The world model serves as a shared, continuously updated knowledge base that synthesizes and stores information from the outputs of all agents. This structured context management is critical for:
 - **Maintaining Coherence:** It allows Kosmos to pursue the specified objective coherently over extended periods (200+ agent rollouts), overcoming the "loss of coherence" limitation of prior systems.
 - **Information Sharing:** Enables seamless exchange of insights and findings between the data analysis and literature search agents.
 - **Guiding Future Tasks:** Kosmos queries the world model to propose new literature search and data analysis tasks for subsequent cycles, ensuring a directed and evolving research strategy.
3. **Large Language Models (LLMs):** LLMs are fundamental to the operation of both the data analysis and literature search agents, powering their programming capabilities and interdisciplinary reasoning.

**Output Generation:**
Once Kosmos determines it has sufficiently addressed the research objective (e.g., after 20 cycles), it synthesizes its key discoveries into **scientific reports** (typically three to four per run). A critical methodological aspect is **traceability**: every statement and figure within these reports is directly linked to the specific code generated by the data analysis agent (e.g., a Jupyter notebook) or the primary scientific literature cited by the literature search agent.

**Evaluation and Validation:**
The authors employed a multi-faceted approach to evaluate Kosmos's performance:
- **Accuracy of Statements:** Expert scientists independently evaluated 102 statements from representative reports, classifying them as "Supported" or "Refuted" based on their own analysis or literature review, without access to Kosmos's internal citations.
- **Efficiency and Equivalent Human Effort:**
 - **Internal Estimate:** Calculated by tallying the number of data analyses and papers read, then estimating the time a human scientist would take for equivalent tasks (e.g., 15 mins per paper, 2 hours per Jupyter notebook).
 - **Orthogonal Expert Estimate:** Academic collaborators independently evaluated Kosmos's output at different cycle numbers (5, 10, 20) and estimated the time their labs would have required to achieve comparable findings.
- **Scientific Value:** Collaborators assessed the number of "valuable findings" generated, and categorized them by "reasoning depth" (shallow to deep multi-step) and "novelty" (little to completely novel).
- **Case Studies:** Seven diverse scientific case studies were presented to illustrate Kosmos's capabilities:
 - Reproducing unpublished or recently published findings.
 - Independently reproducing published findings without prior access.
 - Adding novel support to existing discoveries.
 - Independently developing new analytical methods.
 - Making novel, clinically relevant discoveries not previously identified by human researchers.
 These discoveries spanned metabolomics, materials science, connectomics, statistical genetics, proteomics, and transcriptomics, demonstrating Kosmos's versatility. Human validation steps were explicitly detailed for each discovery, often involving re-running analyses, comparing results to original studies, or using independent datasets.

This comprehensive methodology, combining advanced AI agents with a structured world model, iterative learning, and rigorous human validation, underscores Kosmos's sophisticated approach to autonomous scientific discovery.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The results section provides compelling evidence for Kosmos's capabilities, demonstrating its capacity for extended, coherent scientific inquiry, high accuracy in foundational tasks, and the generation of valuable scientific insights across diverse fields.

**Overall System Performance:**
- **Scale and Coherence:** Kosmos successfully runs for up to 12 hours, performing up to 200 agent rollouts. Its structured world model is identified as the key enabler for maintaining coherence over these extended operations. During a typical run, Kosmos executes an average of 42,000 lines of code (a 9.8x increase over the authors' prior system, Robin) across 166 data analysis agent rollouts and reads 1,500 full-length scientific papers across 36 literature review agent rollouts.
- **Accuracy of Statements:** Independent expert evaluators assessed 102 statements from Kosmos reports for accuracy. Overall, 79.4% of statements were deemed accurate. The accuracy varied by type: 85.5% for data analysis-based statements, 82.1% for literature review-based statements, but only 57.9% for interpretation statements, highlighting a current limitation in sophisticated scientific synthesis.
- **Efficiency (Equivalent Human Time):**
 - Internal calculations, based on estimated human time per paper and data analysis task, suggest Kosmos performs approximately 4.1 expert-months of research per run.
 - Orthogonal estimates from collaborating academic groups were even higher, indicating that a 20-cycle Kosmos run achieved findings equivalent to 6.14 months of their own research time on average. Importantly, these groups reported that the expert-equivalent research time scaled linearly with Kosmos cycles.
- **Scientific Value:** Collaborators reported that the number of valuable scientific findings generated also scaled linearly with Kosmos cycles. Furthermore, these valuable findings demonstrated high to moderate reasoning depth and moderate to complete novelty, indicating that Kosmos is capable of producing scientifically meaningful contributions.

**Seven Discoveries Highlighted (Case Studies):**

1. **Nucleotide Metabolism in Neuroprotection (Metabolomics):** Kosmos accurately reproduced an unpublished discovery concerning the metabolic mechanisms underlying cooling-induced neuroprotection. It identified nucleotide metabolism, specifically the activation of nucleotide-salvage pathways, as the dominant pathway altered under hypothermic conditions in the brain, with nearly identical effect sizes and directional changes as the human study.
2. **Thermal Annealing Humidity in Perovskite Solar Cells (Materials Science):** Kosmos corroborated findings from a preprinted manuscript, identifying thermal annealing humidity as a critical "fatal filter" for perovskite solar-cell performance. It also made a novel contribution by revealing a previously unreported linear decrease in short-circuit current density with rising Dimethylformamide (DMF) solvent partial pressure during spin-coating, which was subsequently validated by human researchers.
3. **Log-Normal Connectivity in Neural Networks (Connectomics):** Given a broad objective, Kosmos independently reproduced key findings from an existing preprint (not accessed at runtime). It concluded that neuronal morphology metrics (degree, synapse counts) are best described by log-normal distributions, not power-law or random models, and identified power-law scaling relationships between these metrics, suggesting multiplicative processes in neurodevelopment.
4. **SOD2 as a Driver of Myocardial Fibrosis (Statistical Genetics):** Kosmos successfully applied Mendelian Randomization to publicly available datasets, identifying superoxide dismutase 2 (SOD2) as the primary causal protein for myocardial fibrosis, with highly concordant effect estimates and colocalization evidence compared to human analysis. It also proposed a novel post-transcriptional mechanism for SOD2 regulation, suggesting areas for further wet-lab validation.
5. **Cis-regulation of SSR1 in Type 2 Diabetes (Statistical Genetics):** Without explicit instruction, Kosmos developed a "Mechanistic Ranking Score" to prioritize causal mechanisms from GWAS data. It identified the variant rs9379084 and its cis-regulation of SSR1 as a plausible protective pathway in T2D, linking it to ATF3 binding and chromatin changes in pancreatic islet cells. This hypothesis was supported by independent data sources and existing literature.
6. **Temporal Ordering of Events in Alzheimer’s Disease (Proteomics):** Kosmos demonstrated its ability to propose novel analytical methods. It developed a segmented regression approach to pinpoint the timing of cellular events along a disease continuum. Applying this to AD proteomics data, it identified a breakpoint for extracellular matrix (ECM) protein decline along a pseudotime axis for tau accumulation, suggesting ECM failure occurs downstream of tau pathology. This was validated by bootstrapping and an orthogonal sliding window correlation method, and replicated in an independent transcriptomic dataset.
7. **Mechanism of Entorhinal Cortex Vulnerability in Aging (Transcriptomics):** Kosmos uncovered a novel, clinically relevant mechanism of neuronal aging not previously identified by the original human researchers. It found an age-related, widespread downregulation of phospholipid flippases (e.g., Atp10a) in vulnerable entorhinal cortex neurons, concurrently with an upregulation of phosphatidylserine (PS)-sensing phagocytic genes in microglia. This suggests a mechanism for targeted removal of vulnerable neurons in the aging brain, validated in both mouse and human AD datasets.

These findings collectively underscore Kosmos's capability to perform complex scientific research, from hypothesis generation and data analysis to literature synthesis, resulting in accurate, efficient, and often novel discoveries across a broad spectrum of scientific fields.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Kosmos represents a significant leap forward in the ambition to develop autonomous AI scientists, with profound potential to reshape the landscape of scientific research. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized in several key areas:

- **Accelerating Scientific Discovery:** The most immediate impact of Kosmos is its ability to drastically accelerate the pace of data-driven discovery. By autonomously performing iterative cycles of data analysis, literature search, and hypothesis generation over periods equivalent to months of human effort, Kosmos can rapidly explore vast datasets and uncover insights that might otherwise take years. This frees human scientists to focus on higher-level conceptualization, experimental design, and the critical interpretation of complex findings, effectively augmenting their intellectual capabilities.

- **Enhanced Exploration and Unbiased Analysis:** Kosmos facilitates large-scale, unbiased exploration of high-dimensional datasets. Its ability to process thousands of papers and execute tens of thousands of lines of code ensures that a broad range of hypotheses can be tested and contextualized without the inherent biases or limited bandwidth of individual human researchers. This could lead to unexpected connections and novel discoveries in overlooked areas.

- **Increased Rigor and Reproducibility:** A critical contribution is Kosmos's built-in traceability. By citing every statement in its reports with either specific code trajectories or primary literature, it addresses a major challenge in modern science: reproducibility. This level of transparency allows for independent verification and replication of its findings, fostering greater trust in AI-generated science and setting a new standard for computational research.

- **Broad Applicability Across Disciplines:** The demonstration of Kosmos's utility across diverse fields—from metabolomics and materials science to neurobiology and statistical genetics—highlights its potential as a truly general-purpose AI scientist. This versatility means it could be deployed to accelerate research in virtually any data-rich scientific domain, breaking down traditional disciplinary silos.

- **Novel Methodological Development:** Kosmos's capacity to not only execute standard analyses but also reason and propose novel analytical approaches, such as the segmented regression model for temporal ordering in AD, demonstrates a nascent form of scientific creativity. This ability to independently develop methods could lead to new tools and techniques that benefit the wider scientific community.

- **Clinically Relevant Insights:** The discovery of a novel mechanism for entorhinal cortex vulnerability in aging, validated in human AD datasets, showcases Kosmos's potential to uncover clinically relevant insights that can inform our understanding of disease pathology and identify new therapeutic targets.

**Limitations and Future Directions (Self-Acknowledged):**
The authors are transparent about Kosmos's current limitations, which also represent avenues for future impact:
- **"Scientific Taste" and Interpretation:** Kosmos sometimes struggles with nuanced interpretation, occasionally conflating statistical significance with scientific value, leading to lower accuracy in interpretation statements. Improving its "scientific taste" to prioritize truly valuable and coherent insights is a key area for development.
- **Human Evaluation Bottleneck:** While Kosmos accelerates discovery, the process of human scientists evaluating its findings for novelty and significance remains time-intensive. Future work could focus on AI-driven pre-screening or prioritization of its own outputs.
- **Technical Constraints:** Limitations in handling raw data (images, sequencing files), dataset size (5GB limit), autonomous access to external public data, and stochasticity are practical challenges that, once addressed, will significantly broaden its capabilities.
- **Interactive Control:** The current lack of intermediate interaction points for scientists limits opportunities to guide Kosmos down fruitful avenues. Future interactive versions could optimize the "scientist-in-the-loop" model further.

In conclusion, Kosmos is a pioneering system that demonstrates the feasibility of an AI scientist capable of conducting complex, extended research investigations. Its innovative architecture, robust performance, and proven ability to generate novel and validated discoveries mark a critical step towards a future where AI and human intelligence synergistically drive scientific progress on an unprecedented scale.
