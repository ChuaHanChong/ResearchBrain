# Latent Adaptive Planner for Dynamic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Latent Adaptive Planner (LAP) enables robots to perform dynamic nonprehensile tasks by learning from human demonstration videos, which are translated into robot-specific kinematic-dynamic states. This system employs a latent-variable policy with a variational replanning framework for real-time adaptation, achieving high success rates and significantly improving energy efficiency (e.g., 11.47 J for Box A on Robot A) in complex tasks like box catching across different robot platforms.

## Problem

- Dynamic manipulation (e.g., catching, throwing) is challenging for robots due to varied object properties, rapid contact changes, and the difficulty of real-time analytical modeling.
- Existing robotic approaches for dynamic tasks rely on extensive, costly, and potentially damaging self-supervised robot trials, while imitation learning often suffers from slow inference and lack of dynamic information in datasets.
- Bridging the embodiment gap to effectively transfer human-like compliant and energy-efficient skills from human demonstration videos to diverse robot platforms remains a significant hurdle.

## Method

- A model-based data regeneration pipeline translates human demonstration videos into robot-specific kinematic and dynamic states (positions, velocities, accelerations, torques) using ==proportional mapping== and ==inverse dynamics==.
- The **Latent Adaptive Planner (LAP)** formulates dynamic planning as inference in a low-dimensional latent space, using a ==causal Transformer== policy trained with ==classical variational Bayes==.
- A ==variational replanning== framework enables real-time adaptation by efficiently updating the latent plan via Bayesian inference, integrating with a ==Model Predictive Control (MPC)== system for robust execution.

## Results

- LAP achieved high success rates in dynamic box catching (e.g., **29/30** or **30/30** trials for Box A on Robot A/B), comparable to model-based planners and outperforming other learning-based baselines.
- The system demonstrated significantly superior energy efficiency (e.g., **11.47 J** for Box A on Robot A), consuming less energy than other learning-based methods (e.g., **31.39 J** for Behavior Cloning, **42.53 J** for Diffusion Policy).
- Learned skills, including impact-aware compliant motions, successfully generalized and transferred across two distinct robot platforms using the identical set of human demonstration videos.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Model-based proportional mapping effectively translates human movements and scene context, allowing the same human demonstration dataset to train robots with different kinematics and dynamics.
> - Representing entire trajectories as low-dimensional latent plans enables coherent long-horizon planning and efficient real-time adaptation through incremental variational updates.
> - Learning from human demonstrations facilitates the acquisition of energy-efficient, compliant motions, such as impact-absorbing 'retreat trajectories,' which are difficult to model analytically or through traditional optimization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Dynamic manipulation, encompassing tasks such as throwing, catching, and rapid transfers, presents a significant challenge in robotics. Unlike humans who perform these actions with apparent ease, robots typically execute slow and conservative movements. The difficulty arises from the need for real-time consideration of diverse physical object properties (e.g., mass, friction, elasticity), rapid changes in contact states, and precise management of joint torques. These dynamic characteristics are challenging to model analytically and exhibit substantial variability across different objects.

Existing robotic approaches to dynamic manipulation, such as Tossingbot, FlingBot, and methods by Chi et al., have demonstrated progress in specific scenarios but largely depend on self-supervised learning from thousands of robot trials. This reliance on extensive robot interaction limits scalability, particularly for tasks involving heavier objects or mobile platforms like humanoids, where failures can result in significant hardware damage. While simulation offers a safer training environment, achieving accurate physical modeling for complex dynamic interactions remains difficult, thereby hindering effective sim-to-real transfer.

Imitation learning (IL) has shown promise for static or quasi-static manipulation tasks. However, current IL approaches face several limitations when applied to dynamic scenarios. For instance, techniques like diffusion policy often suffer from slow inference speeds, making them unsuitable for real-time dynamic manipulation. Furthermore, many policy training datasets primarily focus on end-effector positions, neglecting crucial dynamic information such as contact states, joint torques, and grasping forces. Collecting more comprehensive data through teleoperation is often impractical due to high implementation costs, logistical complexities for achieving scale and diversity, and technical challenges in capturing complete physical interaction data. Additionally, demonstration-based learning methods frequently struggle with maintaining temporal consistency and trajectory smoothness when transferring skills from human demonstrations to robotic systems. Although generative modeling techniques, including sequence modeling and diffusion models, have partially addressed these issues, they still exhibit limitations in managing the long-term dependencies and environmental contingencies inherent in dynamic manipulation tasks.

A related area of research involves generating robot training data from human demonstration videos to overcome the embodiment gap between human and robot kinematics and dynamics. While some methods require hybrid human and robot datasets or rely on object tracking and inverse modeling to extract actions, many cross-embodiment techniques still necessitate some form of robot-specific data. Recent works, such as Phantom, have explored zero-shot transfer from human demonstrations, and DROID has demonstrated that diverse data can create robust policies. Of particular relevance, DexMV established a framework for learning dexterous manipulation from human videos by translating demonstrations into robot joint torques via inverse dynamics, an approach that aligns with the data processing methodology presented in this paper.

Within the field of imitation learning, latent variable models have been explored to capture trajectory-level information and long-term dependencies, thereby addressing temporal consistency challenges. Works by Yang et al. and Paster et al. investigated latent variable solutions for Decision Transformer models, and the Latent Plan Transformer formulates planning as latent space inference, separating trajectory generation from return estimation. However, earlier VAE-based models for extended policies typically lack the adaptive replanning capabilities necessary for real-time dynamic tasks.

The Latent Adaptive Planner (LAP) aims to bridge these gaps by formulating planning as inference in a low-dimensional latent space. It integrates a principled variational replanning framework to ensure temporal consistency and efficient real-time adaptation. By employing a novel model-based proportional mapping, LAP translates human demonstration videos into accurate kinematic-dynamic robot states, thus facilitating the transfer of human-like compliant and energy-efficient manipulation skills to diverse robotic platforms using the same human-generated data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research were to develop a robotic manipulation system capable of performing complex dynamic nonprehensile tasks with real-time adaptation and efficiency, learned from human demonstrations. The specific objectives and their underlying motivations can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enable Dynamic Nonprehensile Manipulation:** The core objective was to move beyond slow, conservative robot movements and enable robots to execute dynamic tasks involving rapid contact changes and complex physical interactions, such as catching objects. This is motivated by the fact that such tasks are fundamental to human dexterity but remain a significant challenge for robotics due to difficulties in analytical modeling of diverse object properties and contact dynamics.

2. **Achieve Real-Time Adaptation:** The system needed to adapt instantaneously to unpredictable changes in object trajectories, physical properties (e.g., mass, elasticity), and contact states. This motivation stems from the inherent variability of dynamic environments and the limitations of pre-programmed or open-loop planning methods, which cannot cope with such contingencies.

3. **Learn Effectively from Human Demonstration Videos:** The goal was to leverage the rich, adaptive, and energy-efficient qualities of human manipulation skills. This was motivated by the desire to bypass the limitations of robot-centric data collection methods, which are often unsafe, costly, require extensive trials, and can cause hardware damage in dynamic scenarios. Human demonstrations offer a scalable and safer alternative.

4. **Bridge the Human-Robot Embodiment Gap:** A crucial objective was to translate human movements from demonstration videos into executable robot commands, overcoming the kinematic and dynamic differences between human and robot bodies. This was motivated by the need to make human demonstrations directly usable for robot training, eliminating the requirement for labor-intensive and expensive robot teleoperation or hybrid datasets. The aim was to create versatile datasets suitable for diverse robotic platforms using the same human source material.

5. **Achieve Human-like Compliant and Energy-Efficient Motions:** The system sought to learn and reproduce the nuanced, compliant behaviors observed in human manipulation, particularly those involving impact absorption. This objective was motivated by the inefficiencies and high energy consumption associated with rigid, force-driven robot controls, as well as the inherent difficulty in analytically formulating compliant motions through optimization methods.

6. **Ensure Transferability Across Heterogeneous Robot Platforms:** A practical objective was to demonstrate that the developed policy could be successfully deployed on different robot hardware configurations without requiring new demonstrations for each platform. This was motivated by the need for scalable and generalizable robotic solutions, reducing the development effort for new robotic systems.

The challenging task of "box catching" was chosen to validate these objectives. This task requires real-time adaptation to unpredictable object trajectories, managing objects without secure grasps, predicting contact locations and orientations, coordinating multi-arm movements with high precision, and modulating contact forces to absorb impact. By tackling this task, the authors aimed to demonstrate that imitation learning could achieve energy-efficient dynamic manipulation, bypassing the intractable formulations typically required by optimization-based methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Latent Adaptive Planner (LAP) integrates a novel data regeneration pipeline from human demonstration videos with a latent-variable policy framework that uses variational inference for real-time adaptation.

#### 3.1. Robot Model-Based Data Regeneration from Human Demonstration Videos

To address the embodiment gap and leverage human demonstrations, a three-component pipeline was developed to regenerate robot training data from human videos:
1. **Scene State Estimation:** This initial stage involved detecting and tracking box objects in video frames to determine their positions and dimensions in a scene coordinate system. Concurrently, human pose estimation was performed to track the demonstrator's joint positions, providing the foundational motion data. The implementation utilized OpenMMLab's RTMDet-M for detection and HRNet-W48 for precise keypoint estimation, and YOLOv8 for box pose estimation, achieving real-time inference rates.
2. **Object-Robot Proportional Mapping:** To adapt the spatial context from human demonstrations to the robot's workspace, object positions and dimensions were scaled and transformed. Object positions (`S_p_obj`) in the scene frame (`S`) were mapped to the robot base frame (`R`) using a transformation matrix (`T_R_S`): `R_p_obj = T_R_S * S_p_obj`. Object dimensions (`S_d_obj`) were scaled by a factor (`s`) derived from the ratio of robot arm length (from the robot model) to human arm length (measured from video pixels): `R_d_obj = s * S_d_obj`.
3. **Kinematic-Dynamic Joint State Reconstruction:** The robot's kinematic and dynamic states were reconstructed from the proportionally mapped human data.
 - **Joint Positions:** Human joint positions (`q_human`) were directly mapped to robot joint positions (`q`) using a function `f_map` that accounts for kinematic differences between human and robot: `q = f_map(q_human)`.
 - **Joint Velocities and Accelerations:** These were numerically calculated by differentiating the reconstructed joint positions with respect to time, using the video's frame rate as `Δt`: `q_dot ≈ (q_t+Δt - q_t) / Δt` and `q_ddot ≈ (q_dot_t+Δt - q_dot_t) / Δt`.
 - **Joint Torques:** With the full kinematic state (`q`, `q_dot`, `q_ddot`), inverse dynamics was applied to compute the required joint torques (`τ`). The inverse dynamics equation included terms for inertia (`M(q)q_ddot`), Coriolis and centrifugal effects (`C(q, q_dot)q_dot`), gravitational forces (`G(q)`), and, critically, external forces (`J^T(q)F_ext`) encountered during object interaction: `τ = M(q)q_ddot + C(q, q_dot)q_dot + G(q) + J^T(q)F_ext`. The inclusion of external forces was vital for accurate modeling of dynamic manipulation.

#### 3.2. Latent Adaptive Planner (LAP) Model

LAP formulates planning as inference in a low-dimensional latent space. It models a joint probability distribution `p_θ(x, z) = p(z)p_θ(x|z)`, where `x = (o_t, a_t)` is a trajectory of observations (`o_t`) and actions (`a_t`), and `z ∈ R^d` is the latent plan vector. The prior `p(z)` is an isotropic Gaussian `N(0, I_d)`. The trajectory generator `p_θ(x|z)` is a conditional autoregressive model that produces actions based on the current state, historical context of length `K`, and the latent plan `z`. The observation `o_t` comprises box position, contact state, and previous joint states (position, velocity, torque), while action `a_t` consists of current joint states. The model parameter `θ` is implemented using a causal Transformer, with `z` influencing action generation via cross-attention mechanisms. Actions are assumed to follow a unimodal Gaussian distribution.

#### 3.3. Classical Variational Bayes Learning

LAP is learned using classical variational Bayes (VB). Instead of training an inference network (as in VAEs), the latent plan vectors (`μ`, `σ^2`, representing the mean and diagonal covariance of a Gaussian posterior approximation `q(z|x)`) for each trajectory are directly optimized through gradient descent. These are "local" parameters unique to each trajectory, while `θ` are "global" parameters shared across all samples. The learning objective is to maximize the evidence lower bound (ELBO): `L(θ, μ, σ) = E_{q(z|x)}[log p_θ(x|z)] - D_KL(q(z|x)||p(z))`.
The training procedure alternates between:
1. Optimizing local parameters `(μ_i, σ_i)` for each trajectory `x_i` in a mini-batch by maximizing `L(θ, μ_i, σ_i)` for `T_local` gradient steps (set to 16).
2. Updating global parameters `θ` by maximizing the average `L(θ, μ_i, σ_i)` over the mini-batch. This mirrors a "fast-slow learning" intuition, with rapid local adaptation and slower global knowledge integration.

#### 3.4. Variational Replanning for Test-time Adaptation

During execution, LAP employs variational replanning for real-time adaptation.
1. **Initial Plan:** Given an initial observation `o_1`, an initial latent plan `z` is sampled by optimizing `(μ_0, σ_0)` using `T_local` gradient steps on `log p_θ(x_{0:1}|z) - D_KL(N(μ_0, σ_0^2)||p(z))`.
2. **Adaptive Replanning Loop:** As new observations `x_{t+1:t+Δ}` become available, the latent plan `z` is adaptively updated. The previously inferred posterior `q(z|x_{0:t}) = N(μ_t, σ_t^2)` is treated as a prior. A new posterior `q(z|x_{0:t+Δ}) = N(μ_{t+Δ}, σ^2_{t+Δ})` is optimized by maximizing `E_{q_{t+Δ}}[log p_θ(x_{t+1:t+Δ}|x_{0:t}, z)] - D_KL(q_{t+Δ}||q_t)`. This Bayesian updating is computationally efficient, requiring only `T_replan = 1` gradient step, contrasting with the more extensive initial planning. This approach balances the adaptiveness of closed-loop planning with the efficiency of incremental updates, ensuring the updated posterior converges towards the optimal plan if complete information were available.

#### 3.5. Integrated Perception-Planning-Control Architecture

The overall system architecture for LAP integrates perception, planning, and control. Camera input undergoes segmentation for object detection, providing box states to LAP. LAP operates with a dual-rate hierarchy: updating the latent plan at 30Hz and generating motion commands (reference joint positions, velocities, torques) at 100Hz. These reference commands are then fed to a Model Predictive Control (MPC) system. The MPC refines the commands, ensuring safe and smooth execution by respecting robot dynamics and physical constraints (joint position, velocity, acceleration, and torque limits). Real-time joint states and contact information are fed back to LAP, enabling continuous replanning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of LAP was evaluated through challenging box catching experiments, a dynamic nonprehensile manipulation task. Performance was assessed against various baselines across two distinct robot platforms (Robot A and Robot B) with different actuator configurations and kinematic structures, using multiple box types with varying weights and dimensions.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Task:** Box catching, requiring robots to intercept, temporarily control, and securely grasp boxes of diverse properties tossed by human operators. This task demands real-time trajectory adaptation, impact anticipation, and force modulation.
- **Hardware:** Experiments were conducted on two heterogeneous robot platforms (Robot A and Robot B), both equipped with ZED2 stereo cameras for real-time object tracking. The data regeneration process was tailored to each robot's specific characteristics, but trained using the *same human demonstration videos*.
- **Baselines:**
 1. **Model-based Planner:** This approach determined optimal end-effector positions and arm configurations using inverse dynamics for tracking. An impedance control variant was also implemented, offering compliant motions.
 2. **Transformer-based Behavior Cloning (BC):** A learning-based method trained on human demonstrations.
 3. **Diffusion Policy:** A state-of-the-art visuomotor policy learning method, implemented with a 1D UNet architecture.
 - LAP used an identical architecture to BC but with an additional cross-attention layer for the latent plan, set with 16 latent variables, each of dimension 64, and `T_local = 16` for plan optimization.

#### 4.2. Performance Comparison with Baselines

Quantitative results (Table 1 in the paper) consistently demonstrated LAP's superior performance compared to the baselines across all metrics and box types on both robot platforms.

- **Model-based Planning:** Achieved the highest success rates (e.g., 30/30 trials) for box catching. However, this came at a significant energy cost (e.g., 74.99 J for Box A on Robot A), attributed to its conservative control strategy of applying high torque values to ensure secure grasps. The impedance control variant offered better energy efficiency through compliant motions but suffered from adaptability issues, requiring manual gain tuning for different box properties and impact forces.
- **Learning-based Methods (BC and Diffusion Policy):** These methods demonstrated a capacity to learn human-like behaviors but exhibited lower success rates (e.g., 26/30 for BC, 20/30 for Diffusion for Box A on Robot A) and less stable action outputs compared to LAP. While integration with MPC improved motion smoothness, they did not reach optimal performance in terms of success or efficiency (e.g., 31.39 J for BC, 42.53 J for Diffusion for Box A on Robot A).
- **LAP Performance:** LAP achieved high success rates, often comparable to the model-based approach (e.g., 29/30 or 30/30 for Box A on Robot A/B, respectively). Crucially, LAP demonstrated significantly superior energy efficiency compared to all other policy-based methods (e.g., 11.47 J for Box A on Robot A). This efficiency was attributed to its effective encoding of contact dynamics and learned human-like "retreat motions" in the latent variable space, which allowed the robot to absorb impact forces and minimize energy consumption.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Observations

LAP successfully learned and reproduced "impact-aware retreat trajectories" similar to those observed in human demonstrations (Figure 3 in the paper). Humans naturally absorb the impact of an incoming object by yielding their arm along the object's trajectory before returning to a nominal pose. LAP replicated this compliant motion, demonstrating that it could learn to implement such behaviors through motion planning rather than solely relying on control-based impedance. This learned compliance contributed to both high success rates and energy efficiency.

#### 4.4. Generalizability

The success of LAP was demonstrated on two distinct robot platforms (Robot A and Robot B) using the *same set of human demonstration videos*. This cross-platform transferability validated that the model-based data regeneration pipeline effectively translated human skills to different robot kinematics and dynamics, enhancing the scalability of the approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Latent Adaptive Planner (LAP) represents a notable advancement in the field of dynamic nonprehensile manipulation, addressing several long-standing challenges in robot control and learning from demonstrations.

#### 5.1. Summary of Contributions

- **Novel Trajectory-Level Latent-Variable Policy:** LAP introduces a new approach to planning for dynamic manipulation by framing it as inference within a low-dimensional latent space. This allows for coherent long-horizon planning while retaining flexibility for real-time adaptation.
- **Effective Human-to-Robot Skill Transfer:** The research successfully bridges the embodiment gap between humans and robots through a model-based proportional mapping and kinematic-dynamic joint state reconstruction pipeline. This enables the effective learning of complex manipulation skills directly from human demonstration videos, eliminating the need for extensive, robot-specific data collection.
- **Principled Real-Time Adaptation:** LAP integrates a variational replanning framework that employs Bayesian updating in the latent space. This mechanism allows the robot to incrementally refine its plan as new observations arrive, providing efficient and stable real-time adaptation during dynamic interactions without the computational burden of full re-optimization at every step.
- **Superior Performance in Dynamic Tasks:** Through rigorous experimentation on box catching, LAP demonstrated high success rates comparable to or exceeding existing learning-based methods, while simultaneously achieving significantly improved energy efficiency and trajectory smoothness. This is largely attributed to its ability to learn and reproduce human-like compliant motions for impact absorption.
- **Cross-Platform Generalizability:** The capability to transfer learned skills to heterogeneous robot platforms using the identical set of human demonstration videos highlights the robustness and scalability of the LAP framework, reducing development overhead for new robotic systems.

#### 5.2. Implications for Robotics

The findings of this research have several implications for the future of robotic manipulation:

- **Expansion of Robot Capabilities:** LAP's ability to handle dynamic nonprehensile tasks, previously a domain where robots struggled, could enable robots to perform a wider array of real-world activities that involve fast, uncertain interactions with objects, such as parcel handling, domestic assistance, or collaborative manufacturing.
- **Safer and More Efficient Robot Operation:** The learned compliant motions and energy efficiency can lead to robots that interact more safely with their environment and objects, reducing the risk of damage to hardware or manipulated items. This also contributes to lower operational costs and extended robot lifespan.
- **Democratization of Robot Skill Learning:** By providing an effective method to learn from readily available human video demonstrations, LAP can significantly lower the barrier to entry for developing complex robot skills. This reduces the reliance on costly robot-specific data collection or expert teleoperation, making advanced robot capabilities more accessible.
- **Foundation for Adaptive Intelligence:** The framework for real-time adaptive planning via latent space inference offers a strong foundation for future research in generalizable and intelligent robotic systems that can continuously learn and adapt to unforeseen circumstances in dynamic environments.

#### 5.3. Identified Limitations and Future Directions

The authors acknowledge several limitations that serve as clear directions for future work:

- **Simplified Contact Modeling:** The current approach uses a binary contact representation and approximates contact locations at the wrist. Future work aims to incorporate continuous contact models with multi-point force estimation and varying force distributions, which are crucial for dexterous manipulation.
- **Kinematic Constraints:** The human-to-robot mapping was restricted to three primary degrees of freedom for the human arm, and the robot assumed a fixed-base manipulator. Future efforts will focus on extending to mobile platforms and integrating whole-body coordination strategies, which humans naturally employ.
- **Perception Limitations:** Reliance on 2D pose estimation from monocular video inherently limits accurate 3D trajectory reconstruction and handling of occlusions. Strengthening 3D perception capabilities is identified as an important next step.
- **Prior Model Limitations:** The use of a simple isotropic Gaussian latent prior may not fully capture the complexity and multi-modality of expert human behaviors. Exploring more structured and expressive prior models, such as energy-based models or diffusion models, could enhance planning capabilities and generalization.

Overall, the research establishes a robust framework for dynamic manipulation by combining data regeneration from human demonstrations with an adaptive latent space planner. Addressing the identified limitations will be crucial for moving closer to human-level dexterity while maintaining the real-time performance essential for dynamic robotic tasks.
