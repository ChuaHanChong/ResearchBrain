# Scalable Trajectory Generation for Whole-Body Mobile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> AutoMoMa is a GPU-accelerated data-generation framework for whole-body mobile manipulation. It folds mobile base, arm, and object kinematics into one Augmented Kinematic Representation chain, then batches trajectory optimization and collision checking on GPU. It reaches 5,000 episodes per GPU-hour, roughly 80x faster than CPU planners, yielding over 500k physically valid trajectories across 330 scenes and multiple embodiments for imitation learning.

## Problem

- Teleoperation captures true whole-body motion but operator fatigue and hardware limits cap datasets at hundreds to thousands of episodes, making it fundamentally unscalable.
- CPU-based augmented-kinematics planners guarantee physical validity yet generate only about 60 trajectories per hour and are typically restricted to fixed grasp poses.
- Existing mobile-manipulation datasets compromise on scale, scene diversity, or kinematic fidelity, and many record decoupled base-arm or scripted end-effector actions instead of joint-space coordination.

## Method

- **AutoMoMa**'s ==Augmented Kinematic Representation== construction: a virtual planar base, a virtual grasp joint, and kinematic-tree inversion re-root the object, merging robot and object into one serial chain.
- ==GPU-batched trajectory optimization== in the AKR configuration space minimizes weighted velocity and acceleration cost under ==ESDF collision fields==, sphere-approximated link geometry, and attachment, joint, and smoothness constraints.
- Four-stage pipeline: task specification, problem instantiation with ==IK clustering== and ==multi-stage re-grasp== for confined spaces, constraint-violation post-filtering, then Isaac Sim RGB-D and point-cloud rendering.

## Results

- Generation reaches **5,000 episodes per GPU-hour**, over **80x** the CPU baseline's **60 per hour**, producing **500k** trajectories across **330 scenes** and three robot platforms.
- In a single scene the mobile-base DP3 policy saturates near **70%** success at **3,200** trajectories, while a fixed-base policy reaches **100%** with under **800** trajectories.
- Scaling to **30 scenes** and **30k** trajectories raises both seen and unseen success to roughly **75%**, and DP and ACT show the same density-driven gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying robot, base, and object into one kinematic chain turns coupled whole-body manipulation into a single constrained optimization that parallelizes cleanly on GPU.
> - Data scarcity, not algorithm design, binds whole-body policies: one articulated-object task still needs tens of thousands of demonstrations to approach 80% success.
> - Scene diversity and per-scene trajectory density help comparably; dense data in one scene causes manifold memorization that collapses on unseen start configurations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic mobile manipulation in unstructured environments presents challenges related to coordinating whole-body motion, which involves the simultaneous movement of a mobile base and an arm. This coordination significantly expands the state space, making it difficult to find valid kinematic solutions under articulation and collision constraints. Learning robust policies for such tasks typically requires extensive datasets, yet acquiring these datasets at scale remains an open problem.

Existing data acquisition methods exhibit limitations:
- **Teleoperation:** While yielding high-fidelity demonstrations, it is labor-intensive and not scalable, with datasets typically limited to hundreds or thousands of episodes.
- **Simulation-based Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Automates data collection but is often sample-inefficient and can suffer from sim-to-real gaps, making it difficult to transfer learned policies to physical robots.
- **Planning-based methods:** These methods, particularly those leveraging the Augmented Kinematic Representation (AKR) framework, ensure physical validity. However, their reliance on CPU-based solvers has made them computationally prohibitive for large-scale data generation. For instance, prior AKR implementations generated approximately 60 trajectories per hour.
- **End-to-end learning methods:** These approaches aim to infer solutions directly but are constrained by the availability of high-quality, whole-body demonstrations.
- **Existing datasets:** Prior datasets for mobile manipulation often compromise on scale, diversity, or kinematic fidelity. They might focus on stationary bases, decoupled base-arm control, or a narrow range of tasks and robot platforms.

This research addresses the core bottleneck of scalable generation of large-scale, physically valid, coordinated trajectory data across diverse robot embodiments and environments. It positions itself by bridging the gap between precise kinematic modeling and the throughput of modern parallel computing, which prior work has been unable to achieve simultaneously. By offering a high-throughput, physically accurate data generation pipeline, this work aims to remove the data scarcity constraint that has hindered the development of generalizable policies for whole-body mobile manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the computational and logistical challenges associated with acquiring large-scale, physically valid trajectory data for whole-body mobile manipulation. The coordination of a mobile base and an arm in unstructured environments leads to a combinatorially growing state space, making physically valid kinematic solutions sparse and difficult to generate or discover. Current methods for data acquisition, such as teleoperation and CPU-based motion planning, are either labor-intensive or computationally prohibitive, preventing the creation of datasets sufficient for learning robust, generalizable policies. This data scarcity is identified as a fundamental constraint preventing state-of-the-art learning algorithms from achieving high success rates in whole-body mobile manipulation tasks.

Against this background, the key objectives of the AutoMoMa project are:
1. **Develop a Scalable Trajectory Generation Framework:** To create a pipeline capable of generating large volumes of physically valid, coordinated whole-body trajectories efficiently.
2. **Integrate Augmented Kinematic Representation (AKR) with GPU Acceleration:** To unify the AKR modeling, which consolidates base, arm, and object kinematics into a single chain, with GPU-accelerated trajectory optimization to significantly increase data generation throughput.
3. **Construct a Comprehensive Dataset:** To generate a dataset exceeding 500,000 physically valid trajectories that demonstrate diversity across multiple dimensions, including 330 distinct scenes, a variety of articulated and rigid objects, and multiple robot embodiments. This dataset is designed to overcome the limitations of prior datasets in terms of scale, diversity, and kinematic fidelity.
4. **Empirically Validate the Necessity of Data Scale:** To conduct experiments with downstream Imitation Learning (IL) policies to demonstrate that achieving high success rates for whole-body mobile manipulation tasks requires tens of thousands of demonstrations, thereby confirming that data scarcity, rather than algorithmic limitations, has been the primary barrier.
5. **Enable Generalizable Robot Policies:** To provide the necessary infrastructure for training robust IL policies that can generalize coordinated whole-body motion to unseen environments and complex tasks, such as grasp switching.

These objectives aim to establish AutoMoMa as a foundational framework for research in embodied AI, bridging high-performance planning with large-scale learning for mobile manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The AutoMoMa framework utilizes a GPU-accelerated pipeline to generate large-scale, physically valid whole-body trajectories. The methodology is structured around four integrated stages: task specification, problem instantiation, trajectory generation, and rendering.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Augmented Kinematic Representation (AKR)

The foundation of the planning approach is the AKR, which integrates the mobile base, manipulator, and target object into a single serial kinematic chain.
- **AKR Construction:** It takes the robot's and object's kinematic trees (e.g., URDFs) and the grasp pose as inputs. A virtual joint is inserted to encode the grasp pose between the robot's end-effector and the object. For articulated objects, the object's kinematic model is inverted so that the attachment link (grasp point) becomes the new kinematic root, forming a continuous chain. A virtual base is introduced to model the mobile base's planar motion (two prismatic and one revolute joint) relative to the world frame. This results in a unified configuration space `x = [q_B, q_M, q_O]^T`.
- **AKR-Based Planning:** The planning problem is formulated as finding a collision-free trajectory `x_{1:T}` within the AKR configuration space that satisfies kinematic and task-specific constraints. Constraints include:
 - `h_chain(x_t) = 0`: Kinematic constraints of the object's environmental attachment (e.g., door hinge).
 - `||f_task(x_T) - g_goal||_2 <= ξ_goal`: Task completion, ensuring the terminal state is within tolerance of the goal.
 - `x_min <= x_t <= x_max`: Joint position limits.
 - `||Δx_t||_∞ <= Δx_max`: Joint velocity limits.
 - `||Δ^2x_t||_∞ <= Δ^2x_max`: Joint acceleration limits.
 - Collision avoidance is handled implicitly by the underlying motion planner.

#### 3.2. AutoMoMa Data Generation Pipeline

1. **Task Specification:**
 - **Household Scene Layouts:** Diverse scenes (`S`) are sourced via procedural generation (Infinigen-based pipeline, 300 scenes) and augmentation of existing scene datasets (AI2-THOR, 30 scenes) by replacing static appliances with articulated counterparts from the SAPIEN dataset.
 - **Interactive Objects:** The object set (`O`) includes both rigid and articulated entities, characterized by meshes, URDF descriptions, joint limits, and inertial parameters. State-dependent grasp poses for articulated objects are handled by re-rooting the object at the grasp point.
 - **Robot Embodiments:** Robot models (`R`) are defined via URDFs, including a virtual mobile base and a manipulator. They are augmented with spherical collision approximations, self-collision masks, and joint-weight vectors. Tested embodiments include Summit Franka, TIAGo, and R1.

2. **Problem Instantiation:**
 - **Environment Collision Models:** Scenes are converted into Euclidean Signed-distance Fields (ESDFs) for efficient collision queries. Planning is restricted to an axis-aligned bounding box around the object's start and goal states to focus computation.
 - **AKR Construction:** The object model is integrated with the robot's kinematics. Objects are resized to fit the environment, link components are merged, and joint origins are recalculated to maintain kinematic integrity. The object is coupled to the robot's end-effector via a virtual joint.
 - **Collision Processing:** For GPU-accelerated planning, link geometries are approximated using fitted spheres. Meshes are downscaled before sphere fitting to prevent volume overestimation. Sphere cloud centroids are realigned to preserve geometric fidelity. Negligible collision pairs are masked. A dynamic collision strategy clears environmental voxels intersecting the object in the approach phase and removes the object's static mesh in the manipulation phase to prevent false positives.

3. **Trajectory Generation:**
 - **Task Objectives and Goals:** The objective `J(x_{1:T})` minimizes total travel distance and trajectory non-smoothness, weighted by `W_v` and `W_a`. Task goals are defined as target SE(3) poses for rigid objects or specific joint configurations for articulated objects.
 - **Task Constraints:** Constraints are derived from object-scene relationships; e.g., heavy rigid objects are restricted to SE(2) planar motion, while stationary articulated objects have strict pose constraints on the AKR end-effector.
 - **Optimization Problem Formulation:** Valid AKR start and goal configurations are computed using Inverse Kinematics (IK). Similar IK solutions are clustered in joint space to retain a diverse set of representative candidate configurations. For complex tasks, a multi-stage strategy involving an intermediate state `ϕ_mid` and re-grasp actions is used.
 - **Trajectory Post-processing:** Optimized trajectories are filtered. Each waypoint is validated against constraints using translational and rotational deviations for object-world attachment and, for planar constraints, vertical displacement and orientation deviation. Trajectories violating thresholds are discarded.

4. **Rendering:**
 - NVIDIA Isaac Sim is used to synthesize high-fidelity multi-modal observations from validated trajectories. Synchronized egocentric and fixed-viewpoint RGB-D cameras are configured. At each waypoint, RGB and depth images are rendered and projected into 3D point clouds. The framework is designed for extensibility, allowing customization of camera placements, lighting, and sensor modalities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The AutoMoMa framework generated a dataset of over 500,000 physically valid trajectories across 330 scenes, diverse articulated objects, and multiple robot embodiments (Summit Franka, TIAGo, R1). Each trajectory contains 30 joint-space waypoints and synchronized multi-modal observations (RGB-D images and 4,096-point point clouds, rendered at 120 frames per trajectory).

#### 4.1. Dataset Statistics and Diversity Analysis

- **Grasp and Configuration Diversity:** To achieve broad coverage, each object was paired with approximately 20 AO-Grasp annotations, leading to around 30 IK solutions per grasp. These solutions were clustered to retain a diverse set of start states, ensuring trajectories spanned a wide distribution of feasible robot base placements.
- **Pipeline Performance Benchmarks:** AutoMoMa demonstrated a generation throughput of 5,000 episodes per GPU-hour, representing an 80x speedup compared to CPU-based baselines. Throughput decreased in more confined scenes due to increased collision-checking overhead. Analysis of translational and rotational efforts showed the planner's ability to synthesize compensatory whole-body motions in diverse environments.

#### 4.2. Policy Learning Results

Experiments were conducted by training Imitation Learning (IL) policies using the generated data on the Summit Franka mobile manipulator, primarily with DP3 (3D Diffusion Policy) and additionally with DP (Image-based Diffusion Policy) and ACT (Action Chunking with Transformers). The task involved microwave door opening, with success defined by reaching a target angle within 300 steps, averaged over 50 randomized trials.

- **Configuration Space Complexity (Figure 6a):** Fixed-base manipulation achieved 100% success with fewer than 800 trajectories. In contrast, mobile manipulation policies saturated at approximately 70% success on seen configurations even with 3,200 trajectories, highlighting the greater complexity and data requirements of whole-body coordination. A significant performance gap was observed between seen and unseen IK states in single-scene contexts, indicating manifold memorization rather than robust generalization.
- **Environmental Diversity Scaling (Figure 6b):** Increasing the number of training scenes from 1 to 30 consistently improved generalization to unseen environments. Policies trained on limited scenes tended to overfit to specific clutter and geometry, while increased geometric variety led to learning more transferable whole-body strategies.
- **Trajectory Density in Multi-Scene Scaling (Figure 6c):** When the number of scenes was fixed at 30, increasing the per-scene trajectory density yielded improvements comparable to expanding scene diversity. Higher density enabled the model to capture richer distributions of approach angles and motion variations, leading to consistent generalization (approximately 75% success) across both seen and unseen scenes.
- **Architectural Generalization (Figure 7):** Both DP and ACT architectures exhibited consistent performance gains with increasing trajectory density, confirming that the benefits of the AutoMoMa dataset are largely model-agnostic, though DP3 performed superiorly due to its 3D modalities.
- **Performance Stability Across Objects (Figure 8):** The DP3 policy trained with 100k trajectories achieved over 50% success on seen configurations for the majority of five evaluated SAPIEN objects (7221, 11622, 101773, 103634), demonstrating robust scaling across diverse kinematic constraints. Performance variance on specific objects (e.g., ID 46197) was primarily attributed to object articulation limits restricting the robot's valid workspace, rather than data scarcity.

#### 4.3. Additional Ablation Experiments (Appendix E)

- **Scaling Analysis up to 100k Trajectories (Figure A9):** Consistent upward trends in success rates for both seen and unseen settings were observed as trajectory counts increased, confirming improved zero-shot generalization with larger data volumes.
- **Impact of Unique Start States (Figure A10):** Increasing the diversity of initial configurations consistently improved policy generalization, even when the total number of trajectories was fixed, indicating that variety in initial conditions is a significant factor.
- **Pick Task Performance (Figure A11):** A policy trained on 1,000 AutoMoMa trajectories achieved a 51.92% success rate in a rigid-object pick task, outperforming a baseline trained on an equivalent amount of MoMaGen data (35%), suggesting the importance of physically valid whole-body motions.
- **Impact of Grasp-Switching (Figure A12):** Trajectories generated with grasp-switching enabled larger object opening angles by avoiding link collisions, demonstrating its utility in constrained mobile manipulation scenarios.
- **Real-World Validation (Figure A8):** The planning pipeline was validated on a physical UR5-Ridgeback system, successfully executing planned trajectories for drawer opening and cabinet door opening without collisions or constraint violations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents AutoMoMa as a significant development in the field of whole-body mobile manipulation, primarily by addressing the long-standing bottleneck of scalable, high-fidelity data acquisition.

**Significance:**
- **Data Scarcity Resolution:** The framework effectively resolves the issue of data scarcity for complex whole-body mobile manipulation tasks by demonstrating an 80-fold speedup in trajectory generation over prior CPU-based methods. This capability to generate over 500,000 physically valid trajectories across diverse scenarios is a substantial contribution.
- **Bridging Planning and Learning:** AutoMoMa bridges the gap between high-performance motion planning (via GPU-accelerated AKR) and large-scale imitation learning, which has historically been challenged by the lack of appropriate training data.
- **Empirical Validation of Data Needs:** The empirical findings demonstrate that state-of-the-art policies require tens of thousands of demonstrations to achieve high success rates in mobile manipulation, affirming that data volume, not just algorithmic sophistication, is a critical enabling factor for robust policy learning.
- **Comprehensive Dataset:** The generated dataset is comprehensive in its coverage of diverse scenes, articulated objects, and robot embodiments, providing a resource that simultaneously addresses scale, diversity, and kinematic fidelity, a combination previously unavailable.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Acceleration of Embodied AI Research:** By providing a scalable data generation infrastructure, AutoMoMa can accelerate the development and evaluation of imitation learning and other learning-based approaches for complex whole-body robot control.
- **Enhanced Generalization:** The availability of large-scale, diverse, and kinematically valid data can lead to the training of more robust and generalizable policies, enabling robots to operate effectively in varied and unstructured real-world environments.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** Automating the generation of high-quality training data reduces reliance on labor-intensive teleoperation, thereby lowering the cost and effort associated with developing new mobile manipulation capabilities.
- **Foundation for Downstream Tasks:** The multi-modal dataset (joint states, RGB-D, point clouds) can serve as a robust foundation for a wide range of downstream tasks, including visual servoing and affordance detection, beyond direct policy learning.
- **Community Resource:** The framework and potentially the dataset itself could become a valuable resource for the embodied AI community, fostering further research into advanced mobile manipulation capabilities.

The paper acknowledges limitations, such as reliance on known scene geometries and kinematics, and lack of support for dynamic human-robot interaction or deformable objects. Future work aims to integrate learning-based generation methods and develop community-driven tools to extend the framework. Despite these, AutoMoMa presents a significant step towards enabling generalizable whole-body robot policies for real-world deployment.
