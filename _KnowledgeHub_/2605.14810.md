# CaMeRL: Collision-Aware and Memory-Enhanced Reinforcement Learning for UAV Navigation in Multi-Scale Obstacle Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> CaMeRL, a reinforcement learning framework, facilitates robust UAV navigation in environments with obstacles of diverse scales by integrating collision-aware perception and temporal memory, demonstrating superior success rates across a range of obstacle sizes, including ultra-small and extra-large scenarios, and successful real-world deployment.

## Problem

- Existing learning-based UAV navigation methods inadequately address the challenges posed by obstacles of widely varying scales, focusing more on obstacle density or number.
- Small obstacles are difficult for onboard depth sensors to reliably detect and avoid due to their fine granularity, leading to potential collisions.
- Large obstacles frequently occlude the UAV's field of view, causing partial observability and hindering effective pathfinding and detouring decisions.

## Method

- The framework incorporates a Collision-aware Representation Learning module that uses a VAE to extract safety-relevant latent features from raw depth images, supervised by pre-processed collision-aware depth maps.
- A Memory-enhanced Representation Learning module, using an LSTM, processes sequential latent vectors to integrate temporal context, mitigating issues caused by partial observability from large obstacles.
- A policy network, trained with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), uses the combined collision-aware and memory-enhanced observations to generate high-level UAV control commands.

## Results

- CaMeRL consistently achieved the highest success rates in all simulated test environments, notably improving performance in ultra-small obstacle environments (0.77 success rate, compared to MAVRL's 0.29) and extra-large obstacle environments (0.72 success rate, compared to MAVRL's 0.44).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the collision-aware representation and the temporal memory modules synergistically contribute to the enhanced navigation performance across different obstacle scales.
- The system successfully demonstrated real-world navigation on a physical UAV in a dense forest environment featuring multi-scale obstacles, avoiding both slender poles and thick tree trunks at an average speed of approximately 1.4 m/s.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly designing mechanisms for both fine-grained collision awareness and broader spatial context reasoning (via temporal memory) is critical for unified robust navigation across multi-scale obstacle environments.
> - Utilizing a collision-aware preprocessing step to generate supervision targets for representation learning effectively guides the model to encode safety-relevant features without needing runtime computation for safety margins.
> - The optimal temporal interval for memory integration plays a crucial role, with moderate intervals providing the best balance of historical context and relevance for decision-making.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous navigation for Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) is a critical area of research, supporting applications such as aerial photography, surveying, mapping, and logistics. Traditional navigation systems often rely on a sequential pipeline of perception, mapping, planning, and control, which can lead to error accumulation and high computational demands. Learning-based methods, particularly reinforcement learning (RL), have emerged as an alternative, directly mapping observations to control commands, often demonstrating robust performance in complex scenarios.

Within learning-based UAV navigation, much of the existing research has focused on characterizing scene complexity through metrics like the number of obstacles or their spatial density. However, the influence of obstacle *scale*—ranging from ultra-small to extra-large—has received comparatively less attention. This oversight presents challenges: small obstacles are difficult for sensors to reliably detect and are easily overlooked, while large obstacles can cause significant occlusions, obscuring free paths within the UAV's limited field of view. These scale-dependent effects directly impact perception quality and avoidance behavior, leading to performance degradation in environments where diverse obstacle scales coexist.

Current methods often address small and large obstacles separately. Approaches for small obstacles typically focus on enhancing geometric representations or safety modeling to improve detection and avoidance of slender structures. For large obstacles, solutions frequently involve path generation, local sub-goals, or the use of privileged information to navigate around occlusions. However, a unified framework capable of simultaneously ensuring safety around small obstacles and managing partial observability due to large obstacles in a multi-scale environment remains an open challenge.

This work positions itself to address this gap by explicitly considering obstacle scale variations. It proposes a framework that integrates mechanisms specifically designed to handle both the fine-grained detection requirements for small obstacles and the broader spatial context reasoning necessary for navigating around large-obstacle occlusions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust UAV navigation system capable of operating effectively in environments characterized by obstacles of widely varying scales. The authors identify a gap in current learning-based obstacle avoidance methods, which predominantly focus on obstacle number or density while largely overlooking the impact of obstacle scale. This neglect leads to notable performance degradation in "multi-scale obstacle environments," encompassing both ultra-small and extra-large obstacles.

The motivation for this work stems from two specific challenges inherent to multi-scale environments:

1. **Challenges with Small Obstacles**: Small obstacles are inherently difficult for onboard depth sensors to reliably capture due to their limited size and resolution. They can be easily overlooked, leading to potential collisions if not detected and considered with fine granularity. Existing methods that extract purely geometric features from single-frame depth observations often compress or smooth out these fine structures, reducing sensitivity to small collision risks. The objective is therefore to improve the system's ability to preserve and process fine-grained, collision-relevant perceptual cues to ensure timely detection and avoidance of small obstacles.

2. **Challenges with Large Obstacles**: Large obstacles, due to their extensive physical presence, can severely occlude the UAV's field of view. This partial observability can hide viable paths and critical spatial context, making it difficult for the UAV to make informed detouring decisions. Current methods often struggle to reason about spatial context beyond the immediate visual input, leading to suboptimal or failed navigation when large obstacles block direct lines of sight. The objective is to enable the system to integrate observations across temporal frames, thereby mitigating the effects of partial observability caused by large obstacle occlusions and facilitating more reliable pathfinding.

To address these dual challenges, the authors propose CaMeRL, a framework designed to integrate two complementary mechanisms: "collision-aware latent representation" and a "temporal memory module." The overall motivation is to establish a unified and effective reinforcement learning framework for UAV visual navigation that achieves reliable performance across a spectrum of obstacle scales, from ultra-small to extra-large.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CaMeRL framework is a Collision-aware and Memory-enhanced Reinforcement Learning system for UAV visual navigation. It follows a three-stage training pipeline: initial policy training for data collection, representation learning, and final policy training.

**A. Dataset Collection and Collision-Aware Processing**
Initially, a basic goal-reaching policy is trained with randomly initialized and fixed representation modules. This policy generates continuous UAV trajectories in simulated nominal-scale obstacle environments, collecting raw depth-image sequences. These sequences inherently contain temporal correlations, which are utilized in subsequent memory module training.

Following data collection, a collision-aware preprocessing step is applied to the raw depth images ($I_{raw}$) to account for the UAV's body size ($d_{UAV}$) and generate $I_{ca}$:
1. **Edge Inflation**: Obstacle contours are extracted from $I_{raw}$, back-projected to 3D, and inflated by $d_{UAV}$ (UAV's effective size). This generates an edge-inflation image ($I_{infl}$) that emphasizes collision risks near obstacle boundaries.
2. **Distance Correction**: For obstacle interiors, raw depth values are corrected by subtracting half of the UAV size, yielding $I_{corr} = I_{raw} - d_{UAV}/2$.
3. **Fusion**: The final collision-aware depth image is obtained by a pixel-wise minimum operation: $I_{ca} = \min(I_{infl}, I_{corr})$. This image serves as a collision-aware supervision target for representation learning.

**B. Collision-Aware Representation Learning**
A Variational Autoencoder (VAE) is trained to learn collision-aware latent representations from the raw depth images.
1. **Encoder**: The VAE encoder, consisting of six convolutional and two fully connected layers, takes $I_{raw_t}$ as input and outputs the mean ($\mu_t$) and standard deviation ($\sigma_t$) of a Gaussian latent distribution. A latent vector $z_t$ is sampled from this distribution using the reparameterization trick.
2. **Decoder**: The VAE decoder (six deconvolutional layers) reconstructs a collision-aware image ($\hat{I}_{ca_t}$) from $z_t$. The ground-truth $I_{ca_t}$ (from the preprocessing step) is used as the supervision target. This forces the latent space to encode geometric structures and safety-relevant cues without requiring runtime preprocessing.
3. **Loss Function**: The VAE is trained using a loss $L_{CA}$ comprising a mean squared error (MSE) reconstruction loss ($L_{coll}$) between $I_{ca_t}$ and $\hat{I}_{ca_t}$, and a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence regularization term ($L_{KL}$) for the latent distribution: $L_{CA} = L_{coll} + \lambda_{KL}L_{KL}$.

**C. Memory-Enhanced Representation Learning**
After pretraining the VAE, its encoder is used to map raw depth observations $I_{raw_t}$ to latent vectors $z_t$. A single-layer Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network then processes these sequential latent vectors.
1. **LSTM Processing**: The LSTM maintains a 256-dimensional hidden state ($h_t$) which summarizes the temporal context from past observations.
2. **Temporal Reconstruction**: A prediction head maps $h_t$ to two predicted latent codes, $\tilde{z}_{t-T}$ and $\tilde{z}_t$, corresponding to a past frame at time $t-T$ and the current frame at time $t$.
3. **Supervision**: The frozen VAE decoder then reconstructs the corresponding collision-aware images, $\hat{I}_{ca_{t-T}}$ and $\hat{I}_{ca_t}$, from these predicted latent codes. The LSTM is trained to minimize the sum of MSE reconstruction errors between the ground-truth $I_{ca_{t-T}}, I_{ca_t}$ and their reconstructions, $L_{LSTM} = MSE(I_{ca_t}, \hat{I}_{ca_t}) + MSE(I_{ca_{t-T}}, \hat{I}_{ca_{t-T}})$. This encourages $h_t$ to retain temporally consistent scene structure and collision information, particularly when current observations are partially occluded.

**D. Reinforcement Learning for Navigation**
The UAV navigation task is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP).
1. **Policy Network**: A multilayer perceptron policy $\pi_\theta(a_t|o_t)$ is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
2. **Action Space**: The action $a_t$ consists of high-level control commands: desired acceleration in x, y, z directions ($a_x, a_y, a_z$) and yaw rate ($v_{yaw}$).
3. **Observation Space**: The observation $o_t$ concatenates the UAV state information ($s_t$) and the memory-enhanced representation ($h_t$). The UAV state $s_t$ includes horizontal and vertical distances to target, normalized target direction, linear velocity in world frame, Euler-angle attitude, and body-frame angular velocity.
4. **Reward Function**: The reward $r_t$ is a combination of terminal rewards and a dense progress reward:
 - **Terminal Rewards**: Fixed rewards are given for reaching the goal ($r_{arrive}$), colliding with an obstacle ($r_{collision}$), or leaving the flight region ($r_{exceed}$).
 - **Progress Reward ($r_{prog}$)**: This term encourages goal reaching (via distance and direction terms) and penalizes excessive speed, large angular velocities, and unwanted lateral/backward movements, promoting stable and safe flight.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates CaMeRL through systematic simulation and real-world experiments.

**A. Simulation Setup**
Experiments were conducted using Flightmare, a quadrotor simulator, adapting the reinforcement learning setup from MAVRL. A key aspect was the use of seven distinct environment configurations with cylindrical obstacles of varying diameters: one nominal-scale for training (10-50 cm) and six test environments, including ultra-small (1-5 cm), small (10-30 cm), medium (40-80 cm), large (100-200 cm), extra-large (400-500 cm), and mixed-scale (1-500 cm). Obstacle density was varied using Poisson placement. A dataset of approximately 10,000 depth images from 200 episodes in the nominal environment was collected for representation learning.

**B. Representation Analysis**
1. **Effect of Temporal Interval T**: An analysis of the temporal interval $T$ in the memory module showed that a moderate value significantly improved performance. A $T=10$ frames resulted in the highest success rate (0.96) in mixed-scale environments, with success rates decreasing for excessively short or long intervals. This indicates the importance of appropriate historical context.
2. **Ablation Study**: Four policy variants were compared: VanillaRL (no modules), CaRL (collision awareness only), MeRL (memory enhancement only), and CaMeRL (both).
 - VanillaRL exhibited poor performance, particularly in ultra-small, extra-large, and mixed-scale environments.
 - CaRL showed notable gains in ultra-small and mixed environments, indicating improved sensitivity to fine structures.
 - MeRL improved success rates across scales, with more pronounced benefits in medium and large environments, suggesting its role in mitigating partial observability.
 - CaMeRL achieved the highest success rate in all test environments while maintaining competitive flight speeds, demonstrating the complementary advantages of collision awareness and memory enhancement.

**C. Baseline Comparisons**
CaMeRL was compared against MAVRL (RL-based) and Agile-autonomy (supervised learning-based) in the AvoidBench suite across the six test environments.
- CaMeRL consistently achieved the highest success rates in all six environments, while maintaining competitive average speeds.
- Significant gains were observed in extreme-scale settings:
 - **Ultra-small environment**: CaMeRL improved the success rate from 0.29 (MAVRL) to 0.77, indicating enhanced sensitivity to tiny obstacles.
 - **Extra-large environment**: CaMeRL improved the success rate from 0.44 (MAVRL) to 0.72, suggesting stronger robustness under severe occlusions.
- Trajectory analysis visually reinforced these quantitative findings, showing CaMeRL producing more coherent, safer, and efficient detouring behavior compared to baselines, especially in ultra-small and extra-large settings.

**D. Network Attention Visualization**
Grad-CAM visualizations were used to inspect the regions of input depth images that influenced the policy output.
- For small and medium obstacles, CaMeRL assigned stronger attention to the obstacles themselves and potential risk regions further away, while MAVRL's responses were often weaker or more diffuse.
- For large obstacles, CaMeRL's attention was more concentrated on obstacle bodies and contours, including surrounding boundary regions, suggesting improved sensitivity to safety margins. MAVRL's attention was distributed more broadly. These patterns correlated with CaMeRL's superior navigation performance.

**E. Real-World Tests**
CaMeRL was deployed on a 250 mm quadrotor equipped with an Intel RealSense D435 depth camera, NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX, and Pixhawk 6C Mini flight controller.
- The policy ran at 10 Hz, with the Agilicious MPC module converting commands at 100 Hz.
- Sim-to-real transfer was facilitated by aligning camera parameters and fine-tuning the VAE and LSTM modules on approximately 5,000 real-world depth images from the test environment. The policy network remained frozen.
- Flight tests were conducted in a dense forest containing multi-scale obstacles (thick tree trunks and slender poles ~2 cm diameter).
- The quadrotor successfully navigated through this environment, avoiding both types of obstacles and reaching the designated goal at an average speed of approximately 1.4 m/s. This qualitatively demonstrated CaMeRL's ability to be deployed on a real platform for multi-scale obstacle avoidance with onboard sensing and computation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a framework that addresses a previously less explored aspect of UAV navigation: robust operation in environments with diverse obstacle scales. By proposing "Collision-aware and Memory-enhanced Reinforcement Learning" (CaMeRL), the authors provide a unified solution for challenges posed by both ultra-small and extra-large obstacles.

The significance of this work lies in several areas:
- **Addressing a Neglected Problem**: It highlights and provides a solution for the critical issue of obstacle scale variation, which current state-of-the-art methods often fail to adequately handle, leading to performance degradation in complex real-world settings.
- **Complementary Mechanisms**: The integration of collision-aware representation learning and temporal memory modeling represents a systematic approach to tackle fine-grained detection of small obstacles and spatial context reasoning under large-obstacle occlusions simultaneously. This design offers a blueprint for more robust perceptual systems in autonomous agents.
- **Demonstrated Performance Gains**: The comprehensive simulation experiments, including extensive ablation studies and comparisons with established baselines (MAVRL, Agile-autonomy), confirm that CaMeRL consistently achieves higher success rates across various obstacle scales, especially in the challenging ultra-small and extra-large scenarios, while maintaining competitive flight efficiency.
- **Real-World Applicability**: The qualitative real-world outdoor flight tests in a dense forest environment, featuring multi-scale obstacles, provide evidence for the deployability of CaMeRL on a physical UAV platform using onboard resources. This step is crucial for transitioning research from simulation to practical applications.

The potential impact of CaMeRL is substantial for advancing autonomous UAV capabilities. It could enhance the reliability and safety of UAVs in a broader range of complex, unstructured environments, such as dense forests, urban canyons, or disaster zones, where obstacles of varied sizes are common. This could lead to more effective deployments in applications like environmental monitoring, infrastructure inspection, search and rescue, and autonomous delivery, where current systems might struggle with the perception and navigation challenges posed by multi-scale obstacles. The approach contributes to the development of more generalizable and robust visual navigation systems for robotic platforms operating in unknown and dynamic real-world settings. Future work on dynamic obstacles and more complex environments further indicates the potential for continued development and broader impact.
