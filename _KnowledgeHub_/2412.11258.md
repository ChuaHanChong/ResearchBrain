# GaussianProperty: Integrating Physical Properties to 3D Gaussians with LMMs

> [!summary] Summary
> GaussianProperty is a training-free framework that assigns physical material properties to 3D Gaussians by combining SAM for part segmentation and Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) for property reasoning from multi-view 2D images. This approach achieves higher material segmentation accuracy, enables realistic dynamic simulations, and facilitates adaptive robotic grasping with a 100% success rate.

## Problem

- Estimating intrinsic physical properties from visual data is ambiguous and difficult due to their unobservability and the scarcity of labeled ground-truth 3D data.
- Existing methods for integrating physical properties into 3D models often require manual assignment, are scene-specific, or lack generalization across diverse materials.
- Automating physically plausible dynamic simulations and material-sensitive robotic manipulation is hindered by the absence of a generalized and accurate way to attribute properties to 3D representations.

## Method

- A training-free framework, **GaussianProperty**, leverages ==SAM== for precise 2D part segmentation and Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) for physical property reasoning.
- LMMs are guided by a global-to-local knowledge framework, providing contextualized inputs (global image, part mask, cropped part) and structured prompts to infer properties from a curated material library.
- Estimated 2D properties are projected onto ==3D Gaussian== representations using a frequency-based voting strategy across multiple camera views to ensure consistent and robust 3D property attribution.

## Results

- GaussianProperty achieved an mIoU of 55.83% on the ABO dataset for material segmentation, significantly outperforming the Nerf2Physics baseline (25.59%).
- The framework enabled realistic physics-based dynamic simulations by directly assigning properties to 3D Gaussians, streamlining the workflow by eliminating manual property annotation.
- In robotic grasping, it achieved a 100% success rate (100% Picked-up Rate, 100% No-damage Rate) on 16 real-world objects, demonstrating superior adaptive grasping performance over fixed-force baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) can effectively infer diverse physical properties from visual cues by leveraging their extensive pre-trained knowledge, especially when provided with structured prompts and contextual information.
> - Integrating precise 2D segmentation (e.g., SAM) with LMM-based reasoning enables accurate, part-level physical property estimation from multi-view images.
> - A multi-view frequency-based voting mechanism effectively lifts 2D property predictions to consistent 3D Gaussian representations, enhancing robustness against single-view ambiguities and errors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The estimation of physical properties from visual data is a foundational task in computer vision, computer graphics, and robotics. This capability underpins various applications, including augmented reality (AR), physical simulation, and robotic manipulation. However, the field faces significant challenges, primarily stemming from the inherent ambiguities in physical property estimation, the difficulty in acquiring labeled ground-truth data (as intrinsic properties are not directly observable), and limitations imposed by restricted views of object surfaces.

Traditional approaches to physical property estimation often focus on specific material types or properties, requiring task-dependent data collection and demonstrating limited generalization capabilities. Recent advancements have explored integrating physical properties into 3D models, but these often involve manual assignment or methods that are time-consuming and scene-specific. For example, some works leverage Large Language Models (LLMs) to propose candidate materials for objects and estimate properties through kernel regression or to reason about Physically Based Rendering (PBR) materials for texture generation. While these methods demonstrate progress, they do not fully address the challenge of generalized, comprehensive physical property assignment to explicit 3D representations.

In parallel, the field of 3D reconstruction and rendering has seen rapid development with Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and, more recently, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). These explicit 3D representations offer high-quality rendering and efficient processing, with extensions enabling the representation of dynamic scenes. However, existing dynamic rendering methods typically rely on video supervision for deformation and often require manual input for physical properties.

The emergence of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), such as GPT-4V, has introduced models with robust visual understanding and extensive open-world knowledge, mirroring human cognitive abilities to infer physical properties from visual cues. This capability presents an opportunity to overcome the limitations of previous methods.

This work contributes to the landscape by integrating the powerful recognition capabilities of LMMs with the segmentation precision of models like Segment Anything Model (SAM) to estimate diverse physical properties from multi-view 2D images. It then projects these properties onto 3D Gaussian representations, which are an explicit 3D format. By doing so, "GaussianProperty" aims to bridge the gap between advanced 2D visual understanding and explicit 3D physical property assignment, offering a generalized, training-free solution that supports critical downstream applications in simulation and robotics. This represents a step towards enhancing the physical intelligence of 3D models without requiring extensive labeled 3D data or task-specific training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, named GaussianProperty, capable of assigning physical properties of materials to 3D Gaussians. This framework is designed to be training-free, leveraging existing advanced models for its operations.

The detailed objectives and motivations are as follows:

- **Address Ambiguities in Physical Property Estimation**: The core motivation stems from the inherent ambiguities in estimating physical properties from visual data. Intrinsic physical properties are not directly observable, and the task is compounded by the difficulty of acquiring large-scale, labeled ground-truth datasets for training. GaussianProperty seeks to mitigate these issues by utilizing the robust recognition capabilities of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), which possess extensive prior knowledge from vast pre-training data.
- **Integrate 2D Visual Understanding with 3D Representations**: The research aims to develop a module that can reason about physical properties from 2D images by combining the segmentation prowess of models like SAM with the recognition intelligence of LMMs such as GPT-4V. This is motivated by the observation that predicting properties for complex scenes with multiple components requires both global context and detailed local information, which LMMs alone may struggle with. The integration provides a "global-local physical property reasoning module."
- **Project 2D Properties to Explicit 3D Models**: A key objective is to effectively transfer the estimated 2D physical properties to a 3D representation. The choice of 3D Gaussians is motivated by their explicit, point-cloud-like format, which facilitates the direct attribution of properties, and their efficiency in multi-view reconstruction and real-time rendering. A robust projection mechanism is needed to ensure consistency and accuracy across multiple views, addressing occasional errors from single-view estimations.
- **Enable Downstream Applications**: A significant motivation is to demonstrate the practical utility of 3D Gaussians enriched with physical properties. The authors specifically target two crucial application domains:
 - **Physics-based Dynamic Simulation**: Existing simulation methods often require manual assignment of physical properties to 3D models, a time-consuming and unrealistic process. By automating this assignment, the framework aims to enable more realistic and efficient dynamic simulations, such as those leveraging the Material Point Method (MPM).
 - **Material-sensitive Robotic Grasping**: Robotic manipulation tasks often require adaptive grasping forces based on object materials to prevent damage or slippage. Traditional methods face limitations in generality and implementation complexity. The goal is to develop a vision-based strategy that predicts optimal grasping force ranges by inferring material properties, thereby enhancing robotic autonomy and safety.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for a generalized, accurate, and automated approach to physical property estimation for 3D models, overcoming current data and processing limitations by leveraging the advanced capabilities of LMMs and explicit 3D representations to empower practical applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GaussianProperty framework operates as a training-free pipeline designed to assign physical properties to 3D Gaussians using multi-view 2D images and Large Multimodal Models (LMMs). The method systematically progresses from 2D image analysis to 3D property attribution and subsequent application.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The objective is to attribute physical properties to each Gaussian within a reconstructed 3D Gaussian representation. The specific properties required (e.g., material density, Young’s modulus, Poisson’s ratio, friction coefficient, volume, thickness, maximal tolerable curvature) are determined by the target downstream application (physics-based dynamic simulation or robotic grasping).

#### 3.2. Part-Level Segmentation

To account for objects comprising multiple parts with distinct physical characteristics, the framework first performs part-level segmentation on input 2D images.
- **Tool**: The Segment Anything Model (SAM) is utilized for its ability to predict masks with precise boundaries at whole, part, and sub-part levels.
- **Process**: For each image, a grid of 32x32 point prompts is fed to SAM. The resulting masks are then refined by eliminating superfluous masks based on predicted Intersection over Union (IoU) scores, stability scores, and overlap rates. The focus is on middle-level segmentation to balance detail and computational efficiency. This approach aims to dissect objects into semantically coherent parts, addressing ambiguities arising from varied physical attributes within a single object.

#### 3.3. Physics Property Matching

Following segmentation, the segmented 2D parts are matched with their corresponding physical properties.
- **Material Candidates**: A curated library of over 600 materials, grouped into 15 ubiquitous families, is established. This library includes diverse material properties such as density ranges (e.g., aluminum 2700 kg/m³, steel 7750-8050 kg/m³), which serves as a comprehensive reference for the LMM and minimizes ambiguity in predictions.
- **Combined Global-Local Reasoning Module**: To enhance accuracy, a global-to-local knowledge framework is implemented. Instead of directly asking the LMM about a part, which can be ambiguous, the LMM (GPT-4V) is provided with three contextual inputs:
 1. The original global image of the object.
 2. A partial segmentation map with the specific part's mask highlighted.
 3. A cropped image focusing solely on the part.
 This multi-modal input enables the LMM to understand the connection between the global object and its local parts, leveraging global context while focusing on local cues (texture, color, adjacent parts) for precise material identification.
- **Gradual Prompt Guidance**: A structured prompt is designed to guide the LMM (GPT-4V) progressively. The prompt instructs the LMM to first briefly describe the part, then identify its material from the predefined candidates, and finally specify its mass density, Young’s modulus, Poisson’s Ratio, and Shore hardness (A or D). This systematic questioning ensures comprehensive and accurate property inference.

#### 3.4. Lift 2D to 3D via Voting

The 2D physical property estimations are then projected onto the 3D Gaussian representation.
- **3D Reconstruction**: Multi-view 2D images are used to reconstruct an explicit 3D Gaussian representation using 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). This representation consists of a set of 3D Gaussian kernels defined by their centers, opacities, covariance matrices, and spherical harmonic coefficients.
- **Frequency-based Voting Strategy**: For each 3D Gaussian point, its spatial position is projected onto all visible 2D images using camera intrinsic and extrinsic parameters. Visibility is estimated using Gaussian-estimated depth. From each visible 2D view, the corresponding physical property estimated for that point's segment is retrieved. To ensure consistency and robustness against single-view errors, a voting strategy is employed: the attribute (e.g., material type) with the highest frequency across all visible views for a given 3D Gaussian is chosen as its final attributed physical property.

#### 3.5. Material-sensitive Robot Grasping

The attributed physical properties are leveraged for adaptive robotic grasping.
- **Force Range Prediction**: The method calculates a lower bound (F_min) and an upper bound (F_max) for the grasping force.
 - F_min is the minimum force required to lift the object without slipping, dependent on object mass (density and volume), gravity, lifting angle, and friction coefficient between the gripper and object.
 - F_max is the maximum force to apply without causing damage or undesirable deformation, determined by the object's yield stress and maximal tolerable bending curvature (related to Young's modulus, surface area, and thickness).
- **Optimal Grasping Force**: An optimal grasping force (F*) is calculated as a midpoint between F_min and F_max, while being clipped within the robotic gripper's operational range and incorporating a safety margin (η) to avoid operation near input bounds. Physical properties (density, friction, Young's modulus) are derived from the estimated material.

#### 3.6. Physics-based Dynamic Simulation

The framework integrates directly with physics simulation engines, such as the Material Point Method (MPM).
- **Automated Property Assignment**: Unlike previous methods requiring manual property assignment, GaussianProperty directly predicts and assigns essential physical attributes (e.g., density, Young’s modulus, Poisson’s ratio) to each 3D Gaussian point. This automation streamlines the simulation workflow, enabling realistic dynamic interactions based on inferred material properties, and significantly reducing manual effort.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides both quantitative and qualitative evaluations of GaussianProperty, demonstrating its effectiveness across material segmentation, physics-based dynamic simulation, and robotic grasping tasks.

#### 4.1. Datasets and Evaluation Protocol

- **Datasets**: Evaluations were conducted on synthetic and real-captured data from the Amazon Berkeley Objects (ABO) dataset (100 validation objects) and the MVImgNet dataset (100 objects). An additional 16 real-world objects with diverse materials were collected for robotic grasping experiments. Detailed material labels for each part of the objects were manually annotated.
- **Metrics**:
 - **Material Segmentation**: Mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) was used to assess the accuracy of material segmentation maps rendered from 3D Gaussians.
 - **Robotic Grasping**: Picked-up Rate (PUR, object lifted without slipping), No-damage Rate (NDR, no damage/deformation), and Success Rate (SR, both PUR and NDR met) were employed.
 - **Hardness/Mass Estimation (Supplementary)**: Absolute difference error (ADE), Absolute log difference error (ALDE), Absolute percentage error (APE), Min ratio error (MnRE), and Pairwise Relationship Accuracy (PRA).

#### 4.2. Implementation Details

- 30 camera views were used for 3D reconstruction, with 10 views selected for part-level segmentation and property matching.
- 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) was used for 3D reconstruction, trained for 5 minutes on an NVIDIA RTX-A6000 GPU.
- GPT-4V was the LMM used for property matching.
- PhysGaussian was employed for dynamic simulation.
- Robotic grasping experiments utilized a Jacobi.ai JSR-1 robot platform with a TEK CTAG2F90-C gripper.

#### 4.3. Material Segmentation

- **Quantitative Results**: GaussianProperty significantly outperformed the baseline, Nerf2Physics, on both synthetic and real-captured data.
 - On the ABO dataset, GaussianProperty achieved an average mIoU of 55.83%, compared to Nerf2Physics's 25.59%. This improvement was consistent across categories like Wood (61.53% vs 27.87%), Metal (33.41% vs 13.01%), and Ceramic (78.40% vs 38.44%).
 - On the MVImgNet dataset, GaussianProperty achieved an average mIoU of 34.83%, compared to Nerf2Physics's 4.02%.
- **Qualitative Results**: Visualizations (Figure 4) demonstrated that GaussianProperty produced boundary-accurate physical material predictions.

#### 4.4. Generative Dynamics

- The framework successfully integrated the predicted physical properties directly into 3D Gaussians, enabling realistic physics-based dynamic simulations.
- This integration eliminated the need for manual querying and annotation, substantially speeding up dynamic rendering workflows.
- Qualitative examples (Figure 5) showed objects like a chair, garbage, and pillow exhibiting physically plausible movements under applied forces, demonstrating the direct applicability of the estimated properties.

#### 4.5. Robot Grasping

- **Quantitative Results**: GaussianProperty demonstrated superior performance compared to fixed-force baselines (MinGF, MidGF, MaxGF) on 16 real-world objects.
 - GaussianProperty achieved 100% Picked-up Rate (PUR), 100% No-damage Rate (NDR), and 100% Success Rate (SR).
 - In contrast, the MaxGF baseline achieved 100% PUR but only 75% NDR, leading to 75% SR. MidGF achieved 87.50% PUR, 81.25% NDR, and 68.75% SR. MinGF achieved 50.00% PUR, 100% NDR, and 50.00% SR.
- **Qualitative Results**: Sample cases (Figure 6) illustrated the effectiveness of the adaptive grasping force strategy derived from the estimated physical properties, enabling safe and successful manipulation of objects with diverse materials.

#### 4.6. Ablation Study

- **Global-to-Local Knowledge Integration**: This module was shown to be critical for material segmentation accuracy. Without it, using only local part images, the average mIoU was 22.17%. With the integration of global-to-local knowledge, mIoU increased significantly to 51.28%, highlighting its role in accurate material classification.
- **Frequency-based Voting Strategy**: The voting strategy further improved property estimation accuracy. With global-to-local knowledge but without voting, mIoU was 51.28%. Incorporating the frequency-based voting strategy boosted the mIoU to 55.83%, confirming its importance for consistency and reliability across multi-view projections.

#### 4.7. Supplementary Results (Hardness and Mass Estimation)

- **Hardness Estimation**: On a real-captured dataset, GaussianProperty (Ours\*) achieved an ADE of 28.583 and a PRA of 0.686, outperforming NeRF2Physics (ADE 35.917, PRA 0.575).
- **Mass Estimation**: On the ABO dataset, GaussianProperty (Ours\*) showed improved performance with an ADE of 5.960 and MnRE of 0.559 compared to NeRF2Physics (ADE 12.761, MnRE 0.498), indicating more accurate quality assessments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a framework that addresses a fundamental challenge in computer vision and robotics: the accurate and automated estimation of physical properties for 3D models from visual data. Its significance and potential impact span several domains:

- **Advancement in 3D Physical Property Understanding**: The work presents an early exploration into leveraging Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) like GPT-4V for zero-shot physical property estimation, specifically for explicit 3D Gaussian representations. This approach demonstrates a method for robust property inference that can generalize across a wide range of materials without requiring explicit training on property-labeled 3D datasets. This capability is crucial for enhancing the "physical intelligence" of digital 3D models.
- **Enabling Realistic Simulations**: By automatically assigning material properties such as density, Young's modulus, and Poisson's ratio to 3D Gaussians, the framework eliminates the labor-intensive manual annotation previously required for physics-based dynamic simulations. This streamlines simulation workflows, facilitating more realistic and scalable generative dynamics for applications in virtual prototyping, movie production, and scientific modeling.
- **Improved Robotic Manipulation**: The development of a material-sensitive robotic grasping strategy, informed by the estimated physical properties, offers a direct impact on the reliability and safety of robotic systems. By predicting adaptive grasping force ranges (both lower and upper bounds), robots can handle diverse objects more effectively, minimizing slippage for heavy items and preventing damage to delicate ones. This enhances the autonomy and versatility of robots in manufacturing, logistics, and service industries.
- **Bridging 2D and 3D Perception**: The methodology successfully integrates advanced 2D computer vision capabilities (segmentation from SAM and multimodal reasoning from GPT-4V) with 3D reconstruction techniques (3D Gaussian Splatting). This provides a comprehensive pipeline that can derive rich 3D semantic and physical information from multi-view 2D imagery, contributing to a more holistic understanding of the physical world in digital representations.
- **Overcoming Data Scarcity and Ambiguity**: The framework's training-free nature and reliance on pre-trained LMMs for property reasoning directly address the challenges of data scarcity and inherent ambiguities in physical property estimation. By leveraging the vast knowledge embedded within LMMs and a curated material database, the method offers a practical solution where direct ground-truth 3D property data is scarce or expensive to acquire.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: This work lays a foundation for future research in several areas, including more sophisticated material recognition under challenging visual conditions (e.g., ambiguous textures, as noted in limitations), integration with other explicit 3D representations, and expansion to a broader range of physical properties or interactive capabilities for AR/VR.

In conclusion, GaussianProperty offers a significant advancement in automatically attributing physical properties to 3D models, with validated efficacy in critical downstream applications, thereby moving closer to physically intelligent virtual and robotic systems.
