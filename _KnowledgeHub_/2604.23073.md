# RL Token: Bootstrapping Online RL with Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A method named RLT is introduced, which enables general-purpose Vision-Language-Action models to achieve precision and speed for real-world manipulation tasks. By employing a compact "RL token" and lightweight online reinforcement learning, RLT policies demonstrated up to a 3x speedup and substantial success rate increases on precision-critical tasks with only a few hours of real-robot practice, discovering emergent strategies superior to demonstrations.

## Problem

- General-purpose Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models frequently lack the precision and speed needed for "last millimeter" real-world manipulation, constrained by demonstration data quality.
- Real-world robotic reinforcement learning (RL) faces tight sample budgets, making direct fine-tuning of large, billion-parameter VLA models computationally and sample-inefficient.
- Existing RL fine-tuning approaches either require extensive computation by updating entire VLAs or sacrifice the broad generalization capabilities of VLAs by training smaller models from scratch.

## Method

- A pretrained **VLA** is adapted to expose a compact "==RL token==," a learned compressed embedding summarizing internal **VLA** features as an efficient, low-dimensional state for RL.
- A lightweight ==actor-critic== network is trained online on the RL token combined with proprioceptive robot state, operating on short action chunks for increased reactivity.
- The RL actor is directly conditioned on the VLA's sampled reference action chunks and regularized towards them with an L2 penalty, incorporating a dropout mechanism to prevent simple copying.

## Results

- RLT improved critical phases across four manipulation tasks, achieving up to a **3x** speedup on easier tasks and boosting success rates from **20%** to **65%** for more challenging ones, like screw installation.
- On Ethernet insertion, RLT matched the base VLA's success rate while reducing the mean steps to completion by **2x**, demonstrating superior reliability and efficiency compared to alternative RL methods like DAgger and DSRL.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the RL token, action chunking, and behavioral cloning regularization towards VLA reference actions were each essential components contributing to RLT's performance.
- The RLT-trained policy achieved a median episode length of 66 timesteps for Ethernet insertion, significantly faster than expert human teleoperation (146 timesteps) and the base VLA policy (228 timesteps), by discovering emergent compliant insertion strategies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The compact "RL token" effectively encodes manipulation-relevant structure from the VLA, serving as an efficient low-dimensional state for online RL without full VLA fine-tuning.
> - Operating on action chunks, rather than single-step actions, is crucial for effective credit assignment and sample efficiency in real-world tasks with sparse rewards and long horizons.
> - Conditioning and regularizing the RL actor towards VLA-predicted reference action chunks stabilizes online training and enables efficient refinement of robust initial behaviors.
> - The RLT method allows for the emergence of novel and more efficient manipulation strategies, surpassing initial demonstrations and even expert human teleoperation speeds.
