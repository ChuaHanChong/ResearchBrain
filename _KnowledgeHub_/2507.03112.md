# RLVER: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Emotion Rewards for Empathetic Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> RLVER introduces a "Heart-in-the-Loop" reinforcement learning framework that cultivates empathetic abilities in Large Language Models by leveraging verifiable emotion rewards from a self-consistent user simulator. The approach achieves a nearly six-fold increase in empathetic performance on a specialized benchmark for a 7B model, demonstrating practical efficacy comparable to or exceeding much larger proprietary models.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often lack emotional intelligence (EQ) despite advanced cognitive abilities, limiting nuanced human-like communication.
- Existing methods for enhancing LLM emotional intelligence, such as supervised fine-tuning or rule-based systems, suffer from data scarcity, generalization issues, and rigid dialogue structures.
- Applying Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to subjective domains like emotional intelligence faces obstacles due to the lack of stable environments and consistent, verifiable reward signals for multi-turn dialogue.

## Method

- **RLVER** introduces a "==Heart-in-the-Loop==" reinforcement learning paradigm that leverages verifiable emotion rewards generated by a self-consistent user simulation engine (SAGE).
- SAGE employs an LLM-powered "Sentient Agent" as a user simulator, providing deterministic emotion scores (0-100) as reward signals that reflect simulated user satisfaction.
- The framework trains LLMs using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) or Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), incorporating a "Think-Then-Say" template that compels the model to outline its internal reasoning before generating responses.

## Results

- RLVER increased the empathetic performance of a Qwen2.5-**7B**-Instruct model on the Sentient-Benchmark from a baseline of 13.3 to 79.2 (a nearly six-fold increase), with success rates jumping from **2%** to **42%**.
- Models trained with the explicit "think-then-say" scaffold consistently outperformed their "non-thinking" counterparts, showing higher scores in Empathic Depth (3.56 vs. 2.92) and Core Insight (3.44 vs. 2.76).
- RLVER training preserves general LLM capabilities, with performance on benchmarks like MATH500, LiveCodeBench, and IFEval largely maintained or even slightly improved, indicating minimal catastrophic forgetting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit "think-then-say" reasoning scaffolds significantly enhance empathetic depth and core insight in LLMs, improving training stability and overall performance.
> - Overly challenging user simulator configurations do not necessarily lead to better training outcomes; moderately demanding environments provide richer feedback and facilitate broader strategy exploration.
> - Reinforcement learning can effectively shift LLM interaction styles from solution-centric to empathy-oriented, while largely preserving general capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) has seen remarkable progress, primarily in cognitive abilities such as logical reasoning, mathematical problem-solving, and code generation. However, as the authors rightly point out, these models often lack emotional intelligence (EQ) and the nuanced social understanding that is fundamental to authentic human communication. This paper explicitly positions itself at the intersection of three key research areas: Large Language Models (LLMs), Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), and Emotional Support Conversation (ESC).

Existing approaches to enhancing LLM emotional intelligence in dialogue systems have largely relied on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on annotated corpora or rigid rule-based templates. The paper critiques these methods for their inherent limitations, including data scarcity, lack of generalization, and rigid dialogue structures. This creates a clear gap that RLVER aims to fill.

Concurrently, Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for training LLMs in domains where external verifiers can provide deterministic, ground-truth feedback (e.g., correct/incorrect for math problems, executable code for programming tasks). Prior work in "Zero RL" (e.g., DeepSeek-R1) has shown that LLMs can acquire complex skills purely through RL signals without initial supervised warm-up. However, the application of RLVR to dialogue, particularly for subjective, high-level attributes like emotional intelligence, has remained largely unexplored due to challenges in creating stable, scalable, and consistently rewarding conversational environments.

This paper makes a crucial contribution by bridging these two areas. It extends the RLVR paradigm to the domain of emotional intelligence, a highly nuanced and traditionally difficult area for automated evaluation and reward design. By leveraging a psychologically grounded, self-consistent user simulator (SAGE framework), RLVER provides a *verifiable emotion reward* signal, transforming the subjective task of empathic dialogue into an optimizable reinforcement learning problem. This differentiates it from traditional RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback), which often relies on learned reward models that can be prone to reward hacking or instability. RLVER's focus on deterministic and interpretable emotion scores from a simulated user offers a novel, scalable, and stable alternative for training empathetic agents. It represents a significant step towards enabling LLMs to acquire higher-order social cognition without requiring costly human annotations for every interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **cultivate higher-order empathetic abilities in Large Language Models (LLMs) using an end-to-end reinforcement learning framework with verifiable emotion rewards.**

The authors are motivated by several key observations and challenges in the current landscape of LLM development:
1. **EQ-IQ Disparity in LLMs:** While LLMs demonstrate exceptional cognitive prowess in logical and algorithmic reasoning, their emotional intelligence (EQ) lags significantly. This imbalance limits their ability to engage in nuanced human-like interactions, especially in emotionally sensitive contexts.
2. **Limitations of Existing EQ Enhancement Methods:** Current approaches for improving LLM emotional intelligence, such as supervised fine-tuning on counseling datasets or rule-based systems, suffer from inherent drawbacks. These include the scarcity and high cost of annotated empathetic dialogue data, the rigid nature of rule-based systems, and their limited ability to generalize across diverse conversational scenarios.
3. **Untapped Potential of RLVR for Dialogue:** Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has shown great success in other domains (e.g., mathematics, coding) by enabling LLMs to acquire new skills directly from structured feedback. However, its application to dialogue, particularly for complex attributes like emotional intelligence, has been underexplored due to significant technical hurdles.
4. **Obstacles to Applying RLVR in Dialogue:** The paper explicitly identifies three major obstacles:
 - **Lack of a Stable and Scalable Environment:** There's no readily available, realistic, and scalable environment for multi-turn conversational rollouts that can consistently provide feedback on emotional responses.
 - **Absence of Consistent and Verifiable Rewards:** Designing a reliable and generalizable reward system for abstract abilities like emotional intelligence is challenging, as traditional human feedback is costly and often inconsistent.
 - **Stability of Multi-turn RL with LLMs:** Stable training of LLMs using multi-turn reinforcement learning remains an open research problem.

To address these motivations and overcome the identified challenges, the authors propose RLVER. Their goal is to demonstrate that a practical and robust pathway exists for LLMs to acquire genuine empathy and broad conversational capabilities by optimizing directly against verifiable emotion rewards from simulated users, thereby shifting LLM behavior from a solution-centric to a genuinely empathic style.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RLVER framework introduces a novel "Heart-in-the-Loop" reinforcement learning paradigm, which leverages verifiable emotion rewards generated by a self-consistent user simulation engine.

#### 3.1. Emotion Rewards from Self-consistent User Simulation Engine (SAGE)

The core innovation lies in repurposing the Sentient Agent as a Judge (SAGE) framework (Zhang et al., 2025a) as a live, dynamic training environment and reward source.
- **Sentient Agent as User Simulator:** SAGE employs an LLM-powered "Sentient Agent" that mimics human-like emotional responses. Each agent is instantiated with a detailed persona, dialogue background, explicit conversation goal, and hidden intention, ensuring diverse and realistic user simulations.
- **Verifiable Emotion Scores:** During a conversation, the Sentient Agent processes the LLM's response and performs a multi-hop reasoning process to:
 1. **Simulate Emotional Change (f_emo):** Updates its numerical emotion score (0-100) and generates interpretable "inner thoughts" justifying the emotional shift.
 2. **Generate Coherent Reply (f_reply):** Formulates its next response based on its updated emotional state, persona, and goals.
- **Deterministic Rewards:** The emotion score, which is consistently and deterministically derived through principled reasoning, serves as the reward signal. To combat reward hacking (a common issue with learned reward models), RLVER uses these deterministic scores as interpretable proxies for simulated user satisfaction. The final reward ($r_{\phi}$) for a dialogue is the terminal emotion score normalized to, capturing overall conversation quality. Per-turn rewards ($r_t$) can also use instantaneous emotion scores.

#### 3.2. Heart-in-the-Loop Reinforcement Learning

This establishes a closed feedback loop:
- **Dialogue Rollouts:** At each training step, the LLM engages in multi-turn dialogues with a sampled Sentient Agent.
- **Policy Optimization:** The LLM (policy $\pi_{\theta}$) generates responses ($y_t$), receives user replies ($x_t$), and an updated emotion score ($e_t$) from the simulator. The conversation continues until a max turn limit or the emotion score falls below a threshold.
- **Algorithms:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) (Schulman et al., 2017) is the primary on-policy algorithm used, chosen for its stability in high-variance language modeling environments. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024), a more conservative baseline, is also used for comparison.
- **Initialization and Regularization:** The model is initialized from a modestly aligned Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct checkpoint (without domain-specific emotional supervision) to accelerate convergence. Entropy regularization and reward-weighted imitation loss are employed as auxiliary objectives to prevent overfitting to simulator behavior and encourage output diversity.

#### 3.3. Think-Then-Say for Enhanced Emotional Reasoning

A key ablative analysis involves two distinct training templates for the LLM's response generation:
- **Think-Then-Say Template:** This innovative approach requires the model to include an explicit `<think>... </think>` block before every utterance. This compels the model to outline its internal reasoning process, plan its empathetic strategy, consider the user's emotional state, and anticipate the impact of its words before generating the final response. This structure regularizes the policy space, potentially leading to more stable learning and sophisticated behaviors. Outputs violating this format are penalized.
- **Without Think Template (Control):** This standard template prompts the agent for a direct reply without an explicit thinking step, serving as a baseline to measure the contribution of the reasoning scaffold.

By systematically comparing models trained with and without the thinking scaffold, and with different RL algorithms and simulator configurations, the authors aim to gain practical insights into how training strategies, algorithms, and environment design influence the development of empathetic capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments reveal several significant findings regarding RLVER's effectiveness in cultivating empathetic abilities while preserving general LLM capabilities:

#### 4.1. Empathetic Performance Boost

- RLVER drastically improves the empathetic performance of a lightweight Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model. Its Sentient-Benchmark score soars from a baseline of 13.3 to an impressive 79.2 (with PPO + Thinking), representing a nearly six-fold increase. The success rate (final emotion score > 100) jumps from 2% to 42%, while the failure rate (score < 10) drops from 76% to 9%.
- This performance rivals or exceeds that of much larger proprietary models like GPT-4o and Gemini2.5-Flash-Think, demonstrating RLVER's practical efficacy.

#### 4.2. Impact of "Thinking" Scaffold

- Models trained with the explicit "think-then-say" scaffold consistently outperform their "non-thinking" counterparts on both the Sentient Benchmark and a general "Chit Chat" task.
- Qualitative analysis (Figure 2) shows "thinking" models excel in **Empathic Depth** (3.56 vs. 2.92 for PPO) and **Core Insight** (3.44 vs. 2.76 for PPO), indicating a stronger ability to identify user needs and validate emotions. This suggests explicit reasoning fosters higher-order empathetic strategies.
- "Non-thinking" models, however, show greater gains in **Solution Crafting** (3.77 for PPO-non-thinking), possibly compensating for a lack of deep reasoning by focusing on actionable suggestions.

#### 4.3. Comparison of RL Algorithms (PPO vs. GRPO)

- Both PPO and GRPO are effective, but exhibit different learning characteristics.
- GRPO generally provides more stable and balanced improvements across various capabilities.
- PPO, especially when combined with the "thinking" scaffold, can push certain capabilities to a higher performance ceiling (e.g., 79.2 Sentient score for PPO-Thinking vs. 72.0 for GRPO-Thinking).
- Learning curves (Figure 4) show that the "think-then-say" scaffold significantly improves stability and performance for both algorithms, preventing catastrophic collapse observed in PPO-non-thinking.

#### 4.4. Robustness of General Capabilities

- RLVER training achieves specialization in empathetic reasoning with minimal impact on general capabilities. Performance on MATH500, LiveCodeBench (code generation), and IFEval (instruction following) benchmarks is largely preserved or even slightly improved (Table 2), indicating that the fine-tuning does not lead to catastrophic forgetting.

#### 4.5. Impact of Training Environment Difficulty

- Counter-intuitively, more challenging user simulator configurations do not necessarily yield better training outcomes. Models trained with a "vanilla" (moderately demanding) simulator consistently outperform those trained with a "challenging" (stricter, more reserved) simulator (Table 3).
- This suggests that overly strict environments may limit feedback, making it difficult for models to discover useful growth trajectories, whereas moderate environments provide richer feedback, facilitating diverse strategy exploration.
- "Thinking" models demonstrate greater robustness to environment variations than "non-thinking" models, maintaining better performance under challenging conditions (Table 3, Figure 3).

#### 4.6. Shift in Interaction Style and Strategy Use

- RLVER-trained models consistently migrate from a "solution-centric" to an "empathy-oriented" interaction style in the Social Cognition Coordinate space (Figure 7), confirming a fundamental shift in behavior.
- The "think-then-say" scaffold accelerates and amplifies this empathy shift and can even nudge models towards more "creative" (less rigid) interaction styles (Figure 7).
- Analysis of empathetic strategies (Figures 5 and 6) shows that RLVER successfully steers the agent towards more effective and appropriate use of "Praise" and "Deep Empathy" strategies, which initially had negative contributions. The model learns to apply strategies like "Advice Provision" less frequently but with significantly higher positive contribution, indicating a learned nuance in strategy application rather than mere frequency increase. This refutes the "verbosity-as-shortcut" hypothesis, as higher emotion scores are not simply due to longer texts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RLVER framework represents a significant advancement in the field of artificial emotional intelligence and reinforcement learning for large language models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

**A Practical Pathway to Emotionally Intelligent AI:** RLVER demonstrates a concrete and scalable methodology for instilling complex empathetic abilities into LLMs without the prohibitive cost and inconsistency of large-scale human annotation for reward signals. By leveraging verifiable emotion rewards from psychologically grounded simulated users, it offers a pragmatic route to developing agents capable of nuanced emotional understanding and response.

**Bridging the EQ-IQ Gap in LLMs:** The research successfully addresses the long-standing challenge of equipping LLMs with emotional intelligence comparable to their cognitive reasoning abilities. Achieving near-frontier empathetic performance with a 7B open-source model while preserving general capabilities is a major step towards creating more holistic and human-like AI. This balanced development ensures that specialized emotional intelligence does not come at the cost of catastrophic forgetting of other critical skills.

**Novel Application of RLVR to Subjective Domains:** This work extends the highly successful Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards paradigm to a traditionally subjective domain – emotional intelligence. The use of deterministic, interpretable emotion scores from a simulator as reward signals provides a stable and scalable alternative to human feedback, opening new avenues for applying RL to other complex, human-centric objectives where verifiable proxies can be designed.

**Insights into LLM Cognitive Processes:** The systematic analysis of "thinking" vs. "non-thinking" models provides valuable insights into how explicit reasoning scaffolds influence the development of empathetic strategies. It suggests that compelling LLMs to internally plan their responses can lead to deeper empathy and insight, offering a blueprint for designing more sophisticated and controllable language agents. The findings regarding PPO vs. GRPO and environment difficulty also offer practical guidance for future RL-based LLM training.

**Foundations for Advanced Conversational AI:** The ability to develop truly empathetic agents has profound implications for various applications:
- **Mental Health Support:** More sensitive and effective AI companions for emotional support.
- **Customer Service:** Agents that can better understand and de-escalate customer frustration.
- **Education and Tutoring:** AI tutors that can adapt to students' emotional states.
- **Digital Companions:** More engaging and human-like virtual assistants and companions.
- **Robotics and Human-Robot Interaction:** Improving social interaction capabilities for physical robots.

**Catalyzing Future Research:** By releasing code, checkpoints, and environment scripts, the authors actively encourage further research. Future work can build upon RLVER to explore richer multi-party simulations, adaptive persona switching, and the integration of multimodal affective cues (e.g., tone of voice, facial expressions) to achieve truly holistic social intelligence in AI agents. This work lays a strong foundation for the next generation of socially intelligent AI.
