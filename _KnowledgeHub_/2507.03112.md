# RLVER: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Emotion Rewards for Empathetic Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> RLVER introduces a "Heart-in-the-Loop" reinforcement learning framework that cultivates empathetic abilities in Large Language Models by leveraging verifiable emotion rewards from a self-consistent user simulator. The approach achieves a nearly six-fold increase in empathetic performance on a specialized benchmark for a 7B model, demonstrating practical efficacy comparable to or exceeding much larger proprietary models.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often lack emotional intelligence (EQ) despite advanced cognitive abilities, limiting nuanced human-like communication.
- Existing methods for enhancing LLM emotional intelligence, such as supervised fine-tuning or rule-based systems, suffer from data scarcity, generalization issues, and rigid dialogue structures.
- Applying Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to subjective domains like emotional intelligence faces obstacles due to the lack of stable environments and consistent, verifiable reward signals for multi-turn dialogue.

## Method

- **RLVER** introduces a "==Heart-in-the-Loop==" reinforcement learning paradigm that leverages verifiable emotion rewards generated by a self-consistent user simulation engine (SAGE).
- SAGE employs an LLM-powered "Sentient Agent" as a user simulator, providing deterministic emotion scores (0-100) as reward signals that reflect simulated user satisfaction.
- The framework trains LLMs using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) or Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), incorporating a "Think-Then-Say" template that compels the model to outline its internal reasoning before generating responses.

## Results

- RLVER increased the empathetic performance of a Qwen2.5-**7B**-Instruct model on the Sentient-Benchmark from a baseline of 13.3 to 79.2 (a nearly six-fold increase), with success rates jumping from **2%** to **42%**.
- Models trained with the explicit "think-then-say" scaffold consistently outperformed their "non-thinking" counterparts, showing higher scores in Empathic Depth (3.56 vs. 2.92) and Core Insight (3.44 vs. 2.76).
- RLVER training preserves general LLM capabilities, with performance on benchmarks like MATH500, LiveCodeBench, and IFEval largely maintained or even slightly improved, indicating minimal catastrophic forgetting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit "think-then-say" reasoning scaffolds significantly enhance empathetic depth and core insight in LLMs, improving training stability and overall performance.
> - Overly challenging user simulator configurations do not necessarily lead to better training outcomes; moderately demanding environments provide richer feedback and facilitate broader strategy exploration.
> - Reinforcement learning can effectively shift LLM interaction styles from solution-centric to empathy-oriented, while largely preserving general capabilities.
