# Enhancing Multimodal In-Context Learning for Image Classification through Coreset Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Key-based Coreset Optimization (KeCO) introduces a framework to refine a compact set of in-context learning examples for Large Vision-Language Models by iteratively updating their visual features using information from larger, 'untapped' datasets. Its diversity-based variant, KeCO-DS, improves average performance by over 20% for OpenFlamingo-3B and 10% for IDEFICS-8B compared to using an unrefined coreset, and notably surpasses methods using the entire, larger support set by approximately 10% and 4% respectively.

## Problem

- Existing in-context learning (ICL) methods for Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) incur high computational and memory costs due to storing and processing entire support sets for demonstration selection.
- Traditional coreset selection techniques are often ineffective for multimodal (image) data, struggling to adequately represent diverse visual features within a small, fixed subset.
- Conventional coreset strategies lead to information loss by discarding the majority of the support set, leaving valuable 'untapped' samples unused in downstream inference.

## Method

- Introduces ==Key-based Coreset Optimization (KeCO)==, an iterative framework that refines a compact coreset of image features by leveraging 'untapped' data from the larger support set.
- Employs a ==feature-level update mechanism== where visual 'keys' of coreset samples are adjusted towards averaged features of related untapped samples using linear interpolation.
- Investigates three target sample selection strategies (==Random, Similarity-based, Diversity-based==) to determine which coreset samples should be updated by incoming untapped data, and extends this to simulated online scenarios.

## Results

- KeCO-DS achieved average performance improvements exceeding **20%** for OpenFlamingo-3B and **10%** for IDEFICS-8B over unrefined coresets of the same size.
- KeCO-DS consistently surpassed methods using the entire, five-times larger support set, demonstrating approximately **10%** improvement for OpenFlamingo-3B and **4%** for IDEFICS-8B on average.
- The framework demonstrated robust and stable performance in simulated online scenarios, maintaining superior results compared to baselines when data arrives sequentially, with KeCO-DS achieving approximately **5%** improvement on CUB-200 for Qwen2-VL.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically refining coreset representations with 'untapped' data significantly enhances ICL performance, often outperforming methods using the full, unoptimized support set.
> - Diversity-based target sample selection (KeCO-DS), which updates coreset samples with *least similar* query samples of the same class, is most effective for filtering out category-irrelevant attributes.
> - KeCO brings larger performance gains to fine-grained image classification tasks, suggesting its utility in improving LVLM understanding of subtle visual distinctions often overlooked by models pre-trained on coarse-grained data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical and rapidly evolving area at the intersection of large-scale artificial intelligence models, multimodal learning, and efficient knowledge transfer. It sits squarely within the paradigm of **In-Context Learning (ICL)**, a powerful capability of Large Language Models (LLMs) and, more recently, Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), where models adapt to new tasks by conditioning on a few demonstrations without requiring parameter updates.

The broader research landscape for ICL has seen significant advancements since its popularization, demonstrating its efficacy in various downstream tasks. However, a major challenge has been the **selection of informative in-context examples (ICEs)**. Early approaches often relied on random selection, which proved suboptimal, leading to the development of more sophisticated strategies like similarity-based or diversity-based retrieval. These methods, while effective, incur substantial computational and memory overhead, particularly when dealing with large support sets, as they often require storing and traversing the entire dataset.

The work directly targets these limitations, especially in the **multimodal domain** (image classification with LVLMs). While coreset selection techniques have been explored in Natural Language Processing (NLP) to identify smaller, representative subsets and reduce retrieval costs, their direct application to visual data presents unique challenges. Visual inputs are inherently richer and more diverse than textual tokens, making it harder for a fixed, small coreset to adequately capture the underlying feature space and semantic variations within a category. Moreover, existing coreset methods typically discard a large portion of the support set, leading to unnecessary information loss from "untapped" samples.

This paper distinguishes itself by proposing **Key-based Coreset Optimization (KeCO)**, which is claimed to be the "first attempt to introduce coreset optimization into the ICL paradigm" for multimodal settings. Instead of merely selecting a fixed coreset and discarding the rest, KeCO innovatively leverages the "untapped data" to *refine and enrich* the representations within a compact coreset. This approach marks a significant shift from static coreset construction to a dynamic optimization process, aiming to provide a more compact, informative, and adaptive set of demonstrations for LVLMs. The emphasis on resource-constrained scenarios and online learning also aligns with the growing demand for efficient and practical AI systems that can continuously learn from data streams.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent limitations of existing In-Context Learning (ICL) strategies for Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), particularly in the context of image classification:

1. **High Computational and Memory Costs:** Current methods for selecting informative demonstrations, such as similarity-based retrieval, necessitate storing and processing the entire support set. This becomes prohibitively expensive for large datasets, hindering the scalability and practical deployment of LVLMs in resource-constrained environments.
2. **Ineffectiveness of Existing Coreset Methods for Vision:** While coreset selection has shown promise in NLP by identifying small, representative subsets, these methods are often less effective for image classification. Visual data's high dimensionality, rich features, and diverse variations (e.g., pose, background) within a single class make it challenging for a fixed, small coreset to adequately represent the entire feature space.
3. **Information Loss from Untapped Samples:** Conventional coreset strategies discard a significant portion of the support set after initial selection. Even though these samples might be evaluated during selection, their inherent information remains unused during downstream inference, leading to unnecessary data loss and potentially suboptimal ICL performance.

Given these limitations, the **key objective** of this paper is to propose a novel and efficient framework called **Key-based Coreset Optimization (KeCO)**. KeCO aims to construct a compact yet highly informative coreset for multimodal ICL in image classification tasks by actively leveraging the "untapped" data.

More specifically, the paper's objectives include:
- To develop a mechanism to update and refine coreset representations at the feature level, using information from the previously discarded (untapped) samples.
- To investigate different strategies for selecting which coreset samples should be updated by incoming untapped data, thereby understanding the dynamics of effective coreset evolution.
- To demonstrate that this coreset optimization process can significantly enhance LVLM ICL performance for image classification, even surpassing methods that use the entire, unoptimized support set.
- To evaluate the framework's practical applicability in a simulated online scenario, where data arrives sequentially, showcasing its robustness and efficiency in real-world streaming data settings.
- To shed light on optimal coreset initialization methods and the impact of hyperparameters on the optimization process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is the **Key-based Coreset Optimization (KeCO)** framework, designed to iteratively refine a compact coreset using information from the larger untapped portion of the support set. KeCO operates in three main stages:

1. **Key-based Coreset Initialization:**
 - **Initial Coreset (C) Formation:** A small, initial coreset of size `m` is randomly sampled from the entire support set `S` (size `n`, where `m < n`). This initialization ensures class balance by selecting an equal number of samples per category. The remaining samples form the "untapped set" `S' = S - C`.
 - **Key Extraction:** For each sample in the initial coreset `C`, its visual feature is extracted using a pre-trained vision encoder (e.g., CLIP, Siglip). These features serve as "keys" (`k`) that represent the visual content of the sample and will be used for both retrieval and subsequent updates.
 - **Alternative Initialization (Explored in Analysis):** The authors also explore K-center initialization (to maximize diversity within each class) and InfoScore initialization (prioritizing samples with high information gain based on LVLM feedback) in their ablation studies.

2. **Target Sample Selection:**
 - For each "query sample" `s` from the untapped set `S'`, the framework needs to identify a "target sample" `t` within the coreset `C` to be updated. To maintain class relevance, `t` is always chosen from the subset of `C` that shares the same class label as `s` (denoted `C_c`).
 - Three strategies are investigated for selecting `t`:
 - **Random Selection (RS):** A target sample is chosen uniformly at random from `C_c`.
 - **Similarity-based Selection (SS):** The target sample `t` from `C_c` that is most similar to `s` (based on cosine similarity of their keys/features) is selected. The intuition is to update `t` with highly relevant `s`.
 - **Diversity-based Selection (DS):** The target sample `t` from `C_c` that is *least* similar to `s` (based on cosine similarity) is selected. This counter-intuitive strategy aims to diversify the coreset's representations by incorporating information from visually distinct but class-consistent samples.

3. **Key-based Sample Update:**
 - The untapped set `S'` is processed in mini-batches. For each batch, query samples `s` are grouped by their selected target sample `t_j` in the coreset `C`, forming subsets `S'_i,j`.
 - The key `k_t_j` of each target sample `t_j` is updated using a linear interpolation rule:
 `k'_t_j = k_t_j - α * (1 / |S'_i,j|) * Σ_(s ∈ S'_i,j)_ (k_t_j - φ(I_s))`
 where `α` is the update rate (0 to 1), `|S'_i,j|` is the number of query samples grouped with `t_j`, and `φ(I_s)` is the visual feature of query image `I_s`. This effectively moves `k_t_j` towards the averaged features of its associated query samples.
 - This update process is repeated for a specified number of epochs (`e`).

**Simulated Online Scenario:**
KeCO is extended to an online setting where samples arrive sequentially:
- **Online Initialization:** A "Filling-based" strategy is used. Incoming samples are added directly to the coreset until a per-class quota (`m/j`) is met. After this, subsequent samples are used for updates.
- **Online Update:** For each incoming sample `s`, a target `t` is selected (using RS, SS, or DS), and `t`'s key is immediately updated:
 `k'_t = (1 - α)k_t + α * φ(I_s)`
 This allows continuous learning without storing the entire stream.

**Inference and Evaluation:**
After the coreset `C` has been optimized into `C'`, for a given test image `Î`, the top-k most relevant samples are retrieved from `C'` based on the similarity between `φ(Î)` and the stored keys `k`. These retrieved samples form the in-context sequence, which is then fed into a frozen LVLM (e.g., OpenFlamingo, IDEFICS, Qwen2-VL) for label prediction. The performance is measured using top-1 accuracy on coarse-grained (ImageNet-100) and fine-grained (CUB-200, Stanford Dogs) image classification datasets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the significant effectiveness of Key-based Coreset Optimization (KeCO) across various settings and benchmarks:

1. **Substantial Performance Enhancement over Baselines:**
 - KeCO methods (KeCO-RS and KeCO-DS) consistently and significantly outperform "Fewshot in Coreset" (FS-IC), which uses an unrefined coreset of the same size. For OpenFlamingo-3B (OF-3B), KeCO-DS achieved average improvements of over 20%, and for IDEFICS-8B (IDE-8B), over 10%. This clearly validates the benefit of optimizing coreset representations.
 - Remarkably, KeCO-RS and KeCO-DS even surpass "Fewshot in Support Set" (FS-IS), which utilizes the *entire* (five times larger) support set without optimization. KeCO-DS provided approximately 10% improvement for OF-3B and 4% for IDE-8B over FS-IS on average. This highlights KeCO's ability to create a more informative and effective set of demonstrations from a much smaller memory footprint.

2. **Critical Role of Target Sample Selection:**
 - **Diversity-based Selection (KeCO-DS) consistently yields the best results.** The rationale is that by averaging the key of a target sample with the features of a *least similar* query sample from the same class, KeCO-DS effectively filters out category-irrelevant attributes (like diverse backgrounds) while preserving and reinforcing invariant, category-relevant features. This leads to more robust and focused representations within the coreset.
 - **Random Selection (KeCO-RS) also performs well**, showing substantial gains over FS-IC and FS-IS.
 - **Similarity-based Selection (KeCO-SS) performs the worst**, sometimes even underperforming FS-IC. This is attributed to the merging of highly similar samples, which can inadvertently retain and reinforce category-irrelevant or misleading attributes (e.g., specific backgrounds common to similar images), thereby polluting the coreset's representations and misleading the LVLMs during inference.

3. **Greater Impact on Fine-grained Classification:**
 - KeCO methods exhibit larger performance gains on fine-grained image classification datasets (CUB-200, Stanford Dogs) compared to the coarse-grained ImageNet-100. For instance, OF-3B saw a 13.16% improvement on CUB-200 but only 6.34% on ImageNet-100 with KeCO-DS (2-shot).
 - This finding suggests that LVLMs, often pre-trained on coarse-grained knowledge, benefit more from KeCO's ability to synthesize and refine fine-grained category distinctions in the coreset, addressing their inherent knowledge gaps in subordinate-level categories.

4. **Robust Performance in Simulated Online Scenarios:**
 - In a simulated online scenario, where untapped samples arrive sequentially, KeCO-RS and KeCO-DS maintain strong and stable performance, consistently outperforming both FS-IC and FS-IS.
 - Evaluations with the high-performance commercial model Qwen2-VL further confirm KeCO-DS's efficacy in online settings, achieving approximately 5% improvement on CUB-200 from an already high baseline. This underscores KeCO's practical value for real-world applications with streaming data and constrained resources.

5. **Insights from Coreset Initialization Analysis:**
 - Surprisingly, **random initialization** of the coreset proved effective when followed by KeCO optimization, often outperforming more sophisticated K-center (diversity-maximizing) and InfoScore (information gain-maximizing) initializations.
 - K-center initialization, popular in active learning, was found to be inferior, suggesting that excessive diversity in ICL demonstrations might introduce irrelevant noise that interferes with LVLM inference.
 - InfoScore also underperformed, possibly due to imperfect alignment between vision encoders and LLMs in current LVLMs, leading to a reliance on textual over visual information when assessing "informativeness."
 - Crucially, the KeCO framework significantly improved performance across *all* initialization strategies, reinforcing its robust optimization capabilities regardless of the initial coreset quality.

6. **Impact of Untapped Data and Coreset Size:**
 - Increasing the amount of untapped data used for optimization generally leads to better performance, as more information is integrated into the coreset.
 - While larger coreset sizes improve baseline performance (FS-IC), in online scenarios where untapped data can only be utilized once, it was observed that a configuration with more untapped data (even with a slightly smaller coreset) could be more beneficial than simply a larger initial coreset.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The KeCO framework introduces a significant advancement in the field of multimodal In-Context Learning, offering substantial theoretical and practical implications:

1. **Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability for ICL:** KeCO directly addresses the critical bottleneck of high computational and memory costs associated with selecting demonstrations from large support sets. By creating a compact yet highly informative coreset that even outperforms inference with the full support set, KeCO makes ICL with LVLMs more efficient and scalable, enabling their deployment in resource-constrained environments. This efficiency is crucial for the broader adoption of advanced AI models.

2. **Novel Paradigm for Coreset Construction:** This work pioneers the concept of "coreset optimization" within the ICL paradigm, particularly for multimodal data. Unlike traditional coreset methods that statically select a subset and discard the rest, KeCO's dynamic, feature-level update mechanism leverages previously "untapped" data to enrich representations. This opens new avenues for research into adaptive and continuously evolving coresets.

3. **Improved LVLM Performance in Challenging Tasks:** The substantial performance gains, especially in fine-grained image classification, highlight KeCO's ability to help LVLMs overcome their inherent limitations in discerning subtle visual distinctions. This makes LVLMs more robust and effective for complex visual tasks where nuanced understanding is required, expanding their applicability in areas like medical imaging, species identification, and quality control.

4. **Practical Applicability in Real-world Scenarios:** The strong performance in the simulated online scenario is particularly impactful. Many real-world applications involve data streams rather than static datasets, requiring models to adapt continuously. KeCO's ability to integrate new information with minimal adjustments under streaming conditions makes it highly relevant for online learning systems, real-time analytics, and adaptive AI agents.

5. **Deeper Insights into Multimodal ICL Dynamics:** The analysis of different target sample selection strategies provides valuable insights into what constitutes an "informative" update for visual feature representations. The finding that "diversity-based selection" (updating with least similar samples) is most effective for preserving category-relevant information while blurring irrelevant attributes is a counter-intuitive but significant contribution to understanding how to best refine visual features for ICL. It challenges prior assumptions about maximizing similarity or general diversity.

6. **Guidance for Future LVLM Development:** The observation that KeCO brings larger gains to fine-grained datasets and the varying performance of different coreset initialization strategies (e.g., K-center, InfoScore) offer crucial guidance for future LVLM research. It suggests that while KeCO can mitigate weaknesses, there is still a need for LVLMs trained on more balanced and fine-grained corpora, and for better alignment between visual and linguistic components.

By making multimodal ICL more efficient, effective, and adaptive, KeCO contributes significantly to the development of more capable and deployable Large Vision-Language Models, fostering their broader impact across various scientific and industrial applications. The public release of code further ensures reproducibility and encourages subsequent research building upon this innovative framework.
