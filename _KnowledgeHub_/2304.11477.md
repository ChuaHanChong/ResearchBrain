# LLM+P: Empowering Large Language Models with Optimal Planning Proficiency

> [!summary] Summary
> The LLM+P framework integrates large language models with classical planners to enable natural language-driven, optimal planning for robot tasks. This approach consistently produces correct and often optimal plans across various domains, demonstrating superior performance compared to directly prompting LLMs for planning.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with complex, long-horizon reasoning and adherence to strict logical constraints required for effective planning, often producing infeasible or incorrect plans.
- Classical automated planners are highly proficient at finding correct and optimal plans, but require problems to be formally specified in symbolic languages like PDDL, which is inaccessible to average users.
- Bridging the gap between natural language instructions and reliable, optimal plan generation for robotic systems remains a challenge.

## Method

- A three-stage pipeline where the LLM first translates a natural language problem description into a PDDL problem file, aided by a pre-provided PDDL domain file and an in-context learning example.
- The generated PDDL problem is then fed into an off-the-shelf classical planner (==FAST-DOWNWARD==) to find a correct and/or optimal sequence of actions.
- Finally, the LLM translates the symbolic PDDL plan output by the planner back into a human-readable natural language format.

## Results

- LLM+P consistently produced optimal plans for the majority of benchmark problems across seven diverse robot planning domains, achieving significantly higher success rates than LLM-only approaches.
- Direct LLM planning methods (LLM-AS-P, including with Tree of Thoughts) largely failed to generate feasible or correct plans for complex, constrained problems, demonstrating their lack of underlying logical reasoning.
- A real-world home robot demonstration validated LLM+P's ability to generate an optimal task plan from natural language, outperforming a directly prompted LLM which produced a sub-optimal plan.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models (LLMs) excel at translating natural language into structured formats like PDDL, but are not inherently proficient at complex, long-horizon planning.
> - Delegating symbolic planning and optimal search to dedicated classical planners, while using LLMs for human-understandable input/output, creates a robust and effective hybrid system.
> - In-context learning, via a single natural language-to-PDDL example, is essential for the LLM to accurately generate the PDDL problem file.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "LLM+P" operates at the intersection of two rapidly evolving fields: Large Language Models (LLMs) and Automated Planning. The broader research landscape is characterized by both the astonishing capabilities and inherent limitations of modern LLMs, alongside the long-standing strengths of classical symbolic AI.

**LLMs' Emergence and Limitations:** The advent of LLMs like GPT-4 and ChatGPT has revolutionized human-computer interaction, demonstrating unprecedented "linguistic competence"—the ability to generate plausible and grammatically correct text. However, as the authors rightly point out, these models often lack "functional competence" or true understanding, particularly when it comes to complex reasoning, arithmetic, or long-horizon planning. Examples abound where LLMs fail on straightforward logical or planning tasks that require a deep understanding of cause-and-effect and sequential decision-making. This failure stems from their core design as probabilistic next-token predictors rather than symbolic reasoners. Researchers have observed that LLMs often struggle with tasks requiring multi-step logical deduction, adherence to strict constraints, or optimality guarantees, frequently producing "hallucinations" or infeasible plans.

**Classical Planning's Strengths:** In stark contrast, classical automated planning, a cornerstone of symbolic AI, excels precisely where LLMs falter. Techniques like those based on the Planning Domain Definition Language (PDDL) use efficient search algorithms to find correct, and often optimal, sequences of actions to achieve a given goal. These planners are sound (guaranteed correctness if a solution is found) and complete (guaranteed to find a solution if one exists). They have been widely applied in robotics (e.g., Shakey the robot, PRODIGY, HTN systems) for tasks ranging from mobile robot navigation to complex manipulation. However, their primary limitation is the need for problems to be formalized in a highly structured, symbolic language (like PDDL), which is inaccessible to average users and often time-consuming for experts to write.

**Bridging the Gap: Existing Hybrid Approaches:** The idea of augmenting LLMs with external tools is a growing trend. Researchers have integrated LLMs with calculators for numerical tasks, search engines for information retrieval, and code interpreters for logical execution. Similarly, in the context of planning, several recent works have explored combining LLMs with planning capabilities. Some approaches attempt to make LLMs directly perform planning through sophisticated prompting (e.g., Chain-of-Thought, Tree-of-Thoughts) or fine-tuning on planning-related datasets. Other efforts, like SayCan, leverage LLMs to ground language commands in robot affordances. However, many of these approaches still rely on the LLM's inherent reasoning, which, as demonstrated by previous benchmarks and this paper, often falls short in guaranteeing correctness or optimality for complex, long-horizon problems. Concurrent work has also explored LLM-PDDL integration, but often with limited scope or less rigorous domain definitions.

**LLM+P's Unique Position:** LLM+P distinguishes itself by explicitly *not* relying on the LLM for the planning itself. Instead, it strategically assigns the LLM a role it excels at: language translation and structured text generation. By offloading the complex, symbolic reasoning to a proven classical planner, LLM+P aims to achieve the "best of both worlds": the natural language flexibility of LLMs combined with the correctness and optimality guarantees of classical planners. This approach positions LLM+P as a robust framework for creating reliable, natural language interfaces for robot planning, addressing a critical bottleneck in embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind LLM+P stems from the complementary strengths and weaknesses of Large Language Models (LLMs) and classical symbolic planners. While LLMs exhibit remarkable zero-shot generalization and natural language processing capabilities, they notoriously struggle with tasks requiring precise, long-horizon reasoning and adherence to strict logical constraints, which are fundamental to effective planning. Conversely, classical planners are highly proficient at finding correct, and often optimal, plans for complex problems, but they demand that these problems be formally specified in a structured language like PDDL, which is opaque to human users and laborious to construct.

Given this dichotomy, the key objective of LLM+P is to **empower Large Language Models with optimal planning proficiency by seamlessly integrating the strengths of classical planners into the LLM workflow.** The authors aim to enable LLMs to reliably solve long-horizon robot planning problems, returning correct or even optimal plans, all initiated by a natural language description from a user.

More specifically, the framework pursues three distinct yet interconnected aims:
1. **Natural Language to PDDL Conversion:** To accurately convert a natural language description of a planning problem into a formal PDDL problem file. This leverages the LLM's linguistic competence as a "machine translation" task.
2. **Leveraging Classical Planners for Solution Generation:** To utilize a robust classical planner to efficiently identify a correct and/or optimal plan based on the PDDL formulation. This offloads the complex, symbolic search and reasoning, where classical planners excel, from the LLM.
3. **PDDL Plan to Natural Language Translation:** To translate the symbolic PDDL plan generated by the classical planner back into a human-readable natural language format, making the robot's actions intelligible to the user.

Crucially, a foundational motivation for LLM+P is to achieve these objectives **without altering the LLMs themselves**, meaning no fine-tuning or re-training of the LLM parameters is required. This commitment underscores a desire for a broadly applicable, plug-and-play solution that can enhance existing LLMs' capabilities through external tool integration. The work implicitly posits that the strength of LLMs lies not in their ability to reason like a symbolic planner, but in their capacity to bridge the gap between human language and structured computational formalisms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LLM+P framework proposes a three-stage pipeline that strategically delegates tasks to either the Large Language Model (LLM) or a classical symbolic planner, exploiting their respective strengths. The core insight is to treat the conversion between natural language and PDDL as a "machine translation" problem, a task at which LLMs are known to excel, while leaving the complex combinatorial search for a plan to a dedicated, sound, and complete planner.

**A. LLM+P Pipeline Overview (Referencing Figure 1):**
1. **Natural Language to PDDL Translation (LLM as PDDL Writer):**
 - The process begins with a user providing a planning problem described in natural language (e.g., "Move this block to that location").
 - The LLM's primary role in this stage is to convert this natural language prompt into a PDDL *problem file*. This file specifies the objects involved, their initial state, and the desired goal conditions.
 - To facilitate this translation, the LLM is provided with two crucial pieces of information:
 - A **PDDL Domain File**: This file, assumed to be provided by a human expert prior to addressing any specific problem instance, defines the general rules of the world, including available predicates (properties of objects and relations) and actions (their preconditions and effects). This is domain-agnostic but specific to the set of actions the robot can perform.
 - An **In-Context Learning Example**: A single, simple example consisting of a natural language problem description and its corresponding ground-truth PDDL problem file is provided as context. This demonstration is critical for the LLM to learn the desired PDDL syntax, predicate usage, and the mapping from natural language concepts to their PDDL equivalents for that specific domain. The paper shows that without this context, LLMs frequently generate syntactically plausible but logically incorrect or incomplete PDDL.
2. **Classical Planning (Planner):**
 - Once the LLM generates the PDDL problem file, it is combined with the pre-provided PDDL domain file.
 - This complete PDDL problem definition is then fed into an off-the-shelf, general-purpose classical planner. The authors specifically use the **FAST-DOWNWARD planner**, exploring both its optimal (SEQ-OPT-FDSS-1) and sub-optimal (LAMA) aliases.
 - The planner's task is to efficiently search for and identify a sequence of actions (a PDDL plan) that transitions from the initial state to a goal state, satisfying all preconditions and achieving the desired effects. The planner guarantees correctness and, depending on the alias, optimality.
3. **PDDL Plan to Natural Language Translation (LLM as Plan Translator):**
 - The symbolic PDDL plan output by the classical planner (e.g., `(unstack b5 b3) (putdown b5)`) is often difficult for humans to interpret directly.
 - The LLM is invoked again to translate this structured PDDL plan back into a more intuitive, natural language sequence of steps that a human user or a robot action executor can understand.

**B. Key Assumptions:**
The LLM+P methodology relies on a few key assumptions:
1. **Trigger Mechanism:** A robot system (or higher-level AI) knows when to detect that a user's natural language prompt is a planning problem suitable for the LLM+P pipeline. (Acknowledged as a future research direction.)
2. **Domain PDDL Availability:** For each specific problem domain (e.g., BLOCKSWORLD, BARMAN), a human expert has provided a complete and correct PDDL domain file. This defines the actions and predicates for that domain.
3. **In-Context Example Availability:** A minimal example of a natural language problem description paired with its corresponding PDDL problem file is available for the LLM to use for in-context learning.

**C. Experimental Setup:**
- **LLM Model:** GPT-4 (most recent model as of Sept 2023) is used with temperature set to 0 (deterministic responses) and top probability response.
- **Classical Planner:** FAST-DOWNWARD planner, with both optimal (SEQ-OPT-FDSS-1) and sub-optimal (LAMA) configurations. A maximum search time of 200 seconds is applied.
- **Benchmarks:** Seven diverse robot planning domains, adapted from International Planning Competitions, each with 20 automatically generated tasks: BLOCKSWORLD, BARMAN, FLOORTILE, GRIPPERS, STORAGE, TERMES, TYREWORLD. Each problem includes NL description and ground-truth PDDL.
- **Baseline Comparisons:**
 - **LLM-AS-P (without context):** LLM directly generates a natural language plan without any PDDL examples.
 - **LLM-AS-P (with context):** LLM directly generates a natural language plan, provided with an example NL problem and its NL solution.
 - **LLM-AS-P (TOT - Tree of Thoughts):** An LLM-based deliberate reasoning method where the LLM iteratively expands a search tree and evaluates partial plans.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Success rate (percentage of problems for which a correct and/or optimal plan is found), and real-world robot demonstration.

This methodical approach ensures that the LLM is utilized for its strong linguistic capabilities, while the planning expertise is delegated to a specialized, robust symbolic planner, creating a powerful hybrid system.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across seven diverse robot planning domains vividly highlight the complementary strengths of LLMs and classical planners, affirming the effectiveness of the LLM+P framework.

**A. Performance of LLM-AS-P (LLM as a Direct Planner):**
- **Lack of Feasibility and Reasoning:** The most striking finding is that LLM-AS-P (whether with or without context, or using Tree of Thoughts) consistently fails to provide feasible or correct plans for the vast majority of problems. While LLMs can generate grammatically coherent action sequences, they fundamentally lack the ability to reason about action preconditions, effects, and the overall state of the world over a long horizon. This leads to logically inconsistent or physically impossible plans.
- **Context's Limited Impact on Direct Planning:** Providing an example plan as context does not significantly improve the LLM's ability to directly generate correct plans. This underscores that merely showing an LLM a successful plan does not endow it with the underlying logical planning capability.
- **Struggle with Complex Constraints:** LLM-AS-P methods completely fail in domains involving complex spatial relationships or non-obvious constraints. Examples include:
 - **BLOCKSWORLD:** Inability to track `ON` and `CLEAR` properties.
 - **BARMAN:** Failure to account for the need to clean shot glasses.
 - **FLOORTILE:** Generating actions like "paint tile 1-2 black" when the robot can only paint neighboring tiles, or misinterpreting movement constraints.
 - **TERMES and STORAGE:** Ignoring requirements that a robot cannot unload at its current position.
- **Tree of Thoughts (TOT) Limitations:** While LLM-AS-P (TOT) attempts to introduce deliberate reasoning, it often times out due to the large number of LLM calls required for search tree expansion and evaluation. This makes it impractical for long-horizon planning problems, demonstrating that even sophisticated prompting techniques do not resolve the core reasoning limitations of LLMs for complex, constrained tasks.

**B. Performance of LLM+P:**
- **Superior Correctness and Optimality:** LLM+P overwhelmingly outperforms LLM-AS-P methods. It is able to produce **optimal plans for the majority of the benchmark problems**. For domains where the optimal planner timed out, LLM+P still achieved a high success rate with the sub-optimal planner. This is a crucial finding, demonstrating the framework's ability to consistently generate plans that are not only feasible but also efficient.
- **Crucial Role of In-Context Learning:** The experiments confirm that the in-context learning component is **essential** for LLM+P's success. Without a simple example of a natural language problem mapped to its corresponding PDDL problem file, the LLM struggles to produce syntactically and semantically correct PDDL output, leading to unsolveable planning problems. This highlights LLMs' strength as a "translation" or "re-writing" tool when given a clear structural example.
- **Primary Source of Failures:** The few instances where LLM+P failed were primarily due to the LLM mis-specifying the PDDL problem file. This typically involved missing crucial initial conditions (e.g., leaving tiles disconnected in FLOORTILE), which rendered the planning problem unsolvable by even a perfect classical planner. This indicates that while the LLM is good at translation, ensuring perfect fidelity in every complex scenario remains a challenge, yet it is a significantly more manageable failure mode than outright logical errors in planning.

**C. Robot Demonstration:**
- **Real-World Applicability:** The practical demonstration on a real home robot tackling a complex "tidy-up" task (moving a mustard bottle and a soup can) provided compelling evidence of LLM+P's real-world utility.
- **Optimal vs. Sub-optimal:** LLM+P generated the optimal plan for this task (cost 22), cleverly integrating the two sub-tasks by having the robot drop the bottle, address the soup can, and then re-grasp the bottle. In contrast, LLM-AS-P produced a sub-optimal plan (cost 31) that involved unnecessary travel. This validates LLM+P's ability to generate efficient, real-world robotic task plans from natural language instructions.

In summary, the results strongly support the paper's central thesis: LLMs are not inherently good at planning, but they are exceptionally good at transforming natural language into structured formats. By coupling this transformation ability with the logical rigor of classical planners, LLM+P provides a highly effective and robust solution for complex, long-horizon planning tasks in robotics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LLM+P framework represents a significant step forward in integrating the capabilities of large language models with traditional symbolic AI methods, particularly in the domain of automated planning for robotics. Its significance and potential impact can be understood in several dimensions:

**A. Bridging the Gap between Human Language and Robot Autonomy:**
- **Natural Language Interface:** LLM+P provides a robust and intuitive natural language interface for complex robot tasks. This democratizes access to sophisticated planning capabilities, allowing non-experts to instruct robots using everyday language, rather than requiring them to learn specialized programming or formal planning languages like PDDL. This could be transformative for home robots, industrial automation, and service robotics.
- **Enhanced Accessibility:** By simplifying the interaction paradigm, LLM+P can accelerate the deployment and adoption of intelligent robotic systems in diverse real-world settings.

**B. Leveraging Complementary Strengths (Hybrid AI):**
- **Optimal Division of Labor:** The work brilliantly demonstrates how to effectively divide labor between LLMs and classical planners. LLMs are leveraged for their strength in understanding and generating human-like text and translating between different linguistic formats (natural language to PDDL), while classical planners are utilized for their unparalleled ability to perform sound, complete, and optimal symbolic reasoning and search. This hybrid approach overcomes the limitations of using either technology in isolation for complex planning tasks.
- **Addressing LLM Weaknesses:** LLM+P directly tackles the notorious weakness of LLMs in long-horizon reasoning, logical consistency, and adherence to strict constraints. By offloading these critical functions to a guaranteed solver, the framework mitigates the "hallucination" and infeasibility problems often seen when LLMs attempt to plan directly.

**C. Generality Beyond Planning:**
- **Tool-Use Paradigm:** While demonstrated on planning problems, the general methodology of augmenting LLMs with external, sound, and complete solvers is broadly applicable. As the authors suggest, this principle can be extended to any domain where specific, reliable tools exist (e.g., calculators for arithmetic, theorem provers for logical deduction, databases for structured queries). This reinforces the emerging paradigm of LLMs as intelligent "coordinators" or "translators" that can effectively interface with specialized tools to extend their capabilities.

**D. Advancing Embodied AI and Robotics:**
- **Reliable Robot Behavior:** For robot systems, reliability and correctness are paramount. LLM+P's ability to generate optimal and feasible plans from natural language directly contributes to more reliable and efficient robot behavior in dynamic environments. This is crucial for tasks requiring precision, safety, and resource optimization.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper establishes a strong baseline and a challenging benchmark dataset for future research in LLM-enhanced planning. It clearly highlights remaining challenges, such as the LLM's occasional mis-specification of PDDL and the need for LLMs to autonomously recognize when to trigger such a pipeline.

**E. Economic and Efficiency Impact:**
- **Reduced Development Time:** For developers, the ability to specify complex robot tasks in natural language can significantly reduce the time and expertise required for programming, debugging, and maintaining robotic systems.
- **Optimized Resource Usage:** By consistently yielding optimal plans, LLM+P can lead to more efficient use of robot resources (e.g., energy, time, movement), which translates to cost savings and improved operational performance in industrial or logistical settings.

**Potential Future Work:** The authors themselves outline critical next steps:
1. **Auto-detection:** Enabling LLMs to intelligently decide *when* a given prompt requires the LLM+P pipeline, rather than relying on a hardcoded trigger.
2. **Reduced Human Dependency:** Investigating ways to reduce the reliance on human-provided information, such as domain PDDL files or in-context learning examples, possibly through more sophisticated prompt engineering or targeted fine-tuning of the translation component of the LLM.

In conclusion, LLM+P offers a compelling and practical solution for empowering LLMs with robust planning capabilities. By elegantly integrating the strengths of natural language processing with formal symbolic reasoning, this work not only pushes the boundaries of human-robot interaction but also lays crucial groundwork for the development of more intelligent, reliable, and accessible autonomous systems.
