# MARSHAL: Incentivizing Multi-Agent Reasoning via Self-Play with Strategic LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> MARSHAL is an end-to-end reinforcement learning framework that trains Large Language Models using self-play in strategic games to foster multi-agent reasoning abilities. This approach yields LLM agents with enhanced strategic skills, demonstrating up to a 10% performance gain on benchmarks like AIME within cooperative multi-agent systems.

## Problem

- Applying reinforcement learning to multi-turn, multi-agent LLM scenarios faces challenges with long-horizon credit assignment, where the impact of individual actions is hard to attribute over many steps.
- Heterogeneous agent roles and asymmetric information in multi-agent environments lead to high variance in advantage estimation, destabilizing the training process for LLMs.
- Existing LLM-based multi-agent systems primarily focus on interaction frameworks rather than intrinsically training LLMs for enhanced multi-agent reasoning capabilities.

## Method

- **MARSHAL** is an end-to-end reinforcement learning framework that builds on ==Group-Relative Policy== Optimization (GRPO), leveraging self-play across a diverse set of six competitive and cooperative strategic games.
- It introduces a 'turn-level advantage estimator' that uses a 'sum-then-normalize' approach on Monte Carlo returns to provide stable, fine-grained credit assignment over long interaction horizons.
- The framework incorporates 'agent-specific advantage normalization' by partitioning trajectories based on player roles, enabling independent advantage estimation relative to each role's average outcome, which stabilizes training in heterogeneous environments.

## Results

- MARSHAL agents demonstrate strong strategic ability in training games and generalize effectively to more complex, held-out games (e.g., a generalist model achieved a **28.7%** improvement on Leduc Hold'em and **22.9%** on Simple Hanabi over baselines).
- The acquired skills generalize to downstream multi-agent systems, with the generalist MARSHAL model achieving an average of **3.51%** gain in the competitive MAD framework and up to a **10.00%** gain on AIME in the cooperative AutoGen framework.
- Qualitative analysis shows MARSHAL significantly reduced 'Inter-Agent Misalignment' failures (**11.5%** reduction) in the MAD framework, indicating improved listening and task focus among agents, and the framework successfully scaled to Qwen3-**8B** models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-play is critical for developing robust and generalizable multi-agent policies, as training against fixed opponents leads to overfitting and strategic mode collapse.
> - Both turn-level advantage estimation and agent-specific advantage normalization are essential for effective multi-turn, multi-agent learning, providing stable credit assignment and preventing performance degradation.
> - Training with MARSHAL cultivates emergent multi-agent reasoning patterns in LLMs, including role-aware strategy adaptation and intent recognition (Theory of Mind), which generalize to out-of-domain tasks.
