# StageACT: Stage-Conditioned Imitation for Robust Humanoid Door Opening

> [!summary] Summary
> FieldAI researchers developed StageACT, a stage-conditioned imitation learning framework, enabling a Unitree G1 humanoid robot to autonomously open doors in real-world office environments, achieving a 55% success rate on an unseen door, which is more than double baseline performance. This framework demonstrated robust failure recovery and the ability to guide robot behaviors through explicit stage prompting.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots face difficulties performing complex, long-horizon tasks requiring tightly coupled locomotion and manipulation, such as autonomous door opening.
- Standard imitation learning struggles with partial observability and 'mode collapse' when applied to multi-stage tasks, leading to poor robustness in dynamic, real-world settings.
- Achieving autonomous door opening by humanoids in unstructured environments often relies on external sensors or privileged information, limiting generalizability.

## Method

- The **StageACT** framework extends the ==Action Chunking Transformer (ACT)== by incorporating explicit task-stage inputs.
- The door-opening task is decomposed into five distinct stages (Search, Approach, Rotate, Push, Stop), which are annotated and fed as one-hot vectors to the policy's transformer decoder.
- A dataset of 135 whole-body teleoperated demonstrations was collected from a Unitree G1 humanoid in real office environments, using an Apple Vision Pro for upper-body control and an Xbox controller for locomotion.

## Results

- StageACT achieved a **55%** success rate for autonomous door opening on a previously unseen door, a substantial improvement over baseline ACT (**20%**) and ACT with history (**10%**).
- The framework enabled robust recovery from task failures and adaptation to new scenarios through explicit stage prompting, a behavior not observed in baseline policies.
- StageACT demonstrated lower upper-body and root tracking errors and a shorter average task completion time of **20.7** seconds, indicating more precise and efficient task execution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit stage-conditioning successfully resolves observational ambiguities in multi-stage tasks, preventing mode collapse by providing temporal context to the policy.
> - Decomposing long-horizon tasks into discrete stages enables the policy to intelligently recover from failures by reverting to earlier stages and retrying actions.
> - Stage prompting offers a powerful form of controllability, allowing the robot to adapt its behavior for novel task variations or to intentionally skip certain stages.
