# EFLUX: Elastic Multi-Robot Formation Navigation and Adaptation with Agentic LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> EFLUX presents a framework that enables elastic multi-robot formation navigation by integrating agentic Large Language Models (LLMs) with deterministic geometric grounding. This system facilitates joint reasoning over continuous formation deformation and discrete reconfiguration, leading to robust adaptation with higher success rates in complex, confined environments, validated through simulations and hardware experiments.

## Problem

- Existing multi-robot formation navigation methods struggle with limited adaptability in cluttered spaces, often leading to deadlocks or suboptimal paths when rigid formations conflict with collision avoidance.
- Current approaches typically decouple continuous formation deformation (reshaping) and discrete reconfiguration (splitting/merging), addressing them separately or linking them with heuristic rules, lacking a unified, intelligent reasoning framework.
- Applying LLMs to robotics for dynamic planning faces challenges due to their probabilistic nature and potential for "hallucination," requiring explicit geometric grounding and real-time feasibility.

## Method

- **EFLUX** employs a three-stage ==agentic LLM pipeline==: deterministic environment analysis, ==hierarchical LLM reasoning==, and waypoint generation with agentic validation.
- It processes raw environmental data into ==narrow-region records== and ==semantic scene context== to inform LLM decisions on high-level adaptation strategies for deformation and reconfiguration.
- A closed-loop system of LLM-generated waypoint proposals, ==deterministic geometric validation==, and ==feedback-guided retry/replanning== ensures the geometric feasibility and safety of robot plans.

## Results

- EFLUX achieved consistently high success rates (e.g., **100%** in **0.7m** narrow corridors and large obstacle scenarios) across diverse constrained simulation environments, outperforming DEFORM, VRB, and SF baselines.
- The deterministic validation and retry mechanism in Stage III was critical for robustness, increasing the success rate from **50.0%** without it to **100%** with it in challenging scenarios and reducing planning failures.
- Hardware experiments with multiple mobile ground robots validated EFLUX's real-time performance, demonstrating coordinated formation adaptation including splitting, merging, and compression in physical settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying continuous formation deformation and discrete reconfiguration within a single framework significantly enhances multi-robot adaptability in complex and constrained environments.
> - Integrating LLMs for high-level semantic reasoning (scene interpretation and strategy selection) with deterministic geometric grounding provides a robust approach to overcoming LLM precision limitations in robotics.
> - A closed-loop validation, local refinement, and feedback mechanism is crucial for ensuring the geometric feasibility and safety of LLM-generated plans, effectively mitigating hallucination and enabling reliable execution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-robot formation navigation is a fundamental capability for numerous collaborative tasks, including search and rescue, cooperative transportation, and environmental exploration. A persistent challenge in this field involves enabling robot teams to navigate cluttered or confined environments while maintaining a desired formation, avoiding collisions, and adapting to spatial constraints.

Existing methodologies for multi-robot formation navigation primarily fall into three categories: control-based, optimization-based, and learning-based approaches.
- **Control and Optimization-based Methods:** These often rely on fixed structures, predefined templates, or handcrafted switching logic. While effective in open environments or for maintaining static formations, they typically exhibit limited adaptability in cluttered spaces, frequently leading to deadlocks or suboptimal trajectories when rigid formation maintenance conflicts with collision avoidance. Some approaches allow for continuous geometric deformation (e.g., affine transformations like shearing and compression) but generally do not support discrete topological changes such as splitting the team into subgroups and merging them back.
- **Learning-based Methods:** These approaches, often employing techniques like Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) or Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL), can provide reactive adaptation and obstacle avoidance. However, they frequently lack explicit high-level reasoning capabilities regarding when and how to adapt formations, often relying on learned policies that may not generalize robustly to novel, complex bottleneck configurations. They also typically treat deformation and reconfiguration as separate problems or use predefined rules to switch between them.

A significant gap in the current landscape is the absence of a unified framework that can jointly and intelligently reason over both continuous **deformation** (reshaping the geometry of a connected formation) and discrete **reconfiguration** (splitting the team into subgroups and later merging them) based on environmental cues. Existing methods either address these behaviors independently, connect them through heuristic or handcrafted rules, or lack explicit geometric criteria for invoking specific adaptation strategies. This decoupling can lead to excessive formation distortion, loss of coordination, or navigational failures in environments requiring dynamic changes in formation shape, connectivity, and effective team composition.

Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in semantic interpretation, contextual reasoning, and code generation. In the context of multi-robot coordination, LLMs have been applied to tasks such as static pattern generation, shape assembly, or automated reward design. However, their use for dynamic, closed-loop reasoning about formation adaptation *during* navigation, particularly when requiring geometric precision and real-time feasibility, has been limited. A key challenge in applying LLMs to robotics is their probabilistic nature and potential for "hallucination," which can lead to geometrically infeasible plans without explicit grounding.

This work, EFLUX, positions itself at the intersection of these fields. It aims to integrate the high-level reasoning and semantic understanding capabilities of LLMs with deterministic geometric grounding and robust validation mechanisms. By doing so, EFLUX seeks to overcome the limitations of prior work by providing a unified, geometry-grounded LLM-agentic framework that enables automatic and elastic multi-robot formation navigation through joint reasoning over both deformation and reconfiguration, addressing the need for robust adaptation in complex, confined environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the EFLUX research is to develop an automatic and elastic multi-robot formation navigation framework capable of adapting both the formation geometry (deformation) and the group topology (reconfiguration) in cluttered and confined environments. This overarching goal is driven by several motivations and aims to address specific limitations in existing multi-robot coordination strategies:

1. **Addressing Limitations of Rigid and Partially Adaptive Formations:** In challenging environments, rigid formation maintenance can lead to deadlocks in narrow corridors or choke points. Conversely, prioritizing collision avoidance without regard for formation coherence can result in excessive distortion and loss of coordinated behavior. Existing methods often rely on fixed structures, predefined templates, or reactive policies that adapt formations only in limited ways, such as continuous deformation, but struggle with discrete topological changes. The motivation is to move beyond these limited adaptations to allow for more flexible and robust navigation.

2. **Unifying Deformation and Reconfiguration:** Formation adaptation requires two complementary behaviors: continuous geometric reshaping (deformation) and discrete topological changes (reconfiguration, such as splitting into subgroups or merging back). Prior work frequently addresses these behaviors separately, or links them through handcrafted rules, or lacks clear geometric criteria for when to invoke each. This decoupling can lead to suboptimal trajectories, excessive oscillations between adaptation modes, or deadlocks in environments that require both types of adjustments. The motivation is to create a unified framework that can jointly reason about and seamlessly integrate both deformation and reconfiguration.

3. **Automatic Adaptation Decision-Making:** A key challenge is not merely executing a predefined formation change but automatically determining *when* to preserve, deform, or reconfigure the formation in response to environmental constraints. This requires interpreting scene structure and selecting appropriate adaptation strategies online. The motivation is to automate this high-level decision-making process, reducing the need for manual pre-programming or rule definition.

4. **Leveraging LLMs for High-Level Reasoning:** Recent advances in LLMs offer potential for semantic interpretation, contextual reasoning, and complex strategy generation. The motivation is to harness these capabilities to enable LLMs to act as high-level reasoning modules that can interpret scene geometry and select appropriate formation adaptation strategies. This could provide a more flexible and intelligent approach to adaptive navigation compared to traditional rule-based or purely reactive methods.

5. **Ensuring Geometric Grounding and Feasibility for LLM Outputs:** While LLMs offer strong reasoning capabilities, their direct outputs are often probabilistic, lack geometric precision, and are prone to "hallucination," which can lead to infeasible or unsafe plans for robotic systems. A significant motivation is to develop a mechanism that grounds LLM reasoning in deterministic geometric constraints, spatial requirements, and feasibility checks. This involves a closed-loop generation, verification, and correction pipeline to translate high-level LLM decisions into executable, collision-free, and safe per-robot waypoints.

In summary, EFLUX aims to address the limitations of current multi-robot formation navigation by enabling elastic adaptation through a geometry-grounded LLM-agentic framework. This framework is designed to intelligently reason over both continuous deformation and discrete reconfiguration, ensuring that the robot team can safely and efficiently navigate complex, cluttered environments while maintaining coordinated behaviors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EFLUX employs a three-stage agentic LLM pipeline integrated with deterministic geometric grounding modules to achieve elastic multi-robot formation navigation. The core approach follows a representation chain: Narrow-region records (B) → Semantic scene context (C) → Adaptation strategies (A) → Waypoint plans (Π).

**A. Deterministic Environment Analysis (Pre-LLM)**
This initial module preprocesses the raw environmental data into abstract geometric summaries, which are then provided to the LLM.
1. **Centroid Reference Path Generation:** A coarse, collision-free path for the team's centroid (γ) is generated using a sampling-based planner. This path ensures a minimum clearance for a single robot (radius `r` + safety margin `δ_safe`) from obstacles.
2. **Path Upsampling and Evaluation:** The centroid path is upsampled into finer "measurement waypoints" (`{q_m}`). At each `q_m`, two critical metrics are evaluated:
 - **Corridor Width (`w_cor_m`):** Estimated by casting multiple opposing ray pairs (e.g., `N_ray`) over 360 degrees from `q_m` and taking the minimum summed first-hit distance. This approximates the narrowest free-space cross-section.
 - **Formation Margin (`c_form_m`):** The minimum robot-to-obstacle or robot-to-boundary clearance when the nominal formation is placed at `q_m` and oriented with the local path direction.
3. **Narrow-Region Records (B) Generation:** Consecutive waypoints where the nominal formation cannot clear obstacles (`c_form_m < δ_safe`) are grouped into discrete "narrow regions" (`NR_j`). Each `NR_j` is summarized with its minimum corridor width, length, and bounding obstacles, forming the structured representation `B`. This abstraction reduces the raw map complexity for the LLM.

**B. Hierarchical LLM Reasoning**
This constitutes the core decision-making pipeline, where the LLM interprets the environment and formulates adaptation plans.

1. **Scene Interpretation (B → C):**
 - **Purpose:** To translate the quantitative geometric summaries (`B`) into a qualitative, semantic scene context (`C`). The LLM does not perform geometric estimation but interprets pre-computed data.
 - **Input:** Obstacle layout, the centroid reference path (`γ`), and the narrow-region records (`B`).
 - **Process:** The LLM is given role instructions ("Spatial Structure Analyst") and a guided protocol. It analyzes bottleneck contexts by identifying local obstacle layouts, classifying bottleneck patterns (e.g., funnel, corridor, chicane, single-side squeeze), and assessing nominal-formation passability for each `NR_j`. It also identifies inter-region topological relations (e.g., independent, continuous, progressive narrow stretches).
 - **Output:** An augmented semantic scene context (`C`), enriching `B` with qualitative descriptions and reasoning.

2. **Formation Adaptation Strategy (C → A):**
 - **Purpose:** To select and schedule high-level formation adaptation strategies (deformation or reconfiguration) for each identified narrow region.
 - **Input:** The semantic scene context (`C`), reference path (`γ`), nominal formation (`F`), and precomputed geometric summaries (e.g., width ratio `ρ_j = w_j / W_nom`, where `W_nom` is nominal formation width; safety-reduced admissible width `w_safe_j`).
 - **Process:** The LLM follows a guided Chain-of-Thought (CoT) protocol.
 - **Decision Heuristics:** The LLM prioritizes deformation to preserve formation coherence when feasible. Reconfiguration is considered if a connected deformation is not width-admissible (`w_safe_j < 2r` for robots of radius `r`), or if the scene offers multiple traversable corridors, or if deformation alone would cause excessive distortion.
 - **Pattern-Strategy Mappings:** The prompt provides canonical mappings (e.g., a narrowing funnel suggests progressive scaling; a dual-bypass obstacle suggests parallel subgroup reconfiguration).
 - **Adaptation Specification:** For deformation, the LLM specifies the mode (scaling, shearing, line formation), compression axis, target width, and lateral bias. For reconfiguration, it specifies the split type (parallel, sequential, hybrid), subgroup assignments, traversal channels, and merge locations.
 - **Validation:** A final internal validation step verifies that the plan (`A`) covers all narrow regions and maintains internal consistency.
 - **Output:** A high-level adaptation plan (`A`) including strategy types and execution schedules along `γ`.

**C. Waypoint Generation (A → Π) and Agentic Validation/Retry**
This stage translates the high-level strategies into executable robot commands and ensures geometric feasibility.
1. **Waypoint Generation:**
 - **Purpose:** To generate coordinate-level synchronized waypoint sequences (`Π`) for each robot based on the adaptation plan.
 - **Input:** Scene, formation, path, and constraint context from previous stages.
 - **Process:** The LLM acts as a heuristic waypoint proposer, conditioned to produce a JSON waypoint plan satisfying safety, synchronization, directional consistency, and temporal ordering.
2. **Agentic Validation, Retry, and Replanning (Closed-Loop):** This is a critical component for geometric grounding and robustness.
 - **Deterministic Validator (M):** A module that applies strict geometric checks to the LLM-generated waypoint plan (`Π_k`). These checks include inter-robot separation, obstacle clearance, boundary containment, segment clearance, path monotonicity, and centroid-direction consistency (Table II).
 - **Local Refiner:** If the plan contains minor, non-blocking violations, a constrained local optimization attempts to repair these coordinate-level issues by minimally adjusting waypoints (`Eq. 2`). This refiner preserves fidelity to the proposed plan while ensuring local feasibility within a trust region.
 - **Feedback Loop:**
 - If the plan (`Π_k`) or its refined version (`~Π_k`) still contains blocking violations after revalidation, structured feedback (`H`) is generated. This feedback details the violation type, involved robots/obstacles, waypoint indices, and quantitative clearance errors.
 - This `H` is appended to subsequent prompts for the LLM during a retry loop (up to `K` attempts). The LLM is prompted to make minimal edits (LOCAL-FIX) or perform full regeneration (FULL-REPLAN) based on the feedback.
 - This iterative process continues until a valid plan is found or the retry budget is exhausted.
3. **Online Replanning:** The framework supports dynamic replanning in response to external triggers (e.g., goal changes, robot failures/additions). It constructs a snapshot from current robot states, remaining path, and unexecuted waypoints, then initiates the planning pipeline from that point.

**D. Low-Level Execution:**
Accepted multi-robot waypoints are time-parameterized into synchronized reference trajectories and executed by distributed MPC waypoint trackers on each robot, prioritizing waypoint tracking, control smoothness, and short-range obstacle avoidance.

The methodology strategically combines the high-level, semantic reasoning of LLMs with a robust deterministic geometric validation and correction loop, addressing the inherent precision limitations of language models and enabling reliable, adaptive multi-robot formation navigation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The validation experiments for EFLUX were conducted in both simulation and hardware, evaluating its performance against representative baselines across diverse constrained scenarios, and analyzing its internal mechanisms, scalability, and generalizability.

**A. Benchmark Evaluation in Diverse Scenarios (Simulation)**

EFLUX was benchmarked against three baseline methods: DEFORM (adaptive formation reconfiguration via MPC), VRB (virtual rigid-body with potential fields), and SF (distributed swarm trajectory optimization for formation flight). The scenarios included narrow corridors (with 1.2 m and 0.7 m gaps), a single large obstacle requiring splitting, and sparse/dense obstacle environments.

- **Overall Performance (Table III, Fig. 3):** EFLUX demonstrated robust performance, achieving consistently high success rates (SR) across almost all environments and formation types (triangle and square formations). It often matched or exceeded the success rates of baselines while maintaining coordinated behavior.
- **Narrow Corridors:**
 - In the 1.2 m gap corridor, EFLUX achieved a 100% SR, comparable to VRB and SF, and slightly shorter total paths than most baselines.
 - In the more constrained 0.7 m gap corridor, EFLUX maintained a 100% SR. In contrast, VRB consistently failed due to local minima traps. DEFORM, while capable of line-like templates, deadlocked due to conflicts between template convergence and local collision avoidance. SF successfully traversed but exhibited pronounced transient formation distortion. EFLUX achieved similar formation distortion error (`e_sim`) to SF but with more reliable navigation.
- **Reconfiguration Scenarios (Large Obstacle, Cluttered Environments):**
 - EFLUX's ability to reason about and execute split-merge reconfiguration was particularly advantageous in scenarios requiring topological changes. For the "single large obstacle" scenario, EFLUX achieved a 100% SR, while DEFORM and VRB largely failed, and SF had a very low success rate, highlighting the limitations of methods without explicit topological adaptation.
 - In sparse and dense obstacle environments, EFLUX consistently achieved higher SRs compared to most baselines (e.g., up to 100% in sparse, 88.8% in dense for square formations), often with comparable or lower `e_sim` values over successful runs. Baselines struggled with deadlock, collisions, or high distortion in these complex environments.
- **Formation Distortion (`e_sim`):** While `e_sim` is averaged over successful runs, EFLUX demonstrated comparable formation similarity errors to methods like SF, which explicitly optimize for this metric, indicating controlled deformation and rapid recovery.

**B. Numerical Analysis of EFLUX (Challenging Scenarios)**

A detailed analysis of EFLUX's internal mechanisms was performed on challenging scenarios requiring both deformation and reconfiguration (Fig. 4).

- **Stage-wise Ablation Study (Table IV):**
 - Removing **Stage I (Scene Interpretation)** or **Stage II (Adaptation Strategy)** decreased the one-shot success rate and increased the average number of Stage III waypoint-generation attempts (`N_att`), suggesting that these stages significantly improve the quality of the initial LLM-generated plan.
 - Removing both Stage I and Stage II further degraded performance, indicating the value of intermediate semantic and strategic representations.
 - The **retry mechanism** (closed-loop Stage III with feedback-guided replanning) was identified as critical for robustness. Without it, the success rate dropped to 50.0%, demonstrating its role in recovering from initial invalid proposals.
 - **Waypoint refinement** (local optimization of waypoints) also contributed to performance, reducing planning time, token usage, and retry attempts by correcting minor geometric violations before triggering full LLM regeneration.
 - Removing "geometric grounding" (implying the deterministic environment analysis and validation components) resulted in a significant drop in SR to 60.0%, underscoring the necessity of deterministic checks for feasible plan generation.
- **LLM Backbone Comparison (Fig. 5):** The framework exhibited generalizability across different LLM backbones (Gemini, OpenAI, Claude). While performance metrics varied slightly between models, the framework's core functionality was not tied to a single LLM, suggesting robustness to model choice. Gemini-3.1-Pro showed the best performance among the tested models.
- **Scalability with Robot Number (Table V):**
 - EFLUX maintained a 100% SR for 4- and 6-robot teams while keeping the formation footprint fixed.
 - As the number of robots increased (from 4 to 6 to 8), planning time (`T_plan`), token usage (`N_tok`), and first-attempt blocking violations (`V_1st`) all increased, reflecting the higher coordination complexity and spatial density.
 - For the 8-robot team, the SR decreased to 60.0% within the fixed retry budget, indicating that generating valid waypoints becomes more challenging with higher robot counts under fixed spatial constraints.

**C. Hardware Experiments**

Indoor hardware experiments with multiple mobile ground robots validated EFLUX's real-time performance (Fig. 4, left). The robots demonstrated coordinated and collision-free formation adaptation in complex scenarios, including splitting to bypass obstacles, merging back, compressing to traverse narrow passages, forming a line, and subsequently recovering the nominal formation. This confirms the practical applicability and effectiveness of the proposed framework in a physical setting.

In summary, EFLUX consistently outperformed baselines in constrained navigation tasks by effectively integrating deformation and reconfiguration. Its performance is critically dependent on its geometry-grounded LLM pipeline, with the deterministic validation and retry mechanisms proving essential for robustness and feasibility. The framework also demonstrates generalizability and scalability across various conditions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EFLUX framework represents a notable advancement in the field of multi-robot formation navigation, particularly for operations in complex and confined environments. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

1. **Unified and Elastic Formation Adaptation:** EFLUX introduces a unified framework that jointly reasons over continuous formation deformation and discrete, topology-changing reconfiguration. This is a significant departure from previous methods that often treat these adaptation types separately or rely on handcrafted rules, which can lead to suboptimal performance, deadlocks, or limited adaptability. By enabling seamless transitions between preserving, deforming, and reconfiguring the team, EFLUX offers a more elastic and comprehensive solution for navigating intricate spaces.

2. **Enhanced Robustness and Safety:** The integration of deterministic geometric grounding with LLM-guided adaptation planning leads to improved success rates and a reduction in navigation failures and deadlocks compared to established baselines. The closed-loop validation, refinement, and retry mechanisms ensure that LLM-generated plans are geometrically feasible and collision-free, significantly enhancing the safety and reliability of multi-robot operations in constrained settings.

3. **Effective Integration of LLMs in Robotics:** EFLUX provides a methodology for leveraging the high-level reasoning, semantic interpretation, and contextual understanding capabilities of Large Language Models in a practical robotics application. Critically, it addresses the inherent challenges of LLMs, such as their lack of geometric precision and propensity for "hallucination," by embedding them within a robust, deterministically validated pipeline. This work offers a blueprint for how LLMs can be reliably integrated into robot motion planning and control systems, serving as an intelligent, high-level decision-making agent.

4. **Improved Generalizability and Scalability:** The framework has demonstrated generalizability across different LLM backbones, various team sizes (up to 8 robots in some configurations), and different formation shapes. Its capability to handle dynamic membership changes, such as robot failures or additions, further enhances its practical applicability in real-world, dynamic scenarios where team composition may change during a mission.

5. **Potential for Wider Application in Autonomous Systems:** The principles demonstrated by EFLUX—combining abstract semantic reasoning with precise geometric execution through an iterative, feedback-driven process—could extend beyond formation navigation. This approach may inform the development of more intelligent, adaptive, and robust autonomous systems for complex tasks in unstructured or dynamically changing environments, such as:
 - **Search and Rescue:** Teams could dynamically adapt their formation to navigate rubble, narrow passages, and complex terrains.
 - **Exploration and Surveillance:** Robots could maintain optimal coverage while adapting to environmental obstacles or changing line-of-sight requirements.
 - **Cooperative Logistics and Construction:** Teams could transport objects or assemble structures in constrained areas, dynamically adjusting their configurations.

In conclusion, EFLUX represents a significant step towards more autonomous and intelligent multi-robot systems. By enabling robots to flexibly adapt their collective behavior to environmental challenges through a robust LLM-grounded framework, it opens new possibilities for deploying robotic teams in previously inaccessible or highly constrained operational domains.
