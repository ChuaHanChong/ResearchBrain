# Spatially Aware World Action Model via Geometric Latent Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> SA-WAM is a spatially aware World Action Model that repurposes a pretrained video diffusion transformer to jointly denoise action chunks, future RGB, and future depth in one latent sequence. Metric depth is log-normalized into a three-channel image so the frozen Wan2.1 VAE tokenizer encodes it without 3D-specific modules. It reaches 76.6% on RoboCasa with 50 demos and 86.6% on LIBERO-Plus.

## Problem

- Existing World Action Models jointly generate future video and actions but operate purely on RGB, ignoring 3D geometry that manipulation-intensive tasks depend on.
- 3D-aware VLA policies prove geometry helps, yet they rely on dedicated point-cloud or modality-specific encoders that break the pretrained video-model architecture and its priors.
- Metric depth is positive and unbounded, incompatible with the bounded input domain of frozen video tokenizers, so geometry cannot be injected naively into video-pretrained backbones.

## Method

- ==3D modality injection==: depth is encoded as extra latent frames paired per camera view with RGB, using the same frozen ==Wan2.1 VAE==, alongside proprioception and action slots in the ==DiT== sequence.
- ==Nonlinear log-scale depth normalization== with camera-specific 2nd/98th-percentile clipping maps metric depth to a replicated three-channel image in [-1,1], compressing far field, preserving near-field resolution.
- Single ==EDM denoising objective== on a Cosmos-Predict2 2B DiT jointly supervises action chunk, future RGB, and future depth slots, with no action head or modality-specific losses.

## Results

- RoboCasa: **76.6%** average success over 24 tasks with 50 demos per task, **+9.5** over matched Cosmos-Policy and above methods trained on **6-20x** more data; **+16.7** on pick-and-place.
- LIBERO-Plus zero-shot weighted average of **86.6%**, beating pi-0.5 by **2.0** and Cosmos-Policy by **5.2**; real UR5 scores **90.0%** clean and **77.5%** randomized versus **75.0%/48.8%**.
- Ablation: log normalization gives lowest tokenizer round-trip AbsRel (**0.63%**) and highest policy success (**61.7%**) versus inverse (**59.5%**), linear (**56.5%**), RGB-only (**52.7%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adapting a signal to a frozen tokenizer's expected input domain can beat training a dedicated encoder, preserving pretrained priors while adding a new modality.
> - The choice of nonlinear parameterization matters: resolution should be allocated where task precision matters, near-field for manipulation, not uniformly across the metric range.
> - Predicted-versus-realized geometric error on the target object correlates with rollout success (AUC 0.88), suggesting world-model prediction quality as a runtime failure diagnostic.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

This research is situated within the field of robot policy learning, specifically focusing on the integration of visual and action information for robotic control. The broader landscape has seen significant advancements with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which leverage large-scale pre-training on vision, language, and robot data. However, many VLA models are initialized from Vision-Language Models that are predominantly trained on static image and text data, potentially limiting their understanding of dynamic sequences, motion, and physical interactions critical for robotics.

An alternative paradigm has emerged with video generative models, which are trained on dynamic sequences and offer a stronger prior for temporal evolution. Early approaches utilized these priors to generate future video observations and subsequently decode actions. More specialized models include Video-Action Models (VAMs), which augment pretrained video backbones with action-prediction heads, often requiring multi-stage training. Building upon this, World Action Models (WAMs) or Unified World Models (UWMs) represent a unified approach, integrating video and action generation within a shared generative architecture. These models have demonstrated that jointly learning actions and future states can enhance policy performance.

A recognized limitation within current WAMs is their primary reliance on RGB observations, typically without leveraging explicit 3D information. Concurrently, other research in 3D-aware VLA policies has underscored the importance of 3D data, particularly for manipulation-intensive tasks where object geometry and spatial relationships are critical. These 3D-aware policies have explored various representations, including point clouds, 3D positional encodings, and dense 3D coordinate maps.

This work, SA-WAM, specifically addresses the gap in WAMs by integrating 3D spatial awareness. It aims to combine the strengths of video-based world modeling with the benefits of explicit geometric information. The paper also connects to research on metric geometry encoding for pretrained visual models, which explores how to adapt modalities like depth to existing visual model representations, often through non-linear parameterizations to manage the unbounded nature of depth while preserving precision in critical ranges. SA-WAM positions itself as a development that brings this 3D spatial understanding into the WAM framework, maintaining compatibility with existing, powerful video diffusion model backbones.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a Spatially Aware World Action Model (SA-WAM) that integrates explicit 3D geometric information into the world-action modeling paradigm, thereby enhancing robot policy performance.

The primary motivations and specific objectives can be detailed as follows:

- **Addressing the Limitation of RGB-Only WAMs:** Current World Action Models (WAMs) primarily operate using only RGB observations. While these models have shown promise by inheriting rich visual and physical priors from large-scale video data, their lack of explicit 3D information is a significant limitation for robotic tasks that require precise spatial understanding and interaction with objects. Manipulation tasks, in particular, often depend critically on object geometry and spatial relations for successful execution.

- **Leveraging 3D Information for Enhanced Manipulation:** The research is motivated by the documented benefits of 3D information in other robot policies, where explicit geometry has improved performance in tasks involving intricate object manipulation. The objective is to bring this demonstrated value of 3D awareness to the WAM framework to improve policy robustness and accuracy.

- **Developing a Unified, 3D-Aware WAM Architecture:** The goal is to repurpose a pretrained video diffusion model for joint action, RGB, and depth prediction within a single diffusion backbone. This aims to enable 3D-aware world modeling and action prediction without necessitating separate, modality-specific 3D encoders or extensive architectural modifications that might disrupt the valuable priors learned during large-scale video pre-training.

- **Solving the Depth Encoding Challenge for Frozen VAEs:** A specific technical objective is to devise a method for encoding unbounded metric depth signals into the bounded input domain expected by a frozen, pretrained Variational Autoencoder (VAE) tokenizer. This is crucial for reusing existing tokenizers without 3D-specific fine-tuning, thereby incorporating geometric information without sacrificing the pretrained visual and physical priors that make WAMs effective. The motivation is to explore and identify a non-linear normalization strategy that compresses far-field depth information while preserving high resolution in the near-field, where manipulation precision is most critical.

- **Demonstrating State-of-the-Art Performance:** The research aims to evaluate SA-WAM's performance on established robotics benchmarks and in real-world settings, with the objective of achieving state-of-the-art results. This includes demonstrating sample efficiency and robustness, particularly in challenging environments with visual randomization or distractors.

- **Analyzing the Impact of 3D Information on World Modeling and Policy Success:** A further objective is to investigate how the integration of geometric information influences the quality of the world model's future state predictions (both RGB and depth). The work also seeks to quantitatively analyze the correlation between the quality of these future predictions and the ultimate success of policy rollouts, aiming to provide diagnostic insights into WAM performance and potential avenues for improvement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SA-WAM is designed to integrate 3D geometric information into a World Action Model by adapting a pretrained latent video diffusion model. The methodology involves specific strategies for modality injection, depth encoding, and training.

#### 3.1. A. 3D Modality Injection for World Action Modeling

The core of SA-WAM involves repurposing a pretrained latent video diffusion model to jointly predict robot actions and the evolution of the visual-geometric world.

- **Model Architecture:** The system adapts a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone, initialized from the Cosmos-Predict2 model, which is a pretrained video model. The Wan2.1 spatiotemporal VAE tokenizer associated with this model is kept frozen throughout training.
- **Input and Conditioning:** The model is conditioned on a task instruction, the robot's current proprioceptive state (`q_t`), and multi-view RGB-D observations from multiple cameras (wrist, left, and right).
- **Joint Prediction:** SA-WAM jointly denoises an action chunk (`a_t`) and the corresponding future RGB-D observations (`v_c_t'`, `g_c_t'`) at a specific prediction horizon `H` (e.g., `t' = t + H`).
- **Latent Sequence Representation:**
 - Robot proprioception and action chunks occupy dedicated positions within the DiT's latent sequence.
 - RGB observations (`I_c_t`) and their corresponding metric depth maps (`d_c_t`) are encoded into latent frames using the *same frozen VAE encoder (E)*. This design choice is crucial for reusing the rich visual priors of the pretrained video model without requiring modality-specific encoders. The RGB latent frame is `v_c_t = E(I_c_t)`, and the geometric latent frame is `g_c_t = E(I_c_d,t)`, where `I_c_d,t` is a tokenizer-compatible three-channel representation of the metric depth.

#### 3.2. B. Depth Injection and Normalization

A key challenge is transforming the unbounded metric depth signal into a format compatible with the VAE encoder, which expects bounded inputs.

- **Camera-Specific Bounding:** For each camera, robust camera-specific minimum (`d_c_min`) and maximum (`d_c_max`) depth bounds are estimated from the 2nd and 98th percentiles of valid depth values in the training dataset. Each depth observation is then clamped within these bounds: `d_c,⋆_t(u,v) = clip(d_c_t(u,v), d_c_min, d_c_max)`.
- **Normalization and Replication:** A monotone normalizer `f(·; a, b)` maps the clamped metric depth `[a, b]` to ``. The output is then rescaled to the VAE's `[-1, 1]` input range and replicated across three channels to form `I_c_d,t`, making it compatible with the VAE.
 `ˆd_c_t(u,v) = 2 * f(d_c,⋆_t(u,v); d_c_min, d_c_max) - 1`
 `I_c_d,t(u,v) = [ˆd_c_t(u,v), ˆd_c_t(u,v), ˆd_c_t(u,v)]`
- **Normalization Strategies:** Three different normalization functions (`f`) are compared:
 - **Linear:** `(z - a) / (b - a)`. Provides uniform representational resolution across the depth range.
 - **Inverse-depth:** `(1/z - 1/b) / (1/a - 1/b)`. Concentrates resolution strongly on nearby geometry (sensitivity proportional to `1/z^2`).
 - **Log-scale:** `log(z/a) / log(b/a)`. Offers an intermediate behavior, allocating greater resolution to the near field while maintaining useful precision at larger distances (sensitivity proportional to `1/z`).
Each mapping is invertible, allowing metric depth recovery from predictions.

#### 3.3. C. Training Objective

- **EDM Formulation:** The model is optimized using the Elucidating the Design Space of Diffusion-Based Generative Models (EDM) formulation.
- **Target Sequence:** `x_0` represents the clean target sequence, comprising the action chunk and the tokenizer latents of the future RGB and geometric frames.
- **Denoising Task:** `x_0` is corrupted with Gaussian noise `n`, and a denoiser `D_θ` is trained to predict `x_0` from the noisy input `(x_0 + n)` and conditioning information `C_t`.
- **Loss Function:** `L_EDM = E_λ(σ)∥D_θ(x_0 + n; σ, C_t) - x_0∥^2_2`, where `λ(σ)` is a noise-dependent weighting.
- **Unified Supervision:** The same objective jointly supervises the action, future RGB, and future depth prediction slots, enabling all modalities to be learned within a single generative sequence without dedicated prediction heads or modality-specific losses.

#### 3.4. D. Experimental Setup

- **Simulation Benchmarks:** RoboCasa (24 atomic kitchen tasks), LIBERO (SPATIAL, OBJECT, GOAL, LONG suites), and LIBERO-Plus (perturbed LIBERO suites for robustness evaluation under zero-shot conditions).
- **Real-World Platform:** A UR5 robotic arm with an RG6 parallel gripper and an Orbbec Femto Mega RGB-D camera observing the workspace. Robot states and actions are represented in end-effector space as 8-D vectors.
- **Training Details:** Fine-tuning of the 2B-parameter Cosmos-Predict2 DiT, frozen Wan2.1 VAE tokenizer. Specifics on GPU count, batch size, AdamW optimizer, learning rate schedule (warm-up followed by linear decay), and noise sampling strategy (hybrid of CosmosPredict2 log-normal and uniform distributions). Prediction horizon `H=32` for simulation (first 16 steps executed open-loop) and `H=3` for real-world (first 2 steps executed).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across depth normalization, simulation benchmarks, world modeling quality, and real-world robot experiments.

#### 4.1. A. Ablations on Depth Normalization Approach

An ablation study was conducted on an eight-task subset of RoboCasa to evaluate depth normalization strategies.

- **Metric Reconstruction Analysis:**
 - **Linear normalization** showed good preservation of medium- and far-range geometry but significantly lost precision in the near-field (6.68% AbsRel error below 0.3 m).
 - **Inverse-depth normalization** exhibited the opposite behavior, being highly accurate in the near-field (0.62% AbsRel) but degrading sharply at medium and far distances (4.16% and 13.19%, respectively).
 - **Log-scale normalization** provided a balanced profile, achieving the lowest overall Absolute Relative Error (AbsRel) at 0.63% and maintaining comparatively low error across all depth ranges for the wrist camera.
- **Policy-level Ablation:** Log-scale depth normalization resulted in the highest policy success rate (61.7%), outperforming the RGB-only method (52.7%), linear normalization (56.5%), and inverse-depth normalization (59.5%). Based on these results, log-scale normalization was selected for SA-WAM in subsequent experiments due to its balanced metric precision and superior policy performance.

#### 4.2. B. Comparison to State-of-the-Art in Simulation

SA-WAM was evaluated against existing robot policy baselines on RoboCasa and LIBERO-Plus.

- **RoboCasa Benchmark:**
 - SA-WAM achieved a 76.6% average success rate, which represents state-of-the-art performance, utilizing only 50 demonstrations per task. This significantly surpassed prior approaches that often required 6–20 times more training data.
 - Compared to Cosmos-Policy, which used the same demonstration budget, SA-WAM improved overall success by 9.5 points.
 - The largest improvements were observed in Pick-and-Place (PnP) tasks (+16.7 points) and Turn/Toggle tasks (+11.2 points), suggesting that explicit geometric conditioning is particularly beneficial for tasks requiring precise object localization and spatial interaction.
- **LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus Benchmarks:**
 - On the standard LIBERO benchmark, SA-WAM achieved an average success rate of 98.4%, performing on par with leading policies.
 - On the more challenging LIBERO-Plus benchmark, under zero-shot evaluation, SA-WAM obtained the highest weighted-average success rate at 86.6%. This exceeded the performance of the strongest VLA baseline, π0.5, by 2.0 points and improved over Cosmos-Policy by 5.2 points, indicating enhanced robustness to perturbations due to explicit geometric conditioning.

#### 4.3. C. World Modeling Quality Analysis

The impact of explicit geometry on the visual world-modeling quality of SA-WAM was assessed.

- SA-WAM demonstrated improved future RGB frame prediction quality compared to Cosmos-Policy across all evaluated metrics: PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS (Table IV).
- These gains were most prominent for the dynamic wrist camera view, where close-range interaction and camera motion pose greater prediction challenges.
- Improvements were also maintained under masked evaluation (focusing on robot, gripper, and target object regions), indicating that the benefits extended to manipulation-relevant areas rather than solely global image statistics.
- Qualitative examples further supported that SA-WAM produced RGB predictions that were more consistent with its realized rollouts. These findings suggest a correlation between stronger future-state modeling and improved policy performance.

#### 4.4. D. Prediction Quality versus Rollout Success

A quantitative study was conducted on Pick-and-Place (PnP) tasks to correlate future prediction consistency with rollout outcomes.

- An object-centric geometric error was calculated from the wrist camera's depth prediction by comparing the model-predicted future with the realized simulator rollout using the ground-truth object mask.
- Successful rollouts were found to have lower 3D prediction error, maintaining better alignment between the predicted and realized object states. Conversely, failed rollouts exhibited larger divergences around manipulation events.
- A threshold applied to this object-level error achieved an Area Under the Curve (AUC) of approximately 0.88 for detecting failures, indicating that 80% of failures could be detected at a 13% false-alarm rate. This result suggests that task-relevant geometric future-prediction error can serve as a diagnostic tool for WAM failure modes.

#### 4.5. E. Real-World Experiments

SA-WAM was evaluated on a UR5 robotic arm in a real-world tabletop manipulation setting, including clean and randomized environments.

- **Clean Environments:** SA-WAM matched or exceeded baselines, achieving an aggregate completion score of 90.0%, compared to 75.0% for Cosmos-Policy and 21.3% for π0.
- **Randomized Environments:** Under conditions with distractor objects and visual perturbations, SA-WAM maintained a strong performance of 77.5% completion, while Cosmos-Policy and π0 saw their scores decrease to 48.8% and 11.3%, respectively. This indicated improved robustness for SA-WAM when appearance cues alone were insufficient due to randomization.
- Qualitative examples in randomized environments showed SA-WAM successfully completing tasks where Cosmos-Policy failed, underscoring the benefit of depth in disambiguating visually similar distractors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of SA-WAM represents an advancement in the field of robot policy learning, particularly in the domain of World Action Models (WAMs). Its significance and potential impact can be considered from several perspectives:

- **Enhancement of World Action Models:** SA-WAM addresses a notable limitation of previous WAMs by explicitly integrating 3D geometric information. By repurposing existing, powerful pretrained video diffusion models and their VAE tokenizers for depth, it allows WAMs to leverage spatial awareness without requiring complex, modality-specific 3D encoders. This integration provides a more comprehensive understanding of the environment beyond mere RGB appearance, which is crucial for physical interaction.

- **Improved Robotic Policy Performance and Robustness:** The consistent state-of-the-art results across diverse simulation benchmarks (RoboCasa, LIBERO-Plus) and in real-world UR5 experiments demonstrate SA-WAM's capacity to achieve higher success rates. The particular gains in manipulation-intensive tasks and under randomized, visually ambiguous environments highlight enhanced robustness and generalization capabilities. This suggests that explicit geometric grounding helps robots perform more reliably in varied and challenging real-world scenarios.

- **Practical Implications for Robot Learning:** The method's demonstrated sample efficiency (e.g., strong performance with only 50 demonstrations per task on RoboCasa) is a significant practical advantage. In robot learning, data collection can be costly and time-consuming. SA-WAM's ability to achieve high performance with less data can accelerate the deployment of advanced robotic manipulation skills. Its strong real-world transferability further underscores its practical utility for developing generalist robots.

- **Methodological Innovation in Multimodal Integration:** The proposed non-linear depth normalization strategy, particularly the log-scale approach, provides an effective and elegant solution for making unbounded metric depth compatible with a frozen VAE tokenizer designed for bounded image data. This technique is a generalizable approach for integrating new modalities into foundation models, bypassing the need for extensive fine-tuning or new architectures for each modality. This could inspire similar strategies for other sensor types or data representations.

- **Diagnostic Tools for WAMs:** The research establishes a quantitative correlation between the quality of future-state predictions (specifically, object-centric 3D error) and policy rollout success. This finding offers a valuable diagnostic tool for understanding WAM failure modes. Such insights can guide future research towards more geometrically consistent training objectives or provide mechanisms for real-time failure detection and recovery in robotic systems.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** While SA-WAM represents a significant step, the paper also identifies limitations, such as the persistence of inconsistent future predictions and the need for improved inference efficiency. These points open avenues for future work, including the development of geometrically consistent training and verification methods, as well as optimizing WAMs for real-time robotic applications.

In summary, SA-WAM significantly advances the capability of World Action Models by seamlessly integrating 3D spatial awareness. This not only pushes the boundaries of performance on challenging robotic tasks but also offers methodological innovations and practical implications for more robust and data-efficient robot learning.
