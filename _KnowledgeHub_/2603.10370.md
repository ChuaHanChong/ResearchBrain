# GeoSense: Internalizing Geometric Necessity Perception for Multimodal Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> GeoSense equips multimodal large language models with the ability to autonomously determine when to utilize 3D geometric information, enhancing spatial reasoning capabilities while preserving general visual understanding. This adaptive integration strategy yielded superior performance across a diverse suite of benchmarks, outperforming baselines and models with rigid geometric integration.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) lack explicit geometric representations, which limits their spatial reasoning abilities in real-world applications.
- Current methods for incorporating 3D geometric information rigidly inject these features, often introducing noise, increasing computational overhead, and degrading performance on tasks that do not require spatial understanding.
- MLLMs lack an internal mechanism to recognize when their standard 2D inputs are insufficient for a given task, preventing them from adaptively requesting necessary 3D cues.

## Method

- **GeoSense** introduces a decoupled input architecture that treats 3D geometric information as an independent, on-demand modality, preventing contamination of 2D visual streams.
- A two-stage supervised fine-tuning strategy, including 'Perception Tuning,' trains the MLLM to make 'Internal Sense Decisions' by triggering a specific token () when geometric features are deemed necessary.
- A model-adaptive dataset is curated by analyzing performance discrepancies (with and without 3D geometry) for each sample, generating training signals that teach the model to activate or suppress geometric integration based on context.

## Results

- GeoSense achieved the highest overall rank across 10 diverse benchmarks, with an average score of **56.6** on spatial reasoning and **55.9** on general visual benchmarks, demonstrating significant improvements over baselines.
- The framework enhanced spatial reasoning performance (e.g., on BLINK benchmark) while successfully maintaining robust general visual reasoning, dynamically suppressing geometric features for non-spatial tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the adaptive selection scheme outperformed rigid geometric fusion, activating VGGT features for approximately **35.68%** of samples on average and demonstrating the critical role of 'Perception Tuning' in balancing spatial gains with general understanding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Rigidly injecting 3D geometric features can be detrimental, introducing irrelevant noise and computational overhead for tasks that do not require explicit spatial understanding.
> - Endowing MLLMs with the internal ability to perceive the necessity of geometric information allows for adaptive, efficient, and robust reasoning across diverse tasks.
> - Training models with a curated dataset that highlights the benefits and drawbacks of geometric feature usage in specific contexts enables the learning of empirical priors for dynamic decision-making rather than fixed rules.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated progress in various applications, including autonomous driving, embodied AI, and industrial robotics. However, a limitation identified in their transition to real-world deployment is their spatial reasoning capability. Standard MLLMs, primarily designed with 2D visual encoders, often lack explicit geometric representations, which can lead to failures in tasks requiring structural consistency, depth perception, or viewpoint manipulation.

Previous research has explored enhancing spatial understanding in MLLMs by incorporating geometric information. These efforts can be broadly categorized:
1. **2.5D strategies:** Injecting depth cues into MLLMs.
2. **3D-native models:** Processing point clouds or other intrinsic 3D data. These methods often entangle appearance and geometry at the input level.
3. **Hybrid approaches:** Fusing features derived from 3D foundation models (such as VGGT or MoGe-2, which reconstruct 3D representations from 2D inputs) directly into 2D visual embeddings. Examples include Spatial-MLLM and VG-LLM.

The GeoSense framework addresses a specific limitation within these existing hybrid approaches. Many current methods for incorporating geometric information operate under a "tightly coupled" or "rigid" paradigm. This means 3D geometric information is treated as a mandatory input, irrespective of the task's actual requirements. The paper posits that this indiscriminate inclusion of geometric signals can be counterproductive, introducing irrelevant noise and computational overhead for tasks that do not inherently require 3D understanding (e.g., optical character recognition, general visual question answering, or simple object recognition). This can lead to a degradation in general reasoning capabilities and, in some cases, even hallucinations.

GeoSense positions itself by introducing an adaptive mechanism. Instead of rigidly injecting geometric signals, it aims to endow the MLLM with an "awareness of perceptual insufficiency." This allows the model to autonomously determine *when* geometric features are necessary for reasoning and to engage them selectively. This approach diverges from previous methods by treating 3D features as a distinct, independent input modality that is engaged on-demand, rather than being fused element-wise into existing 2D features. The work thus contributes to the development of more efficient, robust, and self-aware multimodal intelligence by seeking to resolve the "perception information gap" – the discrepancy between information perceived from standard 2D inputs and the specific context-dependent knowledge required for complex spatial reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance multimodal large language models (MLLMs) by addressing their limitations in spatial understanding and the inefficiencies of current methods for incorporating geometric information. The motivation for GeoSense stems from several observations:

1. **Inherent Limitation of MLLMs in Spatial Reasoning:** Despite advancements in various domains, MLLMs exhibit a critical bottleneck in spatial reasoning tasks, which impacts their reliability and decision accuracy in real-world applications such as autonomous driving and robotics. Current 2D-native MLLM architectures lack explicit geometric representations required for tasks demanding structural consistency, depth relations, or viewpoint transformations.

2. **Inefficiency and Detrimental Effects of Rigid Geometric Injection:** Existing methods that incorporate geometric information, often derived from 3D foundation models, typically inject these geometric signals into every input. This approach assumes geometry is a universal necessity, which is not always the case. The paper identifies that geometry's utility is highly context-dependent. For instance, while depth cues are crucial for navigation, they can act as irrelevant noise for non-spatial tasks like table OCR or certain mathematical problems. The forced activation of geometric signals can:
 - Degrade general reasoning capabilities by introducing unnecessary complexity.
 - Lead to performance degradation in tasks where geometry is not required.
 - Increase computational overhead.
 - Exacerbate issues like hallucinations and cross-modal confusion, particularly as model scale and geometric data increase.

3. **The "Perception Information Gap":** The researchers identify a "perception information gap" where MLLMs lack the internal awareness to recognize when their standard 2D inputs are insufficient for a given task or query, and thus cannot assess their own information requirements. They cannot autonomously determine the necessity of integrating 3D geometric cues.

To address these issues, the key objectives of the GeoSense framework are:

1. **Endow MLLMs with Autonomous Geometric Necessity Perception:** The central objective is to empower the model with the intrinsic capability to decide, solely based on input content and query, whether to integrate 3D geometric cues, without requiring explicit spatial instructions. This moves beyond rigid, human-defined rules.
2. **Develop a Decoupled Geometric Input Channel:** To enable this autonomous decision-making, the framework aims to introduce 3D geometric information as a standalone, on-demand modality through an independent input channel, rather than fusing it directly with 2D features. This design is intended to prevent contamination of the high-resolution 2D visual stream responsible for general recognition.
3. **Activate Internal Spatial Awareness through Data Curation:** A critical objective is to curate a model-adaptive, spatially-aware supervised fine-tuning dataset. This dataset is designed to activate the model’s latent internal cues by reformulating performance discrepancies (with and without geometric features) into training signals, allowing the model to learn its own empirical priors.
4. **Achieve Balanced Performance:** The framework aims to significantly improve spatial reasoning performance without compromising the model's existing 2D visual and general reasoning capabilities. This ensures a robust, efficient, and self-aware multimodal intelligence suitable for diverse tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GeoSense proposes a multimodal framework designed to integrate 3D geometric cues adaptively, based on an internal perception of geometric necessity. This approach employs a decoupled framework where geometric information functions as an on-demand resource, enabled by a request mechanism learned through a two-stage supervised fine-tuning strategy.

#### 3.1. Independent Geometry Adaptation

To enable autonomous spatial awareness without compromising native 2D perception, GeoSense utilizes a decoupled input architecture. Unlike prior methods that fuse geometric features element-wise, this approach treats 3D geometry as an independent, on-demand modality.

- **Model Architecture:** The framework builds upon the visual encoder of Qwen2.5-VL-3B for 2D visual processing and VGGT-1B as the 3D geometric encoder. Both encoders are kept frozen during training to preserve their pre-trained representations.
- **Feature Projection:** Upon receiving visual input (V), both the 2D visual encoder (E_vision) and the 3D geometric encoder (E_geo) extract features. These features are then projected via dedicated learnable MLP modules (W_2D_proj and W_3D_proj) into a shared embedding space (D_MM) compatible with the MLLM's backbone.
- **Geometry Sequence Formulation:** The structured geometric features F_geo extracted by E_geo are projected to T_geo. These projected geometry tokens are serialized as an independent segment, delimited by special boundary tokens (<E_start_g>, <E_end_g>).
- **Multimodal Prompt Construction:** The input to the LLM backbone (H_input) is constructed by concatenating text tokens (T_text), 2D visual tokens (T_vision), and the delimited geometry tokens (E_start_g ⊕ T_geo ⊕ E_end_g). This structure allows geometric features to be included conditionally.

#### 3.2. Activating Internal Spatial Awareness (Two-Stage Training Strategy)

The model's adaptive spatial capabilities are instilled through a two-stage training process:

- **Stage 1: Geometric Feature Alignment:**
 - **Objective:** To project 2D and 3D features into a unified embedding space and align 3D tokens semantically with the existing visual-textual space.
 - **Process:** The LLM backbone, the 2D/3D projection layers (W_2D_proj, W_3D_proj), and the boundary tokens (<E_start_g>, <E_end_g>) are optimized, while the feature extractors (E_vision and E_geo) remain frozen.
 - **Data:** A mixture of temporal and spatial grounding datasets is used, including LLaVA-Hound-64k and Spar-234k.

- **Stage 2: Spatial-Aware Supervised Fine-Tuning (Perception Tuning):**
 - **Objective:** To evolve the model into an active perceiver capable of making "Internal Sense Decisions" – dynamically evaluating visual input and task intent to determine if geometric cues are necessary.
 - **Mechanism:** The model is trained to trigger a specific signal, represented by the <vggt> token, for tasks requiring high spatial precision. This token acts as a request for geometric embeddings. If the signal is not triggered, the model suppresses the geometry channel, relying solely on 2D visual reasoning. This mitigates noise and computational overhead.
 - **Data Curation for Perception Tuning:** This is a crucial component for learning adaptive behavior.
 - **Dataset Source:** A hybrid dataset is constructed from VSI-590k, SophiaVL-R1-130k, and Mantis-Instruct.
 - **Dual-Condition Inference:** Each sample in the curated dataset undergoes inference under two conditions: (i) with 3D geometric features injected, and (ii) with geometry inputs suppressed (e.g., via zero-padding).
 - **Categorization:** Samples are categorized into four quadrants based on accuracy in these two conditions:
 - **True-True (T-T):** Correct with/without 3D (Robust).
 - **True-False (T-F):** Correct with 3D, incorrect without 3D (3D Essential/Necessary).
 - **False-True (F-T):** Incorrect with 3D, correct without 3D (3D Noise).
 - **False-False (F-F):** Incorrect with/without 3D (Hard/Negative).
 - **Differentiated Data Reconstruction:**
 - **Necessary Geometry (Strategy A - for T-F samples):** For these samples, the data is restructured into a two-turn dialogue. The first turn prompts the model to generate a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) that explicitly concludes with the <vggt> trigger signal, indicating the need for geometric integration. The second turn provides the final answer.
 - **Geometry as Noise (Strategy B - for F-T samples):** For these samples, the original labels are retained, and the model is specifically trained to suppress the <vggt> trigger signal, thereby relying solely on 2D visual context.
 - **Methodological Significance:** This strategy decouples scene context from task types, ensuring that similar scenes can trigger opposing supervision signals (activation vs. suppression). This is designed to prevent shortcut learning and compel the model to make "Internal Sense Decisions" based on genuine perception of information necessity rather than memorizing background templates. The total dataset comprises 117k task-aware training samples.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

- **Base Model:** Qwen2.5-VL-3B is used as the foundational MLLM.
- **3D Foundation Model:** VGGT-1B provides geometric features.
- **Training Infrastructure:** Experiments were conducted on 8 NVIDIA A100 (80GB) GPUs.
- **Optimization:** Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 1 × 10^-6 and a 0.03 warmup ratio.
- **Epochs and Batch Sizes:** Alignment phase runs for one epoch with a batch size of 32. Spatial-Aware phase uses a batch size of 96.
- **Training Duration:** Approximately 14 hours for alignment and 20 hours for spatial-aware fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The GeoSense framework was evaluated against various MLLMs on a suite of 10 diverse benchmarks, encompassing both spatial reasoning and general visual understanding tasks.

#### 4.1. Empirical Foundation for Adaptive Integration

Initial quantitative analysis using a standard VG-LLM on a mixed dataset of spatial reasoning and general VQA tasks provided an empirical foundation. Out of approximately 700k samples, about 8% exhibited performance variance depending on geometry usage. Specifically, 5% of samples required 3D features for correctness, while 3% suffered performance degradation when geometric features were added. This indicated that geometry can act as noise. Furthermore, scaling up training data (from 385k to 940k) with a rigid integration paradigm (VG-LLM) led to degraded performance on general visual benchmarks (e.g., POPE score dropped from 86.9 to 74.2, and average score from 52.0 to 48.2), confirming the suboptimality of rigid integration.

#### 4.2. Performance Comparison

GeoSense demonstrated competitive performance across the evaluated benchmarks (Table 2).

- **Overall Performance:** GeoSense achieved the highest overall rank, with an average score of 56.6 on spatial reasoning benchmarks and 55.9 on general visual benchmarks. This compares to the baseline Qwen2.5-VL-3B, which scored 43.4 and 48.3, respectively.
- **Spatial Reasoning:** GeoSense significantly outperformed the Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline on spatial reasoning tasks. For example, it achieved an average spatial score of 56.6, compared to 43.4 for the baseline. It also showed competitive spatial reasoning performance relative to the larger Qwen2.5-VL-7B model (50.5 spatial average). On the BLINK benchmark, which assesses diverse spatial subtasks, GeoSense attained the highest overall score. When compared to a fine-tuned VG-LLM (940k data), GeoSense showed improved spatial reasoning, indicating that gains were attributable to the adaptive mechanism rather than solely data quality.
- **General Visual Reasoning:** GeoSense maintained robust performance on general visual tasks. It dynamically suppressed geometric features, triggering them for approximately 3% of samples, which mitigated cross-modal interference. The model also achieved superior performance on the WeMath dataset, which the authors attribute to a significant portion of its test set involving spatial-geometric mathematical problems. This suggests that the activated perceptual capability allows the model to retain spatial imagination for complex problem-solving even without explicit external geometric inputs.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Geometry Injection Scheme (Table 3):** An ablation study compared three VGGT integration schemes: without VGGT (baseline), Visual Fusion (element-wise addition), and GeoSense's Adaptive Selection. GeoSense's adaptive scheme activated VGGT features for an average of 35.68% of samples (43.7% on VSI-Bench, 27.58% on MindCube-Tiny), demonstrating flexibility in usage. GeoSense generally outperformed the other schemes on most VSI-Bench tasks, but lagged on "Relative Direction" tasks. For the MindCube Rotation task, all VGGT-utilizing schemes showed performance degradation, with Visual Fusion performing slightly better than GeoSense in this specific case, potentially due to VGGT's limitations with wide-angle views and the implicit dilution of negative impacts in feature fusion.
- **Effectiveness of Components (Table 4):** A study across different training stages (Initialized Model, Alignment-Tuned Model, Perception-Tuned Model) revealed:
 - **Spatial Reasoning:** Consistent monotonic improvement, with the average score increasing from 51.0 (vanilla Qwen2.5-VL-3B) to 63.4 (Perception-Tuned GeoSense). Significant gains (+35.4) were observed on BLINK.
 - **General Visual Reasoning:** Exhibited a "dip-then-recover" trajectory. The initial model was robust due to pre-trained projection. Alignment SFT caused a temporary performance degradation (e.g., MME dropped to 1803), attributed to the disruption by special tokens. Perception SFT reversed this decline (MME recovered to 1922) by selectively gating VGGT embeddings, confirming the design objective of enhancing spatial reasoning while preserving general understanding.
- **Confidence of Trigger Token (Figure 4):** Analysis of the trigger token's confidence showed high scores exclusively after perceptual fine-tuning for geometry-reliant tasks. A control experiment with random noise input significantly attenuated the confidence, confirming that the model's decision for spatial information requirements relies on a multi-modal synergy between textual prompts and visual semantics, not solely textual priors.

#### 4.4. Capability on Embodied Task (Table 5)

On the Quantiphy-validation benchmark for embodied tasks, GeoSense achieved the best overall performance (37.36%) compared to general-purpose and specialized models of similar scale. It consistently ranked within the top three for all four sub-scenarios (static/dynamic 2D and 3D tasks), demonstrating a balanced trade-off between 2D perception and 3D geometric reasoning. Performance in dynamic scenarios was generally lower, which the authors conjecture is due to a distribution bias in the fine-tuning data with fewer samples involving significant motion and viewpoint variation.

#### 4.5. Case Study (Figure 5)

Case studies illustrated the model's internalized perception. GeoSense explicitly requested geometric features to distinguish a real kitten from its mirror reflection (Figure 5a) and to filter out occlusion, preventing misclassification (Figure 5b). For general visual tasks, the model predominantly suppressed geometric feature activation. In some simple directional queries, the model derived the correct answer via semantic and commonsense reasoning without triggering explicit geometric input (Figure 5f), demonstrating context-dependent decision-making.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GeoSense framework represents a contribution to the field of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) by addressing a core challenge: the efficient and intelligent integration of geometric information.

The primary significance of this work lies in its **novelty in internalizing geometric necessity perception.** Unlike prior methods that rigidly inject geometric features, GeoSense endows MLLMs with the autonomous capability to determine *when* geometric information is required for a given task. This is achieved through a model-adaptive training pipeline that extracts empirical priors directly from the model's own inference discrepancies, allowing it to learn contextual awareness rather than relying on pre-defined rules.

The **decoupled input architecture** and **two-stage training strategy** are methodological advancements that treat geometric features as an on-demand resource. This design specifically aims to prevent the "pollution" of high-resolution 2D visual streams by potentially irrelevant geometric embeddings, thereby preserving the model's general visual reasoning capabilities.

The **main findings** demonstrate that GeoSense achieves a balanced performance profile:
- It establishes competitive performance on spatial reasoning benchmarks, indicating its effectiveness in enhancing understanding of 3D relationships, configurations, and transformations.
- Crucially, it **preserves robust general multimodal reasoning capabilities**, mitigating the performance degradation and increased computational overhead observed in models that rigidly incorporate geometry. This efficiency gain, particularly in terms of selectively invoking additional processing, makes the approach suitable for deployment on resource-constrained edge devices, as highlighted by the authors.

The **potential impact** of GeoSense extends to several domains:
- **Robustness and Reliability:** By enabling MLLMs to dynamically adapt their reasoning pathways, GeoSense can lead to more robust and reliable systems in real-world applications where spatial reasoning is critical, such as autonomous driving, robotics, and embodied AI. Improved spatial understanding can enhance decision accuracy and navigation capabilities.
- **Efficiency:** The "on-demand" nature of geometric feature utilization means computational resources are conserved when 3D information is not necessary. This efficiency is particularly relevant for scaling MLLMs and deploying them in environments with limited computational power.
- **Self-Aware AI Systems:** The framework moves MLLMs closer to a form of self-awareness, where models can introspectively assess their informational needs for a given task. This capability could be a step towards more intelligent and adaptable AI agents.

Future work outlined by the authors includes exploring the integration of diverse 3D representations (beyond VGGT, e.g., point clouds) and further optimizing the internal trigger mechanism for even greater efficiency on edge devices. These directions suggest that the foundational concept of internalizing geometric necessity perception could be expanded and refined, further cementing its significance in the development of advanced multimodal intelligence.
