# A Survey on Open-Vocabulary Detection and Segmentation: Past, Present, and Future

> [!summary] Summary
> A survey from The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology offers a novel taxonomy to comprehensively review Open-Vocabulary Detection and Segmentation, detailing the paradigm shift driven by Vision-Language Models and weak supervision, and outlining current challenges and future research directions.

## Problem

- Traditional object detection and segmentation models are constrained by a 'closed vocabulary,' limiting them to recognizing pre-defined categories.
- The high cost and labor intensity of manual annotation restrict the scalability and real-world applicability of these models to novel, unseen objects.
- Current AI systems lack the ability to classify and segment arbitrary visual concepts, a core human perception capability.

## Method

- Developed a comprehensive taxonomy that categorizes Open-Vocabulary Detection and Segmentation methods based on their use of ==weak supervision signals==.
- Systematically analyzed diverse methodologies, including ==visual-semantic space mapping, pseudo-labeling, knowledge distillation==, and transfer learning.
- Distinguished between =='Zero-Shot' (without weak supervision) and 'Open-Vocabulary' (with weak supervision)== settings across various vision tasks.

## Results

- The survey consolidates diverse research into a structured framework, demonstrating a **'huge performance leap'** in open-vocabulary capabilities over traditional zero-shot methods.
- It highlights the central role of Vision-Language Models in achieving state-of-the-art results across detection and segmentation tasks.
- The work identifies critical challenges and outlines future research directions, aiming to guide the development of more generalizable and adaptable AI perception systems for real-world applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A significant performance improvement in OVD/OVS is directly linked to the adoption of weak supervision signals and large pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs).
> - VLMs are foundational for state-of-the-art approaches, utilizing their text encoders as generalized classifiers and image encoders for feature extraction or knowledge distillation.
> - Effective methodologies include region-aware training, pseudo-labeling, knowledge distillation, and transfer learning from VLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey positions itself at the forefront of modern computer vision, addressing one of the most critical limitations of traditional deep learning models: their **closed-vocabulary nature**. For the past decade, remarkable progress in object detection and segmentation has been driven by deep learning, yielding models that excel at recognizing pre-defined categories within specific datasets. However, the manual labeling required for these datasets is prohibitively expensive, leading to models that are often limited to a small, fixed set of classes (e.g., 20 classes in PASCAL VOC, 80 in COCO, or 1,203 in LVIS). This constraint severely limits their applicability in real-world scenarios where systems frequently encounter novel, unannotated objects.

The work directly contributes to the emerging and rapidly expanding field of **Open-Vocabulary Detection (OVD) and Segmentation (OVS)**. This field aims to develop models capable of classifying and segmenting objects "beyond pre-defined categories," much like human perception can associate arbitrary visual concepts with natural language descriptions. The survey traces the evolution from earlier attempts at **Zero-Shot Detection (ZSD) and Segmentation (ZSS)**, which typically relied on semantic embeddings from text corpora but lacked strong visual-semantic alignment, to the current era of OVD/OVS.

A pivotal development highlighted by the survey is the advent and widespread adoption of **large pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**, such as CLIP. These models, trained on massive image-text pairs, have demonstrated a superior ability to align visual and linguistic modalities. OVD and OVS leverage VLMs' text encoders as robust, frozen classifiers and their image encoders for feature extraction or knowledge distillation, leading to a significant performance leap compared to their zero-shot predecessors.

The survey also carefully distinguishes OVD/OVS from closely related domains:
- **Visual Grounding:** While both involve aligning language with visual regions, grounding typically focuses on explicit phrase localization or referring expression comprehension within a closed set of described concepts, whereas OVD/OVS aims for open-ended classification.
- **Weakly-Supervised Detection and Segmentation:** These techniques utilize image-level labels but still operate within a closed set of categories. OVD/OVS extends this by generalizing to unseen classes.
- **Open-Set Detection and Segmentation:** This task focuses on identifying "unknown" classes without further subclassifying them, whereas OVD/OVS explicitly aims to classify novel, unknown categories.
- **Open-World Detection and Segmentation:** This involves incrementally learning new classes over time, but typically assumes an "oracle" for labeling unknown instances, which is distinct from the generalization ability sought in OVD/OVS.
- **Out-of-Distribution Detection:** Primarily concerned with rejecting samples outside the training data distribution, often without specific classification of the "out-of-distribution" class.

By providing a comprehensive, structured overview of OVD/OVS, the authors aim to consolidate the vast and rapidly growing body of research, offering a critical resource for researchers. This work serves as a foundational reference, helping to define the field, organize its methodologies, identify its challenges, and chart its future trajectory, thereby playing a crucial role in shaping the research agenda for more generalized and adaptable AI perception systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this survey is to offer a **detailed, comprehensive, and up-to-date review of recent advancements in Open-Vocabulary Detection (OVD) and Open-Vocabulary Segmentation (OVS)**. The authors recognize the explosive growth of research in this area and the increasing difficulty for researchers to keep pace with new developments.

The core motivation stems from the fundamental limitations of conventional object detection and segmentation models. These models, while powerful, operate within a "closed vocabulary" – they can only recognize and delineate objects belonging to categories present in their training datasets. This limitation is a significant bottleneck for real-world applications such as autonomous driving, robotics, and intelligent surveillance, where systems must encounter and understand novel objects not seen during training. The reliance on expensive and labor-intensive manual annotation for new categories further exacerbates this problem.

Specifically, the authors are motivated to:

1. **Resolve the Closed-Vocabulary Constraint:** The overarching goal of the surveyed field is to enable computer vision models to classify objects beyond pre-defined categories, bridging the gap between current AI capabilities and human-like open-ended visual perception.
2. **Organize Diverse Research Efforts:** With a "plethora of methods" emerging, the authors aim to impose structure on this complex landscape by developing a **novel taxonomy**. This taxonomy is designed to organize different tasks and methodologies, making the field more accessible and understandable.
3. **Thoroughly Analyze Methodologies:** The survey intends to delve into the "main design principles, key challenges, development routes, methodology strengths, and weaknesses" of various OVD and OVS approaches. This in-depth analysis is crucial for understanding *why* certain methods succeed and *where* future improvements are needed.
4. **Cover a Broad Scope of Tasks and Settings:** Unlike existing surveys that might focus on limited tasks, this work explicitly aims for broad coverage, including zero-shot and open-vocabulary settings across detection, semantic, instance, and panoptic segmentation, as well as extending to 3D and video understanding. This holistic view reflects the pervasive nature of the open-vocabulary challenge across different perception tasks.
5. **Benchmark State-of-the-Art:** The inclusion of benchmark tables (in the appendix and online) serves to provide a quantitative comparison of leading methods, offering researchers a reference for performance and key components.
6. **Stimulate Future Research:** By identifying current challenges and proposing "several promising directions," the authors aim to guide and encourage future research efforts, pushing the boundaries of open-vocabulary perception.

In essence, the survey is a critical academic endeavor to synthesize, categorize, evaluate, and prognosticate in a rapidly advancing subfield of computer vision that holds immense promise for practical AI applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach (of the Survey)

The methodology of this survey is highly structured and comprehensive, designed to systematically review and categorize the vast body of literature on Open-Vocabulary Detection (OVD) and Segmentation (OVS). The authors’ approach can be broken down into several key components:

1. **Problem Definition and Contextualization:** The survey begins by meticulously defining the problem of OVD/OVS, distinguishing it from traditional closed-set tasks and closely related domains such as zero-shot learning, open-set recognition, weakly-supervised learning, and visual grounding. This foundational step ensures clarity and sets the scope for the subsequent review. It establishes the critical difference between zero-shot (no access to unannotated novel objects) and open-vocabulary (allows weak supervision signals) settings.

2. **Development of a Universal Taxonomy:** This is a central methodological contribution of the survey. The authors propose a novel taxonomy to organize diverse tasks and methodologies. The key discriminator for this taxonomy is the **"permission and usage of weak supervision signals."**
 - **Settings:** The top level differentiates between "Zero-Shot" (w/o weak supervision signals) and "Open-Vocabulary" (with weak supervision signals).
 - **Methodologies:** Within these settings, methods are categorized based on their core technical approach:
 - **Zero-Shot:**
 - *Visual-Semantic Space Mapping:* Learning explicit mappings between visual features and semantic embeddings.
 - *Novel Visual Feature Synthesis:* Generating synthetic features for unseen classes to augment training data.
 - **Open-Vocabulary:**
 - *Region-Aware Training:* Leveraging image-text pairs (without Vision-Language Model image encoders, VLMs-IE) to align regions and words, often using grounding or contrastive losses.
 - *Pseudo-Labeling:* Employing large pre-trained VLMs or self-training to generate explicit pseudo region-word or region-caption pairs for novel classes.
 - *Knowledge Distillation:* Transferring visual knowledge from pre-trained VLMs-IE (as teachers) to a student detector/segmentor, typically via distillation losses.
 - *Transfer Learning:* Directly utilizing frozen VLMs-IE as feature extractors, fine-tuning them, or adapting them with lightweight modules like prompts or adapters.

3. **Detailed Methodological Analysis:** For each category in the taxonomy, the survey provides an in-depth analysis:
 - **Design Principles:** Explaining the underlying ideas and theoretical foundations.
 - **Key Techniques:** Describing specific algorithms, architectural components (e.g., type of backbone, detection/segmentation head), and loss functions (e.g., polarity loss, bipartite matching, contrastive loss, L1 distillation loss).
 - **Development Routes:** Tracing the evolution of ideas within each methodology.
 - **Strengths and Weaknesses:** Critically evaluating the advantages and limitations of each approach. For instance, pseudo-labeling offers hard alignment but requires knowing novel class names, while region-aware training offers soft alignment but can be noisy.

4. **Broad Task Coverage:** The taxonomy is designed to be "universal across different tasks," meaning it applies to:
 - Object Detection
 - Semantic, Instance, and Panoptic Segmentation
 - 3D Scene Understanding (Detection, Semantic Segmentation, Instance Segmentation)
 - Video Understanding (Instance Segmentation)

5. **Benchmarking and Resources:** The survey complements its qualitative review with quantitative data. It includes an appendix (and an online resource, `awesome-ovd-ovs`) that benchmarks state-of-the-art methods across various datasets and evaluation metrics. This benchmarking helps validate the effectiveness of different approaches and provides a practical guide for researchers.

6. **Identification of Challenges and Future Directions:** Based on the comprehensive review, the authors synthesize current limitations (e.g., overfitting, confusion with background, noisy correspondence, VLM adaptation, inference speed) and propose concrete avenues for future research. This forward-looking aspect is crucial for a survey, guiding the community's efforts.

By adopting this systematic and multi-faceted approach, the survey provides a structured and insightful overview, not just listing papers, but critically analyzing the underlying principles and trajectory of open-vocabulary perception research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results (Insights from the Survey)

The comprehensive survey reveals several key findings and insights regarding the development and current state of Open-Vocabulary Detection (OVD) and Segmentation (OVS):

1. **Paradigm Shift Driven by Weak Supervision and VLMs:** The most significant finding is the **"huge performance leap"** achieved by OVD/OVS compared to earlier Zero-Shot Detection (ZSD) and Segmentation (ZSS) methods. This leap is primarily attributed to the strategic adoption of **weak supervision signals**, particularly vast amounts of image-text pairs and the emergence of **large pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs)** like CLIP. VLMs effectively align visual and semantic modalities, enabling models to generalize to novel concepts more robustly than methods relying solely on unsupervised semantic embeddings (e.g., Word2Vec).

2. **Dominance of VLM-Centric Approaches:** VLMs are now central to nearly all state-of-the-art OVD/OVS methods.
 - **Text Encoder as Classifier:** The frozen text encoder of VLMs (e.g., CLIP's text encoder encoding template prompts) serves as a powerful, generalizable classifier for open-vocabulary categories.
 - **Image Encoder for Representation/Distillation:** The VLM image encoder is extensively used either as a frozen feature extractor or as a teacher model for knowledge distillation, imparting its rich visual-semantic understanding to downstream detectors/segmentors.

3. **Effective Methodologies Leveraging Weak Supervision:** The survey's taxonomy highlights several successful methodological paradigms:
 - **Region-Aware Training:** Methods that establish implicit and soft alignment between image regions and natural language words in captions (e.g., using bi-directional contrastive losses) effectively expand the model's vocabulary and improve generalization without explicit instance-level supervision for novel classes.
 - **Pseudo-Labeling:** Approaches that generate explicit "hard" pseudo-labels (bounding boxes or masks) for novel objects, often guided by VLMs or self-training frameworks, prove highly effective in training. The success hinges on the quality and iterative refinement of these pseudo-labels.
 - **Knowledge Distillation:** This paradigm, where a student network learns to mimic the region embeddings or overall visual-semantic space of a powerful VLM teacher, is a prevalent and successful strategy. It effectively transfers VLM's zero-shot recognition capabilities to dense prediction tasks while often mitigating the computational cost of direct VLM inference.
 - **Transfer Learning:** Directly adapting or fine-tuning VLMs, or employing lightweight adapters and prompt tuning, has shown strong performance. This approach minimizes training from scratch and preserves much of the VLM's pre-trained knowledge, offering a parameter-efficient way to adapt powerful foundation models.

4. **Challenges and Limitations Persist:** Despite significant progress, the survey identifies several persistent challenges:
 - **Overfitting and Bias:** Models still struggle with overfitting to base classes, leading to reduced performance on novel categories. Issues include lower quality/quantity of novel proposals and misclassification of novel objects into base classes or background.
 - **Confusion with Background:** Effectively discriminating novel objects from complex background clutter remains a challenge, as background representations are often simplified or under-represented.
 - **Noisy Region-Word Correspondence:** Even with image-text pairs, the exact alignment between specific visual regions and individual words can be weak and noisy, potentially hindering precise open-vocabulary understanding.
 - **VLM Adaptation Issues:** The domain gap between VLM pretraining (e.g., low-resolution full images) and downstream detection/segmentation tasks (e.g., high-resolution crops with specific contexts) can degrade performance. Catastrophic forgetting during fine-tuning also remains a concern.
 - **Inference Speed and Evaluation:** Many state-of-the-art models, built on heavy backbones, are slow for real-time applications. Furthermore, evaluation metrics might be overly strict for open-world scenarios, for example, not accounting for synonyms.

5. **Expansion Beyond 2D Images:** The principles and methodologies of OVD/OVS are successfully extending to **3D scene understanding and video analysis**, often by leveraging 2D image-based VLMs as an intermediate bridge due to the scarcity of direct 3D or video-text paired data. This indicates the versatility and foundational nature of the OVD/OVS research direction.

In summary, the survey highlights a field rapidly evolving through the integration of large VLMs and sophisticated weak supervision techniques, while also clearly delineating the remaining hurdles that demand future research attention.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds significant implications for the field of computer vision and its applications, with a potential to impact both academic research and real-world technological advancements.

1. **Enabling Generalizable AI Perception for Real-World Applications:** The most profound impact lies in advancing AI's ability to perceive and understand the world in a more human-like, open-ended manner. By tackling the "closed-vocabulary" problem, OVD and OVS are critical for developing robust and adaptable AI systems in dynamic environments. This directly benefits crucial applications such as:
 - **Autonomous Driving:** Vehicles need to recognize unforeseen objects (e.g., unusual road debris, new types of vehicles, rare animals) for safe navigation.
 - **Intelligent Robotics:** Robots operating in diverse human environments must identify and interact with a vast array of objects they haven't explicitly been trained on.
 - **Content Moderation and Search:** Enabling identification of arbitrary visual concepts in large-scale image and video databases.
 - **Medical Imaging:** Potentially assisting in the detection of novel anomalies or diseases not extensively covered in training data.

2. **Structuring and Accelerating Research:** As a comprehensive and meticulously structured review, the survey serves as an invaluable resource for the research community. Its novel taxonomy provides a clear framework for understanding, categorizing, and comparing diverse methodologies. This organization can:
 - **Onboard New Researchers:** Quickly bring new academics and practitioners up to speed on the state-of-the-art.
 - **Guide Research Directions:** The thorough analysis of strengths, weaknesses, and key challenges, combined with concrete "future directions," will directly inform and stimulate new research proposals, experiments, and innovations. It helps avoid redundant efforts and focuses attention on unsolved problems.
 - **Facilitate Collaboration:** A shared understanding of the landscape can foster collaboration between different research groups.

3. **Highlighting the Role of Foundation Models:** The survey underscores the transformative impact of large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as "foundation models" in computer vision. It demonstrates how these broadly pre-trained models can be effectively adapted for downstream, specialized tasks like object detection and segmentation, shifting the paradigm from training task-specific models from scratch to leveraging and fine-tuning powerful, general-purpose representations. This emphasizes a broader trend in AI research towards transfer learning and multimodal integration.

4. **Promoting Cross-Modal and Multi-Task Integration:** By reviewing OVD/OVS across various 2D tasks and extending to 3D and video understanding, the survey implicitly promotes research into unified, multi-modal perception systems. The identified future directions, such as unifying OVD and OVS, combining different foundation models (SAM, CLIP, DINO, T2I Diffusion), and integrating with Multimodal Large Language Models (LLMs), point towards a future where AI systems possess a more holistic and integrated understanding of visual and linguistic information.

5. **Identifying Critical Bottlenecks:** The explicit discussion of challenges like overfitting, background confusion, noisy supervision, and VLM adaptation issues provides a clear roadmap for researchers to focus their efforts. Addressing these bottlenecks will be crucial for the continued progress and eventual deployment of truly open-vocabulary perception systems.

In conclusion, this survey is not merely a summary of existing work but a strategic document that maps the current landscape, articulates the critical challenges, and charts a promising course for the future of AI perception, paving the way for more intelligent, adaptable, and robust visual AI systems.
