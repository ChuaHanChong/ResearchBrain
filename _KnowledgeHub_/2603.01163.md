# BeautyGRPO: Aesthetic Alignment for Face Retouching via Dynamic Path Guidance and Fine-Grained Preference Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Sun Yat-sen University and vivo BlueImage Lab developed BeautyGRPO, a reinforcement learning framework for face retouching that effectively aligns outputs with human aesthetic preferences while preserving high visual fidelity. This system leverages a new fine-grained preference dataset and a multi-dimensional reward model, combined with Dynamic Path Guidance (DPG) to stabilize stochastic exploration, resulting in superior aesthetic quality and naturalness in retouched images as confirmed by quantitative metrics and user studies.

## Problem

- Existing supervised face retouching methods optimize for pixel-level reconstruction, often failing to capture subjective human aesthetic preferences and leading to rigid or unnatural results.
- Online reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks for image generation, when applied to face retouching, suffer from a "fidelity-exploration conflict," where stochastic noise for exploration introduces visual artifacts and compromises high-fidelity requirements.
- There is a lack of fine-grained reward models and suitable datasets for face retouching that can provide detailed perceptual feedback on subtle aesthetic aspects like skin texture, blemish removal, and identity preservation.

## Method

- Curated ==FRPref-10K==, a large-scale dataset of 10,000 high-resolution preference pairs with multi-dimensional aesthetic annotations collected via a hybrid VLM-Human pipeline, and trained a specialized reward model on this data.
- Developed Dynamic Path Guidance (DPG), an innovative mechanism that stabilizes SDE-based stochastic exploration by dynamically guiding the sampling trajectory towards high-fidelity anchors while introducing controlled noise to prevent "stochastic drift."
- Integrated the fine-grained reward model and DPG into an extended FlowGRPO framework, optimizing a flow-matching image editing policy to enable reward-driven aesthetic alignment beyond fixed supervised targets.

## Results

- BeautyGRPO consistently achieved superior scores across nearly all no-reference perceptual and aesthetic metrics (NIMA, MUSIQ, MANIQA, NRQM, TOPIQ, NIQE) on FFHQR and in-the-wild datasets, outperforming state-of-the-art specialized and general image editing models.
- A user study confirmed BeautyGRPO's strong alignment with human preferences, achieving significantly higher win rates (**63.25%**) compared to all baselines and demonstrating accurate alignment with human judgments on fine-grained aesthetic dimensions.
- The framework produced natural and realistic retouched images, effectively removing blemishes and preserving natural skin texture and identity features, avoiding the common "plastic-like" or over-smoothed appearance of other methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving true aesthetic alignment in face retouching requires moving beyond pixel-level supervision to models that can actively learn from and respond to nuanced human preferences through reward signals.
> - The fidelity-exploration conflict in SDE-based online RL can be resolved by dynamically guiding stochastic trajectories with high-quality anchors, allowing for effective exploration without sacrificing visual stability.
> - A dedicated, fine-grained reward model, trained on multi-dimensional human preference data, is crucial for providing accurate and perceptually relevant feedback to guide reinforcement learning in subtle aesthetic tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Digital portraiture and face retouching have become ubiquitous with the rise of social media and advanced imaging devices. This field demands sophisticated solutions that can enhance aesthetic appeal by removing imperfections while preserving the unique identity and natural features of a face. The research landscape in face retouching has primarily evolved along two main avenues, both of which face significant limitations that BeautyGRPO aims to address.

**Traditional and Supervised Learning Approaches:**
Historically, face retouching moved from traditional filtering and restoration methods to advanced deep learning architectures, including CNN-based and Transformer-based models. More recently, general image editing models have also demonstrated capability in this domain. However, the vast majority of these methods rely on supervised learning, where models are trained to mimic pixel-level labels or reference images. The paper identifies several critical drawbacks of this paradigm:
- **Pixel-level Mimicry vs. Subjective Aesthetics:** Supervised objectives compel models to reproduce labeled references, often failing to capture the complex, subjective nature of human aesthetic preferences. This leads to outputs that might be pixel-accurate but perceptually rigid or unnatural.
- **Data Scarcity and Overfitting:** Curating large, high-quality datasets of retouched faces with pixel-level annotations is challenging and expensive. Supervised learning often overfits to specific retouching styles present in limited datasets, hindering the model's ability to generalize or discover superior aesthetic solutions.
- **Inability to Surpass Training Data:** By optimizing for reconstruction, supervised models are fundamentally constrained by the aesthetic quality of their training data and cannot learn to generate results that surpass it.

**Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Image Generation:**
In parallel, reinforcement learning has emerged as a powerful framework for aligning generative models with human preferences, particularly in text-to-image (T2I) generation. Methods like FlowGRPO integrate online RL into flow-matching models, enabling active exploration and discovery of solutions beyond supervised training data. This represents a significant step towards preference alignment. However, the paper highlights two major challenges when directly applying T2I-RL frameworks to face retouching:
- **Fidelity-Exploration Conflict:** Online RL frameworks, especially those like FlowGRPO, often inject stochastic noise into the sampling process to encourage exploration. While beneficial for diversity in T2I, this stochasticity is detrimental for high-fidelity tasks like face retouching, where precision and visual stability are paramount. Accumulated noise can cause trajectories to drift, introducing noticeable artifacts.
- **Lack of Fine-Grained Reward Models:** Existing reward models for T2I or general image editing primarily focus on global aesthetics or instruction following. They lack the fine-grained perceptual sensitivity needed for face retouching, which requires accurate evaluation of subtle aspects like skin smoothing, blemish removal, texture preservation, and identity consistency.

BeautyGRPO strategically positions itself to bridge these gaps. It proposes an RL framework that leverages the preference alignment capabilities of RL while explicitly addressing the high-fidelity constraints of face retouching and the need for granular aesthetic feedback. By introducing a novel dataset and reward model for fine-grained preferences, and a dynamic path guidance mechanism to stabilize stochastic exploration, this work aims to advance face retouching beyond the limitations of both supervised learning and general RL applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the BeautyGRPO research are clearly articulated, stemming from the limitations of current face retouching paradigms:

1. **Align Face Retouching with Human Aesthetic Preferences:** The core motivation is to move beyond pixel-level label mimicry, which often results in rigid or unnatural retouching, towards a system that understands and responds to the nuanced, subjective aesthetic preferences of human users. This is seen as a fundamental requirement for creating truly appealing and natural digital portraits.
2. **Overcome the Fidelity-Exploration Conflict in Online RL:** While online RL offers a promising avenue for preference alignment, its inherent stochastic exploration, particularly in SDE-based sampling, conflicts with the high-fidelity demands of face retouching. The objective is to devise a mechanism that allows for reward-driven exploration without introducing visual instability, noise artifacts, or deviation from a high-quality manifold.
3. **Develop a Fine-Grained Reward Model for Face Retouching:** Current general-purpose reward models are insufficient for the subtle and multi-dimensional evaluations required in face retouching. A key objective is to create a specialized reward model capable of assessing perceptual differences across critical aesthetic dimensions (e.g., skin smoothing, blemish removal, texture quality, clarity, and identity preservation) to provide accurate feedback for RL.
4. **Construct a High-Quality, Multi-Dimensional Face Retouching Preference Dataset:** To train such a fine-grained reward model, a dedicated, large-scale dataset of human preferences, annotated across specific retouching dimensions, is essential. The creation of such a resource is a prerequisite for achieving the first three objectives.
5. **Enable Discovery of Superior Aesthetic Solutions:** By moving away from fixed supervised targets, the aim is to empower the model to actively explore and discover retouching solutions that surpass the aesthetic quality of existing training data or references, leading to more innovative and pleasing results.

In essence, BeautyGRPO is motivated by the desire to build an intelligent face retouching system that is perceptually aligned with human tastes, robust in its output quality, and capable of autonomous aesthetic improvement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BeautyGRPO is proposed as a reinforcement learning framework built upon three interconnected components to achieve aesthetic alignment and high-fidelity face retouching.

#### 3.1. Fine-Grained Preference Data Curation (FRPref-10K)

Recognizing the lack of suitable data for nuanced aesthetic judgment, the authors constructed **FRPref-10K**, a dataset of 10,000 high-resolution preference pairs with detailed multi-dimensional annotations.
- **Data Collection & Candidate Generation:** Source portraits were curated from FFHQR and a proprietary high-resolution collection, ensuring demographic diversity. For each input image, a diverse pool of retouched candidates was generated using multiple state-of-the-art models (NanoBanana, Flux.1 Kontext, RetouchFormer) with varied random seeds. Preference pairs (`Input, Edited_A, Edited_B`) were formed through both inter-model comparisons and output-vs-label evaluations.
- **Hybrid VLM-Human Annotation Pipeline:** A three-stage protocol was designed to combine the reasoning capabilities of large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with human verification:
 1. **Multi-Dimensional Assessment:** An ensemble of VLMs (GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, Gemini 2.5 Pro) evaluated each pair across five critical aesthetic dimensions: Skin Smoothing, Blemish Removal, Texture Quality, Clarity, and Identity Preservation. VLMs provided quantitative scores and structured reasoning, which were then aggregated. Trained human annotators audited these initial assessments.
 2. **Expert Adjudication:** Discrepancies between human judgment and VLM consensus were resolved by senior experts.

#### 3.2. Multi-Dimensional Retouching Reward Model

Leveraging the FRPref-10K dataset, a specialized reward model $R_{\phi}(I_{in}, I_{out})$ was trained to provide dense, interpretable feedback. Following the UnifiedReward-Thinking paradigm, a three-stage training strategy was used, initializing from Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
- **Stage 1: Structured Reasoning Initialization (SFT):** The model was fine-tuned on a 2K subset of FRPref-10K to generate structured reasoning across the five dimensions within a `<think>` block and a final preference decision in an `<answer>` block.
- **Stage 2: Self-Training with Consistency Filtering:** The Stage 1 model generated multiple reasoning trajectories for the remaining 8K samples. A consistency filtering mechanism ensured preference correctness (alignment with ground truth) and reasoning coherence (semantic consistency between reasoning and decision, evaluated by a high-capacity VLM). Consistent trajectories were used for a second round of SFT.
- **Stage 3: Robustness Enhancement via GRPO:** For inconsistent samples, GRPO (Generalized Policy Rejection Optimization) was applied. The model explored diverse reasoning paths, with evaluation based on outcome rewards (preference accuracy) and process rewards (reasoning coherence, assessed by a lightweight verifier). This phase aimed to strengthen the model's ability to produce stable and human-aligned aesthetic judgments.

#### 3.3. BeautyGRPO with Dynamic Path Guidance (DPG)

The trained reward model provides feedback for aligning a flow-matching image editing policy model $\pi_{\theta}$. BeautyGRPO extends the FlowGRPO framework with **Dynamic Path Guidance (DPG)** to stabilize SDE-based exploration, reconciling stochasticity with high-fidelity requirements.
- **The Fidelity-Exploration Conflict:** Standard FlowGRPO introduces stochastic noise to the ODE trajectory (Eq. 1) for exploration. While this enhances diversity in T2I, it causes "stochastic drift" in face retouching, accumulating noise and leading to artifacts, pushing the trajectory away from the high-fidelity manifold.
- **Dynamic Path Guidance (DPG) for Anchored Exploration:** DPG stabilizes the SDE trajectory by softly guiding it towards an ideal deterministic path while maintaining controlled stochasticity.
 - **Stability Anchor ($x_{anchor_0}$):** High-preference samples from FRPref-10K are used as anchors. Crucially, these anchors are *only* used within the sampling process for stability, not as pixel-level supervision targets during policy optimization. Their role is to regularize the trajectory around a high-fidelity manifold.
 - **Dynamic Trajectory Replanning:** At each sampling timestep $x_t$, DPG dynamically computes an anchor-based ODE path. It replans a straight-line ODE trajectory connecting $x_t$ to $x_{anchor_0}$. The target state for the next timestep $x^*_{t-\Delta t}$ on this path is a linear interpolation (Eq. 6).
 - **Guided Correction with Controlled Stochasticity:** A correction vector $z_{anchor_t}$ is introduced to steer the predicted mean $\mu_t$ towards $x^*_{t-\Delta t}$ (Eq. 7). To prevent collapsing exploration, this correction vector is linearly blended with standard Gaussian noise $z_{std_t}$ to form $z_{mix_t}$ (Eq. 8). The blending coefficient $\lambda(t)$ is time-dependent, decreasing along the reverse-time trajectory. This means early steps (high $t$) use stronger anchor guidance for structural corrections, while later steps (low $t$) rely more on stochastic noise for fine-grained exploration.
 - **DPG Formulation (Eq. 9):** This yields a guided update $x_{guided_{t-\Delta t}}$ that balances between the model's drift term, the anchor-guided target, and stochastic noise.
 - **Log Probability for Policy Optimization:** The modified transition probability $p_{\theta}(x_{t-\Delta t} | x_t)$ under the DPG rule is used to compute the likelihood ratio $r_t(\theta)$ for the GRPO objective (Eq. 3). For computational efficiency, DPG updates are applied to one randomly selected timestep in each of three segments of the sampling trajectory, with ODE updates for others.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of BeautyGRPO is demonstrated through extensive quantitative evaluations, visual comparisons, and user studies, alongside detailed ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

Table 1 presents a quantitative comparison of BeautyGRPO against state-of-the-art specialized face retouching models (e.g., RetouchFormer, VRetouchEr), general image editing models (e.g., NanoBanana, Flux.K w/LoRA), and a direct RL baseline (Flux.K+LoRA w/FlowGRPO). The evaluation focuses on no-reference perceptual and aesthetic metrics, as pixel-level full-reference metrics (like PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) are deemed unsuitable for subjective aesthetic enhancement tasks.
- **Superior Aesthetic and Perceptual Quality:** BeautyGRPO consistently achieves the best scores across nearly all no-reference metrics on both the FFHQR and an in-the-wild dataset. It shows significant gains in NIMA, MUSIQ, MANIQA, NRQM, and TOPIQ (higher is better), and a noticeably lower NIQE (lower is better), indicating superior naturalness, aesthetic appeal, and overall perceptual quality.
- **Identity Preservation:** Despite aesthetic enhancements, BeautyGRPO maintains high ArcFace scores (0.952 on FFHQR, 0.944 in-the-wild), ensuring robust preservation of facial identity.
- **FID Discussion:** While its FID score is slightly higher than the best supervised baseline, this is an expected outcome. By explicitly optimizing for human preference, BeautyGRPO explores a broader range of plausible retouching styles, moving beyond the label distribution that FID measures, and discovering solutions that better align with human aesthetic judgments.

#### 4.2. Visualization Results

Visual comparisons (Figure 4) strongly support the quantitative findings.
- **Natural and Realistic Retouching:** BeautyGRPO effectively removes blemishes cleanly while meticulously preserving natural skin texture, pores, natural gloss, and balanced skin tone. Crucially, it retains distinctive identity features such as moles and subtle wrinkles, avoiding the "plastic-like" or over-smoothed appearance common in other methods.
- **Addressing Baseline Limitations:** Specialized retouching models often suffer from inaccurate blemish detection, incomplete removal, or excessive smoothing that erases essential skin texture. General editing models tend to overedit, misidentifying natural features as defects, leading to an unnaturally glossy or synthetic look. BeautyGRPO avoids these pitfalls, yielding visually refined and natural results.

#### 4.3. User Study

A user study involving 100 participants confirmed BeautyGRPO's strong alignment with human preferences.
- **Overall Preference (Questionnaire I):** BeautyGRPO achieved significantly higher win rates (63.25%) compared to all baselines (ranging from 6.50% to 12.00%), demonstrating a clear preference for its outputs among users. This advantage was consistent across various age and gender demographic groups (Figure 12).
- **Dimension-wise Ratings (Questionnaire II):** The study also showed that our specialized reward model aligns more closely with human judgments on the five fine-grained perceptual dimensions (Figure 5) compared to general VLMs, underscoring its accuracy in capturing subtle retouching preferences.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Reward Model Effectiveness (Table 3):** Integrating BeautyGRPO with our fine-grained reward model showed consistent gains across NIMA, MUSIQ, MANIQA, and TOPIQ compared to using existing general image editing reward models (EditReward, EditScore, UnifiedReward-Edit). This validates the importance of fine-grained, multi-dimensional preference modeling for face retouching.
- **Backbone Generalization (Table 4, Figure 6):** Applying BeautyGRPO to a different backbone, Qwen-Image-Edit, demonstrated its generalizability. The BeautyGRPO-trained variant significantly outperformed both the official pretrained and LoRA-tuned Qwen-Image-Edit models across all metrics, producing more natural skin texture and cleaner blemish removal.
- **DPG Steps K (Figure 7):** An ablation on the number of Dynamic Path Guidance (DPG) steps K during sampling revealed that K=3 provided comparable MUSIQ and NIMA scores to K=5 but with faster sampling, leading to its adoption as the default setting.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The BeautyGRPO framework represents a significant step forward in the field of face retouching and, more broadly, in preference-aligned generative AI. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging the Fidelity-Preference Gap:** The work successfully addresses the long-standing challenge of aligning generative models with subjective human aesthetic preferences while maintaining the high fidelity required for sensitive tasks like face retouching. This is a critical advancement beyond traditional supervised learning's pixel-level limitations and generic RL's potential for visual instability.
2. **Novel Data and Reward Modeling:** The creation of **FRPref-10K**, a large-scale, fine-grained preference dataset, and the development of a specialized, multi-dimensional reward model trained with a hybrid VLM-Human pipeline, is a substantial contribution. This resource and methodology open new avenues for evaluating and optimizing aesthetic quality in detail, providing a blueprint for similar tasks in other domains.
3. **Innovative Dynamic Path Guidance (DPG):** DPG is a novel and elegant solution to the fidelity-exploration conflict in SDE-based online RL. By dynamically guiding stochastic trajectories towards high-fidelity anchors with controlled noise, it enables effective reward-driven exploration without sacrificing visual stability. This mechanism has implications beyond face retouching, potentially benefiting any high-fidelity image or video generation task requiring subtle aesthetic adjustments.
4. **Superior User Experience:** The consistent outperformance of BeautyGRPO in quantitative metrics and, critically, in human user studies, demonstrates its ability to produce more natural, aesthetically pleasing, and user-aligned face retouching results. This directly translates to a better user experience in consumer applications, particularly for mobile photography and social media platforms.
5. **Practical Application and Generalizability:** The involvement of vivo BlueImage Lab underscores the practical relevance of this research for real-world product integration. Furthermore, the demonstrated generalization of BeautyGRPO across different backbone models (e.g., FluxKontext and Qwen-Image-Edit) suggests its broad applicability and potential for widespread adoption within the generative AI community.
6. **Future Research Avenues:** The work opens several promising directions for future research, including systematic studies of alternative anchor selection strategies (e.g., diversity-aware, multiple anchors), exploring scenarios without explicit high-quality anchors (e.g., pseudo-anchors, self-distilled policies), and investigating inference-time variants of DPG for enhanced control and flexibility.

In conclusion, BeautyGRPO offers a robust, principled, and highly effective framework for aesthetic alignment in face retouching. Its contributions in data, reward modeling, and guided exploration are likely to influence the development of next-generation generative AI tools that are more attuned to human perception and capable of producing truly high-quality, customized visual content.
