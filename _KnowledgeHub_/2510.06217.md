# TaTToo: Tool-Grounded Thinking PRM for Test-Time Scaling in Tabular Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> TATTOO, a tool-grounded Process Reward Model (PRM), enhances Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) by providing reliable step-level supervision for tabular reasoning tasks, utilizing external tools for verification. This approach yields an average 30.9% performance improvement on five tabular benchmarks and achieves state-of-the-art results with 9x greater parameter efficiency compared to larger PRM baselines.

## Problem

- Existing Process Reward Models (PRMs) struggle to provide reliable step-level supervision for tabular reasoning, failing to accurately verify table retrieval and schema interaction steps.
- Current PRMs introduce supervision errors themselves due to inaccurate table lookups or an inability to properly interpret complex tabular interactions.
- These limitations lead to performance bottlenecks for Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) during test-time scaling on tabular tasks, preventing them from fully leveraging increased computational resources.

## Method

- Developed TATTOO, a ==generative PRM== designed with table-aware and tool-integrated supervision to evaluate reasoning steps involving structured tables.
- Implemented a scalable data curation pipeline that synthesizes over 60,000 high-quality, tool-augmented training instances with explicit tool invocations and step-level rewards.
- Utilized a dual-stage training paradigm combining Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) with a specialized tool-grounded reward shaping to align the PRM with robust table-based verification.

## Results

- TATTOO improved the performance of downstream LRMs by an average of **30.9%** at inference across five challenging tabular reasoning benchmarks.
- The **8B**-parameter TATTOO surpassed strong PRM baselines, including models up to **72B** parameters, demonstrating up to **9x** parameter efficiency.
- The PRM exhibited stronger scalability with increased test-time compute (e.g., Best-of-N, Beam Search, DVTS), overcoming the performance saturation observed in existing PRMs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing step-level rewards into inner-thinking and table-grounded components allows for more targeted and precise supervision of complex tabular reasoning processes.
> - Integrating external computation and table lookup tools directly into the PRM's verification mechanism provides objective and accurate assessment of table-involved operations, overcoming prior PRMs' limitations.
> - A dual-stage training approach, especially with a tool-grounding term in the RL reward shaping, is crucial for effectively teaching the PRM to utilize tools and verify tabular reasoning steps reliably.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research by Zou et al. on TATTOO is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: large reasoning models (LRMs), process reward models (PRMs), test-time scaling (TTS), and tabular reasoning.

**Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) and Tabular Reasoning:** LRMs, such as large language models (LLMs), have demonstrated impressive capabilities in various domains, but reasoning over semi-structured tabular data remains a significant challenge. Tables require not only natural language understanding but also precise logical inference across rows, columns, and schema to produce accurate answers. This has led to a surge in research focused on enhancing LRMs' tabular reasoning abilities, including works like the Table-R1 series and Reasoning-Table, often incorporating reinforcement learning (RL) techniques for better alignment.

**Process Reward Models (PRMs) and Test-Time Scaling (TTS):** PRMs have emerged as a powerful paradigm for improving LRMs' reasoning by providing fine-grained, step-level supervision during test-time scaling. Instead of just evaluating the final answer, PRMs assess the correctness of each intermediate reasoning step, thereby guiding the LRM towards more robust and accurate trajectories. TTS strategies (e.g., Best-of-N, Beam Search) leverage these PRM scores to select, resample, or refine LRM-generated responses during inference, effectively scaling compute to improve performance.

**The Identified Gap:** Despite the advancements in both tabular reasoning and PRMs, a critical gap exists: the lack of a PRM specifically designed to supervise tabular reasoning steps. Existing general-domain PRMs, while effective for text-only reasoning, notably struggle with table-specific operations such as sub-table retrieval and schema interaction. This limitation prevents PRMs from reliably verifying LRM outputs on tabular tasks, leading to performance bottlenecks during TTS. The paper's preliminary analysis empirically confirms this, showing that current PRMs fail to distinguish correct table retrieval from incorrect ones and overlook long-range dependencies required for schema interaction.

**Tool Integration with LLMs:** Concurrently, there's been a growing trend of augmenting LLMs with external tools (e.g., code interpreters, APIs, databases) to expand their problem-solving capabilities. Frameworks like ReAct, Toolformer, and ToolRL enable LLMs to dynamically select and use tools to interact with the environment, perform calculations, or retrieve external information. TATTOO cleverly integrates this tool-use paradigm not into the LRM's generation process, but into the *PRM's verification process*, making the supervision itself more accurate and grounded in executable operations.

TATTOO bridges this critical gap by developing the first tool-augmented "thinking" PRM for tabular reasoning. It extends the concept of PRMs by making them "table-grounded" and "tool-integrated," thereby addressing the unique challenges of structured data. This work represents a significant step towards developing more reliable and verifiable AI systems capable of handling complex, real-world data reasoning tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind TATTOO stems from a critical limitation observed in existing Process Reward Models (PRMs) when applied to tabular reasoning tasks. While PRMs have proven effective in providing step-level supervision for text-only reasoning paths, their utility significantly diminishes when reasoning involves interactions with semi-structured tables.

The key motivations are meticulously identified through empirical analysis:
1. **Struggle with Table-Specific Operations:** Existing PRMs, designed predominantly for free-form text, fail to reliably verify reasoning steps that involve table-specific operations. Two critical failure modes were identified:
 - **Table Retrieval:** PRMs cannot effectively supervise whether an LRM correctly extracts the relevant sub-region of an input table corresponding to a given query. They are "insensitive to table retrieval correctness," often assigning positive rewards even to steps involving incorrect or irrelevant table snippets.
 - **Schema Interaction:** PRMs struggle to detect "attention collapse" or misinterpretations when LRMs reason over retrieved table contents, especially when these interactions require long-range dependencies back to earlier retrieval steps. Due to their inherent locality bias, current PRMs' evaluations are highly localized and do not adequately capture such dependencies.
2. **Introduction of Supervision Errors:** Beyond their inability to handle tabular input modalities, current PRMs frequently introduce supervision errors themselves, often stemming from inaccurate table lookups or failed operations during their own internal evaluation process. These errors amplify bias and noise, leading to persistent performance bottlenecks during test-time scaling (TTS).
3. **Performance Bottleneck in Test-Time Scaling:** The limitations of existing PRMs directly translate into a performance ceiling for LRMs on tabular tasks, even with increased test-time compute. The paper demonstrates that PRM efficacy quickly saturates, failing to fully exploit additional computational resources for guiding LRM improvements.

Given these identified shortcomings, the **key objectives** of the TATTOO framework are:
1. **Develop a reliable step-level supervision mechanism for LRMs in tabular reasoning.** This is the overarching goal, aiming to overcome the inherent difficulties of existing PRMs with tabular data.
2. **Design a novel table-grounded PRM framework (TATTOO) that explicitly reasons over tabular reasoning steps.** This involves creating a PRM that understands and evaluates operations specific to tables, rather than treating them as generic text.
3. **Integrate tool-based verification into the PRM to provide precise reward supervision.** By enabling the PRM to leverage external tools (like code interpreters or table lookup utilities), TATTOO aims to achieve objective and accurate verification of tabular operations.
4. **Create a scalable data curation pipeline** capable of generating high-quality, step-level annotations that incorporate table verification rationales and tool-based executions, essential for training such a specialized PRM.
5. **Propose a dual-stage training paradigm** (Supervised Fine-Tuning followed by Reinforcement Learning with tool-grounded reward shaping) to effectively capture tool-use patterns and align the PRM with robust table-based verification.
6. **Demonstrate significant improvements in downstream LRM performance** on diverse and challenging tabular reasoning benchmarks, surpassing existing strong PRM baselines in both accuracy and parameter efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TATTOO is designed as a generative Process Reward Model (PRM) that provides step-level reward supervision specifically tailored for tabular reasoning tasks. Its methodology is built upon two core innovations: a large-scale data curation pipeline and a dual-stage training paradigm.

#### 3.1. Table-Aware and Tool-Integrated Supervision

TATTOO fundamentally rethinks how rewards are assigned for reasoning steps involving tables.
- **Table-Aware Reward Decomposition:** Instead of a single, monolithic reward, TATTOO decomposes the step-level reward `r_i` into two distinct components: `r_i,rea` for inner-thinking steps (text-based reasoning) and `r_i,tab` for table-grounded operations (table retrieval or schema interaction). This allows for targeted supervision, addressing the specific challenges of tabular data.
- **Tool Integration in Verification:** A crucial aspect is enabling TATTOO to leverage external tools for accurate verification. This directly counters the inability of prior PRMs to reliably supervise table-involved steps. The tools include:
 - **Computation tools:** For executing code snippets (e.g., Python, SQL) to perform arithmetic or aggregation.
 - **Table Lookup tools:** For using DataFrame APIs (e.g., Polars) or CSV/Excel readers to retrieve specific rows, columns, or cells.

#### 3.2. TATTOO Data Curation Pipeline

To train TATTOO, a scalable data curation pipeline was developed to synthesize high-quality, tool-augmented, table-aware reward data. This pipeline has three main stages:

1. **Reasoning Trajectory Generation:**
 - Expert LRMs (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, Claude-Opus-4.1) generate multiple reasoning trajectories for table-based questions from diverse benchmarks (TableInstruct, HybridQA, ToTTo, WikiTQ).
 - A dual-verification process involving human annotators and expert LLMs filters these responses to ensure a high-quality pool (`T_pool`) containing both correct and incorrect reasoning patterns.

2. **Verification Synthesis & Reward Assignment:**
 - For **Table Retrieval steps:** The retrieved sub-table is extracted, and an LLM-as-a-judge assesses its relevance to the query. A table reward `r_i,tab` (correct/incorrect) is assigned based on this assessment, explicitly training the PRM to validate retrieval accuracy.
 - For **Schema Interaction steps:** The *correct* sub-table (either from LRM's correct retrieval or manually corrected) is prepended as a "table prefix" to the verification rationale. LLM-as-a-judge then assesses the correctness of the schema interaction, assigning `r_i,tab`. This mitigates locality bias by providing direct context.
 - For **Inner-thinking steps:** An LLM-as-a-judge evaluates the reasoning quality, assigning `r_i,rea`.

3. **Tool Use Synthesis:**
 - The collected verification rationales are augmented with explicit tool invocations, execution results, and feedback.
 - Any manual reasoning steps involving calculations or table lookups within the rationale are replaced with the corresponding tool call and its output. This teaches TATTOO to "think with tools" for verification.
 - This process yields over 60k high-quality training instances with complete verification rationales and step-level rewards.

#### 3.3. Tool-Grounded Dual-Stage Training

TATTOO is trained using a dual-stage paradigm to capture complex tool-use and verification patterns:

1. **Table-Aware Verification with Tools via Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - The PRM (R_θ) is initially finetuned on the curated dataset as a language modeling task.
 - During SFT, TATTOO learns to auto-regressively generate verification rationales and assign rewards. This stage teaches it to: (i) identify accurate sub-table regions, (ii) dynamically incorporate retrieved table prefixes, and (iii) generate rationales that integrate tool-use patterns.

2. **Tool-Grounded Reward Shaping in Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - The SFT-trained checkpoint is further optimized using a modified GRPO (Group-Relative Policy Optimization) algorithm.
 - A dense, per-step reward signal `s_i` is designed for policy optimization:
 - **Label-matching:** `1{ˆr_i = r_i}` rewards correct prediction of the ground-truth step-reward.
 - **Confidence calibration:** `-λ_cal * log R_θ(r_i | T, q, τ)` encourages the PRM to assign higher probability to the ground-truth reward, stabilizing training.
 - **Tool-grounding:** `λ_tool * support(ˆv_i)` explicitly rewards the PRM for generating rationales that correctly incorporate tool outputs, fostering effective tool manipulation.
 - These per-step signals are aggregated into a trajectory-level reward, normalized for group-relative advantages, and used to update the PRM.

#### 3.4. Inference-Time Policy Improvement – An Intuitive View (Theoretical Intuition)

The paper provides theoretical intuition (Theorem 4.1) suggesting that TATTOO's decomposable reward design `r_i` allows each component (`r_i,rea` and `r_i,tab`) to additively contribute to policy improvement. This is contingent on the reward components being individually aligned with the policy advantage function. This theoretical backing highlights how TATTOO's targeted, table-aware rewards ensure precise supervision on both inner reasoning and table-involved operations, thereby enhancing the LRM's Q-value function.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of TATTOO demonstrates significant advancements in tabular reasoning capabilities for Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) when supervised by the proposed PRM.

1. **Overall Performance Superiority:**
 - TATTOO, an 8B-parameter PRM, consistently improves the performance of downstream policy LRMs (specifically DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B) by an average of **30.9% at inference** across five challenging tabular reasoning benchmarks. These benchmarks cover numerical reasoning, fact-checking, and data analysis (TableBench: TB-NR, TB-FC, TB-DA; WTQ; MMQA).
 - It **surpasses strong PRM baselines**, including much larger models like Qwen-2.5-Math-PRM-72B and GenPRM-32B, with up to **9x parameter efficiency**. For instance, on TB-DA, TATTOO (8B) achieves 34.3% accuracy at N=32, significantly outperforming Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-72B (72B) which reached 32.4%.

2. **Effective Scaling with Increased Compute (Best-of-N):**
 - A key limitation of existing PRMs identified in preliminary analysis was performance saturation beyond a certain number of candidate responses (N) in Best-of-N strategies. TATTOO successfully overcomes this bottleneck.
 - It demonstrates **stronger scalability**, yielding consistent performance gains as N increases. For example, on TB-NR, while Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-72B saturates after N=16 (74.9% → 75.3% at N=32), TATTOO continues to improve from 74.2% at N=8 to 78.1% at N=32, showcasing its ability to provide better discriminatory power among a larger pool of responses.

3. **Generalizability Across Diverse Test-Time Scaling (TTS) Strategies:**
 - Beyond Best-of-N, TATTOO exhibits robust generalizability when integrated with other TTS strategies, including **Beam Search** and **Diverse Verifier Tree Search (DVTS)**.
 - It consistently provides steady improvements as the number of responses/paths (N) increases, outperforming baseline PRMs that often plateau. For instance, in Beam Search, TATTOO improves from 45.0% to 54.8% average accuracy, whereas GenPRM saturates around 51%. This highlights TATTOO's versatility and effectiveness in guiding different search mechanisms.

4. **Impact of Dual-Stage Training Paradigm:**
 - **RL's Contribution:** The dual-stage training (Supervised Fine-Tuning followed by Reinforcement Learning) is crucial. The RL policy optimization stage consistently improves performance over the SFT-only variant by an average of **10.2% accuracy** across tasks. This indicates that SFT provides a solid foundation, while RL further refines the PRM's reasoning and tool-use capabilities during verification.
 - **Reward Shaping Effectiveness:** Ablation studies on the RL reward shaping components confirm their significance:
 - The **tool-grounding term** (`λ_tool`) yielded the largest performance drop when removed (e.g., ↓4.0% on TB-DA at N=32), underscoring its critical role in encouraging effective tool utilization during RL training.
 - The **confidence calibration term** (`λ_cal`) also contributed positively, reducing performance by 1.6% on average when excluded, showing its role in stabilizing reward signals.
 - The standard rule-based GRPO reward provided only marginal improvements over SFT, highlighting the specialized design of TATTOO's reward shaping.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TATTOO represents a significant advancement in the field of large language models and their application to structured data, offering both immediate and long-term implications.

1. **Addressing a Critical Research Gap:** The paper successfully identifies and addresses a crucial limitation of existing Process Reward Models: their inability to provide reliable step-level supervision for tabular reasoning. By explicitly designing a "table-grounded" and "tool-integrated" PRM, TATTOO fills this void, enabling LRMs to tackle complex tabular tasks more effectively. This is vital as real-world data often resides in tables, and robust reasoning over such data is increasingly demanded in various applications.

2. **Methodological Innovation in Reward Modeling:** TATTOO introduces several novel methodological contributions:
 - **Decomposable Reward Design:** The separation of rewards for inner-thinking and table-specific operations allows for more targeted and precise supervision.
 - **Scalable Tool-Augmented Data Curation:** The pipeline for generating 60k high-quality, tool-integrated verification rationales is a substantial contribution, addressing the challenge of acquiring fine-grained labels for complex reasoning.
 - **Dual-Stage Training with Tool-Grounded Reward Shaping:** Combining SFT for initial pattern learning with RL for optimized tool utilization and verification alignment provides a powerful paradigm for training robust PRMs. The empirical validation of the reward shaping components highlights the efficacy of this approach.

3. **Enhanced LRM Performance and Efficiency:** The significant improvement (30.9% average gain) in downstream LRM performance on diverse tabular benchmarks directly translates to more capable and reliable AI systems for tasks like numerical analysis, fact-checking, and data analysis. Furthermore, achieving state-of-the-art results with an 8B-parameter model, outperforming 72B-parameter baselines, demonstrates remarkable parameter efficiency. This has practical implications for deployment, reducing computational costs and making advanced tabular reasoning accessible to a wider range of applications and resources.

4. **Catalyst for Future Research in Structured Reasoning:** TATTOO opens several avenues for future work:
 - **PRMs for other Structured Data:** The principles of table-grounded and tool-integrated verification could be extended to other forms of structured or semi-structured data, such as graphs, ontologies, or multimodal inputs (e.g., integrating charts and image-based tables).
 - **More Efficient Reward Modeling:** While RL enhances performance, it adds computational overhead. Future research could focus on more efficient RL objectives or lightweight reward modeling strategies to reduce resource demands.
 - **Auditing Verifier Reliability:** The reliance on automated verification necessitates mechanisms for auditing PRM reliability, especially to prevent the amplification of errors if tools or training data are flawed.
 - **Broader Applications:** Improved tabular reasoning capabilities can directly impact critical domains such as scientific discovery, financial analysis, healthcare data interpretation, and intelligent decision support systems.

In conclusion, TATTOO not only delivers a highly effective solution for a pressing challenge in LLM reasoning but also establishes a new paradigm for building robust, verifiable, and efficient process reward models, paving the way for more reliable and intelligent AI systems interacting with complex, structured information.
