# Generation Models Know Space: Unleashing Implicit 3D Priors for Scene Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> VEGA-3D integrates a pre-trained video diffusion model to provide dense geometric cues, improving fine-grained geometric reasoning and spatial understanding in Multimodal Large Language Models. The framework shows consistent performance gains in tasks like 3D visual grounding and robotic manipulation without explicit 3D supervision.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit 'spatial blindness,' struggling with fine-grained geometric reasoning, precise spatial localization, and understanding physical dynamics in complex scenes.
- Existing solutions rely on explicit 3D inputs (e.g., point clouds, depth maps) or complex geometric scaffolding, which are limited by scarce 3D annotated data, poor generalization, and high architectural complexity/training overhead.
- No prior method existed to quantify whether a pretrained generative model actually encodes useful implicit 3D structure, leaving the choice of which generator to repurpose largely unvalidated.

## Method

- A plug-and-play framework, **VEGA-3D**, repurposes a pre-trained ==video diffusion model== as a ==Latent World Simulator== to extract dense geometric cues.
- It activates the generative model's internal understanding by perturbing the input video's ==latent representation== with noise and forcing it through a ==denoising process==.
- An ==Adaptive Gated Fusion== mechanism dynamically integrates these extracted generative (structural) features with semantic features from a discriminative encoder, balancing their contributions.

## Results

- VEGA-3D consistently improved performance across 3D scene understanding benchmarks (e.g., ScanRefer Acc@0.5 boosted from **51.7 to 56.2**, SQA3D EM from **58.6 to 61.3**), particularly in localization-centric tasks.
- The framework generalized effectively to spatial reasoning tasks on VSI-Bench and enhanced robotic manipulation success rates on the LIBERO suite, demonstrating transferability of implicit physical knowledge.
- Ablation studies confirmed that DiT-based architectures provide superior 3D priors, and the Adaptive Gated Fusion mechanism effectively balances semantic and geometric cues, outperforming other fusion strategies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale video generation models, particularly Transformer-based diffusion models (DiTs), implicitly learn robust 3D structural priors and physical laws from synthesizing temporally coherent videos.
> - A 'Multi-view Correspondence Score' can quantify a generative model's implicit 3D awareness, with higher scores strongly correlating with better downstream 3D task performance.
> - Moderately noisy latent representations and intermediate layers of the generative model's backbone yield the most informative 3D priors for fine-grained structural understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has demonstrated advanced semantic capabilities, but these models frequently exhibit limitations in fine-grained geometric reasoning and understanding of physical dynamics, often referred to as "spatial blindness." Current approaches to address this issue predominantly fall into two categories:
1. **Explicit 3D Modality Integration:** Methods that directly incorporate explicit 3D inputs, such as point clouds or depth maps, to provide definitive geometric grounding. Examples include PointLLM, Point-Bind, and GPT4Point.
2. **Geometric Scaffolding:** Approaches that aim to lift 2D features into 3D space, often involving extra reconstruction modules, distillation from pre-trained 3D backbones, or the use of auxiliary geometric supervision like depth or camera poses. This category includes multi-view methods like Video-3D LLM and GPT4Scene, and techniques like Ross3D, 3DRS, and ThinkWith3D.

Both existing paradigms face challenges such as data scarcity for 3D annotations, limited generalization across diverse scenes, and often require complex multi-stage training pipelines or task-specific geometric labels.

This paper introduces an alternative paradigm that positions itself within an underexplored area: leveraging the implicit spatial and physical priors learned by large-scale video generation models. Recent advancements in video generation models suggest that to synthesize temporally coherent and plausible videos, these models must inherently align appearance with 3D geometry and adhere to consistent physical dynamics. This implicit learning, without explicit 3D supervision, forms a strong learned 3D prior. The work hypothesizes that these implicit physical priors can be repurposed to enhance downstream 3D visual understanding in MLLMs. This approach differs from previous work that couples generation with explicit geometric supervision (e.g., Ross3D, Omni-View) by instead extracting a label-free 3D prior from frozen video diffusion models.

The work also relates to spatial reasoning research, which seeks to mitigate MLLM spatial blindness through data scaling (e.g., SpatialVLM, VLM-3R) or mental simulation (e.g., MindCube, CVP). Unlike these, the presented work treats spatial reasoning as a representational problem, aiming to ground MLLM reasoning in a physically consistent world model using generative priors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with robust 3D spatial awareness and fine-grained geometric reasoning capabilities, overcoming their inherent "spatial blindness." This objective is driven by several motivations:

1. **Addressing MLLM Spatial Blindness:** MLLMs, despite their advanced semantic capabilities, often struggle with tasks requiring precise geometric understanding, spatial relationships, and physical dynamics. For example, they may fail to accurately localize objects or understand relative positions in complex 3D scenes. The authors aim to rectify this deficiency.

2. **Overcoming Limitations of Explicit 3D Methods:** Existing solutions that rely on explicit 3D modalities (e.g., point clouds, depth maps) or complex geometric scaffolding (e.g., 2D-to-3D reconstruction, knowledge distillation) are constrained by the scarcity of high-quality 3D annotated data. This data dependency limits their scalability, generalization to novel environments, and often necessitates complex, task-specific training pipelines. The motivation is to find a more scalable and data-efficient alternative.

3. **Leveraging Implicit Priors in Video Generation Models:** The central hypothesis is that large-scale video generation models, in their process of synthesizing temporally coherent and plausible videos, inherently learn a rich understanding of 3D structural priors and physical laws. To create videos where objects maintain identity during occlusion, camera motion reveals depth, and interactions follow consistent dynamics, these models must encode geometry-consistent structure and motion within their latent representations. The authors observe, for instance, that video diffusion models exhibit remarkable multi-view consistency, indicating a robust internal representation of 3D geometry (as evidenced in Figure 2a).

4. **Repurposing Generative Models as Latent World Simulators:** The research is motivated by the compelling question: if video generators already possess an implicit understanding of space and physics, can these implicit physical priors be repurposed to improve downstream 3D visual understanding? The authors demonstrate that incorporating these generative priors can sharpen the scattered attention of baseline MLLMs, enabling more precise localization for fine-grained 3D reasoning (Figure 2b).

5. **Developing a Plug-and-Play Framework:** A key motivation is to introduce a modular and scalable solution. The proposed framework, VEGA-3D, is designed to be plug-and-play, allowing for easy integration with existing MLLMs without requiring modifications to their core architecture or extensive re-training. This design aims to make the benefits of generative priors accessible and transferable, ensuring that advances in video generation models can directly lead to stronger 3D understanding without additional annotation costs.

In essence, the work seeks a paradigm shift: instead of explicitly training for 3D understanding with specific 3D data or supervision, it proposes to "unleash" the geometric and physical knowledge already implicitly encoded within large-scale video generation models, thereby providing MLLMs with a foundational, physically consistent world model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, VEGA-3D (Video Extracted Generative Awareness), is a plug-and-play system designed to integrate implicit 3D priors from pre-trained video generation models into MLLMs. It operates through a dual-branch visual encoding mechanism and involves three main stages: 3D Awareness Analysis, Latent World Simulation, and Bridging the Generative and Semantic Gap.

#### 3.1. 3D Awareness Analysis via Multi-view Feature Consistency

To confirm the presence and utility of implicit 3D priors, the authors first establish a quantitative measure of geometric capability.
- **Hypothesis:** Robust 3D scene understanding correlates with multi-view feature consistency, meaning the same physical 3D point maps to a unified latent representation across different camera viewpoints.
- **Metric:** The **Multi-view Correspondence Score** is introduced. This metric involves projecting encoder features from multiple posed RGB frames (e.g., from ScanNet) into a shared global voxel grid using ground-truth camera extrinsics and depth. For any voxel observed in two different views, the cosine similarity between its corresponding feature vectors is computed. The scene-level score is an average over all valid voxel pairs. A higher score indicates better implicit alignment of 3D structure.
- **Correlation Analysis:** This score is correlated with a "Normalized Overall Score" (NOS) derived from downstream 3D understanding benchmarks. A strong positive correlation is observed (Figure 3b), validating multi-view consistency as a reliable predictor of 3D performance.
- **Architectural Selection:** The analysis reveals that DiT (Diffusion Transformer)-based models (e.g., Wan2.1) exhibit significantly higher multi-view consistency (e.g., >96%) compared to UNet-based models or traditional discriminative models. This is attributed to DiT's global attention mechanisms, which capture holistic context and long-range geometric alignment more effectively than local convolutions. Based on this, DiT-based architectures are selected for providing robust spatial priors.

#### 3.2. Video Generative Model as a Latent World Simulator

This stage details how implicit 3D knowledge is extracted from a frozen, pre-trained video generation model.
- **Generative Encoder Selection:** Wan2.1-T2V 1.3B is chosen as the default Latent World Simulator due to its text-conditioning interface and strong localization performance. The framework is also shown to be compatible with other video generative backbones.
- **Latent Space Conversion:** An input video sequence $V \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times H \times W \times 3}$ is initially mapped to a low-dimensional latent space $z_0 = E(V)$ using the model's Variational Autoencoder (VAE).
- **Noise Injection:** To activate the generative model's reasoning capabilities, the clean latent $z_0$ is perturbed along a Flow Matching noising path. Gaussian noise $\epsilon \sim N(0, I)$ is sampled, and a noisy latent $z_k = (1 - t_k) z_0 + t_k \epsilon$ is constructed, where $t_k$ is a normalized discrete timestep. The rationale is that the model's understanding of structure is revealed during the active denoising process.
- **Feature Extraction:** The noisy latent $z_k$ is fed into the frozen backbone $\Phi(\cdot)$ with an empty text prompt ($c_{\text{text}} = ""$). This ensures that the extracted features primarily reflect the visual signal and the model's learned physics, minimizing semantic hallucination from textual conditioning. Features are extracted from a specific intermediate DiT layer $l$ (empirically chosen as the 20th layer), as these layers offer an optimal balance between spatial precision and abstract spatiotemporal context.
- **Feature Shaping:** The raw features $f_{\text{raw}}$ are then subjected to Adaptive Average Pooling to match the tokenization of the semantic stream, yielding generative representations $f_{\text{gen}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times N \times D_{\text{gen}}}$.

#### 3.3. Bridging the Generative and Semantic Gap: Adaptive Gated Fusion

The generative features ($f_{\text{gen}}$) and semantic features ($f_{\text{sem}}$) originate from different manifolds. This stage focuses on effectively integrating them.
- **Projection:** Both generative ($f_{\text{gen}}$) and semantic ($f_{\text{sem}}$) features are projected into the LLM's shared hidden dimension $D_{\text{llm}}$ using independent MLP projectors $P_{\text{gen}}$ and $P_{\text{sem}}$, resulting in $F_{\text{gen}}$ and $F_{\text{sem}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times N \times D_{\text{llm}}}$.
- **Adaptive Gated Fusion:** A token-level gating mechanism is employed to dynamically weigh the contributions of semantic and structural cues for each spatial token. For the $i$-th token, a scalar gate $g_i \in$ is computed:
 $g_i = \sigma \left( W_g^\top \text{Concat}(\text{LN}(F_{\text{gen},i}), \text{LN}(F_{\text{sem},i})) + b_g \right)$, where $\sigma$ is the sigmoid function, LN is Layer Normalization, and $W_g$ and $b_g$ are learnable parameters.
- **Feature Fusion:** The final fused representation $F_{\text{fused},i}$ is a convex combination:
 $F_{\text{fused},i} = (1 - g_i) \cdot F_{\text{gen},i} + g_i \cdot F_{\text{sem},i}$.
 This gate acts as a semantic-geometric arbitrator, allowing the model to dynamically prioritize semantic priors for recognition or generative world knowledge for spatial reasoning tasks.

**Implementation Details:**
- Experiments use 32 frames per scan.
- Flow-Matching time interval $t \in$ is discretized into $K=1000$ steps; features are extracted at $k=300$ ($t_k=0.3$) from the 20th DiT layer by default.
- Voxel size of 0.1 is used for correspondence score calculation.
- Optimized with Adam, batch size 128, warm-up ratio 0.03. Learning rates for LLM and visual backbone are $1 \times 10^{-5}$ and $2 \times 10^{-6}$, respectively.
- Experiments are conducted on 8 H100 NVIDIA GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates VEGA-3D across three main axes: 3D scene understanding, spatial reasoning, and embodied manipulation, consistently demonstrating its effectiveness.

#### 4.1. 3D Scene Understanding Benchmarks (Table 1)

- **Consistent Improvements:** VEGA-3D consistently improves performance over the Video-3D LLM baseline across most metrics on five 3D scene understanding benchmarks (ScanRefer, Multi3DRefer, Scan2Cap, ScanQA, SQA3D).
- **Localization-Centric Tasks:** Significant gains are observed in tasks requiring precise localization, such as ScanRefer (Acc@0.5 boosted from 51.7 to 56.2) and SQA3D (EM from 58.6 to 61.3). This suggests that the generative priors effectively provide a robust spatial anchor, mitigating the spatial ambiguity in standard MLLMs.
- **Semantic-Geometry Trade-off:** A slight drop in CIDEr score for Scan2Cap is noted, indicating a potential trade-off where emphasizing structural cues might subtly weaken fine-grained lexical details. However, the Adaptive Gated Fusion mechanism aims to balance this.
- **Competitiveness with Explicit 3D Supervision:** VEGA-3D achieves an overall average rank of 2.2, which is highly competitive with state-of-the-art methods that rely on extensive explicit 3D supervision (e.g., 3DRS, Ross3D which uses BEV reconstruction). This finding suggests that label-free implicit generative priors can be as effective as explicit geometric supervision for localization-centric understanding tasks.

#### 4.2. Generalization to Spatial Reasoning (VSI-Bench, Table 2)

- **Enhanced Spatial Skills:** When augmenting the Qwen2.5VL-7B baseline, VEGA-3D shows consistent gains across the VSI-Bench, a comprehensive benchmark for visual-spatial reasoning from videos. Improvements are seen in the overall average and specific sub-categories like relative distance and route planning.
- **Lightweight and Plug-and-Play:** These improvements are achieved with a lightweight, plug-and-play approach, reinforcing the idea that generative priors can provide robust spatial grounding for MLLMs without complex specialized training.

#### 4.3. Generalization to Robotic Manipulation (LIBERO, Table 3)

- **Improved Action Grounding:** VEGA-3D enhances the performance of the OpenVLA-OFT Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model on the LIBERO robotic manipulation suite. Clear gains are observed in object-centric and long-horizon tasks, which demand precise contact localization and persistent spatial memory. This indicates that the spatial regularities embedded in the Latent World Simulator transfer to action grounding, even without explicit action-conditioned training.
- **Real-Robot Transfer:** Further validation is provided by real-robot manipulation experiments on Agilex Piper arms, where VEGA-3D improves the success rate on a "Click Bell" task from 40% to 55%.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Generative vs. Discriminative Priors (Table 4, Figure 3b):** The study confirms a strong positive correlation between multi-view consistency and 3D scene understanding performance. DiT-based generative models significantly outperform UNet-based models and discriminative models (e.g., DINO V3, V-JEPA v2) in capturing robust spatial priors due to their global attention mechanisms.
- **Dynamics of Internal Representations (Noise Levels and Layers, Figure 6):**
 - **Noise Ratio ($t_k$):** Performance peaks at intermediate noise levels (e.g., $t_k=0.3$). Clean latents underutilize denoising, while excessive noise degrades structural signals. Moderate noise optimally activates the model's learned physics to restore 3D structures.
 - **Layer Selection:** Intermediate DiT layers (e.g., layer 20) provide the optimal abstraction, balancing low-level textures from early layers and pixel-level rendering from deeper layers.
- **Effectiveness of Adaptive Gated Fusion (Table 5):**
 - Relying solely on generative features results in a substantial performance drop, underscoring that generative priors are complementary to semantic representations, not replacements.
 - Adaptive Gated Fusion outperforms naive fusion methods (ee.g., Add, Channel Concat+MLP, Sequence Concat, Cross-Attention variants) on most metrics, demonstrating its ability to dynamically balance semantic and geometric priors, thus providing more consistent gains across diverse tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces VEGA-3D, representing a significant contribution to 3D scene understanding, spatial reasoning, and embodied AI. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Paradigm Shift in 3D Understanding:** VEGA-3D proposes a new approach to imbue MLLMs with 3D awareness by leveraging implicit spatial priors from pre-trained video generation models. This shifts away from the prevailing reliance on explicit 3D modalities, specialized 3D annotations, or complex geometric scaffolding. This change in paradigm could alleviate the challenges associated with data scarcity and annotation costs inherent in 3D-specific datasets.

2. **Scalability and Data Efficiency:** By utilizing frozen, large-scale video generation models as "Latent World Simulators," the framework benefits directly from advances in video generation. This means that improvements in video synthesis capabilities can translate into stronger 3D priors for downstream tasks without requiring additional 3D-specific annotations or re-training of the generative model. This offers a highly scalable and data-efficient pathway for enhancing MLLMs.

3. **Enhanced Spatial Reasoning and Localization:** The consistent performance gains observed across 3D scene understanding benchmarks, particularly in localization-centric tasks (e.g., ScanRefer, SQA3D), demonstrate VEGA-3D's effectiveness in mitigating MLLM's "spatial blindness." The framework provides dense geometric cues that serve as robust spatial anchors, enabling more precise object grounding and understanding of spatial relationships.

4. **Broad Generalization:** The work demonstrates the versatility of generative priors by showing consistent improvements not only in passive 3D scene understanding but also in active domains like spatial reasoning (VSI-Bench) and robotic manipulation (LIBERO, and even real-robot tasks). This suggests that the physically consistent world model learned by video generators is transferable across a wide array of AI applications that require a deep understanding of the physical world.

5. **Modularity and Plug-and-Play Design:** VEGA-3D's plug-and-play architecture allows it to augment existing MLLMs and VLA models without requiring changes to their core language or action backbones. This modularity simplifies integration and facilitates the adoption of generative priors into diverse systems.

6. **Towards World-Action Models (WAMs):** The success in robotic manipulation tasks hints at a future where frozen video generators could serve as foundational World Models, directly grounding action policies. This could lead to more robust and generalizable robotic agents by providing them with an inherent understanding of physical dynamics and space.

While acknowledging an increased inference cost due to the additional video diffusion backbone (Figure 7), the authors argue that the substantial and consistent performance gains justify this trade-off. Future work could focus on distilling these priors into more lightweight encoders to address this limitation, extending the framework to more dynamic scene understanding, and scaling the plug-and-play generative prior to even larger Vision-Language-Action models for closed-loop embodied control.
