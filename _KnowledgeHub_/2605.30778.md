# Object-Informed Model Predictive Path Integral Control for Non-Prehensile Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of California San Diego developed a hierarchical Model Predictive Path Integral control framework for non-prehensile manipulation, which decouples object trajectory planning from robot control. This approach demonstrated increased task success rates by up to 40% in simulation and 20% on hardware, and reduced execution times, for complex object pushing tasks in cluttered environments.

## Problem

- Non-prehensile manipulation tasks face challenges from discontinuous contact dynamics, where small robot movements can cause abrupt, non-smooth changes in object behavior.
- The target object's motion is implicitly actuated through robot contact, requiring planners to simultaneously account for robot kinematics, contact geometry, and object response, leading to a large and complex search space.
- Standard sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) often struggles with long-horizon non-prehensile tasks due to their short-sighted planning and inefficient exploration in complex environments.

## Method

- The authors introduce a hierarchical decomposition of ==Model Predictive Path Integral== (MPPI) control, splitting the problem into an 'object-level' and a 'robot-level' planning task.
- The object-level planner optimizes a long-horizon object trajectory by treating external interaction forces as direct control inputs, focusing on object goals and obstacle avoidance.
- The robot-level planner then tracks this object reference trajectory over a shorter horizon by computing robot control inputs that ensure physically consistent robot-object interactions and manage robot-environment contacts.

## Results

- Object-informed MPPI methods (SOI **82%** SR, CLOI **72%** SR) substantially outperformed standard MPPI (**42%** SR) in simulation tasks, achieving higher success rates for long-horizon non-prehensile manipulation.
- In hardware experiments, both SOI and CLOI reached a **90%** success rate, exceeding standard MPPI's **70%**, despite operating with real-world perception noise and actuation limits.
- Object-informed approaches generally reduced average execution times (e.g., SOI was **22%** faster than standard MPPI in simulation) and often achieved higher control frequencies, indicating improved computational efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing non-prehensile manipulation into object and robot planning problems simplifies the overall control task, enabling more effective long-horizon reasoning for the object.
> - Guiding the robot's low-level actions with an object-level reference trajectory improves the sample efficiency of MPPI, leading to more reliable and goal-directed robot behaviors.
> - The hierarchical approach helps overcome the inherent difficulties of implicitly actuated systems with discontinuous contact, allowing robots to navigate complex environments more effectively.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Non-prehensile manipulation, which involves interactions like pushing rather than stable grasping, represents a complex and challenging domain within robotics. This field aims to expand the capabilities of robot systems, particularly in scenarios where grasping is not feasible or advantageous. The research landscape for non-prehensile manipulation is characterized by several inherent difficulties:

- **Discontinuous Contact Dynamics:** Interactions between robots and objects often involve making and breaking contact, leading to discontinuous dynamics. Small variations in robot motion can result in qualitatively different object behaviors, rendering large portions of the robot's configuration space ineffective for task progression.
- **Underactuation and Implicit Actuation:** The object is not directly actuated but its motion arises implicitly through contact with the robot. This necessitates that the robot's planner simultaneously considers robot kinematics, contact geometry, and the object's response, significantly expanding the search space for control actions.

Previous research has explored various approaches to address these challenges. One category involves analytical models of contact dynamics, often utilizing complementarity constraints or second-order cone programs (SOCP). Methods in this area focus on optimizing through these models by forming local approximations, smoothing formulations, or constructing explicit solutions. While physically grounded, these approaches can require carefully designed, differentiable models, which may be difficult to obtain for arbitrary objects.

Another significant stream of research utilizes sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) algorithms, such as the Cross Entropy Method (CEM), Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control, and Diffusion-Inspired Annealing (DIAL) MPC. These methods leverage fast, parallelizable multi-body physics simulators to achieve real-time control without requiring gradient information from the dynamics. They iteratively update a distribution over control sequences based on the cumulative cost of online interactions within the simulation environment.

The current work by Raicevic et al. builds upon the success of sampling-based MPC, particularly MPPI, to handle complex contact dynamics via parallel forward simulation. It introduces a hierarchical structure to MPPI, drawing inspiration from prior approaches that impose structure for non-prehensile manipulation. Related structured approaches include:
- **Consensus Complementarity Control (C3):** This method applies ADMM to decompose non-prehensile manipulation into iterative steps, one without complementarity constraints and another projecting the solution to satisfy them.
- **Explicit Contact Mode Choice:** Other works have shown benefits in performance and computational efficiency by explicitly choosing contact modes.
- **Object-Centric Planning:** Approaches like those in and first plan an object-only trajectory to reach a goal and then solve for robot motion to achieve that trajectory.

This paper positions itself within this object-centric planning paradigm, extending it by incorporating receding horizon object planning and providing a clear mathematical formulation for the decomposition between robot-level and object-level planning problems based on a general dynamic model. The proposed hierarchical MPPI aims to address the long-horizon planning difficulties by breaking down the complex problem into more manageable subproblems, thereby potentially improving planning efficiency and task success in cluttered environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient control strategy for long-horizon non-prehensile robot manipulation tasks. The authors identify two main challenges that motivate their approach:

1. **Discontinuous Contact Dynamics:** The non-smooth nature of contact events means that minor changes in robot motion can lead to significant, discontinuous changes in object behavior. This makes it difficult for standard planners to effectively search the robot's configuration space, as many actions may not contribute meaningfully to task progress or might lead to undesirable outcomes.
2. **Implicit Actuation and Large Search Space:** The target object in non-prehensile manipulation is not directly controlled by the robot's actuators. Instead, the robot must indirectly influence the object's motion through contact. This implicit actuation significantly expands the planning search space, as the controller must simultaneously account for robot kinematics, contact geometry, and the resulting object response. This complexity makes long-horizon planning computationally intensive and prone to local minima for standard methods.

To address these challenges, the authors propose a hierarchical formulation of Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control. The core motivation behind this decomposition is to simplify the planning problem by breaking it into two coupled optimal control problems operating at different levels of abstraction:

- **Object-Level Planning:** This subproblem focuses solely on the object's trajectory. By assuming the object can be directly actuated through virtual control inputs (e.g., interaction forces), the planner can reason about the object's motion in its own, smaller configuration space over a longer horizon. This simplifies the task of finding a feasible and desired path for the object to reach its goal while avoiding environmental obstacles.
- **Robot-Level Planning:** This subproblem focuses on the robot's actions. Given a desired object trajectory generated by the object-level planner, the robot-level planner's task becomes one of tracking this reference. This reduces the effective planning horizon for the robot, as it no longer needs to simultaneously discover a global object path and its own low-level movements. Instead, its objective is to generate physically consistent robot-object interactions to follow the pre-computed object plan.

By separating these concerns, the hierarchical approach aims to:
- Reduce the complexity of the robot's control problem.
- Enable longer-horizon reasoning at the object level, which is crucial for tasks requiring non-myopic obstacle avoidance and strategic object movements.
- Guide the robot-level search towards more promising regions of the control space, thus improving efficiency and success rates compared to a direct, joint robot-object planning approach.

The paper introduces two specific variants of this hierarchical MPPI: Sequential Object-Informed (SOI) MPPI, which performs object planning once offline, and Closed-Loop Object-Informed (CLOI) MPPI, which re-computes the object trajectory in a receding horizon, providing online adaptability.

The key contributions summarized by the authors are:
- The introduction of CLOI and SOI, two hierarchical MPPI variants that decompose non-prehensile manipulation into object-level and robot-level planning problems.
- Demonstrating, through simulated and real experiments with a 6-DoF robotic manipulator, that these object-informed MPPI algorithms improve success rates (by 40% in simulation and 20% on hardware) compared to standard MPPI for long-horizon non-prehensile manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on a hierarchical decomposition of the non-prehensile manipulation problem, solved using Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control.

**Problem Statement:**
The system comprises a robot manipulator and a rigid target object. The robot dynamics are modeled as a discrete-time input-affine system: $x^r_{t+1} = f^r(x^r_t) + g^r(x^r_t) u^r_t + h^r(x^r_t) w^r_t$, where $x^r_t$ is the robot's configuration, $u^r_t$ is the control input, and $w^r_t$ represents external forces. The object dynamics are similarly modeled as $x^o_{t+1} = f^o(x^o_t) + h^o(x^o_t) w^o_t$, where $x^o_t$ is the object's configuration and $w^o_t$ are external forces. The coupling between robot and object occurs through these external force terms, which are defined by contact interactions and complementarity constraints (e.g., non-penetration and contact force conditions). The environment, including obstacles, is treated as static. The overall goal is to find a sequence of robot inputs to move the object to a target configuration while avoiding environment contacts, formulated as a finite-horizon optimal control problem minimizing a sum of object costs, robot costs, and a terminal object cost. The authors assume access to a physics simulator that implements these dynamics.

**Background on MPPI:**
MPPI is a derivative-free optimal control method. It maintains a nominal control sequence and, at each iteration, generates multiple perturbed control sequences. These sequences are rolled out through the system dynamics to obtain trajectories and associated costs. An improved nominal control sequence is then computed as a weighted average of the perturbed sequences, with weights determined by their costs (lower cost sequences receive higher weights). This process is executed in a receding horizon manner.

**Hierarchical Decomposition:**
The paper exploits the separable nature of robot and object costs and the coupling through external forces to decompose the problem into two subproblems:

1. **Object-Level Subproblem:** This problem optimizes the object's trajectory by treating the external interaction force ($w^o_t$) as a direct control input. The objective for this subproblem focuses on minimizing object deviation from the goal and penalizing object-environment contact. This subproblem is solved over an object-level horizon $H_o$.
 $\min_{\{w^o_t\}} \sum_{t=0}^{H_o-1} \ell^o(x^o_t) + \ell_f(x^o_{H_o})$
 subject to object dynamics and non-penetration/complementarity constraints with the environment.

2. **Robot-Level Subproblem:** This problem determines the robot's control inputs ($u^r_t$) to achieve the object trajectory generated by the object-level planner. The objective for this subproblem includes minimizing object deviation from its reference trajectory ($x^{o*}_{t}$), regularizing robot motion and effort, and avoiding robot-environment contact. This subproblem is solved over a robot-level horizon $H_r$.
 $\min_{\{u^r_t\}} \sum_{t=0}^{H_r-1} (\ell^o(x^o_t) + \ell^r(x^r_t) + \ell_c(x^o_t, x^{o*}_t)) + \ell_f(x^o_{H_r})$
 subject to joint robot-object dynamics and robot feasibility constraints. Here, $\ell_c$ is a coupling stage cost that measures the squared distance between the current object position and the object reference.

**Object-Informed MPPI Control Variants:**
The paper proposes two variants for integrating these hierarchical problems:

1. **Closed-Loop Object-Informed (CLOI) MPPI:** In CLOI, the object-level MPPI is re-executed at each real-time step $t$ using the current observed object state ($x^o_t$). This generates an updated object reference trajectory ($x^{o*}_{t:t+H_o}$). This updated reference is then fed to the robot-level MPPI, which computes and executes a robot control action. The observed next state ($x_{t+1}$) is then fed back to the object planner for the subsequent re-planning step, creating a closed-loop system robust to deviations.

2. **Sequential Object-Informed (SOI) MPPI:** In SOI, the object-level MPPI is executed once offline (or at the beginning of an episode) to generate a complete, global object reference trajectory ($x^{o*}_{0:H_o}$) that guides the object to the goal. During robot execution, the robot-level MPPI operates in a receding horizon fashion, tracking segments of this pre-computed, static object reference ($x^{o*}_{t:t+H_r}$). This variant decouples planning and execution in time, but its performance is dependent on the initial object plan's validity under actual dynamics and disturbances.

**Cost Functions:**
Detailed cost functions are defined for both levels:
- **Object-level cost ($\ell^o(x^o_t)$):**
 - Weighted squared distance to the goal position and orientation.
 - Hinge loss penalizing object-environment contact forces ($\lambda^2_t$) above a threshold ($f_0$), tolerating minor incidental contacts.
- **Robot-level cost ($\ell^r(x^r_t)$):**
 - Hinge loss penalizing distance between the end-effector and object beyond a radius ($r_0$), encouraging initial approach but allowing freedom once close.
 - Alignment cost ($\psi_{align}$): Encourages the end-effector to be "behind" the object relative to the goal direction for pushing, using the cosine angle between vectors.
 - Tilt cost ($\psi_{tilt}$): Penalizes deviations from a vertical orientation of the end-effector (squared sum of roll and pitch angles), encouraging a stable pushing pose.

**Experimental Setup:**
Experiments were conducted in both simulation and hardware using a 6-DoF xArm6 manipulator.
- **Simulation:** Five tasks were designed, involving pushing T-blocks and letters (e.g., "S" to form "IROS") through environments with varying complexities and obstacles. Each method (standard MPPI, CLOI, SOI) was run for ten trials with different starting object poses.
- **Hardware:** Two tasks were performed: object pushing with and without an obstacle. Five start/goal locations were considered. Object pose estimation was performed using SAM-6D with an Intel RealSense D-455 camera, noting an estimation frequency of 1 Hz and occasional errors/occlusions. A Tomato Soup Can from the YCB dataset was the target object.
- **Metrics:** Success rate (SR), position error (averaged over all trials $\epsilon_d$, and over successful trials $\epsilon^s_d$), average control frequency ($\bar{f}$), and total execution time (T) were reported. For SOI, pre-planning time was included in the total execution time.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments compared the performance of standard MPPI with the proposed CLOI and SOI methods across various simulation and hardware tasks.

**Simulation Experiments (Table I):**
- **Success Rate (SR):** SOI achieved the highest average success rate at 82%, followed by CLOI at 72%. Standard MPPI achieved a significantly lower success rate of 42%. This difference was particularly pronounced in harder tasks (Tasks 3 and 4), where MPPI frequently failed to find feasible paths around obstacles.
- **Position Error ($\epsilon_d$, $\epsilon^s_d$):** CLOI and SOI generally resulted in lower average position errors, both overall ($\epsilon_d \approx 0.32$m for both) and for successful trials ($\epsilon^s_d \approx 0.29$m for both), compared to MPPI ($\epsilon_d = 0.42$m, $\epsilon^s_d = 0.32$m). This was attributed to the object-informed methods' more reliable obstacle avoidance.
- **Execution Time (T):** Both CLOI (194.77s) and SOI (182.81s, including 100.68s pre-planning) demonstrated lower average execution times compared to standard MPPI (235.53s).
- **Control Frequency ($\bar{f}$):** CLOI exhibited the highest average control frequency (3.01 Hz), while SOI (2.37 Hz) and MPPI (2.38 Hz) were comparable. The faster frequency of CLOI suggests efficiency gains in online replanning.

**Hardware Experiments (Table II):**
- **Success Rate (SR):** CLOI and SOI both achieved a 90% average success rate, outperforming standard MPPI at 70%. This consistent improvement across hardware tasks suggests the hierarchical guidance is beneficial even with real-world perception and actuation limitations.
- **Position Error ($\epsilon_d$, $\epsilon^s_d$):** In hardware, standard MPPI attained the lowest position error for successful trials ($\epsilon^s_d = 0.25$m) and overall ($\epsilon_d = 0.31$m). CLOI ($\epsilon^s_d = 0.34$m, $\epsilon_d = 0.35$m) and SOI ($\epsilon^s_d = 0.32$m, $\epsilon_d = 0.34$m) had slightly higher errors. The authors attribute MPPI's lower error to its direct tracking of the goal without intermediate waypoints in simpler hardware environments, while still acknowledging that in more cluttered settings (like in simulation), the reference trajectories of CLOI and SOI are more effective for obstacle avoidance.
- **Execution Time (T):** SOI yielded the lowest overall execution time (155.23s, including 16.65s pre-planning), followed by CLOI (171.68s) and MPPI (173.95s). This indicates that the reduced online planning burden of SOI translates to faster task completion in real-world scenarios.
- **Qualitative Observations (Figs. 4 and 5):** Standard MPPI frequently drove directly towards the goal and stalled at obstacle boundaries due to its short-horizon objective. CLOI and SOI generally adopted obstacle-avoidant paths consistent with their object-level plans. However, a failure mode for CLOI and SOI was observed when the planned object reference trajectories provided insufficient clearance for the real manipulator, particularly near obstacles, due to perception errors and actuation limits. In such cases, the local collision-avoidance cost dominated, preventing further progress.

In summary, the object-informed hierarchical MPPI methods, particularly SOI and CLOI, demonstrated substantial improvements in task success rate and often in execution time across both simulated and real-world non-prehensile manipulation tasks compared to standard MPPI. The object-level guidance mechanism was effective in inducing non-myopic, obstacle-avoidant behaviors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces an object-informed, hierarchical formulation of Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control, which represents a contribution to the field of non-prehensile robot manipulation. The significance of this work lies in its approach to addressing the inherent challenges of long-horizon planning in dynamic and contact-rich environments.

### 6. Significance

- **Improved Long-Horizon Planning:** The hierarchical decomposition effectively tackles the combinatorial complexity and short-sightedness often associated with planning in implicitly actuated, underactuated systems with discontinuous dynamics. By separating the object's task-space trajectory planning from the robot's low-level control, the method enables longer-horizon reasoning at the object level, which is critical for tasks requiring complex detours or multi-step interactions.
- **Enhanced Reliability and Success Rates:** The empirical results demonstrate a substantial increase in task success rates, both in high-fidelity simulation and on real hardware, compared to a standard MPPI implementation. This suggests that providing object-level guidance helps bias the robot's exploration towards more promising and feasible control sequences.
- **Efficiency Gains:** The approach shows improvements in execution time, particularly with SOI, by reducing the online planning burden for the robot. CLOI also achieves a higher control frequency in simulation, indicating more efficient computation for online replanning.
- **Robustness to Real-World Imperfections:** Despite operating with imperfect object pose estimation in hardware experiments, the object-informed methods maintained high success rates, suggesting a degree of robustness to real-world sensing and actuation inaccuracies.

**Potential Impact:**
The proposed object-informed MPPI could have several impacts on robotic applications:
- **Expanded Robotic Capabilities:** By making long-horizon non-prehensile manipulation more reliable and efficient, this work could enable robots to perform more complex tasks in unstructured or cluttered environments that are currently challenging for prehensile methods. Examples include clearing debris, organizing items, or performing assembly tasks without precise grasps.
- **Foundation for Future Control Systems:** The hierarchical structure provides a modular framework for integrating higher-level task planning with lower-level dynamic control. This could serve as a basis for developing more sophisticated, hybrid control systems for complex manipulation scenarios.
- **Advancements in Sample-Efficient Planning:** By guiding the MPPI rollouts, the method implicitly improves sample efficiency by directing the search towards relevant parts of the control space, which is valuable for learning and control in real systems.

**Identified Limitations and Future Work:**
The paper acknowledges two primary limitations:
1. **Model Mismatch:** Discrepancies between the simplified object model used for planning and the actual robot-object interaction dynamics can lead to dynamically infeasible object reference trajectories for the robot to execute.
2. **Computational Overhead:** Object-level planning, while beneficial, adds to the total computational requirement by necessitating an increased number of parallel simulations.

Future work aims to address these limitations by incorporating uncertainty-aware guidance during object-level planning and developing more contact-aware object models. Specifically, explicitly accounting for feasible contact wrenches and randomizing problem parameters (e.g., object friction or pose) during rollouts are proposed to enhance robustness and realism.

Overall, this research provides a practical and effective method for improving non-prehensile manipulation capabilities, with clear pathways for future development to further enhance its robustness and applicability in diverse robotic scenarios.
