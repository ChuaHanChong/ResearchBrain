# Beyond Rigid: Benchmarking Non-Rigid Video Editing

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Harbin Institute of Technology introduce NRVBench, the first comprehensive benchmark for non-rigid video editing, featuring 180 physics-grounded videos and 2,340 fine-grained instructions. They also propose NRVE-Acc, a VLM-based metric that evaluates physical plausibility and temporal consistency, and VM-Edit, a training-free baseline achieving superior background preservation (lowest Structure Distance 8.69 x 10^3) and competitive adherence scores.

## Problem

- Existing text-driven video editing models primarily focus on rigid transformations and struggle with non-rigid deformations, leading to physically implausible distortions and temporal flickering.
- A scarcity of suitable benchmarks designed for non-rigid motion editing exists, with current benchmarks lacking targeted protocols and metrics for assessing physical adherence and temporal consistency.
- The absence of a dedicated benchmark hinders fair comparisons, systematic progress, and the identification of specific weaknesses in non-rigid video editing techniques.

## Method

- NRVBench: A comprehensive benchmark dataset consisting of 180 high-quality videos taxonomized into six physics-grounded categories, annotated with 2,340 fine-grained instructions, multiple-choice questions (MCQs), and pixel-accurate masks.
- NRVE-Acc: A novel, VLM-based evaluation metric that quantifies instruction alignment, physical plausibility (via category-specific criteria), and temporal consistency (via optical-flow visualization) through a weighted geometric mean.
- VM-Edit: A training-free, structure-aware baseline model utilizing a mask-guided dual-region ==denoising== strategy with a 'dual-clock' mechanism to balance foreground plasticity and background preservation during non-rigid edits.

## Results

- VM-Edit demonstrated superior background preservation (lowest Structure Distance of 8.69 x 10^3 and highest PSNR of 35.79 dB) and the best perceptual quality (NIQE score of 7.36) on NRVBench compared to baselines.
- On the NRVE-Acc metric, VM-Edit secured the second-best overall score (34.71%), demonstrating competitive performance across its sub-components: physics plausibility (71.44%), temporal consistency (49.44%), and instruction alignment (68.89%).
- Human evaluation confirmed a high degree of quantitative agreement and perceptual alignment with NRVE-Acc's VLM-based assessments, validating the metric's reliability for evaluating non-rigid video edits.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Existing video editing methods, including state-of-the-art inversion-based and inversion-free techniques, exhibit substantial limitations in coherently handling non-rigid motion, frequently introducing physical distortions and temporal flickering.
> - A dedicated, physics-aware evaluation metric like NRVE-Acc, leveraging Vision-Language Models and optical flow, is crucial for accurately assessing the complex dynamics and physical adherence in non-rigid video editing, overcoming the shortcomings of traditional metrics.
> - The dual-clock denoising mechanism in VM-Edit effectively addresses the stability-plasticity dilemma in non-rigid editing, allowing for precise foreground deformation while rigorously preserving background context.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in text-to-video generation and video editing have enabled sophisticated manipulation of visual appearance and, increasingly, the underlying physical dynamics of scenes. This progress in models like diffusion and rectified flow architectures has led to a growing demand for evaluation methodologies that extend beyond traditional metrics of appearance fidelity, semantic alignment, and frame-level temporal smoothness.

Existing video editing benchmarks, such as TGVE, V2VBench, VEditBench, and FiVE, primarily focus on assessing edits at the task, object, or semantic level. While these benchmarks are valuable for evaluating transformations like style transfer, object replacement, or scene changes, they often do not explicitly account for material-specific deformation realism or physical plausibility. Similarly, conventional metrics like CLIP similarity, LPIPS, PSNR, MSE, SSIM, and generic motion scores are effective for measuring visual fidelity, background preservation, and global semantic consistency but are inherently limited in their ability to verify physically plausible non-rigid deformation. Consequently, current models can achieve high scores on these metrics even when producing outputs that are physically implausible, such as rubber-like limbs, ghosting, object popping, texture melting, or topology violations in deformable objects. This discrepancy creates a challenge for reliably measuring progress and comparing methods in scenarios requiring dynamic physical manipulation.

This research addresses a critical gap by proposing a diagnostic benchmark and an evaluation protocol specifically designed for *physics-aware non-rigid video editing*. Non-rigid editing, which involves modifying deformable objects like cloth, liquids, fire, or animal bodies, serves as a uniquely revealing testbed because distinct materials impose distinct physical constraints. The work aims to equip the research community with tools to assess whether edited videos not only follow instructions and preserve unedited regions but also maintain material-specific physical plausibility and temporal coherence, thereby moving evaluation beyond purely visual or semantic criteria.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce a dedicated diagnostic benchmark and a structured evaluation protocol for physics-aware non-rigid video editing. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Bridging the Gap in Evaluation**: As video generation models become capable of manipulating physical dynamics, existing evaluation methods, which largely focus on appearance fidelity and semantic alignment, are becoming insufficient. There is an urgent need for benchmarks that can assess whether edited motions respect material-specific physics, a critical aspect that current metrics overlook.
2. **Addressing Physically Implausible Outputs**: Current video editing methods, despite achieving high scores on conventional metrics, often produce physically implausible artifacts when dealing with deformable objects. These failures include "rubber-like" deformations, ghosting, object popping, texture melting, and topology violations. The lack of diagnostic power in existing benchmarks makes it difficult to identify and rectify these fundamental physical inconsistencies.
3. **Establishing a Stress Test for Physics-Aware Generation**: Non-rigid video editing provides an ideal stress test scenario for physics-aware video generation. The task requires generating deformable motion that adheres to specific instructions, preserves unedited regions, and crucially, maintains material-specific physical plausibility. The diverse constraints of different materials (e.g., a cloth fold versus a liquid surface or a flame) naturally highlight the need for physics-aware evaluation.
4. **Enabling Fair Comparison and Progress Measurement**: The mismatch between conventional metrics and physics-aware perceptual editing success hinders fair comparison of methods and reliable measurement of progress. A method might preserve the background and align with text prompts but still exhibit physically implausible or temporally incoherent motion in the edited region. A dedicated benchmark is required to provide a standardized, diagnostic framework for evaluating such nuances.
5. **Proposing a Structured Diagnostic Protocol**: Existing VLM-based evaluators, while more flexible, often yield a single global score that lacks diagnostic interpretability. The motivation is to develop a structured protocol that decomposes editing success into interpretable components: instruction following, material-aware deformation plausibility, and temporal coherence. This allows for a more granular understanding of model performance and failure modes.
6. **Exploring Promising Directions**: Beyond just evaluation, the work is motivated to introduce a simple, region-conditioned editing baseline (VM-Edit) to explore methods that can effectively manage the stability–plasticity trade-off in non-rigid editing, potentially pointing towards improved controllability and physical plausibility in future models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces **NRVBench**, a diagnostic benchmark, and **NRVE-Acc**, a structured VLM-based evaluation protocol, along with **VM-Edit**, a region-conditioned editing baseline.

#### 3.1. NRVBench: Diagnostic Benchmark for Non-Rigid Video Editing

NRVBench is designed to evaluate physics-aware non-rigid video editing, focusing on deformable motion, preservation of unedited regions, and material-specific physical plausibility.

- **Benchmark Composition**:
 - **Core Dataset**: Comprises 180 curated videos, each 60 frames long, spanning six physics-grounded non-rigid categories:
 - Articulated Soft Bodies (ASB): Animals, humans, plush objects with articulation and soft-tissue deformation.
 - Cloth and Thin Shells (CTS): Curtains, flags, clothing, paper-like surfaces.
 - Deformable Solid Objects (DSO): Dough, clay, rubber, sponges.
 - Gas, Smoke, and Fire (GSF): Clouds, smoke plumes, flames, with evolving topology.
 - Hair, Fur, and Feather (HFF): Fine fibrous structures.
 - Liquid Free Surfaces (LFS): Water surfaces, ripples, waves, splashes.
 - **Annotations**: Each video is enriched with:
 - Fine-grained editing instructions.
 - Source and target prompts.
 - Diagnostic multiple-choice questions (360 total).
 - Negative prompts.
 - Pixel-accurate editing masks.
 - This results in 2,340 prompt-instruction pairs.
 - **Long-Video Subset**: An additional subset of 150-frame videos for analyzing performance under extended temporal evolution, organized into three difficulty levels: L1 (motion), L2 (fluid dynamics), and L3 (elasticity).

- **Benchmark Construction Pipeline (Five Stages)**:
 1. **Source Video Curation**: 30 clips per category from DAVIS and Pexels are selected, featuring stationary/near-stationary cameras, dominant deformable subjects, and clearly observable non-rigid motion. Videos are resampled to 60 or 150 frames.
 2. **Difficulty Design**: Editing tasks are categorized into three levels:
 - L1: Dynamics modulation.
 - L2: Coupled or structural deformation.
 - L3: Topology or material-regime change.
 This provides a diagnostic grid for model behavior analysis.
 3. **Editing Task Authoring**: A human–MLLM collaborative process. MLLMs generate initial captions, human annotators specify desired edits and difficulty, MLLMs propose target prompts, and annotators revise to ensure identity preservation, scene layout, viewpoint, and the requirement of genuine deformation.
 4. **Diagnostic Question Construction**: GPT-4o is employed to generate multiple-choice diagnostic questions for each instance, covering instruction following, material-specific deformation plausibility, and temporal coherence.
 5. **Mask Annotation and Quality Control**: Per-frame editing-region masks are generated using SAM2 and manually verified frame-by-frame. A three-role manual verification workflow (Annotator A, Reviewer B, Lead C) ensures mask quality, category consistency, and unambiguous instruction/QA design, with category-specific rules for challenging materials (e.g., HFF, LFS/GSF).

#### 3.2. NRVE-Acc: Structured VLM-based Evaluation Protocol

NRVE-Acc is designed to overcome the limitations of holistic VLM scores by decomposing evaluation into interpretable dimensions.

- **Evaluation Dimensions**: For an edited video *V*, NRVE-Acc assesses three complementary dimensions, each contributing to a normalized score `_S_k(V) ∈`:
 1. **Instruction Alignment (_S_instr)**: Measures if the video satisfies the requested non-rigid change. A VLM judge answers a multiple-choice question `q_instr` (derived from the target instruction). Scores are 1.0 (correct), 0.5 (partial/ambiguous), or 0.0 (failed).
 2. **Physics Plausibility (_S_phy)**: Evaluates if the generated deformation is consistent with the target material regime. A material-conditioned query `q_phy` (specifying material type and evaluation criteria) is posed to the VLM judge, which scores the mask-guided edited region on a 1-5 scale, normalized to. This focuses on *perceptual diagnostic* of physical plausibility rather than direct simulation.
 3. **Temporal Consistency (_S_temp)**: Assesses the coherence of non-rigid motion over time. The VLM judge is queried on a flow-based motion-evidence image `Φ(V_edit)`, constructed from optical flow between sampled frames (first, middle, last). This makes the metric sensitive to flicker, teleportation, abrupt geometry changes, and discontinuous deformation.
- **Aggregation**: The final NRVE-Acc score is computed as a geometric mean of the normalized sub-scores: `NRVE-Acc(V) = 100 * Product_k(_S_k(V) + epsilon)^(w_k)`, with uniform weights `w_k = 1/3`. The geometric mean ensures that failure in any single dimension significantly reduces the overall score, promoting balanced editing success.
- **Validation**: NRVE-Acc's reliability is validated through extensive experiments using multiple open-source and closed-source VLM judges (Qwen2.5-VL-7B/32B/72B-Instruct, Gemini-2.5 Pro, Kimi-2.5) and comparison with human annotations.

#### 3.3. VM-Edit: Region-Conditioned Editing Baseline

VM-Edit is a training-free, region-conditioned video editing method designed to address the stability–plasticity dilemma by intelligently allocating editing freedom.

- **Approach**: Built upon a pre-trained Image-to-Video (I2V) diffusion transformer (Wan2.2), VM-Edit modifies only the sampling procedure.
- **Inputs**: It takes an appearance anchor (first frame of source video), target textual instruction, source video prior (for latent reference trajectory), and a spatial layout mask sequence.
- **Dual-Clock Region-Conditioned Sampling**:
 - The core idea is to apply different noise schedules to the foreground (edited region) and background (unedited region) during the reverse diffusion process.
 - It uses two timestep indices: `τ_fg` for foreground plasticity and `τ_bg` for background stabilization (`τ_fg > τ_bg`).
 - During `τ_fg ≥ t > τ_bg`, the foreground evolves freely via the diffusion model (allowing non-rigid deformation), while the background is explicitly anchored to the source latent trajectory to suppress temporal flicker.
 - When `t ≤ τ_bg`, both regions are refined jointly to ensure boundary coherence.
- **Adaptive Dual-Clock Scheduler**: This scheduler automatically determines `τ_fg` and `τ_bg` based on:
 - An `edit-demand score` (`d_edit`): Incorporates normalized text distance, foreground motion magnitude, and relative mask area.
 - A `coupling-risk score` (`d_risk`): Measures mask-boundary complexity and background motion magnitude.
 This adaptive mechanism removes the need for manual timestep selection and tailors the sampling schedule to the physical and structural properties of each input.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on NRVBench using NRVE-Acc and conventional metrics yielded several key findings:

- **Mismatch Between Metrics and Perceptual Success**: A significant finding is the clear mismatch between conventional metrics (e.g., PSNR, LPIPS, CLIP scores) and physics-aware perceptual editing success. Methods that achieved high scores on appearance preservation or global semantic alignment frequently failed under non-rigid dynamics, producing visually plausible but physically implausible or temporally incoherent motions (e.g., rubber-like artifacts, ghosting). This validates the core motivation for NRVBench and NRVE-Acc.

- **Limitations of Current Inference-Time Methods**: Representative inference-time video editing methods (TokenFlow, AnyV2V, VidToMe, Pyramid-Edit, Wan-Edit) generally struggled with physics-aware non-rigid editing tasks on NRVBench. Their NRVE-Acc scores were relatively low, indicating a need for models explicitly designed to handle physical dynamics.

- **Performance of VM-Edit**:
 - **Preservation and Perceptual Quality**: VM-Edit demonstrated superior performance in structural and background preservation. On the standard NRVBench, it achieved the best scores for structural distance (8.69), PSNR (35.79), LPIPS (47.89), MSE (4.74), and SSIM (95.72), significantly outperforming other methods. Notably, its NIQE score (7.36) even slightly surpassed the reference source, indicating very high perceptual image quality. This strong preservation capability extended to the long-video subset.
 - **Physics Plausibility (NRVE-Acc Component)**: VM-Edit achieved the highest physics-plausibility component score (S_phy = 71.31) among all methods, suggesting that its region-conditioned approach is more effective at preserving material-level deformation cues and generating physically consistent outputs.
 - **Judge Stability**: VM-Edit exhibited the lowest cross-judge standard deviation in NRVE-Acc scores (3.58), indicating strong robustness and less sensitivity to the specific VLM judge used, making its evaluation results more reliable.
 - **Trade-off**: While VM-Edit showed excellent preservation and physics plausibility, its motion score on the long-video subset was lower than some baselines (43.69 vs. TokenFlow's 62.70). This suggests a trade-off where its conservative approach to preserving source structure and background might sometimes limit the magnitude of apparent motion, even if the motion it generates is more plausible.

- **NRVE-Acc Reliability and Human Agreement**: The human evaluation confirmed the reliability of NRVE-Acc. Rank correlations (Spearman’s ρ and Kendall’s τ) between VLM judges and human annotations showed positive agreement, particularly for larger or closed-source VLMs. The inter-annotator agreement for human evaluation (Fleiss’ κ and Krippendorff’s α) was substantial to strong, validating the consistency of the human assessment protocol.

- **General Challenges in Temporal Coherence**: The temporal consistency component (S_temp) of NRVE-Acc remained consistently low across all methods, including VM-Edit (e.g., Wan-Edit: 44.35, VM-Edit: 42.80). This indicates that maintaining coherent non-rigid motion over time is a significant unresolved challenge for current video editing models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work carries substantial significance and potential impact for the field of video generation and editing:

- **Shifting Evaluation Paradigms**: The introduction of NRVBench and NRVE-Acc represents a critical shift in how video editing models are evaluated. By focusing on *physical plausibility* and *material-specific dynamics* rather than just aesthetic quality or semantic alignment, it pushes the research community towards developing models that can truly manipulate dynamic scenes in a physically coherent manner. This is crucial for applications requiring realistic simulations or precise control over complex deformations.

- **Enhanced Diagnostic Capabilities**: NRVE-Acc's decomposed evaluation into instruction alignment, physics plausibility, and temporal consistency provides fine-grained diagnostic power. Researchers can now identify specific failure modes (e.g., an edit failing due to implausible deformation rather than just incorrect instruction following), which is essential for targeted model improvement and understanding underlying limitations. This moves beyond opaque, single-score evaluations.

- **Benchmarking Current Limitations**: The benchmark results clearly demonstrate that current inference-time video editing methods, even those performing well on conventional metrics, often fall short in generating physically plausible non-rigid motion. This highlights an important area for future research and development, urging the community to treat physical dynamics as a central objective rather than an incidental property.

- **Foundational Resource for Future Research**: NRVBench provides a reproducible and richly annotated dataset that serves as a robust foundation for future studies in physics-aware video editing. Its curated videos, fine-grained instructions, pixel-accurate masks, and diagnostic questions facilitate standardized evaluation and foster comparability across different models. The inclusion of a long-video subset is particularly valuable for assessing temporal robustness under extended physical processes.

- **Guiding Model Development**: The promising performance of VM-Edit, particularly its high physics-plausibility score and robust preservation capabilities, suggests that region-conditioned editing with adaptive control strategies is a viable and effective approach for improving controllability and physical realism in non-rigid video editing. This offers a concrete direction for designing future architectures and sampling strategies.

- **Inspiring Advanced Evaluation Metrics**: The paper itself acknowledges limitations (e.g., VLM-based evaluation is perceptual, not simulation-grade physical verification) and suggests future extensions. This includes incorporating synthetic scenes with ground-truth physics, 3D reconstruction for geometry-aware validation, topology consistency checks, and flow consistency measurements. This forward-looking perspective indicates that NRVBench and NRVE-Acc are stepping stones towards even more rigorous, physically grounded video editing assessment.

In conclusion, this work significantly advances the evaluation of video editing by introducing a principled approach to assessing physics-aware non-rigid motion. By providing a diagnostic benchmark and a structured evaluation protocol, it will enable researchers to better understand, measure, and improve the physical realism and temporal coherence of next-generation video editing models.
