# WALA Learning Executable Latent Actions from Action-Labeled Demonstrations and Action-Free Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> The WALA framework enables robots to learn executable latent actions by combining action-labeled robot demonstrations with abundant action-free videos. This approach achieves state-of-the-art results on RoboCasa with a 75.2% average success rate and demonstrates that action-free human videos can enable 74.2% success in real-world tasks with significantly fewer robot demonstrations.

## Problem

- Training generalizable robot policies often requires extensive and costly action-labeled robot demonstrations that are difficult to scale.
- Existing vision-language-action (VLA) models provide limited and indirect supervision about the physical consequences of actions, which is insufficient for complex manipulation tasks.
- A vast quantity of action-free human videos containing rich physical interaction information remains underutilized for robot policy learning due to the lack of explicit action labels.

## Method

- A two-stage framework, **WALA**, is developed that first pretrains a ==Semantic-Geometric Latent Action Model (LAM)== on action-free videos to learn action-relevant scene evolution.
- The pretrained LAM is integrated into policy learning with a trainable ==latent world model== and a ==vision-language backbone==.
- Policy training employs a multi-component objective that combines ==robot action prediction== (for labeled data), ==latent action target matching== (from the frozen LAM encoder), and ==future semantic-geometric dynamics prediction== (from the trainable LAM decoder).

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on the RoboTwin 2.0 (Random) benchmark with a **92.8%** success rate and surpassed the DIAL baseline by **5.0 percentage points** on RoboCasa-GR1-Tabletop, reaching **75.2%** average success.
- Demonstrated improved real-world performance, achieving an **83.3%** average success rate on multi-task manipulation by augmenting **200** robot demonstrations with **400** action-free human videos.
- In a low-data real-world setting, WALA achieved **74.2%** success with only **50** robot demonstrations plus **400** human videos, matching the performance of WALA with **200** robot demonstrations alone, while maintaining low inference latency (**70ms**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Supervising latent actions with future semantic (DINOv3 features) and geometric (dense depth) deltas, rather than raw pixels, effectively guides policy learning towards task-relevant scene changes.
> - Learning stable latent action targets from a pretrained, frozen LAM encoder enables the effective use of action-free videos for grounding robot control.
> - Action-free human videos can significantly compensate for a limited budget of action-labeled robot demonstrations, improving policy performance and facilitating zero-shot transfer to novel tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalizable robot policies often relies on extensive robot demonstrations accompanied by explicit action annotations. However, such data is expensive to acquire, challenging to standardize across different robotic platforms, and limited in its ability to cover the vast array of physical interactions encountered in real-world environments.

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have emerged as a prominent area of research, aiming to enable language-conditioned manipulation by transferring knowledge from large-scale vision-language pretraining to robot control. Despite progress in this domain, existing VLA approaches typically depend on action-labeled robot demonstrations, which introduces data scarcity and collection challenges. Furthermore, their primary training objective, action-supervised learning, offers indirect and often weak supervision regarding the future physical consequences of executed actions. For complex tasks involving long horizons, contact-rich interactions, or precise spatial requirements, a policy benefits from understanding how objects, contacts, and scene geometry evolve following an action.

In contrast, a vast quantity of human videos is readily available, recording diverse physical interactions such as object pose changes, contact events, occlusions, tool use, and goal-directed behaviors. These videos implicitly contain rich information about how the physical world evolves under various manipulations. However, they generally lack ground-truth robot action labels, making them unsuitable for direct use in behavior cloning or low-level action supervision for robots.

This work addresses the challenge of leveraging action-free video data to enhance robot policy learning, thereby complementing the limitations of action-labeled robot demonstrations. It draws inspiration from two related research streams:
- **World Models and World Action Models (WAMs):** These models, including LingBot-VA, DreamZero, Motus, Fast-WAM, and LDA-1B, supervise policies by predicting future visual states, latent dynamics, or action-conditioned scene evolution. They demonstrate the value of future dynamics in robot control and show that future prediction can primarily serve as a training-time signal. WALA extends this by applying dynamics supervision directly to the latent actions of the policy, predicting future changes in structured DINOv3 and dense depth spaces, rather than raw pixels, to focus on task-relevant semantic and geometric information.
- **Latent Action Learning from Videos:** Methods like LAPA, Moto, UniVLA, UniT, and villa-X infer compact, action-like variables from observation transitions in videos to exploit data without explicit robot action labels. WALA distinguishes itself by learning latent actions that are simultaneously grounded in future scene evolution and explicitly tied to executable robot control through integration with action-labeled demonstrations.

By combining these perspectives, WALA aims to bridge the gap between large-scale, action-free observational data and the requirements for learning executable, generalizable robot policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, named WALA, that can jointly learn executable latent actions by leveraging both action-labeled robot demonstrations and action-free videos. This endeavor is motivated by several challenges and opportunities within the field of robot learning:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity and Cost:** The current paradigm for training generalizable robot policies heavily relies on robot demonstrations that include precise action annotations. Collecting such data is costly, time-consuming, and difficult to scale to the diversity and volume required for real-world generalization. This limits the scope and robustness of learned policies.
- **Enhancing Dynamics Understanding beyond Action Prediction:** Existing vision-language-action (VLA) models primarily learn a direct mapping from observations and language instructions to robot actions. This approach provides limited and indirect supervision regarding the physical consequences of actions. For complex manipulation tasks, such as those involving long horizons, contact, or fine spatial precision, a robot policy needs an understanding of how objects, contacts, and overall scene geometry evolve after an action is performed.
- **Leveraging Abundant Action-Free Video Data:** A vast and readily available resource exists in the form of human videos, which capture rich physical interactions, object transformations, and goal-directed behaviors. These videos inherently contain information about how the physical world changes, offering a potential source of dynamics supervision. However, the absence of explicit robot action labels in these videos prevents their direct use with conventional behavior cloning techniques.
- **Bridging Scene Evolution and Executable Control:** A central challenge is to extract meaningful, action-relevant dynamics from action-free videos and translate this information into a training signal that effectively improves robot policies capable of executing specific actions. The goal is to ensure that the learned latent actions are both predictive of future scene evolution and directly align with real robot actions when labeled data is available.

To achieve these aims, WALA sets out to:
1. **Develop a two-stage learning framework:** First, pretrain a semantic-geometric latent action model on action-free videos. Second, integrate this model into policy learning.
2. **Design a novel latent action representation:** This representation should focus on task-relevant semantic and geometric changes (deltas in DINOv3 features and dense depth) rather than raw pixel reconstruction, thereby reducing the influence of superficial appearance details.
3. **Establish a robust training methodology:** This involves a joint supervision scheme that combines robot action prediction (from labeled demonstrations), latent action target matching (derived from observed future changes), and future dynamics prediction (from a latent world model). This ensures the learned latent actions are simultaneously grounded in future scene evolution and tied to executable robot control.
4. **Demonstrate efficient deployment:** The framework should not introduce additional computational overhead during inference, ensuring the learned policies are practical for real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WALA employs a two-stage framework to learn executable latent actions, integrating both action-labeled demonstrations and action-free videos.

#### 3.1. Semantic-Geometric Latent Action Model (LAM) Pretraining

The first stage involves pretraining a latent action model (LAM) on videos, which may or may not include action annotations. The goal is to enable the model to learn representations of action-relevant scene evolution.

- **Observation Processing:** For a given current RGB-D observation $o_t = (I_t, D_t)$ and a set of $K$ sparsely sampled future RGB-D observations $\{o_{t+\tau_k}\}_{k=1}^K$, WALA extracts semantic and geometric features.
 - **Semantic Features:** A frozen DINOv3 encoder ($\phi$) extracts patch-level semantic features $X_t = \phi(I_t)$ from the current RGB image and $X_{t,k}^+ = \phi(I_{t+\tau_k})$ from future RGB images.
 - **Geometric Features:** Dense depth maps $D_t$ and $D_{t,k}^+$ are obtained from depth observations or a frozen depth estimator.
- **Future Delta Formulation:** Instead of predicting absolute future states, WALA focuses on predicting *future deltas*—changes from the current state.
 - **Semantic Deltas:** $\Delta X_{t,k} = X_{t,k}^+ - X_t$.
 - **Geometric Deltas:** $\Delta D_{t,k} = D_{t,k}^+ - D_t$.
 This approach encourages the model to focus on action-induced transitions rather than static appearance.
- **LAM Architecture:** The LAM consists of an encoder and a decoder.
 - **Encoder ($E_\theta$):** Takes the current DINOv3 features ($X_t$), current dense depth map ($D_t$), and the observed future semantic ($\Delta X_t^+$) and geometric ($\Delta D_t^+$) deltas. It infers a set of latent action tokens, $z_t$, representing the scene's evolution.
 - **Decoder ($G_{rgb_\psi}$, $G_{dep_\psi}$):** Predicts the future semantic ($\hat{\Delta X}_{t,k}$) and geometric ($\hat{\Delta D}_{t,k}$) deltas, conditioned on the current state features and the latent actions $z_t$.
- **Pretraining Objective ($L_{LAM}$):** The objective function optimizes the LAM to accurately predict future deltas.
 - **RGB Prediction Loss ($L_{rgb}$):** Combines an $\ell_1$ term and a cosine similarity term between predicted and target DINOv3 feature deltas, focusing on semantic changes.
 - **Depth Prediction Loss ($L_{dep}$):** Includes an $\ell_1$ regression term and a depth gradient consistency term, matching horizontal and vertical depth gradients of predicted and ground-truth depth changes, focusing on spatial structure.
 - The total pretraining loss is $L_{LAM} = L_{rgb} + \lambda_{dep}L_{dep}$. This avoids raw pixel reconstruction, dedicating model capacity to task-relevant semantic and geometric transitions.

#### 3.2. Policy Learning with Latent World Supervision

In the second stage, the pretrained LAM is integrated into the policy learning process for robot control.

- **Stable Latent Action Targets:** The pretrained LAM encoder ($E_\theta$) is frozen. Given the current observation and observed future deltas, it generates stable latent action targets, $z_t^* = \text{sg}[E_\theta(X_t, D_t, \Delta X_t^+, \Delta D_t^+)]$, where $\text{sg}[\cdot]$ denotes the stop-gradient operation, ensuring the target space remains consistent.
- **Trainable Latent World Model:** The pretrained LAM decoder is incorporated as a trainable latent world model.
- **Vision-Language Backbone and Action Head:** A vision-language backbone (implemented with Qwen3-VL-4B) processes multi-view RGB observations ($o_{mv_t}$), language instructions ($l$), robot states ($s_t$), and learned action queries ($q$) to generate unified latent actions, $\hat{z}_t = \Pi_\omega(o_{mv_t}, l, s_t, q)$. An action head ($A_\omega$) then predicts robot actions $\hat{a}_{t:t+H-1} = A_\omega(\hat{z}_t)$ from these latent actions.
- **Policy Training Objective ($L_{policy}$):** The policy is trained with a multi-component objective:
 - **Robot Action Prediction Loss ($L_{act}$):** A loss for predicting ground-truth robot actions, applicable only to action-labeled demonstrations (indicated by a binary variable $m$).
 - **Latent Action Target Matching Loss ($L_{align}$):** An $\ell_1$ and cosine loss that aligns the policy-generated latent actions ($\hat{z}_t$) with the frozen latent action targets ($z_t^*$), ensuring the policy's actions lead to observed scene evolution.
 - **Future Dynamics Prediction Loss ($L_{wm}$):** The trainable latent world model decoder uses the policy-generated latent actions ($\hat{z}_t$) to predict future semantic and geometric deltas. This loss grounds the policy's actions in future scene dynamics.
 - The total policy loss is $L_{policy} = m L_{act} + \lambda_{align}L_{align} + \lambda_{wm}L_{wm}$. For action-free videos, $L_{act}$ is masked out, but $L_{align}$ and $L_{wm}$ still provide supervision.

#### 3.3. Training and Inference

- **Training:** During LAM pretraining, only the LAM encoder, fusion module, and decoder are optimized, with the DINOv3 encoder and depth estimator frozen. During policy training, the LAM encoder remains frozen, while the LAM decoder, adapters, vision-language backbone, and action head are jointly trained.
- **Inference:** For deployment, WALA exclusively utilizes the vision-language backbone and action head. The LAM encoder, DINOv3 encoder, depth estimator, and latent world model decoder are not required, which prevents additional inference overhead.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of WALA was conducted across various simulated benchmarks and real-robot experiments, addressing specific research questions.

#### 4.1. Simulated Manipulation Performance

- **RoboTwin 2.0:** WALA achieved a 90.6% success rate in the Clean setting and 92.8% in the Random setting. The Random setting result represents a new state-of-the-art among compared methods, with the Clean setting remaining competitive. This indicates that future-dynamics supervision can enhance policy robustness without increasing inference time.
- **RoboCasa-GR1-Tabletop:** WALA demonstrated an average success rate of 75.2%, which surpassed the previously reported strongest baseline, DIAL, by 5.0 percentage points. Qualitative analysis (Fig. 3) supported these results by showing that predicted future DINOv3 features preserved task-relevant semantic changes, and predicted depth changes captured coarse spatial structure.

#### 4.2. Scaling with Labeled and Action-Free Videos

- **Action-Labeled Data Scaling (RoboCasa):** Experiments showed that WALA consistently outperformed a Base Policy (trained with robot action supervision only) across different amounts of action-labeled demonstrations. For instance, with only 10% labeled data, WALA achieved 53.9% success, compared to 18.1% for the Base Policy. This performance advantage was maintained as labeled data increased, reaching 75.2% for WALA versus 54.2% for the Base Policy at 100% data.
- **Action-Free Video Scaling (RoboCasa):** When the amount of action-labeled data was fixed at 10%, integrating additional action-free RoboCasa videos further improved WALA's performance. The success rate increased from 53.9% (without additional action-free videos) to 67.8% (with 90% additional action-free videos). These results support the premise that videos lacking ground-truth robot action labels can still contribute useful dynamics supervision for policy learning.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study on Component Contributions

An ablation study on RoboCasa-GR1-Tabletop assessed the contribution of different supervision sources:
- Adding semantic-geometric world supervision alone (without LAM pretraining) improved the Base Policy from 54.2% to 67.8%.
- With LAM pretraining, semantic prediction enhanced performance, and including depth-based geometric prediction further raised the success rate to 71.0%.
- Utilizing only the LAM target for supervision also improved over the Base Policy but performed less effectively than direct semantic-geometric prediction.
- **The full WALA framework, combining LAM pretraining, latent action target matching, and future semantic-geometric prediction, achieved the highest performance of 75.2%.** This suggests that both latent action target matching and future semantic-geometric prediction offer complementary constraints, with the robot action loss grounding latent actions in executable control and the latent world loss rooting them in future scene evolution.

#### 4.4. Analysis of Learned Latent Actions

Qualitative analysis of the latent actions learned during LAM pretraining (Fig. 5 and 6) indicated:
- **Latent Action Retrieval:** Latent action tokens effectively clustered transitions that shared similar manipulation semantics (e.g., grasping, placing, releasing), irrespective of variations in object appearance, scene layout, or camera view.
- **RGB Attention Visualization:** The encoder's attention maps concentrated on regions directly involved in physical interaction, such as hands, manipulated objects, and contact zones.
These findings suggest that WALA learns latent actions that specifically capture action-induced scene changes and interaction regions, rather than merely low-level image variations.

#### 4.5. Real-World Experiments

WALA was evaluated on four real-world multi-task manipulation scenarios (Basic Pick-Place, Stack Paper Cups, Insert Flowers, Disassemble Blocks):
- **Performance against Baselines:** WALA (75.0% average success rate with 200 robot demonstrations per task) outperformed π0 (51.7%) and π0.5 (58.3%).
- **Impact of Action-Free Human Videos:** Adding 400 action-free human videos (collected in similar scenes) per task to the 200-demonstration setting further improved WALA's average success rate to 83.3%.
- **Low-Label Setting Effectiveness:** In a scenario with only 50 robot demonstrations per task augmented by 400 action-free human videos, WALA achieved an average success rate of 74.2%, closely matching the performance of WALA trained with 200 robot demonstrations alone (75.0%). This low-label configuration also surpassed both baselines trained with 200 robot demonstrations. This indicates that action-free human videos can substantially compensate for limited real-robot data.
- **Inference Latency:** WALA demonstrated lower inference latency (70ms) compared to π0 (170ms) and π0.5 (190ms), confirming that its design avoids adding world-model inference overhead during deployment.
- **Zero-Shot Transfer with Out-of-Distribution Human Videos:** For an unseen "grasping bread and placing it onto a dinner plate" task, WALA achieved 3/10 successes without relevant human videos. After incorporating 400 action-free egocentric human videos for this specific task (without any robot demonstrations), the success rate improved to 9/10. This suggests that action-free human videos can inject task-relevant scene dynamics into the latent action space, facilitating transfer to novel tasks without requiring direct robot action labels.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The WALA framework presents several contributions and potential impacts on the field of robot learning:

- **Enhanced Data Utilization and Scalability:** WALA offers a method to leverage the abundance of readily available action-free video data to complement and reduce reliance on expensive and scarce action-labeled robot demonstrations. This approach is critical for scaling robot learning to more diverse and complex real-world tasks. By converting future scene evolution from videos into meaningful dynamics supervision, WALA addresses a significant bottleneck in data acquisition for robot policies.
- **Improved Policy Robustness and Generalization:** By explicitly grounding latent actions in future semantic and geometric scene evolution, WALA encourages policies to develop a deeper understanding of the physical consequences of their actions. This results in enhanced performance, increased robustness, and better generalization across varied manipulation tasks in both simulated and real-world environments, as evidenced by its state-of-the-art results on RoboCasa and strong performance on RoboTwin.
- **Effective Bridge Between Perception and Control:** The framework effectively bridges the gap between high-level visual understanding (leveraging pretrained vision-language models like DINOv3) and low-level executable robot control. By learning latent actions that represent task-relevant semantic and geometric changes, WALA translates abstract video-based dynamics into signals that are directly useful for robot policy learning.
- **Deployment Efficiency:** A key design aspect of WALA is that its world model components are utilized solely during training. This ensures that the deployed policy operates with low inference latency, relying only on the vision-language backbone and action head. This efficiency is crucial for the practical application of robot learning in real-world scenarios.
- **Compensation for Limited Labeled Data:** The real-world experiments demonstrate WALA's ability to utilize action-free human videos to partially compensate for a limited budget of robot demonstrations. This is particularly significant for tasks where collecting extensive robot data is impractical, making advanced robot capabilities more accessible.
- **Potential for Zero-Shot Transfer:** The observed zero-shot transfer capability to new tasks by integrating action-free human videos suggests that WALA can inject task-relevant dynamics into the latent action space, potentially enabling robots to perform novel tasks without requiring any direct robot demonstrations for those specific tasks.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work identifies limitations, such as the current scale of data used for LAM pretraining and the scope of zero-shot evaluation. These limitations point towards future research avenues, including leveraging larger and more diverse video datasets and exploring broader, more challenging zero-shot transfer scenarios, which could further unlock the potential of action-free interaction data for robot control.
