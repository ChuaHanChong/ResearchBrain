# AgentV-RL: Scaling Reward Modeling with Agentic Verifier

> [!summary] Summary
> AgentV-RL introduces an agentic verifier framework that uses bidirectional, multi-turn, tool-augmented reasoning to scrutinize LLM-generated solutions for complex problems. It achieves state-of-the-art results in mathematical reasoning, outperforming larger models by up to 25.2 percentage points on MATH500 by integrating a comprehensive verification process.

## Problem

- Existing Generative Reward Models (GenRMs) often employ single-turn reasoning, making them susceptible to error propagation and easily misled by superficially plausible but incorrect solutions.
- Verifiers frequently lack robust external grounding, leading to hallucinations and unstable performance in computation-intensive or knowledge-heavy tasks without tight integration with symbolic solvers or external tools.
- There is a need for more interpretable and comprehensive feedback beyond simple scalar rewards, moving towards human-like deliberative checking to explain why a solution is correct or incorrect.

## Method

- The **Agentic Verifier** framework was developed, employing a ==multi-agent system== with a ==Forward Agent== for sufficiency checking and a ==Backward Agent== for necessity checking, both utilizing a ==Plan-Validate-Verdict== strategy.
- The framework integrates ==autonomous interaction with external tools==, such as a ==Python interpreter==, to perform concrete calculations and ground reasoning in verifiable facts.
- **AgentV-RL**, a ==two-stage training recipe==, distills the multi-agent capabilities into a single LLM through ==synthetic trajectories sampling==, ==supervised fine-tuning (SFT)==, and ==reinforcement learning (GRPO)==.

## Results

- Agentic-Verifier-Qwen3-4B achieved state-of-the-art performance in Best-of-N sampling across benchmarks, surpassing INF-ORM-Llama3.1-70B (a **70B** parameter model) by **25.2 percentage points** on MATH500.
- In iterative refinement, it provided high-quality critiques leading to correction rates up to **41.6%** on MATH500 in the first turn, with minimal degradation and faster convergence compared to other verifiers.
- The framework demonstrated superior generalization performance on LiveCodeBench (code generation) and HotpotQA (multi-hop question answering), indicating its applicability to diverse reasoning domains beyond mathematics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bidirectional checking by specialized forward and backward agents provides more rigorous and comprehensive verification, identifying errors that single-directional methods might miss.
> - Integrating external tools is crucial for grounding reasoning in precise calculations and factual knowledge, significantly reducing hallucinations and improving accuracy in complex domains.
> - The agentic, multi-turn deliberative process itself, even without external tools, substantially enhances verification capabilities, with tool integration providing further gains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has led to significant advancements in reasoning capabilities, with recent achievements noted in complex domains such as the International Mathematics Olympiad. A prevalent trend in pushing these capabilities further involves scaling inference-time compute, a strategy known as Test-Time Scaling (TTS). TTS can manifest through parallel methods, such as Best-of-N (BoN) sampling, or sequential refinement processes. The efficacy of TTS is fundamentally dependent on the quality of the "Reward Model," also known as a verifier, which guides the search process and assesses solution quality.

Existing reward models generally fall into three categories: outcome-level Reward Models (ORMs) that assign scalar values to complete responses, process-level Reward Models (PRMs) that provide dense signals by supervising intermediate steps, and Generative Reward Models (GenRMs) that reformulate reward modeling as next-token prediction to generate natural-language feedback. While GenRMs offer interpretability, they typically employ single-turn reasoning. This prevalent single-turn paradigm faces two critical limitations, particularly in complex domains like mathematics:
1. **Error Propagation:** LLMs, primarily trained on correct or near-correct solutions, may struggle to provide accurate verdicts when presented with flawed solutions, often being misled by superficially plausible but incorrect answers.
2. **Lack of External Grounding:** Verifiers frequently encounter difficulties in computation-intensive or knowledge-heavy tasks. Without robust integration with symbolic solvers or external tools, they are prone to generating hallucinations, leading to unstable performance.

This research aims to address these identified limitations by proposing a paradigm shift towards agentic reward modeling. It positions itself within the landscape of enhancing LLM reasoning and alignment by introducing multi-turn reasoning augmented with external tools, thereby seeking to improve the reliability, comprehensiveness, and interpretability of solution assessment. It builds upon prior work in reward modeling, TTS, and the use of auxiliary models for critique and self-improvement, but differentiates itself by casting verification as a bidirectional, multi-turn, tool-augmented process, integrating both sufficiency and necessity checking.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a more robust, reliable, and interpretable framework for reward modeling that can effectively scrutinize and evaluate complex reasoning solutions generated by LLMs. This framework, termed "Agentic Verifier," aims to overcome significant limitations present in current reward models, particularly in the context of Test-Time Scaling (TTS) for tasks requiring deep reasoning.

The primary motivations driving this work are:

- **Addressing Error Propagation in Existing Reward Models:** Current Generative Reward Models (GenRMs) often employ single-turn reasoning. This makes them susceptible to error propagation, where initial flaws in a candidate solution can mislead the verifier into accepting a seemingly plausible but ultimately incorrect answer. The authors are motivated to create a verifier that can proactively identify and trace errors, even within complex, multi-step reasoning chains, rather than being swayed by superficial correctness.
- **Enhancing External Grounding for Computation and Knowledge-Intensive Tasks:** Many reasoning tasks, especially in mathematics, require precise calculations or access to external knowledge. Traditional reward models frequently struggle with such computation-intensive or knowledge-heavy domains, leading to hallucinations or unreliable performance when external tools like code interpreters are not tightly integrated. A key motivation is to empower the verifier with the ability to autonomously interact with external tools to perform concrete checks and ground its reasoning in verifiable facts, thus reducing reliance on internal, potentially fallible, knowledge.
- **Improving Interpretability and Comprehensiveness of Verification:** Beyond merely assigning a scalar reward, there is a need for verifiers to provide interpretable feedback that explains *why* a solution is correct or incorrect. The authors are motivated to emulate rigorous human-like checking, which often involves deliberative, multi-turn processes and bidirectional reasoning (checking from premises to conclusion and vice-versa). This approach aims to yield a more comprehensive assessment than single-turn evaluations, offering richer insights into solution quality.
- **Facilitating Scalable Deployment of Agentic Verification:** While a multi-agent framework offers enhanced verification capabilities, practical deployment requires distilling these complex interactions into a single, efficient model. The motivation here is to develop a scalable training recipe, AgentV-RL, that can imbue a single LLM with the agentic proficiency (multi-turn, long-horizon, tool-integrated reasoning) necessary for practical applications, without requiring the explicit orchestration of multiple independent agents at inference time.
- **Advancing LLM Capabilities in Challenging Domains:** The overall motivation is to push the boundaries of LLM reasoning, particularly in challenging academic benchmarks like mathematics olympiads. By developing a more reliable verifier, the goal is to provide a more effective compass for guiding LLMs through complex problem-solving processes, whether through selecting the best solution from multiple candidates or iteratively refining an initial attempt.

In summary, the research aims to fundamentally improve the reliability and interpretability of reward modeling by introducing an agentic, multi-turn, tool-augmented verification framework and a scalable training methodology to enable its practical application.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on two main components: the **Agentic Verifier** framework, which defines the multi-agent verification process, and **AgentV-RL**, a scalable training recipe designed to distill this multi-agent capability into a single LLM. The work primarily focuses on interactive Test-Time Scaling (TTS) between an actor (problem solver) and a verifier (feedback provider) in mathematics.

#### 3.1. Agentic Verifier Framework

The Agentic Verifier is conceived as a multi-agent system that emulates rigorous human-like checking through a bidirectional, multi-turn, tool-augmented deliberative process. It coordinates two specialized agents:

- **Forward Agent:** This agent is responsible for **sufficiency checking**. It sequentially traces the logical flow of a candidate solution from its premises (problem statement) to its conclusion. Its role is to validate whether each step in the solution is correct and if the preceding step provides a sufficient condition for the subsequent derivation.
- **Backward Agent:** This agent performs **necessity checking**. It is designed to identify errors that the forward agent might overlook, such as solutions that are superficially logical but fail to satisfy original problem constraints or omit necessary proofs. It reasons in reverse, starting from the final answer or conclusion and tracing back to verify its grounding in the problem statement and its underlying premises.

Both agents operate using a "Plan-Validate-Verdict" strategy across multiple turns:

1. **Planning Stage:**
 - **Objective:** Decompose the candidate solution into a sequence of atomic, verifiable sub-steps (Π).
 - **Process:** The agent formulates an explicit plan outlining the logical flow for verification. This plan includes specific checkpoints and necessary calculations, providing a structured template. The forward agent plans from premises to conclusion, while the backward agent plans from the conclusion back to the problem statement.
 - **Output:** A list of verification steps describing *what* to check.

2. **Validation Stage:**
 - **Objective:** Examine the correctness of each atomic step (v_i) in the plan, ensuring logical sufficiency (for forward) or necessity (for backward) between steps.
 - **Process:** The agent engages in multi-turn reasoning following a Thought-Action-Observation (ReAct-like) loop. It articulates its thoughts, performs actions (e.g., invoking a Python interpreter for numerical calculation or symbolic manipulation), and receives observations (results from tool execution). This iterative process allows decomposition of complex checks and integration of external tool results into the reasoning chain.
 - **Tool Use:** Python interpreter calls are invoked within `<python>...</python>` blocks, with results returned in `<result>...</result>`. Tool usage is limited to a maximum of three calls per rollout.

3. **Verdict Stage:**
 - **Objective:** Transition from local, step-wise analysis to a global, holistic assessment and issue a definitive judgment.
 - **Process:** The agent aggregates all evidence collected during the validation stage to determine the overall correctness of the solution.
 - **Output:** A strict binary classification (`True`/`False`) enclosed by `<answer>...</answer>`. The logits of the `True` token serve as the confidence score.

For aggregation, in Best-of-N (BoN) settings, the confidence scores from forward and backward traces are averaged. In sequential refinement, a conservative aggregation rule is applied: a solution is deemed correct only if *both* agents judge it as correct. If either agent identifies an error, the solution is marked incorrect, and the verifier generates specific modification suggestions.

#### 3.2. AgentV-RL Training Recipe

To enable a single LLM to acquire the multi-agent capabilities of the Agentic Verifier, a dedicated two-stage training recipe, AgentV-RL, is introduced:

1. **Synthetic Trajectories Sampling:**
 - **Purpose:** Address data scarcity and ensure comprehensive coverage of diverse reasoning patterns, including tool-integrated verification.
 - **Process:**
 - Curate a base dataset by filtering high-quality public datasets (e.g., Polaris, DeepScaleR-40K, AReaL-boba-106k).
 - For each question, `k` candidate solutions are sampled (e.g., k=8), and questions where all solutions are uniformly correct/incorrect are filtered out to ensure diversity.
 - An LLM (role-playing as either a forward or backward agent) automatically generates tool-augmented verification trajectories.
 - **Quality Control:** Only trajectories where the LLM's predicted verdict matches the ground truth are retained, forming the final synthetic dataset D_sft, which includes both positive (correct) and negative (incorrect) verification examples.

2. **Two-Stage Training Schema:**
 - **Rejection Fine-tuning (SFT):**
 - **Purpose:** Endow the verifier with foundational agentic proficiency by aligning its behavior with multi-turn, decision-making processes and tool interaction.
 - **Process:** Supervised fine-tuning is performed on the D_sft dataset. The objective is to encourage the model to reproduce the stepwise reasoning, actions (Python calls), and observations (tool results) from the synthetic trajectories. The loss function optimizes the likelihood of generating the correct token sequence for the trajectory, excluding user inputs and tool outputs from loss calculation.
 - **Reinforcement Learning (GRPO - Group Relative Policy Optimization):**
 - **Purpose:** Further unlock the model's reasoning potential and incentivize autonomous exploration for multi-turn, long-horizon, and tool-integrated reasoning.
 - **Process:** Following SFT, the model is optimized using GRPO. For each question-solution pair, a group of candidate verification trajectories are sampled from the current verifier policy (mixing forward and backward agent behaviors).
 - **Reward Function:** A binary outcome-level reward is assigned: `+1` if the verifier's predicted verdict matches the ground truth, and `-1` otherwise.
 - **Optimization:** GRPO is applied, calculating normalized advantages within groups of rollouts (filtering groups with zero reward variance). The objective function balances the importance sampling ratio with a KL divergence penalty against a reference policy to maintain training stability. Tool execution segments are excluded from loss calculation to mitigate memorization of environment observations.

This comprehensive methodology aims to create a highly capable and reliable reward model that can effectively operate in complex reasoning environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that Agentic Verifier consistently yields performance gains across various settings, outperforming existing baselines in both parallel and sequential Test-Time Scaling (TTS) paradigms.

#### 4.1. Agentic Verifier Improves Best-of-N Sampling Performance

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Agentic Verifier achieves leading results across all evaluated benchmarks: GSM8K, MATH500, Gaokao2023, and AIME24.
- **Efficiency and Superiority over Larger Models:** Notably, the Agentic-Verifier-Qwen3-4B variant (a 4-billion parameter model) achieves significantly higher accuracy than much larger models. For instance, on MATH500, it reaches up to 79.0%, surpassing INF-ORM-Llama3.1-70B (a 70-billion parameter outcome reward model) by a margin of 25.2 percentage points. Similar trends were observed across other benchmarks.
- **Scalability with Samples:** The performance of Agentic Verifier consistently improves as the number of Best-of-N (BoN) samples (N) increases from 32 to 128, particularly on challenging datasets like AIME24. This indicates its effectiveness in discerning higher-quality solutions from larger pools.
- **Bidirectional Verification and Tool Integration:** The substantial performance gains are attributed to the framework's bidirectional verification capabilities and its seamless integration of external tools, which enable a deeper and more rigorous scrutiny of reasoning chains to identify subtle errors.

#### 4.2. Agentic Verifier Provides Effective Guidance for Iterative Refinement

- **High-Quality Critiques:** In sequential scaling (iterative refinement), Agentic Verifier provides critiques that lead to high rates of correction (∆↑) and minimal rates of degradation (∆↓). For example, Agentic-Verifier-Qwen3-4B achieved up to a 41.6% correction rate on MATH500 in the first turn, while maintaining a low 0.6% degradation rate. This indicates its ability to generate useful feedback that helps actors correct errors.
- **Faster Convergence and Robustness:** Compared to other verifiers, Agentic Verifier exhibits faster convergence, with significant performance boosts observed in the initial one or two refinement rounds. Its performance remains stable or shows only slight declines in later iterations, suggesting robustness and sustained rigorous oversight.

#### 4.3. Analysis and Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of Bidirectional Components:** An ablation study confirmed the synergy between the forward and backward agents. While both forward-only and backward-only variants achieved competitive results, the full Agentic Verifier, integrating both, consistently achieved the best performance, validating the benefits of bidirectional checking.
- **Effectiveness of Tool Usage:** The Agentic Verifier without tool integration still significantly outperformed the base model, indicating that the structured multi-turn verification process itself contributes substantially. The addition of Python tools further improved performance, demonstrating the value of external grounding for accuracy. Tool usage was found to be moderate and stable.
- **Impact of Training Recipe:** A controllable study on the training recipe showed that a "Train-free" (zero-shot) variant already yielded competitive results. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on synthetic data led to additional gains. The final stage, Reinforcement Learning (RL) using GRPO, further enhanced the model's reasoning potential by incentivizing autonomous exploration and optimizing behavioral patterns.

#### 4.4. Scaling Effects

- **Scaling Inference-time Compute:** Sampling multiple verification trajectories within Agentic Verifier (and aggregating their scores) was shown to positively scale BoN performance, indicating that allocating more inference-time compute can yield further benefits.
- **Scaling Model Size:** Under a train-free setting, larger models in the Qwen3 series (from 0.6B to 4B parameters) consistently achieved higher accuracy, demonstrating that the agentic framework benefits from increased model capacity.

#### 4.5. Generalization Performance

Agentic Verifier-Qwen3-4B demonstrated superior performance on LiveCodeBench (code generation) and HotpotQA (multi-hop question answering) compared to other models. This indicates that the bidirectional, tool-augmented verification paradigm generalizes effectively to broader reasoning domains beyond mathematics.

#### 4.6. Latency Analysis

While achieving higher accuracy, Agentic Verifier incurs increased computational cost in terms of tokens processed and latency due to its multi-turn deliberative process. However, the forward-only and backward-only variants offer scalable trade-offs for resource-constrained environments.

In summary, the main findings establish Agentic Verifier as a highly effective and robust paradigm for reward modeling, capable of delivering state-of-the-art performance in complex reasoning tasks while offering interpretability and adaptability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Agentic Verifier represents a methodological advancement in the field of reward modeling and the broader development of intelligent AI agents. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Reliability and Robustness in LLM Reasoning:** By transforming reward modeling into a multi-turn, tool-augmented, deliberative process, Agentic Verifier provides a more reliable mechanism for evaluating and guiding LLM-generated solutions. This is particularly crucial in complex reasoning domains where traditional single-turn verifiers are prone to error propagation and lack external grounding. The bidirectional checking (forward and backward agents) emulates a more thorough human-like review, increasing confidence in the final verdict.
- **Improved Interpretability of Evaluation:** The framework’s multi-turn nature and the generation of detailed verification trajectories offer greater interpretability regarding *why* a solution is deemed correct or incorrect. This detailed feedback is valuable not only for selecting the best solutions but also for debugging and refining LLM reasoning processes, paving the way for more transparent and explainable AI systems.
- **Efficiency and Scalability:** The ability of a 4B parameter Agentic Verifier to significantly outperform much larger (e.g., 70B parameter) state-of-the-art Outcome Reward Models demonstrates a notable improvement in efficiency. The AgentV-RL training recipe, with its synthetic data generation and two-stage optimization, provides a scalable approach to distill complex multi-agent capabilities into a single, compact LLM, making advanced verification more accessible.
- **Versatility and Generalization:** The demonstrated superior performance on diverse benchmarks, including mathematical reasoning, code generation (LiveCodeBench), and multi-hop question answering (HotpotQA), indicates the framework's versatility. This suggests that the agentic paradigm for verification is not domain-specific but rather a general approach applicable to a wide range of complex tasks requiring logical consistency and factual accuracy.
- **Advancing Test-Time Scaling (TTS) and LLM Alignment:** Agentic Verifier significantly bolsters the efficacy of TTS methods, both parallel (Best-of-N) and sequential (iterative refinement). Its capacity to provide high-quality critiques and facilitate faster convergence in refinement cycles directly contributes to better LLM alignment by enabling more effective policy updates based on reliable feedback.
- **Foundation for Future Agentic AI Research:** This work contributes to the growing body of research on agentic AI, specifically by providing a principled method for autonomous verification. It opens avenues for future work on optimizing efficiency, broadening tool integration to encompass more diverse external knowledge sources, and improving adaptability to an even wider array of reasoning challenges, ultimately contributing to the development of more capable and trustworthy AI agents.

Despite its advancements, the paper acknowledges limitations related to the reliance on synthetic data, increased computational costs, and dependence on the coverage and reliability of external tools. Future research will likely focus on addressing these practical considerations to further enhance the framework's real-world applicability.
