# 3D RL-DWA: A Hybrid Reinforcement Learning and Dynamic Window Approach for Goal-Directed Local Navigation in Multi-DoF Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> A hybrid control framework, 3D RL-DWA, integrates Reinforcement Learning with the Dynamic Window Approach to enable adaptive 3D local navigation and shape optimization for high-degree-of-freedom deformable robots. This method demonstrated near 100% path completion and stable deformation in simulated vascular networks, outperforming pure RL and model-based baselines while maintaining real-time inference speeds under 2 ms.

## Problem

- Current local navigation methods like DWA struggle with adaptability in complex 3D environments and high-degree-of-freedom (DoF) robots due to fixed parameter tuning requirements.
- Pure Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches for complex 3D navigation often face slow convergence, instability during training, and difficulty generalizing across diverse scenarios.
- Existing hybrid RL-DWA solutions are predominantly limited to 2D motion planning, leaving a gap for adaptive 3D navigation in high-DoF deformable robotic systems, particularly with sparse sensory data.

## Method

- A novel hybrid **3D RL-DWA** framework combines a customized ==3D Dynamic Window Approach== with a ==Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)== Reinforcement Learning module.
- The RL agent dynamically adjusts the DWA's ==cost function weighting factors== (e.g., for speed, direction, obstacle clearance, goal alignment) along with the robot's rotational and deformation velocities.
- The robot is modeled as a ==9-DoF deformable ellipsoid== navigating in a simulated vascular network, utilizing ==sparse laser sensor data== for obstacle avoidance and goal seeking.

## Results

- The 3D RL-DWA framework achieved **near 100%** path completion and approximately **57%** occupied volume in simulated vascular networks, outperforming pure RL and model-based baselines across known and unseen scenarios.
- The hybrid approach exhibited faster training convergence and higher episode rewards, with inference times consistently **below 2 ms** per control step, making it suitable for real-time applications.
- The system demonstrated robustness to sensor uncertainties, maintaining stable deformation control and manageable degradation in path completion even with significant Gaussian noise in sensor readings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating Reinforcement Learning with DWA significantly improves training stability, convergence speed, and overall reward compared to pure RL, by leveraging DWA's structured velocity optimization.
> - The hybrid framework effectively enables simultaneous adaptive control of a deformable robot's 3D motion and shape in highly constrained environments using sparse sensory input.
> - Dynamic adjustment of DWA parameters through RL allows for adaptive prioritization of navigation objectives (e.g., speed vs. obstacle avoidance), leading to more robust and generalized behaviors.
