# 3D RL-DWA: A Hybrid Reinforcement Learning and Dynamic Window Approach for Goal-Directed Local Navigation in Multi-DoF Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> A hybrid control framework, 3D RL-DWA, integrates Reinforcement Learning with the Dynamic Window Approach to enable adaptive 3D local navigation and shape optimization for high-degree-of-freedom deformable robots. This method demonstrated near 100% path completion and stable deformation in simulated vascular networks, outperforming pure RL and model-based baselines while maintaining real-time inference speeds under 2 ms.

## Problem

- Current local navigation methods like DWA struggle with adaptability in complex 3D environments and high-degree-of-freedom (DoF) robots due to fixed parameter tuning requirements.
- Pure Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches for complex 3D navigation often face slow convergence, instability during training, and difficulty generalizing across diverse scenarios.
- Existing hybrid RL-DWA solutions are predominantly limited to 2D motion planning, leaving a gap for adaptive 3D navigation in high-DoF deformable robotic systems, particularly with sparse sensory data.

## Method

- A novel hybrid **3D RL-DWA** framework combines a customized ==3D Dynamic Window Approach== with a ==Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)== Reinforcement Learning module.
- The RL agent dynamically adjusts the DWA's ==cost function weighting factors== (e.g., for speed, direction, obstacle clearance, goal alignment) along with the robot's rotational and deformation velocities.
- The robot is modeled as a ==9-DoF deformable ellipsoid== navigating in a simulated vascular network, utilizing ==sparse laser sensor data== for obstacle avoidance and goal seeking.

## Results

- The 3D RL-DWA framework achieved **near 100%** path completion and approximately **57%** occupied volume in simulated vascular networks, outperforming pure RL and model-based baselines across known and unseen scenarios.
- The hybrid approach exhibited faster training convergence and higher episode rewards, with inference times consistently **below 2 ms** per control step, making it suitable for real-time applications.
- The system demonstrated robustness to sensor uncertainties, maintaining stable deformation control and manageable degradation in path completion even with significant Gaussian noise in sensor readings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating Reinforcement Learning with DWA significantly improves training stability, convergence speed, and overall reward compared to pure RL, by leveraging DWA's structured velocity optimization.
> - The hybrid framework effectively enables simultaneous adaptive control of a deformable robot's 3D motion and shape in highly constrained environments using sparse sensory input.
> - Dynamic adjustment of DWA parameters through RL allows for adaptive prioritization of navigation objectives (e.g., speed vs. obstacle avoidance), leading to more robust and generalized behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous navigation systems typically integrate global and local planning strategies. Global planners compute trajectories from a start to a goal pose, often assuming fully known and static environments. Local planners, conversely, enable real-time adaptation to environmental changes, utilizing onboard sensor data for reactive obstacle avoidance and trajectory refinement. Navigation in complex 3D environments, particularly for robots possessing high degrees of freedom (DoF), presents challenges due to the need for coordinating multiple motion variables while ensuring collision-free movement under various constraints.

The Dynamic Window Approach (DWA) is a widely adopted local navigation technique that optimizes planned paths by evaluating a cost function balancing factors such as obstacle distance and goal alignment. However, conventional DWA relies on fixed parameter tuning, which can limit its adaptability in dynamic or unstructured environments and increase computational costs in high-dimensional motion spaces. This necessitates extensive manual tuning to adapt to diverse scenarios.

Recent advancements in Reinforcement Learning (RL) offer capabilities for robots to dynamically adjust control policies based on environmental conditions. Hybrid approaches that integrate RL with classical planners like DWA have shown promise in enhancing adaptability and robustness. Previous research in RL-DWA combinations has predominantly focused on 2D motion planning in structured environments, leaving a research gap for solutions addressing 3D motion control in high-DoF robotic systems. Other hybrid methods, such as RL with Model Predictive Control (MPC), exist but often involve computationally intensive online optimization and depend on precise dynamical models.

The demand for advanced navigation strategies with adaptive decision-making and real-time reconfigurability is particularly relevant in emerging domains such as microrobotics, where systems operate in highly constrained and unstructured 3D environments with sparse exteroceptive data. Examples include adaptive microparticle swarms and deformable ferrofluidic microrobots designed for biomedical applications like minimally invasive interventions and precision drug delivery. Current adaptive control strategies in microrobotics often exhibit limitations in real-time adaptability and high-dimensional motion planning.

This work contributes to the field by extending hybrid RL-DWA approaches to 3D navigation for high-DoF deformable robots. It specifically addresses the challenge of goal-directed local navigation in constrained 3D environments, such as vascular networks, leveraging sparse sensory data to optimize both motion and shape.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel hybrid navigation framework, termed 3D RL-DWA, that combines Reinforcement Learning (RL) with the Dynamic Window Approach (DWA) for adaptive 3D local navigation in robotic systems with high degrees of freedom.

The specific objectives of the study include:

- **Integration of RL and DWA:** To create a control architecture where an RL agent dynamically adjusts the parameters of a DWA-based local planner, thereby enhancing adaptive control of robot motion and shape.
- **Multi-DoF Control:** To enable simultaneous and adaptive control of a deformable robot's linear, angular, and deformation velocities.
- **Goal-Directed Navigation in Constrained Environments:** To facilitate navigation toward a specific goal in complex and constrained 3D environments, such as simulated vascular networks, utilizing sparse sensory input.
- **Volume Optimization:** To allow the robot to dynamically modify its shape to maximize the occupied volume within the environment while preventing collisions.
- **Performance Evaluation:** To rigorously evaluate the performance of the proposed framework through extensive experimental trials in a simulated environment. This involves comparing the 3D RL-DWA against a pure RL baseline and a model-based navigation method.
- **Assessment of Sensor Resolution Impact:** To investigate how varying levels of sensory data resolution (sparse exteroceptive data) affect the learning process and generalization capabilities of the navigation system.
- **Computational Efficiency Analysis:** To measure the inference times of the proposed method to determine its suitability for real-time applications.
- **Robustness Assessment:** To evaluate the system's resilience to sensor uncertainties by injecting Gaussian noise into the sensor readings.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations in existing autonomous navigation paradigms. Traditional DWA, while effective, suffers from fixed parameter tuning, which compromises its adaptability in dynamic and diverse environments and for complex, high-DoF robots operating in 3D spaces. Pure RL approaches, though adaptable, often exhibit slow convergence, instability during training, and may struggle to generalize effectively, especially when learning complex motion and deformation policies from scratch. Furthermore, model-based methods can be computationally demanding and require accurate dynamical models, which may not always be available or feasible for high-DoF deformable systems. The increasing demand for autonomous microrobots in biomedical applications, navigating highly constrained 3D spaces with limited sensory information, underscores the need for a robust, adaptive, and computationally efficient local navigation strategy that can simultaneously manage motion and morphological changes. This research aims to address these identified gaps by leveraging the strengths of both RL's adaptability and DWA's structured, real-time velocity optimization capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a hybrid control architecture called 3D RL-DWA, designed for adaptive 3D local navigation of a high-DoF deformable robot. The system operates in a closed-loop, receiving observations from the environment and outputting control commands.

**A. System Model and Kinematics**

The robot is modeled as a deformable ellipsoidal system with 9 degrees of freedom (DoF): 6 for its pose (position `p = [x, y, z]` and orientation `q = [qx, qy, qz, qw]`) and 3 for shape deformation (`δ = [δx, δy, δz]`) along its principal axes. It is equipped with N laser sensors, positioned spherically at its center, to provide exteroceptive information, with N varying to simulate different sensing resolutions. The simulation environment, a virtual vascular network, was developed in CoppeliaSim.

The robot is velocity-controlled. Its kinematics are simplified such that the robot's x-axis is constrained to align with the direction of motion, meaning only rotation about the x-axis (`ωx`) is actively controlled. Other rotations are passively induced by alignment. Translational and deformation states are updated based on constant velocities over a control interval `∆t`. The angular velocity comprises a passive component derived from alignment and an actively controlled component for x-axis rotation. The orientation is updated using quaternion integration.

**B. Control Architecture**

The control architecture combines a customized 3D DWA module with an RL module.

1. **3D Dynamic Window Approach (DWA):**
 - The DWA module is tailored for 3D navigation and the robot's specific kinematics.
 - It first computes a set of admissible velocities, forming the "Dynamic Window" (`Vd`), based on the robot's current velocity and maximum accelerations.
 - From `Vd`, the optimal linear velocity is selected by maximizing a cost function `G(v) = α·vel(v) + β·dir(v) + γ·clear(v) + ζ·head(v)`.
 - All terms in the cost function are normalized:
 - `vel(v)`: Penalizes lower translational speeds to encourage faster motion.
 - `dir(v)`: Measures angular deviation between candidate and current robot velocity, penalizing rapid direction changes.
 - `clear(v)`: Evaluates minimum distance to the nearest obstacle along a predicted trajectory, penalizing proximity to obstacles.
 - `head(v)`: Measures deviation between the robot's current motion direction and the target direction, encouraging goal alignment.
 - The weights `α, β, γ, ζ` are dynamically adjusted by the RL module, allowing adaptation to various scenarios by prioritizing different aspects of navigation.

2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Module:**
 - The navigation problem is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP).
 - **Observation (`o_t`):** Comprises the robot's task state (`s_t`), including current deformation (`δ_t`), normalized current velocity, normalized displacement vector to the goal, and `N` distances to obstacles from laser scan data (`l_t`).
 - **Action (`a_t`):** The RL agent outputs a continuous action vector including the DWA weighting factors (`α, β, γ, ζ`, ranging), the rotational velocity around the robot's x-axis (`ω_x`), and the deformation velocities along the three principal axes (`˙δ_t = [˙δx, ˙δy, ˙δz]`).
 - **Reward (`R_t`):** A dense reward function is defined as `R_t = R_def + R_stuck + R_outside + R_goal`.
 - `R_def`: Encourages maximization of robot deformation while ensuring no collisions (volume is zero if collision occurs).
 - `R_stuck`: Penalizes the robot for lack of progress or backward movement.
 - `R_outside`: Penalizes the robot for exiting the designated navigation space.
 - `R_goal`: Provides a positive reward upon reaching the target.
 - **Algorithm:** The Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm is employed due to its sample efficiency, convergence properties, and stability, chosen over Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) after comparative evaluation.

**C. Evaluation Metrics**

Two performance metrics were defined:
- **Path completion percentage (`p%`):** The percentage of the total path length successfully traversed by the robot.
- **Occupied volume percentage (`V%`):** The average ratio of the robot's occupied volume to the volume of the currently observed vessel segment over an episode. This metric is set to zero upon collision.

**D. Experimental Evaluation**

The approach was evaluated in a simulated vascular network.
- **Navigation Task:** The robot starts from a fixed position and navigates through sequences of sparse waypoints to reach a final target, adapting its motion and shape.
- **Sensing Resolution:** Models were trained and tested with three input sizes for laser scan readings: N = 15, 25, and 50.
- **Baselines:** Performance was compared against a pure RL SAC approach (predicts velocities directly) and a model-based controller (computes Delaunay triangulation and Voronoi graph for path, extracts deformation from vessel cross-section).
- **Training:** Involved random selection of intermediate targets, a grid search for optimal hyperparameters, and 1 × 10^6 training steps across 5 runs.
- **Testing:** Conducted on both training and unseen target sequences (6 final locations in total), with 20 trials per path, totaling 1080 experimental trials.
- **Additional Analyses:**
 - **Inference Time:** Measured mean inference time per control step for both RL and DWA components.
 - **Robustness:** Gaussian noise of varying standard deviations (low, moderate, high) was injected into laser scan data, and performance was evaluated across all sensor resolutions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of the 3D RL-DWA framework produced several findings regarding its training performance, navigation capabilities in known and unseen environments, computational efficiency, and robustness to sensor noise.

**A. Training Performance**
- **Superiority of Hybrid Approach:** The 3D RL-DWA consistently achieved higher mean episode rewards, faster convergence, and greater stability during training compared to the pure RL baseline across all tested sensing conditions (N=15, 25, 50).
- **Impact of Sensing Resolution:**
 - 3D RL-DWA with N=50 and N=25 showed rapid reward increases, converging to high values (5657 ± 344 and 5278 ± 438, respectively). N=50 exhibited narrower variance, indicating more consistent convergence.
 - The N=15 variant stabilized at a lower reward (3285 ± 462), suggesting that overly sparse perception limits navigation quality.
 - Quantitatively, the Area Under the Curve (AUC) for N=50 (2.12 × 10^6 ± 0.23 × 10^6) and N=25 (1.98 × 10^6 ± 0.23 × 10^6) was significantly higher than for N=15 (1.17 × 10^6 ± 0.21 × 10^6), confirming that richer sensing contributes to faster and more stable reward accumulation.
- **Pure RL Limitations:** Pure RL baselines achieved significantly lower rewards (e.g., N=50 at 1806 ± 1161) with slower growth rates and high variability, suggesting difficulty in learning effective navigation policies without the DWA component.

**B. Performance in Known and Unseen Scenarios**
- **Overall Performance:** 3D RL-DWA controllers demonstrated the most favorable balance between navigation reliability (path completion) and adaptive deformation (occupied volume) in both known (training) and unseen (test) scenarios.
- **Known Scenarios (Fig. 6a):**
 - **3D RL-DWA (N=50):** Achieved robust navigation with path completion rates near 100% (Mdn: 99.28%) and high reproducibility. Deformation was around 57%.
 - **3D RL-DWA (N=25):** Delivered comparable deformation (around 57%) but with lower path completion (Mdn: 79.26%) and higher variability.
 - **3D RL-DWA (N=15):** Showed acceptable deformation (Mdn: 49.87%) but further reduced path completion (Mdn: 73.28%) and increased dispersion.
 - **Pure RL Baselines:** Performed substantially worse across all metrics, with limited path completion (e.g., N=50 Mdn: 57.06%, N=15 Mdn: 26.81%) and reduced deformation, particularly at lower resolutions.
 - **Model-Based (N=50):** Achieved high deformation (Mdn: 60.36%) but limited navigation success (Mdn: 43.77%). It consistently failed at lower resolutions (N=15, N=25).
- **Unseen Scenarios (Fig. 6b):**
 - **Generalization:** The 3D RL-DWA (N=50) generalized effectively, maintaining near-perfect path completion (Mdn: 99.22%) and stable deformation (Mdn: 53.22%).
 - **Reduced Resolutions:** N=25 maintained comparable deformation (Mdn: 52.8%) but with reduced path completion (Mdn: 75.22%). N=15 showed non-negligible deformation (Mdn: 50.98%) but further decreased completion (Mdn: 71.37%) and high variance.
 - **Baselines in Unseen Scenarios:** Pure RL degraded significantly, with completion rates dropping to 45.75% for N=50 and very limited deformation. The model-based controller (N=50) showed similar performance as in known scenarios, with high deformation but limited path completion, and failed at lower resolutions.

**C. Inference Time**
- **Real-time Suitability:** The 3D RL-DWA achieved fast inference times, consistently below 2 ms per control step, indicating its suitability for real-time applications.
- **Component Analysis:** The RL module's inference time was constant regardless of sensor input size (N). The DWA module's inference time increased with N, due to the computational complexity of evaluating the cost function (particularly the `clear` term) for each velocity candidate, with a per-cycle time complexity of `O(M^k * N * P)`.

**D. Robustness under Uncertainties**
- **Path Completion Degradation:** Increasing sensor noise negatively impacted path completion across all sensor resolutions. N=25 showed the mildest decline (approx. 15% reduction from low to high noise), while N=15 experienced a steeper drop (nearly 30% reduction). N=50, despite a larger drop than N=25 (approx. 17%), maintained the highest overall completion rates at all noise levels.
- **Occupied Volume Stability:** The occupied volume metric exhibited high stability under noise, with variations remaining below 4% across all resolutions and noise levels. This suggests that the deformation control mechanism is more robust to sensor perturbations than the navigation component.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of autonomous robotics by introducing a novel hybrid navigation framework, 3D RL-DWA, that combines the adaptive capabilities of Reinforcement Learning with the structured guidance of the Dynamic Window Approach. This integration addresses critical limitations observed in pure learning-based and purely model-based navigation strategies, particularly for high-DoF robots operating in complex and constrained 3D environments.

The key significance of this work lies in demonstrating that the hybrid design successfully balances adaptability with efficient, real-time velocity optimization. The framework consistently achieved high path completion rates and effective deformation control, even under challenging conditions of sparse sensory data and sensor noise. This performance exceeds that of a pure RL baseline, which suffered from unstable policies and poor generalization, and a model-based solution that lacked robustness under sparse sensory inputs. The observed low inference times (consistently below 2 ms per control step) further underscore the practical applicability of the 3D RL-DWA for real-time deployment.

The potential impact of this research is substantial, particularly for emerging applications involving microrobots in biomedical contexts. The ability to perform adaptive 3D local navigation and shape optimization in highly constrained environments, such as vascular networks, is crucial for developing advanced minimally invasive interventions, targeted drug delivery, and diagnostic procedures. The framework’s demonstrated robustness to noise and efficacy with sparse sensing are highly relevant for real-world scenarios where sensor data may be limited or unreliable.

Beyond biomedical microrobotics, the principles established in this work could be generalized to other high-DoF robotic systems operating in complex 3D spaces, such as manipulators in cluttered industrial environments or inspection robots in infrastructure. The findings suggest a promising direction for designing more reliable and adaptable autonomous systems that can dynamically respond to environmental changes.

Future work outlined by the authors includes adapting this control strategy to real-world magnetically actuated ferrofluidic microrobots, replacing simulated laser scans with 3D reconstructions from imaging modalities (e.g., ultrasound or OCT), and integrating the local planning framework with global path planning methods to enable end-to-end autonomous navigation. These steps could pave the way for the realization of sophisticated, self-navigating micro-robotic platforms with significant implications for healthcare and other industries.
