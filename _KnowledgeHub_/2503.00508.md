# HGDiffuser: Efficient Task-Oriented Grasp Generation via Human-Guided Grasp Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> HGDiffuser, developed by researchers at Southern University of Science and Technology, presents a framework for efficiently generating 6-DoF parallel-jaw task-oriented grasps for robots by integrating human demonstration constraints directly into a diffusion model's sampling process. This approach achieves an 81.21% success rate in simulated task-oriented grasping, reducing inference time by 81.26% compared to prior two-stage methods.

## Problem

- Existing demonstration-based task-oriented grasping (TOG) methods use a two-stage process: extensive task-agnostic grasp sampling followed by filtering with human-derived constraints.
- This two-stage approach suffers from significant inefficiencies due to the vast 6-DoF grasp space requiring exhaustive sampling, leading to prolonged inference times.
- There is a risk of sampling failure if desired task-compliant grasps are sparsely represented or missed in the initial candidate pool.

## Method

- **HGDiffuser** develops a single-stage task-oriented grasp generation framework by directly integrating human-derived constraints into a diffusion model's guided sampling process.
- It utilizes a DiT (==Diffusion Transformer==) block-based architecture as the feature backbone for its Noise Conditional Score Network (NCSN), enhancing feature fusion of object point clouds, grasp representations, and noise steps.
- Explicit task-oriented constraints (hand-object contact regions and human wrist orientations) are incorporated via a non-network-based, differentiable loss function that guides the diffusion model during inference without requiring task-specific training data.

## Results

- HGDiffuser achieved an **81.21%** task-oriented grasping success rate in simulation, which is **9.74%** higher than the best two-stage baseline (RTAGrasp-MV's **71.47%**).
- The method significantly reduced inference time to **0.191** seconds for one grasp sample, representing an **81.26%** reduction compared to RTAGrasp-MV's **1.019** seconds.
- An ablation study demonstrated that DiT blocks improved task-agnostic grasp success by **9.3%** (from **71.35%** to **80.65%**) compared to an MLP-based backbone, while maintaining comparable inference speed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating task-oriented constraints directly into a diffusion model's sampling process, rather than as a post-filtering step, drastically improves efficiency and robustness for task-oriented grasp generation.
> - Diffusion Transformer (DiT) blocks effectively enhance feature fusion in diffusion models for complex geometric tasks, leading to higher quality grasp predictions compared to MLP-based backbones.
> - Using a differentiable, non-network-based loss function for guidance allows for flexible and interpretable integration of human demonstration constraints without needing large-scale, costly task-oriented training datasets.
