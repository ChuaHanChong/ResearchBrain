# Don't Waste Mistakes: Leveraging Negative RL-Groups via Confidence Reweighting

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Likelihood Estimation with Negative Samples (LENS), a method that enhances Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) by deriving a confidence-weighted penalty from a maximum likelihood objective, allowing large language models to learn from previously discarded incorrect responses. LENS consistently improves performance on the MATH benchmark, particularly for harder problems, yielding gains of up to 2.54 Pass@1 and 2.64 Pass@16 percentage points for a Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct model.

## Problem

- Existing RLVR methods like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) discard 'negative groups' (batches of responses where none are correct), leading to wasted computation and reduced learning signals.
- Negative groups constitute a substantial portion of samples (up to 45% early in training), representing a major inefficiency, especially when models struggle with challenging problems.
- Traditional GRPO provides zero advantage for entirely incorrect groups, failing to offer fine-grained feedback or encourage exploration on difficult items.

## Method

- A theoretical framework is developed that reformulates policy learning as a ==Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)== problem for a reward model, connecting it to policy gradients.
- ==LENS== introduces a =='calibrated reward'== that assigns a non-zero, confidence-dependent penalty to incorrect responses, even within negative groups, turning wasted computation into a learning signal.
- The method integrates as a plug-and-play modification into GRPO, using ==length-normalized probabilities== for confidence and estimating a question difficulty factor to generate confidence-weighted advantages.

## Results

- LENS consistently outperforms the GRPO baseline across all evaluation metrics and models (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen-2.5-3B-Base) on the MATH benchmark.
- Performance gains are most pronounced on harder subsets of the MATH benchmark (Levels 4-5), demonstrating LENS's ability to foster exploration and improve on problems where the baseline saturates.
- LENS achieved Pass@1 improvements from **54.09%** to **56.63%** and Pass@16 from **72.70%** to **75.34%** for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, indicating both single-shot accuracy and broader quality improvements in generated responses.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Policy learning can be expressed as a statistical task of estimating a reward probability function, whose MLE gradient naturally provides a confidence-weighted learning signal for both correct and incorrect answers.
> - A model's confidence in its incorrect predictions provides a valuable signal; highly confident wrong answers should incur a larger penalty to encourage exploration and better policy calibration.
> - Leveraging even purely negative groups significantly contributes to performance gains, particularly on challenging reasoning tasks where initial correct answers are scarce.
