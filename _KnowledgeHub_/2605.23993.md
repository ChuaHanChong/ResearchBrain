# Nano World Models: A Minimalist Implementation of Future Video Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> Nano World Models introduces a minimalist, open-source PyTorch framework for future video prediction, centered on diffusion forcing, to enable systematic study of world model design choices. The framework unifies diverse generative objectives, architectures, action-conditioning, and latent spaces, demonstrating the impact of these choices on prediction fidelity and planning success across various environments.

## Problem

- The broader research community lacks compact, reproducible, and extensible implementations for systematically studying world model design.
- The existing research landscape for world models is fragmented across diverse datasets, training recipes, and evaluation protocols, complicating rigorous scientific study.
- Industry-scale world models, while advanced, are often inaccessible, making it difficult to understand fundamental architectural and training choices.

## Method

- Introduces **Nano World Models**, a minimalist, 'batteries-included' open-source codebase for future video prediction, unified by a ==diffusion forcing== interface.
- Provides a modular architecture supporting various ==generative objectives== (x-, ε-, v-prediction, flow-matching), architectural scales (40M to 830M parameters), ==action-conditioning mechanisms== (AdaLN, FiLM, cross-attention), and latent observation spaces (VAE, Web-DINO, V-JEPA).
- Offers a shared interface for diverse environments and datasets (D4RL, DeepMind Control, CS:GO, RT-1) and supports long-horizon generation and downstream applications like 3D scene generation and goal-conditioned planning via MPC.

## Results

- Increasing model size from NanoWM-S/2 (**39.8M** parameters) to NanoWM-L/2 (**~460M**) consistently improved visual fidelity metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID) on RT-1.
- On the RT-1 dataset, x-prediction achieved the best reconstruction metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), while v-prediction delivered the best FID score.
- The SD-VAE latent space enabled **25.0%** planning success for goal-reaching on the PushT environment, while Web-DINO and V-JEPA 2.1 latents resulted in **0.0%** success, failing to learn action-conditioned dynamics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The effectiveness of prediction objectives and action injection mechanisms is task-dependent; for instance, v-prediction yields the best FID, while FiLM often performs best on RT-1, and additive conditioning on PushT.
> - Not all latent spaces are equally effective for downstream planning; reconstruction-oriented VAE latents enabled 25.0% planning success on PushT, while semantic-latent spaces like Web-DINO and V-JEPA 2.1 failed to learn action-conditioned dynamics for planning.
> - Long-horizon video generation preserves coarse scene geometry but accumulates fine-grained perceptual errors over time, which can be partially mitigated by increasing DDIM sampling steps.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

World models have gained prominence as a paradigm for developing predictive simulators that support generative tasks, planning, and decision-making in artificial intelligence. Recent advancements, particularly in industry-scale interactive video generation, have demonstrated capabilities in this area. However, the authors identify a gap: the broader research community often lacks access to compact, reproducible, and easily extensible implementations necessary for systematically studying the fundamental design choices inherent in modern world models.

The existing research landscape is characterized by fragmentation across diverse datasets, varied training recipes, inconsistent evaluation protocols, and a multitude of downstream tasks, which complicates rigorous scientific study. While industry-scale models showcase advanced visual effects, they are often built upon established techniques such as video diffusion, diffusion forcing, and consistency distillation. The authors propose that as world model algorithms stabilize, research should shift from inventing new techniques to developing a nuanced understanding of architectural choices, training objectives, and data composition.

This work positions itself as a response to this fragmentation by offering a minimalist, open-source framework. It contrasts with larger, often closed or resource-intensive industry efforts (e.g., Google DeepMind's Genie 3, LingBot-World) and complements other open-source initiatives (e.g., StableWM, Jasmine) by specifically focusing on a compact PyTorch implementation centered on diffusion-forcing for future video prediction. The paper emphasizes modularity to enable controlled experimentation, distinguishing itself from platforms that might focus on data collection, specific algorithms, or computational efficiency. By providing a unified interface for various components, Nano World Models aims to facilitate a more scientific and reproducible approach to world model research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce Nano World Models, a minimalist, "batteries-included" codebase for future video prediction that is centered around the concept of diffusion forcing. The overarching motivation is to address the difficulty faced by the broader research community in conducting systematic and controlled studies of world model design choices, given the prevalent fragmentation of implementations, datasets, and evaluation methods.

Specific motivations and objectives include:

- **Bridging the Gap:** To provide a usable starting point for researchers to understand and experiment with world models, countering the disparity between the observed capabilities of industry-scale models and the practical accessibility for academic research.
- **Enabling Scientific Study:** To facilitate a careful and scientific approach to world-model design by offering a framework where architectural choices, training objectives, data composition, and scaling behaviors can be systematically investigated. The framework aims to disentangle these components, which are often integrated in other implementations.
- **Unified Interface:** To develop a unified interface that supports diverse generative modeling and prediction objectives (e.g., Diffusion with x, ε, v-prediction; Flow-Matching). This allows different objectives to be studied as controlled experimental variables without altering the core pipeline.
- **Modular Architecture:** To support architectures of varying sizes (NanoWM-S, B, L, XL) and different action-conditioning mechanisms (element-wise addition, AdaLN, FiLM, cross-attention), providing flexibility for scaling and interaction studies.
- **Diverse Environments and Data:** To support a wide range of environments, from simple simulations (D4RL, DeepMind Control Suite) to game simulations (CS:GO) and real-robot manipulation data (RT-1), using a shared dataset and environment interface. This enables comparative studies across different domains.
- **Reproducibility and Open Science:** To promote reproducible research by open-sourcing the entire project, including code, configurations, evaluation scripts, and pretrained checkpoints. This commitment aims to establish a common experimental ground for the world model community.
- **Long-Horizon Generation:** To enable temporally consistent long-horizon video generation beyond the training window through autoregressive and sliding-window techniques.
- **Downstream Applications:** To extend world models beyond next-state prediction by supporting applications such as 3D scene generation from video rollouts and goal-conditioned planning via Model Predictive Control (MPC). This highlights the utility of world models as tools for broader intelligent systems.

In summary, the motivation is to democratize world model research by providing an accessible, modular, and scientifically-oriented framework that allows for systematic exploration and comparison of design decisions, ultimately fostering a more collaborative and reproducible research environment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Nano World Models is designed as a minimalist and modular framework, unifying various components of future video prediction around the concept of diffusion forcing.

#### 3.1. Unification through Diffusion Forcing

The core design principle is to use diffusion forcing as a unified interface. Diffusion forcing extends generative models that produce samples through iterative computation (like diffusion models that denoise corrupted samples or flow-matching models that learn a vector field). It achieves this by allowing different frames in a trajectory to occupy varying stages of the generation process, dictated by a noise index set $K$. By adjusting the noise schedule for this set, the framework can implement teacher-forced prediction, masked future prediction, and autoregressive rollout using the same model interface.

#### 3.2. Generative Objectives

The framework supports multiple generative objectives under the diffusion-forcing interface.
- **Diffusion Objectives:** Includes x-prediction, ε-prediction, and v-prediction targets.
- **Flow-Matching Objectives:** The model predicts the velocity field derived from a chosen interpolant.
The architecture, conditioning interface, dataset loader, and sampling code remain consistent across these objectives, facilitating controlled studies of objective choices.

#### 3.3. Nano World Models Architecture

- **Backbone:** A transformer backbone processes latent video tokens. For VAE-style encodings, frames are divided into spatial patches, projected into a hidden dimension, and processed by transformer blocks employing interleaved spatial-temporal attention.
- **Architectural Sizes:** Four variants are supported: NanoWM-S (40M parameters), NanoWM-B (160M), NanoWM-L (600M), and NanoWM-XL (830M). These models also vary by latent patch size (e.g., NanoWM-B/2, B/4, B/8).
- **Action Conditioning:** To incorporate actions ($a_{T:T+H-1}$) into predictions, several mechanisms are supported:
 - **Element-wise Addition:** Actions are embedded and added to corresponding frame tokens.
 - **Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN):** Actions modulate layer normalization.
 - **AdaLN fused with timestep injection:** Combines AdaLN with timestep conditioning.
 - **FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation):** Actions modulate features through affine transformations.
 - **Cross-attention:** Video tokens attend to action tokens.

#### 3.4. Latent Observation Spaces

Nano World Models predicts encoded observations rather than raw pixel data. The choice of latent space is treated as an experimental variable due to its influence on visual fidelity, planning, and representation quality.
- **VAE (Variational AutoEncoder):** Reconstruction-oriented space (e.g., Stable Diffusion VAE latents) that can be decoded to RGB frames.
- **Web-DINO:** Self-supervised representation emphasizing semantic and geometric information.
- **V-JEPA 2.1 (Video-Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture):** Video-pretrained representation designed for predictive visual features.
Supporting these distinct latent spaces under a shared training interface allows for comparative analysis of reconstruction-oriented versus representation-oriented world modeling.

#### 3.5. Datasets and Environment Interface

A shared interface manages diverse sequential data sources, exposing observation sequences, optional action sequences, frame windows, and metadata. This enables the same modeling code to operate across:
- **Simple Simulation Environments:** D4RL (e.g., Maze, Wall), DeepMind Control Suite (e.g., PushT, Rope, Granular).
- **Game Simulation:** CS:GO dataset.
- **Robot Manipulation:** RT-1 dataset.

#### 3.6. Long-Horizon Rollouts

Although trained on finite windows, the framework leverages diffusion forcing for long-horizon generation. This is achieved through sliding window and autoregressive generation, where previously generated frames serve as context for predicting subsequent frames, maintaining temporal consistency.

#### 3.7. Logging, Evaluation, and Reproducibility

The system incorporates:
- **Logging:** Supports Tensorboard and Wandb for training curves, validation metrics, and qualitative prediction panels.
- **Evaluation:** Fixed-seed reproducible validation, per-step checkpointing, and standardized evaluation scripts.
- **Reproducibility:** Open-sources all code, configurations, and pretrained checkpoints across supported environments and design choices.

#### 3.8. World-Modeling as Tool-Use

Nano World Models supports downstream applications:
- **3D Scene Generation:** Provides an export interface for generated rollouts. Decoded RGB frames and metadata can be fed into external 3D reconstruction pipelines (e.g., multi-view depth/camera estimation) to create persistent 3D representations like point clouds.
- **Goal-Conditioned Planning:** Exposes the world model as a batched rollout function for Model Predictive Control (MPC). A planner can sample candidate action sequences, the world model predicts future trajectories in parallel, and an objective module scores rollouts against a goal, allowing for iterative refinement and execution of actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study systematically evaluated various design choices within the Nano World Models framework across different environments and tasks.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Setup

Unless otherwise specified, evaluations were conducted on 256 fixed validation clips with a seed of 42, using autoregressive sequential scheduling. For 256-resolution models, one context frame and three future frames were generated. Metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID) were computed only on generated frames. For longer videos, FVD was also used. Planning capabilities were assessed using a CEM-style MPC loop, with Success Rate as the primary metric. Diffusion models typically used 250 DDIM sampling steps.

#### 4.2. Prediction Objective Performance

Experiments on the RT-1 fractal dataset (NanoWM-B/2, additive action injection, Stable Diffusion VAE) compared different prediction targets:
- **ε-prediction** underperformed x-prediction and v-prediction in all metrics.
- **x-prediction** yielded the best reconstruction metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS).
- **v-prediction** achieved the best FID score and is the default setting in Nano World Models.
The schedules (cosine + ZTSNR for x/v, linear for ε) were chosen to reflect best practices for each target.

#### 4.3. Architectural Scale

Scaling experiments on RT-1 showed a consistent trend:
- Increasing model size from NanoWM-S/2 (39.8M parameters) to NanoWM-B/2 (158.6M) and NanoWM-L/2 (~460M) monotonically improved all visual fidelity metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID). This indicates that larger models generally achieve better prediction performance.

#### 4.4. Action Injection Mechanisms

Five action-injection mechanisms were compared on RT-1 and PushT: additive, AdaLN, AdaLN-fused, FiLM, and cross-attention.
- **Task-Dependent Performance:** The optimal action injection method varied by task.
- **RT-1:** FiLM achieved the best visual fidelity (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID). Cross-attention generally performed the lowest.
- **PushT:** The simple additive mechanism was the strongest performer and offered a favorable quality-parameter trade-off compared to more complex methods.

#### 4.5. Latent Space Efficacy for Planning

A critical finding emerged from evaluating different latent spaces (SD-VAE, Web-DINO, V-JEPA 2.1) for goal-conditioned planning on PushT.
- **SD-VAE Success:** The SD-VAE checkpoint achieved a 25.0% success rate in goal-reaching episodes.
- **Semantic Latent Space Failure:** Both Web-DINO and V-JEPA 2.1 checkpoints resulted in 0.0% planning success, even with increased sampling/planning budgets.
- **Analysis of Action Conditioning:** Further diagnostics revealed that semantic-latent checkpoints did not meaningfully condition their predictions on actions. Their action embedding Root Mean Square (RMS) was nearly zero, and rollouts were almost identical under ground-truth, zero, or random actions. In contrast, the SD-VAE checkpoint showed strong sensitivity to ground-truth actions, indicating it learned controllable dynamics. This suggests that the semantic-latent diffusion objective did not sufficiently compel the model to utilize actions for counterfactual predictions.

#### 4.6. Long-Horizon Rollouts Behavior

Qualitative evaluation of long-horizon rollouts (50 frames, 46 autoregressively generated) with a CSGO checkpoint showed:
- **Coarse Preservation, Fine-grained Error:** Nano World Models preserved coarse scene geometry and camera motion. However, perceptual errors gradually accumulated in fine visual details (e.g., weapon appearance, local textures).
- **Error Accumulation:** LPIPS curves confirmed a gradual increase in prediction error during self-generated rollouts.
- **Mitigation via Sampling Steps:** Increasing DDIM sampling steps consistently reduced LPIPS across the rollout horizon, suggesting that more accurate per-frame denoising can partially mitigate compounding errors in autoregressive generation.

#### 4.7. Cross-Domain Performance

Evaluations of shipped checkpoints across diverse domains (Point Maze, Wall, Rope, Granular, PushT, RT-1) using the standardized protocol demonstrated:
- **Generalizability of Recipe:** The same training and evaluation recipe was applicable across navigation, tabletop pushing, deformable manipulation, and real-robot data.
- **Domain Complexity Impact:** Performance (fidelity metrics) was strongest on simpler simulated domains (e.g., Point Maze, Wall) and progressively decreased with increasing visual or dynamic complexity (e.g., Rope, Granular, RT-1).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Nano World Models holds significance for the field of embodied AI and generative modeling due to its focus on reproducibility, modularity, and enabling systematic scientific inquiry.

- **Facilitating Reproducible Research:** By open-sourcing its complete codebase, configurations, data, and pretrained model checkpoints, Nano World Models directly addresses the challenge of reproducibility in world model research. This comprehensive release allows researchers to replicate experiments, build upon existing work, and validate findings, contributing to a more robust scientific process.
- **Enabling Controlled Studies of Design Choices:** The framework's modular design, which unifies various generative objectives, architectural scales, action-conditioning mechanisms, and latent spaces under a single diffusion-forcing interface, is crucial. It permits researchers to conduct controlled experiments, isolating the impact of specific design decisions. This capability is vital for moving beyond ad-hoc demonstrations towards a deeper, scientific understanding of what makes world models effective.
- **Insights into World Model Components:** The empirical findings of the paper, such as the differential performance of prediction objectives, the consistent benefits of architectural scaling, the task-dependent nature of action injection, the limitations of certain semantic latent spaces for planning, and the characteristics of error accumulation in long-horizon rollouts, provide concrete insights. These findings inform best practices and highlight critical trade-offs for future world model development.
- **Broadening Accessibility:** By providing a compact and extensible PyTorch implementation, Nano World Models makes advanced world modeling techniques more accessible to a wider research community. This lowers the barrier to entry compared to large-scale, proprietary, or computationally intensive industry implementations.
- **Advancing Downstream Applications:** The framework's support for applications like 3D scene generation from video rollouts and goal-conditioned planning demonstrates the practical utility of world models. By separating the learned dynamics model from specific planning algorithms or 3D reconstruction pipelines, it promotes flexibility and reusability, potentially accelerating research in model-based reinforcement learning and 3D content creation.
- **Fostering Community Collaboration:** The stated mission to build a "Babel tower for world model research" by standardizing language and tools for datasets, objectives, architectures, and tasks aims to foster a collaborative environment. This could accelerate collective progress by allowing researchers globally to contribute to and build upon a shared experimental foundation.

Overall, Nano World Models contributes a foundational tool and a set of empirical insights that can guide the systematic development and understanding of world models, thereby impacting future research in video prediction, planning, and embodied artificial intelligence.
