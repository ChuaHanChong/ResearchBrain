# Detect Anything via Next Point Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the International Digital Economy Academy (IDEA) introduced Rex-Omni, a 3-billion-parameter Multimodal Large Language Model capable of unifying various visual perception tasks. The model achieves state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance across 11 diverse benchmarks by integrating robust language understanding with precise object localization.

## Problem

- Traditional regression-based object detectors, despite their localization precision, possess limited language understanding for complex or nuanced textual descriptions.
- Existing MLLM-based detectors often suffer from low recall rates, coordinate misalignment, and duplicate predictions due to geometric discretization of continuous coordinates and limitations of supervised fine-tuning (SFT).
- A need existed for a single, versatile MLLM to handle a broad range of visual perception tasks with both deep language comprehension and accurate spatial localization.

## Method

- ==Rex-Omni==, a **3B**-parameter MLLM, unifies diverse visual perception tasks under a ==direct coordinate prediction framework== using a quantized relative coordinate representation with special tokens.
- A two-stage training paradigm is employed, combining initial ==Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)== with ==GRPO (Group-based Reinforcement Learning with Policy Optimization)== for precision and behavioral correctness.
- Custom-designed data engines (Grounding, Referring, Pointing, OCR) generate **13.1 million** high-quality samples, augmenting **8.9 million** public datasets, to ensure extensive and fine-grained spatial and language supervision.

## Results

- Rex-Omni achieves superior or competitive F1-scores across **11** diverse visual perception tasks, often outperforming other MLLMs and traditional open-set detectors in zero-shot settings.
- The GRPO post-training stage dramatically mitigates common MLLM failures, reducing duplicate predictions (e.g., SFT removal gain **1.23-15.3%** vs. GRPO **0.06-0.08%**) and large-box predictions (**20.5%** for SFT vs. **3.5%** for GRPO).
- The model demonstrates unique generalization capabilities in complex tasks like layout grounding, OCR, and spatial pointing, outperforming previous specialized models and MLLMs in interpreting and grounding nuanced spatial relationships.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Quantizing relative coordinates and representing them with dedicated special tokens significantly reduces the learning complexity and improves token efficiency for MLLM-based object detection.
> - GRPO-based reinforcement post-training is effective in correcting SFT-induced behavioral deficiencies, such as duplicate and overly large predictions, by providing geometry-aware rewards and encouraging policy exploration.
> - High-quality, large-scale synthetic data generated by specialized engines is crucial for training MLLMs to achieve fine-grained spatial reasoning and robust language grounding across various perception tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of object detection has a rich history, evolving from early Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)-based models like YOLO and Faster R-CNN to more recent Transformer-based architectures such as DETR and DINO. These traditional "regression-based" detectors have achieved remarkable success in precise object localization, largely through continuous, geometry-aware loss functions. A significant trend in recent years has been the development of "open-set" or "open-vocabulary" object detection, where models (e.g., Grounding DINO) leverage pre-trained text encoders to identify arbitrary categories not seen during training, moving beyond closed-set classifications.

Despite these advancements, traditional detectors, even open-set ones, are fundamentally limited by their relatively shallow language understanding. They often struggle with complex semantic descriptions, as their primary mechanism for integrating language is through text encoders that provide category-level representations.

In parallel, Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have emerged as a promising avenue. By integrating the powerful language comprehension of large language models (LLMs) with visual input, MLLMs possess a deeper understanding of natural language prompts. Approaches like Pix2Seq have reframed object detection as a next-token prediction task, where bounding box coordinates are represented as discrete tokens. However, existing MLLM-based methods have largely failed to match the localization precision of traditional regression-based detectors. Common issues include low recall rates, coordinate misalignment, and the generation of duplicate predictions, often attributed to the inherent challenges of discrete classification for continuous spatial data and the limitations of supervised fine-tuning (SFT).

Rex-Omni positions itself at this critical juncture. It aims to bridge the performance gap between the strong language understanding of MLLMs and the precise localization capabilities of traditional detectors. The work directly addresses the known limitations of MLLM-based object detection, seeking to create a unified framework that combines both robust language comprehension and accurate visual perception across a diverse range of tasks. By tackling the core issues of coordinate representation and training paradigms, Rex-Omni seeks to advance MLLMs beyond being merely capable of high-level image understanding to becoming truly precise, general-purpose visual perception systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Rex-Omni is to develop a 3-billion-parameter MLLM that achieves state-of-the-art object perception performance, effectively unifying precise localization with robust language understanding. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and specific objectives:

1. **Overcome Limitations of Traditional Detectors:** Traditional regression-based detectors, while excellent at localization, possess limited language understanding. This restricts their ability to interpret complex, nuanced textual descriptions or generalize to novel, semantically rich visual queries. Rex-Omni aims to leverage the inherent linguistic prowess of MLLMs to address this.
2. **Address Challenges in MLLM-based Detection:** Existing MLLMs, despite their language capabilities, often suffer from critical issues in visual perception, including low recall, coordinate misalignment, and duplicate predictions. The paper motivates Rex-Omni by arguing that these problems stem from two fundamental sources:
 - **Geometric Discretization:** Treating continuous coordinate prediction as a discrete classification task (using cross-entropy loss) makes MLLMs less sensitive to small geometric offsets compared to regression-based models using continuous, geometry-aware losses (e.g., L1, IoU). This hinders precise localization.
 - **Behavioral Regulation Deficiency:** The "teacher-forced" nature of Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) prevents MLLMs from learning to regulate their own output structure during inference. This leads to common errors like missed detections or spurious duplicate predictions.
3. **Achieve Versatility and Unification:** A core motivation is to create a single MLLM capable of handling a broad array of visual perception tasks, including object detection, object referring, visual prompting, GUI grounding, layout grounding, OCR, pointing, keypointing, and spatial referring. This unification under a single coordinate prediction framework simplifies model development and promotes generalist capabilities.
4. **Enhance Data Quality and Scale:** Recognizing that learning fine-grained coordinate mapping and complex language grounding requires vast amounts of high-quality data, the authors are motivated to develop automated data generation engines to overcome the limitations of existing public datasets in terms of scale and semantic richness.

In essence, Rex-Omni's motivation is to elevate MLLMs from strong language comprehenders to equally proficient and versatile visual perception agents, bridging the performance gap with specialized detectors while offering unparalleled language-driven flexibility.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Rex-Omni, a 3B-parameter MLLM, is built upon the Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct model, with minimal architectural modifications, primarily focused on adapting it for relative coordinate representation. Its methodology is structured around three core design principles: Task Formulation, Data Engines, and Training Pipelines.

#### 3.1. Task Formulation

The model unifies diverse visual perception tasks under a **direct coordinate prediction framework**, where each task is formulated as generating a sequence of coordinates.
- **Coordinate Representation:** Instead of using absolute coordinates or representing them with multiple atomic tokens, Rex-Omni adopts a **quantized relative coordinate representation with special tokens**. Coordinate values (0-999) are mapped to dedicated special tokens within the LLM's vocabulary. This significantly reduces learning complexity, confines the classification range, and drastically improves token efficiency (e.g., a bounding box takes only 4 special tokens instead of 15 atomic tokens).
- **Input Format:** A unified text-based interface is used, where tasks are expressed as natural language queries. For **visual prompts**, bounding boxes are converted into quantized coordinate tokens and embedded into the query, allowing the model to reason about visual correspondence through language.
- **Output Format:** Outputs are structured token sequences: `<|object_ref_start|>PHRASE<|object_ref_end|><|box_start|>COORDS<|box_end|>`. `COORDS` varies based on the task: `[x0, y0, x1, y1]` for boxes, `[x0, y0]` for points, `[x0, y0, x1, y1...]` for polygons, and a structured JSON for keypoints.

#### 3.2. Data Engines

To provide the extensive and high-quality supervision needed for fine-grained spatial reasoning and language grounding, Rex-Omni leverages both publicly available datasets (8.9 million samples) and custom-designed data engines that generate an additional 13.1 million samples, totaling 22 million training samples.
- **Grounding Data Engine:** Generates image captions using Qwen2.5-VL-7B, extracts noun phrases via SpaCy, and crucially, **filters phrases** to remove descriptive attributes (e.g., "green lemon" becomes "lemon") to mitigate ambiguity for grounding models. DINO-X is then used to ground the filtered phrases. Images are sourced from COYO and SA-1B.
- **Referring Data Engine:** Generates semantically rich referring expressions (e.g., "a man in a yellow shirt") for objects using Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Molmo is then used to predict a spatial point for the expression, and SAM generates segmentation masks for ground-truth boxes. Points are associated with masks to link expressions to object regions. Images from O365, OpenImages, and the Grounding Data Engine.
- **Pointing Data Engine:** Derives point annotations from existing box-level supervision. SAM generates masks, and the intersection of diagonals of the mask's minimum-area enclosing rotated rectangle serves as the point if it falls within the mask.
- **OCR Data Engine:** Uses PaddleOCR to annotate images with polygonal text boundaries and transcriptions, and then computes bounding box representations. Images are from the COYO dataset.

#### 3.3. Training Pipelines

A **two-stage training paradigm** is employed to teach basic coordinate prediction and then refine it for precision and behavioral correctness.
- **Stage 1: Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** The model is initially trained on the full 22 million annotated samples using standard cross-entropy loss. This teacher-guided approach enables the model to acquire the fundamental ability to interpret and predict quantized spatial coordinates. Online strategies are used to construct diverse conversation templates, multi-phrase queries, and visual prompting scenarios.
- **Stage 2: GRPO-based Reinforcement Post-training:** This stage addresses the geometric discretization and behavioral regulation deficiencies of SFT.
 - **GRPO (Group-based Reinforcement Learning with Policy Optimization):** The model samples groups of complete responses. For each output, a scalar reward is computed and normalized into an advantage signal, which guides policy updates. A KL penalty prevents excessive divergence from the SFT model.
 - **Geometry-aware Rewards:**
 - **Box IoU Reward:** For bounding box tasks, an F1-score-style reward is computed based on IoU and category match between predicted and ground-truth boxes, balancing precision and recall.
 - **Point-in-Mask Reward:** For pointing tasks, a reward of 1 is given if a predicted point falls within the SAM-generated mask of the correct category, otherwise 0. F1-score is derived.
 - **Point-in-Box Reward:** Specifically for GUI Grounding, a binary reward (1 or 0) indicates if the predicted point falls within the target GUI element's ground-truth bounding box.
 - This reinforcement phase allows the model to explore its own output space, receiving continuous geometric feedback and learning to avoid undesirable behaviors like duplicate or overly large predictions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Rex-Omni demonstrates remarkable performance across a diverse suite of visual perception tasks, often setting new benchmarks or achieving highly competitive results in zero-shot settings.

- **Overall Performance:** Rex-Omni consistently achieves superior or competitive F1-scores compared to other MLLMs (e.g., SEED1.5-VL, Qwen2.5-VL, DeepSeek-VL2) and often outperforms traditional open-set detectors (e.g., Grounding DINO-SwinT) across 11 distinct tasks, showcasing its versatility and robustness.
- **Common Object Detection (COCO):** In a zero-shot setting, Rex-Omni surpasses existing MLLMs and even some regression-based models (Grounding DINO-SwinT, DINO-R50) at an IoU threshold of 0.5. At a stricter IoU of 0.95, it remains strong but marginally lags some specialized detectors, suggesting MLLMs still have room for improvement in extreme pixel-level precision.
- **Long-tailed Object Detection (LVIS):** Rex-Omni exhibits competitive F1 scores and achieves state-of-the-art results on the mIoU metric, underscoring MLLMs' advantage in generalizing to low-frequency categories due to stronger language understanding.
- **Dense and Tiny Object Detection (VisDrone, Dense200):** This task exposed common MLLM failure modes like large-box and duplicate predictions in the SFT-only model. Crucially, the full Rex-Omni (with GRPO) substantially mitigates these issues, demonstrating the effectiveness of the two-stage training in challenging, crowded scenarios.
- **Referring Object Detection (HumanRef, RefCOCOg):** MLLMs, including Rex-Omni, significantly outperform traditional open-set detectors due to their advanced language reasoning. Rex-Omni achieves highly competitive results, second only to larger MLLMs like SEED1.5-VL.
- **Visual Prompting (FSC147, COCO, LVIS, Dense200):** Rex-Omni shows strong visual prompting capabilities, particularly in dense and long-tailed contexts, demonstrating its ability to detect objects based on visual examples rather than just text.
- **Object Pointing (COCO, LVIS, Dense200, VisDrone, RefCOCOg, HumanRef):** Rex-Omni achieves the highest F1-scores across these benchmarks, proving its superior ability to localize objects via precise point predictions.
- **GUI Grounding (ScreenSpot-V2, ScreenSpot-Pro):** Among 3B-parameter models, Rex-Omni delivers the highest accuracy, showcasing excellent fine-grained UI element localization driven by natural language queries.
- **Layout Grounding (DocLayNet, M6Doc):** Rex-Omni significantly outperforms other MLLMs, demonstrating unique generalization capabilities to unseen document structures and layout categories, a key advantage over closed-set models.
- **OCR (HierText, ICDAR2015, TotalText, SROIE):** For bounding box outputs, Rex-Omni is competitive with or superior to specialized OCR models like PaddleOCRv5 and significantly outperforms other MLLMs. It also shows competitive performance for polygonal text detection, highlighting its versatility in text localization and recognition.
- **Spatial Pointing (RefSpatial-Bench):** Rex-Omni substantially outperforms previous proprietary and specialist models in interpreting and grounding complex spatial relationships for both location and placement tasks, including unseen scenarios.
- **Keypoint (COCO, AP10K):** Rex-Omni provides more balanced cross-domain generalization for keypoint estimation (human and animal) compared to specialized models like X-Pose, which often struggle with unseen categories.

#### 4.1. Analysis of GRPO's Effectiveness

- **Training Dynamics:** The SFT stage shows gradual improvement, but GRPO yields a rapid and substantial performance jump with minimal additional training steps, indicating it unlocks latent capabilities.
- **Behavioral Correction:** GRPO effectively mitigates **duplicate predictions** (SFT model showed +1.23% to +15.3% F1 gain after duplicate removal, GRPO only +0.06% to +0.08%) and **large-box predictions** (SFT had 20.5% large boxes, GRPO only 3.5%), which are common MLLM failure modes.
- **Coordinate Precision:** While GRPO provides modest direct gains in coordinate precision (e.g., F1@mIoU +0.5 on COCO), the analysis suggests SFT already establishes strong coordinate learning capacity. GRPO's main benefit is behavioral and consistency-oriented.
- **Likelihood of Correct Predictions:** GRPO significantly enhances the likelihood of sampling accurate and coherent predictions, especially for more complex tasks where SFT's best-case sampling still lags.

#### 4.2. Inference Efficiency

- Rex-Omni's use of special tokens for quantized coordinates dramatically improves **tokenization efficiency** (e.g., 7.6 tokens/box on COCO vs. 148.8 for SEED1.5-VL), leading to much shorter output sequences.
- **Inference speed** scales linearly with the number of predicted boxes, indicating MLLM-based detectors are currently slower than highly optimized traditional detectors, though potential for acceleration exists.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Rex-Omni represents a pivotal advancement in the development of general-purpose visual perception systems based on MLLMs. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Towards a Unified Perception System:** By successfully addressing a broad array of visual perception tasks—from basic object detection to complex referring expressions, GUI grounding, and OCR—within a single MLLM, Rex-Omni paves the way for truly unified and versatile perception agents. This contrasts with previous approaches that relied on ensembles of specialized models or MLLMs with limited accuracy in fine-grained tasks.
2. **Bridging the MLLM-Detector Performance Gap:** The work systematically tackles and largely overcomes the key limitations (localization precision, duplicate predictions, behavioral deficiencies) that previously hindered MLLMs from matching traditional regression-based detectors. This demonstrates that MLLMs can indeed achieve competitive, if not superior, performance in zero-shot detection while retaining their deep language understanding.
3. **Innovation in Training Paradigms:** The two-stage SFT+GRPO training pipeline is a crucial methodological contribution. GRPO's effectiveness in correcting SFT-induced behavioral issues and refining predictions is a significant step towards more robust and reliable MLLM-based perception. This approach offers a blueprint for enhancing future MLLMs for spatial tasks.
4. **Advancements in Data Generation:** The development of sophisticated data engines for grounding, referring, pointing, and OCR highlights the importance of high-quality, large-scale data for training advanced MLLMs. These automated generation techniques can accelerate research and development in multimodal AI.
5. **Broad Application Potential:** Rex-Omni's capabilities have profound implications for various real-world applications:
 - **Robotics:** Enhanced spatial pointing and referring, along with affordance grounding, can lead to more intelligent and dexterous robotic systems.
 - **Intelligent Agents:** Improved GUI grounding enables more effective interaction with digital interfaces, from automated tasks to accessibility tools.
 - **Document Analysis:** Advanced layout grounding and OCR can revolutionize how unstructured and semi-structured documents are processed and understood.
 - **Content Understanding:** Fine-grained detection and referring capabilities enrich image and video analysis for diverse industries.
 - **Accessibility:** Precise pointing and object identification can aid visually impaired users in navigating complex visual environments.
6. **Future Research Direction:** While acknowledging limitations like inference speed, Rex-Omni outlines clear paths for future research in model acceleration, advanced reward-guided sampling, and further scaling. It establishes a strong foundation for the next generation of visual perception systems that are not only accurate but also deeply language-aware and adaptable.

In conclusion, Rex-Omni is a landmark work that substantially advances the capabilities of MLLMs in visual perception. By meticulously addressing fundamental challenges in task formulation, data generation, and training, it demonstrates the profound potential of MLLMs to define the future of versatile and intelligent vision-language systems.
