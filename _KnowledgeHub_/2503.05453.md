# Soft Policy Optimization: Online Off-Policy RL for Sequence Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Soft Policy Optimization (SPO) is an online off-policy reinforcement learning method designed for fine-tuning generative sequence models that improves training efficiency and policy diversity. It achieves an 85% speedup over PPO and consistently better `pass@10` performance, leveraging a novel cumulative Q-parameterization to unify policy and value functions.

## Problem

- On-policy reinforcement learning methods like PPO are sample inefficient for fine-tuning Large Language Models, requiring fresh data for each update.
- These methods limit exploration and can lead to a loss of diversity in generated responses, converging to single solutions.
- Computational bottlenecks arise from frequent model transfers in distributed PPO setups and the overhead of a separate value model.

## Method

- **SPO** introduces an online off-policy Soft RL framework that enables learning from arbitrary online and offline trajectories, including diverse data sources.
- It employs a "==Cumulative Q-Parameterization==" which unifies the policy and value functions, inherently satisfying Bellman and Path consistency for intermediate tokens.
- The method simplifies the learning objective by reducing it primarily to matching the terminal reward, removing the need for separate value models and complex consistency losses.

## Results

- SPO consistently outperforms PPO in `pass@10` scores (policy diversity) over 100,000 training steps on CodeContests and TACO benchmarks.
- The method achieves an **85%** speedup in wall-clock training time compared to PPO due to reduced model transfers and the elimination of a separate value model.
- SPO successfully leverages a mix of online and diverse offline data, demonstrating stable learning and performance improvements over purely online methods, and further enhances `pass@10` when using Monte Carlo Q-estimates at pivotal tokens.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The optimal policy in a KL-regularized reward maximization framework can be interpreted as a success-conditioned posterior, which inherently preserves the diversity of correct responses.
> - Parameterizing advantages as log-probability differences and Q-values as cumulative sums ensures intrinsic Bellman and Path consistency without explicit regularization.
> - The ability to stably learn from diverse off-policy data facilitates "knowledge evolution" and amortized exploration, significantly enhancing training efficiency and model robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work on Soft Policy Optimization (SPO) is situated at the intersection of Reinforcement Learning (RL), Large Language Models (LLMs), and the growing field of "Soft RL" or "RL as Inference." It directly confronts the limitations of existing RL methods, predominantly Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), when used for post-training (fine-tuning) generative sequence models like LLMs.

**Current Landscape and its Limitations:**
Traditional RL methods, especially on-policy algorithms like PPO, have been the default for LLM alignment tasks such as Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) (Ziegler et al., 2019; Ouyang et al., 2022). While effective, these methods suffer from several significant drawbacks:
- **Sample Inefficiency:** On-policy methods require new data to be collected with the *current* policy, discarding previous experiences. This means re-discovering good behaviors in each training run, which is particularly costly for complex tasks like code generation where finding a correct solution can take thousands of rollouts.
- **Exploration Difficulties:** The need for on-policy sampling limits the use of diverse decoding strategies or leveraging hard-to-discover solutions from prior runs. This hinders comprehensive exploration and can lead to policies that are less diverse or robust.
- **Computational Cost:** Asynchronous PPO implementations often require frequent and costly model transfers between trainer and worker nodes to keep the behavior policy updated, incurring significant communication overhead and slowdowns. Additionally, PPO typically relies on a separate value model, consuming substantial memory resources.
- **Loss of Diversity:** On-policy methods can converge to a single optimal behavior, potentially losing the diversity of correct responses, which is undesirable for generative models.

**SPO's Position and Contribution:**
SPO addresses these limitations by introducing a novel online off-policy Soft RL method. It positions itself as a more principled, scalable, and memory-efficient alternative to PPO for sequence models.

- **Soft RL Framework:** SPO operates within the well-established Soft RL framework (also known as RL-as-inference or maximum-entropy RL), which seeks to maximize rewards while maintaining high policy entropy (i.e., diversity). This framework underpins many modern RLHF approaches. SPO's interpretation of the optimal policy as a "success-conditioned posterior" provides a clear probabilistic foundation.
- **Off-Policy Learning:** The ability to learn from arbitrary online *and* offline trajectories (including those from previous runs, human experts, or other policies) is SPO's core distinguishing feature. This directly tackles sample inefficiency by enabling knowledge reuse and amortized exploration.
- **Unified Policy and Value Function:** A key innovation is the "Cumulative Q-Parameterization," which unifies the policy and value function. Unlike conventional Q-learning or actor-critic methods that learn these separately, SPO's approach inherently guarantees Bellman and Path consistency for intermediate tokens. This eliminates the need for complex TD or path-consistency losses at every step, simplifying the learning problem to primarily focus on matching the terminal reward.
- **Memory and Speed Efficiency:** By eliminating the need for a separate value model and significantly reducing the frequency of model transfers in asynchronous settings, SPO offers substantial memory and speed advantages over PPO.
- **Comparison to Related Works:**
 - SPO builds on the concept of KL-regularized reward maximization, similar to DPO (Direct Preference Optimization) and DRO (Offline Regularized Reinforcement Learning), but distinguishes itself by handling direct rewards rather than preferences (DPO) and being a hybrid online/offline method (unlike purely offline DRO).
 - It differs from traditional Path-Consistency Learning (Nachum et al., 2017) and Q-Learning with Transformers (Chebotar et al., 2023) because its cumulative Q-parameterization intrinsically satisfies consistency properties that those methods must learn.
 - While other works (e.g., GRPO, VinePPO) also aim to remove the value model from PPO, they generally remain on-policy, thus lacking SPO's ability to leverage diverse off-policy data.
 - Concurrent work on "Process Reward Models" (Yuan et al., Cui et al.) uses similar log-probability differences but primarily to accelerate policy gradients, not as a unified policy/value function with the same theoretical guarantees and benefits for off-policy learning demonstrated by SPO.

In essence, SPO represents a significant step towards more robust, efficient, and versatile RL fine-tuning for LLMs, moving beyond the limitations of on-policy methods by leveraging theoretical insights from Soft RL and probabilistic modeling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for developing Soft Policy Optimization (SPO) stems from addressing critical limitations in the current standard practice of using on-policy Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods, particularly PPO, for post-training Large Language Models (LLMs). The authors articulate several key objectives to overcome these challenges:

**Primary Motivation: Overcoming On-Policy Limitations for LLM Training**
The paper highlights that on-policy RL methods, while effective, are inherently ill-suited for the dynamic and data-rich environment of LLM development. Specific issues include:
- **Sample Inefficiency:** The need to generate fresh on-policy data for each gradient update leads to extreme sample inefficiency. For complex tasks like code generation, it can take "thousands of rollouts to find a correct response," only for that trajectory to be discarded after a small update. This "re-discovery" process wastes computational resources and time.
- **Limited Exploration and Diversity:** On-policy requirements restrict the use of diverse decoding strategies or the re-utilization of valuable, hard-to-discover solutions from prior training runs, human experts, or other policies. This limits the exploration capabilities of the agent and can lead to policies that lack diversity and robustness, favoring only a single path to a solution rather than understanding the breadth of correct responses.
- **Computational Bottlenecks:** Asynchronous PPO implementations, common in large-scale LLM training, suffer from frequent and costly model transfers between compute nodes to maintain policy freshness. The necessity of a separate value model also incurs significant memory overhead.

#### 2.1. Key Objectives of Soft Policy Optimization (SPO)

Based on these motivations, the authors set out to develop a new RL method with the following core objectives:

1. **Develop a Principled Off-Policy Soft RL Method:** To enable learning from *arbitrary* online and offline trajectories, breaking free from the restrictive on-policy paradigm. This is envisioned to allow LLMs to participate in a "knowledge evolution process," learning from collective experiences.
2. **Eliminate the Need for a Separate Value Model:** To improve memory efficiency and simplify the overall architecture. This objective aims to reduce the computational footprint and complexity associated with maintaining and training a distinct value function.
3. **Achieve Scalability and Speed:** By reducing model transfer frequency and memory consumption, SPO aims for significantly faster training and better utilization of distributed computing resources compared to PPO.
4. **Enhance Policy Diversity and Robustness:** To learn "soft" policies that can generate a variety of high-quality responses rather than converging to a single deterministic solution. This is crucial for generative models where multiple correct and creative solutions might exist, fostering a "deeper understanding of the domain."
5. **Provide Strong Theoretical Foundations:** To derive a method that is not only empirically effective but also grounded in solid theoretical principles, particularly within the Soft RL framework. This includes proving consistency properties of its core components.

In summary, the authors are motivated by the desire to push beyond the existing limitations of LLM fine-tuning, seeking a more efficient, flexible, and robust RL algorithm that can leverage a broader spectrum of data and better align with the generative nature of large language models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Soft Policy Optimization (SPO) introduces a novel approach to online off-policy Soft RL for sequence models, grounded in theoretical insights and designed for practical scalability. The methodology can be broken down into its theoretical setup, the core "Cumulative Q-Parameterization," and the resulting learning objectives and implementation details.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Setup and Soft RL Foundations

SPO considers a simplified Reinforcement Learning setup where an LLM policy $\pi(a|x)$ receives a prompt $x$, generates a sequence of tokens $a = a_1, \dots, a_T$, and receives a deterministic terminal reward $r(x,a) \in \{0, -1\}$ (e.g., success or failure in a coding task). There are no intermediate rewards, environment stochasticity, or discounting.

The method operates within the **KL-regularized reward maximization (Soft RL)** framework:
$L(\pi) = E_\pi[r] - \beta KL[\pi, \pi_0]$
where $\pi_0$ is a reference policy (typically the instruction-tuned LLM used for initialization), and $\beta$ controls the entropy regularization. The optimal policy $\pi^*$ in this framework is shown to be $\pi^*(a|x) = \exp(r(x,a)/\beta) \pi_0(a|x)/Z$, which can be interpreted as a success-conditioned posterior. This implies that the optimal policy preserves the diversity of correct responses present in $\pi_0$, aligning with generative modeling goals.

#### 3.2. Token-Level Policy and Value Functions

For sequence models, SPO defines token-level soft Q-values and advantages:
- **Soft Q-value ($Q_t$):** $Q_t = S_{\beta, \pi_0}[r]$ (generalized softmax operator), which represents $\beta \log p(o=1|a_{\le t}, x)$, the log-probability of success given the partial sequence $a_{\le t}$. Notably, $Q_0 = V$ (the soft value of the prompt).
- **Advantage ($A_t$):** $A_t = Q_t - Q_{t-1}$, representing the change in log-likelihood of success from appending $a_t$.
The optimal token-level policy $\pi^*(a_t|a_{<t}, x)$ is then expressed as $\exp(A_t/\beta) \pi_0(a_t|a_{<t}, x)$.

#### 3.3. The Cumulative Q-Parameterization (Core Innovation)

The central methodological contribution is the **Cumulative Q-Parameterization**, which intrinsically links the policy and value functions. For an arbitrary policy $\pi_\theta$:
1. **Advantage Estimation:** The advantage $A^\theta_t$ is *parameterized* as a difference in log-probabilities:
 $A^\theta_t \equiv \beta (\log \pi_\theta(a_t|a_{<t}, x) - \log \pi_0(a_t|a_{<t}, x))$
 This means the advantage is positive when $\pi_\theta$ assigns higher probability to $a_t$ than $\pi_0$.
2. **Q-Value Calculation:** The Q-value $Q^\theta_t$ is then computed as a cumulative sum of these advantages, plus an initial estimated Q-value for the prompt $\hat{Q}_0$:
 $Q^\theta_t \equiv \hat{Q}_0 + \sum_{t'=1}^t A^\theta_{t'}$
 The initial $\hat{Q}_0$ is estimated once before training using Monte-Carlo sampling from $\pi_0$.

#### 3.4. Theoretical Guarantees from Cumulative Q-Parameterization

This parameterization offers crucial theoretical advantages:
- **Equivalence Theorem:** $Q^\theta = Q$ if and only if $\pi^\theta = \pi^*$ and $\hat{Q}_0 = Q_0$. This proves that optimizing $Q^\theta$ implicitly optimizes $\pi^\theta$ towards the optimal policy.
- **Intrinsic Consistency:** SPO proves that the cumulative Q-function $Q^\theta_t$ *by construction* satisfies both **Bellman consistency** and **Path consistency** for all *intermediate* tokens ($0 \le t < T$). This means the typical Temporal Difference (TD) or path-consistency losses, which enforce these consistencies in other RL methods, are unnecessary for intermediate steps in SPO.
- **Simplified Learning Objective:** Due to intrinsic consistency, the learning problem reduces to enforcing consistency only at the *terminal* token: $Q^\theta_T \approx r(x,a)$.

#### 3.5. Learning Objectives and Loss Functions

SPO's training primarily focuses on making the terminal Q-value estimate $Q^\theta_T$ match the observed reward $r$. Various objectives are derived from this:

- **Terminal Q-Regression:** Directly regressing $Q^\theta_T$ towards $r$, using either a **squared loss** ($L(Q^\theta_T, r) = (Q^\theta_T - r)^2$) or a **binary cross-entropy loss** (treating $Q_t$ as a log-probability, with clipping and a modified positive term to prevent incentives for $Q_t > 0$).
- **Non-Terminal Q-Regression (Reverse-Q Targets):** Regressing $Q^\theta_t$ towards targets $R_t = \text{detach}(r - \sum_{t'=t+1}^T A^\theta_{t'})$. This provides a gradient signal to earlier tokens, accounting for subsequent action quality.
- **Advantage Regression:** Regressing $\sum A^\theta_t$ towards $r - \hat{Q}_0$, typically combined with sigmoid warping and cross-entropy loss.
- **Monte-Carlo Targets for Non-Terminal $Q_t$:** To enhance intermediate Q-value accuracy, SPO explores using Monte-Carlo estimates of $Q_t$ for "pivotal tokens" identified via Pivotal Token Search (PTS). PTS identifies tokens where the success probability changes significantly, allowing for targeted Monte-Carlo sampling.

#### 3.6. Asynchronous On- & Off-policy RL Framework

SPO is implemented within a highly scalable asynchronous distributed RL framework (similar to Meta's RLEF).
- **Worker Nodes:** Continuously generate trajectories using diverse sampling strategies (on-policy, random temperature/top-p, or from reference policy) and perform sandboxed code execution for reward evaluation.
- **Trainer Nodes:** Collect batches of trajectories (from workers and offline sources), perform training updates using the chosen loss functions, and periodically send updated model weights to workers.
- **Efficiency Gains:** SPO significantly reduces the frequency of model transfers to workers compared to PPO (e.g., every 10 batches vs. every batch), leading to substantial speedups (85% observed). This is possible because SPO is inherently off-policy and less sensitive to policy staleness.
- **Offline Data Integration:** The framework supports mixing online data from workers with diverse offline data sources (e.g., reference model samples, past SPO/PPO runs, Llama-70B CoT samples, human solutions), demonstrating true off-policy learning.

This comprehensive methodology, combining strong theoretical guarantees with a practical, scalable distributed implementation, allows SPO to address the core challenges of LLM fine-tuning effectively.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments primarily evaluate SPO against PPO on the challenging **CodeContests** and **TACO** competitive programming benchmarks, focusing on `pass@10` (a measure of policy diversity) and `pass@1` (standard success rate). The initial policy is Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct.

#### 4.1. SPO vs. PPO Performance and Efficiency

- **Superior `pass@10` Performance:** SPO consistently outperforms PPO in `pass@10` scores over 100,000 training steps. This is a critical finding, indicating that SPO learns more diverse and robust policies, aligning with the goals of Soft RL to maintain high entropy. PPO's `pass@10` performance tends to decline or stagnate, while SPO's steadily improves.
- **Diversity Preservation:** While PPO shows improvement in `pass@1` (single best solution), this often comes at the cost of `pass@10` (diversity), as the policy quickly learns to sample a single solution per problem. In contrast, SPO improves both `pass@1` and `pass@10`, or at least maintains a consistent gap, demonstrating its ability to preserve and enhance policy diversity.
- **Significant Speedup and Memory Efficiency:** SPO achieves an **85% speedup** in wall-clock training time compared to PPO. This is attributed to:
 - **Reduced Model Transfers:** SPO's off-policy nature allows for much less frequent model transfers between trainers and workers.
 - **No Separate Value Model:** The Cumulative Q-Parameterization unifies policy and value, eliminating the memory and computational overhead of a distinct value network.
- **Effective Use of Offline Data:** The "SPO half-online" variant (trained on a mix of online and offline data) outperforms "SPO online" (purely online data) and PPO. This conclusively demonstrates SPO's ability to:
 - **Stably learn from arbitrary offline data.**
 - **Benefit significantly from diverse offline data sources**, including data from other policies, human solutions, and past runs.
 - **Reduce sampling costs by leveraging existing data.**

#### 4.2. Ablations and Extensions

The authors also conduct ablations to understand the impact of different SPO loss variants and extensions:

- **Loss Function Choice:**
 - When using terminal Q-regression, the **squared loss** (L2) demonstrates faster improvement in `pass@10` compared to cross-entropy loss or sigmoid-warped advantage regression.
 - This finding contrasts with some prior work, which is attributed to SPO's accurate capture of the mathematical relationship between policy and value function.
- **Terminal vs. Non-Terminal Q-Regression:**
 - Using **non-terminal Q-regression with "reverse-Q targets"** (regressing $Q^\theta_t$ towards future-corrected rewards) with squared loss shows no substantial effect on performance.
 - However, combining non-terminal targets with cross-entropy loss significantly degrades performance. This suggests the specific target formulation and loss function interaction are crucial.
- **Monte Carlo Targets with Pivotal Token Search (PTS):**
 - A significant improvement in `pass@10` is observed when using **Monte Carlo Q-estimates for non-terminal tokens** identified by Pivotal Token Search (PTS). This involves sampling from $\pi_0$ at "pivotal" points in trajectories to estimate success probabilities $S_t$, which are then converted to $Q_t$ targets.
 - This extension shows that even a relatively small dataset of 50,000 PTS-annotated trajectories (25% of the offline data) can lead to substantial performance gains, highlighting the value of high-quality intermediate Q-value targets.
 - Simply applying terminal loss on rollouts obtained during PTS performs similarly to the baseline, indicating that the structured Q-targets, rather than just more rollouts, are key.

In summary, SPO demonstrates strong empirical advantages over PPO in terms of `pass@10`, training speed, memory efficiency, and the crucial ability to leverage offline data. The ablations reveal that while the core SPO framework is robust, the choice of loss function and the intelligent generation of non-terminal Monte Carlo targets can further enhance its performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Soft Policy Optimization (SPO) presents a significant advancement in the field of Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Models, with profound implications for both research and practical application.

#### 5.1. Overcoming Core Limitations of LLM Fine-tuning

The most immediate and impactful contribution of SPO is its ability to directly address the critical shortcomings of current on-policy RL methods like PPO, which are the industry standard for LLM alignment.
- **Massive Efficiency Gains:** By enabling off-policy learning, eliminating the separate value model, and reducing model transfer frequency, SPO offers substantial improvements in training speed (85% faster) and memory footprint. These efficiency gains are paramount for training ever-larger frontier models, where computational resources are a primary bottleneck.
- **Amortized Exploration and Knowledge Evolution:** The ability to learn from arbitrary online and offline data (from diverse sources like human experts, other policies, or previous runs) means that exploration efforts are no longer repeatedly duplicated. This allows AI models to "learn from the discoveries and mistakes of others," facilitating a more cumulative and efficient "knowledge evolution process" akin to social learning in humans. This is a paradigm shift from current methods that treat each training run as an isolated endeavor.
- **Enhanced Policy Diversity and Robustness:** SPO's adherence to the Soft RL objective leads to policies that are not just performant but also diverse (as evidenced by `pass@10` improvements). For generative tasks, diversity is often as critical as correctness, allowing models to produce a wider range of high-quality, creative, and robust responses, rather than collapsing to a single "hard-maxed" solution. This aligns better with the inherent nature of generative AI.

#### 5.2. Theoretical Novelty and Practical Simplification

SPO's "Cumulative Q-Parameterization" is a theoretically elegant and practically impactful innovation.
- **Unified Policy and Value Function:** By intrinsically linking the policy and value functions such that one determines the other, SPO simplifies the RL problem. This unification inherently guarantees Bellman and Path consistency for intermediate sequence tokens, sidestepping the complex task of learning these consistencies which is often a source of instability in other Q-learning or path-consistency methods.
- **Simplified Learning Objective:** The theoretical guarantees reduce the learning problem to primarily matching the terminal reward. This simplification streamlines the training process and potentially makes SPO more stable and easier to optimize.

#### 5.3. Future Directions and Broader Impact

The paper itself points to several promising future directions that highlight SPO's potential:
- **Continuous Offline Dataset Growth:** The full benefit of SPO's off-policy capability will be realized when offline datasets are continuously grown and enriched with solutions from various training runs and sources. This iterative knowledge accumulation could dramatically reduce inference costs over time.
- **Advanced Q-Value Estimation:** The success of Monte-Carlo Q-estimates at pivotal tokens suggests that targeted, high-quality intermediate Q-value signals can further accelerate learning. This opens avenues for more sophisticated methods for generating or learning these internal targets.
- **Dynamic Reference Model Updates:** The paper notes that updating the reference model online (and estimating Q0 dynamically) could further improve performance, suggesting avenues for extending SPO to more adaptive settings.
- **Combination with Other Losses:** The flexible framework allows for combining SPO with other loss functions (e.g., policy gradients), offering hybrid approaches that might harness the strengths of different RL paradigms.

In conclusion, Soft Policy Optimization represents a significant leap forward in the efficiency, scalability, and performance of RL-based fine-tuning for LLMs. Its ability to learn from diverse off-policy data, coupled with its theoretical elegance and practical computational advantages, positions it as a strong candidate to become a foundational method for developing the next generation of large language models, particularly for complex, multi-faceted tasks like code generation. The implications extend beyond just speed and memory; SPO fosters a paradigm of cumulative learning that could fundamentally alter how AI models acquire and evolve knowledge.
