# Soft Policy Optimization: Online Off-Policy RL for Sequence Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Soft Policy Optimization (SPO) is an online off-policy reinforcement learning method designed for fine-tuning generative sequence models that improves training efficiency and policy diversity. It achieves an 85% speedup over PPO and consistently better `pass@10` performance, leveraging a novel cumulative Q-parameterization to unify policy and value functions.

## Problem

- On-policy reinforcement learning methods like PPO are sample inefficient for fine-tuning Large Language Models, requiring fresh data for each update.
- These methods limit exploration and can lead to a loss of diversity in generated responses, converging to single solutions.
- Computational bottlenecks arise from frequent model transfers in distributed PPO setups and the overhead of a separate value model.

## Method

- **SPO** introduces an online off-policy Soft RL framework that enables learning from arbitrary online and offline trajectories, including diverse data sources.
- It employs a "==Cumulative Q-Parameterization==" which unifies the policy and value functions, inherently satisfying Bellman and Path consistency for intermediate tokens.
- The method simplifies the learning objective by reducing it primarily to matching the terminal reward, removing the need for separate value models and complex consistency losses.

## Results

- SPO consistently outperforms PPO in `pass@10` scores (policy diversity) over 100,000 training steps on CodeContests and TACO benchmarks.
- The method achieves an **85%** speedup in wall-clock training time compared to PPO due to reduced model transfers and the elimination of a separate value model.
- SPO successfully leverages a mix of online and diverse offline data, demonstrating stable learning and performance improvements over purely online methods, and further enhances `pass@10` when using Monte Carlo Q-estimates at pivotal tokens.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The optimal policy in a KL-regularized reward maximization framework can be interpreted as a success-conditioned posterior, which inherently preserves the diversity of correct responses.
> - Parameterizing advantages as log-probability differences and Q-values as cumulative sums ensures intrinsic Bellman and Path consistency without explicit regularization.
> - The ability to stably learn from diverse off-policy data facilitates "knowledge evolution" and amortized exploration, significantly enhancing training efficiency and model robustness.
