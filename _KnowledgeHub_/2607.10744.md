# Traj-VLN: Learning Pixel-Space Interaction via Autoregressive Trajectory Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Traj-VLN trains Vision-Language Models to autoregressively generate 2D pixel-space trajectories, enabling them to learn spatial interactions for Vision-and-Language Navigation in continuous environments. This approach achieves a 58.4% success rate on R2R-CE Val-Unseen and a 52.6% success rate on RxR-CE Val-Unseen, outperforming prior methods that rely on single pixel-goal supervision.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs), pre-trained on 2D internet data, struggle with the 3D spatial reasoning required for embodied navigation, creating a 2D-3D perception gap for physical instructions.
- Existing VLN-CE methods, including direct action chunk prediction and single pixel-goal supervision, do not sufficiently bridge this 2D-3D gap or provide VLMs with a robust understanding of interaction processes.
- Attempts to integrate explicit 3D geometric information into VLMs have yielded limited improvements, as VLMs lack sufficient pre-training to effectively utilize such complex 3D data.

## Method

- A two-stage conversational navigation process in **Traj-VLN** employs a fine-tuned VLM (==Qwen3-VL-8B==) to first make high-level decisions (turn, stop) or initiate detailed movement planning.
- For detailed planning, the VLM performs ==autoregressive trajectory generation== of a sequence of normalized 2D pixel coordinates on an overhead image, explicitly defining a trajectory in pixel space.
- The generated 2D pixel trajectories are converted into 3D physical waypoints using ==depth maps== and ==camera intrinsics/extrinsics==, which the robot then iteratively executes.

## Results

- Traj-VLN achieved competitive success rates of **58.4%** on R2R-CE Val-Unseen and **52.6%** on RxR-CE Val-Unseen, surpassing several state-of-the-art VLM-based methods including InternVLA-N1 and Goal2Pixel.
- Pixel-trajectory supervision consistently outperformed single pixel-goal supervision, demonstrating higher **Success Rate (SR)** and **Success weighted by Path Length (SPL)** across benchmarks, even when evaluating only the first generated waypoint.
- Fine-grained execution (e.g., one waypoint per prediction) yielded the highest Success Rates for Traj-VLN, improving upon pixel-goal supervision by **4 points** on R2R-CE in this configuration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning the sequential *process* of 2D pixel-space interaction, rather than predicting a static pixel-goal, is more effective for VLMs in 3D navigation and aligns better with their 2D visual priors.
> - Autoregressive pixel-trajectory generation functions as a Chain-of-Thought mechanism, allowing VLMs to decompose complex navigation goals into intermediate, executable visual steps and enhancing final goal prediction.
> - Focusing on interactions within the 2D pixel space better leverages the VLM's existing 2D competencies, providing a more compatible fine-tuning signal than methods requiring explicit 3D reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research operates within the field of Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN), specifically focusing on VLN in Continuous Environments (VLN-CE). VLN tasks require an embodied agent to navigate an environment following natural language instructions, leveraging visual observations. Historically, VLN tasks involved navigation within discrete, pre-defined navigational graphs. However, recent advancements have shifted towards VLN-CE, where agents can move to any unobstructed location, more closely mimicking real-world scenarios.

A prevailing paradigm in VLN-CE involves fine-tuning pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs). These models leverage the extensive priors from large-scale internet pre-training data and the emergent reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) to enhance generalization. However, a significant challenge arises from the inherent mismatch between VLM pre-training and the requirements of 3D navigation. VLMs, particularly their vision encoders, are predominantly pre-trained on 2D RGB images from internet conversations, lacking explicit understanding or exposure to 3D geometric layouts, depth information, or physical interaction processes. Consequently, while natural language instructions describe 3D spatial interactions (e.g., "walk to the end of the sofa and turn left"), VLMs struggle to interpret and execute these instructions accurately in a 3D environment.

Prior work has attempted to address this limitation through several approaches:
- **Direct Action Chunk Prediction:** Many early VLM-based VLN models fine-tuned VLMs to predict sequences of low-level robotic actions (e.g., move forward, turn left/right), which implicitly form a 3D trajectory. However, this approach does not directly bridge the 2D-3D gap for the VLMs themselves.
- **Incorporating 3D Information:** Some methods have sought to explicitly integrate depth information or 3D geometric priors into VLM frameworks. While these approaches aim to provide the missing 3D context, they have shown limited improvements. The primary reason suggested is that VLMs lack sufficient pre-training in utilizing such 3D information, often necessitating additional learnable encoders to align depth embeddings with RGB representations.
- **Pixel-Goal Supervision:** More recently, approaches like InternVLA-N1 and Goal2Pixel have shifted to fine-tuning VLMs with a target pixel-goal as the supervision signal, operating directly within the familiar 2D pixel space. This method has demonstrated performance improvements over action-chunk prediction, suggesting the effectiveness of 2D-space supervision. However, a critical open question remains: how can VLMs accurately infer a target pixel if they still lack an explicit understanding of the 3D scene layout necessary to reach it?

Traj-VLN proposes an alternative perspective. Instead of trying to directly incorporate 3D geometric information or solely relying on a single pixel-goal, this work focuses on enhancing the VLM's ability to reason about *spatial interaction processes* in the pixel space. By supervising VLMs with full 2D pixel-space trajectories, Traj-VLN aims to provide a richer, sequential understanding of navigation that aligns with the VLM's 2D pre-training, effectively sidestepping the need for explicit 3D reasoning. This positions Traj-VLN as an advancement in grounding VLM capabilities for embodied navigation by leveraging their strengths in 2D visual understanding through a novel supervision signal.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the capability of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in Vision-and-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments (VLN-CE) by enabling them to learn spatial interactions directly in 2D pixel space through autoregressive trajectory generation.

The detailed motivations and specific objectives that drive this work are as follows:

- **Addressing the 2D-3D Perception Gap in VLMs:** VLMs are predominantly pre-trained on large datasets of 2D images and associated text. This foundation makes them proficient in 2D visual understanding and language processing but limits their innate capacity for 3D spatial reasoning, which is crucial for embodied navigation. Traditional VLN instructions, such as "walk to the end of the sofa and turn left," inherently describe 3D movements and interactions with the physical environment. Existing VLMs struggle to interpret these instructions effectively without an explicit understanding of 3D scene geometry. The research aims to bridge this gap by formulating 3D navigation processes in a 2D-compatible manner.

- **Limitations of Prior VLN Approaches:**
 - **Action Chunk Prediction:** Many VLN methods supervise VLMs by predicting discrete action chunks (e.g., "move forward 25cm," "turn left 15 degrees"). While these actions compose a 3D trajectory, they do not provide the VLM with an explicit understanding of the overall spatial interaction process or the visual path.
 - **Explicit 3D Information Integration:** Attempts to incorporate depth information or geometric priors into VLMs have yielded limited improvements. This is attributed to VLMs' lack of sufficient exposure to 3D information during their extensive pre-training, making it challenging for them to effectively utilize such data during fine-tuning.
 - **Single Pixel-Goal Supervision:** Recent work has demonstrated that fine-tuning VLMs with a single target pixel-goal significantly improves VLN performance over action-chunk prediction. However, the motivation questions how VLMs can accurately infer such a target pixel without an understanding of the underlying 3D scene layout or the intermediate steps required to reach it. The paper argues that this approach might lead to the VLM learning a distribution of pixel goals rather than a process of interaction.

- **Hypothesis of Pixel-Space Interaction Learning:** The core hypothesis is that learning the *process* of 2D pixel-space interaction, rather than merely predicting a static pixel-goal, can significantly enhance VLN performance. A sequence of pixel coordinates, forming a 2D trajectory on the image plane, serves as a unique projection of the actual 3D trajectory. This 2D projection inherently captures the spatial interaction described by the instruction, such as moving towards an object or traversing a pathway.

- **Alignment with VLM Pre-training Distribution:** By focusing on interactions within the 2D pixel space, the proposed method inherently aligns with the data distribution VLMs encountered during their pre-training. This approach aims to leverage and reorganize the priors embedded in the VLM's pre-training data, ensuring that the fine-tuning process is more compatible and less prone to catastrophic forgetting.

- **Autoregressive Trajectory Generation as Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** The research proposes that autoregressively generating a sequence of pixel coordinates for a trajectory can function as a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) mechanism. This sequential prediction process compels the VLM to decompose the ultimate navigation goal into a series of intermediate, executable steps, fostering more robust spatial reasoning and leading to more accurate pixel-goal predictions. The textual format of the generated coordinates naturally aligns with the VLM's language processing capabilities.

In summary, Traj-VLN seeks to overcome the limitations of 2D-trained VLMs in 3D navigation by introducing a novel autoregressive pixel-trajectory supervision paradigm. This approach aims to provide a more process-oriented understanding of spatial interactions, leverage the VLM's existing 2D competencies, and implicitly enhance reasoning for better navigation performance without relying on explicit 3D geometric information.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Traj-VLN methodology focuses on fine-tuning a general-purpose Vision-Language Model (VLM) to generate autoregressive 2D pixel-space trajectories, which represent the agent's planned movement in continuous environments. This approach aims to teach the VLM the process of spatial interaction within the familiar 2D image plane.

**Model Paradigm:**
- **Backbone:** Traj-VLN is built upon Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, a publicly available, general-purpose VLM.
- **Fine-tuning Strategy:** During fine-tuning, the vision encoder of Qwen3-VL-8B is kept frozen to reduce computational cost and training time (approximately 62.5% reduction). Only the Large Language Model (LLM) component and its projection layer are fine-tuned.
- **Input Composition:** At each time step, the model receives several inputs:
 - A natural language instruction.
 - The current RGB observation ($O_t$).
 - A set of uniformly sampled historical RGB observations ($O_{his} = \{H_1..., H_N\}$), with N fixed at 8. The sampling interval is dynamically adjusted.
- **Image Preprocessing:** Historical observations are resized to 384x384 pixels, while the current observation ($O_t$) is kept at 640x480 pixels. All images are rounded to dimensions that are multiples of 28 to comply with the patch size requirements of Qwen3-VL.

**Two-Stage Conversational Navigation:**
The navigation process is structured as a two-stage conversation loop with the VLM:

1. **Stage 1: High-level Decision (Turning or Planning Initiation):**
 - The VLM, constrained by a chat template, outputs one of four symbolic actions: "STOP", "←", "→", or "↓".
 - If "STOP" is output: The agent terminates the task. Success or failure is determined by its current position relative to the target.
 - If "←" or "→" is output: The agent rotates 15 degrees in the corresponding direction for each symbol. The current conversation is then restarted with updated visual input.
 - If "↓" is output: This signals the VLM's intent to initiate a detailed movement plan. The agent lowers its head by 30 degrees to capture an overhead view. This overhead image is then incorporated into the conversation as an additional input, and the process transitions to Stage 2.

2. **Stage 2: Detailed Trajectory Generation (Pixel-Space Planning):**
 - Given the updated visual inputs, particularly the new overhead image, the VLM is trained to autoregressively generate a sequence of normalized pixel coordinates.
 - The output format is a textual string: "[u1, v1] [u2, v2] ... [uk, vk]", which delineates a trajectory on the overhead image, starting from its bottom center. This textual output aligns with the VLM's linguistic capabilities.

**Navigation Execution using Traj-VLN:**
- **Pixel to 3D Waypoints:** If the VLM outputs "↓" and then generates a pixel trajectory (Traj_pixel), this sequence of 2D pixel coordinates needs to be transformed into 3D physical waypoints for robot execution.
 - The corresponding depth map of the overhead image is utilized to obtain depth information ($d_i$) for each pixel coordinate $(u_i, v_i)$, forming $Traj_{depth} = \{(u_i, v_i, d_i)^T\}$.
 - Camera intrinsics (K) and camera extrinsics ($T_{c2b}$) (transformation from camera to robot base frame) are used to convert these 3D pixel-depth points into 3D vectors $(X_i, Y_i, Z_i)^T$ in the robot's local frame ($Traj_{3D}$).
- **Iterative Movement:** The robot iteratively moves towards the closest 3D point in the $Traj_{3D}$ sequence until the current planned trajectory is deemed complete.
- **Conversation Restart:** Once the current plan is executed, the conversation with the VLM is restarted with updated visual observations, returning to Stage 1.

**Data Collection and Supervision Signal Generation:**
- **Datasets:** Training data is constructed from R2R-CE, RXR-CE, and a subset of ScaleVLN. These datasets combined yield approximately 107.5K trajectories, from which 1.75M training samples are extracted.
- **Sampling Strategy:** Samples are collected at an action interval of 4 steps along the ground-truth trajectories.
- **Ground-Truth Trajectory Generation:** For each sample, the future poses relative to the current agent pose ($T_{cur}$) are projected onto the current overhead image to create the ground-truth supervision trajectory.
 - Future poses ($T_{future}$) are obtained from the full trajectory ($Traj = \{T_1..., T_l\}$).
 - Base positions of these future poses are defined ($T_{base}$) by adjusting the Z-component of their translation vector.
 - These base positions are then projected onto the image plane using the camera intrinsics and the inverse of the current camera pose ($K T_{cur}^{-1} (t_{base_i}, 1)^T$), yielding 2D pixel coordinates ($Traj_{pixel}$).
 - Crucially, invisible and occluded pixels from this $Traj_{pixel}$ are filtered out using the depth image to ensure only executable, visible path segments are used for supervision.
 - The remaining filtered pixel coordinates are formatted into the textual string "[u1, v1] [u2, v2] ... [uk, vk]" for VLM training.

**Training Details:**
- **Flagship Model:** The Traj-VLN flagship model (Qwen3-VL-8B) was trained for one epoch on 8 Ascend 910C NPUs for approximately 91 hours (728 NPU hours). It utilized 1.75M samples with a batch size of 128 and a learning rate of 2e-5.
- **Comparative Models:** For direct comparison with pixel-goal supervision, additional models based on Qwen2.5-VL-7B were trained. These models used LoRA (rank=32) for fine-tuning the LLM, kept the vision encoder frozen, and were trained for one epoch on 4 Nvidia 4090 GPUs for approximately 171 hours. The training data was identical (1.75M samples), with the only difference being the supervision signal (trajectory vs. single pixel-goal).

This comprehensive methodology allows Traj-VLN to train VLMs to perceive and plan navigation as a sequence of pixel-space interactions, which is hypothesized to be more aligned with their pre-training and more effective than traditional methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to address several key questions regarding Traj-VLN's performance, the efficacy of pixel-trajectory supervision compared to pixel-goal supervision, and the role of autoregressive trajectory generation as a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) mechanism.

**Performance against State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Methods (Table I):**
- **Overall Performance:** The flagship Traj-VLN model, built on Qwen3-VL-8B and trained with 1.75M samples, demonstrated competitive performance on the VLN-CE R2R Val-Unseen and RxR Val-Unseen splits. It achieved a Success Rate (SR) of 58.4 on R2R-CE and 52.6 on RxR-CE.
- **Comparison with VLM-based Methods:**
 - Traj-VLN surpassed several prominent VLM-based methods using comparable or larger scales of training data. For instance, it achieved higher SR than InternVLA-N1 (4.72M samples, unfrozen vision encoder) by 3 points on R2R-CE and 2.9 points on RxR-CE, despite using less training data and a frozen vision encoder.
 - It also outperformed Goal2Pixel (2.60M samples, frozen vision encoder), which relies on pixel-goal supervision, by 4.5 points in SR on R2R-CE.
 - Methods that explicitly incorporated 3D information, such as JanusVLN and NaVid-4D, were also surpassed by Traj-VLN, by 5.6 and 1.2 points in SR on R2R-CE and RxR-CE respectively (compared to JanusVLN). This suggests that effective 2D-space supervision can be more impactful than direct 3D alignment for VLMs.
- **Efficiency:** The strategy of freezing the vision encoder, while still achieving high performance, reduced training duration by approximately 62.5%, indicating a more efficient training process.

**Comparison of Pixel Trajectory Supervision with Pixel-Goal Supervision (Table II, Figure 4):**
- **Direct Comparison Setup:** To ensure a fair comparison, two models were trained under identical configurations (Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, LoRA, frozen vision encoder, 1.75M training samples), differing only in their supervision signal: Traj-VLN (trajectory supervision) and InternVLA-N1-System2 (pixel-goal supervision).
- **Superiority of Trajectory Supervision:** Traj-VLN consistently outperformed pixel-goal supervision across various metrics.
 - When evaluating Traj-VLN by considering its *first* generated waypoint as the pixel-goal (referred to as "Final-Pixel Evaluation" or "final pixel as pixel-goal"), it achieved a higher SR (43.2 vs. 39.6 on R2R-CE; 41.54 vs. 36.09 on RxR-CE) and SPL compared to direct pixel-goal supervision.
- **Impact of Execution Granularity (Figure 4):** The study investigated the effect of executing different numbers of waypoints from the predicted trajectory per planning step.
 - Executing fewer waypoints per prediction (which corresponds to increasing the reasoning frequency or replanning more often) generally led to higher Success Rates (SR) for Traj-VLN.
 - The highest SR was observed when executing only one waypoint per prediction (approximately 0.5m movement), with Traj-VLN surpassing pixel-goal supervision by 4 points on R2R-CE and 3.29 points on RxR-CE in this configuration. This suggests that fine-grained, iterative planning contributes to better overall task success.
 - However, executing fewer waypoints also led to a lower Oracle Success Rate (OSR), indicating that while the agent might make more successful progress, the "oracle" (perfect future knowledge) might find a better path if it were given larger planning horizons.
- **Trajectory Execution vs. Final-Pixel Evaluation:** The "Trajectory Evaluation" of Traj-VLN (sequentially executing predicted waypoints) also showed strong performance, achieving comparable SR to its "Final-Pixel Evaluation" counterpart on R2R-CE (43.6 vs. 43.2) and slightly lower on RxR-CE (39.38 vs. 41.54). This indicates the robustness of the trajectory generation itself.

**Trajectory as Chain-of-Thought (Traj-CoT) Validation (Table II, Figure 4):**
- The research hypothesized that autoregressive trajectory generation acts as a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) mechanism, enabling better final pixel-goal prediction.
- To validate this, Traj-VLN was evaluated by taking only the *final* pixel coordinate of its generated trajectory as the target pixel-goal for execution, using the same evaluation pipeline as direct pixel-goal supervision.
- The results, reported under "Final-Pixel Evaluation," showed that this approach still yielded improved performance metrics (e.g., higher SR and SPL) compared to models trained solely on a single pixel-goal (InternVLA-N1-System2).
- This finding supports the hypothesis that the process of sequentially generating intermediate pixel coordinates (the trajectory) aids the VLM in more effectively reasoning towards and identifying a more accurate ultimate target pixel. The explicit articulation of intermediate steps through trajectory generation appears to enhance the VLM's spatial reasoning capabilities for goal prediction.

**Generalization to Unseen Real-World Scenarios:**
- The paper qualitatively states that Traj-VLN demonstrates the capability for correct interactions in cluttered environments, which is a challenge for pre-trained VLMs. While specific quantitative metrics for real-world generalization were not detailed in the provided text, the qualitative observation implies improved robustness in complex settings.

In conclusion, the main findings establish that pixel-trajectory supervision is a more effective method for fine-tuning VLMs for VLN-CE compared to single pixel-goal or action-chunk supervision. The autoregressive nature of trajectory generation appears to provide a valuable chain-of-thought mechanism that improves spatial reasoning and leads to superior navigation performance on standard benchmarks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Traj-VLN: Learning Pixel-Space Interaction via Autoregressive Trajectory Generation" carries several significant implications and potential impacts on the field of embodied AI, robotics, and Vision-Language Models.

- **Bridging the 2D-3D Gap for VLMs in Embodied Navigation:** One of the most significant contributions of Traj-VLN is its novel approach to addressing a fundamental limitation of current Vision-Language Models in embodied navigation. VLMs, predominantly pre-trained on 2D internet data, inherently struggle with 3D spatial reasoning crucial for physical interaction. Traj-VLN effectively circumvents the need for explicit 3D geometric understanding by reformulating 3D navigation processes into 2D pixel-space trajectories. This allows VLMs to leverage their strong 2D visual understanding capabilities for complex 3D tasks, making them more adaptable to embodied applications without extensive architectural modifications or computationally expensive 3D pre-training.

- **Novel and Effective Supervision Paradigm:** The introduction of autoregressive pixel-trajectory generation as a supervision signal represents a new paradigm for fine-tuning VLMs in VLN. Prior methods primarily relied on discrete action chunks or single pixel-goals. Traj-VLN demonstrates that providing a sequential, process-oriented supervision signal, depicting the path of interaction, is more effective. This paradigm can lead to more robust and accurate navigation, as shown by its superior performance over pixel-goal supervision and other SOTA methods. This might encourage researchers to explore more process-level or "chain-of-action" supervision signals for VLMs in other sequential decision-making tasks.

- **Validation of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) for Embodied AI:** The finding that autoregressive trajectory generation acts as an effective Chain-of-Thought mechanism for pixel-goal prediction is particularly impactful. CoT reasoning has primarily been explored in natural language processing and question-answering tasks, enabling LLMs to break down complex problems into intermediate steps. Traj-VLN extends this concept to embodied AI, demonstrating that a visually grounded, sequential output (a trajectory) can serve a similar function, improving the VLM's ability to reason about and achieve a final goal. This suggests a broader applicability of CoT principles in integrating visual perception with action planning, potentially inspiring new forms of "visual chain-of-thought" for other embodied tasks requiring multi-step reasoning.

- **Enhanced Performance and Generalization:** By achieving state-of-the-art success rates on challenging VLN-CE benchmarks with comparable or even less training data than some existing methods, Traj-VLN demonstrates a significant step forward in the performance of VLM-based navigation agents. The reported qualitative capability of navigating cluttered environments further suggests improved generalization to complex, unseen scenarios, which is critical for real-world robotic deployments. Higher success rates directly translate to more reliable and capable autonomous agents.

- **Efficiency in Training:** The strategy of freezing the vision encoder while fine-tuning only the LLM and projection layer, combined with strong performance, highlights an efficient training approach. This reduces computational resources and training time, making the development and deployment of advanced VLN agents more accessible and sustainable.

- **Potential for Real-World Robotics and Assistive Technologies:** The ability of an agent to reliably follow complex natural language instructions in continuous, cluttered environments has direct applications in robotics. This could include improved indoor navigation for service robots, autonomous exploration, and assistive technologies for individuals with mobility impairments. The method's emphasis on learning interactions in pixel space could also make it more adaptable to different camera perspectives and robot platforms, potentially reducing the sim-to-real gap.

In summary, Traj-VLN represents a foundational advancement in enabling VLMs to effectively perform vision-and-language navigation. Its novel supervision approach, integration of CoT principles, and demonstrated performance gains collectively contribute to more intelligent, robust, and generalizable embodied agents, with broad implications for future research and applications in AI and robotics.
