# VLA-Touch: Enhancing Vision-Language-Action Models with Dual-Level Tactile Feedback

> [!summary] Summary
> VLA-Touch enhances Vision-Language-Action models by integrating tactile feedback at both planning and control levels, significantly improving performance in contact-rich manipulation tasks. The framework demonstrates up to 140% higher task success rates in real-world scenarios compared to vision-only VLA models, leveraging linguistic tactile descriptions for high-level reasoning and an interpolant-based diffusion controller for precise action refinement.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models primarily rely on visual and proprioceptive feedback, limiting their ability to perform contact-rich manipulation tasks that require understanding object compliance, texture, and precise contact events.
- A lack of large-scale multi-modal datasets and intrinsic architectural mechanisms in current VLA models prevents effective processing and reasoning over tactile inputs.
- Integrating tactile sensing often requires extensive fine-tuning of base VLA models or is limited to task-specific policies, hindering generalizability and scalability.

## Method

- **VLA-Touch** introduces a modular framework that integrates dual-level tactile feedback into VLA pipelines without fine-tuning the base VLA model, combining a ==Task Planner==, a Tactile-Language Model, and a Tactile-Augmented Controller.
- At the planning level, a VLM (GPT-4o) generates manipulation instructions, receiving linguistic descriptions of tactile-inferred object properties (e.g., hardness, roughness) from a Tactile-Language Model (Octopi) for informed decision-making.
- At the control level, an interpolant-based diffusion controller (BRIDGeR) refines VLA-generated actions using real-time, low-dimensional tactile force signals, enabling precise, contact-aware movements.

## Results

- Linguistic tactile descriptions enabled GPT-4o to achieve **90%** success in force prediction and **75%** for hardness inference in planning, compared to 50-**60%** with raw tactile images and random success with visual-only input.
- The interpolant-based tactile controller improved base VLA task success rates by up to **140%** (Wipe task), outperforming a vanilla VLA by **42%** (Cup) and **67%** (Peel), and a residual controller by 67-**100%** across tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that removing planning-level tactile feedback reduced success rates by 14-**58%**, and removing control-level feedback decreased performance by 33-**58%**, highlighting the necessity of dual-level integration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tactile feedback is crucial for VLM-based planning in visually ambiguous, contact-rich tasks, with linguistic descriptions of tactile information proving more effective for planning than raw tactile images.
> - Both planning-level and control-level tactile feedback are essential for optimal performance; neither level alone achieves the same robustness and precision.
> - An interpolant-based diffusion controller effectively integrates tactile data for low-level action refinement, outperforming simple residual controllers in capturing multi-modal action distributions for precise manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI has seen remarkable progress with the advent of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models integrate visual perception with natural language understanding to enable robots to interpret high-level instructions and perform diverse tasks in unstructured environments. Prominent examples include RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA, and Octo, which have demonstrated general-purpose policy learning capabilities by leveraging large-scale datasets and foundation models.

However, a critical limitation of most state-of-the-art VLA models is their predominant reliance on visual and proprioceptive feedback. While vision excels at global scene understanding, it inherently struggles with "contact-rich" tasks where crucial information like object compliance, surface texture, friction, and precise contact events are vital. These properties are often visually ambiguous or entirely imperceptible through cameras alone. Prior research has explored integrating tactile sensing, but often within the scope of task-specific policies or for control-level improvements without tightly integrating into the higher-level reasoning of generalist VLA models. Challenges include the absence of large multi-modal (vision, language, action, tactile) datasets and the lack of intrinsic mechanisms in current VLA architectures to process and reason over tactile inputs.

"VLA-Touch" directly addresses this gap by proposing a dual-level integration of tactile feedback into existing VLA pipelines *without* requiring extensive fine-tuning of the base VLA model itself. This modular approach distinguishes it from efforts that fine-tune VLA architectures with additional sensory modalities or those that focus exclusively on tactile-language models (e.g., Octopi) or tactile-augmented control policies. By decoupling the tactile integration into planning and control stages, VLA-Touch represents a significant step towards developing more robust, perceptive, and generally capable embodied agents that can navigate and interact with the physical world with greater dexterity and intelligence, mirroring human-like sensory integration. It stands as a scaffold for future research into multi-modal perception for generalist robot policies, emphasizing the crucial, yet often overlooked, role of touch.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the effectiveness of generalist robot policies, specifically VLA models, in contact-rich manipulation tasks by incorporating tactile sensing. The authors hypothesize that tactile feedback is crucial and beneficial at two distinct levels:
1. **Planning Level:** Tactile information can enable higher-level reasoning about object properties (e.g., hardness, roughness) that are visually ambiguous, thus informing better task planning.
2. **Control Level:** Tactile sensing can provide real-time data on contact dynamics (e.g., surface friction, contact events) for precise, low-level action refinement during manipulation.

Motivated by the inherent limitations of vision-only VLA models in contact-rich scenarios and the scarcity of large multi-modal datasets, the paper aims to address two key challenges:
- How to enable a robot to effectively acquire and plan with tactile information when most pretrained VLA models have no prior exposure to tactile data.
- How to integrate tactile sensing into existing VLA pipelines with minimal modification, given that base models lack dedicated tactile input modalities.

The overarching goal is to demonstrate that a dual-level integration of tactile feedback can significantly improve both the efficiency of high-level task planning and the precision of low-level action execution in real-world robotic tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLA-Touch proposes a novel, modular framework for dual-level tactile feedback integration without fine-tuning the base Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model. The system, inspired by human neural systems, consists of three main components: a Task Planner, a Tactile-Language Model, and a Tactile-Augmented Controller. The base VLA model used is Robot Diffusion Transformer (RDT), and the Vision-Language Model for planning is GPT-4o. The tactile sensor employed is the GelSight Mini.

#### 3.1. Tactile-Assisted Task Planning (High-Level)

This component focuses on incorporating tactile information into the robot's reasoning and decision-making process for task planning.
- **Cyclical Task Planning:** The framework employs an interleaved approach where a VLM Task Planner (GPT-4o) generates manipulation instructions based on scene images and a given goal. A VLA model (RDT) attempts to execute these instructions. Upon completion of an instruction or if execution fails, a Tactile-Language Model processes recent tactile signals, converts them into a linguistic description, and feeds this back to the VLM planner. This allows the VLM to update its plan based on tactile-inferred object properties.
- **VLM Prompting:** GPT-4o is prompted with detailed instructions defining the robot's capabilities (single arm, gripper, tactile sensor for properties like hardness, roughness), response format (primitive action + information needed), and interaction protocol (receive feedback, then plan).
- **Tactile-Language Model (Octopi):** A pretrained tactile-language model called Octopi, specifically designed for the GelSight sensor, is used. Octopi processes a sequence of tactile signals (6 frames) to infer properties of contacted objects, such as roughness and hardness. These continuous property values are then converted into linguistic feedback, allowing GPT-4o to understand and utilize the tactile data for informed planning. For force estimation, a marker tracking algorithm estimates force vectors from GelSight images and provides them to GPT-4o.

#### 3.2. VLA Policy Refinement with Tactile Sensing (Low-Level)

This component addresses the precise execution of actions, particularly in contact-rich scenarios, by refining VLA-generated actions with real-time tactile feedback.
- **Interpolant-Based Diffusion Controller (BRIDGeR):** The core of the tactile-augmented controller is BRIDGeR, an interpolant-based diffusion model. Unlike conventional diffusion models that start denoising from Gaussian noise, BRIDGeR uses stochastic interpolants to bridge a source distribution (VLA-generated actions) with a target distribution (expert actions). This allows it to refine the VLA's "coarse" actions into more precise, tactile-aware movements.
- **Input to Controller:** The interpolant controller $\pi_I$ takes as input:
 - A source action chunk $a_{t:t+T_i}$ generated by the base VLA model.
 - The current visual state $s_t$ (RGB images and robot proprioception).
 - Low-dimensional tactile signal $m_t$. The tactile force signal $m_t$ is estimated from GelSight Mini's 7x9 marker array by computing per-marker force vectors and aggregating them into a summed force vector and magnitude (X, Y, M), reducing noise and providing a compact representation.
- **Controller Training:** The controller is trained via supervised learning on a dataset of paired VLA-generated actions and expert action sequences. This dataset is constructed by sampling states from expert demonstrations and using the VLA model to generate source actions for the interpolant model to refine.
- **Inference Strategy:** During inference, a sliding window refinement strategy is used. The controller refines non-overlapping segments of the VLA-generated action chunk sequentially. After executing a refined window, it advances to the next segment until the entire VLA action chunk is refined and executed.

The entire process involves continuous interaction between the VLM planner, the VLA policy, the tactile-language model, and the interpolant controller, creating a robust feedback loop for complex manipulation tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research questions were systematically addressed through three real-world contact-rich tasks: **Cup** (picking, placing, and inferring liquid presence), **Wipe** (selecting a smoother sponge and wiping ink), and **Peel** (identifying a softer mango and peeling it).

**Q1. Does tactile feedback improve task planning performance? Are linguistic descriptions of tactile information sufficient for VLM-based planning?**
- **Significant Improvement with Tactile Feedback:** GPT-4o, when given only visual input, consistently failed to infer tactile properties, resulting in random-level success.
- **Linguistic vs. Raw Tactile Images:** Providing raw tactile images improved performance for roughness (100% success) but was poor for force (50%) and hardness (60%). In contrast, linguistic tactile descriptions generated by Octopi dramatically improved performance, achieving 90% success for force prediction and 75% for hardness.
- **Conclusion:** Tactile feedback is essential for planning in visually ambiguous, contact-rich tasks, and structured, language-based representations are significantly more effective for VLM-based planning than raw tactile inputs.

**Q2. Does tactile feedback benefit VLA policies for control? How does the interpolant-based diffusion compare against a simple residual controller?**
- **Enhanced Manipulation with Tactile Feedback:** Tactile feedback substantially benefited VLA policies for manipulation control.
- **Superiority of Interpolant Controller:** The interpolant-based controller (VLA-Touch) consistently outperformed both the vanilla RDT (base VLA) and an RNN-based residual controller.
 - It improved RDT task success rates by 42% (Cup), 140% (Wipe), and 67% (Peel).
 - It outperformed the residual controller by 67% (Cup), 100% (Wipe), and 42% (Peel) in task success rates.
- **Qualitative Improvements:**
 - **Cup:** Interpolant controller corrected RDT's imprecise grasping, achieving higher pick success (12/20 vs 9/20 RDT) and superior contact awareness during placement (only 1/12 premature release).
 - **Wipe:** Better grasping and pressure modulation, successfully completing full wiping in 12/20 trials (vs. 5/20 RDT).
 - **Peel:** Maintained consistent contact throughout peeling, removing more material and producing fewer short fragments (13 pieces with 3 short fragments vs. 12 pieces with 5 short fragments for residual controller).
- **Conclusion:** Tactile feedback is crucial for precise manipulation, and the interpolant-based diffusion controller is more effective than simple residual controllers in integrating tactile data for action refinement, likely due to its ability to better capture multi-modal action distributions.

**Q3. How does our dual-level tactile feedback system benefit performance in contact-rich tasks? What if we have only high-level or low-level feedback?**
- **Necessity of Dual-Level Feedback:** Ablation studies confirmed that both planning-level and control-level tactile feedback are critical for optimal performance.
 - Removing planning-level tactile feedback ("w/o Planning") reduced success rates by 44% (Cup), 58% (Wipe), and 14% (Peel) compared to the full VLA-Touch system. This led to incorrect object selections (e.g., picking the wrong sponge).
 - Removing control-level tactile feedback ("w/o Control") decreased performance by 33% (Cup), 58% (Wipe), and 43% (Peel). This resulted in failures like failed grasping, insufficient pressure, or premature releases.
- **Conclusion:** The dual-level integration of tactile feedback is essential; neither planning-level nor control-level tactile information alone can achieve the same level of robustness and precision.

**Ablation Study of Interpolant Controller (Multi-modal Necessity):**
- **Importance of Both Modalities:** Both tactile and visual feedback are essential for the interpolant controller.
 - **"w/o Touch":** Degradation was severe in contact-intensive phases (e.g., cup placement success dropped by 50%, wiping by 42%, peeling by 50%), highlighting tactile feedback's role in detecting contact forces and friction.
 - **"w/o Vision":** Less severe but noticeable degradation, particularly affecting spatial positioning and overall task success (e.g., cup placement reduced by 30%, complete wiping by 33%). Visual feedback is crucial for initial grasp positioning and global context.
- **Conclusion:** Multi-modal sensor fusion (vision and touch) is critical for optimal performance in robot manipulation, with tactile feedback being particularly vital for direct contact interactions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VLA-Touch makes several significant contributions to the field of embodied AI and robotics:

- **Pioneering Dual-Level Tactile Integration:** It introduces a novel, modular framework that effectively integrates tactile feedback at both high-level task planning and low-level action refinement, a crucial step beyond vision-centric VLA models.
- **Enhanced Generalist Robot Policies:** By demonstrating improved task planning efficiency (up to 40%) and execution precision (up to 140% higher task success rates in some tasks) in contact-rich scenarios, VLA-Touch significantly expands the capabilities of generalist robot policies.
- **Modular and Non-Invasive Approach:** A key strength is the ability to enhance VLA models *without fine-tuning the base VLA model itself*. This makes the approach highly adaptable and scalable, as it can leverage existing powerful VLA models and tactile foundation models without costly retraining, facilitating rapid deployment and experimentation.
- **Diffusion-Based Tactile Control:** The successful application of an interpolant-based diffusion controller (BRIDGeR) for refining VLA actions with tactile signals showcases a powerful new paradigm for precise, contact-aware manipulation.
- **Empirical Validation in Real-World:** Comprehensive real-world experiments across diverse contact-rich tasks provide strong empirical evidence for the framework's effectiveness, grounding the theoretical advancements in practical robotic applications.
- **Open-Sourced Code:** The open-sourcing of the code promotes reproducibility and accelerates further research in tactile-aware embodied agents.

The potential impact of VLA-Touch is substantial. It paves the way for more dexterous and intelligent robots capable of performing a wider array of real-world tasks that require delicate touch, precise force control, and object property inference. This could revolutionize applications in manufacturing (e.g., assembly of delicate components), healthcare (e.g., assistive robotics, surgical tasks), logistics (e.g., handling diverse packages), and even domestic robotics. The framework serves as a "modular scaffold" for future research, inviting exploration into richer linguistic abstractions of tactile feedback, continuous tactile embeddings, and tighter integration mechanisms. By bridging the gap between perception, language, and the physical nuances of touch, VLA-Touch takes a critical step towards creating truly embodied and robust intelligent agents.
