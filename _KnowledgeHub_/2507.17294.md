# VLA-Touch: Enhancing Vision-Language-Action Models with Dual-Level Tactile Feedback

> [!summary] Summary
> VLA-Touch enhances Vision-Language-Action models by integrating tactile feedback at both planning and control levels, significantly improving performance in contact-rich manipulation tasks. The framework demonstrates up to 140% higher task success rates in real-world scenarios compared to vision-only VLA models, leveraging linguistic tactile descriptions for high-level reasoning and an interpolant-based diffusion controller for precise action refinement.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models primarily rely on visual and proprioceptive feedback, limiting their ability to perform contact-rich manipulation tasks that require understanding object compliance, texture, and precise contact events.
- A lack of large-scale multi-modal datasets and intrinsic architectural mechanisms in current VLA models prevents effective processing and reasoning over tactile inputs.
- Integrating tactile sensing often requires extensive fine-tuning of base VLA models or is limited to task-specific policies, hindering generalizability and scalability.

## Method

- **VLA-Touch** introduces a modular framework that integrates dual-level tactile feedback into VLA pipelines without fine-tuning the base VLA model, combining a ==Task Planner==, a Tactile-Language Model, and a Tactile-Augmented Controller.
- At the planning level, a VLM (GPT-4o) generates manipulation instructions, receiving linguistic descriptions of tactile-inferred object properties (e.g., hardness, roughness) from a Tactile-Language Model (Octopi) for informed decision-making.
- At the control level, an interpolant-based diffusion controller (BRIDGeR) refines VLA-generated actions using real-time, low-dimensional tactile force signals, enabling precise, contact-aware movements.

## Results

- Linguistic tactile descriptions enabled GPT-4o to achieve **90%** success in force prediction and **75%** for hardness inference in planning, compared to 50-**60%** with raw tactile images and random success with visual-only input.
- The interpolant-based tactile controller improved base VLA task success rates by up to **140%** (Wipe task), outperforming a vanilla VLA by **42%** (Cup) and **67%** (Peel), and a residual controller by 67-**100%** across tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that removing planning-level tactile feedback reduced success rates by 14-**58%**, and removing control-level feedback decreased performance by 33-**58%**, highlighting the necessity of dual-level integration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tactile feedback is crucial for VLM-based planning in visually ambiguous, contact-rich tasks, with linguistic descriptions of tactile information proving more effective for planning than raw tactile images.
> - Both planning-level and control-level tactile feedback are essential for optimal performance; neither level alone achieves the same robustness and precision.
> - An interpolant-based diffusion controller effectively integrates tactile data for low-level action refinement, outperforming simple residual controllers in capturing multi-modal action distributions for precise manipulation.
