# SAE as a Crystal Ball: Interpretable Features Predict Cross-domain Transferability of LLMs without Training

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces the SAE-based Transferability Score (STS), a metric that leverages sparse autoencoders (SAEs) to predict how Large Language Models (LLMs) will transfer capabilities across domains after post-training, without requiring actual fine-tuning. The STS achieves high correlations (e.g., Pearson > 0.7) with observed performance shifts across various models and tasks.

## Problem

- A lack of predictive understanding exists regarding how post-training (e.g., SFT, RLHF) impacts the cross-domain transferability of LLMs.
- Current transferability analyses are primarily post-hoc, requiring expensive and time-consuming fine-tuning before evaluating performance shifts.
- Post-training often leads to
- model shifts
- and performance trade-offs, degrading capabilities in untargeted domains, with the underlying mechanisms being poorly understood.

## Method

- The research employs ==Sparse Autoencoders== (SAEs) to disentangle the highly polysemantic internal representations of LLMs into interpretable, monosemantic features.
- It identifies the specific SAE dimensions most affected by post-training through In-Context Learning (ICL), which acts as a proxy for actual fine-tuning.
- Two variants of the SAE-based Transferability Score (STS) are proposed (STS_act and STS_ICL) to quantify the correlation between these predicted feature shifts and downstream domain performance, computed on the pre-trained model without fine-tuning.

## Results

- The STS consistently achieved strong Pearson correlations, often exceeding **0.7** and reaching up to **0.90**, with actual absolute performance changes across Qwen2.5, Llama3, and Gemma2 models fine-tuned on diverse tasks.
- The method demonstrated generalizability, with STS_ICL correlations of **0.77** and **0.71** for code generation and health dialogue training domains, respectively.
- STS was applied to optimize data mixture, enabling a more balanced performance across various domains by strategically allocating additional training data to domains predicted to suffer degradation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Post-training, such as supervised fine-tuning, primarily induces changes in a small, identifiable subset of an LLM's monosemantic SAE features, rather than uniformly across all features.
> - In-Context Learning can reliably identify these task-relevant feature shifts in SAE space, demonstrating significant overlap with shifts caused by actual fine-tuning.
> - The monosemanticity of SAE features is crucial for accurate identification of relevant shifts, as raw, polysemantic model features exhibit more uniform and less informative changes.
