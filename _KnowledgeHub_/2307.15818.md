# RT-2: Vision-Language-Action Models Transfer Web Knowledge to Robotic Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Google DeepMind's RT-2 directly transfers knowledge from internet-scale vision-language models to robotic control systems, enabling robots to leverage extensive semantic understanding for low-level actions. This Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model demonstrates improved generalization across unseen objects, backgrounds, and environments, and exhibits emergent reasoning capabilities.

## Problem

- Translating the vast web-scale knowledge of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to robotic control systems has remained challenging.
- Traditional robot learning often requires extensive, costly, and task-specific training data, limiting generalization to new environments or tasks.
- Previous approaches using VLMs for high-level planning created a disconnect with low-level execution, preventing robots from directly leveraging semantic understanding for actions.

## Method

- Google DeepMind's **RT-2** introduces ==Vision-Language-Action (VLA)== models, directly extending VLMs (==PaLI-X== and ==PaLM-E==) to output tokenized robot actions.
- Robot actions are ==discretized into tokens== that the VLM can predict, treating robot control as a continuation of the ==language modeling task==.
- The model is simultaneously ==co-fine-tuned== on a combination of robot demonstration data and internet-scale vision-language data to integrate general knowledge with robotic capabilities.

## Results

- **RT-2** significantly outperformed previous approaches (RT-1, VC-1, R3M) in generalization across unseen objects, backgrounds, and environments.
- The model demonstrated emergent capabilities not explicitly trained, including reasoning (e.g., interpreting abstract numerical commands), symbol understanding (e.g., brand logos), and human recognition.
- RT-2 successfully executed sequential tasks and followed complex instructions, leveraging its semantic understanding to plan appropriate actions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Web-scale Vision-Language Models (VLMs) can directly transfer knowledge to robotic control, enabling robots to leverage extensive semantic understanding.
> - Co-fine-tuning on both robot demonstration data and internet-scale vision-language data is crucial for maintaining general knowledge while learning robotic actions.
> - Scaling up the VLM, as seen with the 55B parameter model, significantly improves a robot's generalization capabilities and enables emergent behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in understanding and reasoning across text and images. However, translating this web-scale knowledge to robotic control systems has remained challenging. Google DeepMind's RT-2 (Robotics Transformer 2) represents a significant step forward in bridging this gap, directly transferring knowledge from internet-scale vision-language models to robotic control.

Unlike previous approaches that use VLMs primarily for high-level planning while relegating execution to separate controllers, RT-2 directly extends VLMs to output robot actions, creating what the authors term Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This integration allows robots to leverage the extensive semantic knowledge embedded in web-scale models for low-level control tasks.

### 2. Background and Context

Traditional approaches to robot learning typically require extensive task-specific training data collected through robot interactions. This process is time-consuming, expensive, and limits generalization to new environments or tasks. Alternative approaches have explored using VLMs for high-level planning while implementing separate controllers for execution, but this creates a disconnect between the semantic understanding of the VLM and the robot's actions.

RT-2 builds upon previous work including:

1. **Vision-Language Models**: Models like PaLI-X and PaLM-E that combine visual and language understanding.
2. **Robotics Transformer 1 (RT-1)**: A predecessor model that demonstrated the effectiveness of transformer architectures for robotic manipulation.
3. **End-to-end robot learning**: Approaches that map observations directly to robot actions.

The key innovation in RT-2 is the direct integration of VLMs into the robotic control loop, allowing robots to benefit from the rich semantic knowledge these models have acquired from web-scale pretraining.

### 3. Vision-Language-Action Models

RT-2 introduces Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models as an extension of VLMs. These models are designed to:

1. Take in visual observations and text instructions
2. Process this multimodal input to understand the task and environment
3. Output tokenized robot actions that can be directly executed

The architecture builds on existing VLMs (specifically PaLI-X and PaLM-E) by adapting them to predict robot action tokens. This approach allows the model to treat robot control as a continuation of the language modeling task, making it possible to leverage the knowledge embedded in the pre-trained weights.

Key to this approach is the tokenization of robot actions:

```
Robot Action = {ΔTranslation = [0.1, -0.2, 0], ΔRotation = [10°, 25°, -7°]}
```

These continuous robot actions are discretized into tokens that can be predicted by the language model, then detokenized for robot execution. This tokenization approach creates a unified framework where the model can reason about both language and robot actions in the same representational space.

### 4. RT-2 Methodology

RT-2 employs two main architectural variants:

1. **RT-2-PaLI-X**: Based on the PaLI-X model with 55 billion parameters, which uses a ViT (Vision Transformer) for image encoding and a language model decoder.

2. **RT-2-PaLM-E**: Based on the PaLM-E model with 12 billion parameters, which embeds visual tokens directly into the language model.

Both variants follow a similar process:
- Images from robot cameras are processed by a vision encoder
- Image features are combined with text instructions in the language model
- The language model predicts action tokens
- These tokens are detokenized into continuous robot actions

Since these large models are computationally intensive, RT-2 implements a cloud-based inference system to achieve real-time control. The robot sends observations to a multi-TPU cloud service, which runs the model inference and returns actions to be executed by the robot.

### 5. Training Approach

RT-2 employs a novel co-fine-tuning approach that distinguishes it from previous methods:

1. **Pre-training**: The model starts with a pre-trained VLM (either PaLI-X or PaLM-E)

2. **Co-fine-tuning**: The model is simultaneously fine-tuned on:
 - Robot demonstration data (approximately 100,000 episodes)
 - Internet-scale vision-language data (millions of image-text pairs)

This co-fine-tuning approach is crucial to RT-2's success, as it allows the model to maintain its general knowledge from pre-training while learning to apply this knowledge to robotic control. The authors found that without co-fine-tuning, the model could forget its general knowledge when fine-tuned solely on robot data.

The training objective combines:
- A robot action prediction loss (predicting the correct action tokens for robot demonstrations)
- A visual question answering loss (maintaining general vision-language capabilities)

### 6. Experimental Results

RT-2 was evaluated on a range of robotic manipulation tasks to assess its generalization capabilities. The evaluation focused on three key dimensions:

1. **Generalization to unseen objects**: Testing the robot's ability to manipulate objects not seen during training

2. **Generalization to unseen backgrounds**: Testing performance when objects are placed on different surfaces

3. **Generalization to unseen environments**: Testing in completely new settings like kitchens or offices

The results, shown in Figure 3, demonstrate that RT-2 significantly outperforms previous approaches like RT-1, VC-1 (Vision-Conditioned Language models), and R3M (a self-supervised representation learning approach):

RT-2 with PaLI-X-55B (the larger model) achieved the best performance across all generalization scenarios, with RT-2 with PaLM-E-12B also showing strong results. The performance gap between RT-2 and previous approaches is particularly pronounced in challenging generalization scenarios like unseen environments.

The authors also demonstrated that RT-2 can successfully complete sequences of actions through examples like pushing objects to specific locations.

### 7. Emergent Capabilities

One of the most striking findings is that RT-2 demonstrates several emergent capabilities not explicitly present in the robot training data. These include:

1. **Reasoning**: The ability to interpret commands requiring reasoning, such as "move the banana to the sum of two plus one" (placing it on a paper with "3" written on it)

2. **Symbol understanding**: Recognizing and acting on symbolic representations like brand logos, country flags, and letters

3. **Human recognition**: Identifying individuals from photos and following instructions referencing them

These capabilities emerge from the knowledge embedded in the pre-trained VLM, transferred to the robotic control setting through co-fine-tuning. The model can process instructions like "move the coke can to Taylor Swift" by leveraging its understanding of who Taylor Swift is from its pre-training data, despite never being explicitly trained on this task with robots.

RT-2 also demonstrates the ability to follow chain-of-thought reasoning when prompted appropriately. For example, when asked "I need to hammer a nail, what object from the scene might be useful?", the model can reason about object properties and identify a rock as a potential hammer substitute.

### 8. Ablation Studies

The authors conducted extensive ablation studies to understand what factors contribute to RT-2's performance. The key findings include:

1. **Model scale matters**: Larger models (55B parameters) consistently outperform smaller models (12B parameters) across all evaluation metrics.

2. **Co-fine-tuning is crucial**: Models co-fine-tuned on both robot data and web data significantly outperform models fine-tuned only on robot data or trained from scratch.

These results highlight that both the scale of the model and the training approach contribute significantly to RT-2's generalization capabilities.

### 9. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its impressive capabilities, RT-2 has several limitations:

1. **Computational requirements**: The large model size requires cloud-based inference, limiting deployment on edge devices.

2. **Action representation**: The current action space is limited to translation and rotation, which restricts the types of manipulation tasks that can be performed.

3. **Safety considerations**: As with any AI system, there are important safety considerations when deploying these models in real-world scenarios.

Future work directions include:
- Developing more efficient inference techniques
- Exploring quantization and distillation to run models on less powerful hardware
- Expanding the action space to include more complex manipulations
- Incorporating feedback mechanisms for more robust control

### 10. Conclusion

RT-2 represents a significant advance in robot learning by demonstrating that knowledge from web-scale vision-language models can be directly transferred to robotic control. By treating robot actions as an extension of language generation, RT-2 enables robots to leverage the rich semantic knowledge embedded in these large models.

The emergent capabilities shown by RT-2—reasoning, symbol understanding, and human recognition—suggest that this approach can yield robots with more general intelligence that can adapt to a wide range of tasks and environments without task-specific training.

RT-2's success in generalizing to unseen objects, backgrounds, and environments points to a promising direction for future robot learning research, where robots can benefit from the same large-scale pre-training paradigm that has driven recent advances in natural language processing and computer vision.
