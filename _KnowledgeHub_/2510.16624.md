# Self-Supervised Learning to Fly using Efficient Semantic Segmentation and Metric Depth Estimation for Low-Cost Autonomous UAVs

> [!summary] Summary
> Mocanu, Slusanschi, and Leordeanu developed a vision-only autonomous flight system for low-cost UAVs, integrating real-time semantic segmentation with a novel adaptive scale factor algorithm for metric depth estimation. Their system achieved a mean distance error of 14.4 cm for metric depth and enabled robust obstacle avoidance with 100% mission success rates for exploration and landing in simulated and real-world tests.

## Problem

- High cost and complexity of multi-sensor setups (e.g., LiDAR, GPS) for indoor autonomous UAV navigation.
- Difficulty in obtaining accurate, real-time metric depth from monocular cameras, which is crucial for precise obstacle avoidance.
- Computational constraints of low-cost UAV platforms hindering the deployment of resource-intensive perception and control systems.

## Method

- A multi-task learning framework combining efficient semantic segmentation and an adaptive metric depth estimation algorithm for comprehensive scene understanding.
- A novel adaptive scale factor algorithm that converts non-metric monocular depth predictions into real-time metric distances using camera intrinsics and ground plane detection.
- Utilizing knowledge distillation to train a lightweight U-Net for real-time semantic segmentation and an end-to-end student control network for onboard flight policy execution.

## Results

- The adaptive scale factor algorithm achieved a mean metric depth error of **14.4** cm (median **16.28** cm) and a median inference time of **0.0206** seconds (**48.54** FPS).
- The combined depth and segmentation approach demonstrated **100%** mission success rates for obstacle avoidance, exploration, and helipad landing in real-world and digital-twin environments.
- A lightweight student control network (1.6M parameters), trained via self-supervised knowledge distillation, achieved an **87.5%** autonomous mission success rate suitable for onboard execution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Real-time, accurate metric depth estimation from monocular vision is achievable for low-cost UAVs by leveraging camera parameters and ground plane detection, significantly improving obstacle avoidance.
> - Knowledge distillation can effectively transfer complex perception and control policies from robust teacher models to lightweight student networks, enabling deployment on resource-constrained embedded systems.
> - Integrating semantic segmentation with metric depth provides a more robust and efficient scene understanding compared to purely semantic-based approaches for autonomous navigation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of autonomous aerial systems, specifically focusing on vision-only navigation for Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) in structured indoor environments. The broader landscape of autonomous drone navigation has evolved from relying on expensive multi-sensor setups (e.g., LiDAR, GPS, IMUs) to more cost-effective vision-based approaches. However, vision-only systems often face challenges related to accurate depth perception (especially metric depth) and computational efficiency for real-time deployment on resource-constrained platforms.

The paper positions itself within recent advancements in deep learning for perception, guidance, and navigation. It builds upon existing work in monocular depth estimation, particularly self-supervised methods that reduce reliance on extensive ground-truth data, and semantic segmentation, which provides environmental understanding for tasks like obstacle avoidance and landing. Unlike many systems that treat semantic segmentation and depth estimation as separate tasks, this work integrates them into a multi-task learning framework. It also addresses the critical problem of converting non-metric monocular depth predictions into accurate metric distances, a common limitation in vision-based systems. Furthermore, the research explores knowledge distillation and end-to-end learning to achieve a lightweight control network suitable for embedded deployment, aligning with efforts to optimize performance for edge computing on small drones. This approach contrasts with planning-based systems that require global optimization, instead favoring reactive navigation based on real-time visual understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a vision-only autonomous flight system for small UAVs capable of operating in controlled indoor environments without requiring external positioning systems like GPS or expensive sensors such as LiDAR.

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Obstacle Avoidance and Scene Exploration:** The system aims to enable UAVs to autonomously detect and avoid obstacles, navigate through an environment, and explore a scene. This is motivated by the need for safer and more efficient drone operations in confined spaces.
- **Autonomous Safe Landing:** A critical objective is to facilitate autonomous safe landing operations. This involves identifying designated landing zones and executing precise landing procedures, which is essential for mission completion and drone recovery.
- **Accurate Metric Depth Estimation from Monocular Vision:** A significant challenge in vision-only systems is obtaining accurate metric distance measurements from monocular cameras, as most monocular depth prediction systems provide only non-metric (relative) depth. The motivation is to overcome this limitation by developing an innovative algorithm that converts non-metric monocular depth predictions into reliable metric distances, thereby enhancing obstacle avoidance and flight safety.
- **Real-time Semantic Segmentation for Environmental Understanding:** The system seeks to recognize semantic classes within the environment (e.g., obstacles, safe paths, landing areas) in real-time. This is crucial for intelligent decision-making and is motivated by the need for comprehensive scene understanding, particularly for identifying areas suitable for navigation and landing.
- **Computational Efficiency for Low-Cost Platforms:** A core motivation is to ensure the system can operate on resource-constrained, low-cost UAV platforms. This involves developing lightweight models and control policies capable of real-time execution, addressing the computational demands typically associated with complex perception tasks.
- **Self-Supervised Learning for Adaptability:** The research aims to reduce dependency on extensive ground-truth data and external systems by leveraging self-supervised learning methods for depth estimation and a knowledge distillation framework for semantic segmentation. This is motivated by the desire to increase drone adaptability to new environments and minimize human intervention in data labeling.
- **Robust Validation:** The motivation includes thoroughly validating the proposed methods in both real-world and simulated environments to ensure reliability, robustness, and to mitigate risks associated with physical drone testing.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system integrates semantic segmentation and monocular metric depth estimation for autonomous UAV flight. The methodology involves a multi-level approach, starting with teacher models for perception tasks, followed by knowledge distillation to a lightweight student network for end-to-end control.

#### 3.1. A. Architecture and Setup

The system is designed for real-time processing of visual data to make intelligent flight decisions. Initially, it operates on a laptop connected to the drone via Wi-Fi. The core components include a relative depth model, a semantic segmentation model, and an adaptive scale factor algorithm that utilizes camera parameters to convert relative depth to metric depth. These components generate drone control commands (translations and rotations) which then serve as training data for a compact student neural network.

The experimental environment is a controlled 5x4 meter indoor laboratory. Featureless walls and floor initially posed stability challenges for the drone’s optical flow. This was mitigated by introducing a textured 5x4 meter carpet. Eight cardboard box obstacles, wrapped with distinct patterns, were introduced to simulate an urban landscape and serve as collision targets. A designated helipad box was also included. A digital twin environment, replicating the physical setup in Unreal Engine and Parrot Sphinx simulator, was created for extensive testing and algorithm refinement.

#### 3.2. B. Dataset

A custom dataset was collected in the indoor laboratory, featuring four different scene configurations with the obstacles. Data collection involved flying a Parrot ANAFI drone for approximately 2-3 minutes per session, starting from the helipad, with the camera angled at -25 to -30 degrees. Landing sequences were captured with the gimbal at 90 degrees. The dataset comprises nine videos, totaling 39648 frames (22 minutes of flight), divided into seven for training and two for validation. The digital twin replicated these scenes for simulated data collection, generating 124542 frames for training and 30725 for testing. Camera calibration was performed using a checkerboard method, with images downscaled to 320x256 pixels.

#### 3.3. C. First Level - Semantic Segmentation

The semantic segmentation component is designed for efficiency in controlled environments where object textures are known.
1. **Teacher Model (SVM):** A color-based Support Vector Machine (SVM) acts as the teacher. It is trained on HSV features extracted from 40x40 pixel patches manually selected from 17 different classes (background, carpet, helipad, various boxes). The SVM uses a One-vs-Rest classifier. Inference for a single frame with this patch-based SVM was computationally intensive, taking 6-7 seconds. Morphological dilation and connected component analysis were used to refine segmentations and remove misclassifications.
2. **Student Model (U-Net):** To achieve real-time performance, a lightweight U-Net architecture (1.6M parameters) is trained using knowledge distillation. The SVM teacher generates segmentation masks for a subset of the dataset (3474 frames, 224x112 resolution), which serve as ground truth for the U-Net. Augmentations (Hue rotation, Saturation/Value adjustment, translations, rotations) were applied. Categorical Cross Entropy loss over 17-mask segmentation yielded the best results.
3. **Helipad Detection:** The semantic network identifies the helipad by detecting its two components ("H" sign and striped box pattern) and using component analysis (spatial proximity, connectivity, geometric compactness) to filter false positives.

#### 3.4. D. Second Level - Depth Estimation

Monocular depth estimation provides relative depth, which is insufficient for metric control.
1. **Relative Depth Model:** The system utilizes unsupervised SC-Depth variants, fine-tuned on the custom laboratory dataset. These models predict depth values between 0 and 1 for each pixel, using camera intrinsic parameters.
2. **Initial Metric Conversion Attempts:** Early attempts to convert relative depth to metric depth involved dividing by ground truth values or using least squares regression with a shift parameter. These methods proved unreliable, especially for objects farther away, with errors reaching approximately 100 cm.

#### 3.5. E. Enhanced Depth - Adaptive Scale Factor

To overcome the limitations of non-metric depth, an adaptive scale factor algorithm was developed.
1. **Real-time Scale Factor Calculation:** This algorithm leverages the drone's camera intrinsic parameters (K), gimbal steering angle to determine the rotation matrix (R), and known height above the ground plane (translation vector T). By detecting the segmented carpet (ground plane), the system computes the 3D world coordinates of 50 randomly selected points on the carpet. The Euclidean distance from the camera to these points is then used to calculate an adaptive scale factor in real-time. This method accounts for camera offset along the Z-axis (C) and focal length (f).
2. **3D Virtual Safety Corridor:** For obstacle avoidance, a virtual safety box is established around the drone. This consists of five planes at predetermined metric distances (e.g., lateral planes at 0.5 meters, frontal plane at 2 meters, vertical planes at ±0.8 meters). The algorithm computes intersection points with these planes for any point in the depth map using derived scaling equations (Equations 10a-10e). The minimum Euclidean distance to these planes determines collision risks, guiding drone movements.

#### 3.6. F. Autonomous Control

Two main autonomous control methods were implemented and evaluated, both handling obstacle avoidance, scene exploration, and return-to-base functions.
1. **Self-flying with Segmentation Only:** This lightweight method relies solely on semantic segmentation. It analyzes pixel counts of obstacle classes in an "upper region" and carpet/scene boundary classes in a "lower region" of the image. If an obstacle is detected, the drone rotates towards the side with fewer obstacle pixels. If it approaches scene boundaries, it rotates towards more carpet pixels. Movements are small (e.g., 30 cm forward) and sequential.
2. **Self-flying with Segmentation and Metric Depth:** This enhanced method integrates the adaptive scale factor and 3D virtual safety box. By understanding metric distances to obstacles, the drone can predict maximum safe travel distances, allowing for larger, more efficient movements (e.g., up to 1 meter forward when clear). The 3D safety corridor enables real-time decisions by assessing collision risks in 3D space. Scene boundary maintenance also uses metric distance information.
3. **Return to Base:** Both methods initiate a helipad-finding state after a fixed duration (90 seconds). The drone flies stochastically until the helipad is detected. A PID controller then guides the drone to center the helipad, adjusts the gimbal to a top-down view, and executes a precise landing sequence.

#### 3.7. G. Student Control Network

To enable onboard execution on small drones, a compact student neural network (1.6M parameters) was trained. This network learns complete flight policies (movement decisions) in a self-supervised manner from demonstration data generated by the best-performing teacher algorithms (segmentation and metric depth). The network takes the current RGB frame and the previous two frames (within a 1-second window) as input, predicting the next movement through dense layers, eschewing the upsampling layers of a U-Net.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive validation across real-world and digital-twin environments, demonstrating the capabilities and limitations of the proposed vision-based approaches.

#### 4.1. A. Semantic Segmentation Performance

- **Teacher Model (SVM):** The initial SVM-based teacher model for semantic segmentation had an inference time of approximately 6-7 seconds per frame, which was too slow for real-time applications.
- **Student Model (U-Net):** The lightweight U-Net student network (1.6M parameters) trained via knowledge distillation achieved real-time performance. It produced more accurate and consistent segmentation results compared to the SVM teacher, especially after applying Categorical Cross Entropy loss and specific data augmentations. The U-Net effectively learned to segment 17 classes relevant for navigation, including helipad, obstacles, carpet, and background.

#### 4.2. B. Depth Estimation and Adaptive Scale Factor Performance

- **Relative Depth Models:** Models pretrained on general datasets like NYU Depth V2 showed inconsistent depth estimates when applied to the custom laboratory environment. Fine-tuned SC-Depth variants performed better but required conversion to metric depth.
- **Adaptive Scale Factor:** The novel adaptive scale factor algorithm successfully converted non-metric monocular depth predictions into metric measurements.
 - **Accuracy:** It achieved a mean distance error of 14.4 cm (median 16.28 cm) on ground truth measurements for various points in the scene (Table III). For obstacle dimension estimation over 661 measurements, it demonstrated a mean error of 0.0740 m, a median error of 0.0597 m, and a Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) of 0.0988 m, with a correlation coefficient of 0.8342. This significantly outperformed state-of-the-art models like MoGe and DepthPro in accuracy metrics (Table I), particularly for measurements farther from the camera.
 - **Efficiency:** The adaptive scale factor computation, combined with the depth model, achieved a median inference time of 0.0206 seconds per image, translating to 48.54 FPS (Table II). This is substantially faster than MoGe (3.43 FPS) and DepthPro (0.33 FPS), making it suitable for real-time drone applications.
 - **Requirements:** This method requires knowledge of camera intrinsic parameters, the position of the ground plane, and the drone's height relative to it.
- **3D Virtual Safety Corridor:** The implementation of a five-plane virtual safety box (lateral planes at 0.5m, frontal at 2m, vertical at ±0.8m) enabled real-time assessment of collision risks in 3D space, improving obstacle avoidance.

#### 4.3. C. Vision-Based Autonomous Flying Performance

- **Validation:** Extensive validation involved 30 flight tests in the real-world environment and 100 flight tests in the digital-twin environment. Results showed a strong correlation between real and simulated environments, confirming the digital-twin's utility.
- **Segmentation-Only vs. Depth and Segmentation:**
 - The combined depth and segmentation approach consistently outperformed the segmentation-only method.
 - **Mission Success:** Both the segmentation-only and the combined depth and segmentation approaches maintained 100% mission success rates during the reported testing phase (meaning no collisions or exits from the flight area during the evaluation of these methods).
 - **Efficiency (Time):** The depth and segmentation method demonstrated significantly faster convergence times for helipad detection (distribution peaked around 10-15 seconds) and for the combined helipad finding and landing task (peak density around 20 seconds).
 - **Efficiency (Distance):** The depth and segmentation approach allowed for greater spatial exploration, with random searching distances extending up to 25 meters. In contrast, the segmentation-only method's random searching distances were clustered around 5 meters, indicating more limited spatial exploration. This suggests that metric depth enabled the drone to make larger, more confident movements.

#### 4.4. D. Student Control Network Performance

- **Mission Success Rate:** The lightweight student neural network (1.6M parameters), trained end-to-end from the teacher's demonstration data, achieved an 87.5% autonomous mission success rate (35 successful flights out of 40 attempts).
- **Failure Analysis:** The remaining 12.5% of missions resulted in crashes, primarily due to collisions with obstacle boxes. This indicates that while compact and computationally efficient, the direct policy learning approach from demonstration data currently has limitations in robustness compared to the modular teacher system. Data augmentation focusing on segments near obstacles provided a slight improvement over initial attempts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in vision-only autonomous drone navigation, particularly for resource-constrained platforms operating in structured indoor environments.

- **Enabling Low-Cost Autonomy:** The development of an efficient and accurate adaptive scale factor algorithm for converting non-metric monocular depth to metric measurements is a significant technical contribution. This capability, combined with lightweight semantic segmentation, addresses a fundamental challenge in making autonomous UAVs more accessible by removing the need for expensive sensors like LiDAR or GPS.
- **Enhanced Safety and Efficiency:** The integration of metric depth estimation with semantic understanding allows for the creation of a 3D virtual safety corridor. This enables more precise obstacle avoidance, dynamic flight path optimization, and more efficient navigation (e.g., faster mission completion, greater exploration distance) compared to purely semantic-based approaches. This has direct implications for improving safety margins and operational throughput for autonomous systems.
- **Practical Deployment on Embedded Systems:** The successful application of a knowledge distillation framework to train a compact U-Net for real-time semantic segmentation, and further distilling the entire control policy into a 1.6M-parameter student network, demonstrates practical solutions for deploying complex perception and control systems directly onto small, onboard drone processors. This moves towards fully autonomous operations without external computational dependencies.
- **Reduced Data Dependency:** By leveraging unsupervised learning for depth estimation and a knowledge distillation approach for semantic segmentation from a weakly-supervised teacher, the method reduces reliance on extensively labeled datasets, potentially increasing adaptability to new, controlled environments.
- **Broader Applications:** The demonstrated capabilities in controlled indoor environments (e.g., obstacle avoidance, scene surveying, safe landing) have immediate implications for applications in domains such as warehouse automation, indoor surveillance, and structured public spaces like theme parks. The authors also suggest that with modifications, such as integrating state-of-the-art semantic segmentation models, the core methodology could be adapted for outdoor environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** While the student network's 87.5% success rate indicates room for improvement, its compact size and self-supervised learning paradigm lay a foundation for future work aimed at enhancing the reliability of end-to-end learning for robust, fully embedded autonomous flight. Future directions include addressing dynamic obstacles and more sophisticated path planning.
