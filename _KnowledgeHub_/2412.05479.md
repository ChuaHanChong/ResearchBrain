# LATTE: Learning to Think with Vision Specialists

> [!summary] Summary
> The LATTE framework trains open-source vision-language models to perform complex multimodal reasoning by integrating and orchestrating 15 specialized vision tools. This approach yields 4-5% average accuracy improvements across diverse benchmarks and up to 10-20% gains on complex reasoning tasks like MMVet, outperforming models that do not explicitly leverage external vision specialists.

## Problem

- Open-source vision-language models (VLMs) demonstrate a performance gap compared to proprietary, large-scale models when handling complex multimodal reasoning tasks.
- Smaller VLMs often exhibit generalized perception limitations, underperforming dedicated computer vision specialist models in specific perceptual tasks.
- A lack of large-scale, high-quality training data exists for teaching VLMs to effectively perform multi-step reasoning by interacting with external tools.

## Method

- Develops a novel =='learning to reason with vision specialists'== paradigm, where VLMs generate explicit thoughts and actions to call specialized vision tools for perceptual information.
- Introduces ==`LATTE-trace`==, a `(thought, action, observation)` data format, used to train models on how to utilize 15 diverse vision specialists (e.g., OCR, object detection, depth estimation).
- Generates a large-scale dataset of ==293K `LATTE-traces`== using a dual approach: a GPT-4o-based engine for diverse scenarios and a programmatic engine for specific capabilities, both with rigorous filtering for correctness.

## Results

- LATTE consistently achieves **4-5%** average accuracy gains over vanilla instruction-tuned baselines across six diverse benchmarks, with improvements up to **10-20%** on reasoning-heavy benchmarks like MMVet.
- The method demonstrates superior performance compared to other distillation baselines (e.g., VPD, LLaVa-CoT), which aim to distill both perception and reasoning from larger generalist models.
- Careful data filtering to include only 'LATTE-useful' traces (where specialists lead to correct answers) proves more beneficial than simply increasing data volume, and poor-quality traces can degrade performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling perception from reasoning by delegating perceptual tasks to specialized vision tools is an effective strategy for significantly enhancing the reasoning abilities of open-source VLMs.
> - The quality of reasoning traces, particularly the correctness of tool usage and alignment with ground truth, is more critical for training effective multimodal reasoners than the sheer quantity of data.
> - The accuracy of individual vision specialists (e.g., OCR) directly impacts the overall performance of the VLM, highlighting the importance of high-quality tools within the framework.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

LATTE is situated at the intersection of **vision-language models (VLMs)**, **multimodal reasoning**, and **AI agentic capabilities (tool-use)**. The paper explicitly addresses a critical challenge in the current AI landscape: the performance gap between proprietary, large-scale multimodal models (like GPT-4o) and smaller, open-source VLMs, especially when tackling complex questions requiring both advanced perception and multi-step reasoning.

Historically, computer vision and natural language processing developed as largely separate fields. Recent years have seen a convergence with the rise of VLMs, aiming to unify these capabilities. However, the authors argue that current open-source VLMs, while strong in language understanding, still "lag behind" in "heterogeneous vision capabilities." They highlight that the computer vision community has achieved state-of-the-art results by developing *specialized* models for tasks like depth estimation (e.g., DepthAnything) or object recognition (e.g., GroundingDINO), while unified VLMs often treat visual encoders as an "afterthought." This mirrors the human brain's specialized visual processing pathways (ventral for recognition, dorsal for spatial reasoning).

LATTE proposes a paradigm shift from "learning to perceive and reason" to "learning to reason with vision specialists." This approach leverages the decades of advancements in specialized computer vision models, offloading the intricate perceptual tasks to these highly capable "vision specialists." The VLM then focuses *solely* on acquiring perceptual information from these specialists and reasoning over it, effectively "standing on the shoulders of giants." This contrasts with many existing VLM paradigms that try to train a single, monolithic model to master both perception and reasoning from raw pixels.

The work also contributes to the burgeoning field of **multi-modal tool-use** and **chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning** in VLMs. While prior work like LLaVa-Plus, Visual Program Distillation (VPD), Visual CoT, LLaVa-CoT, and CogCoM have explored training models to use external tools or generate reasoning traces, LATTE differentiates itself by:
- **Scale and Diversity:** Introducing a significantly larger and more diverse dataset of reasoning traces (293K over 31 datasets, including multi-image questions and image-text interleaved traces).
- **Quality of Perception:** Explicitly relying on state-of-the-art *vision specialists* for perception, rather than distilling potentially inferior perceptual abilities from a generalist proprietary VLM (which LLaVa-CoT, for example, does).
- **Structured Reasoning Traces:** Formalizing multi-step reasoning as `LATTE-trace` (thought-action-observation), which provides a clear and interpretable path for the model's decision-making process.

By focusing on a division of labor (specialized perception, generalist reasoning), LATTE attempts to bridge the performance gap for open-source VLMs more effectively, offering a promising direction for developing more capable and efficient multimodal AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of LATTE is to **enhance the reasoning capabilities of open-source vision-language models (VLMs) when tackling complex multimodal questions that require both strong perception and multi-step logical inference.**

The motivations underpinning this objective are multifold:

1. **Addressing the Performance Gap:** Proprietary, large-scale VLMs (like GPT-4o) excel at complex visual question answering (VQA) due to vast training data and model size. However, smaller, open-source models struggle to match this performance within practical computational budgets. The authors aim to narrow this gap for open-source counterparts.
2. **Overcoming Perception Limitations in Small VLMs:** The authors identify that a primary reason for the lag in open-source VLMs is their "perception limitations." While open-source language models have significantly advanced, unified VLMs often possess weaker, generalized vision capabilities compared to specialized computer vision models.
3. **Leveraging Decades of CV Advancements:** Instead of burdening a single VLM with mastering both perception and reasoning from scratch (a "learning to perceive and reason" paradigm), LATTE is motivated by the idea of "learning to reason with vision specialists." This allows VLMs to benefit from the highly accurate and robust perceptual outputs of dedicated, state-of-the-art vision models, enabling the VLM to focus its capacity entirely on higher-level reasoning.
4. **Creating High-Quality Training Data:** To implement this paradigm, a key motivation was the need for a large-scale, high-quality dataset of multi-step reasoning traces that explicitly demonstrate how to integrate and reason over perceptual information provided by vision specialists. Such data is expensive to acquire via human annotation, driving the development of synthetic generation methods.
5. **Gaining Insights into Multimodal Reasoning:** Beyond just improving performance, the authors are motivated to understand "what matters" in multimodal reasoning data, exploring the impact of data sources, formats, and the quality of generated "thoughts" on model performance.

In essence, LATTE seeks to enable smaller, open-source VLMs to achieve "superhuman" perception by outsourcing it to specialists, thereby freeing them to become expert "multimodal reasoners."

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LATTE methodology centers on training vision-language models to perform multi-step reasoning by explicitly interacting with and leveraging a set of specialized vision tools. This is achieved through a novel data format called `LATTE-trace` and a dual approach to large-scale synthetic data generation.

#### 3.1. The LATTE Paradigm: Reasoning with Vision Specialists

The core idea is to decouple perception from reasoning. Instead of a single VLM attempting to do both, LATTE trains VLMs to:
1. **Verbalize their thoughts (t_i)**, outlining the steps needed to solve a problem.
2. **Call specialized "vision specialists" (a_i)** as tools to acquire specific perceptual information.
3. **Receive observations (o_i)** from these tools, which are then integrated into the reasoning process.
This `(thought, action, observation)` sequence forms a `LATTE-trace`. The VLM learns to generate the `thought` and `action`, while `observation` is provided by the external tool.

#### 3.2. Action Space (Vision Specialists)

The action space `A` comprises 15 diverse tools, including:
- **Perceptual Tools:** OCR (JadedAI, GPT-based), GetObjects (RAM++), LocalizeObjects (GroundingDINO), EstimateObjectDepth/EstimateRegionDepth (Depth-Anything-v2), DetectFaces, Crop, ZoomIn, GetImageToImagesSimilarity, GetImageToTextsSimilarity, GetTextToImagesSimilarity (CLIP-based). These tools provide high-quality, fine-grained perceptual information.
- **Reasoning/Auxiliary Tools:** QueryLanguageModel, QueryKnowledgeBase, Calculate, SolveMathEquation (WolframAlpha), and Terminate (for final answer).

#### 3.3. LATTE-trace Generation

Two main data generation engines are developed to create `LATTE-trace` data at scale:

- **4.3.1. Model-based Generation (using GPT-4o)**
 1. **GENERATE:** Images and QA pairs from existing visual instruction tuning datasets (Cauldron, Mantis-Instruct, covering 31 sources, including single and multi-image questions) are fed to GPT-4o (specifically, the 2024-08-06 version). GPT-4o is prompted (using ReAct-style JSON formatting) to generate `LATTE-traces` or simpler Chain-of-Thought (CoT) traces if tools are not necessary.
 2. **VERIFY:** GPT-4o's generated answers are compared against ground truth. Only traces with *correct* final answers are retained as `LATTE-trace-pos` or `CoT-pos`. Incorrect traces (`LATTE-trace-neg`, `CoT-neg`) are converted to a `Direct` answer format (question-answer pair) using the ground truth. This is a critical step for quality control.
 3. **PARSE:** JSON syntax of generated traces is validated. Errors lead to conversion to the `Direct` format.

- **4.3.2. Programmatic Data Generation**
 1. **ANNOTATE:** Dense annotations from Visual Genome (objects, attributes, relationships) are used, supplemented by Depth-Anything-v2 for depth maps.
 2. **GENERATE:** QA pairs and corresponding `LATTE-traces` are programmatically generated using manually written templates. This approach focuses on specific vision capabilities (counting, 2D/3D spatial understanding, multi-image understanding) and aims to diversify the usage of underrepresented tools in the model-based data. Five thought templates are designed for each action, with one randomly sampled.

#### 3.4. Data Filtering and Mixing

After initial generation, three filtering/mixing techniques are applied to refine the training data:
1. **Data Format:** Manipulating the proportion of `LATTE-trace`, `CoT`, and `Direct` formats.
2. **Data Source:** Identifying and focusing on "LATTE-useful" datasets (where GPT-4o frequently used vision specialists and got correct answers) vs. "LATTE-useless" ones.
3. **Program-generated Data:** Experimenting with mixing model-generated data with programmatically generated traces to increase action diversity, particularly for less frequent tools.

#### 3.5. Training and Evaluation

- **Models:** Small 7-8B multi-modal models (Mantis-8B-SigLIP-LLaMA-3, Mantis-8B-CLIP-LLaMA-3, LLaVA-OneVision-7B, InternVL3-8B, Qwen2.5VL-3B/7B) are fine-tuned using the generated `LATTE-trace` data.
- **Baselines:** Compared against vanilla instruction-tuning (Direct answer), other distillation methods (VPD, LLaVa-CoT, VisCoT), and multi-modal agents (LLaVa-Plus, CogCoM).
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** 6 benchmarks covering both perception (RealWorldQA, CV-Bench, BLINK) and perception+reasoning (MathVista, MMStar, MMVet). An LLM judge (GPT-4-turbo) scores open-ended questions.

This comprehensive methodology, from structured trace definition to dual-engine data generation and rigorous filtering, enables the training of VLMs that can effectively delegate perceptual tasks to specialists and focus on robust reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted with LATTE yield several significant findings:

1. **Significant Performance Gains over Vanilla Instruction-Tuning:**
 - LATTE consistently achieves 4-5% average accuracy gains over vanilla instruction-tuned baselines across all 6 benchmarks (perception-focused and perception+reasoning), regardless of the base VLM (Mantis-CLIP, Mantis-SigLIP, LLaVA-OV, InternVL3, Qwen2.5VL).
 - The gains are particularly staggering on complex reasoning benchmarks like MMVet, where LATTE shows a 10-20% improvement.
 - LATTE's training data brings substantially larger gains to the base model compared to data from other multimodal agent baselines (LLaVa-Plus, CogCoM), despite comparable data sizes.

2. **Superiority to Other Distillation Baselines:**
 - LATTE outperforms distillation methods like VPD and LLaVa-CoT, which aim to distill both perception and reasoning from larger models. While these baselines offer some gains (around 1.5% average), LATTE achieves 6.4% average gains.
 - Interestingly, LLaVa-CoT, which distills from GPT-4o, sometimes leads to a degradation in performance on purely perception-focused tasks. This suggests that GPT-4o's perceptual abilities might be inferior to dedicated vision specialists in certain areas, validating LATTE's core hypothesis.
 - This trend of LATTE outperforming baselines holds consistently across different training data scales (98K, 200K, 293K).

3. **Data Quality Over Quantity is Crucial:**
 - A key ablation study reveals that the quality of `LATTE-trace` data is more important than its sheer quantity. The best performance is achieved with 293K carefully filtered `LATTE-traces` (GPT-4o generated and *correctly* answered).
 - Adding more data, such as larger mixtures including `CoT` or `Direct` examples, does not bring additional gains and can even hurt performance. For instance, mixing `LATTE-traces` with `Direct` answers leads to models adopting the `Direct` format more often at inference time, bypassing vision specialists and thus scoring lower on perception tasks.
 - Similarly, including "LATTE-useless" datasets (where GPT-4o rarely used specialists or got answers wrong with specialists) degrades performance, while focusing only on "LATTE-useful" datasets improves it. This highlights the importance of filtering for traces where specialists genuinely contribute to correct answers.

4. **Limitations of Programmatically Generated Data:**
 - While programmatically generated `LATTE-traces` can help on specific benchmarks (e.g., MathVista due to increased `LOCALIZE` instances), they generally do not bring overall gains and can even harm performance. This is attributed to the "worse reasoning quality" stemming from templated and rigid "thoughts" compared to the more nuanced, human-like reasoning generated by GPT-4o.

5. **Inference Setup Insights:**
 - The quality of the **OCR tool** is highly impactful; using a less accurate OCR (EasyOCR) significantly drops performance, especially on reasoning-intensive benchmarks like MMVet.
 - The **`QUERYLANGUAGEMODEL` tool** is found to be less critical for the evaluated VQA benchmarks.
 - A maximum of **10 tool calls** at inference time appears sufficient, as increasing it to 20 does not yield further improvements.

6. **Error Analysis:**
 - LATTE often outperforms baselines in fine-grained perception tasks like counting, where baselines tend to hallucinate.
 - Common errors for LATTE occur when the model falls back on its own (weaker) perceptual abilities by not using tools, or when tools are deemed irrelevant/unhelpful (e.g., for questions requiring domain knowledge not covered by available tools). This suggests opportunities for diversifying tools and training data for niche domains.

In summary, the main findings strongly endorse the "learning to reason with vision specialists" paradigm, demonstrating its empirical effectiveness for open-source VLMs and providing crucial insights into the characteristics of high-quality multimodal reasoning data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LATTE makes significant contributions to the field of multimodal AI, with several potential impacts:

1. **Paradigm Shift for Open-Source VLMs:** The most significant contribution is the demonstration of a viable and highly effective paradigm for building more capable open-source vision-language models. By offloading complex perception to specialized, state-of-the-art vision models, LATTE enables smaller VLMs to focus their learning capacity on higher-level reasoning. This is a crucial step towards bridging the performance gap with proprietary models without requiring massive scaling of the VLM itself. This approach could democratize access to advanced multimodal AI capabilities.

2. **Novel and Large-Scale Dataset:** The introduction of a novel and the largest dataset of 293K multi-modal reasoning traces is a major asset for the research community. This dataset is unique in its scale, diversity (covering 31 data sources, including single- and multi-image questions, and image-text interleaved traces), and high quality (filtered for correctness and derived from GPT-4o's reasoning over specialist outputs). The public release of these artifacts will accelerate research in multimodal reasoning and tool-use.

3. **Empirical Validation and Insights into Data Quality:** The extensive experiments and ablation studies provide robust empirical evidence for the effectiveness of the LATTE approach. Crucially, the findings emphasize that **data quality matters more than quantity** for training reasoning-capable VLMs. Insights into the formats, sources, and thought quality of reasoning traces are invaluable for future data curation efforts, guiding researchers to create more impactful datasets rather than simply larger ones. This includes the finding that programmatic generation, while scalable, can hurt reasoning if the "thoughts" are too simplistic.

4. **Enhanced VLM Robustness and Interpretability:** By explicitly training models to articulate thoughts and call specific tools, LATTE could lead to more robust and interpretable multimodal agents. The `LATTE-trace` format makes the model's reasoning process explicit, allowing developers to inspect intermediate steps, identify where perception or reasoning might fail, and diagnose errors more effectively.

5. **Future Directions and Applications:**
 - The framework encourages the integration of new and improved vision specialists as the computer vision field advances, allowing VLMs to continuously "see further."
 - The error analysis points to clear directions for future work, such as diversifying the toolset for knowledge-intensive VQA, or exploring alternative training methods (e.g., reinforcement learning) for better generalization beyond supervised finetuning.
 - The approach has potential applications in various domains requiring complex visual reasoning, from scientific analysis and medical imaging to robotics and assistive technologies.

While acknowledging limitations like the need for customized tool implementations and increased inference-time compute, LATTE provides a strong foundation and a clear path forward for developing sophisticated, open-source multimodal intelligence.
