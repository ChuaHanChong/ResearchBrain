# Learning agile and dynamic motor skills for legged robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ETH Zurich and Intel developed a methodology to train agile and dynamic motor skills for the ANYmal quadrupedal robot entirely in simulation, then directly deploy them on hardware. Their approach uses a learned actuator network to bridge the reality gap for complex Series-Elastic Actuators, enabling the robot to achieve superior performance in speed, efficiency, and dynamic fall recovery compared to prior model-based controllers.

## Problem

- Developing agile and dynamic locomotion for legged robots is challenging due to their high-dimensional, non-smooth dynamics, and complex environmental interactions.
- Traditional control methods (modular design, trajectory optimization) are laborious, require extensive tuning, or are computationally expensive and often compromise performance due to model inaccuracies.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) promises end-to-end optimization but faces a significant "reality gap" when transferring policies from simulation to real robots, especially those with complex, hard-to-model actuators like Series-Elastic Actuators (SEAs).

## Method

- A hybrid simulation approach that integrates a fast rigid-body simulator with a data-driven "actuator network" trained on a small dataset from the physical robot to accurately model complex actuator dynamics.
- Reinforcement learning (TRPO) is used within this hybrid simulation, employing dynamics randomization, observation noise injection, and curriculum learning to robustify policies against real-world discrepancies.
- Policies learned purely in simulation are directly deployed on the physical ANYmal robot, leveraging their computational efficiency for real-time control without external hardware.

## Results

- The learned controller achieved command-conditioned locomotion with **95%** lower linear velocity error and **60%** lower yaw rate error than the previous state-of-the-art model-based controller, while using 23-**36%** less torque.
- Enabled the ANYmal robot to run at a record-breaking speed of **1.5** m/s (**25%** faster than previous records), demonstrating the policy's ability to exploit hardware limits and discover a novel, efficient flying trot gait.
- Successfully implemented a dynamic fall recovery controller that restored the robot to an upright configuration from nine different challenging initial fall poses, a complex behavior previously unachieved on a real quadrupedal robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Accurately modeling complex, non-linear actuator dynamics via a learned neural network (Actuator Net) is crucial and highly effective for successful sim-to-real transfer of agile behaviors, outperforming hand-tuned analytical models.
> - Reinforcement learning, when combined with a robust hybrid simulation framework, can autonomously discover highly efficient, dynamic, and novel locomotion gaits and recovery behaviors that surpass human-designed controllers.
> - Extensive randomization of dynamics and observation noise during simulation training, coupled with curriculum learning, enables learned policies to be robust to real-world uncertainties and disturbances.
