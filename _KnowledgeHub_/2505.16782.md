# Reasoning Beyond Language: A Comprehensive Survey on Latent Chain-of-Thought Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive survey by Chen et al. (2025) introduces the first unified taxonomy for latent Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, organizing a rapidly growing field into token-wise horizontal and layer-wise vertical approaches. It synthesizes current research, practical applications, and outlines critical challenges for future advancements in LLM efficiency and cognitive capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in LLMs suffers from expressive redundancy, leading to inflated token usage, slower inference, and potential overfitting to stylistic artifacts.
- Explicit CoT faces a semantic bottleneck as discrete language struggles to fully capture abstract, continuous, or multi-conceptual thought processes, limiting reasoning depth.
- The rapidly expanding and fragmented field of latent CoT reasoning lacked a comprehensive, structured overview and taxonomy, making it difficult to understand diverse methodologies and identify research gaps.

## Method

- The paper proposes the first ==unified taxonomy for latent CoT==, categorizing methods into =='token-wise horizontal'== (sequential generation of latent thoughts) and =='layer-wise vertical'== (iterative refinement of hidden states across layers) approaches.
- It provides an in-depth discussion of representative latent CoT works, analyzing their design principles, training strategies, architectural paradigms, and efficiency trade-offs.
- The survey identifies critical open problems in latent CoT, such as ==unsupervised training and interpretability==, and outlines promising future research directions to guide the field.

## Results

- The survey successfully established a unified taxonomy classifying latent CoT methods into token-wise horizontal and layer-wise vertical categories, clarifying the field's structural organization.
- Latent CoT methods demonstrated practical utility across various domains, including textual reasoning, multimodal reasoning and generation, and information retrieval/recommendation systems.
- Theoretical analyses and empirical findings suggest latent CoT can improve multi-step reasoning efficiency by solving structured problems with fewer steps and achieving reasoning depth through iterative computation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Latent Chain-of-Thought reasoning offers advantages such as accelerated inference, richer and more compact cognitive representations, and the ability to explore multiple reasoning trajectories in parallel by decoupling reasoning from explicit linguistic tokens.
> - The proposed taxonomy effectively structures the diverse approaches in latent CoT, revealing fundamental differences in how latent thoughts are represented, optimized, and explored within LLMs.
> - Despite its potential, latent CoT faces significant challenges primarily due to the unobservable nature of its reasoning process, leading to difficulties in training stability, generalization to novel problems, and ensuring interpretability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey is situated at a critical juncture in the evolution of Large Language Models (LLMs). The foundational work of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning (Wei et al., 2022) marked a significant breakthrough, enabling LLMs to articulate step-by-step reasoning in natural language, thereby enhancing both performance and interpretability on complex tasks. However, as LLMs have scaled and become more ubiquitous, the inherent limitations of conventional, explicit CoT have become increasingly apparent.

The authors identify two primary constraints of explicit CoT:
1. **Expressive Redundancy:** Verbalizing every intermediate step in natural language leads to inflated token usage, slower inference, and the inclusion of syntactically necessary but functionally non-essential tokens. This redundancy can also cause models to overfit to stylistic artifacts rather than genuine reasoning signals.
2. **Semantic Bottleneck:** Human cognition often involves abstract, continuous, or multi-conceptual representations that resist precise verbalization. Forcing these complex, non-linguistic thought processes into a linear sequence of discrete linguistic symbols can lead to information loss and limit the depth and flexibility of reasoning.

These limitations have spurred a growing interest in "latent Chain-of-Thought" (latent CoT) reasoning. This paradigm shift involves models reasoning not through explicit language tokens but within continuous, high-dimensional latent spaces. Latent CoT promises to "de-linguistify" the reasoning process, offering advantages such as accelerated inference (by reducing token-level computation), richer and more compact reasoning representations, and the ability to explore multiple reasoning trajectories in parallel.

However, the unobservable nature of latent reasoning introduces new challenges, including difficulties in training, alignment, and transparent evaluation. The field has seen rapid growth, with diverse approaches emerging in architectural design, implicit reasoning, and efficient computation. This survey emerges precisely to address the "growing need for a comprehensive and structured overview" of this burgeoning field. It provides a much-needed systematic taxonomy to organize and clarify the assumptions, objectives, and innovations within latent CoT, thereby aiming to establish a structured foundation for future advancements. By doing so, it contributes significantly to the ongoing efforts to make LLMs more efficient, robust, and capable of more abstract and human-like reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation behind this survey stems directly from the evolving landscape of LLM reasoning. While explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) has demonstrated remarkable capabilities, its reliance on verbalized intermediate steps presents inherent limitations. The paper highlights "expressive redundancy" (e.g., increased token usage, slower inference, susceptibility to stylistic overfitting) and a "semantic bottleneck" (the inability of discrete language to fully capture abstract, continuous, or multi-conceptual thought) as key drawbacks. These constraints restrict the broader applicability of explicit CoT, particularly in abstract reasoning tasks that transcend purely linguistic representation.

In response, there has been a significant and "rapid and enthusiastic growth" in research on "latent CoT reasoning," where models operate within continuous latent spaces. The authors are motivated by the promise of this paradigm, which offers several compelling advantages:
- **Accelerated Inference:** By reducing token-level computation, latent CoT can significantly speed up reasoning.
- **Richer and More Compact Representations:** Latent spaces can encode more dense and abstract cognitive representations than discrete language allows.
- **Parallel Exploration:** Latent CoT facilitates the simultaneous exploration of multiple reasoning paths, enhancing robustness and efficiency.

Despite these advantages, the field faces substantial challenges, primarily due to the "unobservable nature of the reasoning process." This leads to:
- **Training and Alignment Difficulties:** Direct supervision of latent processes is challenging, making it hard to ensure structured internal reasoning or ethical controllability.
- **Evaluation Gap:** Lack of transparency makes it difficult to ascertain whether models are performing genuine reasoning or merely exploiting input-output correlations.

Given the accelerating pace and expanding scope of latent CoT research, the authors identify a critical need for a structured framework. Therefore, the key objectives of this paper are threefold:
1. **Systematic Taxonomy:** To propose the "first unified taxonomy" that organizes latent CoT studies into two primary methodological categories (token-wise horizontal and layer-wise vertical), clarifying their underlying assumptions, objectives, and innovations.
2. **In-depth Discussion:** To conduct a detailed analysis of representative works within this taxonomy, comparing various design principles, training strategies, architectural paradigms, supervision signals, and efficiency trade-offs.
3. **Challenge and Future Directions:** To identify the critical open problems in latent CoT (e.g., unsupervised training, evaluation faithfulness, interpretability) and outline promising avenues for future research.

Ultimately, the paper aims to provide a "structured foundation for advancing this promising direction in LLM reasoning," serving as a comprehensive guide for researchers and practitioners in this dynamic field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This paper adopts the methodology of a comprehensive survey, systematically organizing and analyzing the rapidly expanding body of research on latent Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. The authors' approach is characterized by its structured categorization, in-depth discussion of diverse techniques, and forward-looking identification of challenges and opportunities.

The core of their methodology is the development of a novel, **unified taxonomy** for latent CoT reasoning, illustrated in Figure 2 of the paper. This taxonomy structures the field along two primary axes, inspired by the distinction between horizontal (sequence-level) and vertical (layer-level) computation in Transformer architectures:

#### 3.1. Taxonomic Framework

1. **Token-wise Horizontal Level (§3):** This category encompasses methods that generate a sequence of intermediate latent thoughts along the sequence dimension *before* producing the final answer. It conceptualizes the model "thinking ahead" by forming a reasoning plan in a compact, latent format.
 - **Representation Initialization (§3.1):** How latent thoughts are formed.
 - **Hidden State:** Deriving thoughts from the model’s internal hidden states, either self-contained (e.g., sequential state-passing, projection networks) or via auxiliary models.
 - **Weighted Embedding:** Operating in the model’s input embedding space, creating superpositions of concepts via weighted averages of existing vocabularies.
 - **Special Vector:** Using dedicated learnable parameters, such as internal scratchpads, new "latent tokens," or concept vectors extracted by auxiliary models.
 - **Model Optimization (§3.2):** How latent reasoning capabilities are trained.
 - **Pre-Training:** Integrating latent reasoning into the model's initial pre-training phase, either implicitly or with auxiliary semantic signals.
 - **Post-Training:** Fine-tuning base models.
 - **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Training with supervision signals, either indirectly (loss on human-readable output, knowledge distillation, distributional constraints) or directly (reconstructing linguistic reasoning steps, contrastive loss, matching teacher's hidden states, compressed KV-cache supervision).
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Training models to autonomously discover reasoning strategies by maximizing a reward signal based on the final outcome, often with policy gradient algorithms and KL divergence penalties.
 - **Inference Exploration (§3.3):** How latent reasoning is leveraged during inference.
 - **Sequential Scaling:** Generating latent thoughts one after another to refine a single reasoning chain, often through iterative optimization or adaptive resource allocation.
 - **Parallel Scaling:** Exploring multiple reasoning paths simultaneously to improve solution quality, robustness, or inference latency.

2. **Layer-wise Vertical Level (§4):** This category focuses on methods that deepen reasoning through iterative computation across layers for each token. It mimics a "think-then-speak" dynamic by repeatedly refining hidden states through vertical loops.
 - **Model Architectures:**
 - **Encoder-Based Models:** Repeatedly applying an encoder-only looped Transformer for iterative refinement.
 - **Decoder-Based Models:** Employing a loop-driven decoder that repeatedly re-applies parts of the decoder stack to refine the hidden state before emitting each output token, categorized further into end-to-end adaptive-depth looping and latent-semantic supervision within the loop.

Beyond this core methodological classification, the survey includes dedicated sections for:
- **Analysis (§5):** Examining both **External Behaviors** (observable task accuracy, scaling, reasoning traces) and **Internal Mechanisms** (theoretical reasoning capacity and empirical probing of hidden states using techniques like logit lens).
- **Applications (§6):** Showcasing the successful deployment of latent CoT across various domains, including textual reasoning, multimodal reasoning and generation (vision, speech), and information retrieval/recommendation systems.
- **Challenges and Future Directions (§7):** Identifying critical open problems (e.g., training instabilities, generalization difficulties, interpretability concerns) and outlining promising research avenues (e.g., alternative architectures, interpretability and verification methods, advanced training approaches, integration with LLM agents, and social intelligence).

The authors draw extensively from recent literature, including many arXiv preprints, reflecting an effort to capture the most current developments in this rapidly evolving domain. This comprehensive and systematic approach allows the survey to provide a structured and actionable understanding of latent CoT reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The primary "findings" of this survey are encapsulated in its comprehensive organization of the latent Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning landscape, its synthesis of existing research, and its identification of key trends, successes, and challenges.

#### 4.1. The Proposed Taxonomy and its Implications

The most significant result of this survey is the establishment of the **first unified taxonomy** for latent CoT reasoning. By categorizing methods into "Token-wise Horizontal Level" and "Layer-wise Vertical Level," the authors provide a clear conceptual framework that helps to understand the diverse approaches.
- **Token-wise Horizontal Methods** (sequential generation of latent thoughts) are shown to vary widely in how latent representations are initialized (hidden states, weighted embeddings, or special vectors), optimized (pre-training, supervised fine-tuning with indirect/direct supervision, or reinforcement learning), and explored during inference (sequential or parallel scaling). This highlights the flexibility in integrating latent thinking into the standard token generation process, emphasizing efficiency and expressiveness.
- **Layer-wise Vertical Methods** (iterative refinement of hidden states across layers) demonstrate that reasoning depth can be achieved through parameter reuse and recurrent computation, often mimicking a "think-then-speak" process. This finding underscores that computational depth is a flexible resource, distinct from model size, and can lead to improved reasoning without a proportional increase in parameters.

#### 4.2. Insights from External and Internal Analyses

The survey synthesizes findings from analytical studies, revealing a nuanced understanding of latent CoT:
- **External Behaviors:** Research shows that increasing computational depth (e.g., through repeated layer application) significantly improves multi-step reasoning, validating the vertical-level approaches. Furthermore, models can indeed internalize redundant reasoning steps and develop implicit reasoning shortcuts, supporting the premise that CoT can unfold beyond explicit verbalization.
- **Internal Mechanisms:**
 - **Theoretical Analysis:** Formal comparisons suggest that latent CoT, particularly the token-wise horizontal approach, can solve structured reasoning problems with significantly fewer steps than explicit CoT. Layer-wise vertical approaches are found to be effective across tasks and support parallel computation, offering theoretical efficiency advantages over explicit CoT's sequential nature. These findings provide strong theoretical justification for the latent paradigm.
 - **Empirical Analysis:** The empirical evidence regarding the presence and nature of latent CoT within hidden states is debated. Some studies detect signs of internalized reasoning, while others suggest it is fragile, highly conditional on training data, or even absent in certain settings. This ongoing debate highlights the complexity of probing and interpreting the internal cognitive processes of LLMs, and points to a critical area for future research.

#### 4.3. Real-world Applications

The survey demonstrates that latent CoT is not merely a theoretical concept but has practical utility across various domains:
- **Textual Reasoning:** It enhances performance in mathematical, commonsense, and multi-hop logical reasoning tasks.
- **Multimodal Reasoning and Generation:** It extends beyond language to areas like visual reasoning, speech synthesis, and unified multimodal generation, addressing the inefficiency and semantic brittleness of verbal explanations in these contexts.
- **Information Retrieval and Recommendation:** Latent CoT improves dense retrieval by creating richer document embeddings and enhances recommendation systems by refining user interaction histories or reasoning over cross-domain signals.

#### 4.4. Identified Challenges

Finally, the survey explicitly outlines the critical hurdles facing latent CoT research:
- **Training Instabilities:** Current methods often underperform explicit CoT due to difficulties in developing stable and effective latent training paradigms.
- **Generalization Difficulties:** Models struggle with novel problem structures or reasoning patterns not encountered during training, indicating fragility and a lack of true generalizability.
- **Interpretability Concerns:** The unobservable nature of latent reasoning raises concerns about unfaithful or hidden internal processes, posing a significant challenge for understanding and trusting model conclusions.

In summary, the survey's main findings are its unique and comprehensive categorization of the field, the consolidated evidence supporting the theoretical and practical benefits of latent reasoning, and a clear articulation of the significant challenges that must be overcome for its full potential to be realized.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This comprehensive survey on latent Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning holds significant implications for both the scientific research community and the broader development of artificial intelligence. Its impact can be assessed across several dimensions:

#### 5.1. For Scientific Research and Community Guidance

- **Structured Foundation:** The paper's most immediate and profound impact is providing the "first unified taxonomy" and "structured foundation" for a rapidly evolving and somewhat fragmented field. By systematically categorizing diverse methodologies into "token-wise horizontal" and "layer-wise vertical" approaches, it offers researchers a clear map of the landscape, clarifying assumptions, objectives, and innovations. This organization is invaluable for new entrants to the field and helps experienced researchers identify connections, gaps, and promising avenues.
- **Accelerated Progress:** By offering an "in-depth discussion" of representative works and identifying "critical open problems," the survey helps direct future research efforts. It highlights crucial areas like unsupervised training, evaluation faithfulness, interpretability, and generalization, thereby fostering focused innovation and preventing redundant efforts. The accompanying GitHub repository further reinforces this by serving as a living resource for the community.
- **Conceptual Clarity:** The paper formally defines key concepts and differentiates between explicit and latent CoT, contributing to a more precise scientific discourse. It pushes the boundaries of how LLMs "think," encouraging a move beyond human-readable verbalizations towards more abstract and "native" computational substrates.

#### 5.2. For LLM Development and Capabilities

- **Efficiency and Scalability:** Latent CoT promises to decouple reasoning from explicit language generation, leading to inherently more efficient LLMs. By reducing token-level computation and enabling more compact representations, it can accelerate inference and reduce the computational overhead associated with explicit reasoning chains. This is crucial for deploying LLMs in real-world applications where speed and resource constraints are critical.
- **Richer Cognitive Representations:** The shift to continuous latent spaces allows for the encoding of more abstract, continuous, and multi-conceptual representations, moving beyond the semantic bottleneck of discrete language. This could unlock richer and more flexible reasoning capabilities in LLMs, enabling them to tackle more complex and abstract tasks that are currently challenging.
- **Adaptive and Flexible Reasoning:** The discussed approaches, especially those in the layer-wise vertical level, highlight how models can achieve reasoning depth through iterative computation and adaptive resource allocation. This implies the development of LLMs that can "think" for longer on complex problems and proceed quickly on simpler ones, mimicking human-like cognitive flexibility.

#### 5.3. Broader Impact and Future Potential

- **Multimodal Intelligence:** The successful application of latent CoT in multimodal domains (vision, speech) suggests a path toward more unified and efficient multimodal AI. As modalities proliferate, steering latent spaces for controllable and efficient multimodal intelligence becomes increasingly vital.
- **Enhanced Information Systems:** Improved reasoning in latent spaces can lead to more sophisticated information retrieval and recommendation systems, capable of deeper semantic understanding and more personalized user experiences.
- **Responsible AI Development:** By clearly outlining interpretability and verification as crucial future directions, the survey implicitly contributes to the responsible development of AI. Addressing the "unobservable nature" of latent reasoning is essential for building trustworthy and ethically controllable AI systems.
- **Advanced AI Agents and Social Intelligence:** The paper touches upon the potential for latent CoT to enhance LLM agents by enabling more compact decision-making and faster planning. Furthermore, its potential for modeling "nested mental states essential to Theory of Mind" suggests a pathway toward more socially competent AI, capable of inferring others' beliefs and intentions.

In conclusion, this survey is not merely a review; it is a foundational work that structures an emerging field, highlights its transformative potential for LLM efficiency and cognitive capabilities, and critically outlines the research agenda for future advancements. Its significance lies in its capacity to guide and accelerate the development of the next generation of more capable, efficient, and versatile language models.
