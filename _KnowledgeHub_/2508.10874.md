# SSRL: Self-Search Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Self-Search Reinforcement Learning (SSRL) allows large language models to act as internal search engines during training, significantly reducing the reliance on costly external search engines. This method improves training efficiency by 5.53x compared to prior approaches and demonstrates effective generalization to real-world search scenarios during inference.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) used as agents incur high costs and logistical complexity when frequently interacting with external search engines during reinforcement learning (RL) training.
- Existing full-real and semi-real search methods for LLM agents require extensive rollouts and multi-turn API calls, leading to substantial computational and financial overhead.
- Current retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) approaches rely on static knowledge bases, lacking the dynamic and noisy characteristics of real-time web search.

## Method

- SSRL introduces a =="Full-Sim Search" paradigm== where the LLM policy model functions as both the reasoning agent and the ==internal search engine==, generating queries and information from its parametric knowledge.
- The training methodology includes an ==information token mask== to encourage comprehensive reasoning and a composite reward function (outcome + format rewards) to guide adherence to structured output.
- An on-policy RL approach is used, where the policy model itself acts as the information provider, allowing the model to simultaneously enhance its reasoning and internal knowledge retrieval capabilities.

## Results

- SSRL achieves a **5.53x** reduction in training time compared to simulated search baselines like ZeroSearch, while maintaining robust and steady reward growth.
- SSRL-trained models consistently outperform baselines that utilize external or separately simulated search engines, as well as RAG-based methods, on various information-seeking QA benchmarks.
- The approach demonstrates effective sim-to-real generalization, allowing SSRL-trained models to seamlessly integrate with real external search engines during inference, with an entropy-guided strategy reducing external search frequency by **20-42%** without performance loss.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large language models possess substantial intrinsic world knowledge that can be effectively elicited and scaled via repeated sampling, with smaller models sometimes rivaling larger ones with sufficient samples.
> - Instruction-tuned LLMs demonstrate a stronger ability to utilize their internal knowledge for self-search compared to base models, highlighting the impact of fine-tuning on knowledge elicitation.
> - The use of format-based rewards is critical for guiding LLMs to produce structured, multi-step reasoning trajectories that effectively integrate internal search capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving landscape of large language models (LLMs) and reinforcement learning (RL) agents: the high cost and logistical complexity of enabling LLMs to interact with external tools and real-world environments, particularly search engines.

The current research trajectory for LLMs increasingly involves their development into sophisticated "agents" capable of complex problem-solving, code generation, and tool utilization. Prior work has notably improved LLMs' reasoning abilities through RL with verifiable rewards in domains like mathematics (e.g., OpenAI's O1, DeepSeek-AI's DeepSeek-R1) and code generation (e.g., El-Kishky et al., Cui et al.). Beyond these domains, LLMs are being equipped with "tool learning" capabilities (e.g., Qian et al., Feng et al.), enabling them to invoke external tools like web search engines, execute actions, and observe states in real environments.

However, the authors highlight a significant bottleneck: **costly interactions with real web search engines**. While models like Search-R1 (Jin et al.) and Kimi V2 (Team et al.) have demonstrated strong performance by integrating external search, the extensive number of rollouts and multi-turn tool calls required during RL training incurs substantial API costs and computational overhead.

The paper positions itself by contrasting with existing approaches:
- **Full-Real Search**: Methods like Search-R1 (Jin et al.) and online-search RL (Zheng et al.) directly interact with real search engines (e.g., Google, Bing), offering high fidelity but at high cost.
- **Semi-Real Search**: Approaches like ZeroSearch (Sun et al.) reduce costs by fine-tuning an LLM to *simulate* a web search engine. This provides a stable and reliable knowledge source without real-time external API calls, but still relies on a *separate* LLM as a simulator, often requiring extensive fine-tuning.
- **RAG-based Methods**: Standard Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) (e.g., Li et al.) retrieve information from a static knowledge base, which lacks the dynamic and noisy nature of web search.

The core contribution of this work, **Self-Search Reinforcement Learning (SSRL)**, introduces a novel paradigm termed **"Full-Sim Search."** In this setup, the policy model *itself* acts as both the reasoning agent and the internal search engine. It auto-regressively generates search queries *and* the corresponding "information" based solely on its parametric world knowledge, entirely eliminating the need for any external (real) or separate simulated search component during training. This represents a significant step towards greater autonomy and cost-efficiency in LLM agent training.

The work also connects to:
- **LLMs as World Models**: Inspired by studies suggesting LLMs can serve as internal "world models" by providing state information (Li et al., Hao et al., Gu et al., Tang et al.). This paper explicitly investigates LLMs' potential to act as a "simulator of the web."
- **Inference-time Scaling (TTS)**: Drawing parallels with recent advances in unsupervised RL like TTRL (Zuo et al.) and TTS strategies for LLMs and agents (Brown et al., Snell et al., Zhu et al.), the paper explores how repeated sampling can elicit and scale LLMs' intrinsic search capabilities.

In essence, this research pushes the boundary of LLM agents by demonstrating that, for many search-driven tasks, LLMs contain sufficient internal knowledge to simulate the search process during training, providing a cost-effective and robust environment for RL, and opening new avenues for efficient sim-to-real transfer.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of this research is to investigate and leverage the inherent capabilities of large language models (LLMs) to function as self-contained simulators for agentic search tasks within reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks. The primary motivation stems from the prohibitive costs and practical complexities associated with training LLM agents that rely on frequent interactions with external (real) search engines.

The authors articulate two key research questions that define their objectives:

1. **What is the performance limit of LLMs on search-based Question-Answering (QA) tasks using only internal knowledge?**
 - **Motivation**: LLMs are pre-trained on vast web-scale corpora, implying they encapsulate substantial world knowledge. The researchers aim to quantify how effectively this intrinsic knowledge can be elicited and utilized to perform "search-like" operations purely through internal generation, without any external retrieval mechanisms. This objective seeks to establish an "upper bound" on an LLM's self-search capabilities.

2. **Can full-sim search RL (SSRL) enable effective sim-to-real transfer with real web search during inference?**
 - **Motivation**: While training with internal knowledge offers cost benefits, real-world applications often require access to up-to-date and external information. This objective explores whether skills acquired in a fully simulated (internal knowledge-only) training environment can generalize robustly when the agent is deployed in a "real" environment where it interacts with actual web search engines during inference. This is crucial for the practical utility of a cost-effective training approach.

Beyond these core questions, the paper is motivated by several underlying factors:

- **Cost Reduction**: The repeated, multi-turn interactions with external search APIs during RL training are extremely costly. The authors are motivated to find methods that significantly reduce this expenditure.
- **Training Stability**: External search engines introduce variability and latency into the training loop. An internal, self-contained search mechanism could offer a more stable and controlled training environment.
- **Leveraging LLM Potential**: The belief that LLMs inherently possess "world knowledge" and can potentially function as "world models" that provide state information in response to actions. This research seeks to unlock and enhance this latent capability for agentic tasks.
- **Reducing Hallucination**: By training models to leverage and refine their internal knowledge, the approach aims to reduce the tendency of LLMs to "hallucinate" incorrect information, especially when constrained by structured reasoning.
- **Scalability of Agent Training**: Ultimately, the goal is to pave the way for more autonomous and scalable LLM agents that are less dependent on external resources throughout their lifecycle.

In summary, the key objectives are to systematically measure, enhance, and validate the ability of LLMs to perform search tasks using only their internal parameters, and to demonstrate that this "self-search" capability can be effectively trained via RL to generalize to real-world search scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted methodology, broken down into three main stages: quantifying intrinsic self-search capabilities, developing the Self-Search Reinforcement Learning (SSRL) framework, and evaluating its generalization to real-world scenarios and compatibility with Test-Time RL.

#### 3.1. Inference-time Scaling of Self-Search

This initial phase aims to quantify the intrinsic search capabilities of LLMs using only their internal knowledge.

- **Task Formulation**: The goal is to answer information-seeking queries without external tools. The primary metric is `pass@k`, which calculates the probability of finding at least one correct answer among `k` independent samples. A scaling law `c ≈ exp(ak^b)` is formulated to model coverage `c` as a function of samples `k`.
- **Prompt Design**: A structured prompt guides the LLM to simulate a multi-turn search process within a single generation trajectory. The model uses specific tags: `<think>...</think>` for reasoning, `<search>...</search>` for query formulation, `<information>...</information>` for generating relevant search results (simulated from its internal knowledge), and `<answer>...</answer>` for the final response. This differs from traditional multi-turn systems as the entire process is auto-regressively generated.
- **Experimental Setup**:
 - **Benchmarks**: Seven benchmarks are used, categorized into General QA (Natural Questions, TriviaQA), Multi-hop QA (HotpotQA, Musique, Bamboogle, 2WikiMultiHopQA), and Vague Question Answering (BrowseComp).
 - **Models**: Evaluated across three model families (Qwen2.5, Llama3, Qwen3) with varying parameter scales (0.6B to 72B) to assess scaling behavior.
 - **Sampling Parameters**: Consistent temperature (0.7), top-k (-1), top-p (0.95), and max token (8192) are used.
- **Further Analysis**: Investigates the efficiency of "thinking" tokens (comparing models with/without forced thinking), multi-turn self-search versus naive repeated sampling, and the impact of reflective prompting ("Wait, wait, wait"). It also compares `pass@k` with `maj@k` (majority voting) to assess the reliability of extracting the correct answer from multiple samples.

#### 3.2. SSRL: Self-Search Reinforcement Learning

This phase introduces the core SSRL method to enhance LLMs' self-search capabilities through RL.

- **Task Definition**: The RL objective maximizes the expected reward while regularizing with KL divergence from a reference model. Crucially, in SSRL, the policy model `πθ` simultaneously functions as both the reasoning policy and the *internal search engine*, meaning retrieved information `R` also follows the distribution of `πθ`.
- **Training Methodology**:
 - **Information Token Mask**: A key technique where tokens within `<information>...</information>` tags are masked during loss calculation. This encourages the model to generate more comprehensive reasoning and not simply copy self-retrieved information.
 - **Reward Modeling**: A composite reward function is used:
 - **Outcome Reward**: Binary (1 for correct, -1 for incorrect) based on exact match (EM) with ground truth.
 - **Format Reward**: (Detailed in Appendix C) Ensures adherence to the prescribed structured output format (`<think>`, `<search>`, `<information>`, `<answer>`). This guides the model to produce well-organized multi-step reasoning trajectories.
- **Experimental Setup**:
 - **Benchmarks**: Six benchmarks (excluding BrowseComp for training due to difficulty and data limitations) are used for evaluation, focusing on EM accuracy.
 - **Models**: Primarily Llama-3.2-3B (Base/Instruct) and Llama-3.1-8B (Base/Instruct), based on their strong self-search priors. Qwen series models are also evaluated.
 - **Baselines**: Comprehensive comparison against vanilla prompting (Direct Answer, CoT), RAG-based methods (Standard RAG, Search-o1), and existing RL-based methods (R1, Search-R1, ZeroSearch).
 - **Training Details**: Primarily uses GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) as the RL algorithm, with ablation studies on PPO, REINFORCE++, DAPO, and KL-Cov for robustness. Training on a mix of NQ and HotpotQA datasets, using 8 NVIDIA A800 GPUs. Batch size 256, learning rate 1e-6, 5 rollouts per prompt, KL divergence coefficient 0.001.

#### 3.3. Sim2Real Generalization and Test-Time RL

This final stage evaluates the practical applicability of SSRL-trained models.

- **Sim2Real Generalization**:
 - The SSRL-trained policy model, which follows Search-R1's format, can seamlessly integrate with *real* external search engines (e.g., Google Search, local corpora) during inference.
 - Model-generated information within `<information>` tags is replaced with actual search results for `K` turns.
 - An **entropy-guided search strategy** is proposed to dynamically decide between internal self-search and external real search based on the uncertainty (entropy) of the generated search query.
- **Test-Time RL (TTRL)**:
 - Explores the application of unsupervised RL algorithms like TTRL (Zuo et al.) to the self-search setting, aiming for further performance gains without explicit rewards. Ablation studies with and without information masking are conducted.

This comprehensive methodology allows for a thorough investigation of LLMs' intrinsic search potential, the effectiveness of an RL framework to enhance it, and the practical utility of such an approach for real-world agentic tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings, demonstrating the potential of LLMs as internal search engines and the effectiveness of the SSRL framework:

#### 4.1. Inference-time Scaling of Self-Search

- **Strong Scaling Behavior of Internal Knowledge**: LLMs possess substantial internal world knowledge that can be effectively elicited. Repeated sampling (increasing `k`) consistently and predictably improves `pass@k` scores across all benchmarks and model families. For instance, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct achieves an 87.2% accuracy for `pass@1024` on Bamboogle, a 150% improvement over `pass@1`.
- **BrowseComp Performance**: On the challenging BrowseComp benchmark, which requires difficult search but easy verification, some LLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) surpass dedicated search agents like `o1` when given sufficient samples, highlighting their deep internal knowledge.
- **Model Family Performance**: Contrary to previous findings on mathematical reasoning, Llama models generally outperform Qwen models in the Self-Search setting, suggesting that self-search ability (knowledge priors) is not strongly correlated with reasoning priors.
- **Smaller Models Can Rival Larger Ones**: With increased sampling, smaller models can achieve performance comparable to models with significantly more parameters (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct matches Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct on TriviaQA with 1024 samples).
- **Ineffectiveness of Excessive Reasoning or Multi-Turn Self-Search**:
 - More "thinking" tokens in Qwen3 models do not always lead to better performance; knowledge utilization appears more critical than extensive Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for agentic search tasks.
 - Multi-turn *self-search* (where the LLM generates multiple search-information pairs internally) and "reflection" prompts also hurt performance compared to naive repeated sampling when measured by consumed tokens, suggesting that simpler self-generation is more effective.
- **Limitations of Majority Voting**: While `pass@k` shows high ceilings, naive `maj@k` (majority voting) yields only marginal improvements, indicating that reliably extracting the *single best* answer from multiple samples remains a challenge when the ground truth is unknown. This highlights a gap between latent knowledge and actionable retrieval.

#### 4.2. SSRL: Self-Search Reinforcement Learning

- **Superior Performance**: Models trained with SSRL consistently outperform baselines that rely on external search engines (real or simulated, e.g., Search-R1, ZeroSearch) and R1-like models, demonstrating LLMs' ability to function effectively as both reasoners and internal knowledge retrievers simultaneously.
- **Instruction Models Excel**: Instruction-tuned models utilize internal knowledge more effectively than base models, suggesting that supervised fine-tuning enhances the ability to leverage pre-existing knowledge.
- **Efficiency and Robustness**: SSRL significantly improves training efficiency, achieving a 5.53x reduction in training time compared to ZeroSearch, while maintaining robust and steady reward growth without collapse.
- **Benefits of Information Masking**: Masking information tokens during training consistently enhances performance by encouraging the model to generate more comprehensive reasoning rather than simply copying self-retrieved information.
- **Crucial Role of Format Reward**: The format-based reward mechanism is critical for guiding the model to adhere to the structured reasoning framework, which is essential for combining reasoning and internal search capabilities effectively. Without it, models produce less coherent reasoning paths.
- **Importance of On-Policy Self-Search**: Training with an on-policy approach (where the policy model itself acts as the information provider) is crucial. Freezing the information provider (using a zero-step policy) leads to significant performance degradation and training instability.
- **Compatibility with RL Algorithms**: GRPO-based algorithms generally perform better than PPO and REINFORCE++, aligning with observations from other search agent training.

#### 4.3. Sim2Real Generalization and Test-Time RL

- **Effective Sim2Real Generalization**: SSRL-trained models can seamlessly integrate with *real* external search engines during inference, achieving superior performance compared to baselines with less online searching. This provides preliminary evidence for robust sim-to-real transfer.
- **Entropy-Guided Hybrid Search**: An entropy-guided approach can reduce external search frequency by 20-42% while maintaining comparable performance to full external search, demonstrating intelligent arbitration between internal and external knowledge.
- **TTRL Performance**: Unsupervised TTRL can yield even better performance than GRPO, especially for smaller models (e.g., 59% average improvement for Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct). Interestingly, for TTRL, training *without* the information mask sometimes yields slightly better results, contrary to supervised RL settings.
- **TTRL on BrowseComp**: TTRL-trained models show substantial improvements on BrowseComp, even without external search, and smaller TTRL models unexpectedly achieve higher scores by adopting a "candidate verification" strategy over sequential searching.
- **TTRL Sim2Real Trade-offs**: While TTRL boosts internal performance, it can introduce biases where models over-rely on internal knowledge, making adaptation to real external environments harder and leading to less flexible external search usage compared to GRPO-trained models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings presented in "SSRL: SELF-SEARCH REINFORCEMENT LEARNING" hold substantial significance and promise to exert a considerable impact on the fields of large language models, reinforcement learning, and the development of autonomous AI agents.

#### 5.1. Redefining LLMs as "Intrinsic World Models" and "Search Engines"

- **Paradigm Shift**: The most profound impact is the re-conceptualization of LLMs not just as language generators or reasoners, but as potent "implicit world models" and "internal search engines." This shifts the paradigm from LLMs needing external tools to LLMs *simulating* those tools from within their own parameters, particularly during the crucial training phase.
- **Unlocking Latent Knowledge**: The demonstration of strong `pass@k` scaling laws reveals that LLMs already possess a vast reservoir of world knowledge. The challenge is no longer merely acquiring knowledge but reliably eliciting and operationalizing it for agentic tasks.

#### 5.2. Revolutionizing RL Agent Training Cost and Efficiency

- **Dramatic Cost Reduction**: By enabling "full-sim search" during training, SSRL virtually eliminates the need for expensive API calls to real or even simulated external search engines. The reported 5.53x reduction in training time compared to ZeroSearch underscores a massive cut in computational and financial costs associated with developing search-driven RL agents. This democratizes access to advanced RL agent training, making it feasible for researchers and organizations with limited budgets.
- **Enhanced Training Stability**: Relying on internal knowledge removes the variability, latency, and potential unreliability of external APIs, leading to a more stable and controlled environment for RL training. This can accelerate convergence and improve the robustness of learned policies.

#### 5.3. Advancing Autonomous and Scalable LLM Agents

- **Towards Greater Autonomy**: SSRL paves the way for truly autonomous LLM agents that are less dependent on external infrastructure. Agents can acquire search and reasoning skills internally, fostering self-sufficiency in knowledge acquisition and problem-solving.
- **Improved Scalability**: The cost-efficiency and stability of SSRL enable training of more complex agents over longer durations and on larger datasets, which is crucial for tackling increasingly challenging real-world tasks.
- **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer**: The demonstrated ability of SSRL-trained models to seamlessly integrate with real search engines during inference, and even outperform baselines, provides a critical pathway for moving from simulated training to practical, real-world deployment without significant performance degradation. The entropy-guided hybrid approach further optimizes this, offering a practical balance between internal and external knowledge.

#### 5.4. New Research Directions and Future Work

- **Bridging the Latent-to-Actionable Gap**: The discrepancy between high `pass@k` (knowledge exists) and low `maj@k` (hard to reliably extract) highlights a significant research problem: how to develop robust mechanisms for LLMs to confidently identify and output the "best" answer from their internal knowledge when ground truth is unknown.
- **Refining Internal World Models**: The findings open new avenues for exploring LLMs specifically as textual world models within agentic RL. Future research can focus on enhancing these internal models to provide even more precise, consistent, and context-aware "search results."
- **Understanding Learning Dynamics**: The differing impacts of information masking in GRPO vs. TTRL, and the distinct performance of Llama vs. Qwen models on self-search tasks, warrant deeper investigation into the specific mechanisms through which LLMs acquire and utilize knowledge, and how different RL algorithms interact with these.
- **Complex Agentic Behaviors**: With reduced training overhead, researchers can now explore more sophisticated agentic behaviors, such as advanced planning, long-horizon reasoning, and open-ended exploration, within a more affordable and controlled simulated environment.

In conclusion, SSRL offers a compelling solution to a major bottleneck in LLM agent development, promising to make the training of highly capable, search-driven agents more accessible, efficient, and scalable. The work not only delivers practical advancements but also opens rich avenues for fundamental research into the nature of knowledge representation and utilization within large language models.
