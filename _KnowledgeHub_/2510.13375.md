# DepthVLA: Enhancing Vision-Language-Action Models with Depth-Aware Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> DepthVLA enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by integrating a pretrained depth prediction module within a mixture-of-transformers architecture, improving spatial reasoning for robotic manipulation tasks. The approach yields superior performance, achieving a 74.8% success rate on the simulated WidowX benchmark and a 79% average progress score on a real-world robotic platform.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often struggle with precise spatial reasoning, leading to failures in tasks requiring fine-grained 3D understanding like grasping small objects or collision avoidance.
- Prior attempts to integrate spatial awareness into VLAs either introduce hardware dependencies, lack end-to-end optimization, incur high inference latency, or are inefficient due to extensive data requirements.
- Current VLA backbones, inherited from Vision-Language Models (VLMs), possess limited inherent understanding of 3D geometry and object shapes despite strong semantic perception capabilities.

## Method

- DepthVLA introduces a mixture-of-transformers (MoT) architecture that unifies a Vision-Language Model (VLM), a dedicated depth expert, and an action expert for robust multimodal processing.
- A pretrained depth expert, based on DINOv2-L and Depth Anything V2, is integrated efficiently using shared attention layers with block-wise masking to preserve specialized expert capabilities.
- The model is trained end-to-end using imitation learning with a flow-matching loss, jointly optimizing both action generation and depth prediction, and can operate effectively even with a frozen depth expert.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of 74.8% on the simulated Simpler WidowX benchmark, outperforming the re-implemented π0 baseline (58.8%) and other models in tasks requiring spatial precision.
- Attained the highest average success rate of 94.9% on the challenging LIBERO benchmark when trained jointly on all task suites, surpassing other advanced VLA models including DreamVLA (92.6%).
- Demonstrated robust performance on a real-world dual-arm mobile platform (Galaxea R1 Lite), achieving an average progress score of 79% compared to 65% for the baseline, and showed strong few-shot adaptation capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating a pretrained depth expert significantly enhances VLA spatial reasoning, outperforming methods that rely solely on extensive action-centric pretraining or less direct 3D cues.
> - The block-wise masking in shared attention layers is critical for maintaining the specialized knowledge of the VLM and depth experts, preventing negative interference during joint processing.
> - Internally predicted depth maps can sometimes lead to better task performance than directly using ground-truth depth, suggesting that the act of prediction forces a more effective integration of geometric reasoning into the model's representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen a significant paradigm shift towards generalist policies, enabled by Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models, built upon large-scale pretrained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), leverage vast web-scale data to achieve impressive generalization capabilities and language-guided control across diverse tasks and robot embodiments. Models like RT-2, Octo, and OpenVLA exemplify this trend, demonstrating strong semantic understanding and multimodal grounding.

However, a critical limitation of current VLA models is their struggle with precise spatial reasoning. While VLMs excel at open-vocabulary semantic perception, they inherently possess limited understanding of fine-grained 3D geometry and object shapes. This deficiency translates directly into VLA failures in tasks requiring high precision, such as grasping small or complex objects, executing delicate operations, or effectively avoiding collisions. Existing VLA approaches often attempt to mitigate this by extensive pretraining on action-centric datasets, which is data-intensive, computationally expensive, and still frequently insufficient for robust 3D grounding.

Prior attempts to inject spatial awareness into VLAs include:
- **Augmenting with 3D sensors:** Using LiDAR or RGB-D cameras (e.g., PointVLA) provides explicit 3D data but reduces generalization across platforms due to hardware dependencies.
- **Off-the-shelf depth estimators:** Generating pseudo point clouds as input (e.g., SpatialVLA) is a workaround that doesn't allow for end-to-end optimization, limiting performance.
- **Generative world models:** Predicting future states to improve planning (e.g., DreamVLA, CoTVLA) can enhance planning but doesn't fundamentally improve the encoding of the current scene's geometry.
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning:** Autoregressively generating depth tokens (e.g., MolmoACT) offers spatial insights but introduces significant inference latency (over 2 seconds), making it impractical for real-time robotic control.

DepthVLA directly addresses this fundamental limitation by explicitly integrating a pretrained depth prediction expert into the VLA architecture in an end-to-end, efficient manner. It leverages recent advances in 3D perception foundation models (e.g., Depth Anything V2, VGGT, UniDepth V2) that have demonstrated robust geometry inference from monocular images. By doing so, DepthVLA seeks to bridge the gap between strong semantic understanding from VLMs and the precise spatial reasoning required for dexterous robotic manipulation, without incurring prohibitive computational costs or sacrificing generalization. This work is thus positioned at the forefront of improving the perceptual capabilities of generalist robot policies, making them more capable of handling the intricacies of the physical 3D world.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enhance the spatial reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models without sacrificing inference speed or generalization.**

The motivation stems from the observed weaknesses in existing VLA models:
1. **Limited Spatial Reasoning:** Current VLAs, inherited from their VLM backbones, struggle with tasks demanding precise 3D understanding, such as grasping small objects, intricate manipulation, or collision avoidance. This often leads to failures in real-world scenarios.
2. **Inefficient 3D Grounding:** Relying solely on extensive action-data pretraining to imbue VLAs with 3D awareness is inefficient, data-hungry, and still insufficient for achieving fine-grained spatial perception.
3. **Limitations of Prior Solutions:** Existing approaches to improve spatial awareness either introduce hardware dependencies (3D sensors), lack end-to-end optimization (off-the-shelf estimators), only improve planning without direct scene encoding (world models), or incur unacceptable latency (CoT reasoning).
4. **Leveraging 3D Perception Advances:** The authors recognize the significant progress in dedicated 3D perception models (e.g., monocular depth prediction) and aim to integrate this robust prior knowledge into VLA frameworks effectively.

Therefore, the key motivations are to develop a simple yet effective VLA architecture that explicitly incorporates spatial awareness, is computationally efficient, scalable, and can leverage diverse 3D datasets beyond embodied action data. The ultimate goal is to create more robust, precise, and generalizable robot manipulation policies capable of operating effectively in complex real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DepthVLA proposes a novel architecture that explicitly integrates spatial awareness into VLA models through a pretrained depth prediction module. The core of its methodology revolves around a **mixture-of-transformers (MoT)** design, which unifies a Vision-Language Model (VLM), a dedicated depth expert, and an action expert.

**A. Model Overview and Architecture:**
- **Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT):** Inspired by π0, DepthVLA extends this framework by introducing a third expert: a depth prediction module. This allows for the simultaneous processing of language, semantic visual information, and geometric visual information.
- **Three Experts:**
 - **Vision-Language Model (VLM):** Provides language understanding and open-vocabulary semantic visual perception from RGB images and language instructions. Paligemma-3B is used as the backbone.
 - **Depth Expert:** Dedicated to inferring geometric information (depth) from RGB images.
 - **Action Expert:** Generates continuous robot actions conditioned on the fused representations from both the VLM and depth expert.
- **Shared Attention Layers with Block-wise Masking:** All three experts share the same attention layers within the transformer architecture. However, to preserve the pretrained capabilities of the VLM and depth modules and prevent cross-contamination during their initial stages, a block-wise mask is applied. This mask ensures that VLM tokens primarily attend to other VLM tokens, and depth tokens attend to other depth tokens. Crucially, action tokens are designed to attend to *all* streams (VLM, depth, and proprioceptive tokens), allowing them to integrate semantic, spatial, and robot state information for informed action generation.

**B. Depth Expert Design and Pretraining:**
- **Encoder-Decoder Architecture:** The depth expert is designed as an encoder-decoder network.
 - **Encoder:** Based on DINOv2-L, initialized from the pretrained checkpoint of Depth Anything V2. This leverages powerful 3D foundation models for robust initial spatial priors.
 - **Decoder:** Mirrors the transformer structure of the VLM and outputs depth predictions through a linear head.
- **Intermediate Feature Usage:** Unlike approaches that only use a final depth map, DepthVLA's depth expert performs spatial reasoning across all intermediate layers. The action expert attends to these intermediate features, providing richer geometric cues for action prediction rather than just a low-dimensional depth output.
- **Pretraining:** The depth expert is pretrained on diverse large-scale 3D datasets (WildRGB-D, Scannet, Scannet++, HyperSim) using a monocular depth prediction task. The scale-invariant log loss (L_si) is employed for this task, which effectively teaches the model robust spatial reasoning and distance estimation. This separate pretraining allows the depth expert to acquire strong spatial priors from a broader range of data than typically available in embodied action datasets.

**C. DepthVLA Policy Training:**
- **Imitation Learning:** DepthVLA is trained on embodied action data using an imitation learning objective, maximizing the log-likelihood of actions.
- **Flow-Matching Loss:** To effectively model continuous and diverse action trajectories, a flow-matching loss (L_flow) is adopted. This loss helps in generating smooth and realistic action sequences.
- **Joint Optimization:** A key aspect is that the depth expert's spatial reasoning is maintained during VLA training. The final loss function is a combination of the depth prediction loss (L_si) and the flow-matching loss (L_flow): L = L_si + L_flow. This joint optimization allows DepthVLA to continuously refine its spatial reasoning while simultaneously optimizing for action generation in an end-to-end manner.

This methodology enables DepthVLA to leverage pretrained knowledge from specialized models for both semantic understanding and depth perception, integrate them efficiently through a shared attention mechanism, and optimize them jointly for robotic manipulation tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DepthVLA demonstrates consistent and significant performance improvements across a range of simulated and real-world robotic manipulation benchmarks, validating its core hypothesis about the importance of explicit depth-aware spatial reasoning.

**A. Simulation Benchmarking:**
- **Simpler WidowX Benchmark:**
 - DepthVLA achieved the highest average success rate of **74.8%**, significantly outperforming the re-implemented π0 baseline (58.8%) and other models like SpatialVLA (34.4%) and Octo-Base (16.0%).
 - Notably, DepthVLA showed substantial gains in tasks requiring accurate spatial reasoning and collision avoidance, such as "Stack Block" (62.5% vs. 30.0% for π0) and "Pick Eggplant" (89.2% vs. 59.2% for π0).
 - This demonstrates that the depth expert enhances 3D perception even when trained on real-world data (BridgeData V2) and evaluated in simulation.
- **LIBERO Benchmark:**
 - DepthVLA, trained jointly on all four challenging LIBERO task suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), achieved the highest average success rate of **94.9%**. This surpassed the re-implemented π0 (93.6%) and even models that utilized large-scale action pretraining, such as DreamVLA (92.6%) and MolmoACT (86.6%).
 - This finding suggests that even with extensive action pretraining, standard VLAs often lack sufficient 3D grounding, which DepthVLA effectively provides. The improvements over other spatially-enhanced baselines and world-model-based approaches further underscore the strength of the explicit depth expert design.

**B. Real-World Benchmarking (Galaxea R1 Lite):**
- **Overall Performance:** DepthVLA consistently outperformed the baseline (re-implemented π0) on a dual-arm mobile platform, achieving an average progress score of **79% vs. 65%** in the standard fine-tuning setting.
- **Few-Shot Adaptation:** In a more challenging few-shot setting (only 20 fine-tuning trajectories), DepthVLA still showed robust performance, achieving **63% vs. 45%** for the baseline, highlighting its superior transferability with limited data.
- **Task-Specific Improvements:**
 - **Microwave operation:** DepthVLA demonstrated improved collision avoidance when handling the door and plate, crucial for safe and effective interaction with articulated objects.
 - **Blocks stacking:** Exhibited superior spatial perception for precise pick-and-place skills, even with limited fine-tuning data, where the baseline struggled.
 - **Table bussing:** Performed comparably to the baseline, suggesting that both models handle relatively simpler small-object grasping tasks effectively.
- **Language-Following Capabilities:** The model maintained strong language-following capabilities, indicating that the action expert effectively integrates the strengths of both the VLM and depth expert without degradation.

**C. Ablation Studies:**
- **Necessity of Depth Expert Pretraining:** Randomly initializing the depth expert without pretraining severely degraded performance (average 51.0% vs. 74.8%), confirming the critical role of strong spatial priors.
- **Necessity of Depth Loss during VLA Training:** Removing the depth loss during VLA training also led to a drop in performance (average 56.9%), indicating that continuous optimization of depth prediction alongside action generation is beneficial.
- **Freezing Depth Expert:** Surprisingly, freezing the depth expert during VLA training only resulted in a minor performance drop (average 71.9% vs. 74.8%). This suggests that the pretrained depth expert learns robust and universal spatial representations that are largely transferable, even without further fine-tuning, simplifying deployment without requiring ground-truth depth during fine-tuning.
- **Block-wise Masking:** Allowing VLM and depth tokens to attend to each other without the block-wise mask significantly harmed performance (average 55.6%), particularly in "Stack Block" (2.5%), highlighting the importance of this architectural design to maintain expert specialization and prevent negative interference.
- **Predicted vs. Ground-Truth Depth:** Counter-intuitively, predicting depth internally yielded *stronger* performance than directly feeding ground-truth depth as input (94.9% vs. 93.3% on LIBERO). The authors hypothesize this is due to modality competence, where explicit prediction forces the model to integrate geometric reasoning more effectively into its shared representation rather than over-relying on an external, potentially dominant, ground-truth signal.

**D. Visualization of Depth Prediction:**
- Qualitative results showed that DepthVLA’s predicted depth maps accurately captured detailed 3D structures, including object boundaries, distances, and occlusions, across both real-world and simulated environments. This visual evidence supports the claim that the depth expert provides fine-grained geometric cues essential for precise manipulation and collision avoidance, even in cluttered scenes.

In summary, the main findings robustly demonstrate that integrating a pretrained, end-to-end depth expert within a MoT framework significantly enhances the spatial reasoning of VLA models, leading to superior performance in complex manipulation tasks in both simulation and the real world, with minimal additional latency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DepthVLA research carries significant implications for the field of generalist robot manipulation and the broader application of Vision-Language-Action models.

**A. Addressing a Critical Limitation:**
The most profound impact of DepthVLA is its effective solution to the long-standing challenge of precise spatial reasoning in VLA models. By explicitly incorporating a depth expert, the model overcomes the inherent limitations of VLMs in understanding fine-grained 3D geometry. This directly translates to robots that can perform more dexterous manipulations, navigate cluttered environments with fewer collisions, and interact more reliably with objects of varying shapes and sizes.

**B. Enhanced Performance and Generalization:**
The consistent outperformance of DepthVLA across diverse benchmarks (Simpler, LIBERO, and real-world Galaxea R1 Lite platform) is a strong indicator of its effectiveness. The improvements are particularly notable in tasks requiring high precision, such as stacking blocks or operating a microwave, which are often failure points for less spatially-aware VLA models. This enhanced performance, coupled with strong generalization across varied task types and environments (as shown in LIBERO joint training), makes DepthVLA a more robust and capable policy for real-world deployment.

**C. Training Efficiency and Scalability:**
The mixture-of-transformers design, coupled with a per-expert pretraining strategy, allows each component (VLM and depth expert) to be trained on diverse, large-scale datasets independently. This is a highly scalable approach, as it moves beyond the constraint of requiring massive embodied action datasets for *all* forms of knowledge acquisition. By leveraging existing 3D foundation models and datasets for depth pretraining, DepthVLA improves training efficiency and reduces the data requirements for action-specific training. The finding that a frozen depth expert still yields strong performance further simplifies deployment by removing the need for ground-truth depth during VLA fine-tuning.

**D. Practical Deployability:**
One of the most compelling aspects of DepthVLA is its practical viability. The explicit integration of a depth expert introduces only a marginal increase in inference latency (210 ms vs. 190 ms for π0), which the authors rightly point out as negligible in real-time robotic control scenarios where actions are predicted in multi-step chunks. This efficiency, combined with its robust performance in real-world settings, positions DepthVLA as a strong candidate for industrial and research applications where reliable and precise manipulation is crucial. The use of a commercially available robot platform (Galaxea R1 Lite) further underscores this practical focus.

**E. New Research Directions:**
DepthVLA opens several avenues for future research. While monocular depth prediction is powerful, it has limitations with challenging surfaces (reflective, transparent, texture-less). The authors themselves suggest exploring multi-view depth or point cloud prediction to further enhance spatial accuracy and robustness. This work also encourages further investigation into optimal ways of integrating various perceptual experts (e.g., tactile sensing, force feedback) into generalist policies using similar MoT architectures, pushing towards truly multimodal and spatially intelligent robotic systems. Furthermore, the intriguing finding that *predicted* depth sometimes outperforms *ground-truth* depth could spur new research into "modality competence" and how models learn to effectively utilize and integrate internal representations versus external, raw sensor data.

In conclusion, DepthVLA represents a significant step forward in developing more capable and robust Vision-Language-Action models. By thoughtfully integrating explicit depth-aware spatial reasoning, it addresses a critical bottleneck, bringing us closer to generalist robots that can reliably operate with precision and intelligence in complex physical environments.
