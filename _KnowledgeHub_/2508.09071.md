# GeoVLA: Empowering 3D Representations in Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> GeoVLA introduces an end-to-end framework that integrates 3D point cloud information into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, allowing robots to better perceive their physical environment for manipulation tasks. This architecture consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance on manipulation benchmarks and exhibits robust generalization across various real-world spatial, scale, and viewpoint changes.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models primarily rely on 2D visual inputs, leading to limited spatial awareness and poor depth perception in robotic manipulation.
- This reliance on 2D data hinders VLA models' robustness and adaptability to real-world variations in object height, scale, and camera viewpoints.
- Integrating 3D information often either disrupts the pre-trained alignment of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) or limits the action expert's adaptation to new 3D data, requiring extensive re-training.

## Method

- **GeoVLA** employs a ==dual-path architecture== that processes 2D vision-language information via a pre-trained VLM and 3D point cloud data via a dedicated ==Point Embedding Network (PEN)== in parallel.
- A ==3D-enhanced Action Expert (3DAE)==, based on a ==Diffusion Transformer== with a ==Mixture-of-Experts== architecture, fuses these multi-modal embeddings for precise action generation.
- The PEN utilizes an ==end-effector token== as a spatial anchor for focused 3D feature extraction, and the 3DAE incorporates a ==static routing strategy== to balance contributions from different modalities.

## Results

- **GeoVLA** achieved **state-of-the-art** success rates on the LIBERO benchmark (**97.7%** average) and ManiSkill2 (**77%** overall), surpassing previous methods like OpenVLA-OFT and CogACT.
- In real-world experiments, GeoVLA demonstrated enhanced robustness and generalization, maintaining performance under significant variations in object height, scale, and camera viewpoints.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contributions of the Point Embedding Network, the end-effector token, Rotary Positional Embedding, and the static routing Mixture-of-Experts design in the 3D-enhanced Action Expert.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Processing 2D and 3D information in parallel preserves VLM pre-trained knowledge while enabling effective integration of fine-grained geometric cues for robotic manipulation.
> - The "end-effector token" in the Point Embedding Network provides a task-relevant spatial anchor, enhancing the action expert's ability to model precise contact and geometric relationships.
> - A static routing strategy within the 3D-enhanced Action Expert's Mixture-of-Experts module mitigates bias towards the stronger pre-trained VLM branch, ensuring balanced and effective utilization of both 2D and 3D modalities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models aim to enable robots to understand natural language instructions and execute corresponding physical actions, primarily by leveraging the vast general knowledge embedded in pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Early VLA models like RT-2 and OpenVLA treat actions as discrete tokens, enabling compatibility with standard VLM architectures but often struggling with the precision required for complex manipulation. More recent approaches, such as $\pi_0$ and CogACT, address this by generating continuous actions, often through diffusion processes or flow matching, which improves trajectory modeling.

However, a critical limitation of most existing VLA models is their predominant reliance on 2D visual inputs (RGB images). This overlooks the rich geometric information inherent in the 3D physical world, limiting robots' spatial awareness, depth perception, and robustness to variations in viewpoint, object scale, and height. This lack of 3D understanding poses a significant barrier to deploying VLA models effectively in real-world, unstructured environments where such variations are common.

To address this, the broader research landscape is exploring ways to integrate 3D perception into VLA models. Current approaches generally fall into two categories:

1. **Direct 3D integration into VLMs:** Methods like LLaVA-3D and SpatialVLA attempt to infuse 3D positional encodings or features directly into the VLM's visual encoder. While this makes the VLM geometrically aware, it often disrupts the pre-trained alignment between visual encoders and Large Language Models (LLMs). Re-establishing this alignment typically necessitates extensive and costly 3D embodied instruction-tuning datasets, which are not always readily available.
2. **Injecting 3D information into action experts:** Another line of work, exemplified by PointVLA, focuses on feeding 3D information (like point cloud features) directly into the action expert module, rather than modifying the VLM backbone. PointVLA, for instance, uses a two-stage training scheme where a 2D-VLA model is first trained, and then point cloud features are injected via a zero-initialized ControlNet-style module. The drawback here is that freezing the action expert, while preserving its low-level capabilities, hinders its full adaptation to the newly introduced 3D modality.

GeoVLA positions itself as a novel approach that seeks to overcome the limitations of these existing methods by integrating 3D information in an "elegant way." It differentiates itself by adopting a **parallel, dual-path architecture** that *preserves the pre-trained knowledge and general understanding capabilities of the VLM* for vision-language processing, while simultaneously processing 3D geometric information independently through a *customized point encoder*. This design aims to achieve multi-modal alignment and superior performance without disrupting the core VLM or hindering the action expert's adaptation to 3D data, thus addressing a key challenge in end-to-end 3D-aware VLA frameworks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind GeoVLA stems from the fundamental limitation of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: their over-reliance on 2D visual inputs. This neglect of rich 3D geometric information in the physical world severely constrains their:
- **Spatial Awareness:** Inability to accurately perceive depth, relative positions, and volumetric properties of objects and environments.
- **Adaptability and Robustness:** Poor generalization to variations in real-world scenarios, such as changes in object height, scale, or camera viewpoints, which are ubiquitous in robotic manipulation tasks.

Given these limitations, the key objectives of the GeoVLA research are:

1. **To develop a novel VLA framework that effectively integrates 3D geometric information.** The overarching goal is to enhance robotic manipulation capabilities by providing robots with a more comprehensive understanding of their physical environment.
2. **To preserve the pre-trained knowledge and general understanding capabilities of existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs).** This is a crucial objective to avoid the computationally expensive and data-intensive process of re-training or extensively fine-tuning VLMs on large-scale 3D embodied instruction datasets, which is a common challenge with other 3D integration methods.
3. **To introduce specialized architectural components for efficient and effective 3D feature extraction and fusion.** This includes designing a point encoder capable of extracting fine-grained 3D structural cues and an action expert that can intelligently combine information from diverse modalities.
4. **To demonstrate superior performance and robustness in complex robotic manipulation tasks.** This involves rigorous evaluation in both simulation and real-world environments, specifically testing adaptability to height, scale, and viewpoint variations, which are direct benefits of enhanced 3D spatial awareness.

In essence, GeoVLA seeks to bridge the gap between powerful 2D-based VLM understanding and the inherent 3D nature of robotic interaction, aiming for a VLA model that is both intelligent and spatially competent.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GeoVLA is an end-to-end VLA framework designed to simultaneously process visual, language, and geometric information. Its methodology is characterized by a dual-path architecture and specialized modules for 3D feature extraction and action generation.

#### 3.1. Problem Definition

The standard VLA problem involves a policy $p$ generating an action sequence $a_{1:T}$ given a visual observation $V$ (RGB image) and a language instruction $L$: $a_{1:T} \sim p(a_{1:T} | V, L)$. Each action $a_t$ is typically a relative 7-DoF movement $(\Delta x, \Delta y, \Delta z, \Delta \alpha, \Delta \beta, \Delta \gamma, g)$, where $g$ is the gripper command. GeoVLA extends this by incorporating an additional point cloud $P$ (derived from a depth map $D$), so the policy becomes conditioned on all three modalities: $a_{1:T} \sim p(a_{1:T} | V, P, L)$.

#### 3.2. Overview of GeoVLA Architecture

The GeoVLA pipeline consists of three main components (Figure 2):
1. **Vision-Language Model (VLM):** A pre-trained 2D VLM (e.g., Prismatic-7B) processes the RGB image $V$ and language instruction $L$ to extract fused vision-language embeddings ($F_{VL}$). This branch leverages the VLM's existing knowledge and understanding capabilities.
2. **Point Embedding Network (PEN):** Concurrently, the depth map $D$ is reprojected into a 3D point cloud $P$. The PEN, a customized point encoder, processes $P$ to extract fine-grained 3D geometric features ($F_P$).
3. **3D-enhanced Action Expert (3DAE):** The extracted $F_{VL}$ and $F_P$ are concatenated and fed into the 3DAE. This module, based on denoising diffusion principles, progressively refines a noisy action sequence into precise robot actions, guided by the combined multi-modal context.

#### 3.3. Point Embedding Network (PEN)

The PEN is crucial for extracting compact and clean geometric features from potentially noisy depth maps. It employs a novel dual-path architecture (Figure 3a):
- **Geometric Feature Path:** A lightweight Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) with multi-layer large-kernel convolutions and local pooling processes the point cloud into patch-level geometric tokens ($F_{pc}$). These are then fed into Transformer blocks to aggregate global information.
- **Positional Encoding Path:** The original point cloud is downsampled and its position information is encoded using Rotary Positional Encoding (RoPE), guiding the transformer blocks.

The point cloud is transformed into the end-effector coordinate system, with the current end-effector position at the origin. From the aggregated geometric tokens, PEN selects the token corresponding to this coordinate origin (the "end-effector token") as the anchor token ($F_{anchor}$). This $F_{anchor}$ is then used as the final encoded 3D geometric feature ($F_P$) to guide the action expert (Figure 3b). This "spatial anchor" design offers two benefits:
- **Focused Representation Learning:** Attention mechanisms concentrate on regions relevant to manipulation.
- **Explicit Spatial Relationship Modeling:** Critical geometric relationships between the end-effector and objects are captured, aiding precise contact prediction.

#### 3.4. 3D-enhanced Action Expert (3DAE)

The 3DAE uses a Diffusion Transformer (DiTs) architecture. During training, it takes a noisy future action sequence (generated by perturbing recorded actions via a diffusion process) concatenated with $F_{VL}$ and $F_P$. The DiT predicts the noise to denoise the action sequence. During inference, it uses DDIM to progressively recover the desired action sequence from a noise distribution, conditioned on the multi-modal tokens.

A key innovation in 3DAE is the **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture** within its feed-forward networks (FFNs). This is designed to leverage the complementary strengths of $F_{VL}$ and $F_P$. To address the imbalance between the pre-trained VLM branch and the randomly initialized point cloud branch, GeoVLA introduces a **static routing strategy** for the MoE:
- During training, one modality is randomly dropped in each iteration, leading to three input configurations: (1) vision-language only, (2) language and geometric features (RGB tokens removed), and (3) full multi-modal input.
- Expert activation is deterministically governed by the presence and relevance of input modalities. This static routing prevents bias towards the stronger VLM branch, ensuring that both modalities contribute effectively.

This modular, dual-path, and expert-balanced design allows GeoVLA to integrate 3D information without compromising the VLM's pre-trained capabilities, leading to spatially grounded and precise action generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GeoVLA demonstrates superior performance and robustness across extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

**LIBERO Benchmark:** GeoVLA achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) results across all five LIBERO task suites, designed to test various robotic manipulation capabilities (spatial, object, goal, long-horizon, and extended long-horizon tasks).
- **Overall Performance:** GeoVLA achieved an average success rate of 97.7%, outperforming previous SOTA methods like OpenVLA-OFT (95.3%) and CogACT (93.2%) by clear margins.
- **Long-Horizon Tasks:** Particularly notable improvements were seen in LIBERO-Long and LIBERO-90, where GeoVLA achieved 96.6% and 97.7% success rates, respectively, improving upon OpenVLA-OFT by 5.9% and 5.6%. This indicates enhanced sequential decision-making capabilities informed by better spatial understanding.

**ManiSkill2 Benchmark:** GeoVLA also demonstrated SOTA performance on the ManiSkill2 benchmark, which focuses on basic and challenging pick-and-place tasks.
- **Overall Performance:** GeoVLA achieved the highest overall success rate of 77%, surpassing CogACT (69%) and Dita (66%).
- **Challenging Tasks:** On complex tasks like PickClutterYCB, which involve identifying and manipulating objects in cluttered scenes with high object diversity, GeoVLA achieved a 45% success rate, significantly outperforming Dita (36%) and CogACT (25%). This highlights GeoVLA's ability to maintain precise spatial awareness even with increased object variability, directly attributable to its point cloud-leveraging capabilities.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

The ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of GeoVLA's key architectural components:
- **Point Cloud Encoders:** The proposed PEN achieved a 97.7% success rate on LIBERO, outperforming simpler encoders like MLP (95.8%) and PointNet (95.2%), demonstrating its superior ability to capture relevant geometric structure.
- **Anchor Token Selection:** Using the "End Effector" token as the spatial anchor yielded the best performance (97.7%), surpassing Max pooling (96.3%) and Mean pooling (95.9%), confirming its importance for focused, task-relevant feature extraction.
- **Position Embeddings:** Incorporating Rotary Positional Embedding (RoPE) in PEN improved the success rate from 95.4% (with 1D PE) to 97.7%, validating its role in enhancing spatial encoding.
- **MoE Design in 3DAE:** The Mixture-of-Experts design in 3DAE significantly improved performance, increasing the success rate from 96.0% (non-MoE) to 97.7%. Crucially, the **static routing strategy** (97.7%) outperformed dynamic routing (97.3%), suggesting that manually balancing expert activation effectively mitigates bias towards the pre-trained VLM branch.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

**In-Domain Tasks:** GeoVLA was evaluated on eight real-world robotic manipulation tasks, categorized as basic manipulation and 3D-aware tasks.
- **Overall Performance:** GeoVLA achieved an overall average success rate of 86.3%, significantly outperforming $\pi_0$ (57.5%) and CogACT (76.3%).
- **3D-Aware Tasks:** On tasks requiring precise 3D spatial understanding (e.g., Put Basketball, Pick Hairclip), GeoVLA consistently demonstrated better robustness and spatial awareness, yielding 77.5% average success rate on these tasks compared to baselines that saw significant performance drops.

**Variant Results (Robustness & Generalization):** GeoVLA exhibited remarkable robustness and generalization under unseen task variations:
- **Varying Basket Height (Put Basketball):** While 2D-VLA models like CogACT showed severe performance drops (20-30% at lower heights), GeoVLA maintained reasonable performance (50-70%), achieving 60% even at a higher basket position. This confirms its adaptability to spatial distribution shifts.
- **Varying Doll Sizes (Cover Matryoshka):** GeoVLA consistently achieved higher success rates for different doll sizes (L1 and L2 at 80% success rate) compared to other 2D-VLA models, demonstrating superior scale awareness.
- **Varying Camera Viewpoints (Stack Block):** GeoVLA maintained high performance (70%) even with a 45° camera shift, whereas CogACT's performance dropped significantly (to 0%), highlighting GeoVLA's viewpoint invariance.
- **Removing Sponge Mat (Pick Carrot):** GeoVLA successfully adapted to the change in carrot placement height (50% success rate without mat), demonstrating stronger generalization than baselines ($\pi_0$ 60%, CogACT 10%), which often failed due to fixed grasping strategies.
- **Background and Lighting Variations:** GeoVLA also showed strong robustness to changes in background (93.3% vs 80.0% avg SR) and lighting (93.3% vs 73.3% avg SR) on basic tasks, further validating its generalization capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of GeoVLA represents a significant step forward in the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation. Its core significance lies in its ability to effectively bridge the gap between powerful 2D-based VLM understanding and the inherent 3D nature of physical interaction, without compromising the strengths of pre-trained models.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

- **Explicit 3D Geometric Understanding:** GeoVLA is one of the first VLA frameworks to explicitly and robustly integrate 3D point cloud information in an end-to-end manner. Unlike prior work that either disrupts VLM alignment or poorly adapts action experts, GeoVLA's parallel architecture preserves VLM knowledge while enabling dedicated 3D processing. This provides robots with a much-needed depth and spatial awareness that is crucial for interacting with the physical world.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** The most impactful finding is GeoVLA's superior robustness and generalization in real-world scenarios, particularly under variations in object height, scale, and camera viewpoint. These are common challenges in practical robotic deployments, and GeoVLA's ability to maintain high success rates under such conditions is a testament to its enhanced 3D perception. This directly addresses a major limitation of current 2D-VLA models.
- **Novel Architectural Components:** The proposed Point Embedding Network (PEN) and the 3D-enhanced Action Expert (3DAE) with its Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) and static routing strategy are significant methodological advancements. PEN efficiently extracts fine-grained, end-effector-focused 3D features from noisy point clouds, while the 3DAE intelligently fuses multi-modal cues. The static routing mechanism is particularly clever in mitigating bias towards pre-trained VLM features, ensuring a balanced and effective utilization of both 2D and 3D information.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Achieving state-of-the-art results on challenging benchmarks like LIBERO and ManiSkill2, especially on tasks requiring precise 3D reasoning, validates GeoVLA's effectiveness and scalability.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Advancing Generalist Robots:** By equipping VLA models with robust 3D perception, GeoVLA moves closer to creating more generalist robotic policies capable of performing a wider range of tasks in diverse and unstructured environments. This could accelerate the deployment of robots in areas like logistics, manufacturing, domestic assistance, and hazardous operations.
- **Reduced Data Dependency for 3D:** GeoVLA's design, which avoids extensive re-training of VLMs on large-scale 3D-specific datasets, offers a more data-efficient pathway to 3D-aware robotic control. This is a crucial practical advantage, as acquiring and annotating large 3D embodied datasets is notoriously difficult and expensive.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** Robots with better spatial understanding can interact more intelligently and safely within human environments, interpreting human instructions with greater contextual awareness of objects and their surroundings.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** GeoVLA sets a new benchmark and provides a strong foundation for future research in multi-modal VLA models. It opens avenues for exploring more sophisticated 3D representations, dynamic routing mechanisms, and advanced fusion strategies to further push the boundaries of robotic intelligence and dexterity.

In conclusion, GeoVLA significantly contributes to making robots more spatially aware, robust, and adaptable, thereby pushing the capabilities of VLA models towards more practical and versatile robotic systems.
