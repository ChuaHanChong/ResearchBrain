# Real-Time Compliance and Position Control of a Hyper-redundant Soft Robotic Arm

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Michigan developed a hyper-redundant soft robotic arm and a real-time iterative controller that simultaneously adjusts tip position and task-space compliance. The algorithm-informed hardware design, featuring mechanically isolated universal joints, enables a sparse, locally linearizable model, leading to a 5.4 times faster controller with 15% lower energy consumption compared to global optimization, and demonstrably improved performance in dynamic manipulation tasks requiring passive error correction.

## Problem

- Robots operating in unstructured or partially unobservable environments require both accurate motion and dynamically adjustable physical compliance for passive error correction and interaction.
- Conventional rigid robots simulate compliance with limitations, while existing soft robots often lack the ability to achieve high-precision positioning concurrently with quantitative, real-time tuning of compliance profiles.
- Prior methods for variable stiffness in soft robotics frequently involve complex hardware, nonlinear stiffness changes, or computationally intensive global optimization that is too slow for real-time feedback control, particularly for highly coupled continuum structures.

## Method

- An ==algorithm-informed hardware design== for a 7-link rigid-soft pneumatic arm with 6 custom universal joints and 24 independently regulated McKibben muscles, engineered for mechanical isolation to yield a sparse joint-space stiffness map.
- An ==energy-based compliance model== that analytically derives task-space compliance (CxC_xCx) and a ==compliance Jacobian== (JcJ_cJc) by leveraging the linear pressure-to-stiffness relationship of McKibben actuators and the sparse joint-space stiffness.
- A real-time iterative controller combining ==damped least squares inverse kinematics (IK)== for position and an **inverse compliance (IC)** solver for compliance, using JcJ_cJc and ==null-space projection== for secondary objectives, supported by a ==dual-loop joint-angle/joint-energy pneumatic control system==.

## Results

- The iterative IK/IC solver was approximately **5.4 times** faster and consumed **15%** less energy than a global optimizer in static tasks, and in dynamic tracking, achieved **41%** lower position RMSE, **40%** lower task-space compliance error, and a **22x** higher update rate.
- The analytical compliance model accurately captured the physical arm's directional tip compliance (MuJoCo faithfully reproducing pressure-driven motion), and a reconfigured morphology designed with this framework removed a resting-pose compliance singularity, achieving near-isotropic tip compliance (isotropy **0.88**).
- Hardware demonstrations showed that real-time directional compliance tuning improved performance in tasks such as writing on a moving whiteboard and resolving unobservable misalignments in a key-and-drawer task, by enabling passive self-correction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Purposefully designing soft robot hardware (e.g., with mechanical isolation) to expose a sparse, locally linearizable model enables faster and more predictable real-time control of both position and compliance.
> - An iterative controller integrating a compliance Jacobian with inverse kinematics can achieve continuous, quantitative, and real-time adjustments of both end-effector position and directional compliance, outperforming slower global optimization approaches in dynamic scenarios.
> - The generalizable modeling framework allows for model-based design and optimization of arm morphology (e.g., segment geometry) to tune task-appropriate compliance profiles and eliminate compliance singularities virtually, reducing physical prototyping.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robots designed for operation in unstructured or partially unobservable environments must integrate accurate motion capabilities with physical compliance. This compliance is critical for passive error correction during contact and interaction with the environment, such as manipulating objects in constrained spaces or dealing with unforeseen misalignments. The required compliance is often not a fixed property; it needs to be quantitatively adjustable and directional, enabling a robot to be soft in one direction (e.g., to absorb disturbances) and stiff in another (e.g., to maintain tool position). This requirement motivates the development of robots capable of real-time control over both tip position and task-space compliance.

Conventional rigid robots can simulate compliant behavior through impedance or admittance control. However, this "compliance via control" approach is limited by sensing capabilities, controller bandwidth, and actuator response speed, potentially leading to delays during rapid contact or impact. Structurally soft robots offer intrinsic elastic compliance through their deformable bodies and actuators. However, many existing soft robotic systems have limitations in achieving simultaneous high-precision positioning and quantitative, real-time shaping of their compliance profiles.

Previous attempts to achieve variable stiffness in robots include Variable-Stiffness Actuators (VSAs) in rigid arms, which often introduce additional mass and complexity. For soft robots, methods such as layer jamming, granular jamming, auxetic structures, origami mechanisms, and fluid-driven stiffness modulation can vary stiffness. These methods, however, frequently require additional hardware and exhibit nonlinear stiffness changes, making smooth, real-time tuning difficult, especially when combined with position tracking. Pneumatic antagonistic stiffening is a notable technique, as joint stiffness can scale approximately linearly with pressure. Prior work by Bruder et al. explored McKibben-actuated arms with combined variable stiffness and posture control, but often with limited independent degrees of freedom, leading to coupled control where stiffening a segment affects all directions. Stella et al. demonstrated co-optimization of shape and segment-level stiffness, but their approach relied on global optimization, which is too slow for real-time feedback control and may result in discontinuous joint-space trajectories. Generally, highly coupled continuum structures in soft robotics necessitate computationally intensive global optimization, which is not suitable for dynamic interaction.

The research gap identified is the challenge of building real-time compliance and position controllers that balance hardware complexity, weight, and size against the number of compliance-controllable degrees of freedom and the linearity of the input-to-compliance mapping. Existing impedance control formulations with a compliance Jacobian are promising but are not directly applicable to most soft robot hardware due to excessive coupling between actuators and segments. This paper positions itself by advocating for an "algorithm-informed soft-robot design" where the mechanical structure is engineered to expose a sparse, locally linearizable model, thereby facilitating fast control and addressing the limitations of existing soft and rigid robot designs in simultaneous, real-time, quantitative compliance and position control. The work builds upon previous research on rigid-soft arms, aiming to overcome its limitations regarding load capacity, actuator interference, and restricted range of motion, while generalizing the inverse-compliance methodology.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the increasing demand for robots capable of operating effectively in unstructured and partially unobservable environments. In such settings, conventional rigid robots often struggle because tasks like reaching into a bag, inserting tools into partially occluded openings, or manipulating constrained objects require physical interaction and passive error correction, which goes beyond simply reaching accurate Cartesian positions. Compliance, in these contexts, serves as a crucial mechanism for guiding the end-effector through minor misalignments that are difficult to sense or model explicitly.

Furthermore, the type of compliance required is not static; it needs to be dynamically adjustable. A manipulator might need to be compliant in one direction to absorb an unknown disturbance, while remaining stiff in another to maintain tool position. This directional compliance profile must be capable of changing in real time as the robot performs its task. Therefore, the core motivation is to develop a robot system that can quantitatively adjust its task-space compliance in real time, concurrently with precise position control.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of this work are articulated through its main contributions:

- **Algorithm-informed hardware design:** To develop a hyper-redundant rigid-soft pneumatic arm. This design specifically aims to decouple joint compliance sufficiently to allow for real-time control without relying on repeated global optimization, while still providing a rich tip-compliance profile across various postures. The hardware structure is informed by the requirements of a fast controller, rather than the controller being designed to accommodate highly coupled, non-linear hardware.
- **Real-time position and compliance control:** To implement an iterative controller that integrates standard Jacobian-based inverse kinematics (IK) with a compliance-Jacobian inverse compliance (IC) solver. This controller also incorporates null-space posture relief and a dual-loop joint-angle/joint-energy pneumatic controller, specifically tailored to the characteristics of the developed hardware. The goal is to enable continuous, quantitative command of both compliance and position.
- **Generalized, reconfigurable modeling:** To create a morphology-general compliance and kinematics model. This objective ensures that the modeling and control framework can accommodate diverse arm configurations (e.g., arbitrary segment lengths and inter-segment connector orientations), allowing the same fundamental design to be reconfigured for different task-specific compliance requirements.

In essence, the research aims to provide a practical solution for enabling soft robots to perform complex manipulation tasks in dynamic and uncertain environments by offering simultaneous, precise control over both their physical interaction properties (compliance) and their spatial configuration (position) in real time.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research approach integrates a novel hardware design with a real-time control algorithm and a robust modeling framework.

**A. Hardware Design (Algorithm-Informed)**

The robot's hardware architecture is specifically engineered to facilitate real-time control. It consists of a 7-link rigid-soft chain incorporating 6 custom universal joints (U-joints), organized into three segments. This configuration yields 12 revolute joint coordinates. Each U-joint comprises two perpendicular revolute axes, with each axis actuated by an antagonistic pair of McKibben pneumatic muscles. This results in a total of 24 independently regulated pneumatic muscles driving the arm.

Key design features for enabling the control approach include:
- **Rigid Articulated Backbone:** A rigid structural backbone is integrated into the pneumatically actuated body. This provides a structured, predictable kinematic chain.
- **Custom Universal Joints:** The U-joints are manufactured from water-jet aluminum plates and nylon printed spacers, designed for high antagonistic load capacity, low slack, and reduced mass compared to off-the-shelf components. The cascaded ring layout facilitates tendon routing and offsets neighboring muscles to minimize interference, allowing for a larger joint range of motion.
- **Mechanical Isolation:** Actuators controlling one U-joint are mechanically isolated from those controlling neighboring U-joints. This design choice aims to yield a sparse joint-space stiffness map, where each joint's stiffness is set largely independently.
- **Linearity of Stiffness:** McKibben actuators are chosen because their joint stiffness scales approximately linearly with pressure, making the pressure-to-stiffness mapping efficient to differentiate for control purposes.
- **Distributed Control System:** Each McKibben actuator integrates its own pressure-regulating valve and local microcontroller, a "valve-embedded" design. This minimizes tubing, reduces pressure delay, and supports a high-frequency control and feedback rate (verified at 150 Hz for all 24 actuators) over a CAN bus. This distributed architecture is crucial for real-time operation and precise actuator pressure tracking.
- **External Sensing:** An external motion-capture system tracks markers on the links to reconstruct joint angles and tip pose, providing real-time feedback for control.

**B. Compliance Modeling**

An energy-based formulation is employed to model task-space compliance:
- **Joint Torques:** Generalized joint torques ($\tau$) are derived from the negative gradient of the total actuator potential energy ($U_{total}$) with respect to the joint configuration ($q$).
- **Joint-Space Stiffness Matrix ($K_q$):** This is computed as the negative second derivative of $U_{total}$ with respect to $q$. Due to the mechanical isolation of U-joints, $K_q$ exhibits a sparse block-diagonal structure, with 2x2 blocks representing the stiffness about the two axes of each U-joint and their local coupling.
- **Pressure-to-Stiffness Map:** The entries of each U-joint stiffness block are linearly dependent on the four pressures driving that joint. This linear relationship is described by a matrix $G_j(q)$, which is analytically derived from actuator lengths, tendon moment arms, and the McKibben force model.
- **Joint-Space Compliance Matrix ($C_q$):** This is the inverse of $K_q$.
- **Task-Space Compliance Matrix ($C_x$):** Using the manipulator Jacobian $J(q)$, the task-space compliance at the end-effector is calculated as $C_x(q, p) = J(q)C_q(q, p)J^T(q)$. This matrix provides a quantitative, local description of directional tip compliance.

**C. Real-Time Iterative Controller**

The controller is designed to provide simultaneous, continuous control over tip position and compliance:
- **Inverse Kinematics (IK) for Position Control:** The forward kinematics are modeled using the product-of-exponentials method. For inverse kinematics, a damped least squares (DLS) approach is used to compute joint updates, ensuring numerical stability near singularities. A boundary function repels joints from physical limits, and a null-space projector allows for secondary objectives without disturbing position control.
- **Inverse Compliance (IC) for Compliance Control:**
 - **Compliance Jacobian ($J_c$):** A compliance Jacobian is constructed to relate small changes in actuator pressures (specifically, "leading pressures" of antagonistic pairs) to small changes in the end-effector's task-space compliance. By designating one pressure of each antagonistic pair as the leading pressure and deriving the trailing pressure from static torque equilibrium, each revolute axis effectively contributes a single compliance-control variable.
 - **Derivation of $J_c$:** The sensitivity of $C_x$ to changes in leading pressures is derived by differentiating $C_x$ with respect to each control variable, using the matrix-inverse derivative identity for $C_q = K_q^{-1}$. The sparse structure of $\partial K_q / \partial u_i$ simplifies this calculation. $J_c \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times 12}$ maps twelve leading-pressure updates to the six independent entries of $C_x$.
 - **Solving for Pressure Updates:** Given a compliance error $e_c = \text{vec}(C_{x,d} - C_x)$, the primary update $\Delta u_{primary}$ is computed using a bounded DLS solve. This incorporates a damping factor ($\lambda$) for stability and an interior-point log-barrier penalty ($B(u)$) with a weighting factor ($\mu$) to enforce pressure limits and prevent chatter from clipping infeasible commands.
 - **Null-Space Objectives:** The compliance Jacobian has a non-trivial null space, which is exploited for secondary objectives, such as minimizing stored actuator energy or using joint energy as a posture signal to move joints towards neutral postures with larger feasible stiffness workspaces, without affecting the primary compliance update.
- **Hardware Execution (Dual-Loop Pneumatic Control):** The high-level IC layer converts the calculated pressure update into desired joint energy. A low-level dual-loop PID controller then simultaneously tracks the desired joint angle (regulating differential pressure across an antagonistic pair) and desired joint energy (regulating antagonistic pressure). This forms a practical bridge from model-based compliance updates to physical pneumatic actuation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from algorithmic benchmarking, model validation through simulation and hardware experiments, a simulation study for reconfigurable morphology, and physical hardware demonstrations.

**A. Algorithmic Benchmarking**
Two benchmarks evaluated the proposed iterative IK/IC solver against a global optimization baseline (differential evolution, DE).

- **Static Pose-to-Pose Reaching:** For 200 randomized transitions, both solvers aimed to reach a target tip position ($x^*$) and task-space compliance ($C_x^*$) from an initial pose.
 - The iterative solver was approximately 5.4 times faster (mean 55.6 ms vs. 301.1 ms) and achieved lower mean system energy (343.9 J vs. 406.5 J, ~15% less).
 - The global optimizer exhibited higher compliance accuracy (1.13% mean relative error vs. 6.54% for the iterative method).
 - The iterative method also provided joint-continuous commands, which the global solver, as a one-shot optimizer, would not inherently provide without additional constraints. The conclusion is that the iterative solver, despite slightly lower static accuracy, offers substantial speed, lower energy consumption, and command continuity, making it suitable for real-time applications.

- **Dynamic Trajectory with Compliance Switching:** This benchmark, conducted in the MuJoCo physics simulator, involved tracking a figure-8 trajectory for three cycles while alternating between x-soft/y-stiff and x-stiff/y-soft compliance profiles every half-loop.
 - The iterative controller maintained smoother behavior, achieving lower tip-tracking position RMSE (70.5 mm vs. 120.2 mm) and lower task-space compliance error (0.064 vs. 0.106).
 - It also resulted in lower average system energy (287 J vs. 462 J) and a significantly higher update rate (29 Hz vs. ~1.3 Hz).
 - The iterative method's ability to continuously update compliance and position targets in real time allowed it to maintain superior tracking performance compared to the global optimizer, which suffered from delays and discontinuous commands.

**B. Simulation and Model Validation**
The accuracy of the analytical compliance model and the fidelity of the MuJoCo simulation were assessed through three steps:

- **Real-System Compliance Validation:** The physical arm's tip was subjected to prescribed circular displacements using a Kinova Gen3 arm, while a force-torque sensor measured reaction forces. The analytical compliance matrix was then used to map these measured forces back to displacements. The agreement between the modeled and prescribed displacement ellipses indicated that the analytical model accurately captures the directional tip compliance of the physical arm.
- **Simulated Compliance Validation:** In simulation, external forces were applied directly to the arm's tip. The resulting simulated displacements were compared against the analytical model's predictions. The close match between simulated and model-predicted displacement endpoints confirmed that the simulator accurately reproduces the modeled force-to-displacement response.
- **Simulation-to-Hardware Pressure-Target Posture Validation:** An actuator input sequence recorded on the real arm was replayed in simulation. A head-to-head comparison of the resulting joint-angle trajectories over a 20-second window showed that the simulated system closely tracked the major trends of the physical system's response across all 12 joint coordinates. This confirmed that the simulator serves as a faithful proxy for the physical system's pressure-driven motion.

**C. Model-Based Design for Isotropic Compliance**
Using the validated simulator, the research investigated an alternative arm morphology to address a known limitation: the baseline arm's coaxial segment arrangement leads to a resting-pose compliance singularity at q=0, where one principal direction of tip compliance collapses to near zero (infinite stiffness).

- A reconfigured design was explored, where the middle segment was lengthened, and the second and third connectors were pitched in opposite directions (+90° and -70°). This rearranged the segments into a non-collinear S-curve at rest.
- Simulation results showed that this reconfiguration removed the resting-pose compliance singularity. The baseline's tip-compliance ellipsoid was a flat lens with an isotropy of approximately 0, while the reconfigured arm achieved a near-uniform spherical ellipsoid with an isotropy of 0.88 (principal stiffnesses of 12.8, 11.9, and 11.2 N/m).
- The reachable workspace also significantly changed from a hollow crescent to a broad, filled lobe. This demonstrated that the modeling framework generalizes to different arm configurations and that segment geometry is a design lever for tuning task-appropriate compliance profiles.

**D. Hardware Demonstrations**
The physical arm was tested in two tasks requiring both precision and passive compliance:

- **Dynamic Disturbance Rejection on a Moving Whiteboard:** The robot wrote the word "soft" on a whiteboard that was either stationary or moving unpredictably (1.5 cm/s, 1.5 Hz) along the board-normal direction.
 - With inverse compliance control enabled, directional compliance profiles were selected: x-stiff/y-soft for the stationary board (maintaining pen pressure, reducing lateral jitter) and x-soft/y-stiff for the moving board (absorbing normal disturbances, preserving letter shape laterally).
 - These profiles produced clearer writing compared to compliance-control-off baselines (all-stiff or all-soft), demonstrating that actively tuning directional compliance improves performance during contact with dynamic environments.

- **Unobservable Misalignment Correction in a Key-and-Drawer Task:** The arm performed a sequential task of unlocking and opening a drawer using a rigid end-effector, relying on structural compliance to resolve unobserved contact geometry.
 - **Key Insertion:** The controller selected a soft profile along the insertion direction and a semi-stiff profile orthogonally. The soft axis allowed the key to passively self-seat and correct for unobserved keyhole offsets, while the semi-stiff axis maintained targeting.
 - **Drawer Opening:** The profile switched to stiff along the pulling direction and soft orthogonally. Stiffening the pulling axis allowed the arm to transmit force and resist being dislodged, while the soft orthogonal axis absorbed residual misalignment as the drawer traveled, protecting both the handle and the arm.
 - This task illustrated the utility of real-time compliance tuning, allowing the robot to transition between soft search-and-seat behavior and stiffer load-bearing behavior using the same hardware, without extensive path planning or sophisticated sensing.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a novel robotic architecture and control methodology that addresses key limitations in the field of soft robotics, particularly concerning simultaneous, real-time position and quantitative compliance control. The significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Algorithm-Informed Hardware Design:** The paper introduces and demonstrates the benefits of an "algorithm-informed" design philosophy. By purposefully engineering the mechanical structure (e.g., rigid backbone, isolated U-joints, linear pressure-stiffness mapping) to present a sparse, locally linearizable model, the system facilitates faster and more predictable control. This approach moves away from designing complex controllers for inherently unmodeled soft systems, instead advocating for hardware that simplifies the control problem.
- **Enhanced Real-Time Control Capabilities:** The developed iterative controller, combining inverse kinematics and a compliance Jacobian, enables the hyper-redundant soft robotic arm to adjust both its tip position and its task-space compliance in real time. This capability is crucial for dynamic interactions in unstructured environments, where contact forces and environmental uncertainties are prevalent. The ability to smoothly and continuously change compliance profiles during motion represents an advancement over methods that rely on slower global optimization or exhibit discontinuous behavior.
- **Improved Interaction with Unstructured Environments:** The hardware demonstrations (writing on a moving whiteboard, key insertion, and drawer opening) illustrate that quantitative structural compliance, actively controlled in real time, allows the robot to perform tasks that would be challenging for both purely rigid robots (due to lack of passive error correction) and many existing soft robots (due to lack of precision or real-time tunability). This suggests a path for robots to handle unobservable misalignments and dynamic disturbances more effectively, reducing reliance on complex sensing and path planning.
- **Generalizable Modeling Framework:** The validation that the analytical compliance model and simulation accurately represent the physical system across different configurations is significant. This generalizable modeling framework allows for model-based design, such as the reconfiguration study that demonstrated how changing segment geometry can eliminate compliance singularities and reshape the reachable compliance workspace *before* physical fabrication. This reduces development time and cost by enabling virtual prototyping and optimization.
- **Leveraging Hyper-redundancy and Hybrid Design:** The system effectively utilizes hyper-redundancy and a hybrid rigid-soft design. The redundancy provides a larger null space for secondary objectives (e.g., energy minimization, singularity avoidance), while the rigid skeletal structure provides a predictable kinematic backbone for control, complementing the intrinsic compliance of soft pneumatic actuators. This combination makes soft actuation more robust, modelable, and controllable.
- **Future Robotics Applications:** The findings pave the way for a new generation of manipulators capable of robustly performing delicate tasks, assembly operations, human-robot interaction, or exploration in complex, uncertain, or hazardous environments. The principles demonstrated could be applied to other forms of soft or hybrid robotic systems, broadening their applicability in industries ranging from manufacturing to healthcare.

In conclusion, this work significantly contributes to soft robotics by presenting a principled approach to design and control, where the robot's physical structure is specifically tailored to enable efficient, real-time, and quantitative control of both its position and its physical interaction properties. This advancement makes soft robots more practical and capable for deployment in real-world unstructured scenarios.
