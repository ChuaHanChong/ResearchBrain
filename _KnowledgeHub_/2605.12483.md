# Beyond GRPO and On-Policy Distillation: An Empirical Sparse-to-Dense Reward Principle for Language-Model Post-Training

> [!summary] Summary
> An empirical "reward-density principle" is proposed for LLM post-training, advocating a "teacher-first" data allocation strategy to efficiently utilize scarce labeled data. This approach trains a stronger teacher with sparse rewards, then distills its behavior to a smaller student via a two-stage dense bridge, enabling the student to achieve higher task performance than direct sparse reinforcement learning.

## Problem

- The scarcity of labeled, verifiable training data poses a significant bottleneck for effective post-training of large language models (LLMs).
- Directly applying sparse, sequence-level rewards to smaller, less capable student models often leads to inefficient data utilization and poor learning due to exploration challenges and collapsed gradient signals.
- Traditional knowledge distillation methods may suffer from coverage mismatch issues when transferring complex behaviors from a strong teacher to a "cold" student model.

## Method

- A ==reward-density principle== is introduced, suggesting ==sparse rewards== are best for "discovery" on strong teacher models, while ==dense rewards== are ideal for "compression" into student models.
- A ==multi-stage post-training pipeline== is proposed: (1) teacher-side ==sparse reinforcement learning== on labeled data, (2) a ==two-stage dense bridge== (==Forward-KL warmup== followed by ==On-Policy Distillation==) to transfer teacher behavior to the student, and (3) optional post-bridge student sparse RL.
- This ==teacher-first allocation strategy== prioritizes using scarce labeled data on the most capable model to learn reward-shaped behavior, then efficiently transferring this knowledge downstream.

## Results

- The teacher-first allocation strategy (RL-shaped 8B teacher distilled to a 1.7B student) achieved **79.3% MATH accuracy**, outperforming direct student-side GRPO (**75.9% MATH**) on the same student model.
- Reward-shaping the teacher is crucial; distillation from raw or SFT-trained teachers consistently underperformed or marginally improved over direct student GRPO, indicating that teacher scale alone is insufficient.
- The two-stage FKL-to-OPD dense bridge consistently surpassed single-stage distillation methods (**79.3% vs. 77.6%** for OPD-only on MATH accuracy), and subsequent sparse student RL on new data further improved performance from **75.4% to 78.5%** MATH accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sparse, sequence-level rewards are most productive when applied to stronger models capable of effective exploration and deriving informative gradients, making them suitable for teacher-side "discovery."
> - Dense, token-level rewards derived from a reward-shaped teacher are highly efficient for "compression," enabling smaller student models to acquire complex behaviors without facing long-horizon credit assignment problems.
> - The two-stage dense bridge (FKL then OPD) is critical for effective knowledge transfer, with FKL mitigating initial coverage mismatch and OPD providing robust on-policy supervision for the student.
