# Advancing Multimodal Reasoning: From Optimized Cold Start to Staged Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Chinese researchers develop ReVisual-R1, a 7B multimodal reasoning model that achieves state-of-the-art performance among open-source MLLMs through a three-stage Staged Reinforcement Optimization (SRO) framework combining text-centric cold start initialization, multimodal reinforcement learning with novel Prioritized Advantage Distillation (PAD) to address gradient stagnation in GRPO, and textual RL refinement to prevent capability decay, achieving 53.1% average across 10 challenging benchmarks (+16.8 points over previous open-source SOTA) and outperforming GPT-4o on several tasks including 89.2% on MATH500 and substantial gains on AIME mathematical reasoning benchmarks, supported by the carefully curated GRAMMAR dataset containing 99K samples designed for complex multimodal reasoning development.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal large language models struggle with complex reasoning despite successful reinforcement learning applications in text-only models
- Standard Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) suffers from gradient stagnation in multimodal settings with sparse rewards, and intensive multimodal RL training causes textual capability decay, degrading abstract reasoning abilities
- Conventional multimodal pre-training provides inadequate cold start initialization for sophisticated reasoning tasks

## Method

- Develop ==three-stage Staged Reinforcement Optimization (SRO)== framework: text-centric cold start (40K pure text reasoning samples), then multimodal RL, then textual RL refinement with a frozen vision tower to restore linguistic reasoning while preserving multimodal gains
- Introduce ==Prioritized Advantage Distillation (PAD)== that filters effective samples based on advantage ranges and prioritizes high-value sequences to prevent gradient stagnation
- Create ==GRAMMAR dataset== with 99K diverse samples through multi-stage curation including difficulty classification and topic balancing

## Results

- Achieves **53.1%** average across **10** benchmarks (**+16.8 points** over previous open-source **7B** SOTA), securing the top position among open-source models on **9 out of 10** individual benchmarks including MathVerse (**+5.4%**), MathVision (**+13.9%**), and LogicVista (**+9.6%**)
- Outperforms GPT-4o on multiple tasks including MATH500 (**89.2%** vs **74.6%**) and shows substantial leads on AIME24/AIME25 mathematical reasoning
- Ablation studies confirm effectiveness of three-stage curriculum and PAD's role in improving training stability with higher reward accuracy and faster convergence

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Text-centric cold start outperforms traditional multimodal initialization for complex reasoning, establishing foundational reasoning templates before visual grounding, with sequential training (textual → multimodal → textual RL) proving more effective than alternative orderings
> - Gradient stagnation in GRPO occurs when uniform rewards across response groups yield near-zero advantages, which PAD addresses through selective sampling
> - Efficient-length reward function prevents uncontrolled response verbosity while maintaining reasoning depth and training stability

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has demonstrated advancements in complex cognitive reasoning, with models like DeepSeek-R1 leveraging Reinforcement Learning (RL) to develop intricate reasoning abilities in text-only contexts. A subsequent ambition in artificial intelligence research is to extend these RL paradigms to Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), aiming to unlock similar multimodal cognitive capabilities.

However, the direct application of text-centric RL techniques to MLLMs has encountered challenges, suggesting that multimodal reasoning development requires more than architectural extension. A fundamental issue lies in cultivating abstract linguistic skills while simultaneously grounding them in visual perception, often resulting in a compromise where neither modality's potential is fully realized.

Existing MLLM training pipelines for cold start often rely on basic visual and textual pre-training, which may not adequately prepare models for complex reasoning tasks. Furthermore, the application of standard RL algorithms, such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), to multimodal settings has been observed to suffer from issues like gradient stagnation, impacting training stability and performance. Some concurrent works have also identified limitations in MLLM reasoning models, noting that they often produce relatively short responses, which can hinder the development of thorough self-reflection and comprehensive multimodal reasoning.

This work positions itself within this landscape by undertaking a detailed examination of MLLM training pipelines to identify and address these limitations. It seeks to develop a multi-stage training curriculum that systematically integrates optimized cold-start initialization, a refined multimodal RL phase, and a subsequent textual RL refinement, aiming to enhance deep, self-reflective reasoning and robust visual grounding in open-source MLLMs. By focusing on a structured curriculum and algorithmic enhancements, this research contributes to advancing the reasoning capabilities of MLLMs beyond current architectural or data scaling approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to activate complex reasoning capabilities in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by analyzing and optimizing their training pipelines. The authors identify and address three crucial phenomena that limit the current efficacy of MLLM reasoning, leading to specific motivations for their proposed approach:

1. **Ineffective Cold Start Initialization**:
 - **Problem**: Conventional cold-start phases for MLLMs often rely on simplistic visual and textual pre-training corpora. This initial deficit can critically hinder subsequent Reinforcement Learning (RL) stages from eliciting sophisticated, self-critical reasoning patterns.
 - **Motivation**: To unlock deeper deliberative reasoning, an enriched cold-start initialization is deemed necessary. The authors are motivated to investigate and implement a strategy where initializing with carefully selected text-only data, which instills foundational reflective capabilities and the capacity for extended Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, could prove more powerful than conventional multimodal cold starts. A preliminary observation indicated that text-only cold start can lead to performance surpassing many recent multimodal reasoning models even before multimodal RL, motivating a text-centric approach for this initial phase.

2. **Gradient Stagnation in Multimodal RL**:
 - **Problem**: The standard Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, commonly applied for multimodal RL, exhibits a "gradient stagnation" problem. This issue arises from a predominance of near-zero advantage estimates, especially with sparse binary rewards typical in complex multimodal tasks, which degrades both training stability and performance by halting learning for certain samples.
 - **Motivation**: To address this fundamental limitation and improve the efficacy and stability of multimodal RL, the authors are motivated to propose a novel mechanism, Prioritized Advantage Distillation (PAD). PAD aims to mitigate gradient stagnation by strategically filtering out uninformative (zero-advantage) samples and re-weighting informative trajectories, thereby focusing the learning process on more impactful data.

3. **Decline in Textual Capabilities Post-Multimodal RL**:
 - **Problem**: While multimodal RL is crucial for grounding reasoning in visual information, intensive training in this phase can inadvertently lead to a decline in purely textual capabilities, impacting linguistic expression and logical consistency.
 - **Motivation**: To ensure comprehensive and robust reasoning, the authors are motivated to integrate a final Textual Reinforcement Learning (TRL) stage. This stage is designed to act as a polishing step, sharpening the model’s linguistic fluency, logical consistency, and abstract reasoning without eroding the newly acquired visual grounding. It aims to consolidate the model's capabilities, leading to a synergistic integration of multimodal and textual reasoning.

Synthesizing these insights, the overarching objective is to propose a principled, three-stage training curriculum (text-centric cold-start, multimodal RL with PAD, and text-only RL refinement) that systematically cultivates robust reasoning and diverse competencies in MLLMs. The goal is to develop ReVisual-R1, an open-source MLLM that achieves state-of-the-art performance on challenging multimodal and textual reasoning benchmarks by uniquely integrating deep, self-reflective reasoning with robust visual grounding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ReVisual-R1 framework is based on a principled, three-stage training curriculum designed to systematically cultivate robust reasoning and diverse competencies in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).

**Overview: Optimized Cold Start and Staged Reinforcement Optimization (SRO)**
The framework commences with an optimized text-centric cold start phase, followed by a Staged Reinforcement Optimization (SRO) curriculum, which includes a multimodal RL phase and a subsequent textual RL phase.

#### 3.1. Optimized Cold Start for Multimodal Reasoning

- **Preliminary Study of Cold Start**: The authors conducted an initial investigation into cold-start effectiveness using two open-source multimodal datasets (Vision-R1, R1-One-Vision) and two textual datasets (DeepMath, OpenR1-Math). Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct with 40,000 instances from these datasets revealed that models trained with text-only cold start data exhibited more substantial improvements in both textual and multimodal reasoning tasks compared to those trained solely on multimodal datasets. Analysis of response lengths and pass rates further suggested that current multimodal cold start datasets might lack the complexity needed to inspire advanced reasoning. This led to the decision to adopt text-only data for the cold start stage.

- **GRAMMAR: Generalized Multimodal Reasoning Dataset**: Informed by the preliminary study, the authors developed GRAMMAR, a Generalized Multimodal Reasoning Dataset.
 - **Components**: GRAMMAR comprises two main parts:
 - **Cold Start Data**: Approximately 283K diverse and complex textual samples with explicit reasoning paths.
 - **Reinforcement Learning Data**: An additional 31K complex textual examples and 21K multimodal questions, all annotated with ground truths for verifiable rewards.
 - **Curation Pipeline**: The construction of GRAMMAR involved a multi-stage pipeline:
 1. **Ammassing Data**: Open-source reasoning datasets spanning various difficulty levels were collected (e.g., FigureQA, MAVIS, GeoQA for multimodal; Big-Math-RL, GAIR\_LIMO, DeepMath for text-only).
 2. **Rule-Based Filtering**: Initial filtering excluded problems like proofs or those with difficult-to-verify ground truths to ensure answer verifiability.
 3. **Initial Pruning**: Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct was used to prune overly simple or complex questions.
 4. **Difficulty Classification**: Qwen2.5-VL-32B-Instruct assessed remaining samples and classified them into ten difficulty levels.
 5. **Diversity and Redundancy Minimization**: Questions were encoded using NV-Embedding-V2, clustered with HDBSCAN, topics were assigned to clusters via Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, and balanced sampling was performed across topics and difficulty strata.

#### 3.2. Staged Reinforcement Optimization (SRO)

After the cold start, the SRO framework employs two distinct reinforcement learning stages:

- **Stage 1: Multimodal RL (MRL)**:
 - **Purpose**: This stage is critical for enabling the MLLM to ground textual concepts in visual information and execute cross-modal reasoning, primarily utilizing the multimodal samples from the GRAMMAR dataset.
 - **Core Algorithm**: GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) is used as the base RL algorithm.
 - **Prioritized Advantage Distillation (PAD)**: To address "Gradient Stagnation" in GRPO, particularly severe with sparse binary rewards, PAD was introduced.
 - **Per-Sequence Advantage Calculation**: For each sequence `i` in a batch, its absolute advantage `|Âi|` (learning signal magnitude) is computed.
 - **Effective Sample Filtering**: An "effective set" `E` is formed by selecting sequences whose `|Âi|` falls within a specified informative range `[T_low, T_high]`, where `T_low > 0` explicitly filters out stagnant (near-zero advantage) samples.
 - **Prioritized Sub-sampling from Effective Set**: From the effective set `E`, `k'` sequences are drawn to form a distilled mini-batch. Selection is prioritized based on the sequences' absolute advantages, using a temperature-controlled Softmax distribution. The temperature `τ` is decayed during training to balance exploration and exploitation. This mechanism focuses learning on high-value samples, enhancing stability and efficiency.

- **Stage 2: Textual RL (TRL)**:
 - **Purpose**: This phase aims to restore and enhance the model's purely textual capabilities, which might degrade during intensive MRL, and to further elevate its capacity for sophisticated abstract reasoning. It acts as a polishing step, sharpening linguistic expression and logical consistency.
 - **Data**: High-quality, text-only corpora from GRAMMAR are used, focusing on instruction-following and conversational abilities, as well as complex text-centric problem-solving tasks.
 - **Algorithm**: GRPO, augmented with the PAD mechanism, is again employed for policy optimization.
 - **Specifics**: During TRL, the vision tower of the MLLM is frozen to concentrate learning on textual reasoning. A small Kullback-Leibler (KL) penalty is incorporated alongside entropy annealing to enhance training stability.

**Implementation Details**:
- **Base Models**: ReVisual-R1 models are built upon Qwen-2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and Qwen-2.5-VL-3B-Instruct.
- **Tools**: LLaMA Factory was used for the cold-start phase, and Easy R1 for the MRL and TRL stages.
- **Training Configuration**: The GRPO KL divergence constraint was omitted in MRL to encourage broader policy exploration, while a small KL penalty was included in TRL. Training utilized 8 NVIDIA A100-80G GPUs. Detailed hyperparameters are provided in the appendix of the paper.
- **Training Data**: Cold start used ~283k text entries. MRL used ~26k multimodal entries from GRAMMAR. TRL used ~30k text entries.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents ReVisual-R1, an MLLM trained with a multi-stage curriculum, and evaluates its performance across a diverse set of multimodal and textual reasoning benchmarks.

**Overall Performance:**
ReVisual-R1 demonstrates superior performance compared to existing open-source MLLMs and achieves competitive or superior results against several closed-source commercial models.

- **7B Model Scale (ReVisual-R1-7B)**:
 - Achieves an average score of 53.1% across multimodal and textual benchmarks, representing a 16.8 percentage point improvement over the best-performing open-source baselines.
 - Secures the top position among open-source contenders in nine out of ten individual benchmarks: MathVerse (+5.4% ∆), MathVision (+18.6% ∆), DynaMath (+4.5% ∆), WeMath (+0.2% ∆), LogicVista (+1.5% ∆), AIME24 (+43.3% ∆), AIME25 (+15.4% ∆), GPQA (+10.1% ∆), and MATH500 (+22.0% ∆).
 - When compared to closed-source commercial models, ReVisual-R1-7B's average score (53.1%) surpasses that of OpenAI-GPT-4o (41.6%). On specific demanding benchmarks like MATH500, ReVisual-R1 (89.2%) outperforms both doubao-1.5-vision-pro-32k (85.2%) and OpenAI-GPT-4o (74.6%). It also demonstrates substantial leads over these commercial offerings on AIME24 and AIME25.

- **3B Model Scale (ReVisual-R1-3B)**:
 - Achieves an average score of 46.5%, an improvement of 16.0 percentage points over the best-performing open-source baselines in its scale.
 - Outperforms VLAA-Thinker-3B across all evaluated benchmarks.
 - These results validate the generalization and scalability of the ReVisual-R1 training framework across different model sizes.

**Ablation Studies:**

- **Staged Reinforcement Optimization (SRO)**:
 - An ablation study compared different combinations and orders of training stages (Cold Start (CS), Multimodal RL (MRL), Textual RL (TRL)).
 - The proposed sequence, CS + MRL + TRL, consistently yielded the highest average performance (49.6 Avg) on a subset of multimodal reasoning benchmarks, confirming the hypothesis that an initial MRL phase for visual grounding followed by a TRL phase for textual refinement is effective.
 - Alternative sequences, such as CS + MRL only (47.7 Avg) or CS + TRL + MRL (45.5 Avg), performed less effectively, indicating the importance of the specific staging.
 - A mixed-RL strategy (simultaneously training on mixed-modality data in the RL phase) achieved an average score of 47.6, which was significantly lower than the staged approach, further supporting the efficacy of the MRL-then-TRL ordering.

- **Prioritized Advantage Distillation (PAD)**:
 - Ablation studies assessed the impact of PAD and its components on performance during the MRL phase.
 - Full PAD implementation achieved superior performance (47.7 Avg) compared to GRPO-Baseline (45.1 Avg), GRPO-Filter-only (46.0 Avg), and Random-Sampling (46.2 Avg) on mathematical reasoning benchmarks.
 - PAD also outperformed DAPO (46.1 Avg), another RL strategy.
 - Training dynamics further indicated that PAD's sampling strategy led to higher reward accuracy and faster convergence, corroborating its effectiveness in mitigating gradient stagnation.

**Generalization of ReVisual-R1:**
- The model was evaluated on several knowledge-intensive benchmarks, including MMMU, MMMU-Pro, CMMMU, and on general-purpose multimodal perception (MMStar) and textual understanding (MMLU).
- ReVisual-R1 consistently demonstrated superior performance across these generalization benchmarks, indicating that the developed textual reasoning capabilities transfer positively to general multimodal and textual tasks. For instance, ReVisual-R1-7B achieved an average score of 60.91% across these benchmarks, outperforming other open-source 7B models.

Collectively, these findings validate the efficacy of the proposed training methodology, including the structured three-stage curriculum (optimized cold start, MRL with PAD, and TRL refinement) and algorithmic enhancements like Prioritized Advantage Distillation, for developing powerful and generalized multimodal reasoning capabilities in MLLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by introducing a novel training paradigm and an open-source model, ReVisual-R1, which demonstrates advanced reasoning capabilities. The significance and potential impact can be understood through several key aspects:

- **Challenging Conventional MLLM Training Paradigms**: The work challenges the prevalent approach to MLLM cold-start training by empirically demonstrating that a text-centric, high-difficulty cold-start is a crucial and previously overlooked foundation for unlocking sophisticated multimodal reasoning. This suggests that foundational linguistic reasoning may need to be robustly established before or in tandem with visual grounding, potentially reorienting initial training strategies for MLLMs.

- **Algorithmic Advancement in Reinforcement Learning for MLLMs**: The identification of "gradient stagnation" in standard Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for multimodal RL, and the proposal of Prioritized Advantage Distillation (PAD) as an effective solution, represent an important algorithmic contribution. PAD enhances training stability and sample efficiency by systematically filtering and prioritizing informative samples, which could be impactful for future RL applications in complex multimodal settings with sparse rewards.

- **Advancing Open-Source MLLM Capabilities**: ReVisual-R1, as an open-source 3B and 7B MLLM developed through this principled three-stage curriculum, achieves state-of-the-art performance on a suite of challenging multimodal and textual reasoning benchmarks. This provides the research community with a high-performing, accessible model that embodies advanced reasoning capabilities, potentially accelerating further research and application development in the open-source domain. Its ability to rival or outperform larger or proprietary models on specific tasks highlights the effectiveness of the proposed curriculum over sheer model scale.

- **Demonstrated Scalability and Generalization**: The framework's effectiveness across both 3B and 7B model scales, and its strong generalization performance on knowledge-intensive and general-purpose multimodal/textual benchmarks, indicate its robust design. This suggests that the principles of optimized cold start, staged RL, and PAD could be applicable to a wider range of MLLM architectures and tasks, fostering broader applicability and development.

- **Foundation for Future Research**: The identified limitations, particularly regarding theoretical foundations for the observed empirical gains and validation across larger architectures (e.g., MoE models), pave the way for future research. This includes systematically investigating the interplay between data type, ratio, and quality across training stages to determine optimal curricula for diverse skill clusters (STEM reasoning, perceptual grounding, general domains).

In summary, ReVisual-R1's comprehensive approach to MLLM training, combining data strategy with algorithmic refinement, offers a substantial step forward in cultivating deep, self-reflective multimodal reasoning. Its open-source nature and demonstrated performance have the potential to set new benchmarks and inspire future directions in the development of more capable and intelligent multimodal AI systems.
