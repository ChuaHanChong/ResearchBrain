# Cosmos Policy: Fine-Tuning Video Models for Visuomotor Control and Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from NVIDIA and Stanford University fine-tune a large, pretrained latent video diffusion model, Cosmos-Predict2, into a unified robot policy for visuomotor control and planning. This method achieves state-of-the-art average success rates of 98.5% on LIBERO, 67.1% on RoboCasa, and 93.6% on ALOHA, demonstrating enhanced data efficiency and robustness for complex manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Adapting large foundation models for robotics often requires complex multi-stage training pipelines or custom architectural modifications.
- Existing vision-language-action (VLA) models, typically trained on static image-text data, often lack the dynamic spatiotemporal priors essential for robust robot control.
- Traditional robot learning systems frequently employ separate, loosely coupled modules for policy, world modeling, and value estimation, which can increase system complexity.

## Method

- A large, pretrained ==latent video diffusion model== (**Cosmos-Predict2**) is fine-tuned in a single stage, without architectural changes, to function as a unified robot policy, world model, and value function.
- Diverse modalities such as robot proprioception, ==action chunks==, state values, and multi-camera images are encoded as "==latent frames==" and injected directly into the video model's existing ==latent diffusion== sequence.
- A ==joint training== scheme on robot demonstration and collected rollout data enables simultaneous learning of actions, future states, and expected rewards, leveraging ==auxiliary targets== to enhance performance.

## Results

- Achieved new state-of-the-art average success rates: **98.5%** on LIBERO, **67.1%** on RoboCasa (with significantly fewer demonstrations), and **93.6%** on real-world ALOHA bimanual tasks, outperforming leading baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the pretrained video model backbone improved performance by **3.9%** on LIBERO and significantly more on real-world tasks, validating the importance of rich spatiotemporal priors.
- Model-based planning, utilizing the learned world model and value function, yielded a **12.5-point** average score increase on the most challenging ALOHA tasks, demonstrating effective guidance for decision-making even with limited rollout data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pretrained video generation models inherently learn strong spatiotemporal priors from dynamic internet videos, which are crucial for robust low-level visuomotor control in robotics.
> - A simple single-stage fine-tuning approach with latent frame injection is sufficient to adapt a powerful video foundation model into a multi-functional robot policy without the need for bespoke architectural modifications.
> - Unifying the policy, world model, and value function within a single, pretrained video model architecture creates a more coherent and effective system for both direct control and model-based planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic control is rapidly evolving, with a growing trend towards leveraging large, pretrained foundation models to enable more generalist and capable robots. Cosmos Policy positions itself at the intersection of several key research directions:

- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** A significant trend involves adapting large Vision-Language Models (VLMs), originally trained on static image-text data, to robotics. Works like RT-2, OpenVLA, and π0.5 fall into this category. Cosmos Policy distinguishes itself by arguing that *pretrained video generation models* offer superior spatiotemporal priors compared to VLMs trained on static image-text pairs. Video models inherently learn temporal causality, implicit physics, and motion patterns from vast amounts of dynamic data, which are crucial for dynamic robot control. This forms a core differentiating hypothesis.

- **Video-based Robot Policies:** There has been increasing interest in using video models for robotic manipulation. Prior works in this area often introduce complexity by:
 - Requiring multiple training stages (e.g., fine-tuning video models, then training separate action modules).
 - Introducing new architectural components (e.g., separate action diffusers or inverse dynamics models).
 - Training unified video-action models from scratch, thereby not fully leveraging the benefits of large-scale video pretraining.
 Cosmos Policy aims to overcome these limitations by proposing a *single-stage fine-tuning approach* without any architectural modifications to the base video model. This simplifies the adaptation process and maximizes the utility of the pretrained spatiotemporal priors.

- **World Models and Value Functions in Robotics:** The integration of world models (which predict future states) and value functions (which predict expected rewards) with policy learning is a long-standing pursuit in robotics and reinforcement learning (e.g., Dyna, Dreamer, TD-MPC). These components enable model-based planning, offering benefits like improved sample efficiency and robustness. However, existing approaches often rely on *separate modules* for the policy, world model, and value function, typically trained from scratch. Cosmos Policy unifies these functions into a *single architecture*, initialized from a powerful pretrained video model, making the system more cohesive and potentially more efficient.

In summary, Cosmos Policy attempts to bridge the gap between powerful video generation capabilities and practical robot control. It critically assesses the architectural and training complexities of prior video-based robot policies and VLA models, proposing a streamlined, unified approach that leverages the inherent spatiotemporal understanding of large video foundation models for superior visuomotor control and effective planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a highly effective and architecturally simple method for adapting large, pretrained video generation models into robust robotic policies capable of visuomotor control and planning. The authors are motivated by several key hypotheses and observations:

1. **Leveraging Spatiotemporal Priors:** Recent video generation models exhibit a remarkable ability to capture complex physical interactions and scene evolution. The authors hypothesize that these inherent spatiotemporal priors, learned from millions of internet videos, are invaluable for robotics applications, offering a significant advantage over models primarily trained on static images.
2. **Simplifying Adaptation:** Existing approaches to adapt video models for robotics often introduce considerable complexity through multi-stage post-training or the addition of new architectural components. A core objective of Cosmos Policy is to demonstrate that a powerful robot policy can be achieved through a *single stage of post-training* on robot demonstration data, with *no architectural modifications* to the base video model. This simplicity is a major design principle.
3. **Unified Visuomotor Control and Planning:** The paper aims to create a unified model that can simultaneously perform three critical functions:
 - Predict **robot actions** (visuomotor control).
 - Predict **future states** (world modeling).
 - Predict **expected cumulative rewards (values)** (value function).
 This joint generation within a single latent diffusion process is central to the Cosmos Policy approach, enabling both direct policy execution and model-based planning.
4. **Enabling Effective Planning:** By generating future state images and values, Cosmos Policy seeks to facilitate test-time planning. The motivation here is to enable the robot to evaluate potential action trajectories, imagine their outcomes, and select actions that lead to higher-value future states, thereby increasing the likelihood of task success, especially in challenging scenarios.
5. **Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance:** A fundamental objective is to demonstrate that this novel approach can achieve state-of-the-art performance on a variety of challenging simulated and real-world manipulation benchmarks, outperforming strong baselines including diffusion policies trained from scratch, other video-based policies, and leading vision-language-action models.

In essence, the motivation is to unlock the full potential of large video foundation models for robotics by providing a straightforward, unified, and highly effective adaptation strategy that supports both robust control and intelligent planning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Cosmos Policy's methodology centers on adapting a large, pretrained latent video diffusion model, Cosmos-Predict2-2B-Video2World, into a unified robot policy, world model, and value function through a novel "latent frame injection" mechanism and a joint training scheme.

#### 3.1. Base Video Model: Cosmos-Predict2

The foundation is NVIDIA's Cosmos-Predict2-2B-Video2World, a latent video diffusion model. This model takes an initial image and text description to generate subsequent video frames. It operates on continuous tokens encoded by a spatiotemporal VAE tokenizer (Wan2.1) and is trained using the EDM denoising score matching formulation. The model's architecture is a diffusion transformer, capable of generating temporally coherent videos and implicitly learning complex spatiotemporal dynamics.

#### 3.2. Latent Frame Injection: Incorporating New Modalities

This is the core innovation for adapting the pretrained video model. Instead of adding new modules, Cosmos Policy encodes additional modalities directly as "latent frames" within the video model's existing latent diffusion sequence.
- **Modalities Included:** Robot proprioception (e.g., end-effector pose, joint angles), robot action chunks, state values (expected cumulative rewards), and images from multiple camera views.
- **Mechanism:** Raw images (including blank placeholders for non-image modalities) are first tokenized by the VAE. The latent frames corresponding to these placeholders are then *overwritten* with normalized and duplicated copies of the non-image modalities. For instance, a low-dimensional action chunk vector is normalized to [-1, 1] and then duplicated across the H'xW'xC' dimensions of a latent frame volume. Multiple camera views are handled by simply inserting their latent frames into the sequence.
- **Sequence Ordering:** The modalities are ordered in the latent sequence to represent $(s, a, s', V(s'))$, enabling autoregressive decoding for planning or parallel decoding for direct policy.
- **Flexibility:** This injection is highly flexible, allowing customization for different robot setups (e.g., varying number of cameras).
- **Training during fine-tuning:** Although these new modalities were absent during pretraining, the model learns to generate them effectively through fine-tuning, leveraging its strong prior over high-dimensional, complex data distributions.

#### 3.3. Joint Training of Policy, World Model, & Value Function

Cosmos Policy utilizes a single-stage post-training approach on robot demonstration data, fine-tuning all weights of the pretrained video model.
- **Batch Composition:** Each training batch of $(s, a, s', V(s'))$ tuples is split:
 - **50% from demonstrations:** Used to train the policy ($p(a, s', V(s')|s)$).
 - **50% from rollouts:** Further split into two halves:
 - One half for training the world model ($p(s', V(s')|s, a)$).
 - The other half for training the value function ($p(V(s')|s, a, s')$).
- **Auxiliary Targets:** The policy and world model are trained with auxiliary targets (e.g., policy predicts actions *and* future states *and* values), which the authors find improves performance.
- **Conditioning Scheme:** During training, different parts of the latent sequence are noised/denoised based on whether the model is learning to be a policy, world model, or value function. The "clean" portions serve as conditioning inputs.
- **Decoding:** The model can generate actions, future states, and values either jointly in parallel (faster for direct policy) or autoregressively (potentially higher quality for planning).
- **Noise Distribution Adjustment:** The standard log-normal noise distribution of Cosmos-Predict2 is modified to a hybrid log-normal-uniform distribution, emphasizing higher noise levels. This is crucial for improving action prediction accuracy during generation, as robotic actions require high precision.

#### 3.4. Planning with Cosmos Policy’s World Model and Value Function

- **Learning from Rollout Experiences:** Effective planning requires the world model and value function to generalize beyond successful demonstrations. Cosmos Policy addresses this by collecting policy rollout data (including failures) and using it for a *second stage of fine-tuning*. This refinement focuses more heavily on the world model and value function predictions (90% of batch weight).
- **Dual Deployment:** After this refinement, a "dual deployment" strategy is used: the original Cosmos Policy checkpoint serves as the "policy model" for action generation, while the *refined* checkpoint acts as the "planning model" for predicting future states and values.
- **Model-Based Planning (Best-of-N Sampling):**
 1. Sample *N* candidate action proposals from the policy model.
 2. Use the planning model to predict the future state and value for each action proposal.
 3. Select and execute the action that leads to the predicted state with the highest predicted value.
- **Robustness:** To enhance accuracy, predictions are ensembled (e.g., 3 world model queries, 5 value function queries per state). A "majority mean" aggregation strategy is used to handle multimodal or high-variance value predictions.
- **Efficiency:** The search process is parallelized across multiple GPUs, and full action chunks are executed to avoid further computational overhead.

This integrated approach, from novel modality encoding to sophisticated planning, allows Cosmos Policy to leverage the power of a pretrained video foundation model in a uniquely unified and effective manner for complex robotic tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Cosmos Policy demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across multiple challenging robotic manipulation benchmarks, validating its core design principles.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance as a Direct Policy (without planning)

- **LIBERO Simulation Benchmark:** Cosmos Policy achieved an average success rate of **98.5%**, setting a new state of the art. It outperformed leading diffusion policies (Diffusion Policy, Dita), other video-based policies (UVA, Video Policy), and a wide range of state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (π0, UniVLA, π0.5, OpenVLA-OFT, CogVLA).
- **RoboCasa Simulation Benchmark:** Cosmos Policy again achieved a new state of the art with an average success rate of **67.1%**. Notably, it achieved this using significantly fewer training demonstrations (50 human-teleoperated demos per task) compared to many baselines which used 300 to 3000 demonstrations, highlighting its data efficiency.
- **Real-world ALOHA Robot Tasks:** In a suite of four challenging bimanual manipulation tasks, Cosmos Policy achieved the highest average score of **93.6%**. It outperformed strong real-world baselines like Diffusion Policy, OpenVLA-OFT+, π0, and π0.5, demonstrating its efficacy in complex, contact-rich, and high-precision real-world scenarios.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Performance on Challenging Tasks

On the ALOHA robot, Cosmos Policy excelled particularly in tasks requiring high action multimodality ("put candies in bowl") and high-precision manipulation ("put candy in ziploc bag").
- **Competitor Limitations:** Competitor VLAs like π0.5 and OpenVLA-OFT+ struggled with these tasks. π0.5 often failed to reliably grasp the ziploc bag slider, while OpenVLA-OFT+ sometimes reached between objects instead of directly for one, suggesting difficulties in modeling multimodal action distributions accurately.
- **Cosmos Policy's Strength:** Cosmos Policy demonstrated substantially greater reliability in these challenging conditions, indicating that its video model priors provide a strong foundation for low-level control, even without extensive action-labeled robot data.

#### 4.3. Impact of Architectural Components and Pretraining (Ablations)

Ablation studies confirmed the importance of Cosmos Policy's specific design choices:
- **Auxiliary Losses:** Removing the auxiliary targets (i.e., making the policy predict only actions $p(a|s)$ instead of joint $p(a, s', V(s'))|s)$) led to a **1.5% absolute drop** in average success rate on LIBERO, indicating the benefit of joint training for the policy.
- **Pretrained Video Model Backbone:** Training Cosmos Policy from randomly initialized weights (without the pretrained Cosmos-Predict2 backbone) resulted in a **3.9% drop** in average success rate on LIBERO. On the ALOHA "fold shirt" task, the "from scratch" variant scored 18.7 points lower and exhibited jerky, potentially damaging motions. These results strongly support the hypothesis that rich spatiotemporal priors from the pretrained video model are critical for robust performance.
- **Additional Ablations (RoboCasa):** Further ablations showed that progressively removing the value function training, world model training, and eventually future state supervision for the policy, significantly decreased performance, with the most substantial drop (from 67.1% to 44.4%) when the policy was trained to predict only actions ($\pi(a|s)$). This highlights the crucial role of training the policy to predict future states alongside actions.

#### 4.4. Effectiveness of Model-Based Planning

- **Improved Task Completion:** When deployed with model-based planning (using V(s') formulation) on the two most challenging ALOHA tasks, Cosmos Policy showed a **12.5-point average score increase**. This significant improvement, even with a limited amount of rollout data for refinement, validates the efficacy of model-based planning.
- **Accurate World Model:** Qualitatively, the fine-tuned planning model accurately predicted future states (e.g., predicting errors like losing grasp of a bag slider), enabling the policy to avoid mistakes made by the direct policy.
- **Model-Based vs. Model-Free Planning:** Comparing model-based (V(s')) planning with a model-free Q(s,a) variant, the model-based approach consistently achieved higher performance. This is attributed to its ability to leverage learned environment dynamics for more effective and sample-efficient planning, especially with limited rollout data where learning an accurate Q-function can be challenging.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Cosmos Policy represents a significant step forward in robot learning, particularly in the realm of leveraging large foundation models for generalist robotics. Its contributions and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Simplicity and Efficiency in Adaptation:** The most profound contribution is demonstrating that complex robot policies, world models, and value functions can be unified and adapted from a large pretrained video model through a *single stage of fine-tuning with no architectural modifications*. This dramatically simplifies the development process, reduces engineering overhead, and maximizes the transfer of knowledge from large-scale pretraining. It offers a clear alternative to multi-stage training pipelines or bespoke architectural designs common in prior work.

2. **Unlocking Spatiotemporal Priors for Robotics:** By effectively leveraging the spatiotemporal priors embedded in video foundation models, Cosmos Policy provides a strong foundation for robust low-level control policies. The superior performance over VLA models trained on static image-text data highlights the critical importance of dynamic, temporal understanding for visuomotor tasks. This suggests a paradigm shift towards prioritizing video-based pretraining for future robot foundation models.

3. **Unified Model for Control and Planning:** The ability to jointly generate actions, future states, and values within a single, coherent architecture is a major advancement. This unification streamlines the system, potentially making it more robust and easier to train compared to approaches that rely on separate, loosely coupled modules.

4. **Effective Model-Based Planning with Limited Data:** The demonstration of improved performance through model-based planning, even with a relatively small amount of additional rollout data, is highly impactful. It shows that the learned world model and value function are sufficiently accurate to guide decision-making effectively, enabling robots to anticipate outcomes and achieve higher success rates in challenging, high-precision, and multimodal tasks. This capability moves robots closer to intelligent, proactive behavior rather than purely reactive control.

5. **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Consistently achieving state-of-the-art results across diverse simulation and real-world benchmarks, and notably with higher data efficiency in some cases (e.g., RoboCasa), solidifies Cosmos Policy as a leading approach in the field. Its success in complex bimanual manipulation tasks further underscores its practical utility.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
Despite its strengths, the paper acknowledges several limitations, which also point to promising avenues for future research:
- **Inference Speed for Planning:** Model-based planning significantly increases inference latency (e.g., ~5 seconds per action chunk for N=8 planning). This restricts its applicability to highly dynamic tasks or fast-paced environments. Future work could focus on optimizing the search process, reducing denoising steps while maintaining quality, or exploring more efficient planning algorithms.
- **Data Requirements for Planning:** While planning improved performance with limited rollouts, achieving truly accurate predictions beyond the demonstration distribution still requires substantial rollout data. Research into more data-efficient learning for world models and value functions, perhaps through improved data augmentation or self-supervised learning, is crucial.
- **Limited Planning Depth:** The current approach uses best-of-N planning with a single layer in the search tree (i.e., planning for one action chunk ahead). Extending the world model's prediction horizon and exploring deeper planning trees (e.g., Monte Carlo Tree Search variants) could lead to more sophisticated and long-horizon decision-making.

In conclusion, Cosmos Policy provides a compelling framework for future robot learning, emphasizing simplicity, powerful pretraining, and unified capabilities for control and planning. It establishes a strong benchmark and opens exciting directions for developing more capable and generalist robotic agents.
