# Toward Hardware-Agnostic Quadrupedal World Models via Morphology Conditioning

> [!summary] Summary
> A Quadrupedal World Model (QWM) integrates explicit robot morphology and adaptive reward normalization to learn universal locomotion physics, disentangling environmental dynamics from specific hardware. This enables zero-shot transfer of control policies to unseen quadrupedal robots, including real hardware, achieving stable and adaptive gaits without requiring fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning policies and World Models (WMs) in robotics typically overfit to specific hardware, necessitating extensive retraining for variations in robot morphology.
- Existing multi-robot transfer methods often rely on implicit system identification, leading to an adaptation lag and potential safety concerns for zero-shot deployment on new robots.
- Entanglement of static robot properties within a World Model's latent state reduces its capacity to learn generalizable dynamic phenomena.

## Method

- A Quadrupedal World Model (QWM) based on DreamerV3's Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) is introduced, explicitly conditioned on robot morphology.
- A Physical Morphology Encoder (PME) extracts and normalizes engineering specifications from robot USD files, which are integrated into the World Model's observation encoder and recurrent dynamics for dedicated processing.
- Adaptive Reward Normalization (ARN) employs quantile-based scaling for each robot type to balance learning signals across heterogeneous morphologies with varying reward scales.

## Results

- QWM demonstrated superior stability, faster convergence, and higher performance in mastering locomotion across a diverse cohort of simulated quadrupedal robots compared to existing model-free and World Model baselines.
- The model achieved high-fidelity long-horizon dynamics prediction with minimal error accumulation for various robot embodiments, validating its capability as a neural simulator.
- Policies trained with QWM successfully executed zero-shot locomotion on unseen physical Unitree Go1 and ANYmal-D robots, producing adaptive gaits with tracking errors comparable to specialist controllers and zero falls.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly conditioning the World Model on robot morphology, rather than relying on implicit inference, significantly improves learning stability and sample efficiency across diverse quadrupedal designs.
> - The QWM architecture achieves disentanglement in the latent space, enabling the recurrent state to encode morphological identity and dynamic context, while the stochastic state focuses on instantaneous observations.
> - Adaptive Reward Normalization is critical for effective multi-robot training, preventing dominant reward scales from hindering learning across heterogeneous robot fleets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in applying Reinforcement Learning (RL) and World Models (WMs) to robotics: the "Hardware Lottery." Traditional RL policies and WMs often exhibit high specificity, overfitting to the kinematic and dynamic properties of the particular robot they were trained on. This limits their transferability, necessitating extensive retraining or fine-tuning when robot hardware undergoes modifications or when deploying to a heterogeneous fleet. This contrasts with advancements in fields like Natural Language Processing and Computer Vision, where generalizable models can adapt to diverse contexts without complete retraining.

Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) and WMs aim to improve sample efficiency by learning predictive models of environment dynamics, enabling agents to learn behaviors in imagination. While WMs have demonstrated success in virtual environments and specific control tasks, their application in complex robotics, particularly for legged locomotion, has largely resulted in hardware-locked specialists. Previous efforts in multi-task and transfer learning for robotics often relied on implicit system identification, where robots infer physical properties from interaction history. Morphology representation has explored architectural biases like Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) or Transformer-based methods, or implicit inference from interaction history. However, these methods either require adaptation time or do not fully leverage known engineering specifications available prior to deployment.

This paper positions itself as an advancement towards "Physical World Models" capable of capturing fundamental locomotion physics independent of specific robot embodiments, aligning with the roadmap for Type III: Robotic Agents, which emphasizes embodiment generalization as a critical capability for general-purpose robotic agents. It seeks to bridge the gap between hardware-specific robotics and generalizable AI by explicitly conditioning WMs on robot morphology.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a generalizable Quadrupedal World Model (QWM) that can disentangle environmental dynamics from robot morphology. This aims to overcome the current limitation where WMs are specialized for specific hardware, making them unable to transfer knowledge to different robot embodiments without retraining.

The motivation stems from several critical issues in contemporary robotic control:
- **Overfitting to Specific Hardware:** Standard policies and WMs in robotics tend to overfit to a particular robot's kinematic and dynamic properties. This means a model trained on one robot (e.g., Boston Dynamics Spot) would fail on another (e.g., Unitree Go1) due to mismatches in physical attributes.
- **Computational and Time Costs:** Any modification to a robot's components (e.g., motors, limb lengths) or deployment across a fleet of different robots currently necessitates training a new model from scratch, requiring millions of new samples and significant computational resources.
- **Safety Concerns with Implicit System Identification:** Existing multi-task approaches often rely on implicit system identification, where the agent infers static physical properties (such as mass or limb length) from its motion history. This approach leads to an "adaptation lag," where the robot must interact with the environment (potentially unsafely or sub-optimally) to gather enough data to identify itself before it can learn effective control. This is particularly problematic for zero-shot transfer, where safety is paramount.
- **Inefficient Latent Space Utilization:** When morphology is treated as a latent variable to be inferred, the recurrent state of the World Model must simultaneously encode both the dynamic state of the robot and its static physical properties. This entanglement can lead to interference, reduced capacity, and a failure to capture universal locomotion dynamics.
- **Lack of Generalizability:** Robotics lags behind other AI fields in developing models that can generalize across a wide array of contexts without extensive retraining.

To address these motivations, the authors propose an alternative: explicitly providing the World Model with the robot's engineering specifications. By integrating a physical morphology encoder and a reward normalizer, the goal is to enable the QWM to function as a neural simulator capable of generalizing across diverse morphologies. This capability is intended to unlock zero-shot control, allowing policies to be deployed on entirely unseen quadrupeds without requiring fine-tuning, adaptation periods, or warm-up phases. The ultimate aim is to create a morphology-conditioned world model that captures the fundamental physics of locomotion, independent of a specific robot, thus facilitating the development of foundational tools for robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The QWM framework is built upon the DreamerV3 Model-Based Reinforcement Learning backbone, which utilizes a Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM). The authors introduce three core architectural innovations to enable generalization across morphologies: an explicit Physical Morphology Encoder (PME), morphology-conditioned world model dynamics, and an Adaptive Reward Normalization (ARN) scheme.

#### 3.1. Recurrent State-Space Models (RSSM) Backbone

The base architecture is an RSSM, which decomposes the latent state into a deterministic recurrent state ($h_t$) for historical context and a stochastic state ($z_t$) for current observation information. The RSSM operates through:
- A Recurrent Model ($h_t = f_\phi(h_{t-1}, z_{t-1}, a_{t-1})$) to update memory.
- A Representation Model ($z_t \sim q_\phi(z_t | h_t, o_t)$) for inferring posterior state during training.
- A Transition Predictor ($\hat{z}_t \sim p_\phi(\hat{z}_t | h_t)$) for predicting prior state during planning.
- A Decoder ($ \hat{o}_t \sim p_\phi(\hat{o}_t | h_t, z_t)$) for reconstructing observations.
- Reward and Continuation Heads ($\hat{r}_t, \hat{c}_t \sim p_\phi(\hat{r}_t, \hat{c}_t | h_t, z_t)$) for predicting training signals.

The model is optimized using a variational bound that includes reconstruction losses, reward prediction, and a KL-divergence regularizer to align prior and posterior distributions. DreamerV3's symlog predictions and KL balancing are retained for robustness.

#### 3.2. Explicit Morphology Encoding (PME)

Instead of implicitly inferring robot morphology, QWM explicitly conditions the model on a morphology parameter $\mu$. This parameter is a normalized feature vector derived directly from the robot's Universal Scene Description (USD) file, representing its engineering specifications. The PME formalizes this as a deterministic mapping $\Phi : U \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^d$.
The extracted physical parameters are grouped into:
- **Kinematics & Topology:** Features like hip offset ($l_{hip}$), thigh length ($l_{thigh}$), shank length ($l_{shank}$), and a binary knee configuration style ($k_{cfg}$) that distinguish topological families (e.g., ANYmal's "X-config" vs. Unitree's "dog-like" knees).
- **Geometry:** Stance length ($l_{stance}$), stance width ($w_{stance}$), and their aspect ratio ($l_{stance}/w_{stance}$), characterizing the robot's stability polygon.
- **Dynamics:** Logarithm of total mass ($\log(1 + M_{total})$) and the trunk mass ratio ($m_{trunk}/M_{total}$), capturing mass distribution and rotational inertia.
- **Actuation:** Torque density ($\tau_{spec}$), reflecting the robot's specific force capacity derived from joint effort limits.

These raw features ($\mu_{raw}$) are then min-max normalized based on training cohort statistics to ensure numerically stable gradients and bound values to $[-1, 1]$. This explicit encoding provides grounded structural context, allowing the WM to allocate its latent capacity to dynamic phenomena.

#### 3.3. Morphology-Conditioned World Model

The extracted morphology vector $\mu$ is integrated into the RSSM architecture at two points to prevent the static signal from being "washed out" by dynamic observations:
- **Dual-Tower Encoder:** The observation encoder is split into two pathways. A "dynamic tower" processes high-frequency proprioceptive data ($o_t$) via a multi-layer perceptron (MLP), while a "static tower" processes the morphology vector $\mu$ via a separate, shallower MLP. Their embeddings are then concatenated and fused into a combined embedding ($e_t$) before being fed to the RSSM. This ensures dedicated capacity for morphological representation.
- **Recurrent Dynamics Conditioning:** The morphology vector $\mu$ is directly injected into the recurrent model's transition function. The updated recurrent model becomes $h_t = f_\phi(h_{t-1}, z_{t-1}, a_{t-1}, \mu)$. This explicit conditioning is intended to relieve the RNN of the burden of memorizing static physical properties, allowing $h_t$ to focus on the dynamic state of the system.

#### 3.4. Heterogeneous Reward Normalization (ARN)

To address the challenge of varying reward scales across a heterogeneous robot fleet (due to different physical scales and reward function definitions), QWM employs an Adaptive Reward Normalization (ARN) scheme. This system uses quantile-based scaling, tracking the 5th and 95th percentiles of returns for each robot type independently using an Exponential Moving Average (EMA). The reward scale ($\sigma_R$) is derived from the difference between these quantiles, dynamically normalizing rewards ($r_{norm} = r / \max(1.0, \sigma_R)$). This prevents robots with larger reward scales from dominating the WM's loss function, ensuring a balanced learning signal across morphologies.

#### 3.5. Policy Learning in Imagination

The policy ($\pi_\theta$) and critic ($v_\psi$) are trained entirely in imagination, using trajectories rolled out by the WM's transition predictor. Since the policy interacts only with the WM's latent dynamics, an accurate WM allows for zero-shot adaptation of the policy to new robots without physical interaction or fine-tuning, potentially reducing risks of hardware damage.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments aimed to demonstrate QWM's ability to master locomotion across diverse quadrupedal morphologies with a single model, generalize to unseen robots in a zero-shot manner, and transfer to real-world hardware.

#### 4.1. Heterogeneous Simulation Infrastructure

The authors developed "Hetero-Isaac," a custom environment built on NVIDIA Isaac Lab, which enabled simultaneous training of a single agent across a batch of diverse quadrupedal robots (ANYmal B, C, D; Unitree A1, Go1, Go2, B2; Boston Dynamics Spot). This setup manages distinct USDs, physics, and heterogeneous reward definitions for each robot, facilitating the multi-morphology training.

#### 4.2. Multi-Morphology Mastery

QWM was compared against model-free baselines (Vanilla PPO, PME-PPO, BoT-PPO) and World Model baselines (DreamerV3, PWM, TWISTER) trained simultaneously on the heterogeneous cohort.
- **QWM Performance:** QWM exhibited significantly higher stability and faster convergence in terms of mean reward and episode length compared to other WM baselines. It achieved stable locomotion (reaching max horizon of 1000 steps) and high reward performance.
- **WM Baselines:** Baselines relying on implicit system identification struggled with convergence, often exhibiting "mean-dynamics" collapse where the model approximated average physics rather than specific robot dynamics, leading to high prediction errors and policy divergence.
- **Model-Free Baselines:** PME-PPO, which also uses explicit morphology conditioning, achieved performance comparable to QWM but required approximately twice the environment steps for convergence. BoT-PPO, a graph-neural network architecture, showed significant lag in learning. These results indicated that explicit morphology features (PME) are crucial for control across diverse kinematic chains, and QWM's model-based approach offers sample efficiency advantages.

#### 4.3. Long-Horizon Dynamics Prediction

To validate its function as a neural simulator, QWM's open-loop prediction fidelity was evaluated.
- **Qualitative Visualization:** Open-loop rollouts for ANYmal-D, Unitree Go1, and Spot demonstrated tight synchronization between imagined trajectories and ground truth simulation over 45 prediction steps, capturing distinct physical signatures.
- **Quantitative Accuracy:** Normalized Mean Squared Error (NMSE) of predicted proprioceptive observations showed QWM consistently maintained the lowest error profile across different embodiments, with minimal error accumulation. In contrast, WM baselines suffered from high systematic error (PWM) or rapid divergence (DreamerV3), indicating difficulties in disentangling heterogeneous dynamics without explicit conditioning.

#### 4.4. Zero-Shot Generalization to Unseen Morphologies

QWM's ability to generalize to robots entirely excluded from the training set was assessed in two regimes:
- **Morphological Interpolation:** For held-out robots structurally similar to the training cohort (Unitree Go1, ANYmal-D), QWM achieved locomotion performance competitive with specialist PPO baselines. PME-PPO showed substantially lower performance, confirming that the benefit was due to QWM's learned latent dynamics, not just μ conditioning. Morphological analysis confirmed these robots were within high-density clusters of the training distribution, allowing effective interpolation.
- **Morphological Extrapolation:** On the Unitree B2, a geometric outlier with significantly higher mass and different stance geometry, zero-shot performance degraded significantly for both QWM and PME-PPO. This limitation was attributed to the B2 representing a region of the physical design space largely unexplored by the training distribution, highlighting the reliance on the support of the training data.

#### 4.5. Real-World Deployment

The zero-shot capabilities were validated on physical Unitree Go1 and ANYmal-D robots (held-out during training).
- **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The frozen zero-shot QWM policy successfully generated stable locomotion on both platforms without any real-world fine-tuning.
- **Adaptive Gaits:** The agent automatically adapted its control strategy to the distinct dynamics of each robot, producing a high-frequency trot for the agile Go1 and a slower, more grounded gait for the heavier ANYmal-D.
- **Quantitative Evaluation:** Tracking errors for linear and angular velocity were within approximately 10% of specialist PPO controllers, with zero falls observed across all trials. This confirmed that explicit morphology conditioning successfully encodes fundamental physical properties robust to the sim-to-real gap.

#### 4.6. Ablation Study

An ablation study (detailed in Appendix D) systematically evaluated the contributions of PME, ARN, and the specific conditioning locations:
- **ARN's Critical Role:** All configurations lacking ARN completely failed to learn, demonstrating its necessity for stable optimization across heterogeneous reward scales.
- **Conditioning Location Impact:** Removing morphology conditioning from the encoder led to a catastrophic late-stage collapse, while removing it from the recurrent dynamics degraded sample efficiency and asymptotic reward, highlighting the importance of both injection points.
- **Explicit vs. Implicit:** The "w/o PME" variant (implicit identification) achieved maximum episode length but lower asymptotic reward, confirming that explicit morphology is required for optimal and sample-efficient gait discovery.
- **DreamerV3 Baseline:** A standard DreamerV3 (w/o PME & ARN) performed poorly, demonstrating the overall value of the QWM framework's integrated components.

#### 4.7. Latent State Disentanglement

Visualizations (PCA and t-SNE) and probing analysis (detailed in Appendix F) of the latent states demonstrated:
- **Morphology Clusters:** The full latent space organized into morphology-specific clusters, with dynamic state encoded as smooth gradients within these clusters.
- **Functional Separation:** The deterministic recurrent state ($h_t$) explicitly retained morphological identity and dynamic context due to $\mu$ conditioning. In contrast, the stochastic state ($z_t$) was largely morphology-agnostic (silhouette score near zero), primarily capturing instantaneous observation context. This confirmed that explicit $\mu$ conditioning of $h_t$ prevented redundant encoding of morphology in $z_t$, achieving the intended disentanglement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that progresses World Models in robotics from hardware-specific tools to more generalizable, physics-grounded agents. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

- **Addressing the "Hardware Lottery" and Cost Reduction:** By enabling a single QWM to learn and generalize across diverse robot morphologies, the work significantly reduces the need for retraining for every hardware variation. This can lead to substantial savings in computational resources, data collection efforts, and development time for robotics research and industrial applications.
- **Enhanced Safety and Efficiency in Deployment:** The demonstrated zero-shot transfer capability for unseen robots in both simulation and the real world eliminates the requirement for risky and time-consuming "warm-up" periods or fine-tuning on physical hardware. This directly contributes to safer and more efficient deployment of robotic systems.
- **Foundation for General-Purpose Robotic Agents:** QWM represents a step towards developing "Physical World Models" that capture universal principles of locomotion physics, rather than specific robot behaviors. This aligns with the broader vision of embodied AI, where agents can understand and interact with the physical world in a versatile manner, independent of their specific embodiment.
- **Neural Simulator Capabilities:** The learned World Model functions as a real-time "physics adapter," translating an unseen robot's dynamics into a standardized latent space. This predictive capability extends beyond merely enabling reactive policies; it can serve as a neural simulator for downstream tasks such as Model Predictive Control, safety-aware planning, or zero-shot imagination of novel tasks, thereby expanding the utility of learned models.
- **Advancement in Model-Based Reinforcement Learning:** The explicit conditioning on morphology and adaptive reward normalization schemes are methodological advancements for MBRL, improving stability and sample efficiency in heterogeneous multi-robot learning scenarios.
- **Guiding Future Research:** The work clearly delineates the boundaries of its generalization capabilities, particularly the limitations in extrapolating to morphological outliers outside the training data distribution. This provides clear directions for future research, suggesting the need for more diverse training cohorts to span a broader "physics basis" of mass and geometry, as well as architectural extensions (e.g., GNNs or Transformers) to handle more complex or variable kinematic trees. The exploration of grey-box models that couple differentiable analytical rigid-body models with recurrent imagination is also proposed as a future avenue.
