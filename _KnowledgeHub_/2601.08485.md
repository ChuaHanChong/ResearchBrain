# AME-2: Agile and Generalized Legged Locomotion via Attention-Based Neural Map Encoding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ETH Zurich developed AME-2, an agile and generalized reinforcement learning framework that leverages an attention-based neural map encoder and a real-time uncertainty-aware mapping pipeline. This system enables quadrupedal (ANYmal-D) and bipedal (TRON1) robots to achieve state-of-the-art agility and robustly generalize across diverse, previously unseen real-world terrains, outperforming prior methods on complex locomotion tasks.

## Problem

- Achieving both high agility and broad generalization for legged robots in diverse, unstructured environments is challenging for existing classical and learning-based perceptive locomotion systems.
- Prior methods often face trade-offs between agility, generalization, computational efficiency, interpretability, and robustness to sensing errors or occlusions.
- Traditional mapping approaches are slow and sensitive to noise, while many learning-based maps overfit to training distributions or lack uncertainty modeling, hindering sim-to-real transfer and robustness.

## Method

- The **AME-2** framework combines an attention-based neural map encoder with a learning-based, uncertainty-aware mapping pipeline within a reinforcement learning (PPO) setup.
- A teacher-student learning scheme is used for sim-to-real transfer, where a privileged teacher guides a deployable student policy that incorporates AME-2 and a real-time neural mapping pipeline.
- ==The AME==-2 encoder uses multi-head attention to selectively focus on salient terrain features, integrating global terrain context with local pointwise features for robust terrain reasoning.

## Results

- Both ANYmal-D and TRON1 robots demonstrated state-of-the-art agility, successfully navigating complex parkour courses (ANYmal-D up to 2 m/s) and climbing/descending platforms (TRON1 up to 0.48m climb / 0.88m descent) in a zero-shot manner on unseen terrains.
- The controllers showed robust generalization across a wide range of diverse and unseen real-world terrains, including sparse obstacles, balance beams, and dynamic platforms.
- Ablation studies confirmed the AME-2 teacher's superior performance (**95.2%** success rate on test terrains) over AME-1 (**51.2%**) and MoE (**45.0%**), and the neural mapping pipeline produced high-quality, uncertainty-aware maps in real-time on onboard hardware.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The AME-2 encoder's architecture, combining global features with attention-weighted local features, significantly enhances generalization to unseen terrains compared to prior attention-based models.
> - A lightweight, Bayesian-learned neural mapping pipeline, incorporating uncertainty prediction and a Probabilistic Winner-Take-All fusion strategy, provides robust and efficient real-time terrain information critical for agility and generalization.
> - Extensive domain randomization, a comprehensive reward function, and an asymmetric actor-critic setup are crucial for effective sim-to-real transfer and the emergence of complex, adaptive locomotion behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of agile and generalized legged locomotion for robots in diverse, unstructured environments presents a significant challenge in robotics. This work addresses the critical need for perceptive locomotion systems that can navigate complex terrains while maintaining both high agility and broad generalization capabilities.

Historically, perceptive locomotion systems have largely fallen into two categories:
- **Classical Model-Based Approaches:** These methods integrate explicit state estimation, terrain mapping (e.g., elevation maps), and model-based planning and control. Examples include systems that perform foothold or trajectory planning followed by a model-based tracking controller. While these approaches offer guarantees and can generalize under specific modeling assumptions, their performance is often limited by model inaccuracies, state estimation and mapping errors (especially under occlusions or fast movements), and high computational costs, which can restrict agility. Elevation map updates are often slower than control loops and rely on heuristic filtering, which typically requires terrain-specific tuning.
- **Learning-Based Approaches (Reinforcement Learning - RL):**
 - **RL with Explicit Mapping:** Some RL methods incorporate classical state estimation and mapping pipelines, using elevation maps as policy inputs. These can improve robustness and generalization compared to purely model-based controllers but inherit the limitations of the underlying mapping systems, including computational burden and sensitivity to occlusions. Training often involves assuming fully observed egocentric maps or heavy randomization of noise, which can lead to performance degradation on highly agile tasks or precise foothold placement. More recently, learned neural network-based mapping has been explored, providing efficient real-time terrain reconstruction but often showing limited generalization to unseen terrains due to overfitting to training distributions.
 - **End-to-End Sensorimotor Policies:** Another line of work directly maps raw exteroceptive (e.g., camera, LiDAR) and proprioceptive sensor data to actions. These policies have demonstrated high agility on specific courses but typically exhibit limited generalization beyond their training environments and offer reduced interpretability, as terrain reasoning is implicitly learned. Recent "generalist" models attempt to distill multiple expert controllers into a single policy, showing improved performance but still with generalization limitations to unseen terrains without fine-tuning.

This research positions itself by aiming to bridge the gap between these two paradigms. It seeks to combine the interpretability and generalization potential of explicit mapping with the agility and robustness afforded by learning-based control, specifically addressing the trade-offs observed in prior RL-based methods concerning agility, generalization, mapping efficiency, and interpretability within a unified framework. It specifically builds upon prior work in attention-based map encoding (AME-1) but extends it to further improve generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this work are to develop an RL framework for legged locomotion that simultaneously achieves:

1. **Agility:** The ability to perform dynamic and complex maneuvers, such as traversing challenging parkour courses and climbing/descending obstacles. This is motivated by the limitations of classical methods that often struggle with high-speed, dynamic movements due to computational costs and reliance on precise models.
2. **Generalization to Diverse Terrains:** The capacity for a single trained policy to operate effectively on a wide range of previously unseen and unstructured terrains without requiring re-training or fine-tuning. Existing end-to-end sensorimotor policies often demonstrate limited generalization, and even RL methods relying on explicit maps can overfit to training environments.
3. **Interpretability:** Providing insights into how the control policy reasons about terrain, moving beyond black-box end-to-end models. This is addressed by explicitly encoding terrain information and using attention mechanisms.
4. **Real-time, Uncertainty-Aware Mapping:** Developing an efficient and robust mapping pipeline that can provide accurate terrain representations, explicitly model uncertainties (e.g., occlusions, sensor noise), and run in real-time on onboard hardware while being compatible with massively parallel simulation for training. This is motivated by the common issues in classical mapping (slow updates, heuristic filtering, sensitivity to noise) and learned maps (limited generalization, lack of uncertainty modeling).
5. **Sim-to-Real Transferability:** Ensuring that controllers trained in simulation can be effectively deployed on physical robots. This is a recurring challenge in robot learning, often mitigated by domain randomization and teacher-student schemes.
6. **Unified Framework for Different Robot Morphologies:** Demonstrating that the proposed framework can be applied to both quadrupedal and bipedal robots without significant changes to the core reward functions or training settings, suggesting broad applicability.

The motivation stems from the observed limitations in prior works that typically trade off these desirable characteristics. The authors aim to integrate perception and control tightly, maintain robustness to sensing inaccuracies, and enable whole-body control for precise interaction with complex environments, ultimately leading to more reliable legged robots in the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces AME-2, a unified reinforcement learning (RL) framework comprising an attention-based map encoder and a learning-based mapping pipeline.

#### 3.1. Overall RL Framework

The problem is formulated as a partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP), optimized using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in a massively parallel simulation environment (Isaac Gym). A teacher-student learning scheme is employed to facilitate sim-to-real transfer: a privileged teacher policy is trained with ground-truth elevation maps, and subsequently, a deployable student policy is trained with the proposed neural mapping under the teacher's supervision and additional RL.

#### 3.2. Policy Architecture and AME-2 Encoder

The policy operates at 50 Hz, outputting joint PD targets. The architecture consists of a proprioception encoder, an AME-2 map encoder, and an MLP decoder.
- **Proprioception Encoder:** Different designs are used for teacher and student. The teacher uses a plain MLP over ground-truth proprioceptive observations (base linear velocity, base angular velocity, projected gravity, joint positions/velocities, previous actions, goal commands). The student, to enhance robustness to real-world uncertainties, uses Long-Short I/O (LSIO) over stacked past observations (excluding base linear velocities and commands) to capture temporal dynamics, feeding this into an MLP.
- **AME-2 Encoder:** This module processes map observations.
 1. A Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) extracts local map features, followed by an MLP for positional embedding. These are combined by another MLP to form pointwise local features.
 2. An MLP followed by max pooling extracts global features, summarizing the overall terrain context.
 3. A query vector is formed by combining global features with the proprioceptive embedding via an MLP.
 4. A multi-head attention (MHA) module uses this query vector with the pointwise local features (serving as keys and values). This mechanism allows the policy to selectively focus on salient terrain regions based on the current robot state and global context, producing attention-weighted local features.
 5. The global features and weighted local features are concatenated to form the final map embedding, which is fed to the policy's action decoder.
This AME-2 design, distinct from AME-1, specifically incorporates global features to weigh local features, aiming for improved generalization across varying terrain types.

#### 3.3. Asymmetric Actor-Critic

Asymmetric actor-critic training is utilized. The actor uses the AME-2 architecture, while the critic employs a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) design for computational efficiency and strong function fitting during training. The critic receives additional privileged information, such as contact states of all links, and left-right symmetry augmentation is applied to the critic to improve sample efficiency without adding computational cost to the actor.

#### 3.4. Reward Function and Environments

The reward system is shared across quadruped and biped training:
- **Task Rewards:** Incentivize goal-reaching behaviors, including position tracking, heading tracking, moving towards the goal, and standing stably at the goal. These are time-masked and formulated to encourage complex locomotion rather than constrained paths.
- **Regularization Rewards:** Penalize undesirable events like spinning too fast, leaping on flat terrain, non-foot contacts, stumbling, slippage, and self-collision. Joint regularization (velocity, torque) and link acceleration penalties are also applied.
- **Simulation Fidelity Rewards:** Penalize joint states near limits (position, velocity, torque) that might lead to unrealistic simulation behavior or hardware damage.
Early termination conditions are defined for bad orientation (robot flipped), base collision, high thigh acceleration (based on biomechanical data for impact reduction), and stagnation.

#### 3.5. Terrains and Curriculum

Policies are trained on primitive terrains categorized as dense, climbing, and sparse (e.g., rough, stairs, boxes, obstacles, gaps, pallets, stones, beams). A curriculum scales difficulty, promoting environments based on success rate. A perception noise curriculum and an initial heading curriculum are also applied during teacher training. Test terrains are complex combinations of training and/or unseen elements to evaluate generalization.

#### 3.6. Domain Randomization

Extensive domain randomization is applied during training to enhance robustness and sim-to-real transfer:
- **Robot Dynamics:** Payload, friction coefficients, actuation delays are randomized. For bipeds, PD gains and motor armatures are also randomized.
- **Observation Noise:** Uniform noise is added to proprioceptive and map observations.
- **Mapping (Student Training):** Depth clouds are degraded (missing points, sensor artifacts). A subset of environments has access to complete maps, while others rely on partial online maps, enabling map reuse. Student maps are also corrupted with random height values and high uncertainties. Mapping drifts are simulated for both teacher and student.

#### 3.7. Neural Mapping Pipeline

A lightweight, uncertainty-aware elevation mapping pipeline is developed, running identically in simulation and on real robots.
1. **Depth Projection:** Raw depth images are projected onto local 2D height grids. The maximum z-value is kept for cells with multiple points; empty cells get a minimum value.
2. **Neural Network Prediction:** A lightweight CNN, trained with Bayesian learning (specifically, the $\beta$-NLL loss with $\beta=0.5$), predicts local elevations and their uncertainties (log-variance). This captures measurement noise and occlusions. Training data synthesis involves raytracing from diverse terrain meshes (locomotion training terrains, stacked boxes, heightfields, floating boxes) with extensive augmentations (additive noise, cropping, simulated occlusions, random ranges, missing points/outliers). A total variation (TV) based re-weighting scheme is used during batch optimization to emphasize challenging terrains. The CNN architecture is a shallow U-Net with a gated residual design, combining raw estimation with input for improved accuracy.
3. **Probabilistic Winner-Take-All Fusion:** Local predictions are fused into a global grid map using odometry poses. Instead of standard Bayesian fusion, a Probabilistic Winner-Take-All strategy is used. It calculates an effective measurement variance, lower-bounded by the prior, and determines the probability of overwriting map cells based on relative precision. This prevents overconfidence in consistently predicted but occluded areas and allows rapid updates for high-confidence measurements.
4. **Policy Input:** Egocentric elevations and associated uncertainties are queried from the global map as controller inputs.
5. **Odometry:** CompSLAM with Graph-MSF (quadruped) and DLIO (biped) provide high-frequency, accurate LiDAR-inertial odometry. Linear velocity observations are removed from the student policy inputs due to observed noise and delay.

#### 3.8. Training and Deployment

- **Training Setup:** Controllers are trained in Isaac Gym using RSL-RL. ANYmal-D policies require ~60 RTX-4090-days, and TRON1 policies ~30 RTX-4090-days. Mapping models are trained separately on 54 million frames per robot, converging in less than 1 hour.
- **Deployment Setup:** Controllers are deployed using ONNX Runtime, with policy inference times of ~2 ms on onboard CPUs. The mapping pipeline takes ~5 ms per frame on the onboard CPU (~2.5 ms for model inference).
- **Sim-to-Real Designs:** Actuator dynamics (actuator network for ANYmal-D, identified DC-motor model for TRON1) are modeled. Applied torques are clipped. For continuous deployment, observed goal distances are clipped to 2m, remaining episode time is removed from actor input, and observed yaw commands are randomized if the goal is beyond 2m during training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research reports several findings, supported by both simulation and real-world experiments on ANYmal-D (quadruped) and LimX TRON1 (biped) robots.

#### 4.1. Agility and Generalization

- **State-of-the-Art Agility:** AME-2 enabled both robots to achieve high agility. ANYmal-D successfully traversed challenging parkour courses and rubble pile terrains previously reported as difficult, doing so in a zero-shot manner (unseen during training). TRON1 climbed platforms up to 0.48 m and descended up to 0.88 m, exhibiting capabilities comparable to or exceeding prior work on robots with higher torque and base height. Both robots reached peak forward velocities above 1.5 m/s.
- **Strong Generalization:** The controllers demonstrated robust generalization to a wide range of unseen terrains, including complex combinations of sparse, dense, and climbing elements in the real world.

#### 4.2. Real-World Demonstrations

- **Quadruped Parkour:** ANYmal-D successfully navigated a complex parkour course (climbing, jumping) at speeds up to 2 m/s, despite neither the controller nor the mapping model being trained on this specific course.
- **Sparse Terrain Locomotion:** Both ANYmal-D and TRON1 traversed various sparse terrains (balance beams, unfixed blocks, gaps, stepping stones) effectively, many of which were unseen during training.
- **Biped Maneuvering:** TRON1 demonstrated omnidirectional perceptive locomotion, smoothly traversing a 38-cm platform, a gap, a staircase, and rough terrain.
- **Robustness to Moving Terrains:** TRON1 balanced and traversed an unlocked, tiltable platform cart. ANYmal-D recovered from unfixed tilted stepping stones by utilizing knee contacts, showcasing robustness to unexpected terrain shifts.
- **Mapping Quality:** The learned mapping pipeline produced high-quality elevation maps in real-time on onboard hardware, accurately capturing fine-grained terrain details like gaps and supports, even for unseen terrains. It explicitly modeled occluded regions with high uncertainty, and integrated newly observed geometry based on predicted uncertainty.

#### 4.3. Emergent Behaviors

- **Active Perception:** The controllers exhibited active perception, such as ANYmal-D making initial contact with a high obstacle to expose its higher parts to the map, then using this updated information for a successful subsequent climb. This demonstrates the reuse of learned spatial information over time, addressing a limitation of some prior recurrent approaches.
- **Loco-Navigation:** Controllers showed local navigation capabilities, aligning the robot with terrain features (e.g., turning to align with a beam) and avoiding obstacles to reach goals.
- **Whole-Body Contact:** ANYmal-D actively used knee contacts to stabilize and traverse challenging terrains, illustrating emergent whole-body control strategies.
- **Impact Reduction:** Through the use of thigh-acceleration thresholds in early termination, the robots learned behaviors to reduce impact during descent, such as ANYmal-D gently touching down with its knees or TRON1 retracting a support leg during landing.

#### 4.4. Interpretable Feature Patterns

Visualization of the AME-2 encoder's feature patterns showed that local attention weights emphasized fine-grained terrain details relevant for foot placement, while global features focused on sparse, distinctive points characterizing the terrain type (e.g., obstacle boundaries, platform surfaces, beam centers). These patterns persisted on test terrains, suggesting a mechanism for generalization.

#### 4.5. Ablations and Benchmarking

- **Teacher Architecture:**
 - AME-2 teacher achieved 95.2% average success rate on test terrains, outperforming AME-1 (51.2%) and MoE (45.0%). AME-1 struggled on mixed terrains, and MoE showed limited generalization. This highlights the importance of the AME-2 encoder's global context integration.
- **Student Designs:**
 - The proposed AME-2 student achieved 82.4% average success rate on test terrains.
 - A visual recurrent student from prior work achieved 51.5% and struggled on sparse/mixed terrains, although it performed slightly better on pure parkour with obstacles.
 - An AME-2 student trained without RL losses (only distillation) achieved 60.7% on test terrains, indicating RL is crucial for generalization.
 - An AME-2 student trained without the representation loss achieved 73.6%, demonstrating its contribution to generalization.
- **Mapping Designs:**
 - The proposed mapping pipeline (trained on diverse meshes) achieved an average L0.5 loss of 0.046 on test terrains.
 - Training only on locomotion terrains resulted in a higher average loss of 0.088, suggesting the necessity of diverse training data for the mapping model.
 - A temporal recurrent model baseline, despite extensive training, produced an average L0.5 loss of 0.085 and generated lower-quality maps with less meaningful uncertainty in practice, especially on unseen terrains.
- **Robustness to Visual Noise:**
 - The student policy maintained nominal or slightly improved performance under 20% missing depth points and 3% depth artifacts (due to more conservative behavior in higher uncertainty).
 - Disabling one front camera significantly impacted performance on terrains requiring active perception (e.g., climbing high obstacles), reducing the average test success rate from 82.4% to 56.7%, highlighting the reliance on sensor viewpoints for specific skills.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of legged robotics by providing a framework that addresses several persistent challenges, offering notable significance and potential impact:

1. **Unified Solution for Agility and Generalization:** AME-2 demonstrates a combination of high agility and robust generalization on diverse, unseen terrains. This represents a step towards robots that can operate reliably in complex, real-world environments without extensive prior knowledge or per-terrain tuning, which is a key barrier to widespread adoption of legged robots.
2. **Interpretable and Terrain-Aware Control:** By explicitly incorporating an attention-based map encoder (AME-2), the system provides a more interpretable mechanism for how the robot perceives and reacts to terrain features. This is an improvement over monolithic end-to-end policies where terrain reasoning is opaque, facilitating debugging and potentially enabling safer robot deployment. The learned feature patterns (local attention for fine details, global context for terrain type) offer insights into the policy's decision-making.
3. **Robust and Efficient Real-time Mapping:** The learning-based elevation mapping pipeline is lightweight, runs in real-time on onboard hardware, and explicitly models uncertainty. Its compatibility with massively parallel simulation and robustness to sensor noise and occlusions significantly aids sim-to-real transfer. This reduces reliance on traditional, often fragile, mapping stacks and provides a foundation for more robust perceptive control.
4. **Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer Methodology:** The combination of an asymmetric actor-critic, a comprehensive reward structure, extensive domain randomization, and a teacher-student learning scheme with explicit representation alignment proves effective in bridging the simulation-to-reality gap, yielding deployable controllers.
5. **Applicability Across Robot Morphologies:** The framework's successful application to both quadrupedal (ANYmal-D) and bipedal (TRON1) robots using the same rewards and training settings indicates its potential generality across different legged robot designs, reducing the need for platform-specific control development.
6. **Emergent Complex Behaviors:** The observed emergent behaviors, such as active perception, local navigation, whole-body contact, and impact reduction, indicate that the reward formulation and learning framework can guide robots to discover sophisticated and adaptive locomotion strategies. This can lead to more resilient and capable autonomous systems.

The potential impact of this work is in advancing the practical deployment of legged robots in unstructured environments, including search and rescue, inspection, and logistics. By addressing the critical balance between agility and generalization, AME-2 contributes to making legged robots more autonomous, versatile, and reliable outside controlled laboratory settings. Future work could build upon this foundation to incorporate full 3D perception, handle more severely degraded sensing, or integrate higher-DoF whole-body skills.
