# When Less Language is More: Language-Reasoning Disentanglement Makes LLMs Better Multilingual Reasoners

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Harbin Institute of Technology and collaborators developed a training-free method to improve multilingual reasoning in large language models by disentangling language-specific information from internal reasoning processes. This approach consistently enhanced reasoning performance across 10 diverse LLMs and 11 languages, showing over 10% accuracy gains for some low-resource languages and matching or surpassing more computationally intensive post-training methods.

## Problem

- A significant 'multilingual reasoning gap' exists in LLMs, where performance in low- and mid-resource languages lags far behind high-resource languages.
- Current methods to improve multilingual reasoning (e.g., SFT, RL) are computationally expensive and require vast amounts of high-quality multilingual data, which is scarce for low-resource languages.
- The internal mechanisms of how language and reasoning are encoded and interact within LLMs across different languages are not well understood.

## Method

- Proposes a training-free ==causal intervention== within LLM latent spaces to disentangle language and reasoning representations during inference.
- Identifies language-specific subspaces within the model's hidden states using Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) of mean language representations.
- Ablates or 'projects out' components aligned with these identified language-specific subspaces from hidden representations, with a focus on mid-layers for optimal balance.

## Results

- Consistent improvements in multilingual reasoning performance were observed across 10 LLMs and 11 typologically diverse languages on MGSM, XWinograd, and M-MMLU benchmarks.
- Low-resource languages, such as Swahili, demonstrated significant accuracy gains, sometimes doubling original performance and showing over **10%** improvement.
- The training-free disentanglement method achieved performance comparable to, or superior to, computationally intensive post-training methods like SFT and PPO on mathematical reasoning tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Language and reasoning are separable components within LLMs' latent spaces, and removing language-specific information can effectively improve cross-lingual reasoning.
> - Excessive linguistic information within LLM representations negatively correlates with and can disrupt reasoning processes.
> - English often functions as a dominant, language-invariant anchor in the latent space of multilingual LLMs, likely due to its disproportionate presence in pretraining data.
