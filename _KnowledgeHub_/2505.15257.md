# When Less Language is More: Language-Reasoning Disentanglement Makes LLMs Better Multilingual Reasoners

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Harbin Institute of Technology and collaborators developed a training-free method to improve multilingual reasoning in large language models by disentangling language-specific information from internal reasoning processes. This approach consistently enhanced reasoning performance across 10 diverse LLMs and 11 languages, showing over 10% accuracy gains for some low-resource languages and matching or surpassing more computationally intensive post-training methods.

## Problem

- A significant 'multilingual reasoning gap' exists in LLMs, where performance in low- and mid-resource languages lags far behind high-resource languages.
- Current methods to improve multilingual reasoning (e.g., SFT, RL) are computationally expensive and require vast amounts of high-quality multilingual data, which is scarce for low-resource languages.
- The internal mechanisms of how language and reasoning are encoded and interact within LLMs across different languages are not well understood.

## Method

- Proposes a training-free ==causal intervention== within LLM latent spaces to disentangle language and reasoning representations during inference.
- Identifies language-specific subspaces within the model's hidden states using Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) of mean language representations.
- Ablates or 'projects out' components aligned with these identified language-specific subspaces from hidden representations, with a focus on mid-layers for optimal balance.

## Results

- Consistent improvements in multilingual reasoning performance were observed across 10 LLMs and 11 typologically diverse languages on MGSM, XWinograd, and M-MMLU benchmarks.
- Low-resource languages, such as Swahili, demonstrated significant accuracy gains, sometimes doubling original performance and showing over **10%** improvement.
- The training-free disentanglement method achieved performance comparable to, or superior to, computationally intensive post-training methods like SFT and PPO on mathematical reasoning tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Language and reasoning are separable components within LLMs' latent spaces, and removing language-specific information can effectively improve cross-lingual reasoning.
> - Excessive linguistic information within LLM representations negatively correlates with and can disrupt reasoning processes.
> - English often functions as a dominant, language-invariant anchor in the latent space of multilingual LLMs, likely due to its disproportionate presence in pretraining data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in large language models (LLMs) have been primarily driven by progress in high-resource languages, predominantly English and Chinese. While these models excel in reasoning tasks within these dominant languages, a significant disparity persists in their performance across low- and mid-resource languages. This "multilingual reasoning gap" is a critical concern, hindering global AI applicability and exacerbating linguistic inequities.

Prior research addressing this gap has largely focused on two main categories:
1. **Training-time strategies**: These include constructing high-quality multilingual reasoning datasets and leveraging supervision signals (often from English) to enhance reasoning in low-resource settings, typically through supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or reinforcement learning (RL).
2. **Test-time scaling approaches**: These involve adapting models at inference time without modifying their weights, often through prompt engineering or decoding strategies.

This paper positions itself innovatively by shifting the focus from external interventions (like data curation or fine-tuning) to an **internal, mechanistic interpretation** of multilingual LLMs. It delves into the latent spaces of these models to understand how language and reasoning representations are encoded and interact. This approach aligns with the growing field of **representation engineering** and **mechanistic interpretability** in LLMs, which seeks to understand and control model behavior by manipulating their internal representations. The paper explicitly draws inspiration from cognitive neuroscience, which suggests that human reasoning can function independently of language processing. This unique interdisciplinary perspective distinguishes it from purely computational approaches.

By proposing a "training-free language-reasoning disentanglement" method, the work offers a lightweight and interpretable alternative or complement to existing computationally intensive post-training methods. It seeks to prove that by intervening on internal representations, LLMs can be made better multilingual reasoners without the need for extensive retraining or new data, which is a significant practical advantage, especially for under-resourced languages.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **enhance the multilingual reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models by disentangling language-specific information from reasoning processes within their internal representations.**

The authors are motivated by several key observations and hypotheses:
1. **Multilingual Reasoning Gap:** Despite remarkable progress in LLM reasoning, performance remains disproportionately concentrated in high-resource languages (e.g., English, Chinese), leaving low- and mid-resource languages underserved. This creates significant barriers to global LLM applicability and perpetuates linguistic inequities.
2. **Cognitive Neuroscience Inspiration:** The human brain's language network is largely inactive during reasoning tasks, and language itself is primarily optimized for communication, not reasoning. This suggests a potential functional separability between language and reasoning in human cognition.
3. **Core Hypothesis:** The authors hypothesize that LLMs similarly encode reasoning and language as separable components in their latent spaces. If this is true, then disentangling these components could allow reasoning abilities, once acquired in high-resource languages, to transfer more effectively across diverse languages.
4. **Need for Lightweight Solutions:** Current methods to improve multilingual reasoning, such as supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL), are often computationally expensive and require vast amounts of high-quality multilingual data, which is scarce for low-resource languages. The researchers aim to develop a training-free, computationally efficient, and interpretable strategy.

In essence, the paper seeks to provide empirical evidence for the separability of language and reasoning within LLMs and leverage this understanding to develop a practical intervention that improves cross-lingual generalization in reasoning tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper adopts a **causal intervention** approach within the latent spaces of LLMs to test its central hypothesis: that language and reasoning are separable and disentangling them can improve multilingual reasoning. The methodology involves three main steps:

#### 3.1. Language-Specific Subspace Identification

The first step is to identify the dimensions within the LLM's hidden state space that primarily encode language-specific information.
- For each language *l* and at every layer, a mean representation *m_l* is computed from the final token embeddings of *n* samples.
- These mean vectors *m_l* for all *L* languages are concatenated to form a mean embedding matrix *M*.
- Building on prior work, *M* is then decomposed into two orthogonal components: a language-agnostic subspace (*M_a*) and a language-specific subspace (*M_s*). This decomposition is achieved efficiently using **Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)**. The dimensionality of *M_s* (controlled by hyperparameter *r*) allows for capturing rich language-dependent features, while *M_a* (1-dimensional) captures shared semantics.

#### 3.2. Activation Ablation for Language-Reasoning Disentanglement

Once the language-specific subspace *M_s* is identified, a projection-based intervention is applied during inference.
- For any hidden representation *h* derived from a multilingual input, its components aligned with *M_s* are "projected out" or ablated.
- The modified hidden representation *ĥ* is calculated as *h - λ M_s M_s^T h*, where *λ* is a coefficient controlling the ablation strength. This process aims to remove language-specific variations, leaving a representation *ĥ* that better reflects language-agnostic reasoning.
- By default, this projection is applied throughout all model layers, focusing on the final input token representation. A strategy of disentangling from mid-layers and re-injecting at higher layers is also explored to balance reasoning gains and linguistic coherence.

#### 3.3. Verifying the Linguistic Nature of Removed Components

To validate that the removed components indeed correspond to language-specific signals, two empirical methods are used:
- **Representation Space Visualization:** Using Principal Component Analysis (PCA), the hidden representations of the final token across different languages are visualized before and after ablation. The observation that non-English languages converge toward the English cluster post-ablation indicates increased language invariance, with English often acting as a dominant "anchor."
- **Language Fidelity Evaluation:** The GlotLID tool (a multilingual language identifier) is used to quantify the consistency between the input and output languages (language fidelity). A substantial drop in language fidelity, with models increasingly defaulting to English responses, confirms that the removed components encoded language-specific signals.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Models:** 10 diverse open-weight LLMs are used, including instruction-tuned models (Qwen-2.5-Instruct, Qwen-3-Instruct series) and reasoning-oriented models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill, GLM-Z1-9B, QwQ-32B).
- **Languages:** 11 typologically diverse languages spanning high-resource (English, Spanish, French, German, Chinese, Japanese, Russian), medium-resource (Thai, Telugu), and low-resource (Bengali, Swahili) categories.
- **Benchmarks:** Three major multilingual reasoning benchmarks:
 - **MGSM (Multilingual Grade School Math Benchmark):** Mathematical reasoning.
 - **XWinograd:** Commonsense reasoning.
 - **M-MMLU (Multilingual Massive Multitask Language Understanding):** Knowledge-intensive question answering.
- **Implementation:** The interventions are implemented using the vLLM framework for efficient inference. Layer-wise analyses are performed, and hyperparameters for ablation strength (*λ*) and layer ranges are carefully tuned.
- **Comparison with Post-training:** The training-free intervention is compared against supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) on the Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct model using translated MATH datasets, to assess its practical utility against more computationally intensive approaches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several compelling findings that strongly support the central hypothesis and highlight the efficacy of language-reasoning disentanglement.

1. **Consistent Improvement in Multilingual Reasoning Performance:** The language-reasoning disentanglement consistently boosts multilingual reasoning performance across all 10 tested LLMs and all 11 typologically diverse languages. This improvement is observed on all three benchmarks (MGSM, XWinograd, M-MMLU), including both instruction-tuned and reasoning-oriented models.
 - The gains are even more pronounced in XWinograd and M-MMLU, which require subtle context integration and factual grounding, suggesting that linguistic interference is particularly detrimental in these tasks.
 - Critically, the benefits extend to medium- and low-resource languages, with some (e.g., Swahili) showing over 10% accuracy gains, occasionally doubling original performance. This indicates a balanced and equitable improvement across the linguistic spectrum.

2. **Negative Correlation Between Language-Specific Activation and Reasoning Accuracy:** Quantitative analysis of ablation strength reveals a consistent negative correlation: as more language-specific components are removed (increased ablation strength), MGSM reasoning accuracy improves. Conversely, amplifying language-specific components degrades reasoning performance. This empirically validates that excessive linguistic information can disrupt reasoning processes in LLMs.

3. **Layer-wise Disentanglement and Trade-offs:**
 - Layer-wise analysis demonstrates that disentanglement is effective across most layers, particularly from lower to middle depths, consistently improving reasoning accuracy while maintaining stable output fidelity.
 - However, interventions at upper layers lead to a sharp drop in both reasoning and response fidelity, indicating that while core reasoning logic is language-invariant, upper layers still encode crucial information for language fluency and generation consistency. Middle layers offer the best trade-off between reasoning gains and linguistic coherence.

4. **English as the Language-Invariant Anchor:** PCA visualizations confirm that after removing language-specific components, representations of non-English languages tend to converge toward the English cluster, even in models highly exposed to Chinese (like Qwen). This suggests that English often functions as a dominant, language-invariant anchor in the latent space of multilingual LLMs, likely due to its disproportionate presence in pretraining data.

5. **Training-Free Approach Rivals or Surpasses Post-training Methods:**
 - The training-free language-reasoning disentanglement approach achieves performance comparable to, or even superior to, computationally intensive multilingual post-training methods like supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) on mathematical reasoning tasks.
 - Specifically, it surpasses SFT, which often yields limited or negative gains due to challenges with automatically translated data quality and small models mimicking large teacher models.
 - It achieves results on par with PPO-based RL, highlighting its efficiency and effectiveness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research carries significant implications for the development, understanding, and application of multilingual LLMs:

1. **Mechanistic Understanding of LLMs:** The work provides crucial insights into the internal mechanisms underlying multilingual reasoning in LLMs. By demonstrating the separability of language and reasoning components within latent spaces, it contributes to the growing field of LLM interpretability and representation engineering. This deeper understanding can guide future model design and optimization.

2. **Lightweight and Interpretable Improvement Strategy:** The proposed training-free, causal intervention offers a highly practical and scalable method for boosting multilingual reasoning. Unlike costly post-training methods, it requires no additional data or retraining, making it accessible even for resource-constrained environments and languages. Its interpretability, stemming from directly manipulating internal representations, also allows for a clearer understanding of how improvements are achieved.

3. **Addressing Linguistic Inequity:** By significantly improving reasoning performance in low- and mid-resource languages, the work directly contributes to democratizing access to advanced AI capabilities. This is vital for ensuring that LLM benefits are not confined to high-resource linguistic communities, fostering more equitable AI deployment globally.

4. **Promising Future Research Directions:**
 - **Integration with Latent Reasoning:** The findings resonate with emerging paradigms of latent reasoning (e.g., "continuous thoughts"), suggesting that reasoning can be more effectively modeled and enhanced by focusing on language-agnostic latent representations. Future work could explore integrating disentanglement techniques with such frameworks.
 - **Training-Time Integration:** While currently a post-hoc intervention, the principles could be incorporated into LLM training objectives to encourage language-invariant intermediate representations, potentially leading to even more robust and generalized multilingual models.
 - **Broader Applicability:** The principle of disentangling task-relevant signals from other features might extend beyond reasoning to other complex cognitive abilities in LLMs, such as planning or reflection.
 - **Principled Model Diagnosis:** Analyzing language-specific activation patterns can serve as a diagnostic tool, moving beyond surface-level accuracy to reveal *when* and *where* models fail in cross-lingual generalization.

5. **Challenges and Limitations:** The paper acknowledges limitations, such as the observed trade-off between reasoning accuracy and linguistic fidelity (especially for low-resource languages when ablation is too aggressive) and the "English as anchor" phenomenon, which highlights biases from pretraining data. Future work needs to explore adaptive ablation strategies and delve deeper into how anchor languages emerge.

In conclusion, this research offers a novel, effective, and efficient approach to improving multilingual reasoning in LLMs by demonstrating and leveraging the disentanglement of language and reasoning. Its contributions push the boundaries of LLM interpretability while providing a tangible, equitable benefit for global AI accessibility.
