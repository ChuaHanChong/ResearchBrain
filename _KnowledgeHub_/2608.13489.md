# DreamX-Phi 1.0: Action-Conditioned Video World Model for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> DreamX-Phi 1.0 is an action-conditioned video world model for bimanual manipulation, built on Wan2.2-TI2V-5B, predicting future observations from an initial frame, instruction, and end-effector trajectory. It injects per-arm SE(3) motion via PRoPE-style attention, adds depth and object-centric supervision, and distills to a few-step student. It ranked first on WorldArena 2.0 Track 1 and tied second on Track 2.

## Problem

- Video generators produce photorealistic rollouts but aren't guaranteed faithful to conditioning actions - a convincing video can still move the wrong arm or confuse grasping with release.
- Existing action-conditioned world models encode actions as compact tokens, not explicitly preserving the rigid-body SE(3) geometry of end-effector motion or where it appears in the image.
- A uniform RGB loss averages over all tokens, so small regions occupied by the arm and grasped object, where contact errors matter most, are dominated by background.

## Method

- Builds on **Wan2.2-TI2V-5B** under a ==flow-matching== objective, conditioning on an initial frame, bimanual end-effector trajectory and gripper states, and a language instruction.
- Introduces ==Arm-Grouped PRoPE==: normalizes each arm's SE(3) trajectory into a shared frame and injects it via per-arm attention groups, coupling tokens through relative rigid motion.
- Adds a lightweight depth branch supervised against **Depth Anything 3** targets, and SAM3-mask-weighted, **V-JEPA**-regularized ==object-centric supervision== to keep manipulated-object identity coherent through contact.

## Results

- On WorldArena 2.0 Track 1 (**31** entries), **DreamX-Phi** ranked first with EWMScore-P of **60.65**, ahead of Alpha-World (**60.13**) and FlowWAM-FiveAges (**59.72**).
- On WorldArena 2.0 Track 2, training a pi_0.5 policy inside the model, DreamX-Phi achieved a **67.19%** success rate, tying for second place.
- On offline WorldArena 1.0 Track 1 (Clean-50 protocol), DreamX-Phi reached EWMScore-P of **76.88**, **3.24** points above the leading pinned entry, UNIS (**73.64**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Preserving rigid-body SE(3) structure via geometry-aware attention (PRoPE) is more faithful to per-arm commanded trajectories than compressing actions into generic tokens.
> - Action conditioning alone is insufficient for physically coherent manipulation video - it needs auxiliary supervision targeting scene geometry and object-state evolution around contact.
> - The current model is a Forward Dynamics Model consuming externally given actions; the authors flag joint video-action generation as the natural next step.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of action-conditioned video world models, particularly for robotic manipulation tasks. The broader landscape of world models aims to provide scalable methods for evaluating candidate actions by predicting future scene evolution based on current observations and proposed actions. This capability is instrumental for planning over imagined outcomes in various domains.

Modern video generators, such as Wan and Cosmos 3, offer priors for appearance and motion. However, a prevailing challenge in this area is ensuring that photorealistic predictions are also *faithful* to the conditioning actions. Existing action-conditioned video world models frequently encode actions using compact tokens or feature-wise modulation (e.g., Wu et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2025). While these approaches offer flexibility, they often implicitly represent the rigid-body geometry of end-effector motion and do not explicitly indicate where the commanded motion should manifest in the generated video. This can lead to visually convincing but physically inaccurate rollouts, such as moving the incorrect arm, failing to interact with the target object, or confusing grasping with releasing.

Other lines of research address this by converting robot motion into spatially aligned conditions, such as rendering kinematic skeletons (OSCAR), robot geometry (Robot-Factored World Models), or using optical flow (FlowWAM) (Wu and Gao, 2026; Kim et al., 2026a; Chen et al., 2026). These methods localize motion in the image but may not directly preserve the continuous rigid-body trajectory of each arm. Furthermore, ensuring fidelity requires not only correct robot motion but also preservation of global scene geometry and the state of manipulated objects throughout an interaction. Prior work has explored geometry-aware attention (e.g., GTA, Miyato et al., 2024; PRoPE, Li et al., 2025a), depth supervision (Lipman et al., 2023), object masks, and predictive video features for object evolution (Guo et al., 2026; Carion et al., 2025; Assran et al., 2025).

DreamX-Phi 1.0 aims to advance this field by integrating a geometry-aware action representation with comprehensive physical-consistency supervision. It attempts to bridge the gap between photorealistic generation and action fidelity, particularly in bimanual robotic manipulation, building upon a large video diffusion transformer (Wan2.2-TI2V-5B). The work distinguishes itself by directly encoding SE(3) trajectories and image-space motion cues while also regularizing scene geometry and object-centric temporal consistency. For practical deployment, it incorporates distribution-matching distillation (DMD) to enable efficient, few-step inference.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an action-conditioned video world model for robotic manipulation that generates future observations which are not only photorealistic but also faithful to prescribed actions. This involves accurately predicting scene evolution from an initial observation, a language instruction, and a specified sequence of end-effector poses and gripper states.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges inherent in existing world models for robotics:

1. **Fidelity to Prescribed Actions:** While modern video generators can produce visually compelling predictions, these do not inherently guarantee that the simulated robot actions or object interactions precisely match the commanded inputs. A convincing video might depict a robot moving, but the motion could deviate from the commanded trajectory, miss the target object, or misrepresent a grasping action. The model should exhibit sensitivity, meaning distinct prescribed trajectories should induce correspondingly distinct and accurate robot motions and object interactions, while action-irrelevant scene content remains stable.
2. **Preserving Spatial Structure of Robot Motion:** Current action encoding methods, such as compact tokens or feature-wise modulation, often fail to explicitly preserve the rigid-body geometry of end-effector motion or localize its effects within the image. This implicit representation can hinder precise control and accurate prediction of interactions. The objective is to design an interface that explicitly encodes SE(3) trajectories and relates them to image dynamics.
3. **Maintaining Scene Geometry and Object State:** Even with correct robot motion, a faithful rollout requires preserving overall scene geometry and the consistent state of manipulated objects as an interaction unfolds. Without this, a model might produce scenarios where the gripper penetrates an object, the object does not respond realistically to contact, or its visual identity and state change abruptly. The motivation here is to provide explicit supervision to ensure physical coherence.
4. **Efficiency for Deployment:** Generating long video sequences through iterative denoising steps in diffusion models can be computationally intensive, limiting practical deployment. An objective is to optimize the inference process for efficiency.

To address these motivations, DreamX-Phi 1.0 aims to introduce:
- A geometry-aware action representation that combines structured SE(3) trajectories with image-space motion cues to encode both the commanded 3D end-effector motion and its visual consequences.
- Manipulation-aware supervision, focusing learning on scene geometry and object state through auxiliary depth and object-centric temporal consistency objectives.
- A distilled few-step generator for efficient deployment, derived from the multi-step teacher through distribution-matching distillation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DreamX-Phi 1.0 is an action-conditioned video world model designed to predict future RGB observations. The model is built upon a Wan2.2-TI2V-5B video diffusion transformer. Given an observed RGB frame (x₀), a language instruction (c), and a bimanual action trajectory (a₁:T) comprising end-effector poses and gripper states, the model aims to learn the conditional distribution pθ(x₁:T | x₀, a₁:T, c). The framework integrates structured action conditioning, auxiliary geometric and object-centric supervision, and post-training distillation for efficiency.

The methodology is organized into three main components:

#### 3.1. Action-Conditioned Video Prediction

- **PRoPE Control for Bimanual Actions:** The model utilizes an adapted version of PRoPE (Projective Relative Positional Encoding) to explicitly preserve the SE(3) structure of end-effector motion. This mechanism injects known relative rigid transformations directly into the self-attention mechanism, influencing both attention weights and value aggregation, while maintaining invariance to the global coordinate frame.
 - **Action Representation:** For each arm `k` at frame `t`, its position (pᵏₜ), quaternion (qᵏₜ, converted to Rᵏₜ), and gripper value (gᵏₜ) are used. The end-effector frame (Gᵏₜ) is constructed and then expressed relative to the initial pose of arm 1 ((G¹₁)⁻¹Gᵏₜ), placing all arms in a common reference frame. Translations are normalized by a motion-amplitude factor. The inverted normalized frames (Aᵏₜ) and gripper states (gᵏₜ) are temporally aligned with the VAE latent frames. Missing arms are represented by identity poses and zero gripper values.
 - **Geometric Attention:** Each transformer block incorporates a parallel attention branch with dedicated query, key, value, and output projections, conditioned on `A` and `g`. Attention heads are grouped, with one group per arm. Token-wise transforms are applied using the inverted normalized frames (Dᵢ = Idₕ/₄ ⊗ Aᵏn(ᵢ)), ensuring that token pairs are coupled through relative motion DᵢDⱼ⁻¹ rather than an absolute coordinate frame.
 - **Gripper and Residual:** Gripper opening, a scalar value, is injected as a per-arm bias (bᵏₜ = Wg gᵏₜ + b_g) on the corresponding attention heads after the inverse geometric map. The resulting geometrically-conditioned attention output is added as a residual to the pretrained self-attention output, with the gripper adapter and output projection initialized to zero.

#### 3.2. Auxiliary Depth Supervision for 3D Consistency

- To provide explicit geometric constraints beyond RGB appearance, a lightweight auxiliary depth branch is integrated. This design draws inspiration from X-WAM (Guo et al., 2026).
 - **Architecture:** The final `M` blocks (M < N) of the RGB transformer are replicated to form the auxiliary branch, with the initial `N-M` blocks serving as a shared trunk. The trunk's output initializes both pathways. Each replicated depth block is initialized from its pretrained RGB counterpart.
 - **Cross-Attention:** Cross-attention allows the depth pathway to consume RGB features from the corresponding layer of the RGB branch (hⱼᵈ = DepthBlockⱼ(hⱼ₋₁ᵈ; Kⱼʳᵍᵇ, Vⱼʳᵍᵇ)). The connection is unidirectional, preserving the RGB branch's computation path.
 - **Supervision:** A dedicated output head maps the final depth tokens to predicted depth latents (̂z_d). This prediction is supervised with a latent-space mean-squared error (L_depth = 1/|z_d| ||̂z_d - z_d||₂²), where z_d is obtained by encoding ground-truth depth maps `d` using the frozen video VAE. This objective encourages the shared representation to encode stronger geometric structure.

#### 3.3. Object-Centric Physical Consistency

- To ensure physically plausible interactions, particularly around the manipulated object, two complementary supervision signals are employed:
 - **Object-Aware Supervision:** SAM3-derived binary masks of the manipulated object are used to reweight the RGB objective during training. Tokens within the object mask (mᵢ=1) are assigned a higher weight (λ_m > 1) compared to background tokens, thus prioritizing accuracy in contact-local regions. The weights are normalized to stabilize the overall loss scale.
 - **V-JEPA Alignment for Physical Consistency:** A frozen V-JEPA teacher (Assran et al., 2025) is used to constrain the spatiotemporal evolution of object features. Instead of direct feature matching, the Gram matrices of the student and teacher features (S_bS_bᵀ and Q_bQ_bᵀ) are aligned (ℓ_JEPA^(b) = 1/M_b² ||S_bS_bᵀ - Q_bQ_bᵀ||₁). This relational objective discourages drift in object identity, shape, and state across the interaction. This supervision is applied conditionally, only when the mask provides a sufficient number of tokens and the flow-matching noise is within a stable range.

#### 3.4. Few-Step Post-Training (DMD)

- For efficient deployment, the multi-step generator is distilled into a few-step student using Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD2) (Yin et al., 2024a).
 - **Objective:** The student (`Gη`) is optimized to match the conditional marginals of the real data distribution using a KL objective (L_DMD(η) = E_y∼pdata(y), τ [D_KL(q_η,τ (· | y) || p_data,τ (· | y))]).
 - **Adversarial Training:** This is complemented by a noised non-saturating GAN objective, where a discriminator `D` attempts to distinguish between real and student-generated latents at various noise levels. The student's generator objective (L_G_adv) aims to fool the discriminator.
 - **Overall Optimization:** The student is optimized with L_DMD_student = L_DMD + λ_advL_G_adv. The few-step student uses a fixed `N`-step denoising schedule.

#### 3.5. Data Curation

A heterogeneous training corpus is curated from three sources: action-free egocentric video (Ego4D), real-robot demonstrations (AgiBot World 2026, InternData-A1, Cosmos3-DROID, RoboCOIN), and simulated robot trajectories (RoboTwin 2.0). Data is filtered to remove irrelevant segments (e.g., mobile-base motion, stationary segments) and normalized into a common LeRobot v2.1 representation. RoboTwin videos are super-resolved using DreamX-Refiner. This corpus supports both action-agnostic pretraining for broad visual priors and action-conditioned fine-tuning for grounded predictions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DreamX-Phi 1.0 was conducted on the WorldArena 1.0 and 2.0 benchmarks, assessing both video prediction fidelity and utility for policy training.

#### 4.1. WorldArena 2.0 Results (Snapshot: August 12, 2026)

- **Track 1 (Video Prediction):**
 - DreamX-Phi-1.0-FDM-0730 achieved first place among 31 entries on the WorldArena 2.0 Track 1 leaderboard.
 - It obtained an EWMScore-P of 60.65, which is a composite score averaging 15 component metrics covering visual quality, temporal dynamics, content consistency, physical interaction, 3D structure, and conditioning fidelity.
 - The model demonstrated competitive or leading performance across several specific metrics:
 - **Visual Quality:** Image Quality (63.25), Aesthetic Quality (43.38), JEPA Similarity (92.93).
 - **Motion Quality:** Dynamic Degree (22.90), Flow Score (5.81), Motion Smoothness (63.26).
 - **Content Consistency:** Subject Consistency (71.93), Background Consistency (84.52), Photometric Consistency (14.29).
 - **Physics Adherence:** Interaction Quality (57.36).
 - **3D Accuracy:** Trajectory Accuracy (57.15), Depth Accuracy (98.55), Perspectivity (82.24).
 - **Controllability:** Instruction Following (61.62), Semantic Alignment (90.53).
 - Qualitative rollouts (Figure 3 in the paper) indicated that the model maintained coherence of arms, grippers, and manipulated objects across episodes, even under domain randomization (varied backgrounds, textures, lighting, distractor layouts).

- **Track 2 (World Model for Policy Training):**
 - DreamX-Phi-1.0-FDM-0730 achieved a 67.19% success rate on the "Adjust Bottle" task in RoboTwin 2.0 when used as the rollout environment for training a π₀.₅ policy.
 - This result tied for second place in the WorldArena 2.0 Track 2 leaderboard snapshot.

#### 4.2. WorldArena 1.0 Results (Offline Evaluation, Snapshot: July 15, 2026)

- In an offline evaluation on the WorldArena 1.0 Track 1 test set, DreamX-Phi-1.0-FDM-0730 achieved an EWMScore-P of 76.88.
- This score was 3.24 points higher than the leading official entry in that specific leaderboard snapshot (UNIS, 73.64).
- The model exhibited high scores in metrics such as Flow Score (100.00), Depth Accuracy (93.17), Trajectory Accuracy (96.30), and Semantic Alignment (89.68).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of DreamX-Phi 1.0 represents an advancement in the field of action-conditioned video world models for robotic manipulation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Prediction Fidelity:** By integrating geometry-aware action representations (PRoPE) with manipulation-aware supervision (depth, object masks, V-JEPA), the model addresses a critical limitation of previous world models: the gap between photorealistic generation and faithful adherence to commanded actions. This approach contributes to more physically coherent and controllable predicted rollouts.
2. **Improved Performance on Benchmarks:** The strong performance on the WorldArena 2.0 Track 1 leaderboard (first place) and Track 2 (tied for second), along with its offline WorldArena 1.0 score exceeding existing leading entries, demonstrates the system's effectiveness and robustness in complex robotic manipulation scenarios. These results indicate a higher degree of accuracy in predicting visual outcomes and a more reliable internal dynamics model for policy learning.
3. **Support for Robotic Planning and Control:** A world model that accurately and faithfully predicts future states from proposed actions is foundational for advanced robotic planning, model-predictive control, and reinforcement learning. By providing more reliable imagined outcomes, DreamX-Phi 1.0 can potentially enable policies to be trained more efficiently and perform more robustly in real-world or simulated environments.
4. **Efficient Deployment:** The incorporation of Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) allows for the distillation of the multi-step generator into a few-step student. This addresses the computational cost often associated with diffusion models, making the world model more practical for real-time or near real-time deployment in robotic systems.
5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper's contributions, particularly in structured action conditioning and comprehensive physical-consistency supervision, establish a foundation for further research into more sophisticated embodied world models. The announced public availability of the model and code is expected to foster broader research and development in the community.
6. **Addressing Key Challenges in Embodied AI:** The work contributes to addressing fundamental challenges in embodied AI, where agents need to reason about physical interactions, object permanence, and 3D geometry from visual observations.

While the current model is a Forward Dynamics Model (FDM) and does not generate actions itself, its proven utility as a rollout environment for policy training suggests a pathway towards more integrated World Action Models that can jointly generate video and robot actions, further enhancing autonomous robotic capabilities.
