# HELM: Human-Preferred Exploration with Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> HELM presents a framework that enables robots to incorporate human exploration preferences, expressed in natural language, directly into their autonomous navigation strategies by leveraging large language models. The system demonstrates competitive exploration efficiency compared to traditional methods while effectively adapting to user-specified directional preferences and generalizing to unseen environments without requiring retraining.

## Problem

- Traditional autonomous exploration methods rely on fixed algorithms and predefined parameters, making them difficult to adapt to specific human preferences.
- Existing learning-based methods for robot exploration are constrained by their training data and struggle to adapt to new, unencountered preferences or environments.
- A lack of intuitive interfaces prevents non-experts from easily incorporating real-time human preferences into robot exploration policies.

## Method

- **HELM** integrates ==Large Language Models (LLMs)== into an autonomous exploration system to allow robots to interpret and act on human preferences expressed in natural language.
- The framework converts robot sensor data into a structured graph representation of the environment, which is then described textually for the LLM.
- It combines this textual environment description with human preferences and task context into a 'mixed observation' prompt, enabling the LLM to make sequential planning decisions for the robot.

## Results

- HELM achieves exploration efficiency comparable to state-of-the-art traditional methods like TARE and ARiADNE in both simulated dungeons and high-fidelity Gazebo environments.
- The framework demonstrates adaptability to different environments without requiring retraining, showing strong generalization capabilities.
- HELM effectively incorporates human-expressed directional preferences (e.g., 'prioritize upper-left corner') into its path planning, aligning exploration with user intent.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating large language models (LLMs) with robot sensor data allows for intuitive human preference integration into exploration strategies.
> - Converting environment belief states and human preferences into textual prompts enables LLMs to perform sequential decision-making for robot navigation.
> - The framework's training-free nature allows it to adapt to new environments and varying human preferences without retraining.
