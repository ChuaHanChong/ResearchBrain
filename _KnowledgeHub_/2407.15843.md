# CarFormer: Self-Driving with Learned Object-Centric Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> CarFormer learns object-centric driving by extracting bird's-eye-view scenes into slots via slot attention, then feeding these slots into an autoregressive transformer with block attention to jointly predict driving actions and future scene dynamics. Slot representations outperform both scene-level BEV tokens and explicit object-attribute baselines, achieving state-of-the-art Longest6 driving score with much lower run-to-run variance.

## Problem

- Scene-level BEV token representations bury small but safety-critical objects like vehicles under dominant background classes, causing high infraction rates despite accurate reconstruction.
- Prior object-centric driving methods represent vehicles with hand-specified attribute vectors (position, size, heading, speed) that may be incomplete or fail to capture varying spatiotemporal context.
- Standard causal attention in autoregressive transformers restricts interaction between simultaneous objects and route tokens, limiting the model's ability to reason jointly about scene dynamics.

## Method

- Slot extraction backbone: a frozen ==SAVi (slot attention for video)== model applied to BEV sequences, place objects into learned slots without explicit attribute supervision, with a lighter decoder enabling up to 30 slots.
- **CarFormer** transformer: a ==GPT-2==-style ==autoregressive decoder== consuming mixed continuous (slot, route) and discretized (target point, speed, traffic light) tokens with a modified embedding layer.
- ==Block attention== mechanism: replaces triangular causal masking with a block-triangular mask so all slot and route tokens within a block cross-attend, paired with dual ==GRU== and quantized-action heads plus an auxiliary future-slot forecasting loss.

## Results

- On Longest6, CarFormer with slots reaches driving score **74.89** versus **73.36** for reproduced PlanT and **55.27** for ROACH, with roughly half PlanT's standard deviation.
- Ablations show removing block attention drops driving score from **74.89** to **70.42**, and removing the forecasting loss drops infraction score from **0.79** to **0.63**.
- Increasing slots from **7** to **30** with vehicle enlargement raises driving score from **48.17** to **74.89**, and forecasting evaluation gives CarFormer **0.540** ARI versus **0.412** for a copy-input baseline at t+4.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learned, self-supervised object slots can implicitly encode speed, heading, and context that hand-designed attribute vectors specify explicitly, without added supervision cost.
> - Joint auxiliary forecasting of future object states meaningfully improves policy quality, suggesting driving benefits from being trained as a combined dynamics-and-control task.
> - Small-object under-representation in reconstruction-based slot learning is a systematic failure mode; simple input-space enlargement can correct it without architectural changes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous driving heavily relies on robust scene understanding and decision-making, which are fundamentally influenced by the chosen internal representations of the environment. Historically, self-driving research has explored various representation spaces:
- **Pixel-level/Image-centric:** Directly processing raw camera images (e.g.,).
- **Semantic Segmentation:** Identifying relevant regions like roads, vehicles, and pedestrians in 2D or 3D.
- **Coordinate Space:** Representing objects as points or trajectories, common in trajectory prediction.
- **Bird's Eye View (BEV):** A top-down perspective that aggregates sensor data into a unified, spatially intuitive map. BEV has gained significant traction due to its ability to simplify spatial reasoning for planning and control.

Recent years have seen a convergence towards **BEV representations** due to their intuitive nature for driving tasks. However, even BEV can be high-dimensional and contain redundant information (e.g., vast road regions versus critical vehicles). This motivated the exploration of **object-level representations**, which distill the scene into discrete entities (e.g., vehicles, pedestrians, lanes) and their attributes (position, heading, speed). PlanT is a notable work that uses explicit, hand-engineered object attributes for this purpose, feeding them into a transformer for planning.

Simultaneously, **transformers** have revolutionized sequential modeling across various domains, from natural language processing to computer vision and robotics. Their ability to model long-range dependencies and complex interactions makes them attractive for dynamic self-driving scenarios. The challenge in applying transformers to high-dimensional continuous inputs like sensor data has led to strategies like quantization (VQ-VAE) or using object-centric tokens.

This paper positions itself at the intersection of BEV, object-centric learning, and transformer-based sequential modeling. It addresses a critical limitation of previous object-level approaches, such as PlanT, which rely on *explicit, predefined object attributes*. The authors argue that explicitly specifying attributes might be incomplete or inflexible. Instead, CarFormer leverages **self-supervised object-centric representations**, specifically **slots** extracted from BEV sequences using **Slot Attention (SAVi)**. This approach aims to learn *implicit* object representations that naturally capture relevant information (like speed and orientation) from spatial and temporal context, offering a more scalable and potentially generalizable alternative. By integrating these learned slots into an autoregressive transformer, CarFormer seeks to improve driving performance, robustness, and also act as a world model for forecasting future scene dynamics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind CarFormer are:

1. **Develop Learned Object-Centric Representations:** To move beyond high-dimensional scene-level BEV representations and predefined object attributes by learning object-centric "slots." These slots should implicitly encode relevant information about objects (e.g., position, heading, speed) from their spatio-temporal context in BEV sequences, rather than relying on explicit attribute definitions.
2. **Enhance Driving Performance and Robustness:** To utilize these learned slot representations within a transformer-based architecture to achieve superior self-driving performance, specifically in terms of route completion, infraction reduction, and overall driving score. A key motivation is also to improve the *robustness* of the driving policy, demonstrated by lower variance across multiple evaluation runs.
3. **Jointly Learn Driving Behavior and Scene Dynamics:** To design a model that not only predicts driving actions but also acts as a "world model" by forecasting the future states of objects (i.e., future slot representations). This joint learning is hypothesized to improve the agent's understanding of scene dynamics, leading to safer and more effective decision-making.
4. **Leverage Transformers for Sequential Modeling:** To adapt an autoregressive transformer architecture for the complex, continuous, and dynamic self-driving task, incorporating modifications like "block attention" to better model interactions between objects and the desired route.
5. **Address Limitations of Existing Approaches:** To overcome the challenges associated with raw BEV (high dimensionality, distraction) and explicit object attributes (potential incompleteness, manual engineering) by providing a self-supervised, data-driven approach to object representation for autonomous driving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CarFormer employs a two-stage approach: first, learning object-centric representations, and then using these to train a transformer for driving and forecasting.

#### 3.1. Slot Extraction (Stage 1)

- **Input:** Ground-truth Bird's Eye View (BEV) representations of the scene over the last `T` time steps (e.g., `T=2`). Each BEV map `B_t` is an ego-centric binary map with 8 channels for semantic classes (road, vehicles, etc.).
- **Model:** A pre-trained, frozen **SAVi (Slot Attention for Videos)** model is used. SAVi is an auto-encoding paradigm that takes video sequences and decomposes them into `K` latent "slot vectors" `{z_i^t}`. Each slot is intended to bind to an individual object and encode its relevant features.
- **Self-supervision:** SAVi is trained with a self-supervised objective to reconstruct the input frames, allowing slots to implicitly infer object properties without explicit labels.
- **Improvements for Driving:** The authors found that increasing the number of slots (`K`) and "enlarging" small objects (like motorbikes) in the BEV input before slot extraction significantly improved slot extraction quality, as small objects are often overlooked due to reconstruction loss being dominated by larger objects.

#### 3.2. CarFormer Architecture (Stage 2)

CarFormer is an autoregressive transformer-based model designed to learn driving and scene dynamics.

- **Input Trajectory (τ_t):** At each time step `t`, the model receives a trajectory `τ_t` composed of various tokens:
 - **Goal/Ego-state:** Quantized target point (`g_x^t`, `g_y^t`), traffic light status (`l_t`), and ego-vehicle speed (`v_t`). These are discretized using k-means clustering and embedded.
 - **Scene Representation:** The `K` object-level slot representations `{z_i^t}` extracted from SAVi. (In comparative experiments, these can be replaced by VQ-VAE tokens for scene-level or explicit object attributes like PlanT).
 - **Route Representation:** Two continuous attribute vectors (`r_1^t`, `r_2^t`) representing the desired route, similar to PlanT.
 - **Action (for supervision):** Quantized waypoints `{q_j^t}` representing future driving actions.
- **Encoding:** Discrete inputs (quantized target points, traffic light, speed) are embedded via lookup tables. Continuous inputs (slots, route vectors) are projected to the desired hidden dimension using MLPs.
- **Backbone:** An autoregressive transformer decoder, adapted from GPT-2.
 - **Block Attention:** A key modification is replacing standard causal attention with "block triangular attention." This allows full cross-attention *within* specific blocks of tokens (e.g., all `K` slot representations and the route vectors form a block), while maintaining causal attention *between* blocks. This enables better modeling of interactions among objects and the route.
- **Action Representation & Prediction:**
 - **GRU Head:** A small GRU network, similar to PlanT, takes the last latent vector from the transformer backbone and predicts `W` continuous future waypoints `w_t` autoregressively.
 - **Quantization (Q) Head:** The transformer's language modeling head directly predicts `W` quantized waypoints `q_t` autoregressively, treating waypoint prediction as a "next-word" problem.

#### 3.3. Training

- **Imitation Learning:** CarFormer is trained using imitation learning, supervised by expert driving data collected in CARLA.
- **Loss Function:** The total loss `L` is a weighted sum of two components:
 - **Waypoint Prediction Loss (L_wp):** Combines an L1 loss for continuous waypoints from the GRU head (`L_GRU`) and a cross-entropy loss for quantized waypoints from the language modeling head (`L_LM`).
 - **Auxiliary Forecasting Loss (L_forecast):**
 - For object-level representations (slots or attributes): Mean Squared Error (MSE) between predicted future slot/object vectors and ground truth future slot/object vectors (`L_object`).
 - For scene-level representations (VQ-VAE tokens): Cross-entropy loss for predicted future BEV tokens (`L_scene`).
 - The `L_forecast` term is weighted by `α` (experimentally set to 40). This forces the model to learn to predict future states of objects, acting as a world model.
- **Evaluation Setting:** Training is done on 3x CARLA dataset and evaluated on the Longest6 benchmark, employing metrics like Route Completion (RC), Infraction Score (IS), and Driving Score (DS). Creeping is used to prevent the agent from getting stuck.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

CarFormer demonstrates significant improvements in self-driving performance and robustness, primarily attributed to its learned object-centric slot representations and the transformer architecture.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **Superior Performance with Slots (Table 1):**
 - CarFormer with VQ-VAE-discretized BEV performs poorly (DS 17.07), highlighting the challenge of direct scene-level input.
 - CarFormer with *explicit object attributes* (DS 71.53) performs comparably to PlanT (DS 73.36), indicating the transformer architecture itself is competitive.
 - **CarFormer with *learned object-centric slots* achieves the highest Driving Score (DS 74.89±1.44), outperforming all scene-level baselines (AIM-BEV, ROACH) and even PlanT (DS 73.36±2.97) which uses exact object attributes.**
 - **Robustness:** Notably, the slot-based CarFormer shows significantly *lower variance* (±1.44) compared to PlanT (±2.97) and CarFormer with attributes (±3.52), indicating increased stability across different runs.
- **Ablation Study (Table 2):**
 - **Action Head:** The GRU head for continuous waypoint prediction (DS 74.89) significantly outperforms the quantized action prediction with the transformer's language modeling head (DS 66.87).
 - **Block Attention (BA):** Removing BA leads to a drop in DS (74.89 to 70.42), indicating that allowing full interaction within the object-route block improves scene dynamics modeling.
 - **Forecasting Future Slots:** Removing the auxiliary forecasting loss drastically reduces DS (74.89 to 57.25), particularly impacting the Infraction Score (0.79 to 0.63). This strongly supports the hypothesis that learning to anticipate future object states is crucial for safer driving.
 - **Creeping:** Essential for route completion, preventing the agent from getting stuck (DS 74.89 to 69.16).
- **Slot Representation Optimizations (Table 3):**
 - **Number of Slots:** Increasing the number of slots from 7 to 30 significantly improves driving performance, especially Infraction Score (DS 48.17 to 71.48 with ✕ Enlarged, and 62.93 to 74.89 with ✓ Enlarged). More slots allow for better capture of all vehicles.
 - **Enlarging Small Vehicles:** This technique, used during slot extraction, notably enhances performance, particularly with fewer slots, by ensuring small objects are assigned slots (DS 48.17 to 62.93 for 7 slots). The best performance is achieved with 30 slots and enlarging small vehicles.
- **Forecasting Future Slots (Table 4, 11, 12, 15):**
 - CarFormer significantly outperforms "Input-Copy" (a simple baseline) in predicting future slot representations, demonstrating its ability to learn and predict object dynamics. For `t+4` prediction, CarFormer achieves ARI 0.540 and mIoU 0.454, much higher than Input-Copy's ARI 0.412 and mIoU 0.375.
 - Enlarging slots and using Block Attention consistently improve forecasting performance, further validating their role in better object modeling.
 - Increasing the input context length (e.g., from 1 to 4 timesteps) consistently improves forecasting performance (Table 15), showing the model can utilize temporal information.
- **Forecasting Steps and Weight (Table 16, Fig. 3):**
 - Forecasting further into the future (e.g., `t+4` vs. `t+1`) improves DS, IS, and RC.
 - The forecasting loss weight `α` is crucial, with optimal performance around `α=40`. Too low or too high `α` degrades performance, indicating a balance between driving and forecasting objectives is needed.
- **Efficiency (Table 17):** CarFormer models with slots run in real-time (~14ms per inference, >60 FPS), though slot extraction (SAVi) accounts for approximately half of the inference time.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results (Fig. 2, 4, 5)

- **Proper Dynamics Learning:** Visualizations show CarFormer effectively learns and predicts visual dynamics, inferring vehicle movement, turns, and relative speeds without explicit attributes. Examples illustrate accurate prediction of vehicles moving backward, forward at different speeds, and changing lanes.
- **Problems in Forecasting:**
 - **SAVi Perception Errors:** The model's forecasting is limited by the upstream SAVi slot extraction, which can produce blurry predictions or miss objects in crowded scenes.
 - **False Positives:** Hallucinated vehicles can appear in predictions.
 - **Complex Dynamics/Multi-modality:** The model struggles with complex multi-vehicle interactions or scenarios where a vehicle could take multiple plausible actions (e.g., turning left or right at an intersection), often predicting a mean outcome rather than distinct modes.
- **Effect of Number of Slots (Fig. 4):** Visual comparison confirms that 30 slots significantly improve the ability to capture all vehicles compared to 7 or 14 slots, which often miss smaller or occluded vehicles, or represent them as blurry blobs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CarFormer makes several significant contributions to autonomous driving research:

1. **Pioneering Learned Object-Centric Representations:** This work is the first to propose and successfully demonstrate the use of *learned*, self-supervised object-centric slot representations for end-to-end self-driving. This shifts the paradigm from hand-engineered object attributes to data-driven, implicit representations, potentially leading to more scalable and robust systems.
2. **Improved Performance and Robustness:** CarFormer with slots achieves state-of-the-art performance in the privileged CARLA setting, surpassing models using explicit object attributes (PlanT) and scene-level BEV approaches. The demonstrated lower variance across runs highlights the robustness of slot representations in complex urban environments.
3. **Unified Policy and World Model:** By jointly training for driving actions and forecasting future slot representations, CarFormer functions as both a policy for immediate control and a visual dynamics model. This capability to predict future scene states is crucial for anticipatory planning and understanding other agents' intentions, contributing to safer driving.
4. **Advancements in Transformer Application:** The introduction of "block attention" for enhanced interaction within object groups (slots + route) is a novel adaptation of transformer architecture for continuous, dynamic control tasks, potentially transferable to other complex robotics problems.
5. **Foundation for Generalization:** The self-supervised nature of slot extraction promises better generalization to diverse objects beyond just vehicles, as long as they can be assigned to a slot. This opens avenues for more comprehensive scene understanding.

**Limitations and Future Work:**

- **Reliance on Ground-Truth BEV:** A major limitation is the assumption of access to ground-truth BEV maps. Real-world autonomous vehicles rely on complex perception systems to infer BEV from raw sensor data, which is an active area of research itself. Propagating perception errors from BEV estimation to slot extraction could significantly impact performance.
- **SAVi Perception Errors:** The performance of CarFormer is constrained by the upstream SAVi model's ability to accurately extract slots, especially in challenging scenarios like crowded scenes or for small/occluded objects.
- **Multi-modality:** The current model struggles to predict complex dynamics with multiple plausible future outcomes (multi-modality), often predicting an average.
- **Efficiency of Slot Extraction:** While the overall system runs in real-time, SAVi accounts for a substantial portion of the inference time. More efficient slot extraction techniques are needed for practical deployment.

**Future research directions highlighted by the authors include:**
- **End-to-End Learning:** Integrating slot extraction directly from camera images rather than relying on ground-truth BEV, possibly via a direct BEV slot extraction approach to avoid cascading errors.
- **Multi-step Reasoning with Reward Tokens:** Extending the autoregressive transformer to perform multi-step planning and incorporate reward/return tokens, leveraging insights from transformer-based reinforcement learning.
- **Improved Multi-modality Handling:** Developing methods to explicitly model and predict multiple plausible future trajectories for interacting objects.
- **Generalization to Diverse Objects:** Exploiting the object-centric nature to include and reason about a broader range of scene elements that can be represented as slots.

Overall, CarFormer represents a significant step towards more intelligent and robust autonomous driving systems by demonstrating the power of learned, object-centric representations in a transformer-based framework, coupled with the ability to forecast future scene dynamics.
