# WMNav: Integrating Vision-Language Models into World Models for Object Goal Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> WMNav introduces a data-free knowledge distillation framework that leverages degradation-prompt diffusion models to synthesize domain-related degraded images for multi-weather image restoration, achieving efficient lightweight student models with performance comparable to data-present methods.

## Problem

- Zero-Shot Object Goal Navigation (ZSON) agents face costly and risky physical interactions with unknown environments, requiring extensive exploration.
- Existing VLM/LLM-based navigation methods often lack anticipatory planning, reacting to immediate observations rather than proactively planning based on predicted future states.
- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) can suffer from hallucinations, providing unreliable information in complex planning tasks, necessitating robust mitigation strategies.

## Method

- **WMNav** integrates ==Vision-Language Models (VLMs)== into a world model framework, enabling agents to predict future states and outcomes of actions without direct physical interaction.
- A Curiosity Value Map (CVM) is introduced as a dynamic, quantitative memory strategy to store and update the likelihood of a target's presence, preventing redundant exploration.
- The navigation task is structured through Subtask Decomposition and a Two-stage Action Proposer, enhancing planning efficiency and mitigating VLM hallucination for more reliable action selection.

## Results

- WMNav achieved state-of-the-art performance in ZSON, with **58.1%** Success Rate (SR) and **31.2%** Success Rate Weighted by Inverse Path Length (SPL) on the HM3D benchmark, and **45.4%** SR and **17.2%** SPL on the MP3D benchmark.
- Ablation studies confirmed that Subtask Decomposition, the Curiosity Value Map, and the Two-stage Action Proposer each consistently improved both SR and SPL, highlighting their critical contributions.
- The framework benefits from larger and more advanced VLM backbones (e.g., Gemini **1.5** Pro) for higher performance, but remains robust with smaller proprietary and open-source models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs, pre-trained on vast human perspective images, possess sufficient common sense knowledge of indoor layouts and object relationships to function effectively as predictors within a world model for embodied tasks.
> - Anticipatory planning through a VLM-driven world model significantly reduces the need for constant, costly physical exploration, leading to more efficient and robust navigation.
> - Quantitative memory (Curiosity Value Map) combined with structured subtask planning helps alleviate VLM hallucination, forcing the VLM to generate more rigorous and reliable predictions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses **Zero-Shot Object Goal Navigation (ZSON)**, a critical and challenging problem in embodied AI. ZSON requires an autonomous agent to locate and navigate to a specific, previously unseen object category in an entirely unfamiliar environment without prior training on that object or environment. This capability is crucial for developing truly intelligent and versatile domestic robots.

Historically, embodied navigation methods have largely fallen into two categories:
1. **Network-based navigation:** These methods often rely on reinforcement learning (RL) or imitation learning (IL), requiring extensive training data and computational resources. While effective in known environments, their "zero-shot" performance on unseen objects/rooms is limited due to their dependency on pre-trained policies.
2. **Map-based navigation:** These approaches construct explicit semantic maps of the environment to plan paths. While providing more global context, they often involve complex and time-consuming map construction processes and might struggle with dynamic environments or semantic reasoning for unseen objects.

Recent advancements in **Foundation Models**, particularly **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)** and Large Language Models (LLMs), have opened new avenues for ZSON. LLMs offer powerful common sense reasoning and planning capabilities but struggle with direct visual grounding and spatial understanding. VLMs, on the other than, can process both visual and textual information, making them more suitable for visual navigation tasks by aligning observations with textual goals. Previous VLM-based approaches, such as VLMNav and VLFM, demonstrated promising perception and decision-making by leveraging VLMs for flexible action space generation or value map construction. However, they often lacked a comprehensive anticipatory planning mechanism, still heavily relying on direct environment interaction.

**WMNav introduces a novel paradigm by integrating VLMs directly into a "World Model" framework.** World models, inspired by cognitive science, enable agents to predict future states and outcomes of potential actions without physical interaction, thereby reducing risky and costly exploration. While LLMs have been explored as abstract world models in web environments, their application to visually rich, embodied tasks has been limited. The key novelty of WMNav is its hypothesis that VLMs, trained on vast amounts of first-person human perspective images, inherently possess sufficient common sense knowledge about indoor layouts and object relationships to function effectively as predictors within a world model. This approach moves beyond simply using VLMs for perception or planning by explicitly leveraging their generative and reasoning capabilities to simulate environmental dynamics.

Therefore, WMNav represents a significant step forward by:
- **Bridging VLMs and World Models:** It demonstrates how VLMs can serve as effective world models for embodied navigation, a previously underexplored integration.
- **Reducing Environmental Interaction:** By predicting future states, it mitigates the need for constant, costly physical exploration, enhancing efficiency.
- **Modular and Training-Free:** Unlike many prior methods, WMNav achieves zero-shot navigation without task-specific training or reliance on pre-built maps or external detectors, relying purely on the VLM's inherent capabilities and prompt engineering.

This work carves out a new direction for ZSON, pushing the boundaries of what training-free, VLM-powered embodied agents can achieve in complex, unknown environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for WMNav stems from the inherent inefficiencies and limitations of existing Zero-Shot Object Navigation (ZSON) approaches, particularly in realistic, unseen indoor environments. Current methods, whether network-based or map-based, often require extensive interaction with the environment to gain an understanding of the scene or to train specific navigation policies. This leads to several challenges:

- **Costly and Risky Interactions:** Repeated physical interactions with an unknown environment can be time-consuming, energy-intensive, and prone to collisions or navigation failures.
- **Lack of Anticipatory Planning:** Most existing VLM-based or LLM-based navigation agents primarily react to immediate observations rather than proactively planning based on predicted future states. This limits their ability to perform complex, multi-step reasoning.
- **VLM Hallucination:** While powerful, VLMs can sometimes "hallucinate" or provide misleading information, especially when presented with ambiguous visual cues or complex planning tasks. Current methods often lack robust mechanisms to alleviate this.
- **Suboptimal Decision-Making:** Without a broader understanding of potential outcomes, agents may make locally optimal but globally inefficient decisions, leading to redundant movements and slower task completion.

Given these challenges, the **key objectives** of WMNav are:

1. **Establish a fully modular world model design for ZSON:** To enable the agent to predict the future state of the world and the outcomes of its decisions, thereby reducing the need for direct, risky environmental interactions.
2. **Leverage Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as the core of this world model:** To exploit VLMs' extensive pre-trained knowledge of indoor layouts, object relationships, and general reasoning abilities to simulate environmental dynamics without explicit training.
3. **Develop an innovative memory strategy:** Specifically, the Curiosity Value Map, to quantitatively store and update the likelihood of a target's presence across different regions of the environment, providing dynamic feedback to the navigation policy.
4. **Enhance reliability and efficiency through structured planning:** By decomposing the navigation task into human-like subtasks and employing a two-stage action proposer, aiming to mitigate VLM hallucination and improve exploration efficiency.
5. **Achieve state-of-the-art performance in ZSON:** To demonstrate the effectiveness of the VLM-based world model framework on standard benchmarks, surpassing existing zero-shot methods in success rate and efficiency.

In essence, WMNav aims to enable embodied agents to "think before acting" by empowering them with a VLM-driven world model that anticipates, plans, and learns from simulated outcomes, making navigation more robust, efficient, and human-like.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WMNav proposes a novel **World Model-based Navigation framework powered by Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**, designed to mimic human-like anticipatory planning in unseen environments. The framework integrates perception, prediction, planning, reasoning, and control processes without task-specific training, pre-built maps, or prior knowledge.

The WMNav architecture consists of several interconnected modules, as depicted in Figure 2:

1. **Panoramic Image Input:** At each time step `t`, the agent captures six distinct RGB-D images by rotating its camera, which are then stitched into a single panoramic image `I_pan_t`. This provides a comprehensive 360-degree view of the immediate surroundings, crucial for holistic scene understanding.

2. **World Model:** This is the core predictive component, comprising `PredictVLM` and a dynamic memory. The world model's primary function is to predict future states without direct environmental interaction.
 - **VLM-based State Prediction (`PredictVLM`):** A VLM is prompted with the panoramic image `I_pan_t` and asked to quantitatively predict a "curiosity value" (score from 0 to 10) for each navigable direction. This score represents the likelihood of the target object being present or accessible via that direction. A detailed prompt strategy (Figure 3a) guides the VLM to assign scores (e.g., 0 for walls, 10 for direct goal visibility, 2-9 for potential paths).
 - **Curiosity Value Map Construction:** The predicted scores from `PredictVLM` are projected from the agent's ego-centric view onto a top-down, global "Curiosity Value Map" (`M_cv`). This map, initialized with all values at 10 (indicating unknown potential), is updated by combining current projected scores (`M_nav_t`) with previous map states (`M_cv_t-1`) using a `min` operation (`M_cv_t(u,v) = min(M_cv_t-1(u,v), M_nav_t(u,v))`). This ensures that if a region has been explored and found to lack the target, its curiosity value is permanently reduced, preventing redundant exploration.
 - **Cost Module:** The world model also maintains a "cost" which is essentially feedback derived from the previous step's subtask and a goal flag (indicating if the goal was found). This cost is used to configure prompts for the navigation policy's VLMs, implicitly optimizing their outputs without fine-tuning.

3. **Subtask Decomposition (`PlanVLM`):** To manage the complexity of finding an unseen goal, WMNav breaks down the main task into smaller, manageable subtasks (e.g., "Go to the hallway" before "Go to the bedroom").
 - The updated `M_cv_t` is projected back onto the panoramic image to identify the direction `α` with the highest curiosity value.
 - The `PlanVLM` receives the image corresponding to direction `α` (`I_t(α)`) and the *previous* subtask (`SubTask_t-1`) as input. Its output is a *new* subtask (`SubTask_t`) and a `GoalFlag_t` (True if the goal is found in `I_t(α)`, False otherwise). This structured planning helps guide the agent's focus.

4. **Two-stage Action Proposer (`ReasonVLM`):** This module determines the precise action to execute, divided into two stages for efficiency and accuracy:
 - **Action Space Generation:** A flexible action proposer, inspired by VLMNav, samples polar coordinate vectors (`(r, θ)`) representing movement directions and distances from navigable regions within the selected image `I_t(α)`. These candidate actions are annotated on the image (`I_ann_t`).
 - **Exploration Stage:** If the goal has not been detected (`GoalFlag_t` is False), the `ReasonVLM` (configured as `ActionVLM`) is prompted with `SubTask_t`, the global `Goal`, and `I_ann_t`. It selects the most appropriate action (`a_t`) from the annotated candidates to advance the current subtask. The prompt explicitly links the subtask and goal to the visual input to guide decision-making.
 - **Goal-approaching Stage:** Once `GoalFlag_t` is True (meaning the `PlanVLM` has identified the goal in `I_t(α)`), the `ReasonVLM` (configured as `GoalVLM`) enters this stage. The action proposer generates a denser set of candidate actions, focusing on reaching the detected goal. `GoalVLM` then selects the action that leads closest to the goal. The agent stops when the distance to the goal falls below a predefined threshold (`d_thres`).

The entire process is a continuous loop of observing, predicting with the world model, planning with subtasks, and executing actions based on reasoned choices. The prompt-based configuration of VLMs allows for flexible adaptation without any training or fine-tuning, leveraging the VLM's inherent capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

WMNav demonstrates significant advancements in Zero-Shot Object Navigation (ZSON) across established benchmarks, validating its VLM-based world model framework and proposed modules.

**A. State-of-the-Art Performance:**
The most crucial finding is WMNav's superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art methods in ZSON.
- **HM3D Benchmark:** WMNav achieves **58.1% Success Rate (SR)** and **31.2% Success Rate Weighted by Inverse Path Length (SPL)**. This represents an absolute improvement of **+3.2% SR and +3.2% SPL** over the previous best zero-shot benchmarks. Notably, WMNav's SPL on HM3D is the best among all methods, including supervised ones, highlighting its efficiency.
- **MP3D Benchmark:** WMNav achieves **45.4% SR and 17.2% SPL**. This is an absolute improvement of **+13.5% SR and +1.1% SPL** over previous zero-shot methods. On MP3D, WMNav also achieves the optimal SR across all compared methods, demonstrating its robustness.

These results underscore that WMNav effectively addresses the core challenges of ZSON by improving both the agent's ability to successfully find the target and its efficiency in doing so, without requiring task-specific training.

**B. Ablation Studies:**
A detailed ablation study on HM3D v0.2 provides insights into the contribution of each proposed module and memory strategy:

- **Subtask Decomposition (SD):** Activating SD (comparing row 'a' vs 'b', and 'd' vs 'e' in Table II) consistently improves both SR and SPL, demonstrating its effectiveness in providing denser rewards and guiding the VLM's planning process by breaking down complex tasks.
- **Curiosity Value Map (CVM):** The CVM, as the proposed memory strategy, significantly outperforms alternatives.
 - "No Memory" (row 'b') yields 67.4% SR and 33.1% SPL.
 - "Text-Image Memory" (row 'c'), which attempts to use VLM-generated text descriptions of the observation and a top-down trajectory map as memory, performs worse (62.0% SR, 29.6% SPL). This suggests that directly feeding VLM-generated text/images as memory can induce hallucinations and unreliable information.
 - In contrast, **CVM (Ours)** (row 'e', with SD) achieves **70.1% SR and 34.7% SPL**, demonstrating its critical role. The quantitative nature of the CVM forces the VLM to generate more rigorous and reliable predictions, leading to better memory management and navigation.
- **Two-stage Action Proposer (TAP):** The TAP strategy further boosts performance (row 'f' vs 'e'), with WMNav achieving **72.2% SR and 33.3% SPL** on HM3D v0.2. This confirms that dividing the action-decision process into exploration and goal-approaching stages, with refined sampling, makes the agent more purposeful and accurate in localization.

**C. Effect of Different VLMs:**
The study on various VLM backbones (Table III) reveals:
- **Model Scale Matters:** Increasing the scale of open-source models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-3B vs. 7B) directly correlates with improved navigation capabilities.
- **Proprietary VLMs Lead:** Advanced proprietary models like Gemini 1.5 Pro (58.1% SR, 31.2% SPL) significantly outperform open-source alternatives. However, even smaller proprietary models like Gemini 1.5 Flash (53.5% SR, 27.0% SPL) achieve competitive results on HM3D, indicating that the WMNav framework itself is robust and not solely reliant on the absolute best VLM, but can greatly benefit from VLM advancements.

In summary, the findings robustly support that integrating VLMs into a world model framework, augmented with a quantitative memory map, subtask decomposition, and a two-stage action strategy, forms a highly effective and efficient approach for zero-shot object navigation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The WMNav research introduces a highly significant advancement in the field of embodied AI, particularly for Zero-Shot Object Goal Navigation (ZSON). Its core contribution of integrating Vision-Language Models (VLMs) into a modular world model paradigm opens up several new pathways and implications:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Embodied AI:** WMNav shifts the focus from purely reactive or map-dependent navigation to a more cognitive, anticipatory approach. By enabling agents to "imagine" and predict future states and action outcomes, it moves closer to human-like intelligence in robotics, where foresight and planning are paramount. This world model approach can drastically reduce the amount of physical interaction required for exploration, making embodied agents safer, faster, and more energy-efficient.

2. **Enhanced Efficiency and Robustness in ZSON:** The demonstrated state-of-the-art performance on challenging benchmarks (HM3D, MP3D) validates the practical utility of WMNav. By addressing the inefficiency of redundant movements and mitigating VLM hallucination through structured planning (subtask decomposition) and robust memory (Curiosity Value Map), the framework significantly improves both success rates and navigation efficiency. This is crucial for real-world applications where optimal pathfinding and minimal interaction costs are desired.

3. **Leveraging VLM Capabilities for Complex Reasoning:** The work highlights the untapped potential of VLMs beyond mere perception or direct decision-making. By assigning VLMs roles as predictors, planners, and reasoners within a world model, WMNav effectively harnesses their vast pre-trained knowledge about spatial relationships, object contexts, and common sense, even in unseen environments. This opens avenues for more complex, high-level reasoning tasks in embodied agents.

4. **Training-Free and Modular Approach:** The training-free nature of WMNav, relying on prompt-based optimization rather than fine-tuning, makes it highly adaptable and scalable. This modularity means that as VLMs continue to evolve and improve, WMNav can seamlessly integrate newer, more powerful models, leading to further performance gains without requiring re-training the entire navigation system. This democratizes access to advanced navigation capabilities for robots without needing massive datasets or computational resources for task-specific training.

5. **Reduced Hallucination and Improved Reliability:** The paper explicitly addresses the problem of VLM hallucination, which can be a major impediment to reliable AI systems. By using subtask decomposition with feedback and quantitative memory in the Curiosity Value Map, WMNav forces the VLM to produce more rigorous and accurate outputs, enhancing the trustworthiness of the agent's decisions.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Domestic and Service Robotics:** WMNav could power more intelligent and autonomous domestic robots capable of performing complex tasks like "find my keys on the table" or "bring me the book from the study" in dynamic, unfamiliar home environments.
- **Industrial Automation:** In warehouses or factories, autonomous mobile robots (AMRs) could benefit from WMNav's predictive capabilities to navigate efficiently, locate items, and avoid obstacles in novel or reconfigured layouts.
- **Search and Rescue:** Robots equipped with WMNav could explore unknown disaster zones to locate survivors or critical items more rapidly and safely, reducing human risk.
- **Exploration in Hazardous Environments:** For space exploration or hazardous material handling, robots need to operate with minimal human intervention in completely novel settings. WMNav's anticipatory planning offers a pathway for such autonomous exploration.

In conclusion, WMNav not only delivers state-of-the-art performance in ZSON but also pioneers a new architectural direction for embodied AI by demonstrating the profound advantages of integrating VLMs into world models. This research lays foundational groundwork for the next generation of intelligent robots capable of navigating and interacting with the world in a more intuitive, efficient, and robust manner.
