# Efficient Preference-Based Reinforcement Learning: Randomized Exploration Meets Experimental Design

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from SYCAMORE, EPFL introduce computationally efficient meta-algorithms for preference-based reinforcement learning, integrating randomized exploration with optimal experimental design. These algorithms provide strong theoretical guarantees for online regret minimization and last-iterate suboptimality, while empirically demonstrating a substantial reduction in the human feedback required for learning in both tabular and continuous control environments.

## Problem

- Designing accurate reward functions in Reinforcement Learning (RL) is challenging, leading to suboptimal or unsafe agent behaviors.
- Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) addresses this but existing theoretical approaches often rely on computationally intractable optimization problems for active query selection.
- Many current RLHF methods demand continuous human feedback, making them impractical for real-world applications due to high annotator workload.

## Method

- Developed a ==meta-algorithm== that reduces the RLHF problem to a standard RL problem by leveraging an existing RL oracle.
- Employs a randomized exploration scheme, inspired by Thompson sampling, to overcome the computational intractability of optimistic approaches in RLHF.
- Introduces optimal experimental design to actively select the most informative trajectory pairs for preference queries in batches, thereby reducing the total human feedback burden.

## Results

- RPO-Regret achieved a cumulative regret bound of O(√(κd^3 T log^3(dT/δ))), demonstrating improved dimensionality dependence over prior randomized exploration methods in RLHF.
- RPO-Explore provided a last-iterate suboptimality bound of O(√(κd^3 / T log^3(dT/δ))), representing the first tractable algorithm for efficient preference-free exploration with trajectory-level preferences.
- LRPO-OD-Regret maintained competitive regret while significantly reducing the number of preference queries to ~O(T / (d log T)) compared to round-by-round feedback, confirming the practical benefits of active query selection in empirical evaluations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Randomized exploration, when combined with a time-uniform confidence bound for the reward parameter, offers a tractable and theoretically sound approach for RLHF in general MDPs.
> - Integrating optimal experimental design into the RLHF loop allows for efficient batching of preference queries and intelligent selection of informative comparisons, drastically cutting down human annotation effort.
> - A meta-algorithmic reduction to a PAC-RL oracle provides a general framework for RLHF that can be applied across various underlying RL algorithms and MDP settings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved notable successes in various domains, including game playing, robotics, and autonomous driving. However, a significant practical challenge in RL is the design of reward functions that accurately reflect human objectives. Misspecified reward functions can lead to suboptimal or unsafe agent behaviors. This limitation has motivated the development of alternative paradigms for guiding agent learning.

Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) addresses this challenge by enabling agents to learn from human preference comparisons between trajectories, rather than from manually engineered reward signals. This approach often proves simpler for humans to provide and has demonstrated effectiveness in applications such as robotics and the fine-tuning of large language models.

Despite its empirical successes, the theoretical foundations of RLHF are still under development. Early theoretical work on preference-based learning focused on simpler settings like dueling bandits, where learners select pairs of actions and receive noisy preference feedback. Algorithms for this setting often involve zeroth-order optimization or the principle of optimism. Recent efforts in dueling bandits have aimed to reduce query complexity through optimal experimental design, though these methods are typically limited to finite-armed bandits due to the computational efficiency requirements of the underlying optimization problems.

In the more complex online RL setting, theoretical studies of RLHF have established guarantees in terms of cumulative regret or Probably Approximately Correct (PAC) learning. A recurring challenge in online RLHF is the active selection of informative trajectory comparisons to efficiently learn the underlying reward function. This problem is compounded in RL by two factors: first, the learner's inability to freely choose arbitrary state-action pairs or trajectories, as they must be reached through exploration; and second, the computational intractability of maximizing exploration bonuses that involve norms of state distributions, even in tabular settings. Some existing approaches with theoretical guarantees rely on such intractable maximization problems.

An alternative approach involves RLHF with offline data, where learning occurs over a fixed, pre-collected dataset of trajectory preferences. While this circumvents online exploration and active query selection, its effectiveness critically depends on the a priori availability of sufficiently diverse and informative preference data. This shifts the challenges of exploration and active learning to the data collection phase rather than resolving them.

Previous work has made progress on computational tractability. For instance, randomized exploration algorithms have been proposed for RLHF with regret guarantees in specific settings (e.g., linear dynamics) and have shown empirical efficiency in large language model fine-tuning. Additionally, general reductions from RLHF to standard RL have been introduced, offering PAC-style guarantees under the assumption of access to an RL oracle. However, these prior approaches generally have not simultaneously addressed the dual challenges of reducing the demand for human feedback and implementing tractable active query selection in general MDPs. This paper aims to contribute to filling this gap by developing computationally efficient RLHF algorithms that incorporate active query selection and reduce the human feedback burden, while providing theoretical guarantees.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper identifies a central open challenge in RLHF: the design of tractable algorithms for active preference query selection that can reduce the workload placed on human annotators. Existing theoretical approaches often rely on computationally intractable optimization problems for active exploration, making them difficult to deploy in practice. Furthermore, many current methods require human feedback at every learning round, which is impractical for real-world applications due to the continuous demand for annotator attention.

The primary objectives of this research are:
1. **Develop computationally efficient RLHF algorithms:** The aim is to create algorithms that can operate tractably, even in general Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), by avoiding the computational challenges associated with optimistic approaches.
2. **Reduce the demand for human feedback:** The authors seek to design algorithms that minimize the frequency and total number of preference queries required from human annotators.
3. **Implement active selection of informative queries:** The goal is to intelligently select trajectory pairs for comparison that yield the most valuable information for learning the underlying reward function, thereby improving sample efficiency.
4. **Provide theoretical guarantees:** The proposed algorithms are intended to come with rigorous guarantees, including regret bounds for online performance and last-iterate suboptimality guarantees for the quality of the final learned policy.

The motivation stems from the practical limitations of existing RLHF algorithms. Specifically, the intractability of maximizing exploration bonuses over policies in prior theoretical frameworks hinders their applicability. The necessity for continuous human feedback in many RLHF algorithms creates a bottleneck for deployment. This work proposes to address these issues through a meta-algorithmic approach that leverages randomized exploration, which is more tractable, and integrates experimental design principles for efficient query selection, thereby making RLHF more feasible for real-world deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this work revolves around a meta-algorithm that reduces the RLHF problem to a standard RL problem. This is achieved by combining existing RL algorithms (as an oracle) with randomized exploration and, for query efficiency, optimal experimental design. The approach is designed for general Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) with a linear reward model and utilizes a stochastic Bradley–Terry model for human preferences.

**Core Ingredients:**

1. **Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) for Reward Parameter:** The paper assumes a linear reward model, `r_θ*(s, a) = ⟨θ*, ϕ(s, a)⟩`, and a Bradley–Terry preference model where `P(τ ≻ τ') = σ(⟨θ*, ϕ(τ) - ϕ(τ')⟩)`. Given a dataset of trajectory pair comparisons `D_t = {(x_k, y_k)}`, where `x_k = ϕ(τ_k) - ϕ(τ'_k)` is the feature difference and `y_k` is the binary preference label, the reward parameter `θ*` is estimated via constrained MLE. The negative log-likelihood function `L_{D_t}(θ)` is the logistic loss, which is a convex problem solvable by standard optimization methods. The paper establishes a time-uniform confidence result (Lemma 3.1) showing that `θ*` lies within an ellipsoid around the MLE `ˆθ_t` with high probability. The confidence bound `β_t(δ)` includes a factor `√κ`, where `κ` relates to the Lipschitz constant of the inverse sigmoid function, arising from preference-based feedback. This `√κ` factor is noted as an improvement over prior work that incurred a larger factor of `κ`. The confidence sets are based on `V_t = λI + Σ x_k x_k^⊤`, which allows for efficient rank-one updates, unlike Hessian-based approaches.

2. **Randomized Exploration:** To overcome the computational intractability of optimistic approaches in RLHF (which require maximizing an exploration bonus over policies), the algorithms adopt a randomized exploration scheme inspired by Thompson sampling. This involves sampling a reward parameter `~θ_t` from a Gaussian distribution. This distribution is centered either at the current MLE `ˆθ_t` (for regret minimization) or at zero (for pure exploration), with a covariance structure related to `V_t^{-1}` and scaled by the confidence bound `β_t(δ)`. This randomized strategy is shown to ensure a constant probability of optimism, which is key to bounding regret.

3. **RL Oracle Assumption:** The framework assumes access to an `(ε, δ)`-PAC RL oracle, `A_PAC_RL`. This oracle, given a reward parameter `θ`, an error tolerance `ε`, and a probability `δ`, returns a policy `π` such that `V_θ* - V_π_θ ≤ ε` with probability `1 - δ`. This is considered a mild assumption, satisfied in various RL settings (e.g., tabular and linear MDPs), and can be practically instantiated using policy optimization methods like PPO or Soft Actor-Critic.

**Algorithm Variants:**

The paper proposes three specific meta-algorithms:

1. **RPO-Regret (Algorithm 1):** This is an online preference learning algorithm for regret minimization.
- In each round `t`, it samples `~θ_t` from `N(ˆθ_t, β_t^2(δ') V_t^{-1})`.
- It then uses the `A_PAC_RL` oracle to compute `π_t = A_PAC_RL(~θ_t, ε, δ'/T)`.
- The comparator policy `π'_t` for preference query is simply `π_{t-1}` (the policy from the previous round).
- Trajectories `τ_t` and `τ'_t` are executed, and their feature difference `x_t = ϕ(τ_t) - ϕ(τ'_t)` is calculated.
- A single binary preference `y_t` is queried from the human oracle.
- The MLE `ˆθ_{t+1}` is updated using the augmented dataset `D_{t+1}`.
- This algorithm balances exploration and exploitation by iteratively updating the reward estimate and policies based on online feedback.

2. **RPO-Explore (Algorithm 2):** This variant focuses on preference-free exploration followed by batched reward estimation, aiming for last-iterate suboptimality.
- It samples `~θ_t` centered at zero: `N(0, V_t^{-1})`.
- Policies `π_t` are computed using `A_PAC_RL(~θ_t, ε, δ'/T)`.
- Trajectory pairs `(τ_t, τ'_t)` and their feature differences `x_t` are collected but preference feedback is *deferred*.
- At the end of `T` rounds, a single batch `B` of all collected trajectory pairs is processed.
- All preference queries are made concurrently for this single batch.
- A final MLE `ˆθ` is computed from this batch `D`, and an output policy `ˆπ = A_PAC_RL(ˆθ, ε, δ')` is returned.
- This approach avoids online human feedback during exploration but may incur high regret during the exploration phase.

3. **LRPO-OD-Regret (Algorithm 3):** This improved algorithm addresses the practical limitations of Algorithm 1, specifically the continuous feedback requirement and potentially uninformative queries, while maintaining regret guarantees.
- **Lazy Updates:** It employs a lazy update scheme similar to linear bandits. Trajectory pairs `(τ_t, τ'_t)` are collected into a temporary buffer `D` without immediate preference queries or MLE updates.
- An MLE update, preference querying, and policy re-evaluation (for `π'`) occur only when the determinant of the current design matrix `W_t` (accumulating all `x_t x_t^⊤` from the current batch) significantly exceeds the determinant of the last updated design matrix `V_{t_stop}` by a factor `(1+C)`. This effectively batches preference queries.
- **D-Optimal Design:** When an update condition is met, an "optimal design" subroutine (Algorithm 4) is applied to the collected batch `D`. This greedy algorithm selects a subset `D_opt` of informative trajectory pairs from `D` by maximizing `log det(V_{t_stop} + Σ_{x∈D_opt} x x^⊤)`. This process identifies the most informative comparisons, reducing the total number of preference queries.
- The selected `D_opt` is then used to update the MLE `ˆθ_t`.
- The batch structure also enables parallelization of preference queries, which is relevant for human annotators.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides both theoretical guarantees and empirical validation for the proposed algorithms.

**Theoretical Guarantees:**

1. **RPO-Regret (Algorithm 1):**
- This algorithm, designed for online regret minimization, achieves a sublinear cumulative regret bound.
- Specifically, with high probability (`1-δ`), the cumulative regret `R(T)` is `O(√(κd^3 T log^3(dT/δ)))`.
- This bound matches existing results for algorithms with randomized exploration in RL concerning its dependence on `T`, and includes an additional `√κ` factor due to preference-based feedback, which aligns with recent RLHF literature.
- Compared to prior work, this analysis improves the dimensionality dependence from `d^3` to `d^(3/2)` relative to some randomized exploration approaches (e.g., Wu and Sun) and applies to general MDPs under an RL oracle assumption, rather than being restricted to specific dynamics (e.g., linear MDPs). It also avoids the need for value function truncation.

2. **RPO-Explore (Algorithm 2):**
- This algorithm focuses on preference-free exploration and batched reward estimation, aiming to identify a high-quality policy at the end.
- With high probability (`1-δ`), the suboptimality of the output policy `ˆπ` is bounded by `SubOpt(ˆπ) = O(√(κd^3 / T log^3(dT/δ)))`.
- This implies that `~O(κd^3/ε^2)` iterations are required to output an `ε`-optimal policy.
- Algorithm 2 is positioned as the first tractable algorithm for efficient preference-free exploration with trajectory-level preferences, and the first to use randomized exploration for this purpose in either preference- or reward-based RL.
- It improves the dependence on `κ` compared to prior meta-algorithms that interface with a PAC-RL oracle (e.g., Wang et al., which showed `O(κ^2 d^3 / ε^2)` queries). While some approaches achieve `O(d^2)` dependence on `d` (e.g., Zhan et al.), they rely on intractable maximization oracles, whereas RPO-Explore remains tractable. The `√d` factor in RPO-Explore is an expected consequence of using randomized instead of optimistic exploration.

3. **LRPO-OD-Regret (Algorithm 3):**
- This practical variant, integrating lazy updates and optimal design, also provides regret guarantees.
- With high probability (`1-δ`), the cumulative regret `R(T)` is `O(√((1+C)κd^3 T log^3(dT/δ)))`. This bound shows that the regret increases only by constant factors compared to Algorithm 1.
- A key finding is that the number of times the MLE `ˆθ` is updated (and thus preference queries are collected) is at most `O(d/log(1+C) log(1 + T(2LHγ)^2 / (dλ)))`. This demonstrates that the average size of a preference batch is `~O(T / (d log T))`, significantly reducing the total number of queries compared to round-by-round feedback.
- The optimal design subroutine helps in selecting informative queries, potentially leading to fewer queries in practice despite worst-case lower bounds.

**Empirical Evaluation:**

The algorithms were empirically evaluated in both a tabular gridworld environment and a continuous control task.

1. **Tabular Gridworld:**
- RPO-Regret (Algorithm 1) was compared against an RLHF baseline that uses entropy regularization for exploration.
- RPO-Regret demonstrated considerably lower cumulative regret and reduced variance across multiple runs compared to the entropy-based baseline. This result indicates that entropy-based exploration alone may not be sufficient for achieving low regret in MDPs when learning from preferences.

2. **Continuous Control (Isaac-Cartpole-v0):**
- RPO-Regret, LRPO-Regret (Algorithm 3 without optimal design), and LRPO-OD-Regret (Algorithm 3 with optimal design) were tested on balancing a pole on a cart using PPO as the RL oracle and synthetic preferences derived from the environment's reward function.
- All three algorithms exhibited sublinear regret, with their performance flattening after a few RLHF rounds, indicating competitiveness with reward-based RL (which uses the ground truth reward function).
- LRPO-OD-Regret, while showing slightly higher initial regret, quickly converged to similar performance levels as RPO-Regret and LRPO-Regret. Crucially, LRPO-OD-Regret significantly reduced the number of preference queries required to achieve this performance. This finding suggests that actively selecting informative queries using optimal design can substantially decrease human annotation effort in practical settings.
- Additional experiments (in Appendix F) with RPO-Explore variants showed that they achieve competitive final policy performance compared to ground truth RL, with LRPO-OD-Explore requiring the fewest preference queries, despite the inherent high regret during the exploration phase of such algorithms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work represents an advance in the field of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback by proposing meta-algorithms that integrate strong theoretical guarantees with practical considerations for real-world deployment.

The primary significance lies in addressing key limitations that have hindered the widespread adoption of RLHF:
1. **Computational Tractability:** By leveraging randomized exploration, the proposed algorithms bypass the computational intractability associated with optimistic exploration strategies in complex RL environments. This makes theoretical RLHF approaches more amenable to practical implementation.
2. **Reduced Human Feedback Burden:** Algorithm 3, LRPO-OD-Regret, specifically addresses the impracticality of continuous human feedback by incorporating lazy updates and optimal experimental design. This enables batching of queries and intelligent selection of the most informative comparisons, leading to a significant reduction in the total number of preference queries without sacrificing performance. This feature is crucial for scalability in real-world applications where human annotator time is a valuable and limited resource.
3. **Comprehensive Theoretical Guarantees:** The paper provides a robust theoretical foundation for its algorithms, offering both regret bounds for online learning and PAC-style suboptimality guarantees for the final policy quality. Notably, RPO-Explore (Algorithm 2) introduces a tractable method for preference-free exploration with trajectory-level preferences, a novel contribution to the field.
4. **Meta-Algorithm Framework:** The use of an RL oracle allows the framework to be general, accommodating various underlying RL algorithms and MDP settings (tabular, linear, or potentially deep RL with policy optimization methods). This modularity enhances its applicability.

The potential impact of this research is in broadening the applicability of RLHF to more complex and resource-constrained environments. By making RLHF algorithms more computationally efficient and less demanding on human annotators, this work contributes to overcoming a significant barrier to deploying human-in-the-loop learning systems in domains such as robotics, autonomous systems, and large language model alignment. The empirical results demonstrate that these theoretical advancements translate into practical benefits, with the proposed methods performing comparably to reward-based RL while requiring substantially fewer human inputs.

Future research directions, as acknowledged by the authors, include extending the framework to richer preference models beyond the Bradley-Terry model, investigating methods to reduce the number of RL oracle calls (which can still be computationally intensive), and validating performance on more complex tasks with genuine human or LLM-generated feedback to fully assess practical applicability.
