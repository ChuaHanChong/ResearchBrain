# RoboStereo: Dual-Tower 4D Embodied World Models for Unified Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboStereo presents a dual-tower 4D embodied world model that generates geometrically consistent future states and a unified framework for policy optimization. This system enables pre-execution verification, expert imitation, and open exploration within imagined rollouts, leading to over 97% relative success rate uplift in simulation and 65% in real-world robotic tasks.

## Problem

- Scaling Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models is constrained by the high cost, slowness, and safety risks associated with real-world robot interactions.
- Existing Embodied World Models (EWMs) struggle with 3D geometric inconsistencies and "physics hallucinations," limiting their reliability for complex manipulation planning.
- A comprehensive, unified framework for leveraging high-fidelity EWMs across the full spectrum of policy optimization, from verification to evolutionary refinement, was previously lacking.

## Method

- Developed **RoboStereo**, a symmetric ==dual-tower Diffusion Transformer== architecture that processes both ==RGB videos== and ==3D pointmaps== with ==bidirectional cross-attention== for spatiotemporal geometric consistency.
- Introduced a unified policy optimization framework with three paradigms: ==Test-Time Policy Augmentation (TTPA)== for verification, ==Imitative-Evolutionary Policy Learning (IEPL)== for expert alignment, and ==Open-Exploration Policy Learning (OEPL)== for autonomous skill discovery.
- Integrated a ==4D Gaussian Splatting== head for photorealistic novel-view synthesis and a ==frame-level action conditioning== mechanism for precise control over generated trajectories.

## Results

- RoboStereo demonstrated top performance across Physics Adherence and 3D Accuracy dimensions on the WorldArena benchmark, ranking first or second on the majority of **16** evaluation metrics compared to eight state-of-the-art EWMs.
- The unified policy optimization framework achieved a cumulative relative uplift of over **97%** in average success rate in simulation and improved real-world robotic arm task success from **30%** (baseline) to **65%**.
- Individual policy paradigms showed notable relative improvements: TTPA increased success rates by over **56%**, IEPL by over **55%**, and OEPL by over **72%** in simulated manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bidirectional cross-attention between RGB video and 3D pointmap towers effectively integrates visual semantics with geometric constraints, enabling 4D world model generation with high physical and geometric accuracy.
> - A unified framework allows for a complete lifecycle of policy optimization in a simulated environment, from pre-execution validation to iterative skill acquisition, reducing reliance on physical interaction.
> - Leveraging dense visual imitation rewards and discriminator-based supervision within a high-fidelity world model facilitates emergent fine-grained geometric reasoning and self-correction behaviors in VLA policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of general-purpose robotic manipulation systems, often facilitated by Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, represents a significant area of research in artificial intelligence. These models aim to enable robots to interpret open-ended instructions and execute diverse tasks in dynamic physical environments. However, a fundamental challenge in scaling VLA models is the inherent cost, slowness, and safety risks associated with real-world robot interactions. This constraint impacts both the collection of extensive training data and the safe verification and refinement of policies during deployment.

To address these limitations, Embodied World Models (EWMs) have emerged as a prominent research direction. EWMs function as "differentiable digital twins," capable of predicting future observations conditioned on robot actions, thereby allowing agents to plan, verify, and optimize policies within simulated, imagined rollouts. Despite their potential, existing EWMs encounter two primary limitations:
1. **Geometric Inconsistencies:** Many current EWMs struggle to maintain 3D geometric consistency, often resulting in "physics hallucinations" such as object teleportation, scale drift, or surface penetration. While these models might produce visually plausible frames, the absence of explicit geometric grounding limits their reliability for complex, long-horizon manipulation planning. Prior work has attempted to integrate 3D or 4D structural constraints, such as incorporating geometric branches, using RGB-Depth-Normal streams, or employing 3D Gaussian Splatting, but challenges remain in achieving robust consistency.
2. **Lack of Unified Policy Optimization Frameworks:** Although some research has explored world-model-based policy improvement techniques, these efforts have largely been isolated. A comprehensive, unified framework that can leverage high-fidelity EWMs for the full spectrum of policy optimization, ranging from inference-time verification to training-time evolutionary refinement, has been absent.

This work, RoboStereo, is positioned to address both of these identified limitations. It aims to develop an EWM that ensures spatiotemporal geometric consistency and to introduce a unified framework for policy optimization, thereby enhancing the scalability and reliability of VLA systems by decoupling policy development from direct, costly physical interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop RoboStereo, a dual-tower 4D embodied world model, and integrate it into a unified policy optimization framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This initiative is motivated by critical challenges in scaling embodied artificial intelligence.

Detailed motivations include:

1. **Addressing the Constraints of Real-World Interaction:** A core motivation stems from the prohibitive costs, slow pace, and safety risks associated with training and deploying VLA models in physical environments. Real-world data collection for diverse manipulation tasks is resource-intensive and often requires human supervision, limiting the scale and variety of available training data. Furthermore, verifying and refining robotic actions during deployment carries inherent safety risks, potentially leading to damage to the robot, objects, or the environment. The research aims to shift the burden of policy optimization from physical interaction to a simulated environment.

2. **Overcoming Geometric Hallucinations in Existing Embodied World Models (EWMs):** While EWMs offer a promising avenue for simulated learning and planning, current approaches exhibit limitations in generating physically consistent and geometrically accurate future states. Existing EWMs may produce visually convincing outputs but frequently suffer from "physics hallucinations," such as objects appearing to pass through surfaces, undergo unphysical deformations, or abruptly change position. These inconsistencies arise from a lack of explicit geometric grounding within the models, rendering them unreliable as accurate "digital twins" for robust robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks requiring fine-grained interaction and precise object handling. The objective is to enhance the fidelity of world models by ensuring spatiotemporal geometric consistency.

3. **Establishing a Unified Framework for World-Model-Based Policy Optimization:** Prior research has explored various techniques for improving robotic policies using world models, including planning, verification, and reinforcement learning. However, these methods have typically been applied in isolation, without a cohesive framework that spans the entire lifecycle of policy optimization. There is a need for a comprehensive system that can leverage a high-fidelity EWM for diverse optimization needs, from pre-execution validation during inference to iterative skill discovery and refinement during training. The motivation is to integrate disparate optimization strategies into a single, comprehensive framework.

In summary, the key objectives are:
- To develop a 4D world model, RoboStereo, that rigorously enforces 3D geometric consistency and temporal coherence, thereby mitigating physics hallucinations.
- To introduce the first unified framework for world-model-based policy optimization that encompasses strategies for inference-time action verification (Test-Time Policy Augmentation), training-time learning from expert demonstrations (Imitative-Evolutionary Policy Learning), and autonomous skill discovery (Open-Exploration Policy Learning).
- To enable VLA policy improvement and continuous self-correction by leveraging scalable imagined rollouts within this high-fidelity 4D world model, thereby reducing reliance on expensive and risky physical interactions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research involves the development of RoboStereo, a symmetric dual-tower 4D world model, and the construction of a unified framework for policy optimization that utilizes this model.

#### 3.1. RoboStereo Architecture Design

RoboStereo is structured around a symmetric dual-tower Diffusion Transformer (DiT) framework designed to process both RGB videos and 3D pointmaps.

- **Symmetric Dual-Tower DiT Framework:** The core architecture consists of two identical DiT backbones. One tower specializes in processing RGB video sequences, while the other handles 3D pointmaps, which are represented as 3-channel XYZ coordinate videos. Both towers are initialized with weights from Cosmos 2.5 and designed to share identical latent dimensions, allowing for homogeneous encoding via Video Autoencoders (VAEs) without the need for additional alignment layers.
- **Bidirectional Cross-Attention:** A key feature of the architecture is the implementation of paired cross-attention layers within each DiT. This design facilitates bidirectional information flow: the video branch incorporates geometric constraints by querying the pointmap tower, and concurrently, the pointmap branch refines object geometry by leveraging semantic context from the video tower. This symmetric fusion mechanism aims to ground generated pixels in a consistent 3D space, promoting spatiotemporal geometric consistency and producing temporally coherent 4D pointcloud trajectories.
- **4D Gaussian Head:** Following the generation of RGB-XYZ pairs, a dedicated 4D Gaussian Splatting head is utilized to render these trajectories into photorealistic observations. This head supports flexible novel-view synthesis, which is beneficial for multi-view supervision during policy learning.
- **Frame-Level Action Control:** To achieve precise control over generated trajectories, RoboStereo integrates a dual-path action-conditioned timestep embedding mechanism. Action vectors, representing continuous robot movements (e.g., changes in position, orientation, and gripper width), are processed by two independent Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs). One MLP implicitly fuses with the diffusion timestep embedding, while the other explicitly predicts modulation offsets. These offsets are then combined with timestep-derived parameters to form Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN) coefficients, which are applied within each Transformer block. This explicit modulation aims to enforce frame-level structural guidance, ensuring generated dynamics strictly adhere to the input action sequences.

#### 3.2. Training Strategy for World Model

The training of RoboStereo follows a two-stage paradigm:

- **Data Construction:** A large-scale 4D robotic manipulation dataset is constructed based on Bridge V2, comprising approximately 20,000 third-person videos of diverse kitchen tasks. Videos are standardized to 320x256 resolution at 5 frames per second (FPS). Each frame is associated with a 7D action vector. DepthAnything V3 is used to predict depth maps, which are then converted into 3-channel XYZ pointmaps via inverse perspective projection, maintaining pixel-level correspondence with RGB frames. This unified representation supports paired training of visual and geometric streams.
- **Independent Training (Stage 1):** Both RGB video and 3D pointmap towers are initialized with Cosmos 2.5 pretrained weights and then independently fine-tuned on their respective modality-specific data. This stage uses VAEs to encode inputs into latent representations, samples timesteps and Gaussian noise for Rectified Flow, and trains each model to predict velocity fields via an MSE loss. The objective is for each branch to learn temporal coherence in video generation and geometric accuracy in pointmap prediction.
- **Joint Training (Stage 2):** In this stage, the independently trained towers are integrated. To manage memory, only the self-attention and cross-attention modules are fine-tuned, while Feed-Forward Network (FFN) layers are frozen. Paired data latents are used, but independent Gaussian noise is injected with a shared timestep. Each backbone predicts velocity fields conditioned on its noisy latent, timestep, task conditions, and, critically, the intermediate latent from the *other* modality through bidirectional cross-attention. The overall objective is a weighted sum of the flow matching losses from both modalities (λ_v = 0.85, λ_p = 0.15). This process facilitates implicit correspondence learning between visual semantics and geometric structures.

#### 3.3. World Model-Based Policy Optimization Framework

RoboStereo functions as a learned world model (p_φ) that predicts future visual observations conditioned on current states and action chunks. This enables policy evaluation and optimization within an imagined latent space. The framework comprises three paradigms:

1. **Test-Time Policy Augmentation (TTPA):** This is a zero-shot, pre-execution mechanism designed to verify and refine candidate action sequences. A VLA policy proposes an action sequence, which RoboStereo then simulates to generate an imagined visual trajectory. A separate video understanding model evaluates this trajectory against the task goal, yielding a success confidence score. The action chunk is executed physically only if this confidence exceeds a predefined threshold; otherwise, a replanning step is triggered without real-world interaction, aiming to reduce physical trial costs and enhance safety.

2. **Imitative-Evolutionary Policy Learning (IEPL):** This paradigm leverages RoboStereo's 4D generative capabilities to establish dense visual imitation reward signals from flexible camera viewpoints for policy learning, particularly from expert demonstrations.
 - **Reward Function Design:** Paired 4D imagined rollouts are generated for both expert actions and current policy actions from identical camera viewpoints. The per-step imitation reward is calculated as the negative perceptual distance (LPIPS) between corresponding imagined frames. This provides dense, differentiable rewards that capture nuanced task semantics.
 - **GRPO Optimization:** Policy optimization is performed using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). This involves sampling multiple trajectories, computing their cumulative rewards based on the LPIPS metric, normalizing advantages using group statistics, and updating the policy by minimizing a clipped surrogate objective.
 - **Iterative Rollout Process:** The VLA policy and expert actions iteratively generate paired imagined trajectories in RoboStereo. Every k-th frame of the policy's imagined trajectory is fed back as an updated observation, creating a closed-loop imitation learning process.

3. **Open-Exploration Policy Learning (OEPL):** OEPL enables autonomous skill discovery and self-correction in the absence of expert demonstrations.
 - **Reward Function Design:** A video understanding model serves as a reward model, trained to predict task completion likelihood from visual clips. For an imagined trajectory, it is segmented into overlapping clips, and the reward model assigns a success confidence to each. The task-level cumulative reward aggregates these clip-level predictions using a temporally increasing weight, encouraging progression towards task completion.
 - **GRPO Optimization:** Similar to IEPL, policy optimization is conducted via GRPO, maximizing the rewards generated by the discriminator-based reward model.
 - **Iterative Rollout Process:** The VLA policy predicts action sequences, and RoboStereo generates imagined visual trajectories. Every k-th frame is fed back as an updated observation for closed-loop exploration. Trajectories are evaluated, and the policy is updated to maximize rewards. This decouples policy optimization from physical interaction and allows learning from failures in a safe simulated environment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from comprehensive experiments evaluating both the world model's generation quality and the efficacy of the unified policy optimization framework.

#### 4.1. Video Quality Evaluation of RoboStereo

RoboStereo's video generation capabilities were evaluated against eight state-of-the-art embodied world models using the WorldArena benchmark, encompassing 16 metrics across six sub-dimensions: visual quality, motion quality, content consistency, physics adherence, 3D accuracy, and controllability.

- **Quantitative Performance:** RoboStereo demonstrated strong performance, ranking first or second on the majority of the 16 metrics. Notable advancements were observed in evaluations related to structural integrity and interaction fidelity. Specifically, RoboStereo achieved top performance across all metrics within the Physics Adherence and 3D Accuracy dimensions, indicating its ability to preserve physical plausibility and geometric consistency during complex interactions. In contrast, several baseline models, while providing competitive visual fidelity, often exhibited semantic drift and dynamics that diverged from specified actions.
- **Qualitative Performance:** Visualizations corroborated the quantitative results, showing RoboStereo's ability to model fine-grained robotic interactions. It generated high-fidelity 4D scene evolutions that closely aligned with ground-truth dynamics, demonstrating both visual fidelity and geometric consistency across various scenarios.
- **Architectural Contributions to Performance:** The consistent performance of RoboStereo is attributed to its design. The symmetric dual-tower Diffusion Transformer (DiT) with bidirectional cross-attention enables mutual feature integration: geometric constraints from the pointmap tower refine RGB semantics, while visual and motion cues from the RGB tower enhance structural reasoning. Additionally, the frame-level action conditioning mechanism provides fine-grained temporal control, ensuring generated dynamics adhere to input actions without temporal drift.

#### 4.2. Policy Optimization Evaluation

The effectiveness of RoboStereo as a high-fidelity world model for VLA policy enhancement was evaluated across the three proposed paradigms: Test-Time Policy Augmentation (TTPA), Imitative-Evolutionary Policy Learning (IEPL), and Open-Exploration Policy Learning (OEPL). Experiments were conducted in the MimicGen simulator on three fine-grained manipulation tasks. An OpenVLA-OFT model served as the base policy.

- **Test-Time Policy Augmentation (TTPA):** TTPA delivered a relative success rate uplift of over 56% compared to the base policy. By simulating and verifying action sequences in RoboStereo before physical execution, TTPA enhanced deployment reliability without altering the policy weights.
- **Imitative-Evolutionary Policy Learning (IEPL):** IEPL achieved a relative improvement of over 55% in success rates, outperforming both online Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and offline Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) under identical interaction budgets. This gain is attributed to RoboStereo’s visual simulation enabling dense visual perceptual rewards for evolutionary updates, activating the VLA’s pre-trained visual priors and providing fine-grained guidance.
- **Open-Exploration Policy Learning (OEPL):** OEPL yielded a relative improvement of over 72% in success rates, also surpassing online GRPO and offline DPO. This performance is linked to its ability to drive autonomous skill discovery through discriminator-based supervision, fostering self-correction capabilities. The improvements from IEPL and OEPL were particularly evident in contact-rich tasks.
- **Combined Framework Performance:** Integrating all three paradigms resulted in a cumulative relative uplift of over 97% in average success rate, demonstrating the complementary nature of these components.
- **Qualitative Policy Analysis:**
 - **IEPL:** Enabled emergent fine-grained geometric reasoning. While a baseline policy exhibited coarse actuation and failed in precise insertion tasks, the IEPL-optimized policy performed continuous micro-adjustments for accurate part insertion.
 - **OEPL:** Fostered emergent self-correction. The base policy showed open-loop failure propagation (e.g., continuing a trajectory after a missed grasp). The OEPL-optimized policy autonomously detected grasp slips and initiated corrective re-grasping maneuvers.
- **Inference Efficiency:** RoboStereo demonstrated an inference speed of 1.50 FPS, which is competitive with and often superior to other baselines, supporting scalable closed-loop trajectory generation essential for the optimization framework.
- **Real-World Deployment:** Real-world experiments using a robotic arm on a desktop-clearing task showed that the baseline policy achieved a 30% success rate, while the jointly optimized policy (IEPL + OEPL) achieved a 65% success rate. The optimized policy exhibited self-correction for grasp slippage and fine-grained micro-adjustments during re-grasping, behaviors not present in the imitation learning baseline.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study confirmed the impact of key design choices:

- **Symmetric Dual-Tower Joint Training:** Joint training, as opposed to training only the RGB tower, resulted in substantial improvements in physics- and geometry-critical metrics (e.g., +5.9% in Interaction Quality, +7.3% in Depth Accuracy). This indicates the effectiveness of bidirectional cross-attention in integrating RGB semantics with 3D pointmap constraints.
- **Frame-Level Action Conditioning:** The proposed action-conditioned timestep embedding modulation achieved superior action alignment and motion coherence compared to alternative methods like channel concatenation or cross-attention, demonstrating its ability to provide precise per-frame control without disrupting pre-trained features.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents RoboStereo, a dual-tower 4D embodied world model, and a unified policy optimization framework, contributing to the field of Embodied AI by addressing several fundamental challenges.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

1. **Advancement in World Model Fidelity:** RoboStereo represents an advancement in the development of high-fidelity embodied world models. By employing a symmetric dual-tower architecture with bidirectional cross-modal enhancement, it effectively enforces spatiotemporal geometric consistency and mitigates "physics hallucinations" that often plague existing models. This improved fidelity in 4D generation makes RoboStereo a more reliable "digital twin" for robotic systems, enhancing the accuracy of imagined rollouts for planning and verification.

2. **Introduction of a Unified Policy Optimization Framework:** The establishment of the first unified framework for world-model-based policy optimization is a key contribution. This framework integrates three distinct yet complementary paradigms—Test-Time Policy Augmentation (TTPA), Imitative-Evolutionary Policy Learning (IEPL), and Open-Exploration Policy Learning (OEPL)—to cover the full spectrum of policy improvement needs. This holistic approach provides a structured methodology for leveraging world models from inference-time validation to training-time skill acquisition.

3. **Enhanced Safety and Efficiency in Robotic Deployment:**
 - **TTPA** offers a mechanism for zero-shot pre-execution verification, allowing policies to detect potential failures and replan without physically interacting with the environment. This capability can significantly reduce safety risks and the financial cost associated with real-world trials and errors.
 - **IEPL** and **OEPL** enable policy learning and refinement within the simulated environment, substantially decoupling the optimization burden from expensive and risky physical interactions. This paradigm shifts the bulk of trial-and-error learning to a scalable, safe, and efficient digital realm.

4. **Improved Policy Learning Capabilities:**
 - **IEPL** leverages RoboStereo's high-fidelity visual simulation to provide dense visual perceptual rewards, which are more informative than sparse binary rewards. This facilitates more efficient alignment of VLA policies with expert behavior, combining the sample efficiency of imitation learning with the adaptability of reinforcement learning.
 - **OEPL** supports autonomous skill discovery and self-correction without relying on explicit expert demonstrations. This fosters robustness in complex, open-ended task spaces, allowing VLA agents to adapt to novel situations and recover from suboptimal states.

5. **Unlocking Continuous Self-Improvement for VLAs:** By providing a closed-loop system where policies can be continuously optimized and refined within a high-fidelity imagined simulator, RoboStereo establishes a foundational infrastructure for the scalable and generalizable improvement of VLA models. This framework facilitates iterative learning cycles, leading to more capable and adaptable robotic agents.

6. **Demonstrated Practical Applicability:** The validation of the framework through comprehensive experiments in simulation and successful qualitative deployment on a real-world robotic arm indicates its practical utility and robustness beyond theoretical constructs. The emergent self-correction and fine-grained geometric reasoning observed in optimized policies highlight the potential for more robust and dexterous robot control.

In conclusion, RoboStereo and its unified optimization framework represent a step toward making embodied AI more scalable, safer, and efficient, fostering the development of VLA models capable of continuous self-improvement in complex manipulation tasks.
