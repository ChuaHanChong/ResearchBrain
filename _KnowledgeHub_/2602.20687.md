# How Foundational Skills Influence VLM-based Embodied Agents:A Native Perspective

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Science and Technology of China and Alibaba Group introduced NativeEmbodied, a benchmark for VLM-based embodied agents that uses a low-level action space and a decoupled task hierarchy. The evaluation reveals that while models demonstrate strong perception (GPT-o3 F1: 83.97%) and planning, they face significant challenges in fine-grained spatial alignment and navigation, with many proprietary models achieving single-digit success rates in alignment tasks.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks for VLM-based embodied agents often rely on 'non-native' high-level action spaces, abstracting low-level skills and creating a disparity with real-world operational conditions.
- Current evaluations focus on coupled, high-level tasks with overall success rates, making it difficult to pinpoint specific foundational skill deficiencies and diagnose performance bottlenecks.
- A lack of comprehensive understanding regarding which foundational skills are truly essential and how they impact higher-level task execution for VLM-based agents.

## Method

- Introduces ==NativeEmbodied==, a benchmark employing a 'native' low-level, primitive action space (e.g., 'MoveAhead x meters', 'RotateRight x degrees') derived from AI2THOR, allowing for fine-grained control.
- Features a ==decoupled task hierarchy== with three complex high-level tasks (Exploration, Search, Interaction) and four isolated low-level tasks (Perception, Spatial Alignment, Navigation, Planning) to assess specific foundational skills.
- Utilizes an automatic data generation pipeline with human-machine collaborative filtering, built upon AI2-THOR, to create high-quality, challenging scenarios for evaluation.

## Results

- On high-level tasks, even leading VLMs achieved limited success: the best model (GPT-o3) reached **34.64%** success in Search, **38.34%** in Interaction, and **52.43%** in Exploration.
- In low-level skills, models performed comparatively well in Perception (GPT-o3 F1: **83.97%**) and Planning (GPT-o3 SR: **72.54%**) but struggled significantly with Spatial Alignment (only GPT-o3 above **50%** SR) and Navigation (over half of models below **50%** SR).
- Ablation studies confirmed that navigation and planning are dual bottlenecks for long-horizon tasks, while spatial alignment is crucial for search; enabling 'think mode' improved performance on perception and planning but led to a decrease in success rates for precise actions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current VLMs show strong perceptual understanding and planning capabilities but exhibit pronounced deficiencies in fine-grained spatial alignment and precise navigation, which are critical bottlenecks for complex embodied tasks.
> - Long-horizon tasks (Exploration, Interaction) are dually bottlenecked by both cognitive-level planning and action-level navigation capabilities.
> - While explicit 'think mode' reasoning enhances cognitive abilities for perception and planning, it can paradoxically interfere with the intuitive and precise execution of fundamental motor controls like alignment and navigation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied intelligence, particularly with the integration of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), has seen significant advancements aimed at developing agents capable of operating in simulated or physical environments. These VLM-based agents are designed to perceive visual inputs and execute complex tasks based on natural language instructions. However, a persistent challenge in this domain is the robust and comprehensive assessment of these agents' true capabilities, particularly concerning their ability to function effectively in real-world scenarios.

Existing benchmarks for embodied agents, such as ALFRED, ALFWorld, VLMbench, Behavior-1k, Lota-bench, GOAT-bench, Embodied Agent Interface, EmbodiedBench, and EmbodiedEval, have contributed to progress but exhibit several limitations. A common issue is the reliance on "non-native" action spaces, where low-level actions are abstracted into high-level commands or functions (e.g., "teleport to the desk"). This abstraction simplifies task reasoning and planning but may obscure the agents' proficiency in fundamental embodied skills like spatial alignment and navigation, thereby creating a divergence from real-world operational conditions. Furthermore, many benchmarks primarily focus on high-level, entangled tasks, evaluating overall success rates without providing granular insights into specific skill deficiencies. This coarse-grained evaluation makes it challenging to diagnose the underlying bottlenecks that limit agent performance.

This research addresses these limitations by proposing a benchmark that emphasizes a "native" perspective. It aims to bridge the gap between abstract benchmark settings and real-world conditions by employing a low-level, primitive action space. Concurrently, it seeks to move beyond mere overall task success by decoupling complex tasks into foundational skills, enabling a more diagnostic and fine-grained evaluation. This approach contributes to a more comprehensive understanding of VLM-driven embodied agents' capabilities and limitations, informing future research directions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to address two fundamental questions regarding VLM-based embodied agents:
- **Q1: Which foundational skills are truly essential for VLM-based embodied agents?**
- **Q2: How do these foundational skills affect the execution of higher-level tasks?**

The motivation to pursue these objectives stems from the identified limitations in current VLM-driven embodied agent evaluation benchmarks, which hinder a thorough understanding of agent capabilities. Specifically, these limitations are:

1. **Non-Native Action Space:** Existing benchmarks frequently employ high-level commands or discretized action spaces. For instance, an agent might receive a command like "look at the apple" or "teleport to the desk." While simplifying task formulation and focusing on reasoning, this abstraction bypasses the direct assessment of crucial low-level embodied skills such as fine-grained movement, precise rotation, and collision avoidance. This "non-native" setting creates a considerable disparity between benchmark performance and the demands of real-world physical interaction, where agents must operate using continuous or primitive actions. The motivation is to introduce a native action space that directly mirrors the elemental movements available in a physical environment, thereby providing a more realistic and unconstrained evaluation.

2. **Coupled Task Design:** Current benchmarks often feature high-level tasks that intrinsically intertwine multiple foundational skills. Performance is typically measured by a single overall success rate. This aggregated evaluation makes it difficult to pinpoint specific skill-level deficiencies. For example, a failure in a complex "make coffee" task could be due to poor navigation, inability to manipulate objects, or flawed planning. Without a mechanism to isolate and evaluate individual skills, diagnosing the precise bottleneck becomes challenging. The motivation is to decouple these entangled skills by designing both complex high-level tasks and corresponding low-level tasks, each targeting a specific fundamental embodied skill. This hierarchical evaluation system aims to provide a more comprehensive, granular, and explainable assessment of an agent's capabilities.

By addressing these gaps, the research seeks to establish a benchmark that offers a more realistic perspective on VLM-based embodied agents, facilitates the identification of specific skill bottlenecks, and provides actionable insights for future development in embodied AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces NativeEmbodied, a benchmark designed to assess VLM-driven embodied agents from a "native" perspective, utilizing a unified low-level action space and a decoupled task hierarchy.

#### 3.1. Native Action Space

The core of NativeEmbodied's approach is its native action space, which consists of primitive, parameterizable actions derived from the AI2THOR simulator. Unlike benchmarks that abstract actions into high-level functions, NativeEmbodied requires agents to operate using basic movements and rotations, allowing for fine-grained control. The defined native actions include:
- `MoveAhead x (meters)`
- `MoveBack x (meters)`
- `MoveLeft x (meters)`
- `MoveRight x (meters)`
- `RotateRight x (degrees)`
- `RotateLeft x (degrees)`
- `LookUp x (degrees)`
- `LookDown x (degrees)`
These primitives allow for fully customized action parameters (e.g., distance, angle), capturing the basic semantics of agent-environment interaction in an unconstrained manner across various task levels. For interaction tasks, four additional primitives—`PickUp`, `PutIn`, `Open`, and `Close`—are augmented to the action space.

#### 3.2. High-level Complex Tasks

NativeEmbodied features three representative high-level tasks to benchmark overall agent performance in the native setting:

1. **Exploration:** This task requires agents to fully explore an environment and answer questions related to objects within it. It is subdivided into four types:
 - **Counting:** Determining the number of specified visible objects.
 - **Localization:** Identifying the receptacle an object is located in.
 - **Receptacle Content:** Listing objects present or absent on a specified receptacle.
 - **Co-existence:** Identifying objects on the same receptacle as a specified object.

2. **Search:** Agents must precisely locate and target specified objects. A crosshair in the egocentric view indicates the focal point, and the agent must approach the target object and align the crosshair with it. This task demands both object identification and fine-grained spatial navigation and alignment.

3. **Interaction:** Agents are instructed to interact with objects in the scene, primarily focusing on pick-and-place scenarios (e.g., placing an object into a receptacle). This task may involve opening or closing receptacles and handling objects that are initially visible or hidden.

#### 3.3. Low-level Foundational Skills

To diagnose specific skill deficiencies, NativeEmbodied decouples high-level tasks into four categories of low-level tasks, each targeting a fundamental embodied skill:

1. **Perception:** Evaluates the agent's ability to describe semantic and spatial elements of its egocentric view. Agents must output information in a structured object-location-receptacle triplet format, detailing visible objects, their spatial relationship to the agent, and their receptacle.

2. **Spatial Alignment:** The agent must align the center of its view with a specified object. To isolate this skill, the agent is initialized close to the target, and only view-adjustment actions are permitted, removing the need for search or physical navigation.

3. **Navigation:** Agents are tasked with reaching within 1 meter of a target object. Initialization at the farthest room corner ensures path complexity, while the target object remains visible, focusing the challenge purely on navigation capabilities.

4. **Planning:** This task evaluates an agent's cognitive reasoning for task planning. Motor control is decoupled by abstracting motion primitives into directly callable navigation interfaces. The interactive, multi-stage nature of pick-and-place scenarios makes it suitable for fine-grained planning evaluation.

#### 3.4. Data Collection

Data samples are collected through an automatic generation pipeline followed by human-machine collaborative filtering, built upon AI2-THOR. The simulator's 3D coordinates, state flags, and instance-segmentation masks facilitate batch generation of candidate samples. For example, in exploration tasks, all objects and their receptacles are queried to instantiate questions. A multi-round rollout evaluation by an advanced MLLM tracks success rates. Samples consistently succeeding or failing across rounds are then reviewed by human experts to filter out infeasible or overly simplistic instances, ensuring high quality and appropriate challenge levels.

#### 3.5. Dataset Statistics

The NativeEmbodied benchmark comprises 1,085 samples, encompassing three high-level tasks and four low-level tasks.

#### 3.6. Evaluation Setup

- **Baselines:** The study evaluates 15 open-source and closed-source VLMs from four families: GPT (GPT-4o, GPT-4v, GPT-o3, GPT-o4-mini), Claude (Claude-3.5-Sonnet, Claude-3.7-Sonnet, Claude-4-Sonnet, Claude-4-Opus), Gemini (Gemini-2.0-flash, Gemini-2.5-flash, Gemini-2.5-pro), and Qwen (Qwen-2.5-VL-72B, Qwen-2.5-VL-32B, Qwen-2.5-VL-7B, Qwen-2.5-VL-3B).
- **Environment:** Interactions occur in AI2THOR. Agents receive 640x480 egocentric images (90° FOV) and output one action per turn. Rollout step limits are 15 for alignment and navigation, 20 for planning, and 30 for high-level tasks. Text-image history is truncated to 20 turns. All VLM inferences use a temperature of 0.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Beyond Success Rate (SR), additional metrics include Average Steps (AS) for efficiency, Weighted Average Steps (WAS) penalizing failures, Average Closest Distance (ACD) for proximity to target, and Average Closest Pixel Distance (ACPD) for visual alignment. For Perception, Precision, Recall, and F1 score are used, with positive triplets requiring exact matches to ground truth.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Performance on High-level Tasks

The evaluation of VLMs on high-level tasks under native settings revealed significant challenges. Even strong VLM architectures exhibited limited success. For instance, in the Search task, the best-performing model, GPT-o3, achieved a success rate of 34.64%, while Claude-4-Sonnet recorded a 0% success rate. Similarly, the highest success rates for Interaction and Exploration were 38.34% and 52.43%, respectively. These results indicate that current VLMs are not yet proficient at executing complex tasks in native embodied environments.

#### 4.2. Performance on Low-level Foundational Skills

VLMs demonstrated varying levels of proficiency across different foundational skills:
- **Perception:** Models displayed comparatively strong performance, with GPT-o3 achieving an F1 score of 83.97%, suggesting a reliable capability in understanding and recognizing visual information.
- **Planning:** Proprietary models generally achieved success rates exceeding 50% (e.g., GPT-o3 at 72.54%), indicating robust task planning and reasoning abilities.
- **Navigation:** Performance declined notably, with more than half of the models achieving success rates below 50%. The worst-performing proprietary model registered a 27.78% success rate.
- **Spatial Alignment:** This skill presented a significant challenge. Except for GPT-o3 (64.16%), no other model achieved a success rate exceeding 50%, and several closed-source models had single-digit success rates. This suggests pronounced deficiencies in tasks requiring dynamic spatial interaction and fine-grained control.

#### 4.3. Distinct Behavioral Spectra

Mainstream VLMs exhibited diverse strategic tendencies. In Navigation, GPT-o3 achieved the highest success rate but with longer average step counts, indicating a robust but conservative path-planning approach. Conversely, Claude-4-Opus maintained a success rate above 50% with fewer steps, leading in ACD and WAS metrics, suggesting a more efficient, aggressive exploration style. For Exploration, GPT-o4-mini and Gemini-2.5-Pro showed fewer average steps while maintaining high accuracy rates, indicating agile information gathering and utilization.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study of Foundational Skills

An ablation study using Claude-3.5-Sonnet provided insights into skill bottlenecks:
- **Perception:** Providing ground-truth perception information did not significantly improve model performance, suggesting that current advanced VLMs possess sufficient intrinsic visual understanding capabilities for the task.
- **Dual Bottlenecks in Long-Horizon Tasks:** For Exploration and Interaction tasks, ablating both planning and navigation capabilities led to substantial performance improvements. This indicates that both cognitive-level decision-making (planning) and action-level execution (navigation) are critical limiting factors for multi-step, long-horizon tasks.
- **Fine-Grained Spatial Requirements in Search Tasks:** Improvements in navigation and especially alignment capabilities had significant effects on Search tasks, while planning had a limited impact. This highlights the high precision required for spatial positioning and viewpoint control in search scenarios, which are less reliant on complex planning.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study of Think Mode

An ablation study on reasoning models (Gemini-2.5-Pro, Claude-4-Opus) explored the impact of "thinking" processes:
- **Enhanced Cognitive Abilities:** Enabling thinking mode improved overall performance on perception and planning tasks. This suggests that explicit reasoning processes assist models in comprehending environmental states, identifying key elements, and formulating more coherent high-level strategies, particularly for complex reasoning and long-term planning.
- **Interference with Basic Action Execution:** Conversely, enabling thinking mode led to a significant decrease in success rates for tasks requiring precise actions, such as alignment and navigation. This decline is attributed to potential over-reasoning, which might introduce unnecessary complexity and impede the intuitive execution of fundamental motor controls.

#### 4.6. Error Case Analysis

Three common error patterns were identified:
- **Insufficient Exploration:** Agents often made premature conclusions based on partial environmental information, indicating overconfidence or incomplete exploration strategies.
- **Redundant View Adjustment:** Agents frequently performed repetitive and unnecessary view adjustments, reducing efficiency and sometimes leading to operational dead loops due to repeated observations.
- **Frequent Collision:** Agents demonstrated poor perception and response to environmental collisions, struggling to make effective adjustments, particularly in confined spaces, leading to being stuck.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The NativeEmbodied benchmark and its associated findings contribute to the field of VLM-driven embodied intelligence by providing a more realistic and diagnostic framework for evaluation.

**Pinpointing Key Challenges:** The research explicitly identifies significant limitations in the fundamental embodied capabilities of current VLMs, particularly in fine-grained spatial alignment and precise navigation. The low success rates observed in tasks requiring dynamic interaction within a native action space underscore that while VLMs excel in perception and high-level planning, their ability to translate these into accurate, efficient physical actions remains a substantial bottleneck. This clarifies that the challenge is not solely in high-level reasoning but in the elemental execution layers.

**Guidance for Future Development:** By decoupling complex tasks into foundational skills, the benchmark offers specific guidance for future research. The ablation studies reveal that for long-horizon tasks (Exploration, Interaction), both planning and navigation are dual bottlenecks, suggesting a need for advancements in both cognitive decision-making and efficient movement strategies. For search tasks, the critical role of alignment points to the necessity of improved fine-grained spatial control. The "think mode" ablation highlights a crucial trade-off: while reasoning enhances cognitive abilities, it may impede precise action execution. This suggests that future embodied agent architectures might benefit from a balanced functional division, optimizing between deliberate cognitive reasoning and agile, intuitive motor control, rather than a monolithic approach. Researchers can leverage these insights to target specific skill improvements (e.g., enhanced collision avoidance, more efficient view adjustments, better spatial understanding for precise actions) rather than broadly improving overall agent capabilities.

**Bridging the Reality Gap:** The adoption of a "native" low-level action space moves evaluations closer to real-world conditions, where agents must operate with primitive controls. This approach offers a more accurate assessment of an agent's readiness for physical deployment, fostering the development of models that are robust and adaptable beyond abstract command interfaces.

**Comprehensive Assessment:** The multi-dimensional and multi-granular evaluation system, encompassing both complex high-level tasks and isolated low-level skills, enhances the explainability of capability assessments. This allows researchers to not only observe overall performance but also understand the specific strengths and weaknesses of different VLM architectures in the context of embodied tasks.

The public availability of the NativeEmbodied benchmark, including its code and datasets, provides a standardized tool for the research community, facilitating comparative studies and accelerating advancements in building more capable and reliable VLM-driven embodied agents.
