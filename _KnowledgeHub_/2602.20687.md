# How Foundational Skills Influence VLM-based Embodied Agents:A Native Perspective

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Science and Technology of China and Alibaba Group introduced NativeEmbodied, a benchmark for VLM-based embodied agents that uses a low-level action space and a decoupled task hierarchy. The evaluation reveals that while models demonstrate strong perception (GPT-o3 F1: 83.97%) and planning, they face significant challenges in fine-grained spatial alignment and navigation, with many proprietary models achieving single-digit success rates in alignment tasks.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks for VLM-based embodied agents often rely on 'non-native' high-level action spaces, abstracting low-level skills and creating a disparity with real-world operational conditions.
- Current evaluations focus on coupled, high-level tasks with overall success rates, making it difficult to pinpoint specific foundational skill deficiencies and diagnose performance bottlenecks.
- A lack of comprehensive understanding regarding which foundational skills are truly essential and how they impact higher-level task execution for VLM-based agents.

## Method

- Introduces ==NativeEmbodied==, a benchmark employing a 'native' low-level, primitive action space (e.g., 'MoveAhead x meters', 'RotateRight x degrees') derived from AI2THOR, allowing for fine-grained control.
- Features a ==decoupled task hierarchy== with three complex high-level tasks (Exploration, Search, Interaction) and four isolated low-level tasks (Perception, Spatial Alignment, Navigation, Planning) to assess specific foundational skills.
- Utilizes an automatic data generation pipeline with human-machine collaborative filtering, built upon AI2-THOR, to create high-quality, challenging scenarios for evaluation.

## Results

- On high-level tasks, even leading VLMs achieved limited success: the best model (GPT-o3) reached **34.64%** success in Search, **38.34%** in Interaction, and **52.43%** in Exploration.
- In low-level skills, models performed comparatively well in Perception (GPT-o3 F1: **83.97%**) and Planning (GPT-o3 SR: **72.54%**) but struggled significantly with Spatial Alignment (only GPT-o3 above **50%** SR) and Navigation (over half of models below **50%** SR).
- Ablation studies confirmed that navigation and planning are dual bottlenecks for long-horizon tasks, while spatial alignment is crucial for search; enabling 'think mode' improved performance on perception and planning but led to a decrease in success rates for precise actions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current VLMs show strong perceptual understanding and planning capabilities but exhibit pronounced deficiencies in fine-grained spatial alignment and precise navigation, which are critical bottlenecks for complex embodied tasks.
> - Long-horizon tasks (Exploration, Interaction) are dually bottlenecked by both cognitive-level planning and action-level navigation capabilities.
> - While explicit 'think mode' reasoning enhances cognitive abilities for perception and planning, it can paradoxically interfere with the intuitive and precise execution of fundamental motor controls like alignment and navigation.
