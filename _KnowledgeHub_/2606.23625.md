# Learning to See While Learning to Act: Diffusion Models for Active Perception in Robot Imitation

> [!summary] Summary
> The research introduces See2Act, a diffusion-based imitation learning framework that enables robots to jointly refine actions and actively control camera viewpoints (6-DoF) to resolve occlusions. This method achieves high success rates on various manipulation tasks with partial observability, including 95% on real-world occluded tasks with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer using only 50 demonstrations.

## Problem

- Current robot manipulation methods often assume full observability, failing when task-relevant objects or target locations are hidden by occlusions in real-world environments.
- Learning coupled active perception (where to look) and visuomotor manipulation (how to act) from limited human demonstrations is challenging, as these two behaviors are implicitly entangled.
- Existing active perception approaches typically treat viewpoint control as a separate problem, requiring dedicated policies, reinforcement learning rewards, or explicit planning stages, increasing system complexity.

## Method

- **See2Act** integrates full ==6-DoF camera movements== into a diffusion-based action denoising process, allowing the policy to jointly refine robot actions and camera viewpoints.
- Keyframe actions are represented in the camera frame, and a camera trajectory is generated for each diffusion timestep by interpolating between a broad global view and a close, target-centric view derived from the action estimate.
- During inference, the policy iteratively updates the camera pose based on the current best action estimate, acquiring new visual observations to guide subsequent denoising and action refinement steps.

## Results

- Achieved up to **96%** success rates on simulated Ravens tasks with severe occlusions, significantly outperforming fixed-view (0-**2%**), restricted-motion (0-**8%**), and passive view selection (2-**64%**) baselines.
- Demonstrated successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a UR10 robotic arm, achieving **95%** success rates on real-world occluded tasks with only 50 demonstrations, compared to baselines needing 3e5 layouts.
- Secured the highest average success rate of **81.1%** across nine diverse RLBench tasks, demonstrating that a single, actively controlled 2D view can provide sufficient geometric detail to match or exceed multi-view/3D methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The denoising trajectory of a diffusion model can be leveraged to represent a trajectory over camera viewpoints, enabling a unified framework for action refinement and active visual evidence acquisition.
> - Defining actions in the camera frame allows the policy to implicitly learn to select informative viewpoints that are dynamically tied to the evolving manipulation task and action.
> - Iterative active viewpoint refinement during inference allows the policy to continuously gather fine-grained visual details, which is crucial for high-precision tasks and resolving complex occlusions.
