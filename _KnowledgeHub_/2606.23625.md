# Learning to See While Learning to Act: Diffusion Models for Active Perception in Robot Imitation

> [!summary] Summary
> The research introduces See2Act, a diffusion-based imitation learning framework that enables robots to jointly refine actions and actively control camera viewpoints (6-DoF) to resolve occlusions. This method achieves high success rates on various manipulation tasks with partial observability, including 95% on real-world occluded tasks with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer using only 50 demonstrations.

## Problem

- Current robot manipulation methods often assume full observability, failing when task-relevant objects or target locations are hidden by occlusions in real-world environments.
- Learning coupled active perception (where to look) and visuomotor manipulation (how to act) from limited human demonstrations is challenging, as these two behaviors are implicitly entangled.
- Existing active perception approaches typically treat viewpoint control as a separate problem, requiring dedicated policies, reinforcement learning rewards, or explicit planning stages, increasing system complexity.

## Method

- **See2Act** integrates full ==6-DoF camera movements== into a diffusion-based action denoising process, allowing the policy to jointly refine robot actions and camera viewpoints.
- Keyframe actions are represented in the camera frame, and a camera trajectory is generated for each diffusion timestep by interpolating between a broad global view and a close, target-centric view derived from the action estimate.
- During inference, the policy iteratively updates the camera pose based on the current best action estimate, acquiring new visual observations to guide subsequent denoising and action refinement steps.

## Results

- Achieved up to **96%** success rates on simulated Ravens tasks with severe occlusions, significantly outperforming fixed-view (0-**2%**), restricted-motion (0-**8%**), and passive view selection (2-**64%**) baselines.
- Demonstrated successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a UR10 robotic arm, achieving **95%** success rates on real-world occluded tasks with only 50 demonstrations, compared to baselines needing 3e5 layouts.
- Secured the highest average success rate of **81.1%** across nine diverse RLBench tasks, demonstrating that a single, actively controlled 2D view can provide sufficient geometric detail to match or exceed multi-view/3D methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The denoising trajectory of a diffusion model can be leveraged to represent a trajectory over camera viewpoints, enabling a unified framework for action refinement and active visual evidence acquisition.
> - Defining actions in the camera frame allows the policy to implicitly learn to select informative viewpoints that are dynamically tied to the evolving manipulation task and action.
> - Iterative active viewpoint refinement during inference allows the policy to continuously gather fine-grained visual details, which is crucial for high-precision tasks and resolving complex occlusions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Current research in robot manipulation, particularly within imitation learning (IL) and visuomotor control, frequently operates under the assumption of full observability. In typical tabletop settings, objects are often visible from fixed, overhead camera viewpoints, simplifying the perception problem. However, real-world robotic tasks often involve occlusions, where task-relevant objects or target locations are not immediately visible. This necessitates that a robot not only determines "how to act" but also "where to look" to gather sufficient visual information.

Recent advancements in data-efficient visuomotor control have introduced methodologies such as diffusion policies, 3D representations, symmetry-aware architectures, and camera-conditioned refinement. Keyframe-based approaches have also contributed to efficiency by focusing on critical waypoints in a trajectory. While these methods have improved performance and data efficiency in various settings, they generally maintain a passive stance towards perception. They either rely on fixed camera configurations, pre-defined multi-view inputs, or limited camera motions (e.g., translational zoom along a fixed orientation, as in C3DM). This passive perception paradigm becomes a significant limitation when essential visual information is hidden by occlusions and must be actively acquired through repositioning or reorientation of the camera.

The challenge lies in learning this coupled behavior of viewpoint selection and manipulation from limited demonstrations, as human demonstrations inherently entangle these two distinct but related behaviors. Simply increasing the number of viewpoints in demonstrations leads to combinatorial data requirements without explicitly teaching the policy the utility of specific views. Prior active perception methods typically treat viewpoint control as a separate problem from action prediction, often requiring dedicated viewpoint policies, reinforcement learning rewards, or explicit planning stages.

This work addresses a core gap in the research landscape: the development of policies that can *jointly* learn and perform active perception (where to see) and visuomotor manipulation (how to act) within a unified framework, particularly to resolve partial observability. By integrating full 6-DoF camera movements into the action denoising process, See2Act aims to overcome the limitations of passive perception and restricted camera motion, enabling robots to actively adapt their viewpoint to the task's visual requirements as the manipulation task unfolds. This contrasts with existing methods that either ignore active perception, provide it passively, or decouple it from the main action generation process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the See2Act research are rooted in addressing the limitations of current robot imitation learning methods when confronted with realistic, partially observable environments.

1. **Overcoming Occlusion in Robot Manipulation**: The central motivation is the inherent challenge posed by occlusions in real-world manipulation tasks. Most existing imitation learning (IL) methods assume that all necessary visual information is available from static, pre-defined camera viewpoints. In practice, task-relevant objects, target placement locations, or critical features can be obscured, requiring the robot to actively reposition its sensor to gather information. The goal is to develop a system that can autonomously resolve these occlusions without prior knowledge of the scene layout or explicit human intervention during inference.

2. **Learning Coupled Seeing and Acting from Limited Demonstrations**: The authors identify that human demonstrations for complex tasks implicitly combine viewpoint selection and manipulation. Learning these two intertwined behaviors from limited offline demonstrations is difficult. Existing approaches that learn manipulation (how to act) often fail because they lack the ability to simultaneously infer optimal viewpoints (where to see). A key objective is to enable a policy to learn this coupling implicitly from standard action labels, without requiring explicit viewpoint annotations or separate policies for perception and action. This aims to improve data efficiency by leveraging existing demonstration formats.

3. **Integrating Active Perception within a Unified Generative Framework**: The research is motivated by the insight that manipulation should not be treated as a purely passive action prediction problem from a fixed viewpoint, but rather as an iterative process involving both action refinement and the acquisition of relevant visual evidence. The objective is to embed active perception directly into a single generative process, specifically using diffusion models. This integration aims to allow the policy to continuously update its camera pose in conjunction with refining its action, creating a feedback loop where improved action estimates guide viewpoint selection, and better viewpoints lead to more precise action predictions. This avoids the complexity of separate planning or reward functions for viewpoint control.

4. **Enabling Generalizable and Robust Manipulation**: A core motivation is to enhance the robustness and generalizability of robot manipulation policies. By actively searching for informative views, the robot can better adapt to variations in object placement, environmental clutter, and unexpected occlusions. This capability is crucial for deploying robots in dynamic and unstructured real-world settings. The authors specifically aim for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, reducing the need for costly real-world data collection and fine-tuning, by training in a digital twin and leveraging depth observations to mitigate the sim-to-real gap.

5. **Achieving High Precision and Performance**: The work also seeks to demonstrate that active viewpoint refinement can lead to improved task success rates, particularly in tasks requiring high precision or intricate spatial reasoning. By bringing the camera closer to the target as an action is refined, the policy can acquire fine-grained visual details essential for accurate execution, which might be unavailable from distant or fixed views.

In summary, the motivation is to develop an imitation learning policy that fundamentally treats vision as an active process, enabling robots to dynamically adapt their perception to the task requirements, thereby addressing critical limitations of current methods in handling occlusion, achieving data efficiency, and facilitating robust real-world deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

See2Act introduces a diffusion-based imitation learning framework designed to jointly refine robot actions and camera viewpoints. The core methodological insight is that the denoising trajectory of a diffusion model can also represent a trajectory over camera viewpoints, coupling action refinement with visual evidence acquisition.

#### 3.1. Keyframe Action Extraction and Noising

The method leverages Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) to model the conditional distribution of target actions based on image observations.
- **Action Representation**: Keyframe actions `a_0` are defined as the target end-effector poses (position `p_a,0` and orientation `theta_a,0` in Euler angles) for pick-and-place behaviors.
- **Camera Frame Actions**: Crucially, actions are expressed in the *camera frame* rather than a fixed world frame. The target keyframe action `a_0` (world frame) is transformed into `a_hat_0` in the camera frame `C_t` using the transform `C_t^T W`. This means `a_hat_0` is viewpoint-dependent.
- **Forward Diffusion Process**: A sequence of latent actions `{a_hat_t}` is generated by progressively adding Gaussian noise `epsilon` to `a_hat_0` according to a predefined schedule `alpha_bar(t)`, as described by `a_hat_t = a_hat_0 + sqrt(1 - alpha_bar(t)) * epsilon`. This noising process is applied without drift.

#### 3.2. Generating Camera Poses for Policy Conditioning

A central component of See2Act is the association of each diffusion timestep `t` with a specific camera pose `C_t`. This creates a camera trajectory that evolves from a broad contextual view at `t=T` to a close, target-centric view at `t=0`.
- **Anchor Poses**: The camera trajectory is anchored by two poses:
 - `C_T`: A fixed, pre-chosen pose that provides a global, broad view of the scene for initial context.
 - `C_0`: A close, target-centric view derived directly from the target keyframe action `a_0`. `C_0` is constructed by placing the camera at a fixed offset `d` from `p_a,0` along the action's approach axis (defined by the rotation matrix `R(theta_a,0)z`, where `z` is `^T`), and with its orientation `theta_c,0` set to `theta_a,0`. This ensures the camera is centered on the action frame.
- **Intermediate Poses (`C_t`)**: For intermediate diffusion timesteps `t`, the camera pose `C_t` is obtained by interpolating between `C_0` and `C_T` using the normalized timestep `tau = t/T`.
 - Position `p_c,t` is linearly interpolated: `p_c,t = (1 - tau)p_c,0 + tau * p_c,T`.
 - Orientation `theta_c,t` is interpolated using Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP) on unit quaternions, ensuring a smooth and continuous trajectory in SE(3).
- **Observation Generation**: During training, for each scene state `s` and camera pose `C_t`, an online renderer in a digital twin generates the corresponding observation `O_t`. This creates a dataset where each noisy action `a_hat_t` is paired with an observation `O_t` from a viewpoint `C_t` that is implicitly tied to the target action `a_0`.

#### 3.3. Learning Image-Conditioned Action Denoising

The model consists of a shared visual encoder `e_phi` (ResNet-18 with FiLM conditioning for language instructions) and a noise prediction network `epsilon_psi` (MLP with ReLU activations).
- **Training Objective**: Given a dataset `D` of initial object states `s^(i)` and corresponding target keyframe actions `a_0^(i)`, the model is trained to minimize the standard Mean-Squared Error (MSE) objective: `L(D) = (1/(N*K)) * sum(epsilon_psi(e_phi(O_t^(k,i)), a_hat_t^(k,i)) - epsilon^(k,i))^2`. Here, `N` is the number of demonstrations, `K` is the number of latent action samples per demonstration, and `epsilon^(k,i)` is the ground-truth noise. Training is performed using Adam optimizer.

#### 3.4. Active Viewpoint Inference

At inference time, See2Act couples the reverse diffusion process with camera trajectory optimization, referred to as Active Viewpoint Inference.
- **Initialization**: The process starts from a chosen global camera pose `C_T` and acquires an observation `O_T`. An initial noisy latent action `a_tilde_T` is sampled from a uniform distribution within the action bounds.
- **Iterative Refinement (t from T down to 1)**:
 1. The current world-frame action estimate `a_tilde_t` is transformed into the current camera frame `C_tilde_t` to obtain `a_hat_tilde_t`.
 2. The noise predictor `epsilon_psi` predicts the noise `epsilon_hat_t` from the current observation `O_tilde_t` and `a_hat_tilde_t`.
 3. An estimate of the target action `a_hat_tilde_0` in the camera frame is recovered by denoising `a_hat_tilde_t` using `epsilon_hat_t`.
 4. `a_hat_tilde_0` is transformed back to the world frame as `a_tilde_0`.
 5. This `a_tilde_0` (the current best estimate of the final action) is used to determine the *target* camera pose `C_tilde_0` for the *next* denoising step using the same procedure as in training (Eq. 2).
 6. The camera pose `C_tilde_{t-1}` for the *next* diffusion step `t-1` is derived by interpolating between `C_T` and the newly estimated `C_tilde_0`.
 7. A new observation `O_tilde_{t-1}` is acquired from this refined camera pose `C_tilde_{t-1}`.
- **Rollout Selection**: The inference procedure is run `n_rollout` times. The final action `a_0` from the rollout with the smallest variance in the inferred camera-pose changes is selected, indicating the most stable and consistent camera trajectory.

#### 3.5. Sim-to-Real Transfer

The policy is trained entirely in simulation using a digital twin. For real-world deployment, depth observations are used to reduce the sim-to-real gap, and camera calibration is performed once. This approach enables zero-shot transfer from simulation to a physical robot.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across simulated (Ravens, RLBench) and real-world manipulation tasks provide empirical evidence for See2Act's capabilities, particularly its active viewpoint refinement mechanism.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments: Ravens Benchmark (Occlusion Tasks)

- **Performance on Occluded Tasks**: See2Act achieved high average success rates across four newly designed Ravens tasks with severe front-view occlusions: 96% on `place-red-in-green`, 96% on `bin-picking`, 72% on `put-within-shelf`, and 100% on `bin-search`.
- **Comparison with Baselines**:
 - **Fixed-View Methods**: Baselines using fixed single-view (Conv-MLP) or fixed multi-view (Conv-MLP-MV, Diff-MV) observations largely failed, achieving 0-2% success rates on all occluded tasks. This indicates that pre-configured views are insufficient to resolve complex occlusions.
 - **Restricted Camera Motion (C3DM)**: C3DM, which permits only zoom-based camera motion along a fixed orientation, performed poorly on `bin-picking` (8%) and failed completely on shelf tasks (0%), suggesting that full 6-DoF repositioning and reorientation are necessary to circumvent certain occlusions.
 - **Passive View Selection (Diff-MV-Entropy)**: This baseline, which selects a view from a preset candidate set based on lowest denoising entropy, achieved up to 64% on `bin-search` and 22% on `bin-picking`, but only 2% on `put-within-shelf`. Its performance was limited because it cannot iteratively refine viewpoints beyond a fixed set of candidates.
- **Conclusion on Occlusion**: The substantial performance gains of See2Act over these baselines (up to 96% over Conv-MLP variants, 70% over Diff-MV-Entropy, and 88% over C3DM) indicate that iterative active viewpoint refinement during the denoising process is critical for handling tasks with partial observability.
- **Effect of Refinement Steps**: See2Act's success rate on `bin-picking` improved significantly as the number of denoising (and thus refinement) steps increased from 5 to 20, demonstrating that each viewpoint refinement contributes progressively to action prediction accuracy.
- **Emergent Search Behavior**: In the `bin-search` task, See2Act exhibited emergent search-like behavior. When a selected bin was found empty from the camera's perspective, the policy's action estimate would contradict the observation, leading to abrupt camera reorientations to inspect alternative candidates. This resulted in a 100% success rate on `bin-search`, a task where all baselines scored 0%, demonstrating that active perception can induce exploration without explicit search supervision.

#### 4.1.2. RLBench Benchmark (Diverse Manipulation Tasks)

- **Competitive Performance**: See2Act achieved the highest average success rate of 81.1% across nine diverse RLBench tasks, outperforming several state-of-the-art methods, including those utilizing richer spatial inputs like fused multi-view observations (RVT, RVT-2) or 3D point clouds (PerAct, Act3D).
- **Task-Specific Improvements**: See2Act recorded the best results on `place-wine` (99%), `put-in-safe` (96%), `slide-block` (100%), and `sort-shape` (69%). The largest gain was observed in `sort-shape`.
- **Conclusion on Diverse Tasks**: These results suggest that a single actively-controlled 2D view, when iteratively refined, can provide sufficient geometric detail to match or exceed the performance of methods relying on richer but passively acquired spatial information, even beyond tasks explicitly designed for occlusion.

#### 4.1.3. Robustness to Initial Camera Viewpoints

- **In-Distribution Poses**: When trained with diverse 6-DoF camera poses sampled from a pyramidal region and evaluated on similar in-distribution poses, See2Act achieved high success rates (97.5% on `place-red-in-green`, 95.6% on `bin-picking`).
- **Out-of-Distribution Poses**: On out-of-distribution initial camera poses sampled from a larger, dome-shaped region, performance showed a modest drop of 8-10 percentage points (86.7% and 88.2%).
- **Conclusion on Robustness**: This indicates that the active viewpoint refinement mechanism partially compensates for suboptimal or unseen starting views by guiding the camera towards more informative perspectives during inference.

#### 4.2. Real-Robot Experiments

- **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer**: See2Act was successfully deployed on a UR10 robotic arm with a wrist-mounted depth camera, achieving zero-shot transfer from training on only 50 digital twin demonstrations.
- **Performance on Real-World Occluded Tasks**: On two real-world tasks with occlusions (`shelf-kitting` and `clean-up`), See2Act achieved a high success rate of 95% on both.
- **Comparison with Baselines**: In contrast, Diff-MV (trained on 3e5 layouts) achieved 25% on `shelf-kitting` and 50% on `clean-up`. Diff-MV-Entropy (also trained on 3e5 layouts) scored 50% on both tasks.
- **Precision and Data Efficiency**: The large performance gap on `shelf-kitting`, a high-precision insertion task (1-2mm/degree tolerance), highlights the importance of active refinement. See2Act’s camera moves close to the target, providing fine-grained visual detail necessary for precision, which fixed distant views cannot offer. This performance was achieved with only 50 demonstrations, significantly fewer than the 3e5 layouts used by baselines.
- **Robustness to Calibration Error**: See2Act exhibited greater robustness to sim-to-real camera-pose discrepancies compared to fixed-view methods. Because the camera moves close to the target, small angular offsets result in minor positional errors, which are less amplified than in fixed-view setups that observe from a greater distance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The See2Act framework represents a notable contribution to robot imitation learning and active perception, demonstrating a method for robots to simultaneously learn "where to see" and "how to act." Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Advancement in Active Perception Integration**: See2Act proposes a novel approach to integrate active perception directly into the visuomotor control loop using diffusion models. Unlike prior methods that treat viewpoint selection as a separate planning problem or allow only restricted camera motions, See2Act’s mechanism of jointly denoising actions and 6-DoF camera poses enables active visual information gathering to emerge naturally from the action prediction process. This unified approach simplifies policy design by eliminating the need for separate viewpoint policies, reward functions, or complex planning stages.

2. **Robustness to Partial Observability and Occlusions**: The work directly addresses a critical limitation of current imitation learning systems: their inability to operate effectively in environments with occlusions or partial observability. By demonstrating high success rates on tasks specifically designed with severe occlusions (Ravens benchmarks) and real-world scenarios, See2Act shows that active viewpoint refinement is a powerful strategy for recovering task-relevant visual information, thereby enhancing the robustness of robotic manipulation.

3. **Data Efficiency and Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer**: See2Act achieves high performance with a relatively small number of demonstrations (e.g., 50 for real-world tasks), contrasting with baselines that require orders of magnitude more data. This improved data efficiency, combined with successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer using depth observations, significantly reduces the cost and complexity associated with deploying robotic manipulation skills in physical environments. This capability is critical for accelerating the development and adoption of robotics.

4. **Enhanced Precision and Generalization**: The ability to dynamically reposition the camera to acquire close-up views during the denoising process allows the policy to gather fine-grained geometric details. This contributes to improved performance on high-precision tasks and enhances generalization across diverse manipulation tasks (demonstrated on RLBench), even outperforming methods that use richer but passively acquired multi-view or 3D inputs. The emergent search behavior observed in bin-search tasks further highlights the policy's adaptability and intelligence in handling uncertainty.

5. **Implications for Real-World Robotics**: The successful zero-shot transfer to a real robot under challenging conditions (occlusions, precision requirements) indicates a practical pathway for deploying robust and adaptable manipulation policies. This could facilitate the wider application of robots in unstructured environments such as logistics, manufacturing, and service robotics, where objects are frequently occluded or require intricate handling.

6. **Future Research Directions**: The work opens avenues for future research, such as extending the framework to dense trajectory prediction for more dexterous and dynamic manipulation tasks. While acknowledging the latency cost associated with acquiring new observations at every denoising step, this research provides a foundational step towards more intelligent and adaptive robotic perception-action systems.

In essence, See2Act advances the understanding and implementation of how robots can proactively engage with their visual environment, moving imitation learning closer to real-world applicability by directly addressing the fundamental problem of seeing to act under uncertainty.
