# R3D: Revisiting 3D Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University and ShanghaiTech University introduce R3D, a robust 3D policy learning framework that addresses instability and scaling challenges in robotic imitation learning. It leverages a Layer-Normalization-only 3D encoder, extensive 3D data augmentation, and spatially-aware diffusion decoding, achieving superior performance on diverse manipulation tasks in both simulation and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Existing 3D policy learning methods suffered from a "scaling paradox," where powerful 3D backbones often failed to improve performance or even degraded it.
- Training of 3D policies proved to be unstable and prone to overfitting, particularly as model capacity increased, limiting the adoption of modern 3D architectures.
- Geometric limitations of 2D-centric policies hindered generalization to novel viewpoints and robust cross-embodiment skill transfer in robotic imitation learning.

## Method

- Replaced ==Batch Normalization (BN)== with ==Layer Normalization (LN)== in 3D backbones to stabilize training and enable the effective use of high-capacity encoders.
- Implemented a comprehensive ==3D data augmentation== pipeline, including ==FPS randomization==, ==point cloud color jitter==, and additive noise/dropout, to mitigate overfitting.
- Proposed **R3D**, a novel architecture featuring a pre-trained, ==LayerNorm-only Transformer-based 3D encoder== and a ==spatially-aware Diffusion Transformer== decoder with ==multi-objective decoding== for precise action prediction.

## Results

- R3D achieved an average success rate of **83.8%** on the RoboTwin 2.0 "Easy" benchmark and **64.8%** on the "Hard" setting, outperforming baselines like DP3 (**61.0%** easy) and ManiFlow (**61.6%** hard).
- On the ManiSkill2 benchmark, R3D achieved an average success rate of **55.2%**, substantially higher than ACT+PonderV2 (**42.4%**) and DP3 (**23.0%**), demonstrating superior precision for tasks like "PegInsertion."
- In real-world experiments on an xArm6 robot, R3D consistently surpassed baselines across six diverse manipulation tasks, achieving an average success rate of **60.7%**, and showed stronger robustness to external interference like disco light illumination.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Batch Normalization is a significant contributor to instability and the "scaling paradox" in 3D policy learning, while Layer Normalization enables stable training with powerful backbones.
> - Extensive 3D data augmentation is crucial for mitigating overfitting and enhancing the generalization capabilities of 3D policies.
> - Preserving spatial resolution through dense feature conditioning and utilizing pre-trained 3D encoders on segmentation tasks significantly improves performance and stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic imitation learning has seen significant advancements, largely driven by 2D-centric policies that utilize image-based observations. Approaches like Diffusion Policy and ACT, incorporating diffusion-based action decoding and action chunking, have been successfully scaled into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. However, these 2D methods face inherent limitations, particularly in generalization to novel viewpoints and cross-embodiment skill transfer, due to their reliance on 2D projections of a 3D world.

In response, 3D policy learning has emerged as an alternative, offering advantages such as natural fusion of multi-view camera inputs, inherent viewpoint invariance, and a consistent world frame for robust skill transfer. Existing 3D policy learning methods include diffusion-based approaches (e.g., 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3), 3D Diffuser Actor), transformer-based architectures (e.g., FP3, Act3D, RVT), and extensions into the VLA domain (e.g., 3D-VLA, GeoVLA). Some methods also explore implicit 3D structures or 2.5D representations by leveraging depth cues or multi-view consistency to mitigate geometric limitations of 2D inputs.

Despite its theoretical promise, progress in 3D policy learning has been hindered by practical challenges. Previous work, such as DP3, observed a "scaling paradox" where simpler architectures like PointNet sometimes outperformed more complex 3D backbones. Furthermore, training 3D policies has proven to be unstable, especially as model capacity increases. These issues have limited the adoption of modern, powerful 3D architectures in policy learning.

This work directly addresses these challenges by systematically investigating the causes of these performance limitations in 3D policy learning. It aims to establish a robust and scalable foundation for 3D imitation learning by proposing architectural and training recipe improvements that overcome these identified instabilities and scaling issues.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research were to:

1. **Diagnose the failures in existing 3D policy learning frameworks:** The authors aimed to understand why powerful 3D backbones often fail to improve performance and why training can be unstable, particularly with increasing model capacity. This included investigating the "scaling paradox" observed in prior work where lightweight architectures sometimes outperformed more complex ones.
2. **Identify and address critical oversights in current 3D policy training practices:** Specifically, the research sought to uncover implementation details that contribute to performance degradation and instability.
3. **Propose a novel 3D policy architecture and training methodology:** The goal was to develop a system that is stable at scale, capable of leveraging high-capacity 3D perception models, and designed to benefit from large-scale pre-training.
4. **Demonstrate superior performance and robustness:** The authors aimed to show that their proposed approach significantly outperforms existing 2D, 2.5D, and 3D baselines on challenging manipulation benchmarks in both simulated and real-world environments.
5. **Provide empirical insights for future 3D policy design:** Through systematic experimentation and ablation studies, the work intended to offer valuable references regarding the impact of encoder capacity, decoder depth, and camera configurations.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the recognition of 3D policy learning's potential for superior generalization and cross-embodiment transfer, contrasted with its current limitations due to training instabilities and overfitting. The authors identified a gap where state-of-the-art 3D perception models were not effectively utilized in 3D policy learning due to these issues. By systematically diagnosing and remedying these problems, the research endeavors to unlock the full capabilities of 3D representations for robotic control, moving beyond the geometric limitations of 2D-centric approaches and establishing a more robust paradigm for scalable imitation learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research adopted a systematic approach, beginning with a diagnostic phase to identify the root causes of instability and poor scaling in 3D policy learning, followed by the development of a novel architecture and training recipes.

#### 3.1. Initial Diagnosis and Remedial Training Principles

The authors conducted an investigation using the RoboTwin 2.0 simulation benchmark to test design choices.

- **Overcoming the Scaling Paradox: From BN to LN:**
 - **Diagnosis:** Previous studies indicated that lightweight PointNet backbones sometimes outperformed more powerful architectures when integrated into 3D policies. The authors investigated this "scaling paradox" by replacing a PointNet backbone with a higher-capacity Uni3D encoder. Initial experiments confirmed performance degradation with the stronger backbone.
 - **Identification of Culprit:** Batch Normalization (BN) layers were identified as a primary cause of this degradation. BN layers reportedly struggle with the high variance and small batch sizes typically encountered in imitation learning.
 - **Remedy:** Replacing Batch Normalization (BN) with Layer Normalization (LN) in the Uni3D backbone allowed it to train stably and significantly outperform PointNet. LN was also shown not to degrade PointNet's performance, suggesting its robustness.
- **Mitigating Overfitting through Data Augmentation:**
 - **Diagnosis:** 3D policy training was noted to be prone to overfitting, often leading to unstable learning curves and performance declines over time.
 - **Remedy:** A 3D data augmentation pipeline was introduced, comprising three strategies:
 1. **FPS Randomization:** Randomizing point order during Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) to encourage learning robust spatial representations.
 2. **Point Cloud Color Jitter:** Applying random variations to point cloud RGB channels (brightness, contrast, saturation) to enhance generalization.
 3. **Additive Noise and Dropout:** Adding modest Gaussian noise to point cloud coordinates and proprioceptive vectors, and employing random point dropout, to improve robustness against incomplete or noisy scene representations.
 - **Impact:** These augmentations stabilized training, improved success rates, and enhanced generalization by exposing the policy to a broader range of out-of-distribution data.

#### 3.2. Improved 3D Policy Architecture (R3D)

Motivated by the diagnostic insights, the authors proposed a novel architecture that integrates a high-capacity 3D encoder with a diffusion decoder.

- **High-Capacity 3D Geometric Encoder:**
 - **Design:** The encoder is a Transformer-based backbone, inspired by recent advances in point cloud segmentation (e.g., Point-SAM). Crucially, it exclusively employs Layer Normalization (LN) for stability.
 - **Encoding Process:** It partitions input point clouds using Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) for center points and k-Nearest Neighbors (k-NN) for local patches. These patches are then projected into D-dimensional embeddings via a lightweight PointNet with LN. Self-attention layers, initialized from a pre-trained 2D Vision Transformer (ViT), process these embeddings.
 - **Spatial Resolution Preservation:** Unlike prior work that often collapses features into a global descriptor, this encoder outputs a set of N_C structured point tokens, F_geo, preserving the fine-grained geometry of the scene. This design choice maintains spatial resolution for the downstream decoder.
- **Pretraining Encoder on 3D Segmentation Tasks:**
 - **Strategy:** The encoder structure, being similar to those used in 3D segmentation, allows for pre-training on large-scale 3D scene datasets such as ScanNet, ARKitScenes, and PartNeXt.
 - **Benefits:** Pre-training injects generalizable geometric priors, reduces task-specific sample requirements, accelerates convergence, and improves robustness to real-world distributional shifts.
- **Spatially-Aware Action Decoding:**
 - **Formulation:** The action decoder is a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) that refines a noisy action sequence into a clean one over multiple iterations.
 - **Conditioning Mechanism:** Instead of using a global latent vector, the decoder performs cross-attention directly between action queries and the full set of dense geometric tokens (F_geo). This preserves spatial resolution.
 - **Token Types:** The decoder processes three types of tokens:
 1. **Geometric Tokens:** Historical point cloud tokens (F_geo).
 2. **Proprioception Tokens:** Robot states (F_prio) from an MLP.
 3. **Action Tokens:** Noisy action sequences (F_act) embedded from actions.
 - **Temporal and Diffusion Context:** Learnable temporal embeddings are added, and a sinusoidal embedding for the diffusion iteration is prepended to action sequences. The DiT uses observation tokens (F_geo, F_prio) as context for cross-attention with action tokens (F_act).
 - **Advantages:** Preserving spatial resolution avoids information bottlenecks from global pooling, enabling the DiT to attend to task-relevant regions for precise action predictions.
- **Multi-Objective Decoding for Proprioceptive Grounding:**
 - **Auxiliary Task:** An auxiliary objective is introduced to decode task-space actions (end-effector poses) simultaneously with joint-space actions. This acts as a "free lunch" for additional supervision.
 - **Implementation:** An auxiliary set of action tokens (ee_F_act) representing noisy 7D task-space actions (3D translation, 4D quaternions) is processed. A causal attention mask in self-attention layers ensures that task-space tokens attend to joint-space tokens, but not vice-versa, preventing interference with the primary joint-control objective.
 - **Outcome:** This multi-objective optimization encourages robust internal representations of the robot's state relative to the environment, leading to higher joint control precision.

#### 3.3. Network Scaling Analysis

The authors systematically investigated the impact of encoder size and decoder depth.

- **Encoder Size:** For a fixed point cloud resolution (e.g., 1024 points), smaller ViT-tiny backbones (53MB) were found to be optimal. However, for denser point clouds (e.g., 8192 points in real-world settings), larger encoders like ViT-small (115MB) were preferred, suggesting adaptive scaling with point cloud density. Pre-training was performed with corresponding point counts.
- **Decoder Depth:** Experiments showed that decoders with 4 to 8 attention blocks offered performance advantages, with further increases not yielding significant improvements. A 4-block decoder was selected for subsequent experiments as a trade-off between performance and efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented extensive experimental results demonstrating the efficacy of the proposed R3D approach across various simulation and real-world manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

The method was evaluated on RoboTwin 2.0 and ManiSkill2 benchmarks.

- **RoboTwin 2.0 Benchmark:**
 - **"Easy" Setting (Table 5a):** R3D achieved an average success rate of 83.8%, outperforming all baselines, including 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) (61.0%) and ManiFlow (73.0%). For instance, on "Open Microwave," R3D achieved 100% success compared to ManiFlow's 98% and DP3's 61%.
 - **"Hard" Setting (Table 5b):** This setting, characterized by significant domain randomization and distractors, showcased R3D's generalization capabilities. R3D achieved an average success rate of 64.8%, surpassing ManiFlow (61.6%) and other baselines. Notably, on "Burger Fries," R3D achieved 95% success, while ManiFlow reached 72%.
- **ManiSkill2 Benchmark (Table 6):**
 - R3D achieved an average success rate of 55.2%, significantly higher than ACT+PonderV2 (42.4%) and DP3 (23.0%).
 - For the fine-grained "PegInsertion" task, R3D maintained high success rates through its subtasks: "Grasp" (97%), "Align" (75%), and "Insert" (21%). This contrasts with ACT+PonderV2, which saw significant drops in "Align" (23%) and "Insert" (2%), demonstrating R3D's superior spatial understanding for precise manipulation.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

The ablation studies (Table 7, Fig. 6) provided insights into the effectiveness of specific architectural and training choices:

- **Spatially-Aware Dense Feature Conditioning:** Replacing global feature conditioning with attention-based spatially aware dense feature conditioning (from pre-trained PointSAM) resulted in a substantial performance improvement (e.g., average success rate increased from 62.5% to 77.5%). It also led to more stable training curves (Fig. 6a).
- **Encoder Pretraining:** Utilizing a pre-trained encoder (from 3D segmentation tasks) consistently yielded better performance compared to training the encoder from scratch (e.g., average success rate of 77.5% vs. 65.75%). Pretraining also contributed to more stable training (Fig. 6b).
- **Auxiliary End-Effector (EE) Prediction:** Incorporating the auxiliary task for end-effector pose prediction provided a modest but consistent improvement in the average success rate (e.g., from 77.5% to 79.75%).

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

Real-world evaluations were conducted on an xArm6 robot with two RGB-D cameras across three manipulation tasks (and an additional three tasks in the appendix).

- **Performance on Diverse Real-World Tasks (Table 8, Table A):** R3D consistently outperformed all baselines. On "Place Kettle," R3D achieved 76% success compared to DP's 64% and ManiFlow's 36%. For "Open Drawer," R3D achieved 64% versus ManiFlow's 46%. On "Fold Towel," R3D reached 66% compared to Pi0's 62% and ManiFlow's 60%. Across six diverse tasks (including those in the appendix), R3D achieved an average success rate of 60.7% (Table A), significantly higher than DP (44.0%) and DP3 (29.7%).
- **Impact of Camera Views (Table 9):**
 - All methods showed performance improvements when transitioning from a single eye-to-hand camera view to a two-camera configuration.
 - R3D maintained superior performance in both settings, achieving an average success rate of 51.3% with a single view and 68.7% with two views. This indicates R3D's effective utilization of multi-view geometry while also maintaining robust performance with limited viewpoints.
- **Robustness to External Interference (Table 10):**
 - When evaluated under dynamic disco light illumination, all methods experienced performance degradation.
 - However, R3D demonstrated stronger robustness, maintaining better performance compared to baselines under these challenging lighting conditions. For example, on "Place Kettle," R3D's success rate dropped from 76% to 58%, while DP's dropped from 64% to 44%.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions to the field of robotic imitation learning, with substantial potential impact:

1. **Systematic Diagnosis of 3D Policy Failures:** The work identifies and validates critical issues, specifically the adverse effects of Batch Normalization and the omission of 3D data augmentation, as primary inhibitors to stable and scalable 3D policy learning. This diagnosis provides actionable insights that address long-standing problems like the "scaling paradox" in 3D policies.
2. **Introduction of Robust Training Principles:** By demonstrating the efficacy of Layer Normalization and a comprehensive 3D data augmentation pipeline, the research establishes foundational practices for stabilizing training and mitigating overfitting in large-scale 3D policies. These principles can be widely adopted to improve existing and future 3D policy implementations.
3. **Novel and Scalable Architecture (R3D):** The proposed architecture, coupling a Transformer-based 3D encoder with a diffusion decoder, represents a robust design for leveraging powerful 3D perception models. Key architectural choices, such as the LayerNorm-only encoder, preservation of spatial resolution in features, and spatially-aware cross-attention in the decoder, directly address the identified stability and scaling challenges.
4. **Enabling Pre-training for Knowledge Transfer:** By adapting the encoder from 3D segmentation models, R3D explicitly facilitates pre-training on large-scale 3D datasets. This capability is crucial for injecting rich geometric priors, accelerating learning on robot-specific tasks, and enhancing robustness to real-world variations, thereby bridging the gap between general 3D vision and robot control.
5. **Improved Generalization and Precision:** The empirical results consistently demonstrate that R3D significantly outperforms state-of-the-art 2D, 2.5D, and 3D baselines across diverse simulation and real-world manipulation tasks. Its strong performance on "Hard" RoboTwin 2.0 tasks and fine-grained ManiSkill2 tasks highlights improved generalization and spatial reasoning capabilities crucial for complex robot operations.
6. **Enhanced Proprioceptive Grounding:** The multi-objective decoding strategy with an auxiliary end-effector pose prediction task improves joint control precision by fostering a more robust internal representation of the robot's state relative to the 3D environment.
7. **Empirical Guidance for Future Design:** The systematic analysis of encoder size and decoder depth provides valuable empirical references, guiding future research in designing computationally efficient yet performant 3D policies tailored to specific data densities and task complexities.

In conclusion, this work provides a robust and scalable foundation for 3D imitation learning, addressing critical bottlenecks that have previously hindered its widespread adoption. By enabling the stable integration of advanced 3D perception with effective policy learning paradigms, R3D contributes to the development of more generalizable, precise, and robust robotic agents, moving closer to real-world deployment of visuomotor policies.
