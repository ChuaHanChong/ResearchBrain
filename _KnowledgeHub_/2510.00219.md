# Thoughtbubbles: an Unsupervised Method for Parallel Thinking in Latent Space

> [!summary] Summary
> Thoughtbubbles presents an unsupervised Transformer architecture capable of dynamically creating and pruning parallel computational streams in its latent space, trained solely using the standard language modeling loss. This approach facilitates adaptive resource allocation during inference, leading to lower perplexity than larger baseline models and enhanced zero-shot reasoning performance by focusing computation on areas of higher uncertainty.

## Problem

- Standard Transformer architectures employ a fixed computational budget, processing all tokens with uniform effort regardless of their complexity, limiting their capacity for intricate, multi-step reasoning.
- Current adaptive reasoning methods, such as Chain-of-Thought, are inherently serial, often necessitate explicit supervision or specific prompting, and are not natively integrated into unsupervised pretraining.
- Transformers lack intrinsic mechanisms to adaptively allocate computational resources during pretraining, hindering their ability to develop native reasoning strategies from scratch.

## Method

- The architecture integrates special =="forking layers"== into a GPT-2 style Transformer, allowing tokens to dynamically duplicate their residual streams into =="bubbles"== or delete less important ones, all governed by learned scores.
- ==Cumulative scores==, indicating the importance of each residual stream, modulate both attention mechanisms and residual updates in subsequent Transformer blocks to guide computation.
- A specialized ==Rotational Position Embedding (RoPE) variant== and final weighted averaging of output distributions based on cumulative scores are used to manage forked tokens and produce a single output.

## Results

- Thoughtbubbles models consistently achieved lower perplexity on OpenWebText and peS2o validation sets; for instance, a **319M** parameter model outperformed a **772M** parameter baseline.
- The method demonstrated improved performance on zero-shot tasks requiring reasoning and context extraction, such as LAMBADA and HellaSwag, across various model scales.
- Qualitative analysis showed computation being allocated to uncertain regions and coreferent entities, and dynamic budget scaling enabled sustained performance in autoregressive generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamic, parallel adaptive computation in a Transformer's latent space can be learned in an unsupervised manner using only the standard language modeling loss during pretraining.
> - This adaptive computation enables models to achieve superior performance in language modeling and reasoning tasks with fewer parameters, outperforming larger, non-adaptive baselines.
> - The model learns to interpretably allocate more computational resources (forks) to regions of higher uncertainty within the input sequence, demonstrating task-relevant adaptivity.
