# NoRD: A Data-Efficient Vision-Language-Action Model that Drives without Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Applied Intuition developed NORD, a Vision-Language-Action model for autonomous driving that achieves competitive performance without explicit reasoning or extensive data by adapting Dr. GRPO for reinforcement learning post-training. This approach reached a PDM score of 85.6 on NAVSIM and an RFS of 7.709 on WaymoE2E, utilizing significantly less training data and demonstrating improved runtime efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving incur high costs due to extensive data collection, detailed reasoning annotations, and increased training/inference latency.
- Standard Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training methods often require large datasets with reasoning traces to achieve high performance, limiting scalability.
- Current RL post-training algorithms like GRPO exhibit a 'difficulty bias,' failing to effectively optimize weaker SFT policies on complex driving scenarios with sparse and high-variance reward signals.

## Method

- NORD (==No Reasoning for Driving==) is a Vision-Language-Action model built on Qwen-2.5VL-3B-Instruct, designed for data and token efficiency by eliminating reasoning annotations.
- It employs a two-stage training pipeline: initial Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on limited data to create a '==weak SFT policy==,' followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training.
- The RL post-training phase utilizes Dr. GRPO, a modified policy optimization algorithm that removes the standard deviation term from advantage calculation to mitigate 'difficulty bias' and enable effective learning from high-variance, challenging scenarios.

## Results

- NORD achieved a PDM score of 85.6 on the NAVSIM benchmark and, in a best-of-N configuration, outperformed the reasoning-based AutoVLA-BoN with a PDM score of 92.4 (vs. 92.1).
- On the WaymoE2E benchmark, NORD obtained an RFS of 7.709 and an ADE at 3 seconds of 1.2504, outperforming models that used 12-**17x** more training data and often relied on reasoning.
- The Dr. GRPO modification improved the PDM score of the NORD-BASE policy by +**11.68%** (from 76.66 to 85.62), a substantial gain compared to the marginal +**0.67%** achieved with standard GRPO.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Standard GRPO's 'difficulty bias,' which disproportionately penalizes high-variance scenarios, is a major impediment to optimizing weaker SFT policies for complex driving tasks.
> - Dr. GRPO successfully addresses this bias by allowing 'hard' scenarios to contribute sufficient gradient signals, leading to substantial performance gains in data-efficient, reasoning-free VLA models.
> - High-performance autonomous driving VLAs can be developed without explicit language-based reasoning or extensive datasets, shifting the learning burden to robust RL fine-tuning and challenging the perceived necessity of reasoning traces.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous driving is experiencing a shift towards Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which aim to replace traditional modular pipelines with unified, end-to-end architectures. This paradigm often involves a two-stage training process: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on large datasets with detailed natural language Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning annotations, followed by reinforcement learning (RL) post-training using methods like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to align outputs with driving metrics. This approach has yielded competitive performance on complex driving benchmarks.

However, the prevailing VLA training methodology presents several challenges related to scalability and resource intensity. These include the significant data cost associated with collecting and curating vast quantities of specialized driving scenarios, the high annotation cost for generating detailed reasoning traces, and the increased training and inference costs (due to token load and latency) resulting from explicit reasoning. These limitations motivate an investigation into more efficient alternatives.

Prior research has explored two main areas relevant to this work. First, studies on the "Reasoning-Planning Decoupling Hypothesis" have questioned the causal necessity of explicit reasoning for planning outputs, suggesting that textual priors alone might suffice. Second, some end-to-end models (e.g., EMMA, SimLingo, S4-Driver, ADriver-I, DrivingGPT, DiffVLA) have demonstrated strong performance on nominal driving benchmarks without explicit reasoning. However, these reasoning-free approaches have generally not achieved competitive performance on the more complex, long-tail benchmarks where reasoning-centric models currently excel, such as NAVSIM and WaymoE2E.

Additionally, existing data-efficient VLA research often mitigates data scarcity by leveraging massive external out-of-domain datasets. This work, however, addresses the more specific challenge of training a competitive model using only small-sized, specialized, in-domain driving data.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by investigating whether competitive performance on challenging driving benchmarks can be achieved using a VLA that is both reasoning-free and data-efficient. It specifically identifies and addresses a limitation in current RL post-training methods (GRPO) that hinders their effectiveness when applied to models trained on smaller, reasoning-free datasets, a phenomenon termed "difficulty bias." By proposing a solution to this bias, the work aims to bridge the performance gap between reasoning-free and reasoning-based methods while maintaining high data efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model for autonomous driving, named NORD (No Reasoning for Driving), that achieves competitive performance on challenging driving benchmarks while being both reasoning-free and data-efficient.

This objective is motivated by several identified shortcomings and inefficiencies in existing state-of-the-art VLA training paradigms:

1. **High Data and Annotation Costs:** Current VLA models for autonomous driving heavily rely on massive datasets of specialized driving scenarios. Furthermore, these datasets often require dense, natural language Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning annotations, which are expensive and time-consuming to generate. The paper aims to reduce both the volume of required training data and the need for costly reasoning annotations.

2. **Computational Overhead:** The reliance on reasoning annotations leads to a higher token load during training and inference. This increases training time and introduces inference latency, which can be impractical for real-world autonomous driving systems requiring real-time responses. Eliminating reasoning is expected to improve token efficiency and inference speed.

3. **Ineffectiveness of Standard RL for Weak SFT Policies:** The authors initially trained a reasoning-free, data-efficient VLA model (NORD-BASE) using supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on a significantly smaller dataset than conventional models. This NORD-BASE model exhibited substantially lower performance compared to reasoning-based baselines. Subsequent post-training with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a widely adopted RL method, yielded only marginal improvements. This observation suggested that either explicit reasoning is indeed a necessary component for high-performance driving, or there is an underlying issue with how RL post-training interacts with weak SFT policies.

4. **Identification of "Difficulty Bias" in GRPO:** Through empirical analysis, the authors identified that the limited effectiveness of GRPO stemmed from a "difficulty bias." In the context of the weak, data-efficient SFT policy and the complex, sparse reward signals from driving benchmarks (e.g., PDM score), the distribution of intra-group rewards became highly polarized. Scenarios yielding intermediate mean rewards, often representing complex or challenging maneuvers, were characterized by high-variance rollouts. The standard GRPO formulation, which uses the standard deviation of rewards in its advantage estimation, disproportionately penalizes these high-variance scenarios. This attenuation of the learning signal prevents effective optimization of the majority of challenging cases, giving the false impression that the underlying SFT policy is inherently limited.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to demonstrate that high-performance autonomous driving VLAs can be developed efficiently without the overhead of explicit reasoning or massive datasets, thereby pushing the boundaries of data and inference efficiency in the domain.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The NORD model is built upon the Qwen-2.5VL-3B-Instruct base model and adopts a two-stage training pipeline designed for data and token efficiency.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture and Input/Output Representation

NORD directly predicts action tokens without requiring explicit reasoning traces.
- **Inputs:** The model takes as input the past ego-trajectory, current speed, acceleration, and multi-view RGB images from the front, front-left, and front-right cameras. For NAVSIM, 3 camera frames, 3 past trajectory tokens (1.5 seconds), current velocity, acceleration, and a high-level driving command are used. For WaymoE2E, 6 past trajectory tokens (3 seconds) are used.
- **Output:** NORD predicts the future ego-trajectory at 10 Hz. For NAVSIM, 8 future trajectory tokens (4-second horizon) are predicted, and for WaymoE2E, 10 future tokens (5-second horizon).
- **Trajectory Representation:** To improve token efficiency, trajectories are represented using k-disc tokenization. Future trajectories in the training set are interpolated to 10 Hz, segmented into 0.5-second intervals, and then clustered into 2048 clusters based on contour distance. These cluster centers form a discrete trajectory codebook, allowing reconstruction of any trajectory using vocabulary tokens. These new trajectory tokens are appended to the original Qwen vocabulary and initialized from a multivariate normal distribution.

#### 3.2. Two-Stage Training Pipeline

1. **Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with Limited Data (NORD-BASE)**
 - **Objective:** To offload the majority of the learning burden to the subsequent RL post-training phase, NORD-BASE is intentionally trained on a limited dataset. For example, 80,000 NAVSIM training samples and 12,000 WaymoE2E samples are used.
 - **Process:** Trajectory prediction is framed as a next-token prediction problem, where the model outputs trajectory tokens conditioned on the visual and kinematic inputs.
 - **Expected Outcome:** This stage results in an initially weaker SFT policy (NORD-BASE) with lower performance compared to models trained on larger datasets or with reasoning.
 - **Implementation:** Qwen-2.5VL-3B-Instruct is fine-tuned on NAVSIM and WaymoE2E separately using 16 A100 GPUs. The AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 5 × 10⁻⁵ and a cosine decay schedule is employed, with all layers fine-tuned. Mixed-precision training (bf16) and gradient checkpointing are used for memory efficiency. DeepSpeed ZeRO Stage 3 is applied for WaymoE2E.

2. **Stage 2: RL Post-Training using Dr. GRPO**
 - **Problem with Standard GRPO:** The paper identifies that standard GRPO fails to effectively optimize the weak NORD-BASE policy. Analysis of the reward landscape reveals a "difficulty bias": scenarios with high or very low group-mean PDM scores exhibit low reward variance, while challenging scenarios with intermediate group-mean scores produce high-variance rollouts. GRPO's formulation, which includes a standard deviation term in the denominator of its group relative advantage ($\\hat{A}^{GRPO}_{i,t} = \frac{r(o_i|x) - \frac{1}{G}\sum_{j=1}^{G}r(o_j|x)}{\text{std}_{j=1...,G}(r(o_j|x))}$), disproportionately attenuates the learning signal for groups with high reward variance. This prevents GRPO from optimizing the majority of challenging samples.
 - **Solution: Dr. GRPO:** To mitigate this difficulty bias, the authors adopt Dr. GRPO, an existing policy optimization algorithm previously designed for LLM reasoning. Dr. GRPO modifies the group relative advantage by *removing the standard deviation term* from the denominator. The modified advantage is given by: $\\hat{A}^{DrGRPO}_{i,t} = r(o_i|x) - \frac{1}{G}\sum_{j=1}^{G}r(o_i|x)$. This ensures that "hard" scenarios, characterized by high intra-group reward variance, contribute a sufficient gradient signal, enabling more effective optimization of weak policies.
 - **Additional Techniques:** DAPO-style asymmetric clipping is used to prevent entropy collapse during RL training, and KL-divergence regularization is not employed, following prior work on Dr. GRPO.
 - **Implementation:** RL post-training is performed using 30-32 A100 GPUs. For NAVSIM, NORD-BASE is optimized for 160 steps with a constant learning rate of 5 × 10⁻⁶. For WaymoE2E, 150 steps with a learning rate of 1 × 10⁻⁶ are used. The RL pipeline leverages Verl for distributed training and vLLM for efficient rollout generation. A group size of 8 is used for rollouts, with a sampling temperature of 1.0. Validation uses deterministic sampling at 0.01 temperature.
 - **Reward Functions:** The primary reward functions are PDM score for NAVSIM and Normalized RFS for WaymoE2E. These are augmented with additional rewards for trajectory length and output format (each weighted 0.25), and then normalized to a range.

#### 3.3. Datasets

- **NAVSIM:** Comprises 120 hours of real-world urban driving data. The task is to predict ego-vehicle trajectories for the next 4 seconds at 2 Hz, evaluated by PDM score (safety, progress, comfort) in a simulation environment.
- **Waymo Vision-Based End-to-End Dataset (WaymoE2E):** Contains 4,021 challenging driving segments with 360° camera views and human-rated alternative trajectories. Predicted trajectories are evaluated by the Rated Feedback Score (RFS), which measures similarity to reference preference trajectories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the limitations of standard RL methods for data-efficient, reasoning-free VLAs and the effectiveness of the proposed NORD approach.

1. **Confirmation of GRPO's Limitation on Weak SFT Policies:**
 - Initial training of NORD-BASE, a reasoning-free VLA model SFT-tuned on only 80,000 NAVSIM training samples (a >60% data reduction compared to reasoning-based models), resulted in performance significantly lower than reasoning-based baselines.
 - Applying standard GRPO post-training to NORD-BASE yielded a marginal improvement of only +0.67% (from 76.66 to 77.18 PDM score on NAVSIM), indicating its ineffectiveness in significantly boosting performance for such weak SFT policies. This contrasts with previous works like AutoVLA, which achieved a 9% boost, but on a substantially larger, reasoning-annotated SFT dataset.

2. **Characterization of Difficulty Bias:**
 - Analysis of the reward distribution for NORD-BASE revealed a polarized landscape. Scenarios with very high (≥ 0.8) or very low (≤ 0.15) group-mean PDM scores exhibited low intra-group reward variance, indicating consistent model behavior on simple or extremely difficult tasks.
 - Conversely, scenarios with intermediate group-mean PDM scores (in the range [0.2, 0.65]), often corresponding to complex maneuvers, were characterized by high intra-group reward variance.
 - Observation of GRPO's training evolution showed that it primarily optimized samples in the low-variance regions, leading to an increase in density for scores near 1. However, the density in the high-variance region ([0.2, 0.65]) remained largely unchanged, confirming that GRPO struggled to learn from these more challenging, high-variance samples due to its difficulty bias.

3. **Effectiveness of Dr. GRPO in Mitigating Difficulty Bias:**
 - As a drop-in replacement for GRPO, Dr. GRPO post-training on NORD-BASE resulted in an 11.68% performance improvement (from 76.66 to 85.62 PDM score on NAVSIM). This represents a substantial gain compared to the 0.67% from standard GRPO.
 - Dr. GRPO was observed to effectively optimize samples with high intra-group variance during training, leading to significant overall performance gains.
 - Qualitative results demonstrated that NORD, when trained with Dr. GRPO, successfully learned complex maneuvers such as sharp turns and lane changes without collisions, which GRPO-trained models failed to achieve (Figure 4).

4. **Competitive Performance of NORD:**
 - **WaymoE2E Benchmark:** NORD achieved a Rated Feedback Score (RFS) of 7.709, positioning it as the third best-performing VLA on this benchmark. It was the only top model that did not use reasoning traces or ensembling. NORD accomplished this with a significantly reduced dataset (12,000 SFT samples and 450 RLFT samples), requiring 12 to 17 times less data than competitive models like HMVLM and Poutine. Furthermore, NORD surpassed all other competitive models on the Average Displacement Error (ADE) metric, despite using at least 6 times less training data.
 - **NAVSIM Benchmark:** NORD achieved a PDM score of 85.6. It distinguished itself as the only model that did not require reasoning traces, relied solely on 3 camera frames, and used no additional features like LiDAR or HD Maps. NORD achieved this performance with fewer than 90,000 samples, whereas other VLA models like AutoVLA and RecogDrive required 1.6 to 34 times more training data.
 - **NORD-BoN (Best-of-N):** In a best-of-N setting (selecting the best trajectory out of 6 predictions), NORD-BoN achieved a PDM score of 92.4 on NAVSIM, surpassing even the reasoning-based AutoVLA-BoN.

5. **Efficiency and Scalability:**
 - Pareto-front analyses on both NAVSIM and WaymoE2E demonstrated NORD's position on the "efficiency frontier," indicating it provides competitive performance while operating in a high-data-efficiency regime.
 - NORD exhibited significantly lower inference time and token count compared to other VLAs (Figure 9), highlighting its token and runtime efficiency. This contributes to reduced training costs and faster real-time deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research conducted in this paper, leading to the development of NORD, carries several significant implications and potential impacts on the field of autonomous driving and broader machine learning for robotics.

1. **Challenging the Necessity of Explicit Reasoning and Massive Datasets:** A primary impact of NORD is its empirical challenge to the conventional wisdom that high-performance autonomous driving VLAs inherently require both extensive language-based reasoning annotations and massive training datasets. By demonstrating competitive performance without these costly components, NORD suggests that prior assumptions about their necessity might be reconsidered, particularly in light of more effective policy optimization techniques.

2. **Enhancing Practicality and Scalability of Autonomous Systems:** The elimination of reasoning annotations and the substantial reduction in training data requirements directly translate into lower training costs, faster inference times, and reduced token usage. These efficiencies address critical non-scalable costs associated with current VLA paradigms, making advanced autonomous driving systems more practical, scalable, and viable for real-world deployment, where real-time performance and resource constraints are paramount.

3. **Methodological Advancement in RL for Weak Policies:** The identification and detailed characterization of "difficulty bias" in Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) when applied to weak Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) policies is a notable methodological contribution. The successful application of Dr. GRPO to mitigate this bias in the autonomous driving domain extends the utility of RL post-training to scenarios with limited initial SFT data. This insight could influence future developments in RL algorithms, particularly in fields where initial policies may be weak or data is scarce.

4. **Democratization of Advanced Autonomous Driving Research:** By lowering the data and computational barriers, NORD's approach could democratize access to advanced autonomous driving model development. Researchers and organizations with fewer resources might find it more feasible to develop and iterate on competitive VLA models, fostering broader participation and innovation in the field.

5. **Direction for Future Research:** This work opens several avenues for future investigation. While Dr. GRPO improved performance by mitigating difficulty bias, the paper acknowledges that it remains imperfect. This suggests further research into more robust bias mitigation strategies, alternative policy optimization algorithms, and deeper theoretical understanding of reward landscapes for complex, safety-critical tasks. Additionally, it prompts a re-evaluation of reasoning-based VLAs, encouraging future work to more rigorously justify the benefits of explicit reasoning given NORD's performance without it.

In summary, NORD represents a step towards more efficient and accessible autonomous driving systems, demonstrating that a focus on effective policy optimization can yield high performance even when traditional, resource-intensive components are omitted.
