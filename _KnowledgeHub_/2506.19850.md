# Unified Vision-Language-Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> UniVLA introduces a unified vision-language-action (VLA) model that processes all modalities as discrete tokens within a shared autoregressive Transformer. This framework achieves state-of-the-art results on robotic manipulation benchmarks like CALVIN, LIBERO (95.5% average success rate), and SimplerEnv by leveraging a world model trained on large-scale action-free video data.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often rely on a language-centric paradigm and late-fusion strategies, limiting deep cross-modal integration.
- Prior VLA approaches struggle to capture temporal and causal dependencies within the perception-action loop, particularly for complex, long-horizon tasks.
- The scarcity and high cost of collecting action-annotated robotic demonstration data restrict the scalability and generalizability of policy learning.

## Method

- **UniVLA** develops a unified multimodal model that represents vision, language, and action as ==discrete tokens== within a shared vocabulary, processed by an 8.5 billion parameter ==autoregressive Transformer==.
- A two-stage training paradigm is employed: 1) post-training a ==world model== on large-scale ==action-free video== datasets to learn environment dynamics by predicting future visual content, and 2) fine-tuning for policy learning using action-annotated data.
- The model frames embodied planning as ==sequence modeling==, where observations and actions are causally interleaved, leveraging the autoregressive nature to capture temporal dependencies.

## Results

- UniVLA achieves state-of-the-art performance on the CALVIN benchmark and sets new SOTA across all LIBERO suites with an average success rate of **95.5%**, improving LIBERO-Long to **94.0%**.
- World model post-training provides substantial performance gains, enabling higher success rates on CALVIN using only **10%** of the fine-tuning data and faster convergence.
- The model demonstrates multimodal capabilities beyond policy learning, including spatial reasoning via text output and future state prediction via visual output, with promising preliminary results in autonomous driving.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying vision, language, and action into a single token-based, autoregressive framework enables tighter cross-modal integration and learning of temporal and causal dynamics.
> - Training a world model on abundant action-free video data significantly improves downstream policy learning efficiency and generalization, addressing robot data scarcity.
> - Visual prediction capabilities, even during fine-tuning, naturally integrate world model learning into policy learning, leading to performance improvements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of intelligent agents capable of interacting with the physical world has been a long-standing goal in artificial intelligence. This paper positions UniVLA within the rapidly evolving landscape of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which aim to equip robots with the ability to perceive, reason, and act, guided by human-like instructions.

The authors critically review existing VLA approaches, categorizing them into two main paradigms: "pure action prediction" and "visual-guided action prediction."
- **Pure action prediction** models (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA, π0-FAST) extend Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to directly output actions. While successful in generalization and semantic grounding, they often suffer from a lack of deep spatial understanding and visual prediction capabilities, and primarily treat visual observations as static inputs. This approach typically involves a "late-fusion" strategy where visual features are extracted by a separate encoder before being combined with language for action generation.
- **Visual-guided action prediction** models (e.g., SuSIE, UniPi, GR series) leverage visual pretraining by predicting future visual signals and then deriving actions. While incorporating visual generation, they often require separating generative and action prediction models, which can limit the full potential of VLMs for integrated understanding.

UniVLA addresses the inherent limitations of these prior approaches, particularly their reliance on static, language-centric paradigms that overlook the rich temporal and causal structure of visual observations and real-world interactions. The paper argues that current VLA models often struggle with forming "deeply coupled cross-modal representations" and learning "temporal and causal dependencies across the perception-action loop."

UniVLA introduces a novel architectural alternative by unifying vision, language, and action into a **single, autoregressive, token-based framework with a shared vocabulary.** This unified representation allows for a "native multimodal model" that can concurrently handle diverse outputs (text, vision, action) and learn from large-scale, temporally rich video data. By explicitly modeling causal dynamics through a "world model" during post-training, UniVLA bridges the gap between vision-language understanding and effective policy learning, particularly for complex, long-horizon tasks. This approach represents a significant departure from VLM-centric or separated generative models, aiming for a more holistic and integrated understanding of embodied intelligence. Its focus on leveraging action-free video data for world modeling also addresses a major challenge in robotics: data scarcity for action-annotated demonstrations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of UniVLA is to develop a unified and native multimodal Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that can perceive, reason, and act in the physical world more effectively than previous approaches. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing VLA Models:** The authors identify that most current VLA models suffer from a "language-centric paradigm" and "late-fusion strategy," where visual observations are projected into a semantic space, and actions are subsequently derived. This limits deep cross-modal integration and the learning of temporal/causal dependencies. UniVLA aims to address this by jointly modeling all modalities.
- **Tighter Cross-Modal Integration:** A central question posed is: "Can vision, language, and action be jointly modeled within a unified representation space to facilitate tighter cross-modal integration and more effective policy learning?" UniVLA proposes a solution where all modalities (vision, language, action) are transformed into discrete tokens within a shared vocabulary, enabling truly joint learning.
- **Capturing Temporal and Causal Dynamics:** Real-world interactions are inherently dynamic and causal. Previous models often adopt static, image-to-action mappings that fail to leverage the rich temporal information in videos. UniVLA's autoregressive, Markov chain-based sequence modeling approach is designed to inherently incorporate causal dependencies and reason over temporal dynamics.
- **Leveraging Large-Scale Video Data:** Robotic action data is expensive to collect. The paper motivates the use of "world modeling" during post-training to learn from large-scale robotics videos, including those without explicit action annotations, thereby enabling scalable and generalizable learning.
- **Enhancing Policy Learning, Especially for Long-Horizon Tasks:** The authors hypothesize that capturing causal dynamics through world modeling will significantly enhance downstream policy learning, particularly for long-horizon and out-of-distribution tasks where temporal reasoning is crucial.
- **Enabling Flexible Multimodal and Multi-task Capabilities:** By unifying modalities and sequence modeling across tasks, UniVLA aims to support a broad range of multimodal outputs (e.g., visual prediction, spatial reasoning) and tasks (e.g., perception grounding, policy learning) within a single architecture.

In essence, the core motivation is to move beyond mere action prediction to a more comprehensive understanding of embodied intelligence, where an agent can deeply integrate vision, language, and action for robust and generalizable performance across diverse and complex real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniVLA's methodology is characterized by a "unified paradigm" that encompasses both multimodal and multi-task learning, implemented through a two-stage training strategy leveraging an autoregressive Transformer architecture.

#### 3.1. Unified Multimodal Model

The cornerstone of UniVLA is its approach to representing all modalities—language, vision, and action—as discrete tokens within a shared vocabulary. This is a significant departure from traditional VLA models that might use separate encoders for each modality or only convert language and actions to tokens.

- **Tokenization:**
 - **Vision:** Visual observations (images) are discretized using a Vector-Quantized (VQ) tokenizer, similar to Emu3. This converts high-dimensional continuous image data into a sequence of discrete visual tokens. Special tokens like `boi` (begin of image) and `eoi` (end of image) demarcate image boundaries.
 - **Action:** Continuous action sequences are transformed into discrete action tokens using the FAST tokenizer. This involves applying a Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) to the action sequence within a time window, then quantizing the coefficients to produce a sequence of discrete tokens. `boa` (begin of action) and `eoa` (end of action) tokens are used for action boundaries. Actions are represented as a d-dimensional vector (e.g., [x, y, z, roll, pitch, yaw, gripper]).
 - **Language:** Textual instructions are processed into standard discrete language tokens.
- **Shared Vocabulary and Autoregressive Transformer:** All discrete tokens (vision, language, action) are drawn from a shared vocabulary and concatenated into a single, interleaved multimodal sequence. This sequence is then fed into a purely autoregressive Transformer (specifically, an 8.5 billion parameter model identical to Emu3). The model learns to predict the next token in the sequence, naturally preserving temporal alignment and causal dependencies.
- **Training Objective:** The training objective for all tasks is a standard next-token prediction task using cross-entropy loss. The flexibility of the tokenized sequence allows selective application of loss computation to specific token types based on the task (e.g., only vision tokens for world modeling, only action tokens for policy learning).

#### 3.2. Unified Multimodal Sequence Modeling (Two-Stage Training Paradigm)

The embodied planning problem is framed as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), where observations and actions are interleaved causally. UniVLA adopts a two-stage training strategy:

- **Stage 1: Post-training (World Model Objective):**
 - **Goal:** To learn the dynamics of the environment (transition function P(s_t+1 | s_t, a_t)) and capture causal dynamics from large-scale video data.
 - **Data:** Large-scale robot-centric video datasets, crucially, **without requiring action annotations**. This is a key advantage for scalable learning.
 - **Sequence Formulation:** `S_v = {L_t^1, L_v^1, L_v^2..., L_v^t}`. Here, `L_t^1` is the initial language instruction, and `L_v^i` are visual observations. The model is trained to predict future visual content.
 - **Supervision:** Loss is computed *solely from the vision tokens*.
 - **Initialization:** The model is initialized with a vision-language (VL) aligned checkpoint (from Emu3), providing basic VL capabilities.
- **Stage 2: Fine-tuning (Policy Learning):**
 - **Goal:** To learn optimal actions based on current observations and prior states, refining task-specific behaviors.
 - **Initialization:** The model is initialized with weights from the world-model post-training stage.
 - **Sequence Formulation:** `S_a = {L_t^1, L_v^1, L_a^1, L_v^2, L_a^2..., L_v^t, L_a^t}`. This interleaved format includes language, vision, and action tokens.
 - **Supervision:** Loss is computed *solely from the action tokens*.

This two-stage approach posits that pre-training a robust world model on vast amounts of video data (even action-free) helps the model internalize environment dynamics and causality, which then significantly enhances the efficiency and effectiveness of subsequent policy learning, especially for complex, long-horizon tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

UniVLA demonstrates significant advancements in VLA modeling, achieving state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance across multiple challenging robotic manipulation benchmarks and showcasing broad applicability.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance

- **CALVIN Benchmark (Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation):** UniVLA achieves the highest performance on both ABC→D and ABCD→D generalization settings. For instance, on ABC→D, UniVLA achieves an average length of 4.41 tasks completed in a row, surpassing previous SOTA (e.g., RoboVLMs at 4.25 and Seer-Large at 4.28). This highlights its strong capabilities in multi-task learning and long-horizon planning.
- **LIBERO Benchmark (Lifelong Robotic Manipulation):** UniVLA sets new SOTA across all LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), with an impressive average success rate of 95.5%. This significantly surpasses the previous SOTA, π0-FAST, which achieved 85.5%. Notably, UniVLA shows remarkable gains on long-horizon tasks (LIBERO-Long), improving the success rate from 69.0% (CoT-VLA) to 94.0%, demonstrating superior temporal and compositional reasoning.
- **SimplerEnv Benchmark (Real-to-Sim Manipulation):** UniVLA shows substantial improvement on the Bridge-WidowX setup, increasing the average success rate from 42.7% (SpatialVLA) to 69.8%. It particularly improves performance on previously difficult tasks like "stack block," "put carrot," and "put spoon."

#### 4.2. Effectiveness of World Model Post-Training

- **Enhanced Performance:** A crucial finding is that the world model post-training strategy yields the most substantial gains across all benchmarks, enhancing both generalization and long-horizon planning capabilities. Compared to no post-training, action-only post-training (which performed poorly due to action space inconsistencies), text-to-image training, or video-only prediction, the world model approach (text and video as input, vision loss) consistently leads to superior downstream policy learning.
- **Scalability and Data Efficiency:** This approach enables scalable learning from large-scale video datasets that do *not* require action annotations, a significant practical advantage given the cost of collecting expert demonstrations.
- **Improved Training Efficiency:** Post-training substantially enhances downstream policy learning efficiency. On CALVIN, UniVLA achieves higher success rates using only 10% of the fine-tuning data compared to prior methods. It also demonstrates faster convergence with fewer fine-tuning iterations.

#### 4.3. In-Depth Analysis and Ablations

- **Visual Prediction:** Even without dedicated post-training, fine-tuning with visual loss supervision (leveraging the model's autoregressive nature) naturally integrates world model learning into policy learning, leading to a significant performance improvement.
- **History Context:** Incorporating a history window (past observations and actions) during fine-tuning significantly improves performance, although there are diminishing returns beyond a certain length, consistent with the Markov property.

#### 4.4. Multimodal Capabilities and Broader Applications

- **Qualitative Demonstrations:** UniVLA showcases its ability to interleave multiple modalities, enabling not only policy learning for embodied control but also spatial reasoning through language output and future state prediction via visual output. This highlights its potential for generalizable multimodal understanding.
- **Autonomous Driving Transfer:** Preliminary experiments on the NAVSIM benchmark for autonomous driving demonstrate UniVLA's potential for broader real-world applications. Despite being a pure autoregressive, token-based framework using only front-view camera inputs (no BEV or multi-sensor fusion), it achieves strong performance, indicating its adaptability beyond robotic manipulation.

In summary, UniVLA's results validate its unified token-based, autoregressive framework, especially the efficacy of its world model post-training for robust, efficient, and generalizable policy learning across diverse and challenging robotic tasks, while also showing promise in other embodied AI domains like autonomous driving.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UniVLA paper presents a highly significant contribution to the field of embodied AI and robotic learning, with several profound potential impacts:

#### 5.1. Novel Unified Paradigm

UniVLA introduces a truly unified paradigm for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) modeling. By encoding vision, language, and action into discrete tokens within a shared vocabulary and modeling them autoregressively, it offers a novel architectural alternative to existing VLA models. This design facilitates deeper and more integrated cross-modal understanding, moving beyond late-fusion or separate generative-action models. This paradigm could inspire future foundational models that inherently treat all sensory inputs and motor outputs as a single, cohesive information stream.

#### 5.2. Leveraging Large-Scale Unannotated Data through World Models

One of the most critical challenges in robot learning is the scarcity and cost of collecting high-quality, action-annotated demonstration data. UniVLA's innovative use of a world model during post-training allows it to learn rich temporal dynamics and causality from large-scale *action-free* video datasets. This capability significantly broadens the scope of usable training data, potentially unlocking the vast amount of unlabeled video data available on the internet, thereby enabling more scalable and generalizable robot learning. This could lead to a paradigm shift in how robot policies are trained, making them less reliant on expensive human teleoperation.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Performance and Efficiency in Policy Learning

The substantial performance gains on complex benchmarks like CALVIN, LIBERO (especially long-horizon tasks), and SimplerEnv highlight UniVLA's effectiveness. The observed improvements in data and training efficiency (e.g., higher success with 10% of fine-tuning data, faster convergence) directly translate to more practical and accessible robot learning pipelines. This means that advanced robotic capabilities could be achieved with less data and computational resources for specific tasks once the foundational world model is established.

#### 5.4. Towards Generalist Embodied Intelligence

By demonstrating versatility across diverse manipulation tasks and showing promising preliminary results in autonomous driving, UniVLA pushes the boundaries towards truly generalist embodied intelligence. Its ability to generate multimodal outputs (actions, visual predictions, spatial reasoning via text) within a single framework suggests a more holistic understanding of the world, moving beyond task-specific policies. This generalizability is a key step towards robots that can adapt to a wide array of unforeseen situations and instructions in the real world.

#### 5.5. Open-Source Contribution

The paper mentions UniVLA as "an open-source VLA method supporting large-scale video training." If the full model and training code are indeed released, this would be a significant contribution to the research community, enabling broader adoption, further development, and direct comparison, accelerating progress in the field.

#### 5.6. Foundation for Future Research

UniVLA's success in integrating world models with autoregressive token-based VLA learning opens up numerous avenues for future research. This includes exploring the scalability of world model pre-training with even larger video datasets, deeper integration with reinforcement learning paradigms for more adaptive policies, and extending the framework to more complex real-world embodied agents beyond manipulation and driving. The limitations of current computational resources for scaling indicate a direction for the entire field.

In conclusion, UniVLA offers a compelling new direction for VLA modeling, fundamentally rethinking how perception, language, and action are integrated. Its emphasis on a unified token space and the power of world models to leverage vast amounts of passive video data has the potential to significantly impact the development of more capable, efficient, and generalist intelligent robots.
