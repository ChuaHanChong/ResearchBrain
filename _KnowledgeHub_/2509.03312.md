# AgenTracer: Who Is Inducing Failure in the LLM Agentic Systems?

> [!summary] Summary
> AgenTracer introduces an automated framework to generate annotated failure trajectories for multi-agent LLM systems and trains a specialized, lightweight model (AgenTracer-8B) for precise failure attribution. This system identifies the root cause of failures, outperforming larger proprietary LLMs and enabling self-correction in existing multi-agent systems with performance gains up to 14.2%.

## Problem

- Multi-agent LLM systems suffer from high failure rates (up to 86.7%) due to increased complexity and integration of diverse modules, making them fragile and unreliable.
- Debugging these systems relies on manual, labor-intensive analysis of verbose logs, as existing automated failure attribution methods using general-purpose LLMs achieve accuracies generally below 10%.
- A critical lack of large-scale, high-fidelity annotated failure trajectory data hinders the development and systematic evaluation of robust failure attribution models.

## Method

- An automated pipeline generates a large-scale, high-fidelity dataset (==TracerTraj==-2.5K) of annotated multi-agent failure trajectories using counterfactual intervention on failed traces and programmatic fault injection on successful ones.
- A lightweight, specialized failure tracer, **AgenTracer**-8B (based on QWEN3-8B), is trained using this curated dataset via Online Reinforcement Learning.
- The training incorporates a multi-granular reward design, combining strict format compliance, agent-level accuracy, and a soft, Gaussian kernel-based step-level reward for precise temporal localization.

## Results

- AgenTracer-**8B** consistently and substantially outperforms state-of-the-art proprietary LLMs (e.g., GPT-4.1, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Claude-4-Sonnet) in agent-level accuracy by up to **26.0%** on the Who&When benchmark, while also demonstrating robustness without ground truth.
- It provides actionable feedback that boosts the performance of mainstream multi-agent systems, achieving gains ranging from **4.8%** to **14.2%** across diverse systems and benchmarks, proving superior to conventional reflection.
- The work successfully creates TracerTraj-**2.5K**, a dataset comprising over 2,000 high-fidelity annotated trajectory-error pairs, addressing a critical data scarcity problem for training diagnostic models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - General-purpose, even large proprietary, LLMs are fundamentally inadequate for precise failure attribution in complex multi-agent systems, often achieving surprisingly low step-level accuracy (around 30%).
> - Specialized training with carefully curated, large-scale data generated through automated pipelines, combined with a multi-granular reinforcement learning approach, is crucial for building effective and lightweight diagnostic AI for agentic systems.
> - Accurate and actionable failure attribution provides a mechanism for existing multi-agent systems to self-correct and improve their performance, enabling significant gains over traditional reflection-based methods and fostering self-evolving AI.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of LLM-based agents represents a significant frontier in AI, extending the capabilities of large language models beyond simple prompt-response interactions to more complex, autonomous task execution. These agents demonstrate impressive proficiency across various cognitive faculties, including perception, planning, reasoning, and action, often by integrating with external tools and orchestrating multi-step workflows.

However, as the complexity of tasks and the sophistication of agentic systems grow, a new challenge emerges: **fragility**. Relying on a single monolithic LLM agent often proves insufficient for intricate problems like iterative tool calling, complex document analysis, or multi-step web navigation. This has led to the rise of **multi-agent frameworks**, inspired by concepts like "collective intelligence" and "society of mind," where multiple LLM agents collaborate, specialize in subtasks, and communicate to achieve superior performance. These systems have shown promise in diverse domains, from data science and scientific discovery to software engineering.

Despite their superior capabilities, the integration of multiple autonomous agents, diverse auxiliary modules (e.g., external databases, tools, memory), and complex orchestration protocols inevitably **exacerbates system fragility**. Recent empirical studies have revealed alarmingly high failure rates (up to 86.7%) in popular multi-agent frameworks, with failure modes ranging from improper task decomposition to role disobedience. This high incidence of failure severely undermines the real-world reliability and trustworthiness of multi-agent systems.

A critical missing piece in this landscape is **failure attribution**: the precise identification of faulty components or steps within a system upon task failure. While essential for debugging, improving data efficiency, and enabling grounded self-improvement, this process is predominantly manual and labor-intensive, requiring humans to sift through verbose trajectory logs. Existing attempts at automated failure attribution using state-of-the-art reasoning LLMs have proven woefully inadequate, achieving accuracies generally below 10%. Furthermore, current benchmarks for agentic failure attribution are severely limited in scale, hindering systematic evaluation and development.

AgenTracer directly addresses these critical gaps. It introduces:
1. **A scalable, automated pipeline** for generating large-scale, high-fidelity annotated multi-agent failure trajectories. This tackles the fundamental "training resources" deficiency that has plagued the development of effective failure attribution models.
2. **A novel methodological approach** for building a swift and accurate "tracer" specifically designed for multi-agent failure localization. This moves beyond the limitations of general-purpose LLMs in this specialized task.

By offering a robust solution for failure attribution, AgenTracer not only enhances the debuggability of multi-agent systems but also provides a crucial mechanism for their **self-correction and self-evolution**, moving the field closer to truly resilient and autonomous collective AI. It bridges the gap between complex multi-agent system design and their practical deployability and reliability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind AgenTracer stems from the observed high failure rates and inherent fragility of complex LLM-based multi-agent systems. While these systems offer advanced capabilities, their sophisticated architecture makes them notoriously difficult to debug and improve. The current reliance on manual analysis for identifying error sources is unsustainable and inefficient, especially as agentic systems become more intricate and widely adopted.

The key objectives of this research are multi-faceted:

1. **To develop an automated framework for generating large-scale, high-fidelity annotated multi-agent failure trajectories.** This directly addresses the critical lack of training data for robust failure attribution models. The goal is to move beyond small, manually annotated benchmarks, enabling the training of more capable diagnostic AI.
2. **To create a dedicated, lightweight, and highly accurate failure tracer (AgenTracer-8B) for LLM agentic systems.** This aims to overcome the striking inadequacy of current state-of-the-art reasoning LLMs, which perform poorly in precisely identifying the source of errors within long and complex multi-agent interaction traces.
3. **To empirically demonstrate that AgenTracer-8B can outperform leading proprietary LLMs** (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Pro, Claude-4-Sonnet) in agentic system failure attribution benchmarks. This validates the effectiveness of the proposed methodology and specialized training.
4. **To show that AgenTracer-8B can deliver actionable feedback to existing, off-the-shelf multi-agent systems**, leading to tangible performance gains through self-correction mechanisms. This highlights the practical utility and potential for AgenTracer to empower self-evolving agentic AI.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need to transform multi-agent system debugging from a costly, manual, and unreliable process into an automated, precise, and efficient capability, thereby unlocking the full potential of complex LLM-based collective intelligence for real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of AgenTracer is two-fold: first, an automated pipeline for generating high-quality annotated failure trajectories, and second, the training of a specialized failure attribution model using this curated data.

#### 3.1. AgenTracer: Automatic Trajectory Annotation

This pipeline aims to construct the *TracerTraj-2.5K* dataset, comprising over 2,000 high-fidelity trajectory-error pairs.

- **Collection:** Trajectories are gathered from six mainstream multi-agent systems, encompassing "handcrafted" (MetaGPT, AutoGen, Smolagents), "partially automated" (AgentPrune), and "fully automated" (AFlow, OWL-Workforce) categories. These systems are tested on six diverse benchmarks across coding (MBPP+, KodCode, Blackjack), mathematical reasoning (MATH, GSM8K), and general agentic tasks (GAIA, HotpotQA). This results in a comprehensive corpus of both successful ($T_{succ}$) and failed ($T_{fail}$) trajectories.

- **Locating Decisive Errors (via Counterfactual Intervention):** For each failed trajectory ($\tau \in T_{fail}$), the system employs an "analyzer agent" ($\pi_{analyzer}$, implemented using DEEPSEEK-R1). The analyzer agent, given the full context of a failure (trajectory, environmental feedback, ground-truth solution), proposes a minimally invasive, corrected action for each step $t$ in the trajectory. A "rectification operator" ($R$) then simulates the trajectory with this corrected action. The **decisive error** is identified as the *earliest step* $t^*$ whose correction is sufficient to turn the system's outcome from failure to success. The agent active at this step is labeled as the problematic agent ($i^*$). This process systematically annotates failed trajectories to form dataset $D^-$.

- **Utilizing Successful Trajectories (via Programmatic Fault Injection):** To further augment the dataset and ensure precise annotations, successful trajectories ($\tau \in T_{succ}$) are leveraged. A "perturbation operator" ($\Pi$, also based on DEEPSEEK-R1) synthetically introduces faults into selected steps of a successful trajectory. If this injected fault causes the system to fail, then the injection point (the modified action and its agent) is, by construction, the decisive error. This method generates dataset $D^+$, where the root cause of failure is known precisely.

- **Curated Dataset:** The combined datasets, $D_{tracer} = D^- \cup D^+$, form TracerTraj-2.5K, providing a robust resource for training the failure tracer.

#### 3.2. AgenTracer-8B: Training Agentic Failure Tracers

The next phase involves training AgenTracer-8B, a lightweight failure attributor, using the curated TracerTraj-2.5K dataset.

- **Base Model:** QWEN3-8B is chosen as the backbone LLM for AgenTracer-8B.
- **Online Reinforcement Learning (RL):** The training utilizes a modified Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm. For each failed trajectory, the model's current policy generates a group of candidate decisive error predictions.
- **Multi-Granular Reward Design:** This is a critical component for effective training. The total reward for a candidate prediction ($\hat{p}_k$) is a gated combination of content accuracy and format compliance:
 - **Format Reward ($I_{format}$):** A strict binary reward (1 or 0) that ensures the model's output adheres to a predefined JSON structure, including specific tags for reasoning (<think>...</think>) and the final answer (<answer>...</answer>), with the answer itself formatted as `<agentID> | <stepID>`. This ensures machine-readability and actionability.
 - **Agent-Level Reward ($r_{agent}$):** A binary reward (1 or 0) indicating whether the model correctly identifies the failure-responsible agent ($i^*$).
 - **Step-Level Reward ($r_{step}$):** A soft, Gaussian kernel-based reward that incentivizes temporal proximity to the true decisive error step ($t^*$). The reward decays smoothly as the predicted step moves away from $t^*$, providing partial credit and a smoother learning signal (defined as $exp(-(t_k - t^*)^2 / (2\sigma^2))$).
- This multi-granular reward design, combined with a dynamic clipping parameter in the GRPO objective, allows for a more stable and effective training process, encouraging both precise localization and adherence to output structure.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the significant advantages of AgenTracer-8B over existing LLM-based approaches for failure attribution.

- **Inadequacy of Prevailing LLMs:** Initial evaluations on the Who&When benchmark revealed that even giant proprietary LLMs like DEEPSEEK-R1 and GPT-4.1 achieve surprisingly low step-level accuracy (around 30%) in identifying decisive errors. Smaller models like QWEN3-8B and LLAMA-3.2-3B perform even worse, often below 10% step-level accuracy. This highlights a fundamental challenge that general-purpose LLMs struggle with.
- **Ground Truth Access Not Always Beneficial:** A counter-intuitive finding was that providing ground truth (w/ G) did not consistently improve attribution accuracy for baseline models, and in some cases, even led to a decrease in performance (e.g., CLAUDE-4-SONNET on TracerTraj-math). This suggests that complex multi-agent traces can be misleading even with ideal information, emphasizing the need for robust reasoning and precise localization.
- **AgenTracer-8B's Superior Attribution Accuracy:**
 - **Outperforms Proprietary LLMs:** AgenTracer-8B consistently and substantially surpasses state-of-the-art proprietary LLMs (such as GPT-4.1, Gemini-2.5-Pro, and Claude-4-Sonnet) on the Who&When benchmark. For instance, on Who&When (handcrafted, w/ G), it outperformed GPT-4.1 by 26.0% and Claude-4-Sonnet by 12.2% in agent-level accuracy. It achieved up to an 18.18% improvement over Gemini-2.5-Pro.
 - **Robustness in Realistic Scenarios (w/o G):** Crucially, AgenTracer-8B maintains strong performance even when ground truth is unavailable (the more realistic "w/o G" setting). For example, on TracerTraj-math (w/o G), it achieved 57.63% step-level accuracy, whereas DEEPSEEK-R1 suffered a 9.21% drop without ground truth. This indicates its practical deployability.
 - **Significant Improvement over Backbone:** AgenTracer-8B demonstrates a substantial improvement over its base model, QWEN3-8B, enhancing step-level accuracy on TracerTraj-agentic by 22.68%.
- **Boosting Mainstream Multi-Agent Systems (MAS):**
 - **Actionable Feedback for Self-Improvement:** AgenTracer-8B provides actionable feedback that enables existing multi-agent systems to self-correct and improve their performance.
 - **Superior to Classical Reflection:** Unlike conventional reflection-based approaches like Self-Refine and CRITIC (even when powered by GPT-4.1), which often fail to provide meaningful insights or even degrade performance, AgenTracer-8B consistently improves outcomes.
 - **Tangible Performance Gains:** It achieved performance gains ranging from 4.8% (for OWL-Workforce on GAIA) to 14.2% (for MaAS on MATH-500) across diverse systems and benchmarks, demonstrating its broad applicability and effectiveness.
- **Illustrative Case Study:** A case study vividly illustrates AgenTracer-8B's ability to pinpoint the subtle, early root causes of failure that often go unnoticed by other LLMs. While other models might diagnose a surface-level error (e.g., a code execution failure), AgenTracer-8B correctly identifies an upstream data acquisition error that occurred much earlier in the trajectory, highlighting its deeper understanding of causal chains in agentic workflows.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AgenTracer makes several significant contributions that collectively establish a new foundation for the study and practical application of failure attribution in LLM agentic systems, with far-reaching potential impacts:

1. **Foundational Framework for Failure Attribution:** The paper provides the first automated and principled framework for systematically studying and addressing agentic system failure attribution. This moves the field beyond anecdotal debugging and limited, manual annotation efforts, paving the way for more rigorous, data-driven research.

2. **Addressing Data Scarcity through Automated Annotation:** The AgenTracer pipeline, with its innovative use of counterfactual replay and programmatic fault injection, solves a critical data scarcity problem. The resulting TracerTraj-2.5K dataset is a substantial resource (over 2,000 high-fidelity annotated trajectories) that can catalyze further research and development in this domain. This automated data generation capability is crucial for scaling up diagnostic AI.

3. **Development of an Effective and Lightweight Diagnostic Tool:** AgenTracer-8B demonstrates that a lightweight, specialized failure tracer, trained with multi-granular reinforcement learning, can significantly outperform much larger and more general-purpose proprietary LLMs in a highly complex task. This highlights the value of specialized training and careful reward design, making advanced diagnostic capabilities more accessible and efficient. Its robustness in the absence of ground truth is particularly impactful for real-world deployment.

4. **Enabling Self-Correction and Self-Evolution for Agentic AI:** Perhaps the most profound impact is AgenTracer-8B's proven ability to provide accurate and actionable feedback to existing multi-agent systems. By delivering concrete insights into *who* made an error and *when*, it empowers these systems to perform more effective self-debugging and self-improvement across iterative attempts. This is a crucial step towards building truly resilient, autonomous, and continuously learning collective intelligence, accelerating the development of robust AI agents that can adapt and evolve in complex environments.

5. **Boosting Reliability and Trustworthiness of LLM Agents:** As LLM-based agents are increasingly deployed in critical applications, their reliability and trustworthiness become paramount. AgenTracer directly addresses this by offering a mechanism to pinpoint and understand failures, thereby improving system robustness. This enhanced reliability is essential for the broader acceptance and adoption of multi-agent AI systems in various industries and research fields.

6. **Opening New Research Avenues:** This work opens up several exciting avenues for future research, including:
 - Exploring more sophisticated counterfactual intervention and fault injection strategies.
 - Developing dynamic multi-granular reward functions that adapt to different failure modes.
 - Investigating how the precise feedback from AgenTracer can be best integrated into various agent architectures for optimal self-correction.
 - Applying similar diagnostic approaches to other complex AI systems beyond LLM agents.

In conclusion, AgenTracer represents a pivotal advancement in managing the complexity and fragility of multi-agent LLM systems. By providing an automated means for failure attribution and enabling self-correction, it moves the field closer to developing more intelligent, robust, and autonomous AI agents capable of tackling increasingly challenging real-world problems.
