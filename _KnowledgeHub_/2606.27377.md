# DanceOPD: On-Policy Generative Field Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> DanceOPD is an on-policy generative field distillation framework that enables a single flow-matching model to effectively compose diverse capabilities like text-to-image generation and various image editing tasks. The framework consistently improves target capabilities, such as editing scores by up to 21.9% in specific categories, while preserving or enhancing anchor generation quality by up to 2.0% in T2I metrics.

## Problem

- Integrating diverse and often conflicting generative capabilities (e.g., T2I and various editing) into a single model often leads to performance trade-offs or degradation.
- Challenges arise from target-field ambiguity (conflicting supervision), state-distribution mismatch (training on off-policy states), and trajectory-query correlation (redundant supervision from a single rollout).
- Existing composition methods like data mixing or parameter merging dilute capability-specific supervision or result in compromises rather than enhanced individual strengths.

## Method

- **DanceOPD** employs hard-routed sample-wise field matching, assigning each training sample to a single, semantically defined capability field (e.g., T2I or a specific edit type) to prevent ambiguous supervision.
- It uses on-policy field querying, where the student model is supervised on states generated by its own current rollout, aligning training with inference conditions.
- The framework utilizes semantic-side single queries, selecting only one low-noise state per rollout for supervision to reduce trajectory-query correlation and focus on high-information regions.

## Results

- DanceOPD improved average editing ability (GEditBench) by **8.1%** over the best on-policy distillation baseline and enhanced T2I quality (GenEval) by **2.0%** over the T2I source model, demonstrating successful composition.
- The framework absorbed a realism-oriented quality field, improving realism reward by **9.9%** while maintaining T2I score stability.
- Ablation studies confirmed that hard routing, semantic-side low-noise queries, and single queries per trajectory are key to the performance gains, with the plain velocity MSE objective demonstrating superior stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Preserving the semantic identity of supervision through hard routing is critical for effective multi-capability learning, outperforming soft mixing strategies by up to 15.2%.
> - Aligning supervision states with the student's own generation process (on-policy querying) is crucial for generalization and overall performance.
> - Focusing supervision on a single, high-information (low-noise, semantic-side) state per trajectory is more efficient and stable than dense querying due to reduced correlation, yielding up to 23.7% improvement in editing scores.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Modern image generation, particularly with flow-matching models, increasingly demands a single model capable of diverse functionalities. These include text-to-image (T2I) generation, precise local editing, and broad global editing. However, these capabilities often present inherent conflicts: editing operations can degrade T2I performance, and local and global editing often pull the model towards conflicting preservation versus transformation behaviors. This challenge of effectively composing multiple generative capabilities within a single model is a central focus in current image generation research.

Existing paradigms for combining capabilities, such as data mixing, joint training, parameter-space merging, or adapter composition, typically offer partial solutions. Data mixing or joint training can dilute capability-specific supervision and lead to multi-task gradient conflicts. Parameter-space merging or adapter composition often results in compromise solutions rather than enhanced individual capabilities. Inference-time score composition methods, while useful, leave the composition external to the deployed student model.

This work adopts a field-based perspective, interpreting task abilities as velocity fields within flow-matching image generators. This aligns with a growing trend in generative modeling that conceptualizes tasks and operations as modifications or imitations of underlying velocity or score fields. Furthermore, the paper draws upon the principles of on-policy distillation (OPD), a technique that addresses the mismatch between fixed training states and states generated by the current student model. While OPD has seen applications in language models and recent flow models like DiffusionOPD and Flow-OPD, this research specifically focuses on how to leverage on-policy distillation for multi-capability composition and functional field absorption in flow-matching models, offering a novel combination of these approaches. The work specifically contributes by analyzing the design space of field querying in such a context, addressing issues not fully explored by prior OPD methods in this domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable a single generative model to effectively compose diverse and often conflicting capabilities, such as text-to-image generation and various forms of image editing, into a unified architecture. The goal is to develop a method where a single model can strengthen a target capability (e.g., a specific type of editing) while concurrently preserving or even enhancing an anchor capability (e.g., base T2I generation quality). This is in contrast to approaches that result in a trade-off or compromise between different capabilities.

The motivation stems from the operational challenges observed in training multi-capability image generation models. The authors frame capability composition as a *field-query problem* where each frozen capability source is defined as a velocity field over a shared generative state space. This formulation highlights three coupled design choices, which in turn reveal corresponding challenges:

1. **Which capability field should supervise a given sample?**
 - **Challenge: Target-field ambiguity.** Linearly combining multiple frozen capability fields within a single sample's target can produce a supervision direction that does not correspond to any semantically well-defined capability query. This dilutes the semantic identity of each capability, potentially leading to a diluted or conflicted learning signal.

2. **Where in the state space should the field be queried?**
 - **Challenge: State-distribution mismatch.** Evaluating the capability field on data states, teacher trajectories, or other off-policy states can result in the student being under-supervised on the specific states it actually visits during its own generation process. This creates a gap between training and inference conditions, hindering the student's ability to generalize to its own rollout distribution.

3. **How many states from the student rollout should be used for supervision?**
 - **Challenge: Trajectory-query correlation.** Drawing dense supervision targets from multiple states along the same rollout trajectory leads to highly correlated supervision signals. These states share the same prompt, noise seed, student dynamics, and path history. This overcounting of correlated signals can bias the resulting gradient, potentially leading to inefficient or unstable learning.

DanceOPD is motivated by these identified challenges, aiming to introduce specific design choices to resolve each of these issues systematically.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DanceOPD is presented as an on-policy generative field distillation framework designed for flow-matching models. It approaches multi-capability image generation by treating each frozen capability source as a velocity field over a shared generative state space. The student model then learns to imitate these fields through a carefully designed field-query process.

The methodology is structured around three core design choices, each intended to address one of the identified challenges:

1. **Hard-Routed Sample-Wise Field Matching (Addressing Target-Field Ambiguity)**:
 - Instead of linearly combining multiple teacher fields for a single sample, DanceOPD routes each training sample to *exactly one* frozen capability field. For instance, a sample originating from a text-to-image dataset queries the T2I field, while an image editing sample queries the designated edit field.
 - This hard routing ensures that the target supervision for any given sample retains its semantic identity and corresponds to a well-defined capability query. It avoids injecting potentially conflicting or irrelevant capability directions into a single sample's update, achieving capability composition statistically across different routed updates rather than through internal averaging. The default routing probability is uniform across active capability buckets.

2. **On-Policy Field Querying (Addressing State-Distribution Mismatch)**:
 - To align supervision with the student's actual inference behavior, DanceOPD queries the routed capability field on states generated by the *current student's own rollout*.
 - During training, a trajectory `z_theta_0:T` is induced by the current student model `v_theta` starting from initial noise `z_T`. A semantic coordinate `s` is sampled, mapped to a physical timestep `t`, and the corresponding student-generated state `z_theta_t` is used as the query point. A stop-gradient operation `sg(z_theta_t)` is applied to this state, meaning the update differentiates only the local velocity prediction of the student, not the entire rollout solver. This ensures that the student is supervised on the state distribution it is likely to encounter during deployment.

3. **Semantic-Side Single Query (Addressing Trajectory-Query Correlation)**:
 - Recognizing the high correlation among multiple states from a single rollout trajectory, DanceOPD employs a *single semantic-side, low-noise query* per sample.
 - A query distribution `q_sem` (e.g., Beta(5,2)) is used to bias the sampling of the semantic coordinate `s` towards the low-noise side of the trajectory (closer to the clean image). This region is considered to contain a higher density of capability-specific information (e.g., style, aesthetics, local attributes).
 - By using only one such high-information state for supervision, the method avoids redundant and correlated supervision signals that could arise from dense querying along the entire trajectory, promoting more efficient and stable learning.

**Objective Design**:
The student model is updated using a **plain velocity Mean Squared Error (MSE)** loss:
`L_DanceOPD = E[ ||v_theta(sg(z_theta_t), t, c) - v_m(sg(z_theta_t), t, c)||^2_2 ]`
This MSE objective is chosen because the target is a deterministic velocity field, making it a natural regression target. The authors provide a theoretical derivation showing that KL-style velocity matching reduces to a timestep-weighted MSE form under a local Gaussian transition view, supporting the use of MSE.

**Operator-Defined Fields**:
The framework is also capable of absorbing operator-defined fields, such as classifier-free guidance (CFG). A CFG-guided velocity field, which is an affine combination of unconditional and conditional velocity predictions, can be treated as an additional capability field and integrated into the student using the identical MSE objective.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrated that DanceOPD consistently improves target capabilities while preserving anchor generation quality across various settings, often with a lower computational cost compared to baselines.

**A. T2I and Edit Composition:**
- **Performance:** DanceOPD improved the GEditBench average score (measuring editing ability) by 8.1% over the best reproduced on-policy distillation (OPD) baseline and 8.5% over the standalone edit source. Simultaneously, it slightly exceeded the T2I source on GenEval (measuring T2I quality) by 2.0% and outperformed the strongest composition baseline by 1.6%. Significant gains were observed in complex edit categories like background change (21.9% over DiffusionOPD) and style change (21.3% over DiffusionOPD).
- **Qualitative Observations:** The model demonstrated strong text-to-image generation capabilities while effectively following diverse editing instructions (scene, style, material, object-level), avoiding degradation seen in other baselines.

**B. Local and Global Edit Composition:**
- **Performance:** For composing local (preservation-focused) and global (transformation-focused) editing, DanceOPD improved the GEditBench average by 16.1% over the best competing composition baseline and 7.9% over the local edit source. GenEval overall also improved by 2.5% compared to the strongest composition baseline. Improvements were concentrated in global or attribute-level changes, with background change improving by 33.5% and style change by 12.9% over competing baselines.

**C. Realism-Field Absorption:**
- **Performance:** When absorbing a realism-oriented quality field, DanceOPD improved the realism reward by 9.9% over off-policy distillation and closed 85.3% of the student-to-teacher reward gap. Crucially, the T2I score remained stable, within 0.1% of off-policy distillation and 7.6% above the student anchor model, indicating successful absorption without sacrificing base generation.
- **Qualitative Observations:** The model enhanced photographic details, lighting contrast, and texture realism in generated images while preserving the original scene layout and content.

**D. CFG Absorption:**
- **Performance:** DanceOPD successfully absorbed classifier-free guidance (CFG) effects. The optimal composition of absorbed and external guidance improved the GEditBench average by 7.6% over train-only absorption and 1.4% over eval-only CFG. The study also highlighted that excessive composition of absorbed and external guidance could substantially degrade performance (e.g., a 31.2% drop from the best composition).

**Ablation Studies and Diagnostics:**
- **Hard Routing:** Hard-routed sample-wise field matching improved performance by 15.2% (under MSE) and 10.6% (under KL objective) over soft all-teacher mixing, confirming the importance of preserving semantic identity.
- **Semantic-Side Low-Noise Queries:** Low-t queries (semantic side) improved the GEditBench average by 23.7% over median-t and 19.5% over high-t queries, supporting the focus on high-information states.
- **Single Query:** Using a single semantic-side query per sample significantly outperformed dense-query variants (e.g., 7.9% to 16.6% average improvement), confirming that trajectory-query correlation negatively impacts performance. Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) rollouts partially mitigated dense-query degradation (18.4% gain in a stress case) but were not superior to a single query.
- **Objective Design:** Plain velocity MSE demonstrated the strongest stability and performance, outperforming timestep-weighted MSE, DMD-EMA hybrids, consistency matching, and KL-¯σ^2 matching by 2.8% to 4.5%.
- **Initialization:** Initializing the student with a checkpoint strong in a relevant capability (e.g., local edit) yielded significant performance gains (e.g., 37.2% over merged initialization).
- **Rollout Steps:** Moderate rollout lengths (e.g., 16 steps) for training-time query generation were found to be effective, with longer rollouts not automatically providing better supervision.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DanceOPD presents a practical and principled route for generative field distillation within flow-matching models. By formulating multi-capability image generation as an on-policy field-query problem, the work addresses fundamental challenges that limit the effectiveness of existing composition methods.

The primary significance of this work lies in its demonstrated ability to:
1. **Unify Diverse Capabilities**: Enable a single generative model to robustly handle heterogeneous capabilities such as T2I generation, local editing, global editing, and even abstract quality fields (like realism) or operator-defined guidance (like CFG). This moves beyond compromise solutions typically found in multi-task learning, parameter merging, or inference-time score composition.
2. **Maintain and Enhance Quality**: The method allows for strengthening a target capability without degrading the quality of anchor capabilities, which has been a persistent challenge in multi-functional generative models.
3. **Address Foundational Issues**: The explicit identification and systematic resolution of target-field ambiguity, state-distribution mismatch, and trajectory-query correlation provide a clearer understanding of the difficulties in multi-capability distillation and offer effective solutions.
4. **Efficiency and Stability**: The use of hard-routed, semantic-side single queries contributes to more efficient and stable training, as evidenced by the performance against dense, correlated query methods and the lower computational cost compared to some on-policy baselines.

This research establishes a valuable framework for building scalable and versatile generative AI models, potentially impacting applications requiring fine-grained control and diverse functionalities from a single deployed model, such as advanced creative tools, professional image manipulation software, and broader content generation platforms.
