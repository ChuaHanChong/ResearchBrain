# PreSIST: Vision-Language-Informed Object Persistence Prediction in Open-World Scenes

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called PreSIST proactively estimates how long an object instance will remain in its last seen pose by leveraging vision-language models for context-aware, instance-level predictions. The approach, which works without in-context training, enhances open-world persistence prediction and improves long-term visual relocalization.

## Problem

- Robots operating in dynamic, open-world environments often rely on reactive perception, leading to outdated map information and suboptimal decision-making when objects move.
- Existing object persistence prediction methods are limited to coarse semantic class heuristics, require extensive environment-specific training data, or use simple decay models lacking context-awareness.
- Assigning object dynamics solely based on semantic class is insufficient, as object persistence can vary drastically with context (e.g., a parked car versus a car at a traffic light).

## Method

- **PreSIST** estimates instance-level object ==survival priors== from a single observation, which then initializes a ==probabilistic persistence filter==.
- **PreSIST-Lang** leverages Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Language Models (LLMs) to infer ==persistence quantiles== (q25, q75) from detailed scene and instance descriptions, exploiting their integrated commonsense knowledge in a zero-shot manner.
- **PreSIST-Vis**, a vision-only variant, is trained to mimic **PreSIST-Lang**'s context-sensitive predictions using ==pseudo-labels==, enabling efficient online deployment for robotic applications.

## Results

- PreSIST-Lang demonstrated superior or competitive zero-shot persistence estimation, outperforming unstructured VLM baselines across diverse open-world datasets, adapting predictions based on specific scene context.
- PreSIST-Vis achieved competitive accuracy with PreSIST-Lang while running significantly faster (approximately **0.04 seconds** per query), making it suitable for online applications, and outperformed vision-only baselines.
- Integrating PreSIST priors into long-term visual relocalization improved map quality by filtering out unreliable map points, leading to higher inlier ratios, reduced RANSAC iterations, and lower translation and rotation errors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Vision-Language Models can effectively generate context-aware, instance-level object persistence priors without requiring specific long-term training data from the target environment.
> - Decomposing persistence reasoning into scene description, instance description, and a subsequent reasoning step by an LLM allows for fine-grained, robust estimates.
> - A compact, vision-only network can be trained to approximate the sophisticated VLM-derived priors, offering a practical trade-off between accuracy and computational efficiency for online robotics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robots operating in real-world environments over extended periods must contend with dynamic scenes where objects are frequently moved, added, or removed. Traditional Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) and perception systems often assume static environments or address changes reactively. Reactive approaches update their internal representations (e.g., maps, object lists) only after observing inconsistencies, leading to potential reliance on outdated information in the interim.

Existing predictive strategies for object persistence typically fall into a few categories:
- **Semantic class-based heuristics:** Objects are assigned persistence properties based solely on their semantic class (e.g., all "cars" are equally mobile). This approach is limited because the same object class can exhibit widely varying persistence depending on its context (e.g., a car at a traffic light versus a parked car).
- **In-context observation-driven models:** Some methods learn persistence models from long-term observations within a specific environment. While effective for known environments, this requires extensive, context-specific data collection and may not generalize to novel, open-world settings.
- **Simple decay models:** Survival analysis-based methods, such as those using exponential or Weibull decay, model an object's likelihood of persistence as a function of time since last observation. However, the parameters for these models are often either hand-specified by class or learned from specific environmental data, lacking context-awareness or open-world applicability.

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in commonsense, temporal, and visually grounded reasoning. This has led to their use in providing priors for various downstream tasks in robotics, such as object co-occurrence or high-level planning. Some prior work has used LLMs to provide coarse, class-level static/dynamic labels or to predict object locations at future times, but these generally do not offer fine-grained, instance- and scene-specific persistence priors.

This work, PreSIST, is positioned to bridge the gap between reactive or coarse predictive systems and the need for proactive, context-aware, and instance-specific object persistence prediction in open-world scenarios. It specifically leverages the reasoning capabilities of VLMs to generate such priors without requiring explicit in-context training data, addressing a limitation of many existing approaches. Furthermore, it integrates these context-rich priors into established probabilistic persistence filtering frameworks, aiming to enhance their performance and applicability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enable robots to proactively estimate how long an observed object instance will remain in its last seen pose, rather than reacting only after the object has moved. This proactive capability is motivated by several limitations of current robotic perception systems:

1. **Reactive Perception Inadequacy:** Many systems for long-term mapping and perception are reactive, updating their understanding of the environment only when new observations contradict existing map data. This means a robot may continue to rely on outdated information about object locations, potentially leading to suboptimal planning, failed localization, or inefficient task execution. For example, a robot might attempt to navigate through a space assumed to be clear but is now obstructed by a recently moved object.

2. **Insufficient Semantic Class-Based Reasoning:** Assigning object dynamics solely based on semantic class is often insufficient. While a "car" class might be generally considered dynamic, its persistence can vary from seconds (at a traffic light) to hours or days (in a parking spot). Similarly, a "chair" might be highly persistent in a fixed lecture hall but moved frequently in a dynamic office space. Relying solely on class-level heuristics overlooks crucial contextual cues that influence object stability.

3. **Data Scarcity for In-Context Training:** Methods that learn persistence models from long-term observations in a specific environment require substantial data collection within that target environment. This data is often difficult and time-consuming to acquire and may not cover the full diversity of object behaviors and contexts encountered in open-world deployments. This limits the scalability and generalizability of such data-driven approaches.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of PreSIST are:
- To develop a method capable of estimating instance-level object persistence priors that are sensitive to both object properties and surrounding scene context.
- To achieve this in a "zero-shot" manner, meaning without requiring long-term, task-specific training data from the target environment.
- To leverage the commonsense and temporal reasoning capabilities of large vision-language models (VLMs) for context-aware persistence estimation.
- To integrate these estimated priors with established probabilistic persistence filtering frameworks for efficient, time-indexed prediction and principled incorporation of future observations.
- To provide an efficient, vision-only variant that can approximate the VLM-derived estimates for practical online deployment.
- To create and publicly release a new dataset for evaluating context-aware object persistence in diverse, open-world settings.
- To empirically demonstrate that the proposed methods outperform existing baselines in open-world persistence prediction and improve performance in a downstream robotic task, such as long-term visual relocalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PreSIST is designed to estimate an object-instance survival prior from a single observation, which then initializes a probabilistic persistence filter. The framework operates on an image and an instance segmentation mask, producing parametric survival priors. The core assumption is the use of Weibull distributions for survival priors, characterized by shape ($k$) and scale ($\lambda$) parameters, which are flexible and allow for closed-form calculations. Instead of directly predicting ($k, \lambda$), PreSIST predicts two survival-time quantiles, $q_{25}$ and $q_{75}$, which represent the times by which 25% and 75% of similar instances are expected to have moved, respectively. These quantiles are then deterministically mapped to Weibull parameters ($k, \lambda$).

The method is presented in two interchangeable variants: PreSIST-Lang and PreSIST-Vis.

**A. PreSIST-Lang**

PreSIST-Lang leverages large vision-language models (VLMs) and language models (LLMs) to infer persistence quantiles by exploiting their integrated commonsense knowledge. The prediction process is decomposed into three stages:

1. **Scene Description:** A VLM processes the full input image to generate a natural language caption that describes the overall scene, focusing on environmental processes and human activities relevant to object persistence (e.g., "busy street with traffic," "dining area"). This description is computed once per image and applied to all detected instances within that image.

2. **Instance Description:** For each object instance (defined by an image and its mask), a VLM generates a detailed natural language description. To provide sufficient context for the VLM, the model is presented with the full image, a cropped view around the object, and a full image with a blurred and dimmed background, all with the instance mask outlined. The description focuses on object identity, its support and contact relationships, nearby entities, and any visual cues indicating ongoing or potential future movement.

3. **Persistence Reasoning:** A text-only language model receives both the scene description and the instance-specific description. It then performs a reasoning step to identify potential changes, their timescales, and the factors influencing their likelihood for the particular instance. Based on this reasoning, the LLM outputs the desired survival-time quantiles ($q_{25}, q_{75}$). The prompt used for this step explicitly guides the model toward the desired reasoning process. The VLM used is a locally hosted Gemma 4 31B instruction-tuned model, with Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 explored as an alternative.

**B. PreSIST-Vis**

PreSIST-Vis is a vision-only model designed for efficient online deployment, trained to mimic the context-sensitive predictions of PreSIST-Lang.

1. **Training Data Generation:** PreSIST-Vis is trained using "pseudo-labels" generated by PreSIST-Lang. A diverse collection of images from various indoor and outdoor datasets (COCO, OpenLORIS, KITTI, EPIC-Kitchens, UT CODa, SKU110k, SUN397, PKLot) are used. Instance segmentation masks are obtained by first querying a VLM (Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking) for object classes present in each image, then using SAM 3 to segment instances for each class. PreSIST-Lang then processes these image-mask pairs to generate ($q_{25}, q_{75}$) pseudo-labels, which serve as regression targets.

2. **Architecture:** The architecture is a mask-conditioned cross-attention network. A frozen DINOv2 ViT-L/14 backbone, augmented with lightweight trainable adapters, encodes the input image into a grid of patch-token features. The patch features within the object's instance mask are aggregated to form a single "object query" vector. This object query then cross-attends to all image patch tokens, allowing the model to gather context relevant to the specific instance. A prediction head maps the attended feature vector to the transformed quantile outputs.

3. **Training Objective:** Instead of directly regressing ($q_{25}, q_{75}$), PreSIST-Vis predicts two log-transformed variables: $z_1 = \log q_{25}$ and $z_2 = \log(q_{75}/q_{25} - 1)$. This parameterization ensures scale-invariance and that the decoded quantiles ($q_{25} = e^{\hat{z}_1}$ and $q_{75} = q_{25}(1+e^{\hat{z}_2})$) are always valid and ordered. The targets $z_1$ and $z_2$ are standardized to zero mean and unit variance. Training uses a Huber loss for each target and instance sampling to balance contributions from images with varying numbers of instances. The model is trained with AdamW for up to 100 epochs, with early stopping based on validation performance.

Both PreSIST variants provide the ($q_{25}, q_{75}$) quantiles, which are then converted into Weibull parameters ($k, \lambda$) to initialize a persistence filter. This filter then combines the prior with subsequent observations to maintain a probabilistic belief about the object's persistence over time.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation addressed several questions regarding accuracy, open-world performance, component impact, and downstream utility.

**A. Zero-Shot Persistence Estimation**
- **Dataset:** A new dataset of stationary object track annotations was created, utilizing footage from UT Austin Campus (Single-Day and Multi-Day), Oxford RobotCar, and HD-EPIC datasets. This dataset provides instance segmentation masks and manually annotated presence/absence times for over 250 object tracks and 6500+ masks, spanning diverse timescales and contexts.
- **Performance Comparison:** PreSIST-Lang generally demonstrated superior or near-superior performance (lower MAE, higher balanced accuracy) among VLM-based methods across the multi-day, HD-EPIC, and RobotCar domains, outperforming unstructured VLM baselines. While PreSIST-Lang had a longer per-query runtime than some VLM baselines, this was deemed acceptable for an offline prior generation.
- **Context Awareness:** Both PreSIST-Lang and PreSIST-Vis were observed to adapt their persistence estimates based on scene context, in contrast to class-only or unstructured VLM baselines that provided similar estimates regardless of context (e.g., distinguishing a cutting board in hand vs. on a counter, or a moving car vs. a parked car).
- **PreSIST-Vis Efficiency:** PreSIST-Vis exhibited a small performance drop compared to PreSIST-Lang but ran significantly faster (approximately 0.04 seconds per query), making it suitable for online applications. It substantially outperformed the vision-only CLIP+Class Lookup baseline, which relies only on semantic class for priors. The single-day domain was the most challenging for PreSIST-Lang, but PreSIST-Vis remained competitive.

**B. PreSIST vs. In-Context Training Methods**
- **PKLot Dataset:** Evaluation on the PUCPR subset of the PKLot dataset, which features cars in a parking lot, compared PreSIST against traditional in-context training methods (exponential, log-normal, and mixture models, as well as an In-Context Vision model).
- **Performance with Limited Data:** With limited in-context training data, PreSIST variants (training-free for this task) outperformed the data-driven baselines.
- **Performance with More Data:** As the amount of in-context training data increased, the baselines improved, with the exponential mixture and In-Context Vision models eventually outperforming PreSIST-Vis. However, PreSIST-Lang remained competitive, outperforming all but the In-Context Vision model even with extensive in-context training data for the baselines. This indicates the value of open-world priors, especially when environment-specific data is scarce.

**C. Persistence Model Ablations**
- **VLM Variants:** Using Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 as the VLM backbone in PreSIST-Lang led to overall stronger performance, achieving lower MAE on every domain and higher balanced accuracy on three, while also being faster than the Gemma-based PreSIST-Lang. Directly predicting Weibull parameters (instead of quantiles) resulted in the lowest MAE on HD-EPIC and RobotCar, but slightly reduced balanced accuracy.
- **Vision-Only Variants (PreSIST-Vis):**
 - **Pretraining Significance:** Removing the pretrained backbone entirely (No Pretrained Backbone) severely degraded performance, underscoring the critical role of large-scale visual pretraining for generalization.
 - **Adapter Importance:** A fully frozen backbone (without lightweight adapters) also consistently yielded worse performance than PreSIST-Vis with adapters, highlighting the benefit of adaptation.
 - **Contextual Information:** Using only the masked object region (Masked Region Only) reduced performance compared to using the full image context, supporting the design choice of cross-attention over all patch tokens.
 - **Architecture Choice:** The mask-pooled cross-attention query of PreSIST-Vis generally outperformed a dense prediction approach (predicting per-pixel and then pooling), indicating the effectiveness of the proposed architecture. PreSIST-Vis maintained the best overall performance among vision-only ablations.

**D. Downstream Task: Long-Term Relocalization**
- **ExMaps Dataset:** Evaluation on the ExMaps grocery store dataset demonstrated the utility of PreSIST for long-term visual relocalization. Map points with low persistence probability (below 0.25, estimated by PreSIST) were discarded before pose estimation.
- **Relocalization Improvement:** Filtering map points based on PreSIST predictions resulted in improved relocalization quality. For both PreSIST-Lang and PreSIST-Vis, filtering led to:
 - Higher inlier ratios (Inl. %).
 - Reduced RANSAC iterations (Iter.).
 - Lower translation error (t (m)).
 - Lower rotation error (R ($^\circ$)).
- **Efficiency Gains:** The filtering also reduced the average per-query pose estimation time. PreSIST-Vis provided faster prior construction (21 min for 81,595 points) compared to PreSIST-Lang (2.3 h) for map initialization.
- **Visualized Persistence:** Visualizations showed that at short prediction horizons (0.1 hours), most map points maintained high persistence. However, as the horizon increased (12-48 hours), points on stable structures (shelving) retained high persistence, while those on high-turnover products (e.g., items on shelves) sharply dropped in persistence probability, aligning with real-world dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PreSIST introduces a novel framework for proactive, context-aware object persistence prediction, offering several significant advancements and potential impacts on robotics and related fields:

1. **Enabling Proactive Robot Reasoning:** By providing predictions of how long objects will remain stationary, PreSIST allows robots to move beyond reactive mapping and perception. This proactive capability can lead to more robust and efficient planning, navigation, and interaction in dynamic environments, as robots can anticipate changes rather than merely responding to them. This can reduce the reliance on outdated map information and enhance overall system reliability over long operational periods.

2. **Context-Sensitive, Instance-Level Persistence Priors:** PreSIST's ability to estimate persistence based on both object properties and surrounding scene context addresses a fundamental limitation of prior methods that rely solely on semantic classes or in-context training. This fine-grained, instance-level reasoning, enabled by VLMs, allows for a more nuanced understanding of environmental dynamics, where the same object type can have drastically different persistence based on its immediate use or location.

3. **Zero-Shot Generalization in Open Worlds:** A key contribution is the method's zero-shot capability, meaning it can generate context-conditioned persistence priors for unseen objects and environments without requiring specific long-term training data for that particular context. This significantly reduces the data collection burden for deploying robots in novel or highly dynamic environments, broadening the applicability of long-term autonomy solutions.

4. **Efficient Online Deployment:** The development of PreSIST-Vis, a vision-only model trained to approximate the VLM-derived priors, offers a practical solution for real-time robotic applications. Its efficiency (fractions of a second per query) while maintaining competitive accuracy ensures that sophisticated, context-aware persistence prediction is feasible for online tasks, overcoming the computational cost associated with direct VLM inference.

5. **Improved Downstream Robotic Tasks:** The demonstrated improvement in long-term visual relocalization highlights PreSIST's direct utility for enhancing core robotic functionalities. By filtering out unreliable map points (i.e., objects predicted to have moved), robots can achieve more accurate and efficient localization in changing environments, which is crucial for tasks like persistent surveillance, delivery, and mobile manipulation.

6. **Contribution of a New Dataset:** The creation and release of a manually annotated dataset for open-world object persistence evaluation provide a valuable resource for the research community, enabling further development and benchmarking of methods in this critical area.

In summary, PreSIST represents an advancement in how robots perceive and reason about dynamic environments. By providing a method for proactively predicting object persistence that is both context-aware and deployable in open-world settings, it contributes to the development of more intelligent, autonomous, and adaptable robotic systems capable of operating reliably over extended durations. Future work, such as incorporating short video cues or specializing models with limited in-domain data, could further extend its capabilities and impact.
