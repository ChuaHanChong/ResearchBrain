# Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Robot Learning: A Dexterous Manipulation Study on Reactive Catching

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel learning framework called Domain-Randomized Instance Set (DRIS) was developed to enable robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for dexterous manipulation. This approach explicitly models and propagates uncertainty in simulation, achieving a 68% success rate in challenging flat-plate reactive catching on a real robot without any real-world fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Bridging the "sim-to-real" gap for dexterous manipulation is difficult due to physics-intensive contact interactions and high sensitivity to modeling errors and perception noise.
- Conventional domain randomization, which perturbs only one parameter instance per episode, is insufficient for robust learning as it does not allow policies to explicitly account for how uncertainties affect multiple possible manipulation outcomes.
- Reactive catching with a flat, non-stabilizing surface presents an extremely challenging problem, requiring precise coordination and force modulation under uncertainty, making zero-shot sim-to-real transfer difficult.

## Method

- Introduced **Domain-Randomized Instance Set (DRIS)**, a learning paradigm that simultaneously represents and propagates a set of 'N' randomized instances with independently sampled physical parameters, all evolving under a shared robot action.
- Developed a policy learning pipeline consisting of a pre-trained ==point cloud-based Autoencoder== for encoding the DRIS state into a latent vector, and a ==FiLM-conditioned policy network== trained with ==PPO== using the average reward across all instances.
- Instantiated the problem for a ==flat-plate reactive catching== task, defining ball states, plate actions, and a reward function, and implemented a ==joint-space torque controller== for ==zero-shot real-world deployment==.

## Results

- DRIS policies achieved a **68%** success rate on real-world flat-plate reactive catching in zero-shot transfer, outperforming a hand-crafted baseline (**5%**) and an end-to-end sim-trained baseline (**13%**).
- Simulation experiments demonstrated that DRIS policies significantly improved robustness against observation noise, execution errors, and showed better generalization to out-of-distribution physical parameters compared to baselines.
- The DRIS approach showed computational efficiency, achieving superior robustness with considerably less VRAM usage compared to brute-force massively parallelized single-instance randomization, and also exhibited emergent generalizability to human-thrown and irregularly shaped objects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling and propagating uncertainty through a set of randomized instances (DRIS) allows policies to learn actions that inherently account for a distribution of possible outcomes, leading to significantly more robust learning than conventional single-instance randomization.
> - DRIS acts as an exact particle approximation for system belief propagation, which theoretically reduces gradient variance and promotes implicit robustness by exposing the policy to a more comprehensive representation of dynamic variability.
> - Training a policy on a highly sensitive, flat-plate reactive catching task, without mechanical stabilization, serves as a strong testbed for evaluating the robustness and generalizability of sim-to-real transfer methods for dexterous manipulation.
