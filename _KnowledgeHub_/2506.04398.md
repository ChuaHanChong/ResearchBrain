# Bridging the Performance Gap Between Target-Free and Target-Based Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Iterated Shared Q-Learning (iS-QL) offers a deep reinforcement learning method that closes the performance gap between target-free and target-based approaches, notably reducing memory usage. This technique matches or exceeds the performance of target-based algorithms across a wide range of online, offline, discrete, and continuous control tasks.

## Problem

- Target networks, crucial for stabilizing deep reinforcement learning, introduce a significant memory overhead by duplicating Q-networks.
- Target-free methods, while memory-efficient, frequently suffer from training instabilities, reduced sample efficiency, and degraded final performance.
- Deep reinforcement learning typically presents a binary choice between memory efficiency (target-free) and learning stability/performance (target-based), lacking a robust intermediate solution.

## Method

- The method, **iS-QL**, utilizes a ==single Q-network== where only the parameters of the last linear layer are copied to form a 'target head,' while the feature extractor is shared and updated online.
- Iterated Shared Q-Network (iS-QN) extends this by employing K+1 linear heads with a shared feature extractor, training each online head against the Bellman target derived from the previous head.
- A periodic update mechanism propagates learned value functions down the chain of heads, effectively approximating K consecutive Bellman iterations in parallel, while action sampling encourages exploration across different temporal horizons.

## Results

- iS-DQN K=9 outperformed target-based DQN by **6%** AUC with approximately half the parameters on Atari games, while iS-SAC K=1 recovered performance with a **49%** parameter reduction on DeepMind Control tasks.
- The approach effectively closed the performance gap between target-free and target-based methods across diverse settings, including online/offline, discrete/continuous control, language-based RL, and streaming RL.
- Analysis revealed that iS-QN yielded higher gradient cosine similarity to target-based methods, achieved greater target stability than target-free approaches, and produced more expressive feature representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Stabilizing deep reinforcement learning and achieving target-based performance is possible with significantly reduced memory by sharing feature extractors and maintaining only light-weight target heads.
> - Chained Bellman updates within a single network, where each head learns from the previous one, enhances learning stability and sample efficiency without duplicating the entire network.
> - The proposed method fosters richer state representations and aligns gradient dynamics more closely with stable target-based approaches, contributing to its improved performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) often faces training instabilities when employing non-linear function approximators and off-policy samples, a challenge commonly referred to as aspects of the "deadly triad" (van Hasselt et al., 2018). A prevalent solution to mitigate these instabilities and stabilize learning is the use of target networks, as introduced in Deep Q-Network (DQN) by Mnih et al. (2015). Target networks construct regression targets from an older, periodically updated copy of the online network, thereby reducing the volatility of the learning signal.

While beneficial for stability, target networks introduce two notable drawbacks: they approximately double the memory footprint dedicated to Q-networks and delay the propagation of Bellman updates. This memory constraint becomes particularly problematic in scenarios requiring large network sizes, such as high-dimensional or multi-modal inputs, or for deployment on memory-limited edge devices. Consequently, there is an ongoing interest in developing target-free methods that avoid this memory overhead. However, removing the target network frequently leads to a significant degradation in performance, as observed in numerous ablation studies (Schwarzer et al., 2023; Nauman et al., 2024). Some target-free approaches exist, like those in Gradient Temporal Difference learning (Sutton et al., 2009) or methods for streaming scenarios (Vasan et al., 2024; Gallici et al., 2025), but these often introduce other computational overheads or specific environmental assumptions. Regularization techniques (Kim et al., 2019; Bhatt et al., 2024) have also been explored to improve target-free performance, yet target networks frequently remain advantageous when re-integrated.

This work positions itself at the intersection of these two paradigms. Rather than adhering to a binary choice between target-free and target-based approaches, it introduces an alternative that aims to retain the low-memory advantage of target-free methods while leveraging the stability benefits traditionally associated with target networks. Specifically, it builds upon the concept of "iterated Q-learning" (Vincent et al., 2025b), which seeks to accelerate learning by performing multiple Bellman updates in parallel. Previous iterations of iterated Q-learning also required separate copies of networks, thus increasing memory. This paper adapts this concept to address the memory efficiency concern, contributing a method that integrates stabilization without the full memory penalty of traditional target networks, thereby moving towards more resource-efficient DRL algorithms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to bridge the performance gap observed between target-free and target-based deep reinforcement learning algorithms. Target-based methods, such as DQN, are known for their stability but incur a substantial memory overhead due to the maintenance of a separate target network. Conversely, target-free methods, which utilize only a single online network, offer a lower memory footprint but often suffer from training instabilities and performance degradation.

The specific motivations driving this work include:

1. **Mitigating Memory Constraints:** Target networks typically double the memory required for Q-networks. This limitation constrains the maximum size of the online network that can be trained, particularly on devices with restricted memory (e.g., GPUs with limited vRAM, edge devices). This poses a challenge for applications that inherently require large networks, such as those handling high-dimensional state spaces (Boukas et al., 2021), processing multi-modal inputs (Schneider et al., 2026), or employing mixtures of experts (Obando Ceron et al., 2024). The work aims to develop a method that allows for larger online networks or deployment in memory-constrained environments without compromising learning stability.
2. **Enhancing Sample Efficiency of Target-Free Methods:** While target-free methods offer memory advantages, their reduced stability often translates to lower sample efficiency and final performance. A key motivation is to improve the learning speed (in terms of environment interactions) of these memory-efficient approaches, which is critical for many real-world applications where data collection is expensive or time-consuming.
3. **Exploring Alternatives to Binary Design Choices:** The paper explicitly steps "out of the binary choice" between target-free and target-based algorithms. It is motivated by the idea that a more nuanced approach, combining aspects of both, could yield superior results by maintaining the low memory footprint of target-free methods while incorporating insights from target-based literature to enhance stability.
4. **Leveraging Iterated Q-Learning for Stability:** The research is motivated by the potential of iterated Q-learning (Vincent et al., 2025b) to accelerate learning by performing multiple Bellman updates in parallel. However, previous iterated Q-learning formulations also relied on separate target networks. This work aims to adapt and integrate iterated Q-learning into a memory-efficient framework to simultaneously improve learning speed and stability within a low-memory budget.

In summary, the core motivation is to develop resource-efficient deep reinforcement learning algorithms that achieve performance comparable to, or exceeding, target-based methods, without the associated memory overhead, thereby enabling broader applicability and more efficient learning across diverse and demanding scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, termed **iterated Shared Q-Learning (iS-QL)**, introduces an alternative to conventional target-free and target-based approaches. It addresses the memory overhead of target networks while aiming to improve learning stability and sample efficiency.

**Core Concept: Shared Features**
The fundamental idea is to utilize a single Q-network where only the parameters of the last linear layer are copied to form a "target head," while the remaining parameters (feature extractor) are shared with the up-to-date online network. This configuration, referred to as "Shared Features" (or iS-QL with K=1), allows the regression target to be constructed from an older, frozen version of the final layer, while the computationally intensive feature extraction benefits from the most current online parameters. This design significantly reduces the memory footprint compared to traditional target networks that duplicate the entire network architecture.

**Extension: Iterated Shared Q-Network (iS-QN)**
Building upon the shared features concept, the work integrates "iterated Q-learning" (Vincent et al., 2025b). iS-QN uses a single network structured with K+1 linear heads (ω\_0..., ω\_K), where ω represents the shared parameters (feature extractor) and ω\_k represents the parameters of the k-th head. The full parameter set for the k-th Q-function is denoted θ\_k = (ω, ω\_k).

The training procedure for iS-QN is as follows:
1. **Network Architecture:** A single Q-network is initialized with a shared feature extractor (ω) and K+1 distinct linear heads (ω\_0..., ω\_K).
2. **Loss Function:** The training loss (L\_iS-QN) is defined as the sum of K individual temporal-difference losses. For each sample (s, a, r, s'), each online network Q\_θ\_k (for k ∈ {1..., K}) is trained to regress the Bellman target derived from the *previous* head Q\_θ\_(k-1). Specifically, L\_iS-QN = Σ\_(k=1 to K) (⌈r + γ max\_a' Q\_θ\_(k-1)(s', a')⌉ - Q\_θ\_k(s, a))², where ⌈·⌉ denotes a stop-gradient operation. The head ω\_0 is not directly learned through this process; instead, it serves as the initial target reference.
3. **Target Update Mechanism:** Similar to the periodic updates in DQN, every T training steps, the parameters of each head ω\_k are updated to match the parameters of the subsequent head ω\_(k+1) (i.e., ω\_k ← ω\_(k+1) for k ∈ {0..., K-1}). This mechanism propagates the learned value functions down the chain, effectively allowing the network to approximate K consecutive Bellman iterations in parallel.
4. **Action Sampling:** During environment interaction, an action is sampled using an ϵ-greedy policy from a Q-function corresponding to a head (Q\_θ\_u) chosen uniformly from the available K+1 heads. This encourages interaction with different temporal horizons of the learned value function.
5. **Memory Footprint:** The memory footprint is significantly reduced because only K+1 small linear heads (ω\_k) are maintained in addition to the single shared feature extractor (ω). In typical deep networks, the linear heads are substantially smaller than the feature extractor, resulting in memory usage closer to a target-free approach than a target-based one.

**Experimental Settings:**
The efficacy of iS-QN (and its K=1 variant, "Shared Features") was evaluated across a range of reinforcement learning scenarios to demonstrate its generalizability:
- **Online Discrete Control:** Experiments were conducted on 15 Atari 2600 games (Bellemare et al., 2013) using both CNN (Mnih et al., 2015) and IMPALA (Espeholt et al., 2018) architectures, with and without LayerNormalization (Ba et al., 2016). DQN was the base algorithm.
- **Offline Discrete Control:** The approach was applied to 10 Atari games using Conservative Q-Learning (CQL, Kumar et al., 2020b) with the IMPALA architecture and LayerNorm, using 10% of a collected dataset. An "Ensemble Shared Features" variant (ES-CQL) was also tested.
- **Online Continuous Control:** Evaluation on 7 DeepMind Control suite (DMC) hard tasks (Tassa et al., 2018) using Soft Actor-Critic (SAC, Haarnoja et al., 2018) with the SimbaV2 architecture (Lee et al., 2025) and BatchNorm (Ioffe & Szegedy, 2015).
- **Language-based Offline RL:** The method was tested on the Wordle game (Lokshtanov & Subercaseaux, 2022) using Implicit Language Q-Learning (ILQL, Snell et al., 2023) with a GPT-2 small architecture.
- **Streaming Reinforcement Learning:** Experiments on 7 Atari games in a streaming setting (no replay buffer, batch updates, or parallel environments) using Stream Q(λ) (Elsayed et al., 2024).

**Metrics and Analysis:**
Performance was measured by the Area Under the Curve (AUC) of the Inter-Quantile Mean (IQM) return, normalized by the AUC of the target-based baseline, providing a robust measure of learning speed and overall performance.
Additional analyses included:
- **Gradient Cosine Similarity:** To compare the learning dynamics of iS-QN, target-free, and target-based approaches.
- **Target Churn:** To quantify the stability of regression targets across different methods.
- **Effective Rank (srank):** To assess the expressivity of the learned feature representations.
- **Dormant Neurons:** To investigate activation patterns.
- **Ablation Studies:** On the target update period (T), the number of Bellman updates (K), the width of the feature extractor, and dynamic weighting of Bellman terms using meta-gradients.
- **Pilot Study:** Investigation of mixed-precision training (float16 operations) for further resource reduction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that iterated Shared Q-Learning (iS-QL) consistently reduces the performance gap between target-free and target-based methods across various reinforcement learning settings, often surpassing the performance of target-based baselines while maintaining a low memory footprint.

#### 4.1. Online Discrete Control (Atari DQN)

- **Performance Gap Bridging:** Target-free DQN (TF-DQN) exhibited a performance drop compared to target-based DQN (TB-DQN). For instance, with a CNN architecture and LayerNorm, TF-DQN's AUC was 10% lower. The iS-DQN K=1 variant (Shared Features) reduced this gap. As the number of parallel Bellman iterations (K) increased, the performance gap further diminished.
- **Superior Performance and Memory Efficiency:** Notably, iS-DQN K=9 (using 10 linear heads) not only closed the gap but outperformed TB-DQN by 6% in AUC, while using approximately half the number of parameters. This suggests a significant reduction in memory footprint without performance compromise.
- **Impact of Architecture:** With the IMPALA architecture and LayerNorm, iS-DQN also annulled the performance gap as K increased. In this setting, even K=49 was beneficial, suggesting that richer feature representations from the IMPALA architecture can support more parallel Bellman iterations.
- **Robustness to Normalization:** Even without normalization layers (vanilla CNN), where TF-DQN suffered a major 60% performance drop, iS-DQN K=1 reduced this to an 18% gap. This indicates the effectiveness of the shared linear head concept in stabilizing learning.

#### 4.2. Offline Discrete Control (Atari CQL)

- **Reduced Gap:** In offline RL, TF-CQL showed a 26% performance gap compared to TB-CQL. iS-CQL K=9 reduced this gap to 6%.
- **Generalizability of Shared Features:** The "Ensemble Shared Features" (ES-CQL) variant, which applies the parameter-sharing idea to an ensemble-based approach, also outperformed TF-CQL. This suggests that the principle of sharing feature extractors with light-weight target heads is broadly applicable beyond iterated Q-learning.

#### 4.3. Online Continuous Control (DMC SAC)

- **Performance Recovery and Memory Savings:** For SAC on DMC hard tasks, iS-SAC K=1 recovered the performance drop of TF-SAC. Importantly, this was achieved while reducing the total number of parameters by 49% (due to the critic being a major component of memory usage).
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** iS-SAC K=9 performed on par with K=1, which was attributed to the weighting scheme of Bellman terms. Discounting future terms by 0.25 was found beneficial, indicating some sensitivity to how multiple Bellman updates are aggregated.

#### 4.4. Scaling Up to Language Models (Wordle ILQL)

- **Improved Learning Speed:** On the Wordle game with a GPT-2 small architecture, iS-ILQL K=9 improved the learning speed over TB-ILQL by more than 5% AUC.
- **Significant RAM Savings:** iS-ILQL achieved this while saving 88 million parameters (33% of RAM) compared to the original TB-ILQL, demonstrating scalability to large language models.

#### 4.5. Streaming Reinforcement Learning

- **Enhanced Learning Speed:** In the challenging streaming setting without replay buffers or batch updates, iS-Stream Q(λ) K=3 improved over TF-Stream Q(λ) by over 10% AUC.
- **K-value Sensitivity:** While K=3 was beneficial, increasing K to 9 was not helpful, suggesting that the absence of batch updates and replay buffers can introduce high gradient variance, affecting the stability of shared parameters for a larger number of heads.

#### 4.6. Insights into Effectiveness

- **Gradient Dynamics:** Analysis showed that the cosine similarity between the gradients of iS-DQN K=1 and TB-DQN was higher than that between TF-DQN and TB-DQN. This indicates that iS-DQN's learning dynamics are more aligned with the stable updates of target-based approaches.
- **Target Stability (Target Churn):** The target churn (difference in regression targets before and after a batch update) for iS-DQN K=1 and K=9 was found to be between that of TB-DQN (zero churn) and TF-DQN (constant churn of one). This suggests iS-QN's targets are more stable than target-free ones.
- **Representation Expressivity (srank):** iS-DQN K=1 yielded a higher effective rank of features in the penultimate layer than TF-DQN, closer to TB-DQN. Learning K=9 Bellman iterations further increased the representation capacity, suggesting iS-QN fosters richer state representations.

#### 4.7. Limitations and Further Studies

- **Hyperparameter K:** The number of Bellman updates K is a new hyperparameter, which requires tuning, although performance often shows a stable increase before diminishing.
- **Resource Focus:** The work primarily focuses on the memory of function approximators, not other components like replay buffers or optimizers.
- **Training Time/FLOPS:** Except for the ILQL case (due to single-pass TD error computation), iS-QN does not significantly reduce training time or floating-point operations during training, although it uses the same amount during inference as target-free methods.
- **Ablation Studies:** Demonstrated robustness to target update period T. Showed that increasing K generally improves performance, with diminishing returns or slight decreases at very high K values depending on the richness of the shared features. Meta-gradient tuning of Bellman update coefficients converged to equal weights in the tested Atari setting.
- **Mixed Precision Training:** A pilot study showed that iS-QN maintained its performance benefits even with float16 operations, suggesting compatibility with mixed-precision training for further resource reduction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in deep reinforcement learning by offering a practical solution to the long-standing trade-off between learning stability and memory efficiency. The introduction of **iterated Shared Q-Learning (iS-QL)**, particularly its iterated Shared Q-Network (iS-QN) implementation, demonstrates a method that can bridge, and often eliminate, the performance gap between target-free and target-based approaches.

The primary significance lies in its ability to achieve performance comparable to, or even exceeding, traditional target-based algorithms while maintaining a memory footprint close to that of target-free methods. This has several potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Resource Efficiency:** By requiring significantly less memory for Q-networks (e.g., half the parameters of TB-DQN on Atari, 49% reduction for SAC on DMC, 33% RAM savings for ILQL on Wordle), iS-QL enables the deployment of DRL agents in memory-constrained environments, such as edge devices or mobile platforms, where full target networks are prohibitive.
2. **Scalability to Complex Tasks:** The reduced memory burden allows for the use of larger online networks, which can be critical for handling high-dimensional state spaces, multi-modal inputs, or complex tasks requiring highly expressive function approximators. This can unlock new avenues for DRL applications that were previously limited by hardware constraints.
3. **Improved Sample Efficiency:** The consistent improvement in learning speed and overall performance (measured by AUC) over target-free baselines, and often over target-based baselines, means that agents can learn effective policies with fewer environment interactions. This is invaluable in real-world scenarios where data collection is expensive, time-consuming, or unsafe.
4. **Broader Applicability:** The demonstration of iS-QL's effectiveness across a diverse range of RL settings—including online and offline learning, discrete and continuous control, language-based tasks, and even the challenging streaming scenario—underscores its generalizability and potential as a versatile tool in the DRL landscape. Its compatibility with existing regularization techniques further broadens its utility.
5. **New Theoretical Insights:** The analysis of gradient alignment, target churn, and representation expressivity provides valuable insights into *why* iS-QL is effective. It shows that sharing features and freezing only the last layer can stabilize learning dynamics and foster richer state representations, bridging the gap in fundamental mechanisms between target-free and target-based methods.
6. **Future Research Directions:** The work opens up avenues for further research, such as optimizing the weighting of Bellman terms in the iterated loss, exploring its combination with other resource-saving techniques like mixed-precision training (which showed positive pilot results), or integrating it with methods for optimizing replay buffers or optimizers.

In conclusion, iS-QL represents a significant step towards developing more robust and resource-efficient deep reinforcement learning algorithms. Its practical benefits in terms of memory savings and performance gains, coupled with its broad applicability, position it to have a substantial impact on the design and deployment of DRL systems in increasingly complex and resource-limited real-world applications.
