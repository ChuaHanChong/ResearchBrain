# A Dual Process VLA: Efficient Robotic Manipulation Leveraging VLM

> [!summary] Summary
> The Dual Process VLA (DP-VLA) framework introduces a hierarchical approach to robotic manipulation, balancing the reasoning of large vision-language models with real-time control by separating high-level planning from low-level execution. This architecture, implemented using OpenVLA as the L-Sys2 and BC-Transformer as the S-Sys1, achieved higher task success rates and faster inference speeds of over 10 Hz in the RoboCasa simulation environment.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often face a trade-off between strong reasoning capabilities and real-time performance due to the high computational demands of large language models.
- Models optimized for speed typically sacrifice generalization abilities, while those with robust reasoning are computationally intensive and too slow for real-time robotic applications.
- Current VLA models frequently require expensive fine-tuning when deployed in new environments or with different robot hardware.

## Method

- Introduces a **Dual Process VLA (DP-VLA)** framework, inspired by cognitive psychology, which separates the VLA process into two distinct subsystems.
- Utilizes a ==Large System 2 Model (L-Sys2)== for high-level reasoning and task planning, operating at a lower frequency when significant changes occur.
- Employs a ==Small System 1 Model (S-Sys1)== for real-time motor control and sensory processing, operating continuously and receiving ==latent features== from the L-Sys2.

## Results

- The Dual Process VLA (DP-VLA) framework achieved higher task success rates compared to both its component models, fine-tuned OpenVLA and BC-Transformer alone.
- DP-VLA operated at over **10 Hz** inference speed, a significant improvement over OpenVLA's approximately **1 Hz**, making it suitable for real-time robotic applications.
- Ablation studies showed that latent features extracted from the decoding stage of OpenVLA were more effective than those from the prefill stage, and using features from pre-trained OpenVLA led to better performance than features from fine-tuned OpenVLA.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Latent features extracted from the high-level reasoning model (L-Sys2) remain valid for multiple timesteps, allowing the low-level control model (S-Sys1) to operate continuously and efficiently without constant updates from the computationally intensive L-Sys2.
> - Separating high-level reasoning from real-time control enables a VLA system to balance sophisticated reasoning capabilities with the need for efficient, real-time robot operation.
> - Latent features from the decoding stage of the L-Sys2 (OpenVLA) and features from its pre-trained state proved more effective for transferring knowledge to the S-Sys1 than features from a fine-tuned L-Sys2.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation based on natural language instructions and visual perception represents a significant challenge in robotics research. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have shown promising capabilities in bridging the gap between human instructions and robot execution. However, existing solutions often face a critical trade-off between reasoning capabilities and real-time performance.

The paper "A Dual Process VLA" introduces a novel framework inspired by cognitive psychology's dual-process theory, which separates human thinking into fast, intuitive processes (System 1) and slow, deliberate reasoning (System 2). This approach aims to balance the sophisticated reasoning capabilities of large vision-language models (VLMs) with the need for efficient, real-time robot control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

Recent advancements in VLA models have been driven by large datasets like Open-X-Embodiment (OXE) and simulation environments such as RoboCasa. These datasets have enabled the development of increasingly capable models that combine language understanding with visual perception for robot control.

However, three key challenges persist in the current landscape:

1. **Computational Demands**: Large VLMs provide robust reasoning capabilities but require significant computational resources, resulting in slow inference speeds unsuitable for real-time applications.

2. **Generalization vs. Speed**: Models optimized for speed often sacrifice generalization abilities, while those with strong reasoning capabilities tend to be computationally intensive.

3. **Adaptation Requirements**: Existing VLA models frequently require expensive fine-tuning when deployed in new environments or with different robot hardware.

Current approaches like RT-2, RoboFlamingo, and OpenVLA demonstrate strong reasoning capabilities but suffer from slow inference speeds. Conversely, models like BC-Transformer and ALOHA prioritize speed but struggle with complex reasoning tasks and generalization to new environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. The Dual Process Framework

The Dual Process VLA (DP-VLA) introduces a hierarchical framework that separates the VLA process into two distinct subsystems:

1. **Large System 2 Model (L-Sys2)**: Responsible for high-level reasoning, task planning, and understanding visual scenes and language instructions. This component operates at a lower frequency, processing information only when significant changes occur or new instructions are received.

2. **Small System 1 Model (S-Sys1)**: Handles real-time motor control and sensory processing, operating continuously to adapt the L-Sys2's instructions to the robot's current state.

This separation allows the system to offload computationally intensive reasoning tasks to the less frequently executed L-Sys2, while the S-Sys1 maintains efficient real-time performance for motor control.

#### 3.2. Latent Feature Transfer Formulation

The DP-VLA framework operates through a process of feature transfer between the two subsystems:

1. The L-Sys2 processes visual information and language instructions to extract latent features that encode both reasoning information and environmental context. This can be represented as:

 ```
   z_i = f_ℓ(I_i, L)
   ```

 Where z_i is the latent feature, I_i is the visual input, L is the language instruction, and f_ℓ is the function of the L-Sys2.

2. These latent features are transferred to the S-Sys1, which combines them with current sensory inputs to predict the robot's actions:

 ```
   a_t = f_s(s_t, z_i)
   ```

 Where a_t is the action at time t, s_t is the robot's current state, and f_s is the function of the S-Sys1.

The key insight is that latent features from the L-Sys2 remain valid for multiple timesteps, allowing the S-Sys1 to operate continuously with less frequent updates from the L-Sys2. This significantly reduces the computational burden while maintaining the reasoning capabilities of the large model.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

The researchers implemented the DP-VLA framework using:

- **L-Sys2**: OpenVLA, a state-of-the-art VLA model with strong reasoning capabilities, fine-tuned on the RoboCasa dataset.
- **S-Sys1**: BC-Transformer, a behavior cloning model trained to predict actions based on the robot's state and latent features from the L-Sys2.

The experiments were conducted in the RoboCasa simulation environment, which provides realistic kitchen scenarios for robot manipulation tasks.

For training the S-Sys1, the researchers:

1. Generated latent features from OpenVLA for each instruction and visual scene in the RoboCasa dataset.
2. Trained BC-Transformer to predict actions based on these latent features and the robot's current state.
3. Experimented with different feature extraction approaches, including features from different layers of OpenVLA and features from both pre-trained and fine-tuned models.

The implementation includes a data collection process where the L-Sys2 processes visual information and language instructions to extract latent features, which are then used along with state transitions to train the S-Sys1 model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The DP-VLA framework demonstrated significant improvements over both its component models:

1. **Task Success Rate**: DP-VLA achieved a higher success rate than both fine-tuned OpenVLA and BC-Transformer alone, indicating that the hierarchical approach effectively combines the strengths of both models.

2. **Inference Speed**: While OpenVLA alone operates at approximately 1 Hz, the DP-VLA framework can operate at over 10 Hz, comparable to BC-Transformer. This makes it suitable for real-time robotic applications.

3. **Feature Extraction Analysis**: An ablation study revealed that latent features extracted from the decoding stage of OpenVLA (related to action generation) were more effective than features from the prefill stage (related to text understanding).

4. **Pre-trained vs. Fine-tuned Features**: Interestingly, using latent features from pre-trained OpenVLA resulted in better performance than using features from fine-tuned OpenVLA. This suggests that fine-tuning may have diminished the model's general task understanding capabilities.

The experimental results demonstrated that the DP-VLA framework effectively balances reasoning capabilities with real-time performance, achieving both higher success rates and faster inference speeds compared to existing approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Significance and Future Directions

The DP-VLA framework represents a significant advancement in VLA models for robot manipulation:

1. **Real-time Applications**: By enabling faster inference speeds without sacrificing reasoning capabilities, DP-VLA makes it possible to deploy sophisticated VLA models in real-world robotic applications that require real-time responsiveness.

2. **Scalable Design**: The modular architecture allows for seamless integration of advancements in VLM technology. As more powerful VLMs become available, they can be incorporated into the L-Sys2 without requiring major changes to the overall system.

3. **Reduced Adaptation Costs**: The separation of reasoning and control potentially reduces the computational costs needed to adapt to new environments and robot hardware.

The authors propose several promising directions for future research:

1. **Dynamic Scheduling**: Developing mechanisms to dynamically adjust the execution frequency of the L-Sys2 based on task requirements and environmental changes.

2. **Multi-Level Reasoning**: Decomposing the L-Sys2 into multiple levels, each operating at different frequencies depending on the complexity of the reasoning required.

3. **Exploration of Different VLMs**: Experimenting with various VLMs to leverage latent features that can further enhance the model's capabilities, such as long-horizon planning and Chain of Thought reasoning.

4. **Hardware Implementation**: Testing the framework on physical robots in real-world environments to validate its practical applicability.

#### 5.2. Conclusion

The Dual Process VLA framework represents an innovative approach to addressing the efficiency and scalability challenges in robot manipulation. By drawing inspiration from human cognitive processes, the researchers have developed a hierarchical system that effectively balances the reasoning capabilities of large VLMs with the need for efficient real-time control.

The framework's ability to achieve higher task success rates while maintaining fast inference speeds demonstrates its potential for practical applications in robotics. Moreover, its modular design allows for continuous improvement as advancements in VLM technology emerge.

As robotics continues to move toward more natural interfaces and greater autonomy, frameworks like DP-VLA will play a crucial role in enabling robots to understand and execute complex tasks based on human instructions. This research contributes significantly to the development of more capable, efficient, and adaptable robotic systems.
