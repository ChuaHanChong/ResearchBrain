# CRL-VLA: Continual Vision-Language-Action Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Southampton and Westlake University developed CRL-VLA, a framework for continual post-training of Vision-Language-Action models in robotics that prevents catastrophic forgetting while acquiring new skills. The method, based on controlling goal-conditioned advantage magnitude, demonstrated improved anti-forgetting, achieving a positive Backward Transfer of 0.17 in multi-task scenarios on VLA benchmarks.

## Problem

- Enabling embodied agents to continually learn new robotic manipulation skills without forgetting previously acquired abilities.
- Addressing the stability-plasticity dilemma in Continual Reinforcement Learning for large-scale, multimodal Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.
- Limitations of current CRL methods (e.g., experience replay, second-order regularization, simple KL regularization, backbone freezing) for complex, long-horizon VLA tasks.

## Method

- A continual learning framework, **CRL-VLA**, for VLA models grounded in a theoretical understanding of ==goal-conditioned advantage magnitude==.
- A novel ==dual-critic architecture== featuring a frozen ==Goal-Conditioned Value (GCV) critic== for stability and a trainable ==Monte Carlo (MC) critic== for plasticity.
- ==Asymmetric regulation strategy== through ==GCV consistency==, MC critic learning, and policy divergence constraints (==PPO== + ==KL regularization==) for balanced stability and plasticity.

## Results

- CRL-VLA consistently outperformed baselines in continual VLA benchmarks, demonstrating superior balance of stability and plasticity.
- The CRL-VLA (V) variant achieved a positive Backward Transfer of **0.17** in multi-task learning, suggesting learning new tasks improved performance on prior ones, alongside a Final Average Return of **0.74**.
- Ablation studies validated that the dual-critic Goal-Conditioned Value Formulation and specific regularization terms are essential for CRL-VLA's effectiveness in anti-forgetting and forward adaptation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Forgetting in continual VLA learning is primarily caused by the goal-conditioned advantage magnitude.
> - Stability (retaining old knowledge) and plasticity (acquiring new skills) can be controlled independently by managing critic approximation error on old tasks and leveraging the natural boundedness of Monte Carlo returns for new tasks.
> - A Goal-Conditioned Value Formulation (GCVF) within the critic architecture is crucial for robust language-following and accurate value estimation in VLA.
