# TIMotion: Temporal and Interactive Framework for Efficient Human-Human Motion Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> TIMotion casts human-human motion generation as a MetaMotion pipeline of temporal modeling then interaction mixing, and fixes the temporal stage with Causal Interactive Injection, Role-Evolving Scanning, and Localized Pattern Amplification. Plugs into Transformer, Mamba, or RWKV mixers, cutting parameters and reaching SOTA FID 4.702 and Top1 R-Precision 0.501 on InterHuman.

## Problem

- Single-person-extension methods concatenate two people into one sequence before feeding a single-person module, discarding the causal structure between the two motions.
- Separate-modeling methods encode each person independently then fuse via self- and cross-attention, skipping explicit temporal modeling of the interaction sequence itself.
- Prior active/passive role assignment (e.g. splitting text into active and passive voice) is treated as static, ignoring that roles swap dynamically as an interaction unfolds.

## Method

- **Causal Interactive Injection** interleaves the two single-person sequences into one ==causal interaction sequence== via ==odd/even indexing==, exploiting temporal causal ordering before ==interaction mixing==.
- **Role-Evolving Scanning** builds a ==symmetric causal sequence== with swapped active/passive assignment, concatenates it with the causal sequence, and splits/merges mixer outputs to let roles shift dynamically.
- **Localized Pattern Amplification** applies ==AdaLN==-conditioned ==1D convolutions== per person with a ==residual branch==, then fuses local and global embeddings via concatenation and a linear layer for smoother motion.

## Results

- On InterHuman, TIMotion+RWKV sets new SOTA with FID **4.702** and Top1 R-Precision **0.501**, beating InterGen's FID **5.918** and Top1 **0.371**.
- Ablation shows CII, RES, and LPA added incrementally cut InterGen's **182M** parameters to **127M** while improving Top1 from **0.371** to **0.501** and FID from **5.918** to **4.702**.
- TIMotion+Transformer uses **65M** parameters and **40.4G** FLOPs versus InterGen's **182M** and **80.5G**, with inference time **0.632s** versus **1.991s** per sample.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Prioritizing temporal modeling of the interaction sequence over interaction-mixing design can simplify the mixer and shrink parameter count while improving quality.
> - Treating active/passive roles as dynamically evolving rather than fixed per text description better matches how real interactions unfold over time.
> - A gradient-magnitude analysis shows causal joint sequence modeling yields a strictly larger gradient norm than separate cross-attention modeling, implying faster convergence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative computer vision has seen advancements in human motion generation, particularly with text-to-motion tasks. While significant progress has been made in generating realistic and diverse motions for single individuals, these methods often do not account for the complexities of interactions between multiple people. Human-human motion generation is recognized as a more challenging task due to the dynamic and interdependent nature of interpersonal actions.

Existing approaches to multi-person motion generation broadly fall into two categories:
1. **Single-person-based methods**: These methods typically concatenate representations of multiple individuals into a single input, then process them using models originally designed for single-person motion (e.g., extensions of MDM). This approach often leads to inadequate modeling of interaction dynamics and can result in suboptimal performance.
2. **Separate modeling-based methods**: These approaches model individuals independently and subsequently integrate interaction through mechanisms like self-attention and cross-attention (e.g., InterGen). While attempting to capture interaction, they may still overlook explicit temporal modeling of the interaction sequences themselves, potentially leading to inefficient parameter usage and suboptimal long-sequence generation.

This research addresses these limitations by introducing a framework that explicitly focuses on efficient temporal modeling and interactive dynamics between individuals. It seeks to improve upon existing methods by providing a more structured approach to capturing causal relationships and dynamic roles within multi-person interactions, while also integrating with and evaluating modern sequence modeling architectures such as Transformers, Mamba, and RWKV.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and effective framework for human-human motion generation. The authors aim to improve the realism and coherence of generated interactive motions, particularly for longer sequences and complex interactions, while also reducing computational redundancy.

The motivation for this work stems from several identified shortcomings in the current research landscape:
- **Neglect of Complex Interactive Dynamics**: Most existing human motion generation methods are tailored for single individuals and do not adequately model the nuanced and dynamic interactions between multiple people. This omission limits their applicability in scenarios requiring social intelligence, such as computer animation, game development, and robotic control.
- **Inadequate Temporal Modeling in Existing Multi-person Approaches**: The authors conceptualize human-human motion generation as comprising two phases: temporal modeling and interaction mixing. They argue that prior methods, whether single-person-based extensions or separate modeling, inadequately address the temporal modeling aspect.
 - Single-person extensions concatenate individuals, treating them as a single, larger entity, which oversimplifies the interaction and fails to capture distinct human dynamics. This leads to suboptimal performance.
 - Separate modeling methods focus heavily on the "interaction mixing" phase but often "skip the modeling of interaction sequences," meaning they do not explicitly establish causal or temporal relationships *before* interaction mixing. This can result in suboptimal performance and redundant model parameters because the interaction mixing module must infer these temporal relationships from less structured inputs.
- **Need for Efficiency**: The identified redundancy in model parameters in existing approaches suggests an opportunity for more efficient model designs that achieve superior or comparable performance with fewer computational resources.
- **Dynamic Role-Playing in Interactions**: Human interactions often involve shifts in active and passive roles between individuals. Current methods may not dynamically adapt to these role changes, leading to less natural motion generation.
- **Smoothness and Rationality of Motion**: Globally focused models (like Transformers) sometimes overlook localized, short-term motion patterns, which are crucial for generating physically plausible and smooth individual movements within an interaction.

To address these motivations, the authors propose the TIMotion (Temporal and Interactive Modeling) framework, which introduces three specific technical designs:
1. **Causal Interactive Injection**: To explicitly model the temporal and causal properties of two interacting motion sequences as a unified causal sequence, aiming to simplify subsequent interaction mixing and reduce parameters.
2. **Role-Evolving Scanning**: To enable the network to dynamically adjust to changing active and passive roles between individuals throughout an interaction, rather than relying on static role assignments.
3. **Localized Pattern Amplification**: To capture and integrate short-term motion patterns for each individual, ensuring smoother and more rational movements that might be missed by global interaction modeling.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The TIMotion framework is built upon the foundation of motion diffusion models and is designed to address the specific challenges of human-human motion generation. The overall process is conceptualized within a general framework called MetaMotion, which divides motion generation into two sequential phases: temporal modeling and interaction mixing.

**A. Motion Representation and Diffusion Model:**
The method utilizes a diffusion model, a common approach for generative tasks. Motion states (x_i) are represented in a non-canonical format, including global joint positions, velocities, 6D local rotations in the root frame, and binary foot-ground contact information.
- **Diffusion Process**: A forward process incrementally degrades motion data into isotropic Gaussian noise over T steps.
- **Denoising Process**: A neural network, parameterized by θ, is trained to reverse this process, transforming noise back into coherent motion. The network predicts the added noise (ϵ) conditioned on the current noisy state (x_t), timestep (t), and additional context information (c), such as text descriptions. The training objective minimizes the noise prediction error.

**B. MetaMotion Framework:**
The MetaMotion concept abstracts the human-human motion generation process:
1. **Temporal Modeling**: Two single-person motion sequences (X_a, X_b) are processed to create an intermediate input sequence (X).
2. **Interaction Mixing**: This sequence (X) is then fed into an interaction-mixing module, which could be a Transformer, Mamba, or RWKV architecture, to produce the final generated motion (Y).

**C. TIMotion's Core Technical Designs:**

TIMotion introduces three key components that specifically address the temporal modeling phase and enhance the overall interaction generation:

1. **Causal Interactive Injection (CII)**:
 - This module models the causal relationships between the two individuals' motions.
 - Given two separate motion sequences {x_a, x_b}, CII interweaves their frames to form a single causal interaction sequence (x_cii). This is achieved by alternating frames from x_a and x_b (e.g., x_1 from person A, x_1 from person B, x_2 from person A, x_2 from person B, etc.).
 - This unified sequence explicitly captures that the current motion of both individuals is influenced by their previous actions, simplifying the task for the subsequent interaction-mixing module.
 - After interaction mixing, the individual motion embeddings are separated from the output based on the same interleaving pattern.

2. **Role-Evolving Scanning (RES)**:
 - Recognizing that active and passive roles in human interactions are dynamic and can shift over time, RES prevents static role assignments.
 - It complements CII by creating a *symmetric* causal interaction sequence (x_sym_cii) where the roles of x_a and x_b are swapped during the interleaving process.
 - The original x_cii and the x_sym_cii are then concatenated channel-wise to form the final input (X) for the interaction-mixing module.
 - After the interaction-mixing module produces an output (Y), it is split, and the individual embeddings derived from both the original and symmetric causal sequences are combined (via element-wise sum). This allows the network to dynamically synthesize motion considering both potential active/passive interpretations, adapting to the evolving interaction context.

3. **Localized Pattern Amplification (LPA)**:
 - This module is designed to capture short-term, fine-grained motion patterns for each individual, which global models might overlook, leading to smoother and more rational motion.
 - It applies 1-D convolution layers (with residual connections) *separately* to the individual motion sequences (x_a, x_b) and integrates text conditioning via Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN).
 - The resulting localized motion embeddings are then concatenated with the global motion embeddings obtained from the RES output. A linear layer then restores the channel dimensions for subsequent processing.

**D. Objective Function:**
The overall training loss (L_motion) is a weighted sum of several components:
- **L_simple**: The standard diffusion loss (mean squared error between predicted and true noise).
- **L_foot**: Foot contact loss.
- **L_vel**: Joint velocity loss.
- **L_BL**: Bone length loss.
- **L_DM**: Masked joint distance map loss.
- **L_RO**: Relative orientation loss.
These additional regularization losses, borrowed from InterGen, promote physically plausible and stable motion generation.

**E. Computational Aspects:**
The framework is designed to be compatible with various interaction-mixing architectures (Transformer, Mamba, RWKV). Theoretical analysis (Appendix A) suggests that the causal interactive modeling facilitates faster model convergence due to potentially larger gradient magnitudes compared to separate modeling.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results, alongside ablation studies, to demonstrate the effectiveness of the TIMotion framework.

**A. Quantitative Performance Comparison:**
- **InterHuman Dataset (Table 1)**: TIMotion achieved new state-of-the-art (SoTA) performance. Notably, TIMotion+RWKV demonstrated the lowest Frechet Inception Distance (FID) of 4.702 and the highest Top1 R-Precision of 0.501, outperforming previous methods like InterGen (5.918 FID, 0.371 Top1 R-Precision) across multiple metrics. TIMotion variants using Transformer and Mamba also consistently surpassed InterGen.
- **Inter-X Dataset (Appendix B, Table 7)**: Experiments on the larger Inter-X dataset further confirmed the generalizability of TIMotion. It consistently outperformed comparative methods across FID, R-Precision, Diversity, MM Dist, and MModality metrics, regardless of the chosen interaction mixing structure (Transformer, Mamba, or RWKV).

**B. Effectiveness of Temporal Modeling Approaches (Table 2):**
- A comparison of different temporal modeling strategies across various interaction-mixing structures (Transformer, Mamba, RWKV) showed that TIMotion's causal interactive modeling consistently outperformed both "single-person extension" and "separate modeling." This validates the hypothesis that explicit temporal modeling, as implemented in TIMotion, is crucial for effective human-human motion generation. "Single-person extension" performed the worst, highlighting its limitations for interactive scenarios.

**C. Ablation Studies on TIMotion Components (Table 3):**
- **Causal Interactive Injection (CII)**: Incrementally adding CII to a baseline model (InterGen adapted with RWKV) significantly improved performance (Top1 R-Precision from 0.371 to 0.478, FID from 5.918 to 5.410) while notably reducing model parameters (from 182M to 144M).
- **Role-Evolving Scanning (RES)**: Further incorporating RES resulted in additional performance gains (Top1 R-Precision to 0.494, FID to 5.019) and a further reduction in parameters (to 115M).
- **Localized Pattern Amplification (LPA)**: The final addition of LPA led to the peak performance (Top1 R-Precision of 0.501, FID of 4.702), with a slight increase in parameters (to 127M). This sequential improvement demonstrates the individual contributions and synergistic effects of each proposed module.

**D. Design of Localized Pattern Amplification (Table 4 & Figure 6):**
- **Normalization**: Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN) was found to be the most effective normalization method for LPA, indicating the benefit of integrating text-conditional information into local pattern extraction. Batch Normalization (BN) and Layer Normalization (LN) did not yield similar gains.
- **Kernel Size**: A kernel size of (3,1) for the convolution layers in LPA was determined to be optimal, suggesting that capturing short-term motion patterns is more beneficial than broader local patterns.
- **Spectrum Analysis**: Visualization of motion feature spectra (Figure 6) revealed that LPA reduced the high-frequency components of the generated motion. This reduction correlates with the generation of smoother motions, as high-frequency components often correspond to abrupt changes. Quantitatively, the average proportion of high-frequency amplitude was significantly lower with LPA (0.3729) compared to without LPA (0.9063).

**E. Computational Complexity and Editability (Tables 5 & 6, Figure 5):**
- **Computational Complexity**: TIMotion, especially with a Transformer backbone, demonstrated reduced computational complexity compared to InterGen. TIMotion+Transformer required fewer parameters (65M vs. 182M), fewer FLOPs (40.4G vs. 80.5G), and significantly faster inference time per sample (0.632 s vs. 1.991 s) while achieving superior performance.
- **Editability (Motion In-betweening)**: TIMotion surpassed InterGen in the motion in-betweening task, achieving a higher Top1 R-Precision (0.516 vs. 0.461) and lower FID (3.590 vs. 4.700). Qualitative results (Figure 5) showed that TIMotion generated smooth and natural transitions between fixed start and end frames, consistent with the given text descriptions.

**F. Qualitative Comparisons (Figure 4):**
- Visual comparisons indicated that the motion sequences generated by TIMotion were more consistent and accurate in depicting the textual descriptions of human-human interactions compared to those generated by InterGen.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TIMotion framework represents an advancement in the field of human-human motion generation, addressing identified limitations in existing methodologies. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

- **Enhanced Realism and Coherence**: By explicitly modeling temporal causality, dynamic roles, and local motion patterns, TIMotion is capable of generating more realistic, smoother, and contextually coherent human-human interactions. This directly contributes to bridging the gap between single-person and complex multi-person motion synthesis.

- **Foundational Framework for Multi-person Interaction**: The conceptualization of "MetaMotion" provides a modular framework that separates temporal modeling from interaction mixing. This abstraction offers a principled approach for future research, allowing different temporal modeling strategies and interaction-mixing architectures to be explored and integrated systematically.

- **Computational Efficiency**: TIMotion demonstrates the ability to achieve superior or state-of-the-art performance with a reduced number of model parameters and lower computational costs (FLOPs and inference time). This efficiency is valuable for real-world applications where computational resources might be constrained, such as real-time animation, interactive virtual environments, or mobile applications.

- **Architectural Versatility**: The framework's demonstrated compatibility with diverse interaction-mixing modules (Transformer, Mamba, RWKV) indicates its robustness and adaptability. This allows researchers to leverage advancements in sequence modeling architectures within the context of human-human motion generation.

- **Improved Motion Editing Capabilities**: The superior performance in tasks like motion in-betweening suggests that TIMotion can serve as a more robust foundation for various motion editing applications, enabling natural transitions and adjustments in interactive scenarios.

- **Broader Application Domains**: The advancements made by TIMotion have potential impacts across several industries and research areas:
 - **Computer Animation and Game Development**: Enables the creation of more believable non-player character (NPC) interactions, complex crowd behaviors, and cinematic sequences with multiple interacting agents.
 - **Robotics**: Could inform the development of more natural and socially aware human-robot interaction strategies, allowing robots to understand and generate appropriate social cues and responses.
 - **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR)**: Facilitates the generation of more immersive and interactive virtual characters, enhancing user experience in simulated environments.
 - **Human Behavior Analysis**: Offers a tool for synthesizing diverse interactive scenarios, which can be used to study and understand human social dynamics.

- **Inspiration for Future Research**: The work opens avenues for exploring more complex multi-person interactions beyond two individuals, investigating diverse conditioning inputs, and developing specialized motion editing tools. The theoretical analysis supporting faster convergence also provides insights for optimizing training processes in complex generative models.
