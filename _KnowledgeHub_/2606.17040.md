# R2RDreamer: 3D-aware Data Augmentation for Spatially-generalized 2D Manipulation Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Tsinghua University developed R2RDreamer, a real-to-real data augmentation framework that integrates 3D co-editing with 2D video completion to improve spatial generalization for 2D manipulation policies. It increased success rates for policies trained on a single source demonstration from a baseline of 3.13% to up to 46.9% on various real-world robotic tasks.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation policies often lack spatial generalization, requiring extensive and diverse demonstration data to perform tasks across varied object poses, robot configurations, and camera viewpoints.
- Collecting large volumes of real-world demonstration data for spatial variations is costly, time-consuming, and often repetitive.
- Existing real-to-real data augmentation methods struggle with visual distortions and missing information after 3D transformations due to incomplete RGB-D observations, often relying on restrictive 3D reconstruction or being primarily suitable for 3D pointcloud policies.

## Method

- **R2RDreamer** utilizes lightweight 3D co-editing to transform object pointclouds and robot end-effector poses, ensuring geometric consistency for edited actions, without requiring full 3D scene completion.
- It employs an ==occlusion-aware projection== method to generate masked 2D control videos from the incomplete 3D geometry, explicitly marking visually unreliable regions with black pixels.
- A projection-controlled video completion model, trained self-supervisely using synthetically masked real videos, synthesizes temporally coherent and visually realistic RGB observations to fill the masked regions.

## Results

- R2RDreamer substantially improved spatial generalization, increasing success rates from **3.13%** (single source demonstration baseline) to **21.9%**–**40.6%** for Diffusion Policy and **21.9%**–**46.9%** for π0 across real-world tasks.
- The augmented data from a single source demonstration achieved performance comparable to or exceeding that of policies trained on 5 to 15 human demonstrations, demonstrating efficiency in data usage.
- The approach effectively augmented data for both compact 2D visuomotor policies and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, indicating its broad applicability across different RGB-based policy architectures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Shifting the challenge of visual completion from complex 3D geometry reconstruction to 2D video generation allows for robust augmentation without demanding complete 3D scene information.
> - Accepting incomplete 3D object pointclouds as an intermediate representation makes the method robust to low-quality observations, fast motion, cluttered environments, and manipulation of articulated or deformable objects.
> - Self-supervised training using projection-consistent and random object-drop masks effectively prepares the video completion model to repair the specific types of holes and inconsistencies arising from real-to-real 3D editing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has made progress with visuomotor policies, including diffusion-style policies and vision-language-action (VLA) models. However, a significant challenge remains: achieving spatial generalization. This refers to the ability of a robot policy to perform a task consistently across variations in object poses, robot configurations, and camera viewpoints. Imitation learning, a common paradigm for training such policies, typically requires extensive and diverse demonstration data to achieve this generalization. Collecting such data through real-world teleoperation, especially for varied spatial layouts, is resource-intensive and often redundant, as the core manipulation skill remains constant while only geometric parameters change.

This work addresses the data scarcity problem by focusing on data augmentation from limited source demonstrations. Existing data augmentation strategies fall into two primary categories:

1. **Simulation-based augmentation:** Methods such as MimicGen and its variants (e.g.,) leverage simulated environments to synthesize diverse executions. While these methods offer controllable variation, they require the creation and maintenance of simulation assets, object and environment setup, and often introduce a "sim-to-real gap," where policies trained in simulation may not transfer effectively to real-world robots.
2. **Real-to-real augmentation:** More recent approaches, like DemoGen (e.g.,) and R2RGen (e.g.,), aim to avoid simulator construction by directly editing real RGB-D demonstrations. These methods typically involve parsing observations into 3D, jointly transforming object states and end-effector trajectories, and then using these augmented 3D observation-action pairs for policy training. A central challenge in this paradigm is addressing visual distortion that arises after editing incomplete real observations. RGB-D sensors only capture visible surfaces from the original viewpoint; after a significant spatial transformation, new surfaces that should be visible might be missing, and previously hidden surfaces might incorrectly appear visible. Existing real-to-real methods have attempted to mitigate this by relying on strong 3D scene parsing, object geometry completion, or specific assumptions about augmentation range and task structure. However, these requirements can be restrictive, depending on accurate templates, 6-DoF tracking, or advanced 3D reconstruction techniques, which may not be robust to low-quality observations, fast motion, clutter, or non-rigid objects. Furthermore, the outputs of these methods are primarily optimized for 3D pointcloud policies, making them less suitable for the broader family of RGB-based 2D policies and VLAs.

R2RDreamer positions itself within the real-to-real augmentation landscape. It aims to overcome the limitations of prior real-to-real methods by preserving the geometric consistency of 3D action-observation editing while shifting the complex visual completion problem from 3D geometry to 2D video space. This approach is designed to produce realistic RGB observations suitable for both compact 2D visuomotor policies and larger vision-language-action models, without requiring extensive 3D reconstruction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a real-to-real demonstration augmentation framework, named R2RDreamer, that enhances the spatial generalization capabilities of imitation-learned manipulation policies. This is achieved by generating spatially diverse training examples from a limited set of source demonstrations.

The motivation for R2RDreamer stems from several critical challenges in existing approaches:

1. **Data Scarcity for Spatial Generalization:** Robotic manipulation policies trained through imitation learning typically require a large volume of diverse demonstrations to generalize across varied spatial configurations of objects, robot arm positions, and camera viewpoints. Collecting this extensive data in the real world is expensive, time-consuming, and often repetitive, as the fundamental task skill remains constant. Data augmentation offers a practical alternative to manually collecting such diverse data.
2. **Limitations of Simulation-Based Augmentation:** While simulation provides a controlled environment for generating diverse data, it necessitates complex environment and object setups and often introduces a "sim-to-real gap." This gap refers to discrepancies between simulated and real-world physics or visual appearances, which can hinder the transferability of policies trained in simulation to real robots.
3. **Challenges in Existing Real-to-Real Augmentation:** Prior real-to-real methods (e.g., DemoGen, R2RGen) directly edit real RGB-D demonstrations in 3D to create augmented scenes. While they avoid the sim-to-real gap, they encounter significant visual distortion. After 3D transformations, surfaces that should be visible in the new viewpoint might be missing from the incomplete source RGB-D observation, and conversely, surfaces that should be occluded might remain visible. To address this, these methods often rely on strong assumptions, such as accurate 3D scene parsing, object geometry completion using templates or advanced reconstruction techniques (e.g., SAM 3D), or limiting the scope of augmentation. These requirements make them restrictive for various scenarios, including low-quality observations, fast movements, cluttered environments, or manipulation of articulated and deformable objects, where complete and reliable 3D geometry is difficult to obtain.
4. **Incompatibility with 2D RGB-based Policies:** The augmented observations produced by many existing real-to-real methods are primarily tailored for 3D pointcloud policies. This leaves a significant gap for RGB-based 2D visuomotor policies and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which constitute a broad and increasingly prevalent family of robotic control policies.

R2RDreamer's core objective is to overcome these limitations by:
- Maintaining the geometric consistency of 3D action-observation editing, which is crucial for ensuring that the robot's edited actions remain physically meaningful in the augmented scene.
- Shifting the challenging task of visual completion from 3D geometry reconstruction to 2D video space. This means instead of trying to reconstruct a complete 3D scene, R2RDreamer projects incomplete 3D information into a 2D masked control video, and then leverages a dense-control image-to-video model to synthesize temporally coherent and visually realistic RGB observations.
- Generating augmented data that is directly usable by both compact 2D visuomotor policies and larger VLA-style policies, thereby broadening its applicability.

By achieving these objectives, R2RDreamer aims to provide a more scalable and robust real-to-real data augmentation framework that can significantly reduce the data requirements for achieving spatial generalization in robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

R2RDreamer proposes a real-to-real demonstration augmentation framework that integrates 3D co-editing with 2D video completion to generate spatially diverse data for RGB-based manipulation policies. The methodology is structured around three main components: a problem statement outlining the scope, a lightweight 3D co-editing stage with occlusion-aware projection, and a projection-controlled video completion model.

#### 3.1. Problem Statement

The work considers the problem of imitation learning for a visuomotor policy $\pi$ that maps visual observations (RGB-D frames) and optional language instructions to robot actions (end-effector pose and gripper command). The goal is to augment a limited source demonstration $D_s = [l, \{(I_t, D_t, a_t)\}^{H}_{t=1}]$ into multiple augmented demonstrations $\hat{D}_j = [l, \{(\hat{I}^j_t, \hat{a}^j_t)\}^{\hat{H}^j}_{t=1}]$. These augmented demonstrations must preserve the task's semantics and contact structure while introducing variation in object poses, robot configurations, and camera viewpoints. A key aspect is that the final augmented data consists of RGB/video observations paired with edited actions, making them suitable for both compact 2D visuomotor policies (consuming RGB frames or video histories) and larger VLA-style policies (conditioning on language).

The framework retains 3D editing for ensuring action consistency, meaning the robot action aligns with the edited object state. However, it explicitly states that the edited 3D scene does not need to be a complete, policy-ready observation. Instead, incomplete 3D observations serve as control geometry, and unknown or invalid pixels are masked for repair by a video generation model.

#### 3.2. 3D Co-Editing with Occlusion-Aware Projection

This stage is responsible for generating geometrically consistent augmented scenes and preparing masked 2D control videos.

**Lightweight 3D Co-Editing:**
The process begins by taking a source RGB-D video. Task-relevant objects are segmented in the initial frame and tracked throughout the video using a tool like SAM2. This yields incomplete object pointclouds $P^k_t$ for each object $k$ at time $t$. Unlike prior real-to-real methods that required these partial pointclouds to be completed into policy-ready observations, R2RDreamer accepts incomplete geometry. The robot arm's geometry is reconstructed from its known kinematic model, ensuring its accurate representation.

All editable elements (object pointclouds, robot end-effector poses) are represented in a shared world frame. The source trajectory is divided into "skill segments" (gripper interaction with objects) and "motion segments" (connecting skills). For a skill interacting with an object group $G_i$, a spatial transform $T_i$ is sampled and applied to the relevant object pointclouds $\hat{P}^k_t = T_i P^k_t$ and end-effector poses $\hat{A}_{ee_t} = T_i A_{ee_t}$. This preserves the relative observation-action geometry for the contact-rich skill while relocating it. Gripper commands are preserved, and motion segments are generated via interpolation or motion planning. This approach allows for spatial distribution expansion while maintaining the demonstrated interaction's executability. The acceptance of incomplete pointcloud geometry as an intermediate carrier reduces reliance on demanding 3D reconstruction models, making the method applicable to rigid, articulated, and deformable objects.

**Occlusion-Aware Projection:**
After 3D co-editing, the incomplete pointclouds are projected into the target camera's 2D image plane to form a control video. Regions that are visually unreliable are left black for subsequent video completion. The projection process involves:
1. **Unmasked Control Frame Generation:** An initial unmasked control frame $\tilde{C}_t$ is rendered by projecting the edited object pointclouds, the model-based robot pointcloud, and the unchanged environment pointcloud. Pixels not hit by any projected point are initialized as black.
2. **Self-Occlusion Reasoning:** This step identifies and masks out 3D points that were visible in the source observation but should become hidden (self-occluded) after the object's transformation. A thin "backside proxy" $B_\delta(\hat{P}^k_t)$ is introduced behind the originally visible surface. Occlusion is tested over a local image patch using a local z-buffer radius, determining `CamVis` points. The self-occlusion holes $Q_{self,k_t}$ are defined as the projected object footprint not supported by these trusted camera-visible points.
3. **External Occlusion:** To account for uncertainties from contacts, background regions, or partial occluders not fully reconstructed in 3D, each projected object boundary is approximated with a polygon and dilated to create an "outside ring" $Q_{ext_t}$.
4. **Final Masked Control Frame:** The final black-filled projected control frame $C_t$ is created by masking $\tilde{C}_t$ with the union of all self-occlusion holes and external occlusion regions $Q_t$. Stacking these frames yields the masked control video $\{C_t\}^{H}_{t=1}$.

#### 3.3. Projection-Controlled Video Completion Model

This component is responsible for completing the masked control video into a temporally coherent RGB observation.

**Control-Augmented IT2V Model:**
R2RDreamer adapts the WAN2.2 IT2V video generation model. A dense control branch is added to incorporate the masked control video $C_{1:H}$, which is temporally and pixel-wise aligned with the target video. Both the clean (target) video and the control video are encoded into latents by the same WAN VAE. A flow-matching loss $L_{fm}$ is employed, which includes a weighting function $w(q)$. This weight, $w(q) = 1 + \lambda(1-q)$, assigns a larger penalty to non-black regions ($q$ being the mask at VAE resolution), discouraging the model from modifying pixels already specified by the control video. During inference, long videos are generated chunk by chunk. The initial chunk is conditioned on language and projected video control, while subsequent chunks also use the last generated frame of the preceding chunk as a reference image to enhance temporal continuity. Optionally, non-black pixels from the control video can be explicitly composed with generated pixels in masked regions, with blending at mask edges, to strictly preserve projected control information.

**Self-Supervised Training Data:**
Training the completion model requires pairs of masked control videos and corresponding clean target videos. Since ground-truth completed RGB observations are not available for spatially augmented scenes, a self-supervised approach is used to construct training data from ordinary RGB videos. The original video serves as the clean target, and synthetic masks simulate the unreliable regions found in real-to-real editing. Two types of masks are generated:
1. **Projection-Consistent Mask:** This type simulates geometric artifacts. Object masks from a video are lifted to 3D using measured or estimated depth. A random transform $T_k$ is applied (and reused across the clip for object $k$), and self-occlusion is calculated under the current camera pose. The surviving points are then mapped back to the original image plane to identify regions that would be self-occluded after such a transformation, forming holes $\bar{Q}_{self,k_t}$. External masks are added via polygon dilation. This process, "augment–occlude–augment-back," creates temporally coherent projection-consistent holes while preserving the original video as the target.
2. **Random Object-Drop Mask:** This type addresses irregular holes caused by imperfect segmentation, sparse depth, or approximate occlusion parameters. A sampled percentage of tracked object pixels are randomly designated as holes.

For both mask types, a masked control frame $\bar{C}_t$ is generated by black-filling the identified holes in the original video. This self-supervised training process does not require actions or complete 3D geometry. After training, the model takes the masked control video produced by the 3D co-editing stage and outputs completed RGB observations, which are then paired with the edited actions to form the final augmented demonstrations. The training is performed in stages, starting with ScanNet 3D scene data for clean geometry, then BridgeData V2 manipulation videos (with estimated depth and SAM2 tracks), and finally finetuning on custom offline robot data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to evaluate R2RDreamer's ability to improve spatial generalization from limited source demonstrations in real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

The evaluation was conducted on four real-world manipulation tasks: Pot-Food, Hang-Cup, Build-Bridge, and Cover-Object. This suite included tasks involving rigid objects and those with non-rigid or articulated objects (e.g., cloth in Cover-Object), which are particularly challenging for methods relying on complete 3D object geometry. All tasks required spatial generalization, as policies were evaluated on held-out object locations distinct from the source demonstrations. A real robot platform with an RGB-D camera and a parallel-jaw gripper was used for data collection and policy execution. While R2RDreamer utilized RGB-D for augmentation, the final trained policies consumed only RGB observations.

Two types of policies were tested:
- **Diffusion Policy (DP):** A compact visuomotor policy that uses RGB observations and proprioception.
- **$\pi_0$:** A Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy that also conditions on a language instruction.

The performance of R2RDreamer was compared against:
- **One-source imitation:** Training only on a single human demonstration.
- **Human-demonstration baselines:** Policies trained on 5, 15, and 30 human demonstrations to gauge the relative impact of R2RDreamer versus collecting more real data.

The primary metric was the real-world success rate on held-out task configurations. Qualitative visualizations of the augmentation process (source views, masked control views, and generated views) were also provided.

#### 4.2. Main Results

1. **Spatial Generalization Improvement:** Training policies on a single source demonstration resulted in low success rates (3.13%) across all tasks and both policy types. This indicated significant overfitting to the specific demonstrated configuration. Augmenting the same single source demonstration with R2RDreamer consistently and substantially improved performance. For Diffusion Policy (DP), success rates increased to 21.9%–40.6%, and for $\pi_0$, they reached 21.9%–46.9%.
2. **Comparison to Human Demonstrations:** The gains achieved by R2RDreamer were notably higher than those from the 5-source human demonstration baseline. In most experimental settings, R2RDreamer's performance was comparable to or even surpassed the 15-source human demonstration baseline. While the 30-source human demonstration baseline generally achieved higher success rates, the results suggest that R2RDreamer provides a significant boost in spatial diversity from a limited initial dataset, offering substantial value compared to the effort of collecting additional real demonstrations.
3. **Policy Agnosticism:** The fact that the same augmented data pipeline improved both a compact 2D visuomotor policy (DP) and a VLA-style policy ($\pi_0$) indicates that R2RDreamer produces general-purpose augmented data suitable for different policy architectures that consume RGB observations.
4. **Qualitative Validation:** Qualitative visualizations (Figure 4) illustrated that the 3D co-editing process preserved action-relevant contact geometry. The projected control views effectively masked unreliable pixels, and the video completion model generated temporally coherent RGB observations that convincingly recovered the visual details around edited objects and robot links. This supports the interpretation that performance gains are attributable to action-consistent spatial diversity rather than mere appearance perturbations.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

The ablation studies provided insights into the contributions of R2RDreamer's design choices:
- **Mask-Pair Construction:**
 - **Removing random object-drop pairs:** This led to reduced robustness, particularly to irregular missing regions resulting from segmentation errors, sparse depth, or imperfect occlusion heuristics. The model, overfit to clean geometric masks, tended to produce visual artifacts when encountering fragmented black regions.
 - **Removing projection-consistent pairs:** The model lost exposure to the large, temporally coherent holes caused by 3D motion and self-occlusion. This weakened the completion quality, especially near object boundaries and in regions that should reveal newly exposed surfaces.
- **Inference Composition and Loss Weighting:**
 - **Inference-time blending:** Blending the control video's non-black pixels with the video model's output proved crucial for preserving reliable projected control information. Without blending, the model could subtly alter pixels that should remain fixed, leading to small geometric drifts, particularly around the robot arm, object contact points, and background elements.
 - **Loss weight $\lambda$:** This parameter in the flow-matching loss balanced the preservation of control signals in non-black regions versus the realism of harmonized completions within black regions. Appropriate tuning of $\lambda$ was necessary to prevent either excessive modification of specified control pixels or the preservation of projected artifacts that would reduce visual realism.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

R2RDreamer represents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by offering a practical and scalable solution for generating spatially diverse data from limited real-world demonstrations. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Spatial Generalization:** The primary significance lies in its demonstrated ability to substantially improve the spatial generalization of manipulation policies. By augmenting a single source demonstration, R2RDreamer enables policies to perform successfully on unseen object poses and robot configurations, which is a critical requirement for robust real-world deployment.
2. **Addressing Data Scarcity:** R2RDreamer offers a viable alternative to the costly and time-consuming process of collecting extensive real-world demonstration data for spatial variations. This approach can significantly lower the data barrier for developing and deploying advanced robotic manipulation capabilities.
3. **Bridge Between 3D Geometry and 2D Perception:** The framework innovatively bridges the gap between the necessity of 3D geometric consistency for accurate action editing and the prevalent use of 2D RGB observations by modern visuomotor policies. By performing 3D editing and then shifting visual completion to 2D video generation, R2RDreamer produces data directly consumable by RGB-based 2D policies and VLAs.
4. **Reduced Reliance on Complex 3D Reconstruction:** Unlike previous real-to-real methods that demanded extensive 3D scene parsing, object geometry completion (e.g., using templates, 6-DoF tracking, or advanced 3D completion), R2RDreamer operates with incomplete 3D object pointclouds. This design choice makes the method more robust to challenges like low-quality depth data, fast motion, clutter, and especially, manipulation of articulated or deformable objects, where complete and reliable 3D geometry is difficult to obtain.
5. **Broad Applicability to Policy Architectures:** The augmented demonstrations generated by R2RDreamer are suitable for a range of policy architectures, from compact 2D visuomotor policies (e.g., Diffusion Policy) to larger Vision-Language-Action models (e.g., $\pi_0$). This versatility increases the utility and potential impact of the framework across different robotic learning paradigms.
6. **Scalability and Practicality:** By automating the generation of diverse training data and avoiding complex simulation setup or exhaustive 3D reconstruction, R2RDreamer presents a scalable and practical solution for accelerating research and development in imitation learning and robot control.

The potential impact of R2RDreamer extends to making robotic systems more robust and adaptable to varied real-world conditions. It could foster the development of more generalized policies that require less manual tuning or retraining for new spatial layouts. Future work, as noted by the authors, can build upon this foundation by improving long-horizon temporal consistency in generated videos and exploring how these augmented videos scale for training even larger multi-task policies, further expanding the capabilities of embodied AI.
