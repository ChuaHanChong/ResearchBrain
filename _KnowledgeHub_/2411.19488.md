# Interleaved-Modal Chain-of-Thought

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces Interleaved-modal Chain-of-Thought (ICoT), a method for Vision-Language Models that integrates relevant image patches directly into reasoning steps. Leveraging an Attention-driven Selection (ADS) mechanism, ICoT enhances fine-grained association between visual and textual information, leading to improved performance and interpretability on complex multimodal reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) generates text-only rationales, leading to a lack of fine-grained association with visual details.
- Textual descriptions in CoT often suffer from ambiguity, potential for misunderstanding, overgeneralization, or hallucination without explicit visual grounding.
- Current VLM architectures are not inherently designed to generate fine-grained interleaved visual and textual content as part of their reasoning process.

## Method

- The paper introduces ==Interleaved-modal Chain-of-Thought (ICoT)==, which generates sequential reasoning steps composed of paired visual (image patches) and textual rationales.
- ==Attention-driven Selection (ADS)== is proposed as a training-free, plug-and-play mechanism to enable ICoT on existing VLMs by selecting relevant image patches from the input based on attention scores.
- ADS works by caching ==visual tokens==, triggering selection upon a signal token, and then concatenating selected visual tokens with text for subsequent VLM generation, effectively grounding textual rationales visually.

## Results

- ICoT consistently outperformed all tested baselines (No-CoT, Multimodal CoT, CCoT, DDCoT, SCAFFOLD) across M3CoT, ScienceQA, and LLaVA-W benchmarks.
- The method achieved up to a **14%** ROUGE-L score improvement on the LLaVA-W benchmark, demonstrating its ability to capture and utilize fine-grained visual information for complex reasoning.
- Qualitative analyses showed ICoT's interleaved rationales resolved issues of misunderstanding, overgeneralization, and hallucination observed in text-only CoT, enhancing interpretability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Interleaving visual patches with textual rationales directly addresses issues like misunderstanding, overgeneralization, and hallucination in VLM reasoning, making the process more interpretable.
> - The Attention-driven Selection (ADS) mechanism effectively leverages existing VLM attention to select relevant visual regions for reasoning without requiring additional training or architectural changes.
> - The quality of fine-grained visual information in demonstrations is crucial for guiding VLMs to adopt the interleaved reasoning format, with human-crafted examples outperforming automatically generated ones.
