# Interleaved-Modal Chain-of-Thought

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces Interleaved-modal Chain-of-Thought (ICoT), a method for Vision-Language Models that integrates relevant image patches directly into reasoning steps. Leveraging an Attention-driven Selection (ADS) mechanism, ICoT enhances fine-grained association between visual and textual information, leading to improved performance and interpretability on complex multimodal reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) generates text-only rationales, leading to a lack of fine-grained association with visual details.
- Textual descriptions in CoT often suffer from ambiguity, potential for misunderstanding, overgeneralization, or hallucination without explicit visual grounding.
- Current VLM architectures are not inherently designed to generate fine-grained interleaved visual and textual content as part of their reasoning process.

## Method

- The paper introduces ==Interleaved-modal Chain-of-Thought (ICoT)==, which generates sequential reasoning steps composed of paired visual (image patches) and textual rationales.
- ==Attention-driven Selection (ADS)== is proposed as a training-free, plug-and-play mechanism to enable ICoT on existing VLMs by selecting relevant image patches from the input based on attention scores.
- ADS works by caching ==visual tokens==, triggering selection upon a signal token, and then concatenating selected visual tokens with text for subsequent VLM generation, effectively grounding textual rationales visually.

## Results

- ICoT consistently outperformed all tested baselines (No-CoT, Multimodal CoT, CCoT, DDCoT, SCAFFOLD) across M3CoT, ScienceQA, and LLaVA-W benchmarks.
- The method achieved up to a **14%** ROUGE-L score improvement on the LLaVA-W benchmark, demonstrating its ability to capture and utilize fine-grained visual information for complex reasoning.
- Qualitative analyses showed ICoT's interleaved rationales resolved issues of misunderstanding, overgeneralization, and hallucination observed in text-only CoT, enhancing interpretability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Interleaving visual patches with textual rationales directly addresses issues like misunderstanding, overgeneralization, and hallucination in VLM reasoning, making the process more interpretable.
> - The Attention-driven Selection (ADS) mechanism effectively leverages existing VLM attention to select relevant visual regions for reasoning without requiring additional training or architectural changes.
> - The quality of fine-grained visual information in demonstrations is crucial for guiding VLMs to adopt the interleaved reasoning format, with human-crafted examples outperforming automatically generated ones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen rapid advancements, particularly with the advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) that exhibit impressive natural language understanding and generation capabilities. A key breakthrough for LLMs has been Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, which enables models to break down complex problems into a series of intermediate reasoning steps, significantly improving performance on arithmetic, commonsense, and symbolic reasoning tasks.

However, extending these advanced reasoning abilities to multimodal contexts, specifically Vision-Language Models (VLMs) that process both images and text, presents unique challenges. Early attempts at multimodal CoT primarily focused on training language models to generate text-only rationales based on fused visual and textual embeddings. While these methods, such as those leveraging T5 or introducing triple demonstrations (image, textual rationale, answer), showed promise, they often fell short in expressing fine-grained associations with the original image. For instance, methods like DDCoT (deconstructing problems), CCoT (generating scene graphs), and SCAFFOLD (overlaying coordinate matrices) improved linguistic reasoning but still relied on textual descriptions to refer to visual elements. This limitation means that vague descriptions like "at the top" might not precisely identify specific objects or regions within an image, hindering accurate, granular reasoning.

This paper addresses a critical gap in the multimodal CoT landscape: the inability of current VLMs to natively generate *interleaved visual and textual content* as part of their reasoning process. Existing VLM architectures fall into two main categories:
1. **Perceiver-based VLMs** (e.g., Qwen2-VL, BLIP, LLaVA): These models convert images into visual embeddings that are then consumed by an LLM for text generation. While excellent for fine-grained visual *understanding*, they are not designed for generating visual outputs within the reasoning flow.
2. **Unified-modeling VLMs** (e.g., Chameleon, Unified-IO 2, Emu-3): These models aim for multimodal generation by tokenizing images into discrete visual tokens. While capable of generating images, they often exhibit "inertia" towards multimodal generation and the generated images are typically of fixed, pre-defined resolutions, not the fine granularity required for precise reasoning steps.

"Interleaved-Modal Chain-of-Thought" (ICoT) positions itself as a foundational innovation by proposing to overcome the limitations of text-only rationales. It moves beyond mere textual descriptions of visual elements to actively *incorporate actual visual information* (image patches) directly into the intermediate reasoning steps. This approach is claimed to align more closely with human cognitive processes, where visual and linguistic cues are often processed interactively during problem-solving. By developing a practical, plug-and-play strategy called Attention-driven Selection (ADS) that can be applied to *existing* VLMs, ICoT avoids the need for extensive VLM architectural redesign or retraining for multimodal generation. This work thus marks a significant step towards more interpretable, accurate, and human-like multimodal reasoning in AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind ICoT stems from a fundamental limitation observed in existing multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting methods for Vision-Language Models (VLMs). While CoT has proven highly effective in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) by breaking down complex problems into intermediate textual steps, its direct application to VLMs has primarily resulted in text-only rationales.

The authors identify several key problems arising from these text-only rationales:
1. **Lack of Fine-Grained Association:** Textual descriptions, such as "the object at the top," are often too coarse to precisely identify or refer to specific, intricate visual details within an image. This ambiguity can lead to misinterpretations or an inability to leverage crucial visual evidence.
2. **Potential for Misunderstanding, Overgeneralization, and Hallucination:** Without explicit visual grounding in the reasoning steps, VLMs might misunderstand visual cues, overgeneralize concepts based on limited textual descriptions, or even hallucinate details not present in the image. This diminishes the reliability and interpretability of their reasoning.
3. **Deviation from Human Cognition:** Human reasoning often involves a dynamic interplay between visual perception and linguistic processing. We point to specific visual elements while explaining our thought process, a capability that text-only rationales in VLMs lack.

Given these limitations, the **key objective** of this research is to propose and implement an "image-incorporated multimodal Chain-of-Thought," named **Interleaved-modal Chain-of-Thought (ICoT)**. Specifically, ICoT aims to:
- **Generate sequential reasoning steps consisting of *paired visual and textual rationales***, allowing VLMs to infer the final answer with higher precision and clarity. This means that at each intermediate step, the model would present a relevant image patch alongside a textual explanation.
- **Enhance the precision of fine-grained associations** between the generated rationales and the corresponding image by directly incorporating visual information.

A significant challenge in realizing ICoT is that current VLMs lack the inherent ability to generate such fine-grained interleaved multimodal content. Therefore, a **secondary, crucial objective** is to develop a practical and efficient mechanism to enable ICoT over *existing* VLM architectures without requiring extensive retraining or architectural modifications. This mechanism, named **Attention-driven Selection (ADS)**, aims to:
- **Intelligently select relevant regions from the input image** to serve as visual rationales rather than attempting to generate new images.
- **Be a plug-and-play strategy** that leverages VLMs' internal attention mechanisms without additional parameterization, ensuring broad applicability and minimal overhead.

Ultimately, the motivation is to significantly improve the performance and interpretability of VLMs on complex vision-language reasoning tasks by mirroring a more natural, human-like reasoning process that integrates both visual and linguistic information seamlessly.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of "Interleaved-modal Chain-of-Thought" (ICoT) revolves around transforming the traditional text-only reasoning steps of multimodal CoT into interleaved visual and textual rationales. To address the inherent limitation of current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in generating fine-grained visual content, the authors propose a clever selection-based approach called **Attention-driven Selection (ADS)**.

**ICoT Concept:**
Instead of `r1, r2..., answer = VLM(Prompt, Image, Instruction)` (text-only CoT), ICoT aims for:
`r1, xv1, r2, xv2..., answer = VLM(Prompt, Image, Instruction)`, where `xv_i` represents a fine-grained visual rationale (an image patch) extracted from the original input image. These paired visual and textual rationales are intended to provide clearer and more precise reasoning steps.

**Attention-driven Selection (ADS) Mechanism:**
The key to realizing ICoT with existing VLMs is ADS. Its basic idea is to enable the VLM to *point to and select* relevant image patches from the *input image* rather than generating new ones.

1. **Visual Token Caching:** At the beginning of the VLM's processing, its built-in visual encoder (`E`) extracts visual tokens (`f_v`) from the input image (`xv`). These visual tokens represent different patches or regions of the image and are cached for later selection.

2. **Signal for Visual Rationale:** During the VLM's autoregressive text generation process, ICoT introduces a pre-defined "signal token" (e.g., a line break `\n`). When the VLM generates this signal token, it triggers the ADS mechanism, indicating that a visual rationale is needed before the next textual rationale.

3. **Attention-Based Patch Selection:**
 - Upon detection of the signal token, ADS utilizes the VLM's internal attention mechanism. Specifically, it computes the attention map (`A_t`) between the just-generated signal token and all the cached visual tokens (`f_v`) from the input image.
 - The attention map `A_t` reflects which visual tokens the signal token is "paying most attention to."
 - ADS then selects the `n` visual tokens with the highest attention scores from `A_t`. These selected tokens (`V_selected`) represent the most relevant fine-grained visual information at that specific reasoning step.
 - Crucially, these selected visual tokens are restored to their relative positions (prioritizing rows) to maintain spatial context.

4. **Interleaved Input for Next Generation:**
 - The selected visual tokens (`V_selected`) are then concatenated with the previously generated textual tokens (converted into word embeddings). This combined multimodal input is fed back into the VLM.
 - The VLM then resumes its original autoregressive text generation, but now conditioned on this new interleaved multimodal context. This process effectively allows the VLM to generate a textual rationale directly "referring" to the inserted visual patch.

**Key Characteristics of ADS:**
- **Training-Free:** ADS does not require any additional training or fine-tuning of the VLM parameters. It leverages the VLM's existing attention mechanisms.
- **Plug-and-Play:** Its design allows it to be easily integrated with a wide range of existing VLM architectures (both perceiver-based and unified-modeling models).
- **Ignorable Latency:** Since it involves selection and insertion of existing tokens rather than generating new images, it adds minimal inference overhead compared to text-only CoT.
- **Prompting:** ICoT can be used in zero-shot settings by attaching an instruction like "Let's think step by step," or in few-shot settings with manually designed demonstrations that include interleaved images and textual rationales. The paper emphasizes the importance of these human-designed demonstrations for guiding the VLM's reasoning format.

**Experimental Setup:**
The authors applied ADS to realize ICoT on two state-of-the-art VLMs:
- **Chameleon-7B:** A unified-modeling VLM.
- **Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct:** A perceiver-based VLM.
Experiments were conducted on three benchmarks: M3CoT, ScienceQA, and LLaVA-Bench In-the-Wild (LLaVA-W), evaluating both zero-shot and one-shot scenarios. The number of selected patches (`n`) was empirically tuned, with `n=64` generally yielding optimal performance.

**Explorations and Ablations:**
The methodology also included:
- **Ablation studies:** To understand the individual contributions of ADS and fine-grained visual information (FVI) in demonstrations.
- **KV Cache Exploration:** Investigated whether copying visual information from the VLM's internal Key-Value cache (for efficiency) could replace direct input insertion. This showed performance degradation due to the position-agnostic nature of copied KV cache, indicating that re-inserting patches at the input layer is crucial for maintaining proper contextual grounding.
- **Demonstration Design Analysis:** Compared human-written demonstrations with model-written ones, finding that human-crafted examples with precise FVI are more effective in guiding the VLM.

This comprehensive methodological approach allows ICoT to provide a practical and effective solution for enabling more interpretable and accurate multimodal reasoning in VLMs, directly addressing the limitations of prior text-only CoT methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on ICoT, implemented with Attention-driven Selection (ADS) on Chameleon-7B and Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct, yielded significant and consistent improvements across various benchmarks, validating its effectiveness.

**Overall Performance Gains:**
- **Consistent Outperformance:** ICoT consistently surpassed all tested baselines, including No-CoT (direct generation), Multimodal CoT (text-only), CCoT (Scene Graphs), DDCoT (problem deconstruction), and SCAFFOLD (coordinate overlays), in both zero-shot and one-shot settings.
- **Substantial Gains on LLaVA-W:** ICoT achieved the most significant relative performance improvements on the LLaVA-W benchmark, with gains of up to 14% (ROUGE-L score) compared to the best baseline. This is particularly noteworthy as LLaVA-W's reference labels often contain detailed descriptions sourced from images, highlighting ICoT's ability to capture and utilize fine-grained visual information.
- **Improvements on M3CoT and ScienceQA:** While the percentage improvements were slightly lower than LLaVA-W, ICoT still demonstrated clear advantages on M3CoT (accuracy) and ScienceQA (accuracy), indicating its broad applicability across different reasoning domains (science, mathematics, commonsense).

**Interpretability Enhancement:**
- The authors qualitatively demonstrated through case studies (Figure 3) that ICoT's interleaved-modal rationales directly address common issues observed in text-only CoT, such as:
 - **Misunderstanding:** ICoT accurately identified distinct objects (e.g., inflatable castle, crayons, parachute) and their common properties, while text-only CoT misidentified them as "colored pencils."
 - **Overgeneralization:** ICoT correctly inferred "a windy day" from "few kites flying," avoiding the overgeneralization to "a kite festival" seen in text-only CoT.
 - **Hallucination:** By providing visual cues (street signs, troll statue patches), ICoT prevented the VLM from hallucinating details (e.g., "Scandinavian folklore" leading to "local legend") and guided it to the correct inference about a tourist attraction.
- This visual grounding in reasoning steps makes the VLM's thought process much more transparent and verifiable.

**Ablation Study Insights (on Chameleon-7B, 1-shot):**
- **Importance of ADS (Interleaved Rationales):** Removing ADS (`w/o ADS`), which reverts ICoT to text-only rationales, resulted in substantial performance degradation across all benchmarks (e.g., -3.1% on M3CoT ACC, -3.1% on LLaVA-W ROUGE-L). This firmly established that generating paired visual and text rationales is a key driver of ICoT's improved performance.
- **Importance of Fine-Grained Visual Information (FVI) in Demonstrations:** When demonstrations used randomly sampled patches instead of carefully selected fine-grained ones (`w/o FVI`), performance also dropped (e.g., -1.8% on M3CoT ACC, -1.7% on LLaVA-W ROUGE-L). This highlights that the quality of visual information in the few-shot examples significantly guides the VLM in adopting the ICoT reasoning format.
- **Combined Impact:** The largest degradation occurred when both ADS and FVI in demonstrations were removed (`w/o ADS+FVI`), confirming their synergistic effect.

**Analysis on Realizing ICoT (KV Cache vs. Input Insertion):**
- **Performance Trade-off:** Copying visual information from the VLM's internal Key-Value (KV) cache for efficiency resulted in slight performance degradation compared to directly inserting selected patches at the input end.
- **Reason for Degradation:** The authors attributed this to the "position-agnostic" nature of KV cache copies. While KV-copy makes the VLM attend to the same regions, the precise positional context is potentially lost or diluted once fused into the KV cache, making direct input insertion superior for maintaining fine-grained spatial relationships vital for ICoT.

**Analysis on Demonstration Design:**
- **Human-written vs. Model-written:** Human-written demonstrations (with manually selected FVI and rationales) consistently outperformed automatically model-generated ones. This suggests that precise, human-curated examples are crucial for effectively guiding the VLM's interleaved reasoning process, likely because automatically generated visual patches might introduce noise.

**Analysis on Number of Selected Patches (`n`):**
- Empirical studies showed that selecting an optimal number of patches (`n=64` in their setup) was crucial. Too few patches might miss critical information, while too many could introduce noise or irrelevant details, leading to dispersed attention and higher computational costs.

In summary, the main findings strongly support the hypothesis that incorporating interleaved visual and textual rationales significantly enhances VLM reasoning. The proposed ADS method provides a practical and efficient way to achieve this, leading to notable performance gains and improved interpretability across diverse vision-language tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Interleaved-Modal Chain-of-Thought" (ICoT) represents a significant advancement in multimodal AI, particularly for Vision-Language Models (VLMs), with profound implications for both current research and future applications.

**Core Innovation and Foundational Contribution:**
- **Bridging the Modality Gap in CoT:** ICoT is the first multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) method to actively incorporate images directly into the intermediate reasoning steps, moving beyond the prevalent text-only rationales. This is a foundational innovation, as it acknowledges and addresses the inherent visual nature of many multimodal reasoning tasks.
- **Aligning with Human Cognition:** By generating paired visual and textual rationales, ICoT more closely mimics human thought processes, where visual cues and linguistic explanations are intrinsically linked during problem-solving. This makes the AI's reasoning not only more effective but also more intuitive and understandable for human users.

**Practicality and Broad Applicability:**
- **Attention-driven Selection (ADS):** The proposed ADS strategy is a game-changer because it allows ICoT to be implemented on *existing* VLM architectures without demanding extensive re-training or architectural modifications. Its training-free and plug-and-play nature makes it highly accessible and adaptable to a wide range of state-of-the-art VLMs (both perceiver-based and unified-modeling models).
- **Efficiency:** ADS's reliance on selection rather than generation ensures minimal additional inference latency, making ICoT practical for real-world deployments where speed is critical.

**Demonstrated Performance and Interpretability Gains:**
- **Substantial Performance Improvement:** The empirical results, showing up to 14% performance gains on benchmarks requiring fine-grained visual reasoning (LLaVA-W), unequivocally demonstrate ICoT's superior capability. This suggests that for complex vision-language tasks, precisely grounding reasoning steps in visual evidence is highly beneficial.
- **Enhanced Interpretability:** The explicit display of visual patches alongside textual rationales significantly improves the interpretability of VLM reasoning. Users can directly see *what* the model is looking at and *why* it's drawing a particular conclusion, fostering trust and enabling easier debugging of errors. This is crucial for applications in sensitive domains like healthcare or autonomous systems.

**Potential Impact and Future Directions:**
The success of ICoT has several potential impacts:
- **More Capable VLMs:** It paves the way for VLMs that can tackle increasingly complex, multi-step visual reasoning challenges with higher accuracy and robustness. This could lead to breakthroughs in areas such as:
 - **Advanced Visual Question Answering:** Handling intricate questions requiring deep contextual and compositional understanding of images.
 - **Medical Imaging Analysis:** Aiding diagnosis by allowing models to point to specific regions in medical scans and explain their findings.
 - **Robotics and Embodied AI:** Enabling robots to better understand their visual environment and justify their actions.
 - **Scientific Discovery:** Interpreting complex diagrams, charts, and images in scientific papers for automated knowledge extraction.
- **New Research Avenues:** The paper itself highlights several avenues for future research, including:
 - **Optimizing Patch Selection:** Developing more dynamic and adaptive methods for selecting visual patches (e.g., using segmentation or grounding techniques) rather than a fixed number, to reduce noise and optimize information content.
 - **Memory Efficiency:** Addressing the memory overhead associated with storing attention scores for patch selection.
 - **Generalization:** Evaluating ICoT on a broader array of VLM backbones and benchmarks to further assess its universality.
 - **Automatic Demonstration Generation:** Improving the quality of automatically generated interleaved demonstrations to potentially reduce reliance on human curation.

In conclusion, ICoT is a pivotal step towards developing more intelligent, transparent, and human-aligned multimodal AI systems. By effectively integrating visual information directly into the reasoning chain, it unlocks new possibilities for VLMs to perform complex tasks that require a deep, fine-grained understanding of the interplay between language and vision.
