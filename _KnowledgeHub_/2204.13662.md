# ARCTIC: A Dataset for Dexterous Bimanual Hand-Object Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The ARCTIC dataset provides 2.1 million frames of dexterous bimanual manipulation involving articulated objects, offering synchronized multi-view RGB video and high-quality 3D ground truth for hands, full human bodies, and object meshes, including dynamic contact information. This resource enables the study of physically consistent hand-object dynamics and supports novel tasks like consistent motion reconstruction and interaction field estimation.

## Problem

- A scarcity of high-quality datasets providing comprehensive 3D ground truth for complex human-object interactions, particularly involving dexterous bimanual manipulation.
- Existing datasets primarily focus on single-hand grasping of rigid objects or lack synchronized multi-view RGB data with precise 3D annotations for articulated objects.
- The absence of data capturing physically consistent and synchronized motion between hands and articulated objects hinders the development of AI systems that intuitively understand how human actions cause object state changes.

## Method

- Created ARCTIC, a large-scale dataset featuring 339 sequences of dexterous bimanual manipulation with 10 subjects interacting with 11 articulated objects, totaling 2.1 million RGB images.
- Employed a 54-camera Vicon MoCap system synchronized with multi-view RGB cameras to capture high-accuracy 3D ground truth for human body (SMPL-X), hands (MANO), articulated object meshes, and dynamic contact information.
- Developed two baseline deep learning models, ArcticNet and InterField, to demonstrate capabilities for consistent 3D hand-object motion reconstruction and dense interaction field estimation from monocular video, respectively.

## Results

- ARCTIC is presented as the first dataset combining dexterous bimanual articulated object manipulation with multi-view RGB data and accurate 3D ground truth for hands, full human bodies, and objects, alongside dynamic contact information.
- Temporal baseline models (ArcticNet-LSTM and InterField-LSTM) consistently outperform their single-frame counterparts, showing improved contact consistency (e.g., 38.9mm vs 41.6mm CDev for ArcticNet-LSTM vs SF) and smoother motion predictions.
- Training with the Contact Deviation loss reduced contact error by approximately 7mm, and models pre-trained on ARCTIC generalize to improve performance on rigid object manipulation datasets by up to 9.2%.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Temporal modeling (e.g., LSTMs) significantly improves accuracy and spatio-temporal consistency for both 3D motion reconstruction and interaction field estimation from monocular video.
> - Dexterous bimanual manipulation leads to substantially greater hand pose diversity and more varied contact patterns across the hand compared to single-hand grasping datasets.
> - High-fidelity MoCap systems are critical for acquiring accurate 3D ground truth in complex, highly occluded dexterous manipulation scenarios that commodity sensors struggle with.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of computer vision and robotics is rapidly advancing towards enabling machines to understand and interact with the physical world in a human-like manner. A crucial aspect of this is comprehending human manipulation of objects. However, this area has been significantly hampered by a lack of high-quality, comprehensive datasets that accurately capture the complexities of real-world interactions.

Existing datasets for human-object interaction largely fall into a few categories:
- **Human body with rigid objects:** Datasets like BEHAVE and InterCap capture full-body interaction but often with larger objects, or use RGB-D setups which can be less accurate than MoCap. They generally lack dexterous hand-object detail or bimanual interactions.
- **Single hand with rigid objects:** The majority of hand-object datasets, such as HO3D and DexYCB, focus on single-hand grasping of rigid objects. Hand poses in these datasets are often relatively static or involve limited movement variations.
- **Two hands with rigid objects:** Datasets like H2O and H2O-3D address bimanual interaction, but they are still limited to rigid objects and often feature controlled or less dexterous motions.
- **Articulated objects but limited interaction:** HOI4D is notable for including articulated objects, but it focuses on single-hand interaction from an egocentric view, which introduces significant occlusion challenges and ambiguity for 3D reconstruction. GRAB provides detailed whole-body human grasping of rigid objects captured with MoCap, but it **lacks corresponding RGB images**, which are essential for training vision-based models.

**ARCTIC fills a critical gap by providing the first large-scale dataset that simultaneously addresses several key challenges:**
1. **Dexterous Manipulation:** It moves beyond simple grasping to capture dynamic, unconstrained, and intricate manipulation sequences, where hands constantly adjust their poses and apply forces.
2. **Bimanual Interaction:** It includes scenarios where both hands are involved in manipulating an object, which is common in everyday tasks (e.g., opening a laptop, using scissors).
3. **Articulated Objects:** Crucially, ARCTIC focuses on objects with movable parts (e.g., laptops, scissors, espresso machines), where the object's state changes in sync with hand movements. This introduces the challenge of physically consistent motion between hands and object parts.
4. **High-Quality 3D Ground Truth:** Unlike many datasets relying on RGB-D sensors or manual annotations, ARCTIC uses a sophisticated 54-camera Vicon MoCap system to achieve highly accurate 3D hand and object meshes, along with full human body pose (SMPL-X), minimizing issues of occlusion and depth ambiguity inherent in commodity sensors.
5. **Multi-View RGB Video:** It provides synchronized multi-view (8 allocentric, 1 egocentric) high-resolution RGB frames, essential for training robust vision-based models.
6. **Detailed Contact Information:** It includes dynamic contact information between hands and objects, crucial for understanding physical interactions.

By providing this unprecedented level of detail and complexity, ARCTIC enables the study of physically consistent dynamics of hands and objects during manipulation, a research area that has been under-explored due to data limitations. It offers a stepping stone for developing more intelligent machines that can intuitively understand and predict the physical consequences of human actions on articulated objects. This work directly supports advancements in computer vision, robotics (especially for dexterous manipulation), augmented reality, and human-computer interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind the ARCTIC dataset stems from a fundamental limitation in current machine intelligence: the inability of artificial systems to intuitively understand that inanimate objects do not move independently, but rather their state changes are typically caused by human manipulation. This gap is largely attributed to the lack of comprehensive datasets that provide ground-truth 3D annotations for studying physically consistent and synchronized motion of hands and articulated objects.

Given this deficiency, the paper outlines several key objectives:

1. **Primary Objective: Introduce ARCTIC Dataset:** The paramount objective is to present ARCTIC, a novel, large-scale dataset designed specifically for studying dexterous bimanual hand-object manipulation involving articulated objects. This dataset aims to fill the existing void by providing 2.1 million video frames paired with accurate 3D hand and object meshes, and detailed dynamic contact information. The focus is on capturing scenarios where hand poses and object states evolve jointly in time.

2. **Enable Novel Research Tasks:** The dataset is specifically designed to facilitate research into two novel, challenging tasks, addressing limitations of prior work primarily focused on rigid object grasping:
 - **Consistent Motion Reconstruction:** The goal is to reconstruct the 3D motion of two hands and an articulated object from a monocular video. A critical aspect of this task is ensuring that the reconstructed hand-object meshes demonstrate spatio-temporal consistency, including accurate hand-object contact, realistic object articulation, and smooth motion throughout the interaction. This task directly challenges models to overcome depth ambiguity and severe occlusions common in dexterous manipulation.
 - **Interaction Field Estimation:** This task aims to move beyond binary contact detection by requiring the estimation of dense relative hand-object distances from images. For every vertex on a hand mesh, the goal is to predict its shortest distance to the object mesh, and vice versa. This provides a richer understanding of the dynamic spatial configuration between hands and objects, even when not in direct contact.

3. **Provide Baseline Models:** As an initial step and to encourage future research, the authors aim to introduce and evaluate baseline models for the two proposed tasks. For consistent motion reconstruction, they propose ArcticNet (with single-frame and temporal/LSTM variations), and for interaction field estimation, they propose InterField (also with single-frame and temporal/LSTM variations). These baselines serve as initial benchmarks for future comparative studies.

In essence, the motivation is to provide the necessary high-fidelity, complex interaction data for the research community to develop advanced machine learning models that can truly understand and emulate the intricate dynamics of human manipulation of articulated objects.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work lies in the meticulous data acquisition and annotation pipeline for the ARCTIC dataset, followed by the design and evaluation of baseline models for the proposed tasks.

#### 3.1. Data Acquisition and Annotation (ARCTIC Dataset)

The ARCTIC dataset comprises 339 sequences of dexterous manipulation, featuring 10 subjects interacting with 11 articulated objects, totaling 2.1 million RGB images. The acquisition setup is designed to capture high-quality 3D data alongside synchronized multi-view RGB video.

- **Capture System:** A high-end Vicon MoCap system with 54 infrared Vantage-16 cameras is used to ensure extreme accuracy and minimize occlusion issues common in dexterous hand manipulation. This is synchronized with 8 static allocentric RGB cameras and 1 moving egocentric RGB camera, capturing high-resolution images.
- **Marker Placement:** Small (1.5mm radius) hemispherical MoCap markers are strategically placed on the dorsal side of hands and objects to avoid interfering with natural manipulation and to remain minimally visible in RGB images. Larger markers (4.5mm radius) are used for full-body tracking.
- **Personalized Geometry & Object Models:**
 - **Human:** 3D scans of subjects in varying poses (using a 3dMD scanner) are used to create personalized SMPL-X body and hand template meshes. SMPL-X is fitted to these scans to ensure an accurate canonical geometry for each subject.
 - **Objects:** Each of the 11 articulated objects is 3D-scanned (using an Artec 3D scanner) in a predefined rest pose. Each object is then manually separated into two rigid parts that articulate around a shared axis (e.g., laptop cover and base).
- **Rotation Axis Estimation:** For each articulated object, a precise 1D rotation axis is estimated from calibration MoCap sequences, where the object's parts are articulated. This involves tracking markers on both parts, fitting circles to their trajectories, and then refining a 3D line (the rotation axis) through the circle centers by minimizing reconstruction error.
- **3D Pose Solving:**
 - **Human (Full Body & Hands):** SMPL-X is optimized to fit the observed MoCap markers, providing realistic full-body and hand pose and shape estimates for each frame. This leverages established methods like MoSh++.
 - **Articulated Objects:** The 6D rigid pose (rotation and translation) of the object's base part is solved by computing the transformation from its canonical marker positions to the observed MoCap marker positions. The 1D articulation angle (relative to the rest pose) is computed based on the estimated rotation axis and marker projections.
- **Contact Information:** Ground-truth binary contact labels are computed at the vertex level for hands and object parts using a proximity-based approach similar to GRAB, considering both "under-shooting" (close proximity) and "over-shooting" (interpenetration).

#### 3.2. Tasks and Baseline Models

The paper proposes two novel tasks and provides deep learning baselines for each:

- **1. Consistent Motion Reconstruction:**
 - **Problem Formulation:** Given a monocular RGB video, reconstruct 3D meshes for two hands (using MANO) and an articulated object (using the custom 2-part model) for every frame. The reconstruction must maintain spatio-temporal consistency in hand-object contact, object articulation, and motion smoothness.
 - **ArcticNet Baseline:** An encoder-decoder architecture is employed, with a ResNet-50 CNN encoder extracting image features.
 - **ArcticNet-SF (Single-Frame):** Image features are fed into separate hand (MANO pose and shape, translation) and object (articulation, rotation, translation) decoders. An iterative refinement scheme is used for parameter prediction.
 - **ArcticNet-LSTM (Temporal):** Extends ArcticNet-SF by using a bidirectional LSTM network to aggregate image features from an 11-frame temporal window, enabling joint reasoning about hand and articulated object motions over time.
 - **Loss Functions:** A combination of losses is used: 3D joint/landmark regression, 2D keypoint re-projection, MANO/object parameter supervision, and a Contact Deviation (CDev) loss to explicitly encourage accurate hand-object contact.

- **2. Interaction Field Estimation:**
 - **Problem Formulation:** For each image in a video, estimate a dense "interaction field" representing the shortest distance from every vertex of a hand mesh to the object mesh, and vice versa. This provides a continuous measure of proximity, not just binary contact.
 - **InterField Baseline:** Also uses a ResNet-50 CNN backbone.
 - **InterField-SF (Single-Frame):** Image features are concatenated with subsampled 3D hand/object vertex locations (in canonical pose). These augmented points are fed into a PointNet backbone, followed by regression heads that predict the distance values for each vertex. Distances are then upsampled to the full mesh.
 - **InterField-LSTM (Temporal):** Similar to ArcticNet-LSTM, it incorporates an LSTM to process image features across an 11-frame window before the PointNet and regression, allowing for temporal consistency in distance field predictions.
 - **Loss Function:** An L1 loss is used to supervise the predicted distance fields against the ground-truth distances (thresholded at 10cm for focus on close interaction).

#### 3.3. Evaluation Protocol

- **Data Split:** A subject-wise split is used: 8 subjects for training, 1 for validation (male), and 1 for testing (female), ensuring gender balance in evaluation.
- **Protocols:**
 - **Allocentric (Allo):** Models are trained and evaluated using images from the 8 static allocentric (3rd-person) views.
 - **Egocentric (Ego):** Models are trained using all 9 views (allocentric + egocentric) but evaluated only on the single moving egocentric (1st-person) view, simulating real-world egocentric perception.
- **Metrics:** A comprehensive set of metrics is defined for evaluation:
 - **Consistent Motion Reconstruction:**
 - `Contact Deviation (CDev)`: Average distance between predicted in-contact hand and object vertices (measures contact accuracy).
 - `Motion Deviation (MDev)`: Disagreement in moving direction for stable in-contact vertices across consecutive frames (measures spatio-temporal consistency of contact motion).
 - `Acceleration Error (ACC)`: Measures smoothness of reconstructed hand and object motion.
 - `Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE)`: L2 distance between predicted and ground-truth hand joints.
 - `Average Articulation Error (AAE)`: Absolute error in predicted object articulation angle.
 - `Success Rate`: Percentage of object vertices reconstructed within a small L2 error tolerance (5% of object diameter).
 - `Mean Relative-Root Position Error (MRRPE)`: Measures relative translation accuracy between hands and between hand and object.
 - **Interaction Field Estimation:**
 - `Average Distance Error`: L1 error between predicted and ground-truth distance fields.
 - `Acceleration Error (ACC)`: Smoothness of the predicted distance fields over time.

This detailed methodology ensures high data quality, robust baselines, and a thorough evaluation framework for the complex problem of dexterous bimanual articulated hand-object manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents several significant findings that underscore the value of the ARCTIC dataset and the effectiveness of the proposed baselines.

#### 4.1. Dataset Characteristics and Uniqueness

- **Comprehensive Coverage:** ARCTIC is established as the *first* dataset that combines dexterous bimanual manipulation of *articulated objects* with multi-view RGB images and accurate 3D ground-truth (for hands, full human body, and objects), including dynamic contact information.
- **High Pose and Contact Diversity:** A key finding demonstrated through T-SNE clustering (Fig. 2a) is that ARCTIC exhibits significantly *larger hand pose diversity* compared to existing hand-object datasets (e.g., HO3D, DexYCB, H2O, HOI4D). This is attributed to the unconstrained and dynamic nature of the bimanual manipulation captured.
- **Broader Contact Patterns:** Heatmaps of frequently contacted regions (Fig. 2b,c) show that ARCTIC has *higher contact diversity* on the hands (more "spread out" heatmaps, including palm regions) compared to grasping-focused datasets like HO3D and GRAB. This indicates that dexterous manipulation involves more varied contact points.
- **Superior Capture Quality:** The use of a 54-camera Vicon MoCap system is crucial for achieving high-quality 3D annotations, especially in scenarios with heavy occlusion during fast, dexterous movements, which commodity RGB-D cameras struggle with.

#### 4.2. Consistent Motion Reconstruction (ArcticNet)

The evaluation of ArcticNet-SF and ArcticNet-LSTM for 3D hand and articulated object reconstruction yields important results:

- **Temporal Modeling is Crucial:** ArcticNet-LSTM, which incorporates an LSTM for temporal feature aggregation, consistently *outperforms* ArcticNet-SF (single-frame model) across key metrics (Table 2).
 - **Improved Contact Consistency:** ArcticNet-LSTM shows lower Contact Deviation (CDev) (e.g., 38.9mm vs. 41.6mm on Allo. Test), indicating more accurate hand-object contact predictions.
 - **Better Motion Consistency:** It achieves lower Motion Deviation (MDev) (e.g., 9.3mm vs. 10.4mm on Allo. Test), meaning that in-contact hand and object vertices move more coherently over time.
 - **Smoother Motion:** The Acceleration Error (ACC) is significantly lower for ArcticNet-LSTM (e.g., 5.0/6.1 m/s² vs. 5.7/7.6 m/s² for hands/objects on Allo. Test), demonstrating smoother and more physically plausible motion reconstructions.
- **Overall Reconstruction Quality:** While MPJPE for hands and AAE for articulation angles are similar, the improvements in CDev, MDev, and ACC highlight the LSTM's ability to enforce spatio-temporal consistency.
- **Egocentric vs. Allocentric:** Reconstruction from egocentric views proves more challenging, as reflected by higher errors (e.g., CDev for Ego. Test ArcticNet-LSTM is 43.3mm compared to 38.9mm for Allo. Test). This is expected due to severe occlusions and limited viewpoints in egocentric settings.
- **Impact of CDev Loss:** An ablation study shows that including the Contact Deviation (CDev) metric as a training loss significantly improves contact accuracy (Table 8), reducing CDev by approximately 7mm.
- **View Count Impact:** Increasing the number of allocentric training views (from 2 to 8) monotonically improves hand MPJPE and object success rate, emphasizing the benefit of diverse viewpoints for complex 3D reconstruction.

#### 4.3. Interaction Field Estimation (InterField)

The evaluation of InterField-SF and InterField-LSTM for dense hand-object distance field estimation also highlights the benefits of temporal modeling:

- **Temporal Smoothing:** InterField-LSTM consistently achieves *lower Average Distance Error* and *lower Acceleration Error* (Table 3) compared to InterField-SF. For instance, on Allo. Test, the average distance error for hand-to-object/object-to-hand drops from 9.0/10.0mm (SF) to 8.7/9.1mm (LSTM), and ACC reduces from 2.7/2.7 m/s² (SF) to 1.9/1.9 m/s² (LSTM).
- **Feasibility of New Task:** Qualitative results (Fig. 5b, Fig. 9 in SupMat) demonstrate that the InterField baselines can effectively estimate dense interaction fields, providing a richer understanding of hand-object proximity than binary contact.
- **Generalizability to Rigid Objects:** Pre-training ArcticNet on ARCTIC and then finetuning on HO3D (a rigid object dataset) results in a 9.2% improvement in hand MPJPE and 7.1% improvement in object vertex-to-vertex error. This finding suggests that the diverse and dexterous bimanual interactions in ARCTIC can *generalize and benefit* reconstruction tasks involving simpler, rigid objects.

In summary, the main findings confirm that ARCTIC successfully addresses the data gap for complex human-object manipulation. The baseline results clearly indicate that temporal modeling is critical for achieving physically consistent and smooth 3D reconstructions and accurate interaction field estimations in such dynamic scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of ARCTIC marks a significant stride in the field of computer vision and robotics, with profound implications across several research avenues.

1. **Enabling Research into Physically Consistent Dynamics:** ARCTIC is the first dataset to provide high-quality, synchronized 3D ground truth for two hands and articulated objects undergoing dexterous manipulation, coupled with multi-view RGB video. This unique combination directly addresses the critical need for data that can support the learning of physically consistent interactions. Researchers can now train models to not only estimate hand and object poses but also to understand and predict how forces applied by hands affect the articulation and movement of complex objects, a capability largely missing in current AI systems. This is fundamental for machines to develop a more intuitive understanding of the physical world.

2. **Advancing Robust 3D Reconstruction under Challenging Conditions:** The dataset's focus on dexterous bimanual manipulation inevitably leads to severe occlusions (hand-hand, hand-object, self-occlusion) and high variability in hand poses. The high-fidelity MoCap data provides the ground truth necessary to develop and benchmark robust 3D reconstruction algorithms that can handle these complex, real-world scenarios, surpassing the limitations of prior datasets that focused on simpler grasping or rigid objects. The multi-view RGB data also encourages research into fusion methods for improved accuracy.

3. **Fueling Generative Models for Complex Manipulation:** Currently, many generative models for human-object interaction are limited to static grasps or simple movements of rigid objects. ARCTIC's rich, dynamic sequences of "use" actions (e.g., cutting with scissors, opening a laptop) provide an unprecedented resource for training models that can synthesize realistic and physically plausible dexterous bimanual manipulation motions with articulated objects. This could lead to breakthroughs in animation, virtual reality, and robotics for task planning and execution. The inclusion of SMPL-X ground truth also opens doors for generating full-body interaction sequences.

4. **Novel Task Definitions and Benchmarks:** By formally defining and providing baselines for "consistent motion reconstruction" and "interaction field estimation," the paper pushes the boundaries of current research.
 - **Consistent Motion Reconstruction** emphasizes spatio-temporal coherence and contact accuracy, prompting the development of models that integrate physics and temporal dynamics into their predictions.
 - **Interaction Field Estimation** moves beyond coarse binary contact, offering a dense, continuous representation of hand-object proximity. This richer understanding of spatial relationships can inform more nuanced control in robotics and more precise contact modeling in graphics.

5. **Direct Impact on Robotics:** For robots to perform complex manipulation tasks in unstructured environments, they need to understand human demonstrations and generalize those skills. ARCTIC provides invaluable data for learning dexterous policies, enabling robots to handle articulated objects with human-like proficiency. The detailed contact information and motion dynamics are critical for developing robust robotic grasping and manipulation strategies.

6. **Progress in Augmented/Virtual Reality and Human-Computer Interaction:** More accurate and physically consistent reconstruction of hand-object interactions can significantly enhance the realism and intuitiveness of AR/VR experiences. It can also lead to more sophisticated human-computer interfaces that better interpret user intent during complex tasks involving physical objects.

7. **Open Science Contribution:** Making the dataset, code, and baselines publicly available fosters reproducibility and accelerates research progress within the community. The paper transparently discusses its limitations (e.g., 1 DoF objects, known object models, marker visibility) which serves as a guide for future extensions and improvements, rather than a detractor.

In conclusion, ARCTIC is not just another dataset; it's a foundational resource that enables a new generation of research into understanding and synthesizing the complex, physically consistent dynamics of human interaction with articulated objects. Its high quality and novel scope are set to drive significant advancements in computer vision, robotics, and human-centric AI.
