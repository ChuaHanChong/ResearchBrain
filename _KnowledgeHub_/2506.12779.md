# From Experts to a Generalist: Toward General Whole-Body Control for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called BumbleBee (BB) enables robust, agile, and generalizable whole-body control for humanoid robots by clustering diverse human motions, training specialized expert policies with motion-specific sim-to-real adaptation, and distilling knowledge into a Transformer-based generalist. This approach achieves superior performance against state-of-the-art methods in simulation and demonstrates stable, agile real-world motion on a Unitree G1 robot.

## Problem

- Training a single, unified policy for diverse humanoid motions is challenging due to conflicting control requirements and mismatched data distributions, leading to performance degradation.
- Achieving broad generalization for humanoid robot skills beyond motion-specific policies remains elusive, limiting versatility in complex environments.
- Existing sim-to-real transfer techniques often fall short when applied across highly varied motion types, as the simulation-reality gap itself can be motion-dependent.

## Method

- A self-supervised autoencoder clusters a large human motion dataset into behaviorally similar groups using kinematic features (including foot velocities) and textual annotations.
- Specialized 'expert' policies are trained for each motion cluster, iteratively refined with cluster-specific delta action models that learn to compensate for sim-to-real discrepancies using real robot data.
- Knowledge from these multiple optimized expert policies is distilled into a single, robust generalist policy using a Transformer-based architecture and the DAgger algorithm.

## Results

- The BumbleBee framework achieves state-of-the-art generalist performance, reaching a 66.84% success rate in MuJoCo simulation, significantly outperforming baselines which fall below 51%.
- Autoencoder-based clustering is crucial, yielding a 66.84% success rate compared to 35.36% for randomly partitioned data, confirming its role in reducing cross-task interference.
- Iterative, cluster-specific delta action fine-tuning significantly improves expert success rates from 51.49% to 70.37% and visibly enhances stability and smooth motion tracking on a real Unitree G1 robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing diverse motion control into specialized expert learning through meaningful semantic and kinematic clustering effectively mitigates data conflicts and improves training efficiency.
> - Sim-to-real transfer is significantly enhanced by applying cluster-specific delta action models, accounting for varying dynamic features and simulator discrepancies across different motion types.
> - A final distilled generalist policy, utilizing a Transformer architecture, can consolidate diverse expert knowledge and occasionally outperform individual experts by leveraging stable control behaviors learned across various skills.
