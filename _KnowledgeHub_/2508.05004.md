# R-Zero: Self-Evolving Reasoning LLM from Zero Data

> [!summary] Summary
> Tencent AI Seattle Lab researchers developed R-Zero, a framework enabling Large Language Models (LLMs) to self-evolve reasoning capabilities from zero external data using a co-evolutionary Challenger-Solver loop. The method consistently improved mathematical and general reasoning performance across various LLM backbones, achieving gains such as +6.49 points on average for Qwen3-4B-Base after three iterations, and enhanced performance when combined with supervised fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) rely heavily on vast quantities of human-curated data, which is costly, labor-intensive, and presents a scalability bottleneck.
- Reliance on human data inherently limits AI systems from developing capabilities beyond human intelligence, capping the potential for artificial superintelligence.
- Existing label-free or self-challenging methods often still depend on pre-existing task corpuses or external verification environments, lacking full autonomy.

## Method

- R-Zero utilizes a ==co-evolutionary loop== between two independently optimized LLMs, a ==Challenger== and a ==Solver==, both initialized from the same base model.
- The Challenger is trained via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with an ==uncertainty-based reward function== to generate challenging yet solvable questions at the Solver's capability frontier, while also applying a repetition penalty.
- The Solver is fine-tuned on a curated dataset of pseudo-labeled question-answer pairs, filtered to include only problems where the Solver's empirical correctness falls within an informative ==difficulty band== (e.g., 30-70% accuracy).

## Results

- R-Zero consistently improved performance across diverse LLM backbones on mathematical reasoning benchmarks (e.g., Qwen3-4B-Base gained **+6.49 points**) and generalized these gains to cross-domain general reasoning tasks (e.g., Qwen3-8B-Base gained **+3.81 points**).
- Ablation studies confirmed the criticality of the RL-trained Challenger, repetition penalty, and task filtering for performance, with removing the RL-Challenger causing the largest drop.
- The framework eventually experiences performance degradation or 'model collapse' after multiple iterations, though larger models exhibit greater resilience and delay the onset of this decline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-evolving LLMs can effectively learn and improve reasoning abilities by autonomously generating their own curriculum, demonstrating a path to overcome the human data bottleneck.
> - Training is most efficient when tasks are at the frontier of the learner's capabilities (approximately 50% accuracy), which the Challenger successfully targets with its uncertainty reward.
> - Maintaining distinct Challenger and Solver models with separate parameters is crucial for robust co-evolution, as a unified model suffers from internal bias and overconfidence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of self-evolving Large Language Models (LLMs) is a critical frontier in AI, promising a scalable pathway toward artificial superintelligence by enabling models to autonomously generate, refine, and learn from their own experiences. However, current methodologies for training such models largely depend on vast quantities of human-curated tasks and labels, typically through fine-tuning or reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). This reliance poses a fundamental bottleneck, limiting AI systems from advancing capabilities beyond human intelligence and incurring significant costs in terms of labor and scalability.

Existing efforts to reduce this human dependence fall into several categories:
- **Label-Free Reinforcement Learning**: These methods remove the need for explicit human labels by deriving reward signals directly from the model's own outputs, such as sequence-level confidence scores, output entropy, or consistency across multiple reasoning paths. While innovative, these approaches still require a *pre-existing corpus of tasks*, restricting their scalability in truly self-evolving settings.
- **Self-Challenging Approaches**: These methods train LLMs on tasks generated by the models themselves. While promising, many of these methods often rely on *external code executors or explicit verification oracles* to ensure the feasibility and verifiability of synthesized tasks. This reliance becomes a significant challenge in domains lacking such an oracle, like open-ended reasoning.
- **Self-Play Paradigms**: Inspired by successes in verifiable domains like code generation (e.g., Coder-Tester agents), self-play has been adapted for LLMs. More advanced frameworks aim to generate problems from scratch or a small seed.

R-Zero distinguishes itself from this landscape by introducing a **fully autonomous framework that generates its own training data from scratch, operating from *zero external data***. Unlike label-free methods that still need a task corpus, or self-challenging methods that often rely on external verifiers, R-Zero proposes a novel co-evolutionary loop between two independent models—a Challenger and a Solver—initialized from a single base LLM. This allows R-Zero to construct a targeted, self-improving curriculum for reasoning without *any* pre-existing tasks or human labels, addressing the "zero data" challenge head-on. By extending the self-play paradigm to general reasoning domains that traditionally lack explicit verification environments, R-Zero attempts to push the boundaries of LLM autonomy and self-improvement. The work also indirectly addresses the concerns around "model collapse" in self-consuming training loops, a known limitation when models are exclusively trained on recursively generated data, by analyzing its onset and contributing factors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of R-Zero is to develop a **fully autonomous framework capable of training reasoning LLMs from scratch, without any reliance on human-curated tasks or labels**. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Overcome Data Bottlenecks**: The exorbitant cost, labor intensity, and scalability issues associated with creating vast human-annotated datasets for LLM training pose a fundamental limitation to AI advancement. R-Zero seeks to eliminate this dependency, offering a path to self-sufficiency.
2. **Advance Towards Superintelligence**: By removing human data as a bottleneck, R-Zero aims to unlock the potential for AI systems to evolve capabilities that can eventually surpass human intelligence, as human-curated data inherently limits models to human-level understanding and reasoning.
3. **Create a Self-Improving Curriculum**: Existing methods either rely on static task sets or external verifiers for self-generation. R-Zero's objective is to autonomously generate a dynamic, self-improving curriculum where the difficulty of tasks progressively adapts to the model's evolving capabilities, driving continuous learning.
4. **Extend Self-Play to General Reasoning**: While self-play has shown promise in verifiable domains like coding, a key objective is to apply this paradigm to more abstract and open-ended reasoning domains, where explicit external verification oracles are often unavailable or difficult to define.
5. **Develop a Model-Agnostic Framework**: The design goal includes creating a framework that is effective across different LLM architectures and scales, thereby demonstrating broad applicability and utility for various foundational models.

In essence, R-Zero is motivated by the vision of truly autonomous AI that can learn and evolve indefinitely, free from human limitations and intervention, by creating its own learning environment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

R-Zero proposes a fully autonomous, iterative framework centered around a co-evolutionary loop between two distinct roles: a **Challenger** and a **Solver**, both initialized from the same base LLM. The framework operates without any pre-existing external tasks or human labels, generating its own curriculum through their interaction.

#### 3.1. Overview of the Co-Evolutionary Loop

The R-Zero framework proceeds in an iterative cycle:
1. **Challenger Training**: The Challenger (Qθ) generates new questions that are specifically designed to be challenging for the current Solver.
2. **Solver Dataset Construction**: A training dataset of question-answer pairs is curated from these generated questions, applying filtering and pseudo-labeling.
3. **Solver Training**: The Solver (Sϕ) is fine-tuned on this newly constructed dataset to improve its problem-solving capabilities.
This loop continuously repeats, allowing both models to co-evolve and progressively enhance the Solver's reasoning abilities.

#### 3.2. Challenger Training (Phase 1)

The Challenger, Qθ, is an autoregressive language model tasked with generating difficult questions. It is trained using **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**, an RL algorithm that normalizes rewards within a group of responses to stabilize optimization. The core of Challenger training lies in its composite reward function, which incentivizes generating appropriate questions:

- **Uncertainty Reward**: This is the primary signal, designed to encourage the Challenger to produce questions at the *edge of the Solver’s current abilities*. For a generated question `x`, the Solver `Sϕ` provides `m` responses. A pseudo-label `~y(x)` is determined by majority vote. The Solver’s empirical accuracy `p^(x; Sϕ)` is calculated as the proportion of responses matching `~y(x)`. The uncertainty reward is `r_uncertainty(x; ϕ) = 1 - 2|p^(x; Sϕ) - 0.5|`. This function is maximized when the Solver's accuracy is 50%, implying maximum uncertainty and thus optimal challenge for learning. The theoretical motivation for this is rooted in the KL divergence to an optimal policy, which is lower-bounded by the variance of the Solver's reward, maximized at `p^ = 0.5`.
- **Repetition Penalty**: To ensure diversity and prevent the Challenger from generating highly similar questions within a batch, a penalty `r_rep(x_i)` is applied. This penalty is proportional to the relative size of a cluster of semantically similar questions, identified using BLEU score similarity and agglomerative clustering.
- **Format Check Penalty**: A crucial initial step, this penalty assigns a reward of 0 if the generated question does not adhere to a specific structural format (e.g., enclosed in `<question>` tags).

The final scalar reward `r_i = max(0, r_uncertainty - r_rep(x_i))` is used with GRPO to update the Challenger's policy `Qθ`. The focus for question generation is initially within the domain of mathematics, chosen for its objective nature which simplifies pseudo-label generation.

#### 3.3. Solver Dataset Construction (Phase 2)

After Challenger training, a curated dataset `S` is built to train the Solver.
- **Candidate Generation**: The Challenger `Qθ` samples a large pool of `N` candidate questions.
- **Pseudo-Labeling**: For each question, the current Solver `Sϕ` generates `m` answers, and a pseudo-label `~y_k` is derived via majority vote.
- **Difficulty Filtering**: A question-answer pair `(x_k, ~y_k)` is added to `S` only if its empirical correctness `p^_k` falls within an "informative band," specifically `|p^_k - 0.5| <= δ`. This filters out tasks that are either too easy (high consistency) or too hard (very low consistency/ambiguous). This filtering also acts as implicit quality control, as low consistency often indicates ill-posed or unreliable questions. For experiments, `m=10` and `δ=0.25` (i.e., 3 to 7 answers match the majority vote).

#### 3.4. Solver Training (Phase 3)

The Solver, Sϕ, is fine-tuned on the challenging problems in the curated dataset `S`, also using GRPO.
- **Verifiable Reward**: For a given question `x_i` from `S` with its pseudo-label `~y_i`, the Solver generates a batch of answers. Each answer `y_j` receives a binary reward `r_j = 1` if it matches `~y_i`, and `0` otherwise. This serves as a verifiable reward signal.
- **Policy Update**: The Solver’s policy `Sϕ` is updated by minimizing the GRPO loss, improving its ability to solve the challenging, self-generated questions.

This iterative self-supervision process allows R-Zero to continually enhance LLM reasoning capabilities from a zero-data starting point.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

R-Zero demonstrates significant empirical success and provides crucial insights into the dynamics of self-evolving LLMs.

1. **Consistent Improvement in Mathematical Reasoning**: R-Zero consistently and substantially improves the mathematical reasoning capabilities of various backbone LLMs.
 - For **Qwen3-4B-Base**, three iterations of R-Zero boosted the average score on math benchmarks by **+6.49 points** (from 42.57 to 49.07).
 - For **Qwen3-8B-Base**, average performance increased by **+5.51 points** (from 49.18 to 54.69).
 - Similar gains were observed for **OctoThinker-3B** (+2.68 points), confirming the framework's model-agnostic nature across different architectures and scales.
 - The importance of the Challenger's RL-based training was validated, with initial R-Zero iterations showing marked improvements over baselines where the Solver was trained on questions from an untrained Challenger.

2. **Generalization to General-Domain Reasoning**: Skills acquired through math-focused self-training with R-Zero effectively generalize to complex general-domain reasoning tasks.
 - **Qwen3-8B-Base** showed an average improvement of **+5.13 points** on general-domain benchmarks (SuperGPQA, MMLU-Pro, BBEH).
 - **OctoThinker-3B** improved by **+3.65 points**. This indicates that R-Zero enhances fundamental reasoning capabilities rather than merely domain-specific knowledge. Further experiments removing the math-specific prompt showed even greater gains for larger models in general reasoning, reinforcing this generalization.

3. **Ablation Studies Confirm Component Importance**: Key components of R-Zero proved vital for performance:
 - **Repetition Penalty**: Removing this led to performance degradation, highlighting the necessity of generating diverse questions for effective Solver training.
 - **Task Filtering**: Disabling the difficulty-based curriculum filtering resulted in a significant performance drop, especially on general-domain tasks (over 6 points). This confirms its dual role in calibrating difficulty and implicitly controlling data quality by removing noisy or ambiguous questions.

4. **Iteration Scaling and Performance Degradation (Model Collapse)**: While R-Zero initially shows significant improvements, performance eventually converges and then consistently degrades after multiple iterations.
 - Smaller models (e.g., 0.6B) experience this performance collapse earlier (e.g., after the first iteration), while larger models (e.g., 4B) sustain improvements for longer (up to three iterations) before declining. This "model collapse" points to inherent instability or limitations in the current self-improvement framework when exclusively trained on self-synthesized data.

5. **Synergy with Supervised Data**: R-Zero serves as an effective mid-training method, significantly boosting the performance of subsequent supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on human-labeled data.
 - For the Qwen3-4B model, R-Zero followed by SFT achieved a **+2.35 point gain** over an SFT-only baseline. This suggests R-Zero acts as a powerful performance amplifier, enabling models to better leverage supervised information. Sequential training (R-Zero first, then SFT) was found to be more effective than concurrent mixing of self-generated and human data.

6. **Evolution of Question Difficulty and Data Accuracy**: An analysis with GPT-4o as an external oracle revealed a trade-off:
 - The Challenger successfully generates **progressively more difficult questions** over iterations (e.g., a fixed Solver's performance drops from 59.0% on early questions to 47.0% on later, harder questions).
 - However, the **true accuracy of the self-generated pseudo-labels systematically drops** as questions become harder (from 79.0% in early iterations to 63.0% by the third iteration). This indicates that the Solver's majority vote becomes a less reliable source of truth for increasingly complex problems, presenting a critical bottleneck for long-term stability.
 - Further analysis suggests that while pseudo-label degradation contributes to the collapse, it is not the sole cause, with model scale influencing resilience to this issue.

7. **Importance of Separating Challenger and Solver**: An ablation study showed that separating the Challenger and Solver into two independent models is crucial. A unified "Single-R-Zero" model, where a single agent performs both roles, resulted in earlier performance collapse and significantly lower pseudo-label accuracy, suggesting that internal bias from a shared model leads to overconfidence and less reliable self-supervision.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

R-Zero represents a significant advancement in the pursuit of truly autonomous and self-improving artificial intelligence, with far-reaching implications for the future of LLMs:

1. **Pioneering "Zero-Data" LLM Evolution**: The most profound impact of R-Zero is its demonstration of a fully autonomous framework that eliminates the reliance on any pre-existing human-curated data or labels for LLM reasoning improvement. This pushes the frontier of self-evolving AI beyond current "label-free" or "self-challenging" methods that still require initial datasets or external verifiers.
2. **Scalability Towards Superintelligence**: By circumventing the human-data bottleneck, R-Zero offers a more scalable pathway toward AI capabilities that can potentially surpass human intelligence. This framework could enable continuous, unsupervised learning on a scale unachievable with human intervention.
3. **Dynamic, Self-Improving Curriculum Generation**: R-Zero's co-evolutionary mechanism generates a targeted curriculum that adapts in difficulty to the Solver's current capabilities. This adaptive learning approach is critical for efficient skill acquisition and robust reasoning development, potentially leading to more specialized and powerful models.
4. **Broad Applicability and Model-Agnosticism**: The framework's demonstrated effectiveness across different LLM architectures (Qwen3 and OctoThinker families) and scales suggests its potential as a general-purpose method for enhancing reasoning in various foundational models. The ability to generalize beyond math-specific prompts further enhances its utility.
5. **Synergistic Training Paradigm**: The finding that R-Zero acts as a powerful mid-training method, boosting the effectiveness of subsequent supervised fine-tuning, highlights its practical utility. It suggests that R-Zero can serve as a crucial pre-training or intermediate step, allowing models to acquire robust reasoning foundations before leveraging high-quality human data.
6. **Insights into Self-Learning Dynamics**: R-Zero provides valuable empirical insights into the challenges and limitations of purely self-supervised learning, particularly the eventual performance degradation due to decreasing pseudo-label accuracy and potential model collapse. These findings are crucial for guiding future research toward developing more stable and indefinitely scalable self-improvement loops.
7. **Expansion to New Domains**: While initially focused on mathematics, the framework's core principles of uncertainty-driven challenge generation and iterative self-improvement hold promise for expansion into other reasoning-intensive domains where objective verification might be designed. Future work on more robust labeling mechanisms could unlock highly subjective and open-ended tasks.

In summary, R-Zero represents a significant step forward in making LLMs more autonomous and capable of self-directed learning. Its capacity to bootstrap reasoning from zero external data, coupled with its insights into the dynamics of self-evolution, positions it as a foundational contribution to the field of artificial general intelligence.
