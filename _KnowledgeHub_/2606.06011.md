# Merging model-based control with multi-agent reinforcement learning for multi-agent cooperative teaming strategies

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Georgia Tech and Sandia National Laboratories developed the Multi-Agent Actor-Critic Model Predictive Control (MA-AC-MPC) framework, integrating differentiable MPC into multi-agent reinforcement learning. This framework yielded greater robustness to physical variations and demonstrated superior hardware transferability, achieving a 100% success rate in a drone landing task on a moving robot compared to 60% for a pure learning baseline.

## Problem

- Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) methods often lack robustness, require high sample efficiency, and struggle to enforce physical constraints in safety-critical applications.
- Pure model-based control (MPC) demands differentiable objective functions, fast computation for real-time, and makes manual objective function crafting challenging for complex multi-agent cooperation.
- Existing integrations of MPC and RL in multi-agent settings often do not provide end-to-end learning for complex cooperative strategies, limiting their efficiency and applicability.

## Method

- Developed Multi-Agent Actor-Critic ==Model Predictive Control== (MA-AC-MPC), extending single-agent AC-MPC to cooperative multi-agent tasks.
- The actor policy employs a hierarchical structure where a neural network generates Model Predictive Control (MPC) parameters, which are then used by a differentiable MPC module to produce dynamically feasible control actions.
- Leverages `leap-c` and `acados` to implement differentiable nonlinear MPC, allowing end-to-end learning within a centralized training, decentralized execution (CTDE) framework.

## Results

- In a multi-agent pursuit-evasion scenario, MA-AC-MPC achieved near **1.0** win rates in approximately 2 million environment steps, outperforming pure learning baselines that struggled to reach similar performance after 4 million steps.
- MA-AC-MPC demonstrated superior robustness to physical parameter variations, consistently yielding higher win rates across a range of evader mass changes during execution compared to MA-AC-MLP.
- For the drone landing task on a moving robot, MA-AC-MPC achieved a **100%** success rate (5 out of 5 trials) in hardware experiments with a mean landing error of 0.055m, significantly surpassing MA-AC-MLP's **60%** success rate (3 out of 5 trials).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating differentiable Model Predictive Control (MPC) directly into the actor policy enhances robustness and ensures dynamic feasibility for multi-agent systems.
> - The hierarchical actor structure allows the neural network to focus on high-level strategic planning by offloading low-level control and constraint satisfaction to the MPC module.
> - This combined approach improves sample efficiency for learning complex cooperative behaviors by providing a more stable and physically consistent action space.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the intersection of multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) and model-based control. MARL has gained prominence in robotics and complex game environments for its ability to learn cooperative policies from discrete rewards over long planning horizons. However, pure learning-based techniques often encounter limitations in robustness, sample efficiency, and the ability to strictly adhere to physical constraints, which restricts their application in safety-critical systems.

Conversely, model-based control, particularly Model Predictive Control (MPC), offers advantages by leveraging known physics and constraints to generate dynamically feasible and safe actions. MPC's receding horizon optimization allows for fast online replanning and enhances robustness. Despite these benefits, traditional MPC requires differentiable objective functions and rapid computation, which can be challenging for complex, non-differentiable tasks common in multi-agent cooperation, and necessitates manual objective function crafting.

Previous efforts to integrate MPC and RL generally fall into three categories: using MPC as an expert policy for imitation learning or exploration guidance, employing MPC within the critic function, or directly embedding MPC into the policy. The latter approach, often involving differentiable MPC, allows for end-to-end learning. While significant research exists on combining MPC with single-agent RL, the application to multi-agent RL problems, especially in an end-to-end learning framework for complex cooperative behaviors, remains less explored. Existing multi-agent RL with MPC methods, such as those using distributed MPC for global value functions in centralized learning, often do not operate in an end-to-end learning framework, thereby limiting their capacity for learning intricate cooperative strategies efficiently.

This work addresses this gap by proposing an end-to-end framework that merges the strengths of MARL—specifically, learning from complex, non-differentiable rewards and long-term planning—with the safety and dynamic feasibility guarantees of model-based control. It extends single-agent Actor-Critic Model Predictive Control (AC-MPC) to multi-agent environments, contributing to the development of robust and efficient control strategies for cooperative multi-agent systems, particularly in scenarios where physical constraints and dynamic feasibility are critical.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust control framework for cooperative multi-agent tasks that combines the learning capabilities of multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) with the safety and dynamic feasibility provided by model-based control. This objective is driven by the following motivations:

- **Addressing Limitations of Pure Learning-Based Methods:** Traditional MARL techniques excel at learning complex cooperative policies from non-differentiable rewards over extended planning horizons. However, they frequently exhibit vulnerabilities in out-of-distribution scenarios, demand substantial sample complexity, and struggle to inherently enforce real-world physical constraints. These shortcomings limit their applicability in safety-critical domains where robustness and adherence to physical laws are paramount.
- **Overcoming Constraints of Pure Model-Based Control:** Model Predictive Control (MPC) offers strong guarantees for dynamic feasibility and safety by integrating known system dynamics and constraints. Its fast replanning capabilities contribute to robustness. Nevertheless, MPC typically requires objective functions to be differentiable and solvable quickly enough for real-time applications. Furthermore, manually designing an effective objective function for complex, multi-agent cooperative tasks can be arduous or infeasible.
- **Bridging the Gap for Safe and Adaptive Multi-Agent Systems:** The motivation is to create a synergy where MARL can handle complex, high-level planning and non-differentiable rewards, while MPC ensures that the generated actions are low-level, dynamically feasible, and safe, respecting physical constraints. This combination aims to enable multi-agent systems to learn sophisticated cooperative behaviors while maintaining operational integrity.
- **Extending Actor-Critic MPC to Multi-Agent Settings:** A specific objective is to extend the single-agent Actor-Critic Model Predictive Control (AC-MPC) framework to multi-agent reinforcement learning problems. This extension, termed Multi-Agent Actor-Critic Model Predictive Control (MA-AC-MPC), seeks to leverage differentiable MPC within the actor policy to directly generate control actions that are inherently consistent with physical models.
- **Demonstrating Robustness and Performance in Practical Scenarios:** The research aims to validate the proposed MA-AC-MPC algorithm through its application to challenging multi-agent cooperative tasks, specifically a pursuit-evasion scenario and a drone landing on a moving ground robot. The intention is to demonstrate that MA-AC-MPC can yield improved robustness, sample efficiency, and higher success rates compared to pure learning-based alternatives, especially when deployed in hardware.

The paper summarizes its contributions as:
1. Extending the AC-MPC algorithm to multi-agent reinforcement learning problems involving cooperating agent teams.
2. Evaluating the MA-AC-MPC algorithm across two distinct benchmarks: a 2 vs. 2 multi-agent pursuit-evasion scenario and a cooperative drone landing on a moving rover.
3. Implementing MA-AC-MPC using the `leap-c` open-source project, which integrates `acados` for solving general nonlinear optimal control problems with nonlinear inequality constraints, offering faster performance than previously used differentiable MPC modules.
4. Comparing MA-AC-MPC with a Multi-Agent Actor-Critic Multilayer Perceptron (MA-AC-MLP) model to demonstrate enhanced robustness through the incorporation of MPC as an actor layer, quantified by success rates under varying agent masses and in hardware experiments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work involves integrating Model Predictive Control (MPC) into a Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) framework. The proposed algorithm is referred to as Multi-Agent Actor-Critic Model Predictive Control (MA-AC-MPC).

#### 3.1. Decentralized Markov Decision Processes (Dec-MDPs)

The multi-agent system is formalized as a decentralized Markov Decision Process (Dec-MDP), characterized by a set of agents (K), global state space (S), individual action spaces (A⁽ⁱ⁾), state transition probability (P), individual observation spaces (O⁽ⁱ⁾), individual reward functions (R⁽ⁱ⁾), and a discount factor (γ). For fully cooperative settings, a shared reward is assumed (R = R⁽ⁱ⁾ = R⁽ᴷ⁾). The objective is to find a joint policy Π = {π⁽¹⁾..., π⁽ᴷ⁾} that maximizes the discounted expected cumulative reward. The paper adopts a centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE) paradigm, where additional global state information is used during training to improve learning, but agents rely only on local observations during execution. Parameter sharing is employed for homogeneous agents to accelerate learning.

#### 3.2. Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC)

The single-agent MPC problem is defined as an optimal control problem (OCP) that minimizes a cost function over a finite horizon (N) subject to system dynamics (xₖ₊₁ = fₖ(xₖ, uₖ; θ_MPC)) and state/control constraints (hₖ, h_N). The cost function comprises a stage cost (Lₖ) and a terminal cost (M), which can be in linear least-squares or quadratic form. The parameters (θ_MPC) defining these costs, dynamics, or constraints are learned. For gradient-based optimization in RL, a differentiable MPC framework is required to compute sensitivities of the optimal solution with respect to parameters. The work utilizes `leap-c`, which provides differentiable interfaces for `acados`, an open-source framework capable of handling nonlinear and parametric costs, dynamics, and inequality constraints.

#### 3.3. Actor-Critic Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

The MA-AC-MPC framework uses an actor-critic structure to find the joint policy.
- **Critic:** A feed-forward neural network approximates a centralized value function (V_π(s)) during training, leveraging the full global state of the environment.
- **Actor:** The actor defines the policy π⁽ⁱ⁾(a⁽ⁱ⁾ | o⁽ⁱ⁾). For training, the Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (MAPPO) algorithm is employed.
 - **Actor Loss:** Consists of a clipped surrogate objective and an entropy loss term. The clipped surrogate objective (L⁽ⁱ⁾_clip(θ⁽ⁱ⁾)) uses a ratio of new to old policies, an advantage estimate (calculated via Generalized Advantage Estimate - GAE), and PPO clipping terms (εₐ) to stabilize learning. The entropy loss (L⁽ⁱ⁾_entropy(θ⁽ⁱ⁾)) encourages exploration.
 - **Critic Loss:** Defined as the squared error between the clipped value estimate (V⁽ⁱ⁾_φ,clipped(s_b)) and the discounted reward-to-go (R̂⁽ⁱ⁾_b), with a clipping term (ε_c).
- **Joint Optimization:** The total loss function L(Θ, Φ) combines actor and critic losses for all agents. Parameters are updated via gradient descent, with gradient norm clipping (λ_grad) to ensure stability.
- **Training Enhancements:** For environments where agents can be deactivated (e.g., collisions), "death masking" is used. Inactive agent observations/states are masked by zero vectors, and an agent identifier and active flag are included to help the value network reason about future rewards with deactivated agents. GAE bootstrap cutoff is applied for inactive agents.

#### 3.4. Augmenting MARL with MPC

The paper's primary methodological contribution is how MPC is integrated into the MARL actor:
- **Hierarchical Actor:** The actor policy π⁽ⁱ⁾(o⁽ⁱ⁾) is designed hierarchically. The first layer is a neural network that acts as a "planner," outputting the MPC parameters (y⁽ⁱ⁾_k,ref, y⁽ⁱ⁾_N,ref, W⁽ⁱ⁾, W⁽ⁱ⁾_N) that define the stage and terminal costs (linear least-squares form) for each agent's MPC problem. The second layer is the differentiable MPC module, which uses these parameters to generate dynamically feasible control actions (u⁽ⁱ⁾) by solving the OCP.
- **Distributed Deployment, Centralized Training:** Each agent has its own local MPC solver, ensuring distributed execution. However, during training, a shared value function centralizes information to evaluate the global environment state and optimize individual actor policies towards maximizing collective rewards. The neural network's cost function takes observations containing both the agent's own state and partial state information from other agents.
- **Implementation with `leap-c` and `acados`:** The differentiable MPC component is implemented using `leap-c` (Fichtner et al. 2025), which leverages `acados` (Verschueren et al. 2021) to solve the nonlinear optimal control problems and compute gradients through implicit differentiation of KKT conditions.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

The MA-AC-MPC framework is evaluated on two cooperative multi-agent environments:
1. **Multi-Agent Pursuit-Evasion (MAPE):** A 2 pursuer vs. 2 evader scenario where evaders (using MA-AC-MPC or MA-AC-MLP) must cooperate to induce collisions between pursuers (using a fixed proportional navigation strategy).
2. **Drone Landing on Ground Robot:** A heterogeneous environment where a drone (using its own policy) must land on a moving omni-wheeled rover (also using its own policy), with both agents cooperating to achieve a successful landing.

For training, the SKRL library (Serrano-Mu˜noz et al. 2023) with its MAPPO implementation is used, modified to include MPC initial states in the memory buffer. Curriculum learning is applied in both environments to stabilize training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study evaluates the proposed Multi-Agent Actor-Critic Model Predictive Control (MA-AC-MPC) framework against a Multi-Agent Actor-Critic Multilayer Perceptron (MA-AC-MLP) baseline across two distinct cooperative multi-agent tasks.

#### 4.1. Multi-Agent Pursuit-Evasion (MAPE)

- **Problem Definition:** The scenario involves two evaders and two pursuers in a 3D environment. Evaders, controlled by MA-AC-MPC or MA-AC-MLP, aim to cause pursuers to collide with each other before any evader is captured. Pursuers employ an augmented proportional navigation strategy. The evader win condition requires all pursuers to be dead with at least one evader alive. A detailed reward function with sparse event rewards and shaping rewards (e.g., pursuer proximity, attitude, velocity, thrust smoothness penalties) was crafted for effective training.
- **Model and Training Configuration:** Crazyflie drone dynamics were simulated using the `crazyflow` simulator (Schuck et al. 2026) with a high-fidelity rigid body model for the environment and a simplified `so_rpy` model for the MPC. Evaders shared a single policy, with observations including agent state, teammate/pursuer states (death masked), and an agent ID. Training involved curriculum learning, gradually increasing environmental difficulty (e.g., spawn distances, collision tolerances, disturbances) across 10 levels.
- **Training Performance and Efficiency:**
 - MA-AC-MPC achieved higher evader win rates in significantly fewer environmental steps compared to MA-AC-MLP models of varying sizes (256x2, 512x2, 512x3 layers). For instance, MA-AC-MPC reached curriculum level 10 and a win rate close to 1.0 around 2 million steps, while MA-AC-MLP models struggled to reach or surpass level 9 even after 4 million steps.
 - While MA-AC-MPC required longer training times per iteration (162.2 hours for 2M steps) due to MPC computations compared to MA-AC-MLP (18-30 hours for 4M steps), its superior sample efficiency meant it achieved higher performance in a shorter overall computational budget (2M steps for MA-AC-MPC vs. 4M for MA-AC-MLP).
 - Inference time per agent for MA-AC-MPC was 1.052 ± 0.474 ms, which is higher than MA-AC-MLP (0.120-0.197 ms), but still within real-time constraints for the application.
- **Robustness to Mass Variations:** A robustness test involved varying the masses of individual evaders between 30.6g and 50.6g (nominal 40.6g) during execution, while the MPC dynamics model retained the nominal mass. MA-AC-MPC consistently yielded higher evader win rates across the entire range of mass variations compared to MA-AC-MLP. The difference in win rate showed no instances where MA-AC-MLP outperformed MA-AC-MPC, indicating that MA-AC-MPC exhibited greater robustness to changes in physical parameters not explicitly trained for in the MPC model.
- **Simulation and Hardware Experiments:** Trajectory data from hardware experiments (using Crazyflie drones and Crazyswarm2) showed similar trends to simulations in `Crazyflow` (training environment) and `CrazySim` (firmware-in-the-loop simulator). This suggests that policies learned using MA-AC-MPC transferred effectively from simulation to physical hardware despite real-world sensor noise, disturbances, and model mismatches.

#### 4.2. Drone Landing on Ground Robot

- **Problem Definition:** A heterogeneous multi-agent task where a Crazyflie drone must land on a moving Yahboom ROSMASTER X3 omni-wheeled rover. Each agent had tailored observation vectors and specific reward components (team rewards for progress and landing bonus; drone-only rewards/penalties for crash, boundary, descent, attitude, smoothness; rover-only penalties for stillness, yaw rate, lateral/backward motion, smoothness, boundary).
- **Models:** The drone used the high-fidelity `first_principles` model for environment physics and `so_rpy` for its MPC. The rover dynamics were based on mecanum wheels and a first-order velocity model (Equation 23) for its MPC, which mirrored the X3's command interface.
- **Training and Simulation:** Both MA-AC-MPC and MA-AC-MLP policies were trained with MAPPO for 1.2 million environment steps using 128 parallel environments and curriculum learning (6 levels of increasing difficulty in drone/rover spawn distance and rover speed).
- **Training Performance:** In this specific environment, MA-AC-MLP slightly outperformed MA-AC-MPC in sample efficiency during training, reaching higher landing rates earlier in the curriculum.
- **Hardware Experiment Results:**
 - **MA-AC-MPC:** Achieved a 100% success rate (5 out of 5 trials) in hardware experiments with consistent and repeatable landing trajectories. The mean error in landing position was 0.055 m.
 - **MA-AC-MLP:** Achieved a 60% success rate (3 out of 5 trials) in hardware. The failed trials exhibited larger terminal errors and overshot the landing pad. The mean error for MA-AC-MLP was 0.240 m.
 - The trajectories shown for MA-AC-MPC exhibited tight convergence to the rover landing pad, while MA-AC-MLP trajectories showed greater variability and clear failure cases with substantial terminal errors.
- **Conclusion on Robustness:** Despite MA-AC-MLP showing higher sample efficiency in simulation for this task, MA-AC-MPC demonstrated superior robustness and reliability in hardware deployment, consistently achieving successful landings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents a framework, Multi-Agent Actor-Critic Model Predictive Control (MA-AC-MPC), that combines the strengths of multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) with model-based control. This integration addresses limitations inherent in purely learning-based or purely model-based approaches, offering a method for developing robust and dynamically feasible cooperative strategies for multi-agent systems.

The key significance of this work lies in:

- **Enhanced Robustness and Safety:** By embedding Model Predictive Control (MPC) directly into the actor policy, MA-AC-MPC ensures that generated actions inherently respect physical constraints and dynamic feasibility. This is particularly important for safety-critical applications in robotics where unpredictable or physically impossible actions generated by pure learning-based policies are unacceptable. The empirical results, especially the mass sweep robustness test in the pursuit-evasion scenario and the hardware validation in the drone landing task, indicate that MA-AC-MPC yields more reliable performance under variations and real-world conditions compared to purely neural network-based policies (MA-AC-MLP).
- **Improved Sample Efficiency for Complex Tasks:** While the computational cost per training step is higher for MA-AC-MPC, the framework demonstrated a capability to achieve high performance with significantly fewer environment steps in the pursuit-evasion task. This suggests that offloading low-level dynamics and constraint satisfaction to the MPC component allows the neural network to focus on high-level task planning and cooperation, leading to a more efficient learning process for complex cooperative behaviors.
- **Successful Simulation-to-Hardware Transfer:** The successful deployment of MA-AC-MPC policies to physical Crazyflie drones and a ROSMASTER X3 rover, with consistent and repeatable results (100% success rate for drone landing), validates the practical applicability and transferability of the learned policies. This contrasts with the lower success rates observed for MA-AC-MLP in hardware, highlighting MA-AC-MPC's potential for real-world robotic systems, where policy transfer remains a significant challenge.
- **Advancement in Multi-Agent Control Research:** The extension of single-agent Actor-Critic MPC to the multi-agent domain, particularly using differentiable MPC with tools like `leap-c` and `acados`, provides a concrete architectural and algorithmic contribution to the field. It demonstrates a viable pathway for combining long-horizon, reward-driven learning with short-horizon, constraint-aware optimal control for decentralized execution in cooperative multi-agent tasks.

The potential impact of this work extends to various domains involving autonomous multi-agent systems, including:
- **Robotic Swarms and Fleets:** Enabling safer and more robust coordination for tasks like warehouse automation, search and rescue missions, or environmental monitoring where dynamic feasibility and collision avoidance are critical.
- **Aerial Robotics:** Facilitating the development of complex cooperative maneuvers for drone teams, such as coordinated inspection, cargo transport, or in the demonstrated pursuit-evasion and landing scenarios.
- **Autonomous Driving:** Potentially contributing to frameworks for cooperative navigation and collision avoidance among autonomous vehicles.

Overall, the MA-AC-MPC framework represents a step towards creating intelligent multi-agent systems that can learn complex cooperative strategies while maintaining a high degree of safety, robustness, and reliability in real-world environments.
