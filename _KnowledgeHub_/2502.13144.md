# RAD: Training an End-to-End Driving Policy via Large-Scale 3DGS-based Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Huazhong University of Science & Technology and Horizon Robotics introduced RAD, an end-to-end autonomous driving framework leveraging 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to create photorealistic, interactive environments for Reinforcement Learning (RL) training. The framework combines RL with Imitation Learning (IL) regularization, achieving a 3x lower collision rate compared to state-of-the-art IL-based methods while maintaining human-aligned driving behavior.

## Problem

- Imitation Learning (IL) based autonomous driving policies suffer from 'causal confusion' (learning correlations instead of causations) and the 'open-loop gap', leading to poor generalization and safety issues in out-of-distribution scenarios.
- Applying Reinforcement Learning (RL) to autonomous driving is challenging due to the high safety risks and cost of real-world training, and the lack of photorealistic fidelity in traditional simulators which causes a 'sim2real gap'.
- Pure RL policies can exhibit non-human-like or jerky behaviors, while complex RL tasks often struggle with sparse reward signals, hindering efficient learning.

## Method

- A three-stage training paradigm combining perception pre-training, IL-based planning pre-training, and reinforced post-training (RL + IL synergistically) is employed for an end-to-end driving policy.
- Photorealistic ==3DGS== environments, reconstructed from real-world driving clips, provide a safe and interactive closed-loop simulator for large-scale RL training and exploration.
- A hybrid optimization approach uses Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) for RL steps to learn from interaction, regularized by IL steps to maintain human-like driving behavior, alongside a multi-component reward system and dense auxiliary objectives.

## Results

- RAD achieved a collision ratio (CR) of **0.089**, which is 3 times lower than pure IL (CR **0.229**) and significantly lower than state-of-the-art IL baselines (e.g., VADv2 with CR **0.270**) on a 3DGS-based closed-loop benchmark.
- The hybrid RL+IL approach demonstrated the best balance between safety (lowest CR) and human alignment (competitive Average Deviation Distance of **0.257**, close to IL-only's **0.238**), validating the synergistic effect.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical importance of specific reward components (e.g., dynamic collision reward) and auxiliary objectives for enhancing safety and efficient learning, while consistency analysis validated the realism of 3DGS environments for control policy training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating 3D Gaussian Splatting directly into the RL training loop provides a critical enabler for photorealistic, interactive, and safe environments, effectively bridging the sim2real gap for end-to-end AD.
> - The synergistic combination of Reinforcement Learning for robustness and Imitation Learning for human alignment successfully mitigates the limitations of each paradigm individually, yielding both safer and more human-like driving policies.
> - A comprehensive, multi-component reward function combined with directional auxiliary objectives is crucial for providing dense feedback, combating sparse rewards, and efficiently guiding the RL agent towards safer driving behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous driving (AD) has seen significant advancements, with **end-to-end AD** emerging as a highly promising paradigm. This approach directly maps raw sensory inputs to driving actions, simplifying the system architecture and potentially improving generalization compared to traditional modular pipelines. However, as the authors highlight, the majority of existing end-to-end AD algorithms are built upon the **Imitation Learning (IL)** paradigm.

IL-based methods train neural networks to mimic human driving behavior by learning from large datasets of expert demonstrations. While straightforward, this approach suffers from two critical limitations:
1. **Causal Confusion:** IL primarily learns correlations between observations and actions, rather than the underlying causal relationships. This can lead to "shortcut learning," where policies fail to understand the true drivers behind expert decisions, making them brittle and unable to generalize to novel or safety-critical situations not present in the training data.
2. **Open-Loop Gap:** IL training is inherently open-loop, meaning the policy is trained on pre-recorded demonstrations without interacting with the environment. During real-world closed-loop deployment, small errors can accumulate, leading the vehicle into out-of-distribution (OOD) states where the IL policy performs poorly, raising significant safety concerns.

To address these limitations, **Reinforcement Learning (RL)** presents a theoretically appealing alternative. RL allows an agent to learn through trial-and-error in an interactive environment, inherently modeling causal relationships and enabling exploration of OOD scenarios. However, applying RL to autonomous driving faces its own challenges:
- **Safety Risks and Cost:** Training RL agents in the real world is prohibitively dangerous and expensive.
- **Simulator Realism:** Traditional driving simulators (e.g., CARLA, based on game engines) often lack the photorealism and fidelity required for end-to-end AD, where sensor inputs must closely mimic real-world data. This creates a "sim2real gap," where policies trained in simulators do not transfer well to the real world.

This paper strategically positions RAD within this landscape by introducing **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)** as a critical enabler. 3DGS is a relatively recent technique that allows for the construction of highly photorealistic digital replicas of real-world scenes with real-time rendering capabilities. While prior works (e.g., UniSim, MARS, NeuRAD, HugSim) have explored using 3DGS or similar neural rendering techniques for *closed-loop evaluation* or generating synthetic data, RAD makes a significant leap by integrating 3DGS-reconstructed scenes directly into the **RL training loop**. This provides a photorealistic, interactive, and safe environment for large-scale trial-and-error learning.

Furthermore, the paper acknowledges the "Human Alignment Problem" in pure RL (where agents might learn highly effective but non-human-like or jerky behaviors) and the "Sparse Reward Problem" inherent in complex RL tasks. RAD tackles these by **combining RL with IL as a regularization term**. This hybrid approach builds upon previous works that explored IL+RL (e.g., CADRE, CIRL, Lu et al., Huang et al.) but distinguishes itself by being the *first* to do so within a 3DGS-based photorealistic digital-twin environment, processing real-world sensor inputs end-to-end. This integration is crucial, as previous hybrid methods were often limited to non-photorealistic simulators or relied on structured Bird's Eye View (BEV) representations rather than raw pixel data.

In essence, RAD bridges the gap between the strengths of IL and RL, leveraging the photorealism of 3DGS to create a scalable, safe, and effective training paradigm that directly addresses the most pressing limitations of current end-to-end AD systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of RAD is to develop a more robust, safer, and human-aligned end-to-end autonomous driving policy that overcomes the fundamental limitations of existing Imitation Learning (IL) based approaches. The motivations are clearly articulated through the identified challenges with IL:

1. **Addressing Causal Confusion:** IL's reliance on correlations rather than causations often leads to policies that struggle with rare or unexpected scenarios. The authors aim to enable the AD policy to learn genuine causal relationships between observations and actions, which is crucial for making informed and safe driving decisions, especially in complex and safety-critical situations.
2. **Mitigating the Open-Loop Gap:** The discrepancy between open-loop IL training and closed-loop real-world deployment results in compounding errors and poor performance in out-of-distribution scenarios. A core objective is to facilitate closed-loop training, allowing the policy to experience and correct its own errors over time, thereby improving its robustness in unseen situations.
3. **Achieving Photorealistic and Interactive Training:** Traditional closed-loop RL in the real world is unsafe and costly, while existing simulators often lack realism. The motivation here is to create a training environment that offers both photorealistic sensor data and interactive capabilities. This led to the adoption of 3DGS techniques, enabling the construction of digital replicas of the real world where extensive, safe, and realistic trial-and-error learning can occur.
4. **Ensuring Human Alignment:** Pure RL policies, while potentially effective, can exhibit non-human-like, aggressive, or jerky behaviors. A key objective is to integrate IL into the RL framework as a regularization term, guiding the policy to maintain similarity to human driving behavior, which is essential for passenger comfort and public acceptance.
5. **Overcoming Sparse Rewards in RL:** RL training for complex tasks like autonomous driving often suffers from sparse rewards, slowing down convergence. The authors aim to design a dense and informative reward system, complemented by auxiliary objectives, to provide richer training signals and improve learning efficiency.
6. **Developing a Comprehensive Training Paradigm:** The ultimate objective is to propose a novel, multi-stage training paradigm that synergistically combines the benefits of perception pre-training, IL-based planning pre-training, and RL-based fine-tuning, all within a realistic simulation framework. This structured approach aims to optimize various aspects of the end-to-end AD policy effectively.

In summary, RAD is motivated by the critical need for safer and more reliable autonomous driving systems, which the authors believe can be achieved by moving beyond the limitations of pure imitation learning towards a closed-loop reinforcement learning paradigm, made practical and effective through photorealistic 3DGS environments and judicious integration of IL.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RAD proposes a novel, end-to-end autonomous driving framework that leverages a multi-stage training paradigm primarily centered on 3DGS-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) complemented by Imitation Learning (IL).

#### 3.1. End-to-End Driving Policy Architecture

The policy takes multi-view image sequences as input and outputs a probabilistic distribution of lateral and longitudinal actions.
- **BEV Encoder:** Transforms multi-view image features into a Bird's Eye View (BEV) feature map.
- **Perception Head (Map and Agent Heads):** From the BEV map, a Map Head learns vectorized map elements (lane lines, boundaries) using map tokens, and an Agent Head predicts motion information and multi-modal future trajectories of other traffic participants using agent tokens.
- **Image Encoder:** Processes original images into image tokens, providing dense scene information complementary to the instance-level tokens.
- **Action Space:** A decoupled discrete action representation is proposed, separating lateral ($a_x$) and longitudinal ($a_y$) actions. Each action corresponds to a short 0.5-second horizon, assuming constant linear and angular velocities. This discretization (e.g., 61 options for lateral, 61 for longitudinal) simplifies the action space for efficient RL optimization.
- **Planning Head:** A cascaded Transformer decoder queries scene representation (map, agent, image tokens), along with navigation information and ego-vehicle state, to output the probabilistic distributions for lateral and longitudinal actions ($\pi(a_x|s)$, $\pi(a_y|s)$) and their corresponding value functions ($V_x(s)$, $V_y(s)$).

#### 3.2. Three-Stage Training Paradigm

RAD employs a sequential training approach to ensure stability and efficiency:
1. **Perception Pre-Training:** The BEV encoder, map head, and agent head are trained using ground-truth labels for map elements and agent motion. This stage ensures that high-level scene information is correctly encoded from raw sensory input.
2. **Planning Pre-Training (Imitation Learning):** To prevent an unstable "cold start" for RL, the image encoder and planning head are initialized through IL using large-scale real-world driving demonstrations from expert drivers. This provides a reasonable initial driving policy.
3. **Reinforced Post-Training (RL + IL Synergistically):** This is the core innovation where RL and IL fine-tune the AD policy.
 - **3DGS Environment:** Risky, dense-traffic real-world driving clips are reconstructed into photorealistic 3DGS environments (3968 for training).
 - **Parallel Rollout:** Multiple (N=32) workers run in parallel. Each worker samples a 3DGS environment, and the AD policy controls the ego vehicle, interacting with the environment. Other traffic participants are controlled via log-replay of real-world data to maintain realism. Trajectories ($s_t, a_t, r_{t+1}, s_{t+1}, \dots$) are collected in a shared buffer.
 - **Alternating Optimization:** The policy is optimized by alternating between RL steps (using Proximal Policy Optimization - PPO from the rollout buffer) and IL steps (using expert demonstrations). IL serves as a regularization term, maintaining human-like behavior while RL explores and learns from interaction.
 - **Kinematic Model:** A conventional kinematic bicycle model updates the ego vehicle's pose based on predicted linear velocity and steering angle from the AD policy.

#### 3.3. Reward Modeling

The reward function is crucial for guiding RL and is designed to penalize unsafe actions and encourage alignment with expert trajectories. It comprises four components, each triggering episode termination if exceeded:
- **Dynamic Collision Reward ($r_{dc}$):** Negative reward for collisions with dynamic obstacles.
- **Static Collision Reward ($r_{sc}$):** Negative reward for collisions with static obstacles.
- **Positional Deviation Reward ($r_{pd}$):** Negative reward for exceeding a predefined positional deviation from the expert trajectory.
- **Heading Deviation Reward ($r_{hd}$):** Negative reward for exceeding a predefined heading angle deviation from the expert trajectory.
Rewards are decoupled: $r_x$ (lateral) includes static collision, positional, and heading deviations, while $r_y$ (longitudinal) includes dynamic collision.

#### 3.4. Policy Optimization and Auxiliary Objectives

- **PPO with GAE:** Rewards are propagated forward using Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) to compute advantage estimates ($\hat{A}_x, \hat{A}_y$) for lateral and longitudinal actions. The policy is optimized using a modified PPO objective with independent clipping mechanisms for each dimension.
- **Auxiliary Objectives:** To combat sparse rewards and provide dense supervision, four directional auxiliary objectives are introduced:
 - **Dynamic Collision Auxiliary Loss ($L_{dc}$):** Encourages deceleration if a collision is ahead, acceleration if behind.
 - **Static Collision Auxiliary Loss ($L_{sc}$):** Promotes steering away from static obstacles.
 - **Positional Deviation Loss ($L_{pd}$):** Encourages lateral corrections towards the reference path.
 - **Heading Deviation Loss ($L_{hd}$):** Promotes angular corrections to minimize orientation error.
These objectives adaptively adjust action probabilities and are integrated into the composite optimization objective $L(\theta) = L_{PPO}(\theta) + \sum \lambda_i L_{aux,i}(\theta)$, with $\lambda_i$ as weighting coefficients.

#### 3.5. 3DGS Reconstruction and Rendering Optimization (Appendix)

The 3DGS environment extends StreetGaussian. Key enhancements include: mesh modeling to constrain road surface geometry, separate sky modeling, and optimization of foreground object poses with depth and normal consistency as supervision. These ensure high realism and geometric accuracy, particularly for off-trajectory views, which is vital for RL exploration.

This comprehensive methodology allows RAD to effectively train an end-to-end AD policy that benefits from both photorealistic interactive learning and expert guidance, addressing key challenges in the field.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of RAD is validated through extensive experiments, including ablation studies and comparisons with state-of-the-art IL-based methods, all evaluated on a novel 3DGS-based closed-loop benchmark.

#### 4.1. Ablation Study: Training Strategy Comparison (Table 1)

- **Pure Imitation Learning (IL):** Achieved low Average Deviation Distance (ADD) of 0.238, indicating good adherence to expert trajectories, but suffered from a high Collision Ratio (CR) of 0.229. This highlights IL's struggle with safety-critical events and out-of-distribution scenarios.
- **Pure Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Showed improved safety with a lower CR of 0.143, demonstrating RL's ability to learn collision avoidance. However, it exhibited a higher ADD of 0.345, suggesting deviations from human-like driving behavior, reflecting the "Human Alignment Problem."
- **RAD (RL+IL):** Achieved the best performance, striking an optimal balance between safety and human alignment. It obtained the lowest CR of 0.089 (a **3x lower collision rate** compared to pure IL) while maintaining a competitive ADD of 0.257, close to IL-only performance. This strongly supports the synergistic benefit of combining RL for robustness and IL for human alignment.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study: Reward Source Analysis (Table 2)

- The study systematically evaluated the impact of different reward components (Dynamic Collision, Static Collision, Positional Deviation, Heading Deviation).
- Policies trained with only partial reward terms consistently exhibited higher CRs compared to the full reward setup (ID 6).
- Notably, omitting the **dynamic collision reward term ($r_{dc}$)** (ID 2) resulted in the highest CR (0.238), indicating its critical role in learning to avoid dynamic obstacles.
- The full reward configuration (ID 6) achieved the lowest CR (0.089) while maintaining a stable ADD, demonstrating that a well-balanced, multi-component reward function is essential for both safety and trajectory consistency.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study: Auxiliary Objective Analysis (Table 3)

- This study assessed the contribution of the proposed dense auxiliary objectives (dynamic collision, static collision, positional deviation, heading deviation).
- Removing any auxiliary objective from the full setup (ID 8) led to an increase in CR, highlighting their collective importance in enhancing safety by providing informative gradients.
- The results indicated that while auxiliary objectives are beneficial, they are most effective when combined with the PPO objective. For instance, relying solely on auxiliary objectives without PPO (ID 1) led to a higher CR (0.187) than the combined approach (ID 8: CR 0.089).

#### 4.4. Comparisons with Existing Methods (Table 4)

- RAD was compared against prominent IL-based end-to-end AD methods, including TransFuser, VAD, GenAD, and VADv2, on the 3DGS-based closed-loop evaluation benchmark.
- **RAD significantly outperformed all IL-based baselines across most closed-loop metrics.**
- Crucially, RAD achieved a **3x lower collision rate (CR 0.089)** compared to the best IL baseline (VADv2, CR 0.270), demonstrating its superior ability in collision avoidance.
- RAD also showed competitive or better performance in other safety metrics (DCR, SCR) and consistency metrics (DR, PDR, HDR, ADD), and maintained low jerk values (Long. Jerk, Lat. Jerk) indicating smoother driving. This underscores the effectiveness of closed-loop RL in a photorealistic environment for learning general collision avoidance and robustness.

#### 4.5. Consistency Analysis and Qualitative Comparisons (Figure 5, 6, 7)

- **Consistency Analysis (Fig. 5):** The study showed high behavioral consistency between the 3DGS environment and the real-world environment for the same driving policy, both qualitatively (first and last frames) and quantitatively (temporal evolution of ego vehicle's position). This finding is crucial as it validates the realism and representativeness of the 3DGS simulator for training and evaluation.
- **Qualitative Comparisons (Fig. 6, 7):** Visual comparisons illustrated that the IL-only policy struggled in dynamic and complex scenarios (e.g., failing to yield to pedestrians, handling dense traffic or U-turns). In contrast, RAD consistently performed well, demonstrating effective obstacle avoidance and superior adaptability in challenging situations, further solidifying the benefits of the closed-loop training approach.

In summary, the main findings conclusively demonstrate that RAD, through its novel 3DGS-based RL framework combined with IL regularization, significantly enhances the safety and robustness of end-to-end autonomous driving policies, outperforming state-of-the-art IL-based methods, particularly in reducing collision rates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RAD framework represents a significant advancement in the field of end-to-end autonomous driving, offering several layers of significance and promising substantial potential impact.

1. **Pioneering 3DGS for RL Training:** RAD is the first work to successfully integrate 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) into the *closed-loop Reinforcement Learning training pipeline* for end-to-end autonomous driving. This is a critical distinction from prior works that primarily utilized neural rendering for evaluation or synthetic data generation. By creating photorealistic, interactive digital replicas of the real world, RAD overcomes the long-standing challenge of finding a safe, scalable, and realistic training environment for RL in AD.

2. **Addressing Core Limitations of Imitation Learning:** The work directly tackles the fundamental flaws of imitation learning (IL)—causal confusion and the open-loop gap—which are major impediments to deploying robust AD systems. By enabling the policy to learn through real-time interaction and trial-and-error in a photorealistic environment, RAD fosters a deeper understanding of causal relationships and equips the agent to handle out-of-distribution scenarios and compounding errors that plague IL-only approaches.

3. **Enhanced Safety and Robustness:** The most compelling result is the **3x lower collision rate** achieved by RAD compared to state-of-the-art IL-based methods. This is a paramount achievement for autonomous driving, where safety is the ultimate concern. The ability to significantly reduce collisions, particularly in safety-critical and dynamic environments, signifies a substantial step towards more reliable and trustworthy AD systems.

4. **Effective Hybrid Learning Paradigm:** The synergistic combination of RL and IL is highly impactful. RL provides the robustness and ability to handle edge cases, while IL acts as a regularization term, ensuring that the learned policy maintains human-like behavior, smoothness, and predictability. This hybrid approach successfully mitigates the "Human Alignment Problem" often associated with pure RL, making the learned policies more acceptable and comfortable for passengers.

5. **Scalability and Cost-Effectiveness:** Training AD policies in the real world is incredibly expensive, time-consuming, and dangerous. By providing a photorealistic and interactive digital twin environment, RAD enables large-scale closed-loop exploration and learning without these real-world constraints. This paradigm shift can significantly accelerate the development, testing, and refinement of AD policies, making the process more scalable and cost-effective.

6. **Validation of 3DGS Realism for Control:** The consistency analysis between the 3DGS environment and the real-world performance of the same policy is crucial. It validates that 3DGS environments are not just visually appealing but also accurately represent the physical dynamics and sensory inputs required for training control policies, thereby bridging the sim2real gap more effectively than previous simulators.

7. **Future Research Directions:** RAD opens new avenues for research in several areas:
 - **Improvements in 3DGS:** The authors acknowledge limitations in rendering non-rigid pedestrians, unobserved views, and low-light scenarios, indicating fertile ground for further advancements in 3DGS technology for AD.
 - **Scaling RL:** The work is a stepping stone for scaling up RL to handle even more complex and diverse driving scenarios, potentially integrating more sophisticated RL algorithms and larger digital twin datasets.
 - **Integrated World Models:** The framework lays a foundation for incorporating advanced world models that can predict future states and rewards within the 3DGS environment, further enhancing RL efficiency.

In conclusion, RAD presents a groundbreaking approach that leverages cutting-edge neural rendering to unlock the full potential of reinforcement learning for autonomous driving. Its demonstrated improvements in safety, robustness, and human alignment, coupled with its potential for scalable and cost-effective development, position it as a highly influential work with a profound impact on the future trajectory of end-to-end autonomous driving research and development.
