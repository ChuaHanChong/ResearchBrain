# URDFormer: A Pipeline for Constructing Articulated Simulation Environments from Real-World Images

> [!summary] Summary
> URDFormer builds articulated simulation scenes from a single real image. A forward pipeline renders procedurally sampled URDF scenes and uses texture-guided diffusion to make paired realistic images; the inverse transformer predicts object categories, boxes, and kinematic parent-child structure from RGB. Combined with targeted randomization, the generated scenes train behavior-cloning policies that transfer zero-shot to a real UR5 at 78% success.

## Problem

- Hand-built simulation scenes require a designer per environment, giving high quality but little diversity, and cannot scale to the counts data-driven robot learning needs.
- Procedural generation covers many variations but is not controllable, so it cannot target a specific deployment scene, and struggles with manipulation-level complexity.
- Generative 3D methods produce appealing geometry yet lack kinematic articulation; digital-twin reconstruction needs video, depth, or physical interaction rarely available in internet images.

## Method

- ==Forward pipeline==: procedurally sampled URDF scenes rendered in simulation, then turned into realistic paired images by ==texture-guided Stable Diffusion== that warps generated textures part by part, preserving low-level part consistency.
- **URDFormer** architecture: ==ViT== backbone plus ROI-aligned box features fed to a transformer, with MLP heads predicting base class, discretized 3D position and box, and a parent-child relationship score matrix.
- ==Two-level inversion== (global scene model plus part model) drives a ==real-to-sim-to-real== loop: predicted URDF is depth-rescaled, ==targeted randomization== swaps PartNet meshes and textures, cuRobo collects demos, M2T2 ==behavior cloning== trains the policy.

## Results

- Real UR5 evaluation over **five** cabinets and **ten** tasks reached **39/50** success (**78%**), **85%** on open/close, versus **9/50** for domain randomization, **24/45** for an ICP digital twin, **0/50** for open-vocabulary detection.
- Averaging pretrained and finetuned Grounding DINO weights (model soup) raised part-detection F1 from **53.4%** pretrained and **66.2%** finetuned to **79.7%**, making automatic box extraction usable.
- Texture ablation: diffusion-generated textures lifted global mesh accuracy to **0.578** on kitchens versus **0.42** with random textures, while part-level accuracy stayed flat or dropped **1-2%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Inverting a generative forward process is a practical route to paired supervision when the real label, here articulated scene structure, cannot be collected at scale.
> - Approximate reconstruction suffices: randomizing around a roughly correct targeted twin beats both untargeted randomization and precise-but-brittle digital-twin execution.
> - Photorealism matters mainly for semantic and global inference; box geometry alone carries fine-grained part structure, so spend realism budget selectively.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of realistic and diverse simulation environments is a critical bottleneck in the advancement of data-driven machine learning, particularly in robotics and computer vision. These fields heavily rely on large datasets for training robust policies and models, but real-world data collection is often expensive, time-consuming, unsafe, and lacks the ground-truth information available in simulation.

Historically, simulation environments have been constructed through several methods, each with significant limitations:
- **Manual Asset Creation:** Graphic designers and simulation engineers painstakingly craft scenes and objects. While this can yield high-fidelity, physically accurate models, it is incredibly slow, expensive, and results in environments that lack the diversity needed for generalizable machine learning models.
- **Procedural Generation:** Rule-based systems generate environments algorithmically, offering scalability and diversity. However, these methods often struggle to produce visually and physically realistic scenes that capture the "natural complexity" of the real world, especially for intricate tasks like manipulation. They also lack controllability, making it difficult to generate a simulation tailored to a specific real-world scenario.
- **Purely Generative 3D Models:** Recent advancements in generative AI have enabled impressive text-to-3D models (e.g., DreamFusion, One-2-3-45) that create visually appealing 3D geometries. However, these models typically prioritize visual appearance over physical realism, lacking the precise kinematic (e.g., articulated joints) and dynamic (e.g., mass, friction) properties essential for accurate physical simulation and robotic interaction. They often produce static meshes without underlying functional structure.
- **Digital Twin Construction:** Methods like reconstructing articulated objects from multi-view images, RGB-D data, point clouds, or interactive perception exist. While these can produce high-quality models, they typically require more complex sensor inputs (videos, depth sensors) or physical interaction, which are not always available, especially from web-scale, uncurated imagery.

URDFormer directly addresses these limitations by proposing a novel "inversion through synthesis" paradigm. It uniquely aims to bridge the gap between readily available real-world *images* and the need for *articulated, physically realistic simulation environments (URDFs)*. By leveraging the power of controllable text-to-image generative models, it transforms procedurally generated synthetic data into visually realistic, paired training examples. This allows for the supervised learning of an inverse mapping from a single real image back to a structured, kinematic simulation scene.

This approach significantly pushes the boundaries by:
1. **Enabling Scalability from Web Data:** It allows researchers to convert vast, uncurated web image datasets into diverse simulation assets, a previously untapped resource for robot learning.
2. **Focusing on Articulation and Kinematics:** Unlike many generative 3D models, URDFormer's output (URDF) inherently includes joint types and parent-child relationships, crucial for interactive physics-based simulation.
3. **Improving Realism and Controllability:** By generating simulation environments directly from real images, it ensures greater realism and offers controllability, as specific real-world scenes can be "digitally twinned."
4. **Facilitating Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The generated environments, combined with targeted domain randomization, create a robust training ground for policies that can transfer directly to the real world, reducing the "sim2real gap."

In essence, URDFormer contributes a vital piece to the puzzle of scalable robot learning by automating the creation of high-quality, physically meaningful simulation environments from ubiquitous visual data, thereby overcoming key limitations of prior art.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of the URDFormer project is to develop an automated and scalable pipeline capable of constructing rich, articulated simulation environments from individual real-world images. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Bridging the Data Gap for Robot Learning:** Modern data-hungry learning methods, particularly in robotics, require vast amounts of diverse and realistic training data. Collecting this data in the real world is inherently challenging due to high costs, safety concerns, and the difficulty of acquiring ground truth information. Simulation offers a powerful alternative, but its utility is limited by the availability of high-quality, articulated simulation assets. URDFormer seeks to fill this gap by providing an efficient way to generate such assets.

2. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Asset Creation Methods:**
 - **Manual creation** is too slow and lacks diversity for large-scale learning.
 - **Procedural generation** often fails to capture the intricate realism and specific structures found in the real world, hindering its applicability for tasks like manipulation. It also lacks the ability to generate specific target environments.
 - **Purely generative 3D models** prioritize visual geometry over kinematic and dynamic properties, rendering them unsuitable for physical interaction and simulation in robotics. They often produce static meshes without functional joints.

3. **Enabling Generalizable Robotic Control:** To learn policies that can generalize across a wide range of real-world scenarios, robots need to be trained in diverse environments that accurately reflect the statistics of natural settings. URDFormer aims to achieve this by:
 - **Generating Diverse Scenes:** Leveraging the vastness of web-scale image datasets to produce a wide variety of simulation environments.
 - **Ensuring Realism:** Creating scenes realistic enough to facilitate effective real-to-sim-to-real transfer of learned policies.
 - **Providing Controllability:** Allowing for the targeted generation of simulation environments that correspond to particular real-world scenes of interest, which is crucial for fine-tuning policies for specific deployment contexts.

4. **Facilitating a Real-to-Sim-to-Real Pipeline:** A core motivation is to integrate URDFormer into a practical pipeline where a robot can perceive a real-world scene (e.g., take a picture), generate a corresponding simulation environment, train or fine-tune a control policy within that simulation (potentially with targeted randomization), and then deploy the robust policy back to the real robot. This minimizes human effort and accelerates policy development.

In essence, the researchers are motivated by the fundamental need for scalable, realistic, and controllable simulation data to unlock the full potential of data-driven robot learning, and URDFormer is their proposed solution to this critical challenge.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The URDFormer pipeline tackles the challenging problem of inferring articulated simulation environments (represented as Unified Robot Description Files, URDFs) from single real-world RGB images. Its core methodology revolves around an "inversion through synthesis" approach, which leverages large-scale generative models to create a synthetic paired dataset for supervised learning, and then uses a transformer-based network to solve the inverse problem.

The methodology can be broken down into two main pipelines:

**A. Forward Pipeline: Controlled Generation of Paired Datasets**
Since no large dataset of real-world images paired with their corresponding URDFs exists, the authors first synthesize such a dataset. This process involves:

1. **Procedural Scene and Object Generation:**
 - The first step involves procedurally generating a diverse set of synthetic 3D scenes and individual articulated objects (e.g., cabinets, ovens, fridges) in a simulation environment. These scenes are initially represented as URDFs, defining their kinematic structure (objects, parts, joints, parent-child relationships).
2. **Realistic Image Augmentation using Generative Models:**
 - **Scene-Level Dataset Generation:** For global scenes (like kitchens), a "poorly rendered" image from the simulated URDF scene (lacking realistic textures and lighting) is fed, along with a templated text prompt, into an image-and-text guided diffusion model (specifically, Stable Diffusion). This model generates a visually realistic image that largely preserves the global scene layout from the input rendering while hallucinating realistic textures and appearances. The output is a pair of (realistic RGB image, *partial* URDF label). The URDF label is considered partial because the generative model might alter some low-level details (e.g., change object categories or part counts), but the high-level object positions, bounding boxes, and spatial relationships are largely maintained.
 - **Object-Level Dataset Generation:** For individual articulated objects (e.g., a single cabinet), a more controlled approach is used due to the generative model's tendency to alter fine details. Here, objects are procedurally generated and rendered in isolation from various angles. Instead of generating entire new images, the diffusion model is used to create diverse *texture images*. These textures are then carefully overlaid onto the appropriate parts of the rendered object using perspective warping. The background is also generated using the diffusion model with masking, maintaining consistency of low-level parts. This yields pairs of (*partial* image of a single object, *complete* URDF label for that object and its parts).

This two-pronged dataset generation strategy results in two specialized datasets: `D_scene` (realistic images with high-level scene structure labels) and `D_object` (single-object images with complete low-level kinematic structure labels).

**B. Inverse Pipeline: Learning Inverse Generative Models with URDFormer**

The generated paired datasets are then used to train URDFormer, a transformer-based network, to learn the inverse mapping from RGB images to URDFs.

1. **URDFormer Architecture:**
 - URDFormer employs a Vision Transformer (ViT) backbone to extract global image features.
 - Bounding boxes of objects/parts (known during training from ground truth, and obtained via a detector at test time) are used with ROI alignment to extract localized features.
 - These features, combined with bounding box coordinate embeddings, are processed by a Transformer encoder to produce rich features for each object/part.
 - MLPs decode these features into URDF primitives: base class labels, discretized 3D positions, bounding boxes, and child/parent embeddings.
 - Hierarchical relationships are predicted using a technique from scene graph generation, computing dot products between parent and child embeddings to form an `n x n` relationship score matrix.
 - The scene-level model also incorporates learned embeddings for root objects like walls, floor, and ceiling to anchor large scene elements.
2. **Two-Stage Training and Inference:**
 - **URDFormer (Global):** Trained on `D_scene` to predict the high-level scene structure (object locations, parent relationships) from full images and detected object bounding boxes.
 - **URDFormer (Part):** Trained on `D_object` to predict the detailed kinematic structure of individual objects (base class, part-level relationships, joint types) from cropped object images and detected part bounding boxes.
 - **Test-Time Scene Generation:**
 1. Given a real-world RGB image, an off-the-shelf object detector (Grounding DINO, fine-tuned with a "model soup" approach for better accuracy) first identifies high-level object bounding boxes.
 2. These boxes and the image are fed into the URDFormer (Global) to predict the overall scene layout.
 3. For each detected object, its image region is cropped, and a second detector identifies part-level bounding boxes.
 4. These cropped regions and part boxes are fed into the URDFormer (Part) to predict the object's internal kinematic structure.
 5. The predictions are combined to construct a complete, articulated URDF for the entire scene.

**C. Robotic Control via Real-to-Simulation-to-Real Pipeline**

URDFormer's output enables a powerful real-to-sim-to-real pipeline for robot learning:

1. **Scene Generation:** From a single RGB image of a real-world deployment scene, URDFormer generates a corresponding URDF, effectively creating a "digital twin."
2. **Targeted Randomization:** The generated URDF is imported into a physics simulator (PyBullet). Instead of merely using the "digital twin," *targeted domain randomization* is applied. This involves systematically varying minor details (e.g., replacing parts with equivalents from PartNet, randomizing textures using Stable Diffusion, adjusting sizes) within the semantic configuration predicted by URDFormer. This accounts for URDFormer's prediction inaccuracies and robustifies policies against the sim2real gap. A motion planner (cuRobo) generates optimal trajectories for various tasks.
3. **Policy Synthesis:** A language-conditioned behavior cloning policy (e.g., using an M2T2-like architecture) is trained on the diverse, randomized simulation data, taking RGB point-clouds as input. Image augmentations further aid transfer.
4. **Sim-to-Real Deployment:** The trained policy operates from real-world perceptual input (RGB point-clouds) and language instructions, predicting end-effector poses for execution on a real robot (e.g., UR5, Stretch).

This comprehensive methodology, from synthetic data generation to real-world robot deployment, highlights URDFormer's holistic approach to addressing the challenges of simulation-driven robot learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The URDFormer paper presents a comprehensive set of findings demonstrating the effectiveness of their pipeline across simulation content generation and real-world robotic control.

**A. Real-world Robotic Control Experiments:**
The most compelling results come from evaluating the entire real-to-sim-to-real pipeline with physical robots (UR5 and Stretch).
- **UR5 Robot Performance:** On a UR5 robot performing various manipulation tasks (opening/closing drawers/doors, placing/fetching objects) across five diverse cabinets, the **URDFormer-TR (URDFormer with Targeted Randomization)** pipeline achieved a remarkable **78% overall success rate**, and an 85% success rate specifically for opening/closing tasks.
- **Superiority over Baselines:**
 - **OWL-ViT Baseline:** A zero-shot vision-language model, failed entirely (0% success) due to its inability to detect fine-grained, task-relevant parts (e.g., "top middle drawer," "right door").
 - **Domain Randomization (DR) Baseline:** Training on randomly generated cabinets with un-targeted randomization yielded only 18% success, significantly worse than URDFormer-TR, underscoring the benefits of grounding randomization in a real-world scene.
 - **URDFormer-ICP Baseline:** A learning-free "digital twin" approach that used URDFormer's prediction for planning but relied on ICP for pose updates, achieved 53% success. It struggled with smaller objects and complex tasks like placing/fetching, where ICP could not reliably align with the target object.
- **Stretch Robot for Multi-Step Tasks:** URDFormer was also successfully applied to a Stretch robot for multi-step tasks like "Clean Up the Table Surface" (involving opening a desk drawer, picking up an object, and placing it in the drawer), demonstrating its applicability to different robot platforms and more complex sequences of actions.
- **Robustness from Targeted Randomization:** The results explicitly showed that even with some inaccuracies in URDFormer's initial URDF predictions (highlighted in red boxes in figures), the targeted randomization strategy effectively robustified the learned policies, enabling successful zero-shot transfer to the real world.

**B. Plausibility and Accuracy of Simulation Content Generation:**
The paper rigorously evaluated URDFormer's ability to generate plausible and accurate articulated simulation assets from internet images.
- **Large-Scale Paired Dataset Creation:** The forward pipeline successfully generated a substantial paired dataset: approximately 118K image-URDF pairs for 7 categories of single articulated objects and 200K image-URDF pairs for global kitchen scenes.
- **Improved Part Detection:** A critical component for the inverse pipeline is accurate bounding box detection. By applying a "model soup" approach (averaging weights of pre-trained and fine-tuned GroundingDINO), the F1 score for part detection improved significantly to 79.7%, up from 53.4% (pretrained) and 66.2% (finetuned only).
- **Qualitative Scene and Object Reconstruction:** URDFormer qualitatively demonstrated the ability to reconstruct plausible kitchen environments and individual articulated objects (cabinets, ovens, fridges, etc.) from internet images, largely preserving their semantic and kinematic structure.
- **Ablation Study on Texture Realism:** An ablation study revealed the critical role of realistic textures in dataset generation:
 - **Global Scene Prediction:** Using textures generated by part-consistency Stable Diffusion significantly improved global mesh accuracy (identifying object types like cabinet/oven) and parent accuracy, outperforming random colors, random textures, or carefully selected textures. This highlights that visual realism in training data is essential for higher-level scene understanding.
 - **Part-Level Prediction:** For low-level part structures, texture realism had a comparatively smaller impact, sometimes even performing slightly worse than random textures. This suggests that bounding box position features are often sufficient for predicting simple part hierarchies (e.g., a small box inside a larger one is likely a drawer).
- **RealityGym Introduction:** The paper introduces "RealityGym," a new robot learning suite containing 300 objects and 50 kitchen scenes generated by URDFormer from internet images. These assets are further augmented with PartNet meshes and Stable Diffusion-generated textures to increase diversity and realism, providing a valuable resource for future research.

**C. Generalization to Diverse Objects and Scenes:**
URDFormer exhibited strong generalization capabilities:
- **New Object and Scene Categories:** The system was extended to new object categories (toilet, microwave, desk, laptop, chair) and scene categories (bedroom, bathroom, laundry room, study room) with minimal additional training data (6K examples/object, 10K examples/scene).
- **Unseen Categories:** Interestingly, URDFormer showed generalization to unseen categories like laundry and study rooms, likely due to its two-stage (global and part) training approach, as these scenes often contain objects present in other trained categories.

These findings collectively establish URDFormer as a powerful and practical solution for generating articulated simulation environments from real-world images, directly addressing a critical need for data-driven robot learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

URDFormer represents a significant advancement in the field of robot learning and simulation, with substantial potential impact across various research and industrial domains. Its significance stems from addressing the long-standing challenge of generating high-quality, physically interactive simulation environments at scale, directly from real-world observations.

**1. Enabling Scalable and Data-Efficient Robot Learning:**
The most profound impact of URDFormer is its ability to unlock vast, untapped data resources for robot learning. By converting ubiquitous real-world images (e.g., scraped from the internet) into structured, articulated simulation environments, it provides an unprecedented scale of diverse training data. This drastically reduces the reliance on costly, time-consuming, and unsafe real-world data collection, accelerating the development of more generalizable and robust robotic policies. It moves towards a future where robots can learn complex skills in environments that closely mimic their actual deployment settings, vastly expanding the scope of what robots can learn autonomously.

**2. Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap with Targeted Realism:**
URDFormer's pipeline, particularly its integration with "targeted domain randomization," is crucial for closing the notorious sim-to-real gap. Instead of generating arbitrary simulations, URDFormer creates a "digital twin" of a specific real-world scene. The subsequent targeted randomization, guided by this twin, creates a distribution of environments that are both diverse enough to foster robustness and focused enough to be relevant to the target real-world scenario. This direct link between real observation and simulation environment leads to significantly better zero-shot transfer performance, as demonstrated by the robot experiments. This capability is paramount for deploying learned policies in unpredictable real-world settings.

**3. Democratizing Robot Learning and Development:**
By automating the creation of simulation assets, URDFormer lowers the entry barrier for robotics research and development. Researchers and engineers can rapidly generate functional simulation environments for specific tasks or scenes without needing extensive CAD modeling skills or access to specialized 3D scanning equipment. This democratization could accelerate innovation by allowing more teams to experiment with complex robotic tasks and environments.

**4. Advancing Embodied AI and Perception:**
The ability to infer rich kinematic and semantic structures from raw images pushes the boundaries of embodied AI and perception systems. It implies a deeper understanding of the physical world from visual input, beyond just object detection or segmentation. This can serve as a foundation for developing more intelligent agents capable of understanding and interacting with complex, articulated objects and environments.

**5. New Research Avenues:**
URDFormer opens several promising avenues for future research:
- **Inferring Full Physical Properties:** Extending the system to predict more comprehensive physical properties (mass, friction, material properties) directly from visual cues would enhance simulation fidelity further.
- **More Complex Articulations:** Expanding the supported URDF primitives and joint types to handle even more complex objects (e.g., cars, humanoids, tools) would broaden its applicability.
- **End-to-End Learning:** Exploring more integrated, end-to-end learning frameworks that combine generative modeling and URDF prediction could streamline the pipeline and potentially improve overall consistency.
- **Semantic Scene Editing:** The framework could enable semantic editing of real-world scenes in simulation, allowing users to modify objects or layouts and immediately generate corresponding training data.
- **Expanded RealityGym:** The introduced RealityGym dataset, built using URDFormer, has the potential to become a widely adopted benchmark for robot learning, fostering standardized evaluation and accelerating community progress.

**6. Applications Across Industries:**
Beyond academic robotics, URDFormer has potential applications in various industries:
- **Manufacturing and Logistics:** Rapidly generating digital twins of factory floors or warehouses to simulate robot deployment and optimize workflows.
- **Domestic Robotics:** Training household robots in diverse home environments by simply taking pictures of users' actual homes.
- **Virtual Reality and Gaming:** Generating interactive and physically realistic environments from real-world photos for immersive experiences.

In conclusion, URDFormer addresses a fundamental and pressing need in robot learning by providing a scalable, realistic, and controllable method for generating articulated simulation environments from real-world images. Its practical utility, demonstrated through robust sim-to-real transfer, positions it as a foundational technology that will significantly influence the future of data-driven robotics and embodied AI.
