# AirDreamer: Generalist Drone Navigation with World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> AirDreamer is an end-to-end vision-based drone navigation framework that decouples environment understanding from action selection: a DreamerV3 recurrent state-space world model encodes onboard depth plus state into latents, and an actor-critic policy trained on imagined rollouts selects velocity and yaw commands under a mostly sparse reward. It reaches 59.3% success on cluttered maps, 5.3 points above the best baseline, and transfers to real quadrotors untuned.

## Problem

- Classical perception-planning pipelines depend on hand-tuned cost functions and map representations that are environment-specific, compute-heavy, and generalize poorly beyond their designed setting.
- Learning-based navigators tightly couple observations to actions, so they suffer out-of-distribution failures, need costly per-platform sim-to-real tuning, and generalize weakly to unseen layouts.
- Dense directional shaping rewards impose human navigation preferences and trap policies in local minima around large or non-convex obstacles, since detours temporarily increase goal distance.

## Method

- A ==DreamerV3== recurrent state-space model fuses convolutional depth features with symlog state embeddings into a 6144-dim deterministic plus 32x48 categorical stochastic latent, predicting reward, continuation, and reconstructions.
- An ==actor-critic policy== conditioned on the world-model latents trains purely on ==imagined rollouts==, with normalized-advantage ==REINFORCE==, an entropy bonus, and a smoothness penalty on the policy mean.
- A ==sparse reward==, goal 100 and collision -5 dominating small progress, proximity, and altitude terms, plus ==dynamics domain randomization== over mass, inertia, thrust, drag, and motor gains for tuning-free transfer.

## Results

- Across **150** runs on five cluttered maps, AirDreamer reached **59.3%** success versus **54.0%** for DepthNav, **9.3%** for NavRL, and **6.7%** for the classical EgoPlanner.
- Real quadrotor deployment cleared **six** layouts at up to **1.8 m/s**, including an unseen C-shaped trap where the drone detoured **21.3 m** over a **6 m** direct distance.
- Ablations: scaling **101.7M** to **180.7M** parameters gave no gain, removing domain randomization added **~10%** simulated success but broke real transfer, and fixing yaw sharply degraded performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learned latent world models make sparse-reward training viable, because imagined rollouts propagate terminal goal signal backward and let value estimates exceed the rollout horizon.
> - Leaving yaw and camera heading unrewarded lets active scanning and wall-following emerge, whereas hand-crafted heading objectives lock in one perceptual strategy.
> - Domain randomization costs simulated success while buying deployable policies, a trade to accept explicitly rather than optimizing simulator metrics alone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous drone navigation has experienced advancements, yet deploying aerial robots in unknown and cluttered environments remains a significant challenge. This is primarily due to the vast variability of real-world conditions and the need for reliable generalization across diverse scenarios.

Traditional drone navigation frameworks typically employ a cascaded architecture, segmenting tasks into perception, mapping, planning, and control modules. These systems often rely on predefined heuristic functions or global information, such as semantic or occupancy maps, to guide the drone. While interpretable, such classical methods are often environment-dependent, computationally intensive, and exhibit performance limitations based on the accuracy of each individual module. This modularity can hinder generalization to environments not explicitly considered during their design.

In response to these limitations, learning-based navigation methods have emerged. These approaches typically attempt to learn a direct mapping from sensor observations to actions, thereby avoiding the error accumulation inherent in modular pipelines. However, existing learning-based methods frequently encounter out-of-distribution (OOD) failures, necessitate extensive sim-to-real tuning, and struggle to generalize to novel or unseen environments. A common issue observed in these methods is their susceptibility to local minima in non-convex environments, such as those with large obstacles or complex traps, which can prevent successful task completion. Research efforts like NavRL, DepthNav, and MAVRL have attempted to address aspects of learning-based navigation, but often face challenges related to historical context, training guidance reliance, or generalization to cluttered settings.

More recently, world models have gained prominence as a paradigm for environment modeling within learning-based systems. Prediction-oriented world models primarily focus on forecasting action-conditioned future observations. While these models have demonstrated high prediction fidelity, their direct application to downstream task performance, particularly in complex navigation scenarios, has been less explored. World models have been integrated into embodied navigation and robot learning contexts, with examples such as Pathdreamer, Navigation World Models, DayDreamer, and SkyDreamer. However, these applications often involve explicit planning over predicted observations or are tailored for structured environments like race tracks.

AirDreamer addresses a gap in the existing research by focusing on end-to-end vision-based drone navigation in unknown, cluttered environments without relying on pre-built maps, global information, explicit planning over predicted observations, or restriction to structured settings. It draws inspiration from biological navigation behaviors, such as those observed in rats, which extract general patterns and imagine future rollouts to plan. By decoupling environment understanding from action selection, AirDreamer aims to enhance robustness to OOD scenarios, facilitate smoother sim-to-real transfer, and improve generalization across diverse environments, thereby advancing the capabilities of autonomous aerial robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations guiding the development of AirDreamer are multifaceted, addressing several limitations observed in prior drone navigation methodologies:

1. **Enhance Generalization to Unseen and Cluttered Environments:** A central motivation is to enable drones to navigate reliably in environments that differ significantly from their training data, including varying scene layouts and complex obstacle configurations. Previous methods, often tailored to specific environment configurations or relying on human-designed heuristics, typically demonstrate poor generalization capabilities. AirDreamer seeks to overcome this by learning a robust internal understanding of the environment.

2. **Overcome Local Minima Traps:** Both classical and many learning-based navigation techniques often fall into local optima, particularly in non-convex environments (e.g., U-shaped obstacles, large walls requiring detours). These scenarios demand long-horizon planning and non-greedy decision-making. The project aims to design a system that can effectively escape such traps, allowing for successful navigation to the goal even when immediate progress is not visible.

3. **Achieve Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A significant hurdle for learning-based robotics is the "sim-to-real gap," where policies trained in simulation fail to perform adequately on physical hardware without extensive fine-tuning. AirDreamer is motivated to achieve effective sim-to-real transfer without any tuning during deployment, which would reduce the cost and complexity of real-world application. This is addressed through techniques like domain randomization.

4. **Reduce Reliance on Hand-Crafted Rules and Dense Reward Shaping:** Traditional and some learning-based methods depend heavily on human-designed perception pipelines, predefined rules, and dense reward functions with shaping terms. These elements encode human preferences, can lead to "reward hacking," and limit the discovery of adaptive behaviors. AirDreamer aims to minimize this dependency by employing a sparse reward function, allowing emergent navigation strategies to develop naturally.

5. **Develop an End-to-End, Vision-Based Framework:** The motivation is to create a compact, self-contained navigation system that operates solely on onboard depth images and goal location. This approach eliminates the need for pre-built maps or global obstacle information, making the system suitable for unknown or dynamically changing environments and resource-constrained platforms.

6. **Decouple Environment Understanding from Action Selection:** Inspired by animal navigation, a key motivation is to build an internal model of the environment that is distinct from the policy responsible for action selection. This decoupling is hypothesized to lead to stronger robustness to out-of-distribution scenarios and improved generalization by allowing the policy to reason over a rich, internal representation rather than directly mapping raw observations to actions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AirDreamer is structured as a vision-based drone navigation framework comprising two core, complementary components: a world model and a reinforcement learning policy. This architecture is designed for end-to-end training while maintaining an architectural separation between environment understanding and action generation. The system is trained primarily with a sparse reward function.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The navigation task is formalized as a finite-horizon, partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP). The drone must navigate from a random spawn location to a random goal, maintaining altitude and avoiding obstacles, using only local observations.
- **Coordinate Systems:** A world reference frame (ENU) defines absolute positions and is used for reward computation. A body frame, fixed to the drone, defines local states and camera quantities. Observations are expressed in the body frame to encourage direction-agnostic learning.
- **State (s_t):** Includes world-frame position, linear velocity, body-to-world quaternion, body-frame angular velocity, goal position, obstacle geometry (M), and randomized drone parameters (d). Obstacle geometry and drone parameters are not directly observed by the policy.
- **Action (a_t):** Consists of a normalized body-frame velocity setpoint and a two-component yaw encoding. These commands are issued by the policy at 20 Hz and tracked by a low-level velocity controller.
- **Observation (o_t):** Comprises a 15-dimensional state vector (body-frame goal direction, horizontal/vertical goal distances, drone velocities, quaternion) and a 48x80 depth image.

#### 3.2. World Model and Policy

The core of AirDreamer involves a world model that processes multimodal observations and a policy that makes navigation decisions based on the world model's latent representation.

#### 3.3. World Model

The world model is built upon the Dreamer V3 framework. Its function is to understand environment dynamics and principles relevant to the task. It operates by encoding multimodal observations (depth images and state vectors) into compact latent states and then predicting subsequent latent states, rewards, and continuation probabilities conditioned on its memory and the current action.
- **Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM):** The RSSM is the central component, mapping POMDP inputs to Markovian latents. It consists of:
 - A **sequence model** that updates a deterministic state (h_t ∈ R^6144) using a GRU, incorporating the previous deterministic state, stochastic state, and action.
 - A **posterior** distribution (q_ϕ) that estimates the current stochastic state (z_t ∈ {0, 1}^32x48, 32 categorical variables of 48 classes) based on the deterministic state and current observation.
 - A **prior** distribution (p_ϕ) that predicts the next stochastic state (ẑ_t) from the deterministic state.
 - **Predictors:** Separate heads predict the reward (p_ϕ(r̂_t | h_t, z_t)), continuation probability (p_ϕ(ĉ_t | h_t, z_t)), and reconstruct observations (p_ϕ(ô_t | h_t, z_t)).
- **Multimodal Encoder:** A convolutional encoder processes the normalized depth image, and a dense layer processes the symlog-transformed state vector. The resulting embeddings are concatenated before feeding into the RSSM.
- **Training:** Trajectories collected through interaction are stored in a replay buffer. The world model is trained off-policy using a combined loss function:
 - `L_pred`: Negative log-likelihood of observation, reward, and continuation under the posterior latent.
 - `L_dyn`: Encourages the prior to match the posterior.
 - `L_rep`: Regularizes the posterior towards the prior.
 - During real-world execution, the posterior is used as live observations are available.

#### 3.4. Policy Agent

The policy agent, an actor-critic reinforcement learning structure, operates on the latent representation provided by the world model.
- **Actor (π_θ):** Takes the latent state (z_t) and recurrent hidden state (h_t) from the world model as input and produces an action (a_t). The actor is trained exclusively on imagined trajectories generated using the world model's prior, initialized by the posterior.
- **Critic (v_ψ):** Uses the same latent inputs to estimate the expected return (G_t), typically computed via λ-return. The critic is trained on a mixture of real and imagined trajectories.
- **Actor Loss:** Comprises three terms: a policy-gradient term with a normalized advantage, an entropy bonus for exploration, and a smoothness regularizer that penalizes large changes in the policy's mean action between consecutive steps. This smoothness loss is applied to the policy mean, following SkyDreamer, to stabilize training.
- **Long-Horizon Behavior:** The learned value function, informed by the world model, enables the estimation of future returns beyond short imagined rollouts, facilitating the agent's ability to escape local optima.

#### 3.5. Reward Function

AirDreamer employs a primarily sparse reward function to minimize reliance on human-imposed preferences and encourage emergent behaviors.
- **Sparse Terminal Rewards:**
 - `r_goal = 100`: Awarded upon reaching the goal, terminating the episode.
 - `r_collision = -5`: A one-time penalty for a lethal collision (obstacle within 0.3 m), also terminating the episode.
- **Small Auxiliary Shaping Terms:** These provide minimal stabilization for training:
 - `r_prog = d_{t-1}_{xy} - d_{t}_{xy}`: A small reward for reducing horizontal distance to the goal.
 - `r_safety = -max(0, 1 - d_obstacle)^2`: A quadratic penalty active only when an obstacle is within 1 m, designed to be bounded for narrow gaps.
 - `r_height = -(z_goal - z_drone)^2`: Encourages maintaining the goal altitude.
- **Emergent Yaw Control:** Crucially, no reward is given for camera direction or yaw angle. This design choice allows the policy to develop adaptive perceptual strategies based on task demands rather than fixed human biases. The dominant sparse goal reward propagates through imagined rollouts, incentivizing task completion even if it requires temporary detours.

#### 3.6. Sim-to-Real Transfer

To bridge the sim-to-real gap, domain randomization is applied to drone dynamics parameters during training:
- **Randomized Parameters:** Mass, inertia, thrust-to-weight ratio, force-to-moment ratio, drag coefficient, and motor spin-up/spin-down gains are independently sampled from uniform distributions at the start of each training episode. This variation helps the policy become robust to discrepancies between the simulator and the real drone's dynamics.
- **Observation Handling:** Invalid depth pixels (outside [0.6, 6.0] m) are replaced with the maximum range value (6 m) before being fed to the policy. No noise or bias is simulated in the policy's observation itself.

#### 3.7. Training Details

- **Simulator:** OmniDrones, based on Isaac Sim 4.1.0, utilizing a Hummingbird drone model with a LeePositionController. Policy executed at 20 Hz, physics at 200 Hz.
- **Environment Setup:** Randomization of spawn/goal locations, heights, obstacle dimensions, and initial yaw directions. Static obstacle positions are not randomized during training.
- **Implementation:** Developed using the JAX framework and the Dreamer V3 codebase. The training model has 101.7 million parameters, while the ONNX inference model for deployment has 57.9 million parameters.
- **Duration:** Trained for 2.5 million environment steps (approximately 34.7 hours of experience) using a replay buffer size of 1 million steps. The total training time was 38 hours and 43 minutes on a server equipped with 128GB RAM and two NVIDIA RTX 4090 D GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented extensive evaluations in both simulation and real-world environments to assess AirDreamer's performance, generalization, and sim-to-real transfer capabilities.

#### 4.1. Comparison to State-of-the-Art Methods

AirDreamer was benchmarked against established methods: DepthNav, NavRL, and EgoPlanner. Comparative success rates and average speeds were measured over 150 runs across 5 distinct maps in the XTDrone simulator.
- **Success Rate:**
 - **AirDreamer:** 59.3% ± 15.8% (average speed: 0.45 m/s)
 - **DepthNav:** 54.0% ± 13.9% (average speed: 0.48 m/s)
 - **NavRL:** 9.3% ± 3.9% (average speed: 0.31 m/s)
 - **EgoPlanner:** 6.7% ± 6.3% (average speed: 0.57 m/s)
- AirDreamer achieved a 5.3% higher success rate than DepthNav, the best baseline. Its performance was significantly higher (52.6% increase) compared to classical optimization-based methods like EgoPlanner.
- The performance advantage of AirDreamer increased with environment complexity, while most methods achieved comparable high success rates in simple environments. Classical methods exhibited low success rates due to reliance on local observations and susceptibility to local optima. NavRL's performance was constrained by its yaw control limitations, preventing discovery of non-directly visible paths. DepthNav, despite being learning-based, showed lower success rates attributed to out-of-distribution issues and the biases introduced by dense reward shaping.

#### 4.2. Real-world Performance

To evaluate robustness and sim-to-real transfer, AirDreamer was deployed on a physical quadrotor (280 mm motor-to-motor span). The drone utilized FAST-LIO2 for state estimation, a RealSense D455 for depth observations, a Pixhawk 6X Pro with PX4 v1.14.3 for low-level flight control, and a Jetson Orin NX Super (16GB) for policy execution. The policy network (57.9M parameters) achieved up to 88 Hz inference, deployed at 20 Hz.
- **Successful Navigation:** The drone successfully completed six diverse, challenging scenarios without any platform-specific parameter tuning. These included navigating cluttered environments (Forest), precise flight through narrow gaps (1m Passage), maneuvering around large obstacles (5m Wall), escaping local optima (U-shape), and generalizing to an unseen obstacle type (C-shape).
- **Emergent Behaviors:**
 - **5m Wall Scenario:** When faced with a 5-meter wall obscuring the goal, the drone initially performed a yaw maneuver to scan the environment to its left. Upon determining insufficient clearance, it then steered right. As it approached the wall, the drone slowed and exhibited a wall-following behavior. Subsequently, it actively yawed towards a cuboid obstacle behind the wall. This demonstrated emergent yaw control, developed without explicit directional guidance, enabling effective exploration and path finding in an unseen, complex scenario despite vision blockages.
 - **C-shape Obstacle Scenario:** In an environment featuring a C-shaped obstacle, an out-of-distribution scenario for the policy (only U-shapes seen during training), the drone was initially placed within the obstacle's cavity. The policy successfully escaped the trap by first flying approximately 3 meters away from the goal, thus ignoring a local optimum. Crucially, the drone actively controlled its yaw to expand its visual coverage, revealing a previously unseen passage. After escaping, the drone accelerated from 0.5 m/s to 1.7 m/s while maintaining the obstacles within its field of view. This behavior highlights the system's capacity to navigate complex traps and generalize to novel obstacle configurations.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study investigated the contribution of model capacity, domain randomization, and yaw control to AirDreamer's performance.
- **Model Capacity:** Increasing the policy network size from 101.7M to 180.7M parameters did not yield a measurable improvement in success rate, suggesting that AirDreamer's performance stems from its framework design rather than solely from raw model capacity.
- **Domain Randomization:** Disabling domain randomization resulted in an approximate 10% improvement in simulation success rate. However, this policy was found to be unsuitable for real-world deployment, confirming the trade-off between maximizing in-simulation performance and achieving robust sim-to-real transfer.
- **Yaw Control:** Fixing the drone's yaw angle substantially degraded navigation performance. This finding emphasizes that allowing the policy to control yaw is critical for the agent to adapt its perception strategy to task demands and environmental complexities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AirDreamer framework presents a significant advancement in autonomous drone navigation, offering several contributions and outlining potential impacts on future research and real-world applications.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Generalist Navigation:** AirDreamer introduces a concise, end-to-end framework capable of generalist drone navigation in unknown and cluttered environments. This approach operates without requiring global maps or predefined rules, addressing a key limitation of prior methods.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The system demonstrates state-of-the-art performance, achieving a 5.3% higher success rate than the strongest baseline in challenging simulated environments. It also successfully navigates obstacle configurations not encountered during training, where many previous methods fail.
- **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A notable achievement is the effective sim-to-real transfer without the need for platform-specific parameter tuning during deployment. This capability significantly reduces the practical barriers to deploying learning-based drone navigation systems.
- **Emergent Behaviors from Sparse Rewards:** The framework's ability to learn complex, emergent behaviors—such as active sideways scanning to compensate for a limited camera field of view, wall-following, and navigating out of local optima—from a sparse reward function is a critical finding. This suggests that the policy has internalized a meaningful understanding of depth observations and environment structure, rather than simply memorizing trajectories.
- **Decoupled Architecture:** By decoupling environment understanding (via the world model) from action generation (via the reinforcement learning policy), AirDreamer enhances robustness to out-of-distribution scenarios and improves generalization.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Extension to Complex Tasks:** The foundational framework developed in AirDreamer could be extended to more complex scenarios, including navigation with dynamic obstacles, coordination in multi-robot systems, and target tracking. The modular nature of the world model and policy could facilitate these extensions without extensive redesign.
- **Resource-Constrained Platforms:** The end-to-end neural architecture and minimal sensor requirements (onboard depth camera and goal location) make AirDreamer well-suited for deployment on resource-constrained platforms and future edge-computing hardware. This expands the feasibility of autonomous drone operations in diverse real-world settings.
- **Diverse Real-world Applications:** The capabilities demonstrated by AirDreamer have potential applications in various domains where pre-mapping is impractical or impossible. These include industrial inspection of complex structures, search and rescue operations in disaster zones, and autonomous delivery services in varied urban or rural landscapes.
- **Advancement in Learning-Based Robotics:** By demonstrating effective deep reinforcement learning with world models and sparse rewards for real-world drone navigation, this work contributes to the broader field of embodied AI and robot learning, providing a promising direction for developing more intelligent and adaptive autonomous systems.
- **Community Contribution:** The commitment to make the code publicly available is expected to support further research and development in vision-based drone navigation, fostering innovation within the academic and industrial communities.
