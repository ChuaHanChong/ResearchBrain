# Incentivizing Consistent, Effective and Scalable Reasoning Capability in Audio LLMs via Reasoning Process Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> A deep reinforcement learning framework called CESAR enables Audio Large Language Models to develop consistent and scalable reasoning capabilities by rewarding the quality of the reasoning process itself. This approach resolved the "test-time inverse scaling" phenomenon, leading to a state-of-the-art 77.10% accuracy on MMAU Test-mini, outperforming leading models and demonstrating optimal reasoning depths.

## Problem

- Audio Large Language Models (LLMs) suffer from "test-time inverse scaling," where Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting degrades rather than improves performance, especially with longer reasoning chains.
- Prior training methods, like supervised fine-tuning and outcome-only reinforcement learning, fail to cultivate genuine, consistent reasoning, leading to hallucination and illogical thought processes.
- There is a need for a framework that can transform reasoning in Audio LLMs from an unpredictable, often detrimental, phenomenon into a controllable and scalable skill.

## Method

- Introduces **CESAR**, a deep reinforcement learning framework that shifts from outcome-based to process-oriented reasoning control in Audio LLMs.
- Develops a multi-faceted reasoning process reward suite, including accuracy, format, semantic consistency, structured analytical patterns (keywords), logical rigor, domain knowledge integration, and an "overthinking" penalty.
- Employs Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and systematic data augmentation to train models, allowing them to differentiate and optimize for high-quality reasoning processes.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance with **77.10%** total accuracy on the challenging MMAU Test-mini benchmark, surpassing leading proprietary models like Gemini 2.5 Pro (**71.60%**).
- Successfully resolved the "test-time inverse scaling" phenomenon, showing a performance gain from **73.70%** (without reasoning) to **77.10%** (with reasoning) in Audio LLMs.
- Demonstrated near-human reasoning capabilities on MMSU, with an average reasoning score of **81.07%** (vs. human **86.77%**), and revealed synergistic improvements in auditory perception.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reasoning in Audio LLMs can be transformed from a liability into a performance gain through explicit, process-oriented reward mechanisms, rather than relying on unpredictable emergence.
> - Granular feedback on reasoning quality, including consistency, structure, and logical rigor, is essential for cultivating robust analytical skills and mitigating issues like hallucination and illogical thought processes.
> - "Reasoning sweet spots," or optimal reasoning depths, can be identified and leveraged to maximize performance and efficiency during inference.
