# Proprioceptive Invariant Robot State Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> The University of Michigan introduces DRIFT, a real-time, proprioceptive-only state estimation framework built on invariant Kalman filtering, providing robust and accurate orientation, position, and velocity estimates from IMU and kinematic data. This framework demonstrates improved consistency and accuracy over traditional EKF methods across various robots in challenging environments.

## Problem

- Autonomous robots require accurate, high-frequency state estimates for reliable navigation, motion planning, and control.
- Exteroceptive sensors (e.g., cameras, LiDAR) are crucial for global localization but can be severely degraded in visually challenging or featureless environments.
- Existing Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (InEKF) implementations are often application-specific, limiting their generalizability and ease of deployment across diverse robotic platforms.

## Method

- Developed **DRIFT** (Dead Reckoning In Field Time), a unified, real-time, symmetry-preserving state estimation framework based on the ==Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (InEKF)==.
- Utilizes an ==IMU strapdown model== for state propagation on ==matrix Lie Groups== and incorporates generic velocity and specific contact correction models.
- Includes optional modules like a ==self-supervised deep learning contact estimator== for robots without force sensors and a ==linear gyro filter== to improve performance with low-cost IMUs.

## Results

- The deep learning-based contact estimator achieved an average accuracy of **97-98%** across various terrains, significantly outperforming baseline methods (**71-91%**) by reducing false positive rates.
- On a Fetch wheeled robot, DRIFT with the gyro filter reduced the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of translational Relative Pose Error (RPE) to **0.0590 m/m**, outperforming MEKF (**0.0844 m/m**).
- In a long-horizon **3 km** outdoor test with a Husky robot, DRIFT showed superior dead reckoning with a final XY plane drift of **71.41 m**, significantly less than MEKF's **166.40 m**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Invariant Kalman filtering leverages Lie group theory to provide a robust state estimation framework with state-independent error dynamics, leading to improved consistency and convergence.
> - High-accuracy proprioceptive-only state estimation can serve as a dependable dead reckoning source, enabling robot autonomy in environments where exteroceptive sensors are unreliable or unavailable.
> - Augmenting the core InEKF with specialized modules like a deep learning-based contact estimator and a gyro filter effectively compensates for sensor limitations and enhances performance across diverse robotic platforms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Accurate state estimation, encompassing orientation, position, and velocity, is a fundamental capability for autonomous robots to perform tasks such as navigation and stability control. The broader research landscape for robot state estimation involves various sensor modalities and filtering techniques, each with distinct advantages and limitations.

Traditional approaches often fuse data from exteroceptive sensors like cameras and LiDARs with proprioceptive sensors such as Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) and joint encoders. Exteroceptive sensors provide global localization information but are typically low-frequency and susceptible to perceptual degradation caused by environmental factors like illumination changes, fog, or featureless surroundings. In contrast, proprioceptive sensors operate at high frequencies (100-1000 Hz or higher) and are robust to external visual conditions, making them suitable for agile navigation and challenging environments.

The Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) has been a long-standing method for fusing noisy sensor data due to its simplicity. However, EKFs struggle with highly nonlinear dynamics and can suffer from linearization errors, potentially leading to divergence or spurious correlations, especially when states evolve on manifolds rather than Euclidean spaces. Subsequent developments, such as the error-state EKF (ErEKF) and the Multiplicative EKF (MEKF), addressed some of these limitations by tracking errors or using quaternion representations for rotation, but still faced issues with state-dependent linearization or singularities.

More recently, the Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (InEKF) emerged as a symmetry-preserving approach. InEKF models state evolution on matrix Lie Groups and tracks errors on the corresponding Lie algebra. This framework benefits from a "log-linear" error dynamics property when the system dynamics satisfy a "group-affine" condition, meaning the linearization is independent of the state estimate. This property has demonstrated improved convergence and consistency in various applications, including SLAM, legged robot state estimation, visual-inertial odometry, and aerial/underwater navigation.

Despite the theoretical advantages of InEKF, current implementations tend to be application-specific, requiring significant effort to adapt them to different robotic platforms. This paper addresses this gap by developing a unified, real-time, symmetry-preserving framework for *proprioceptive-only* state estimation. The aim is to provide a robust dead reckoning system that can operate reliably in perceptually degraded environments where exteroceptive sensors may fail, thereby serving as a dependable odometry source for higher-level perception and control systems. The work also contributes to making invariant Kalman filtering more accessible to a broader range of robotics applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is the development of a real-time, invariant, proprioceptive robot state estimation framework named DRIFT (Dead Reckoning In Field Time). This framework is designed to provide robust and accurate state estimates solely from onboard inertial measurement units and robot kinematics.

The motivation for developing DRIFT is multifaceted:
- **Need for Reliable State Estimation:** Autonomous robots require accurate and high-frequency estimations of their orientation, position, and velocity for reliable navigation, motion planning, and control, especially in dynamic and challenging environments.
- **Limitations of Exteroceptive Sensing:** While exteroceptive sensors (e.g., cameras, LiDAR) are vital for global localization, their performance can be severely degraded in visually challenging conditions such as poor lighting, dense fog, sandstorms, or featureless environments. This degradation can critically impact robot autonomy.
- **Robustness of Proprioceptive Sensing:** Proprioceptive sensors (e.g., IMUs, joint encoders) offer an inherently robust sensing modality as they operate independently of external environmental factors and provide high-frequency data. Designing an accurate and real-time state estimation algorithm based purely on these sensors is therefore highly desirable for operations in extreme or perceptually degraded scenarios.
- **Generalization of Invariant Filtering:** Existing implementations of InEKF, while theoretically sound and offering superior consistency, are often specialized for particular applications. This makes them cumbersome to adapt and deploy across diverse robotic platforms. A key motivation for DRIFT is to provide a unified, modular, and open-source library that is readily applicable to a wide variety of robot types without extensive modification.
- **Support for Low-Cost Robotics:** Many robotic platforms, particularly low-cost ones, may lack advanced sensors or robust contact detection capabilities. DRIFT aims to enhance the performance of such robots by incorporating optional modules like a contact estimator (for legged robots without force sensors) and a gyro filter (for low-cost IMUs) to compensate for sensor limitations.
- **Foundation for Dead Reckoning:** The framework is intended to serve as a high-accuracy, local state tracking source (dead reckoning) for other robot systems (e.g., visual SLAM, control) that may need to operate without global perceptual inputs for periods.

Specifically, the work aims to deliver the following contributions:
- An open-source, real-time, symmetry-preserving robot state estimation library capable of running on various robotic platforms.
- A didactic introduction to invariant Kalman filtering to enhance accessibility for a wider robotics community.
- A proprioceptive state estimation framework for dead reckoning that utilizes IMU and kinematic data, complemented by optional contact estimation and gyro filtering modules for low-cost robots.
- Extensive experimental validation through real-world deployments on legged robots, indoor and outdoor wheeled robots, full-size vehicles, and simulation with marine robots to thoroughly assess the framework's performance and limitations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed framework, DRIFT, is built upon the Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (InEKF) theory, which leverages Lie groups to model robot state evolution and Lie algebras to track estimation errors.

**A. Invariant Kalman Filtering Fundamentals:**
The paper provides a didactic overview of invariant Kalman filtering, explaining how state evolution on matrix Lie Groups and error tracking on corresponding Lie algebras lead to robust state estimation. A key concept is the "group affine" property, which ensures "log-linear" error dynamics—meaning the error propagation is independent of the system trajectory. This allows for linearization matrices (A and H) that are state-independent, improving filter consistency. Both Left-Invariant and Right-Invariant EKF propagation and update equations are formally derived, including noise modeling and measurement Jacobian definitions. The paper also details how to seamlessly switch between left and right invariant error representations using the adjoint map.

**B. State Representation:**
The robot's state, crucial for navigation, is represented as an element of a direct isometries group SE$_{l+2}$(3), where 'l' denotes the number of augmented vectors. This state includes the robot's orientation (rotation matrix R), velocity (v), position (p) in the world frame, and augmented vectors (d_l) such as contact foot positions for legged robots or landmark positions. For minimal setups like wheeled or marine robots, this typically reduces to SE$_2$(3).

**C. IMU Propagation:**
DRIFT utilizes an IMU strapdown model for state propagation, which simplifies the process by integrating IMU measurements (angular velocity $\tilde{\omega}$ and linear acceleration $\tilde{a}$) without requiring complex robot dynamic models. IMU measurements are assumed to be corrupted by additive white Gaussian noise.
1. **Continuous Dynamics:** The continuous dynamics for R, v, p, and augmented contact positions are formulated. This formulation is shown to satisfy the group-affine property, leading to state-independent linearized error dynamics, $A_r$, for right-invariant error propagation.
2. **Discrete Integration:** For practical application with discrete IMU samples, closed-form solutions are employed for the exponential map and integrals of rotation matrices (Γ functions) to propagate the mean state. Covariance is propagated using a discretized state transition matrix.
3. **Bias Augmentation:** To account for slow-varying IMU biases, these are augmented as additional states in the system. While this augmentation technically breaks the strict group-affine property, the resulting "imperfect InEKF" still demonstrates superior performance compared to traditional EKF methods. Biases are modeled as random walks.

**D. Correction Models:**
1. **Velocity Correction:** This generic module applies to diverse robotic platforms. It utilizes ground-referenced body velocity measurements. For wheeled robots, pseudo-measurements are derived from wheel encoders combined with nonholonomic constraints (assuming no lateral slip or vertical movement). A right-invariant measurement model is formulated, and the corresponding measurement Jacobian (H) is derived. Observability analysis confirms that roll, pitch, and velocity are observable, but yaw remains unobservable with this correction model.
2. **Contact Correction (for Legged Robots):** For legged robots, contact points provide strong constraints by assuming zero foot velocity in the world frame during contact. When a contact is detected, the contact foot's position is augmented into the state using forward kinematics and the current pose estimate. The covariance is updated accordingly. When the foot lifts off, the contact state is marginalized. A right-invariant correction model is used, with the measurement derived from new encoder readings.

**E. Optional Modules for Low-Cost Robots:**
1. **Contact Estimator:** A self-supervised, lightweight neural network is introduced for robots lacking dedicated foot contact sensors.
 - **Input:** Multi-modal proprioceptive data including joint encoders, IMU readings, and kinematics (foot positions and velocities) over a temporal window.
 - **Output:** A classification of the robot's contact configuration (e.g., which feet are in contact) by mapping the binary contact state vector to an integer.
 - **Architecture:** Comprises convolutional blocks (1D convolutions, max pooling, ReLU) for feature extraction, followed by fully connected layers with dropout to prevent overfitting. Cross-entropy loss is used for training.
 - **Training:** Uses a specialized dataset collected from an MIT Mini Cheetah on various terrains, with ground truth labels generated offline by analyzing foot height signals and applying low-pass filters to mitigate artifacts like foot bouncing.
2. **Gyro Filter:** A linear Kalman filter designed to fuse angular velocity measurements from multiple sources (e.g., multiple IMUs or IMU with encoders) to mitigate issues with inaccurate yaw estimation from low-cost IMUs. Its state consists of angular velocity and gyro biases. Propagation assumes slow-varying biases, while correction uses a linear measurement model from the second angular velocity source.

**F. Software Implementation:**
DRIFT is implemented as an open-source C++ library, with an optional Robot Operating System (ROS) wrapper to facilitate real-time communication and integration with existing robotics software stacks. The modular design allows for easy expansion with new sensor modalities or filtering techniques.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experimental validation across a range of robotic platforms and simulation environments, demonstrating the performance and capabilities of DRIFT.

**A. Contact Estimation:**
The proposed deep learning-based contact estimator was evaluated using an MIT Mini Cheetah dataset across concrete, grass, and forest terrains.
- It achieved an average accuracy of 98.18% on concrete, 97.78% on grass, and 97.08% on forest, significantly outperforming baseline methods like GRF thresholding (average 71-82%) and gait cycle (average 83-91%).
- The method demonstrated significantly lower false positive rates (FPRs) compared to baselines (e.g., 1.45% vs. 37.07% for GRF thresholding on concrete), which is crucial for preventing biased measurements in state estimation.

**B. Legged Robot (MIT Mini Cheetah):**
Experiments with the Mini Cheetah on an outdoor grassy field, with ground truth provided by a motion capture system, showed that DRIFT coupled with the proposed contact estimator produced trajectories that aligned more closely with the ground truth. Baseline contact estimation methods led to noticeable drift in the height (z-axis) estimation, which the authors attribute to their higher false positive rates. This confirms the efficacy of DRIFT for accurate legged robot state estimation, especially for robots without dedicated contact sensors.

**C. Indoor Wheeled Robot (Fetch Robot):**
DRIFT was deployed on a Fetch robot in a lab environment and compared against a Multiplicative EKF (MEKF).
- Both DRIFT and MEKF experienced significant yaw drift due to the low-cost IMU on the Fetch robot.
- The inclusion of the optional gyro filter in DRIFT substantially improved trajectory estimation, particularly by correcting yaw angle inaccuracies.
- DRIFT produced smoother estimated trajectories compared to MEKF.
- Quantitatively, DRIFT consistently outperformed MEKF in terms of the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of Relative Pose Error (RPE) (drift per meter). For translational RMSE, DRIFT averaged 0.0692 m/m compared to MEKF's 0.0844 m/m. With the gyro filter, DRIFT's translational RMSE further improved to 0.0590 m/m.

**D. Outdoor Wheeled Robot (Clearpath Robotics Husky):**
Experiments were conducted with a Husky robot equipped with IMUs and wheel encoders, including short-horizon tests with motion capture and a long-horizon test (3 km over 55 minutes) on a sidewalk using GPS as a ground truth proxy.
- In short-horizon tests, DRIFT averaged better RPE performance than MEKF (translational RPE: 0.0701 m/m vs. 0.0747 m/m; rotational RPE: 1.6888 °/m vs. 2.0485 °/m).
- For the long-horizon experiment, DRIFT showed significantly superior dead reckoning capabilities, with a final drift in the XY plane of 71.41 m, compared to 166.40 m for MEKF. This highlights DRIFT's robustness for prolonged operations using only proprioceptive data.
- Estimated velocity from DRIFT converged to the true velocity, consistent with the theoretical observability analysis.

**E. Off-road Vehicle (Polaris MRZR):**
DRIFT was evaluated on a full-size military test vehicle in a featureless forest environment, using GPS as an approximate ground truth.
- DRIFT consistently achieved lower drift percentages (average 3.18%) compared to MEKF (average 12.3%) over trajectories exceeding 1 km in length.
- This demonstrates DRIFT's ability to provide accurate odometry for full-size vehicles in challenging, featureless off-road conditions, relying solely on an IMU and shaft encoder.

**F. Marine Robot (IQUA Robotics Girona500 AUV - Simulation):**
Simulation experiments in Stonefish software with an AUV equipped with IMU and Doppler Velocity Log (DVL) showed that both DRIFT and MEKF performed well.
- DRIFT exhibited slightly better position estimation in the x and y axes, as well as improved roll and pitch estimation.
- The roll and pitch estimations converged to true values after perturbations, which aligns with the theoretical observability analysis for velocity correction.

**G. Runtime Analysis:**
Runtime evaluations on a personal laptop CPU (Intel i5-11400H) and an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Xavier (CPU-only) confirmed DRIFT's real-time capabilities.
- InEKF propagation and correction steps completed within microseconds (e.g., propagation ~11-22 µs, correction ~9-29 µs).
- The optional contact estimator achieved inference speeds of approximately 1100 Hz on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU and 830 Hz on a Jetson AGX Xavier GPU, further ensuring real-time operation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of DRIFT represents a notable advancement in robot state estimation, particularly in its focus on robust proprioceptive dead reckoning using symmetry-preserving filtering. The significance and potential impact of this work are several-fold:

- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy in Challenging Environments:** DRIFT provides a reliable odometry source that operates independently of external perceptual data. This is critical for robots deployed in environments where exteroceptive sensors (e.g., cameras, LiDAR, GPS) are prone to failure or degradation, such as underwater, underground, in dense fog, dust storms, featureless terrains, or GPS-denied areas. This capability can extend the operational envelope for robots in applications like planetary exploration, search and rescue, logistics, and military operations.
- **Increased Accessibility of Invariant Kalman Filtering:** By offering a didactic introduction to invariant Kalman filtering and releasing an open-source, modular library (DRIFT), the authors aim to lower the barrier to entry for researchers and developers to utilize these advanced, consistency-preserving estimation techniques. This could lead to broader adoption and innovation within the robotics community.
- **Versatility Across Robotic Platforms:** The extensive experimental validation across diverse platforms—including legged robots, indoor wheeled robots, outdoor field robots, full-size military vehicles, and marine robots—demonstrates the generalizability and robustness of the DRIFT framework. This addresses a common limitation of existing InEKF implementations, which are often highly tailored to specific applications.
- **Support for Low-Cost Systems:** The optional contact estimator and gyro filter modules are designed to enhance the performance of robots equipped with lower-cost sensors or lacking specialized contact force feedback. This contributes to making advanced state estimation more accessible and effective for a wider range of robotic hardware, potentially reducing overall system costs without compromising reliability.
- **Improved Accuracy and Consistency:** Leveraging the theoretical advantages of invariant Kalman filtering, DRIFT consistently demonstrates superior accuracy and smoother trajectory estimates compared to traditional EKF or MEKF approaches, especially over longer trajectories. This improved performance provides a more dependable foundation for downstream control and navigation tasks.
- **Foundation for Future Research and Integration:** DRIFT is positioned as a powerful odometry module that can be integrated into larger localization or SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping) systems. Its real-time performance and robustness make it an ideal complement to visual or LiDAR-based SLAM, particularly when those systems temporarily lose tracking due to perceptual challenges. Future work outlined by the authors, such as extending to equivariant filters or integrating with symmetry-preserving perception and control algorithms, further highlights its potential as a foundational tool for advanced robotics research.
