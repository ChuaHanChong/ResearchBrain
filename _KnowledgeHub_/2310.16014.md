# Human-in-the-Loop Task and Motion Planning for Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A system called HITL-TAMP integrates Task and Motion Planning with human teleoperation and imitation learning to efficiently teach robots complex, contact-rich manipulation skills. This approach enables over 3x faster data collection and leads to highly performant policies on challenging tasks in real and simulated environments.

## Problem

- Scaling imitation learning for complex, long-horizon, and real-world robot manipulation tasks is inefficient due to extensive data collection requirements.
- Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) struggles with contact-rich interactions and low-tolerance tasks, often requiring unavailable precise models and perception.
- A gap exists between the strengths of automated planning and learning from human demonstrations, hindering the development of robust and scalable robot skills.

## Method

- A ==TAMP-gated control== mechanism intelligently switches control between an automated ==Task and Motion Planning (TAMP)== planner for structured segments and a human ==teleoperator== for challenging, contact-rich interactions.
- ==Action schemas== and critical constraints for human-executed segments are learned from a small number of human demonstrations, enabling TAMP to plan for human intervention.
- A novel ==asynchronous queueing system== maximizes data collection efficiency, allowing a single human to supervise multiple robot sessions in parallel.

## Results

- HITL-TAMP achieved over **3x** faster data collection rates than conventional teleoperation, with policies trained on this data showing significantly higher success rates (e.g., **90.7%** vs. **24.4%** on Coffee).
- The system enabled non-expert operators to collect high-quality data, leading to performant policies with reduced perceived workload for users.
- TAMP-gated agents achieved near-perfect success rates (up to **100%**) across **9** complex simulation tasks and demonstrated robust real-world performance on physical robots (e.g., **74%** on Coffee, **64%** on Tool Hang).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intelligently combining symbolic Task and Motion Planning with data-driven imitation learning addresses the limitations of each paradigm for complex robot manipulation.
> - Human intervention for data collection is most effective and efficient when targeted only at the specific, contact-rich, or low-tolerance segments of a task.
> - Deploying learned policies within a TAMP-gated control loop fundamentally contributes to robust task execution, even with policies trained on full task demonstrations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation is grappling with the challenge of enabling robots to perform complex, long-horizon tasks, especially those involving contact-rich interactions or requiring high precision. Two dominant paradigms have emerged: Imitation Learning (IL) and Task and Motion Planning (TAMP).

Imitation Learning, particularly with deep neural networks, has shown great promise in teaching robots intricate manipulation skills by learning directly from human demonstrations. Approaches like those in have demonstrated impressive results. However, a major bottleneck remains: scaling IL to real-world, long-horizon tasks is time-consuming and labor-intensive due to the vast amount of data required and the difficulty of collecting comprehensive demonstrations for complex scenarios. Furthermore, not all parts of a complex task are equally difficult; free-space movements, for instance, are relatively straightforward, while delicate insertions or object handling are much harder to demonstrate and learn.

Conversely, Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) systems excel at solving long-horizon problems by decomposing them into a sequence of primitive skills and planning collision-free motions. TAMP is highly automated and provides strong guarantees for symbolic sub-goals. However, TAMP methods traditionally struggle with contact-rich tasks and situations requiring precise interaction because they rely on accurate dynamics models and precise perception, which are often unavailable or difficult to engineer for real-world scenarios. Integrating learning-based components into TAMP has been explored, but these often still require hand-crafted skills or extensive simulation-based training.

This paper, "Human-In-The-Loop Task and Motion Planning (HITL-TAMP)," positions itself precisely at this intersection, aiming to combine the strengths of both IL and TAMP while mitigating their individual weaknesses. It addresses the critical need for efficient data collection in IL and the practical limitations of TAMP in complex physical interactions. By proposing a symbiotic system that intelligently gates control between automated planning and human teleoperation, HITL-TAMP seeks to make data collection for complex tasks significantly more efficient and the resulting learned policies more robust and scalable. It stands as a notable contribution to the growing body of work on hybrid human-robot collaboration and learning, where human expertise is strategically integrated to enhance robot autonomy and capability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel system that effectively combines the benefits of Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) with human teleoperation and imitation learning to teach robots complex, long-horizon, and contact-rich manipulation skills.

The motivations driving this work stem directly from the limitations of existing approaches:
- **Scaling Imitation Learning:** Traditional imitation learning from human demonstrations is highly effective for specific skills but struggles with scaling to long-horizon, real-world tasks due to the prohibitive time and labor required for data collection. The authors aim to significantly increase the efficiency of data collection.
- **Tackling Contact-Rich Tasks with TAMP:** TAMP systems are excellent for structured, long-horizon planning but falter when precise models of contact dynamics or environment interactions are unavailable or difficult to hand-engineer. This limits their applicability to contact-rich tasks like inserting a spring-loaded lid or aligning a tool. The goal is to augment TAMP's capabilities in these difficult-to-model segments.
- **Bridging the Gap between Planning and Learning:** The paper is motivated by the desire to create a synergistic system where TAMP handles the easily automatable portions (e.g., free-space motion, object transportation) while learning from human demonstrations addresses the parts that are difficult for TAMP (e.g., contact-rich interactions, fine-tuned placements).
- **Maximizing Human Data Collection Efficiency:** A key motivation is to enable human teleoperators to manage a fleet of robots, maximizing data throughput by only requiring human intervention for specific, challenging skill segments, rather than full task demonstrations. This reduces human effort and boredom.
- **Training Superior Policies:** The ultimate motivation is to develop a TAMP-gated policy that leverages this hybrid data collection, leading to superior performance in terms of data requirements and learned policy success rates compared to policies trained on full task demonstrations.

In essence, HITL-TAMP seeks to create a more practical and scalable paradigm for robot skill acquisition by intelligently allocating control between an automated planner and a human supervisor, thereby reducing both human effort and computational complexity while improving robot performance on challenging tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HITL-TAMP employs a hybrid approach that synergistically combines the strengths of Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) with human teleoperation and imitation learning. The core of its methodology revolves around a "TAMP-gated control mechanism" and a novel data collection pipeline.

#### 3.1. TAMP-Gated Control Mechanism

The system selectively gives and takes control between a TAMP system and a human teleoperator.
- **TAMP's Role:** The TAMP system, based on the PDDLStream framework, is responsible for planning the overall long-horizon task. It breaks down the task into a sequence of actions, identifying which segments can be automated (e.g., free-space movements, object transportation) and which require human intervention due to their contact-rich or low-tolerance nature. TAMP executes its planned motions using a joint position controller.
- **Human's Role:** When TAMP encounters a segment it cannot productively model or execute (e.g., precise insertion, opening a drawer), it prompts a human teleoperator to take control. The human operates the robot's end effector using a smartphone interface and an Operational Space Controller (OSC), providing full 6-DoF control.
- **Seamless Handoff:** The TAMP system continuously monitors the environment. Once the human teleoperator successfully completes their segment and the predefined postconditions (effects) of the human action are met, control seamlessly switches back to the TAMP system, which then re-plans and continues the task.

#### 3.2. Teleoperation Action Modeling and Constraint Learning

To enable TAMP to plan for human intervention, an approximate model of the teleoperation process is needed.
- **Action Schema:** For skills requiring human input (e.g., `attach` action for insertion or hanging), a high-level action schema is defined. This schema specifies logical preconditions (what must be true before the human acts) and postconditions (what will be true after the human acts).
- **Constraint Learning:** Critical constraints within these action schemas, such as `AttachGrasp` (a suitable grasp for attachment) and `PreAttach` (the desired relative pose between objects just before contact), are learned from a *small number* (e.g., ~3) of human demonstrations. Instead of explicitly modeling these complex dynamics, the system learns statistical distributions of successful pre-contact states from human examples. This allows TAMP to plan to states that are conducive for the human to take over and succeed. For example, `PreAttach(o, p, o')` is true if `p` is a pose of object `o` relative to `o'` immediately prior to the human achieving the attachment. These are logged from demonstrations by searching backward from the `GoodAttach` state until objects are sufficiently apart.

#### 3.3. Scaling Data Collection with a Queueing System

Recognizing that humans only intervene for short segments, HITL-TAMP introduces a novel queueing system to maximize data throughput.
- **Asynchronous Operation:** Multiple robot processes (a "fleet") run asynchronously. Each robot is either controlled by TAMP, waiting for human control, or being controlled by the human.
- **Human Operator Management:** When a robot requires human input, its session is enqueued. A single human operator interacts with a queueing interface, taking control of one robot at a time. Once a human segment is complete, TAMP resumes control of that robot, and the human can dequeue the next waiting session. This allows the human to supervise multiple data collection sessions in parallel, significantly boosting efficiency.

#### 3.4. TAMP-Gated Policy Deployment

- **Policy Training:** After data collection, imitation learning (specifically, Behavioral Cloning with BC-RNN) is used to train policies on the human-controlled segments of the collected demonstrations. These policies learn to execute the contact-rich skills previously performed by humans.
- **Autonomous Deployment:** In deployment, the TAMP-gated control loop is identical to the data collection phase, but the trained policy replaces the human teleoperator for the designated "human" segments. This creates a fully autonomous, hierarchical agent capable of solving the entire long-horizon task. The system is validated in both low-dimensional state observations (ground-truth object poses) and image-based observations (RGB camera feeds).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the significant advantages of HITL-TAMP in terms of data collection efficiency, policy learning performance, and real-world applicability.

#### 4.1. System Evaluation: User Study (15 Participants)

- **Increased Data Collection Rate:** Participants collected **over 3x more demonstrations** with HITL-TAMP compared to a conventional teleoperation system given the same time budget (e.g., 4.5x more on Square Broad, 2.5x on Coffee, nearly 2x on Three Piece Assembly Broad). This translates to a much higher throughput of valuable training data.
- **Performant Policies from Minutes of Data:** Policies trained on just 10 minutes of per-user HITL-TAMP data vastly outperformed those from conventional teleoperation, achieving significantly higher average success rates (e.g., 90.7% vs. 24.4% on Coffee, 80.0% vs. 1.2% on Square Broad).
- **Robustness to Non-Experts and Multi-User Data:** HITL-TAMP enabled non-expert operators to collect high-quality data, leading to performant policies (e.g., 90.0% success on Coffee for novice-trained agents vs. 15.0% for conventional). Furthermore, policies trained on the full multi-user HITL-TAMP dataset achieved high success rates (100.0%, 98.0%, 66.0% on Coffee, Square Broad, and Three Piece Assembly Broad, respectively), outperforming multi-user conventional data.
- **Reduced Perceived Workload:** A NASA-TLX survey revealed that users found HITL-TAMP to require significantly less mental, physical, and temporal demand, while enabling higher performance and causing less frustration compared to conventional teleoperation.

#### 4.2. Learning Results (Across 12 Tasks in Simulation)

- **Broad Applicability and Near-Perfect Agents:** Using HITL-TAMP, a single human operator collected 200 demonstrations per task across 9 complex, contact-rich, long-horizon tasks, including variants with broad object initializations (e.g., Coffee Preparation, Tool Hang Broad). The trained TAMP-gated agents, using both low-dimensional and image-based observations, often achieved near-perfect success rates (100% on Square, Coffee, Three Piece Assembly, and their broad variants; 96-100% on Coffee Preparation).
- **Favorable Comparison to Conventional Teleoperation:** Even with an equal number of demonstrations (200), policies learned from HITL-TAMP data consistently outperformed those from conventional teleoperation. For instance, on Square Broad, HITL-TAMP policies achieved 100% success rate compared to 15.3% for conventional methods using image observations, while requiring significantly less operator time for data collection.
- **Crucial Role of TAMP-Gated Control:** A key finding was that even when conventional teleoperation data (full task demonstrations) was used, deploying the learned policies with TAMP-gated control during evaluation dramatically increased their success rates, often making them comparable to policies trained on HITL-TAMP data (e.g., Square Broad policy success rate increased from 15.3% to 94.7% with TAMP-gating). This highlights the fundamental importance of the TAMP-gated deployment strategy in handling the "easy" parts of the task.

#### 4.3. Real Robot Validation

- **Robust Real-World Performance:** HITL-TAMP was successfully applied to a physical robot setup on tasks like Stack Three, Coffee, Coffee Broad, and Tool Hang. Despite using a simple perception pipeline (RANSAC, DBSCAN, ICP) and not assuming real-time object tracking, the TAMP-gated agents achieved strong success rates (e.g., 74% on Coffee, 64% on Tool Hang). This is particularly noteworthy for Tool Hang, which achieved 64% with HITL-TAMP compared to 3% from 200 human demonstrations in prior work, showcasing the system's robustness to perception noise and its effectiveness in challenging real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HITL-TAMP system represents a significant step forward in robot learning and manipulation, addressing critical bottlenecks that have hindered the deployment of intelligent robots in complex real-world environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Accelerated Robot Skill Acquisition:** By dramatically increasing the efficiency of data collection (over 3x faster than conventional methods), HITL-TAMP directly tackles the primary bottleneck in scaling imitation learning. This allows for the rapid creation of large, high-quality datasets for diverse, complex manipulation tasks, potentially reducing the development cycles for new robotic applications.
- **Enabling Robust Performance on Contact-Rich Tasks:** The ability to effectively handle contact-rich and low-tolerance tasks, where traditional TAMP struggles, is a major breakthrough. By selectively deferring these challenging segments to human teleoperators (and subsequently to learned policies), HITL-TAMP expands the range of tasks that robots can autonomously perform with high reliability. The strong real-world validation, even with approximate perception, underscores this robustness.
- **Lowering the Barrier to Entry for Robot Programming:** HITL-TAMP makes it easier for non-expert users to teach robots complex skills. The user study demonstrated that even novices could efficiently collect high-quality data, requiring less mental and physical effort. This democratizes robot programming, moving it from requiring specialized roboticists to enabling a broader base of users.
- **Scalable Hybrid Autonomy:** The novel queueing system allows a single human operator to manage multiple robots asynchronously, transforming data collection from a one-robot-one-human process into a fleet-management operation. This is crucial for scaling up robot deployments in industrial or service settings, where a human supervisor can oversee several robots simultaneously.
- **Advancement in Human-Robot Collaboration:** HITL-TAMP exemplifies a sophisticated human-in-the-loop paradigm, where human intelligence and dexterity are leveraged precisely where they are most needed, while automation handles the rest. This intelligent division of labor represents a powerful model for future human-robot collaboration, where robots augment human capabilities rather than simply replacing them.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work lays a strong foundation for several future research directions. These include automating the identification of "difficult" TAMP segments, integrating more sophisticated perception uncertainty estimates into TAMP, extending the framework to more diverse object sets and environments, and exploring how the learned policies for human segments could potentially inform or improve the TAMP planning itself (e.g., learning full TAMP operators without explicit modeling).

In conclusion, HITL-TAMP offers a practical, efficient, and scalable solution for teaching robots complex manipulation skills. By intelligently blending the strengths of symbolic planning and data-driven learning, it paves the way for more capable, autonomous, and widely deployable robotic systems in real-world scenarios.
