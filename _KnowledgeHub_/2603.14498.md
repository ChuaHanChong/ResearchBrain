# R3DP: Real-Time 3D-Aware Policy for Embodied Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and collaborators developed R3DP (Real-Time 3D-Aware Policy), a framework that integrates powerful 3D priors from large vision foundation models into real-time embodied manipulation policies. R3DP achieved an average success rate of 69.0% on 10 RoboTwin benchmark tasks, outperforming multi-view Diffusion Policy by 51.4%, and demonstrated efficient real-world performance with a 62.18 ms observation encoding latency on an RTX 4090 GPU.

## Problem

- Existing visuomotor imitation learning policies often rely on 2D visual inputs, lacking explicit 3D scene understanding crucial for contact-rich manipulation and precise spatial reasoning.
- Large-scale 3D vision foundation models offer strong 3D priors but their high computational cost prevents real-time integration into robotic control loops, which require low latency.
- Current multi-view policy designs frequently fuse camera inputs heuristically without leveraging explicit camera geometry, leading to inconsistent 3D representations and underutilization of geometric cues.

## Method

- R3DP introduces an Asynchronous Fast–Slow Collaboration (AFSC) module, using a 'slow' large 3D foundation model for sparse key frames and a 'fast' Temporal Feature Prediction Network (TFPNet) for real-time feature propagation.
- A lightweight Temporal Feature Prediction Network (TFPNet) is pre-trained to distill 3D-aware features from the large foundation model, enabling consistent and efficient temporal context modeling for intermediate frames.
- The Multi-View Feature Fuser (MVFF) incorporates Projective Positional Encoding (PRoPE) to explicitly leverage camera intrinsics and extrinsics, achieving geometry-aware and consistent multi-view feature aggregation.

## Results

- R3DP achieved an average success rate of 69.0% on the 10 RoboTwin simulation tasks, showing significant improvements over baselines, including a 51.4% gain over DP-multi and 11.4% over DP3.
- In real-world experiments on a bimanual robotic system, R3DP outperformed DP and DP3 by 40.9% and 23.3% average success rates respectively, demonstrating robustness to real-world sensor noise.
- The framework maintained real-time efficiency with an observation encoding latency of 40.3 ms in simulation (with τ=8τ=8) and 62.18 ms on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, while offering configurable efficiency via the key frame interval τ with minimal performance impact.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Powerful 3D priors can be efficiently incorporated into real-time policies by asynchronously updating high-fidelity 3D features from a slow foundation model and propagating them with a fast prediction network.
> - Distilling knowledge from a large 3D vision foundation model into a smaller temporal prediction network enables effective real-time feature estimation and consistent temporal context understanding.
> - Explicitly integrating camera geometric parameters (intrinsics and extrinsics) into multi-view feature fusion significantly enhances spatial consistency and robustness in complex 3D manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied manipulation tasks, particularly those requiring contact-rich actions, necessitate an accurate understanding of objects' 3D geometry and their spatial relationships. The broader research landscape in visuomotor imitation learning has seen significant advancements, with many methods primarily relying on 2D visual inputs. While effective for numerous tasks, these 2D-based approaches often lack an explicit, native 3D representation of the scene, which can limit performance in tasks requiring intricate spatial reasoning, depth understanding, or handling occlusions. Furthermore, these methods frequently struggle to model temporal context efficiently, leading to inconsistent 3D understanding over time.

To compensate for the absence of 3D awareness, some prior works have augmented RGB observations with additional modalities like depth maps or point clouds. These methods have demonstrated improved manipulation performance by incorporating 3D information. However, they introduce dependencies on extra sensors or calibrated setups, increasing system complexity and vulnerability to noise on challenging surfaces (e.g., transparent, reflective, or textureless objects), thereby limiting their practical deployment.

Concurrently, large-scale 3D vision foundation models have emerged, offering a promising avenue to extract 3D priors directly from RGB inputs, thus potentially circumventing the need for specialized depth sensors. Despite their capabilities, these models typically incur high computational costs, making per-frame inference too slow for real-time robotic control, especially in multi-view configurations. Attempts to integrate these 3D priors into policies have faced challenges such as feature magnitude mismatch (scale shifting) and prohibitive latency. Moreover, multi-view information is often fused heuristically without explicitly encoding camera intrinsics or extrinsics, underutilizing geometric cues.

R3DP positions itself within this landscape by addressing the critical challenge of integrating powerful, but computationally intensive, 3D foundation model priors into real-time manipulation policies. It seeks to provide 3D-aware control that is efficient, robust to varying sensor conditions, and capable of explicit temporal and multi-view reasoning, thereby bridging the gap between advanced 3D perception and the practical demands of embodied robot control. The work builds upon diffusion policies, a modern imitation learning paradigm, by enhancing its perception module.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the R3DP research are motivated by the limitations of current visuomotor imitation learning methods, particularly concerning 3D scene understanding and real-time performance.

The key objectives and their underlying motivations are:

1. **Integrate Powerful 3D Priors into Manipulation Policies**:
 - **Motivation**: Traditional 2D image-based policies often lack an explicit understanding of scene geometry, making them less effective in tasks requiring precise spatial reasoning or interaction with objects in complex 3D environments. Recent large-scale 3D vision foundation models (e.g., VGGT) can extract rich 3D information from RGB images, potentially endowing policies with robust spatial perception without requiring additional depth sensors.
 - **Objective**: Develop a framework that effectively leverages these 3D priors to enhance the policy's understanding of objects and their spatial relations, leading to more accurate and robust manipulation.

2. **Achieve Real-Time Performance for Robot Control**:
 - **Motivation**: While large 3D foundation models offer strong priors, their high computational cost typically results in prohibitive inference latency. Real-time robot control requires low-latency policy execution (e.g., typically >10-20 Hz) to react dynamically to changes in the environment and execute contact-rich actions effectively. A direct integration of these models at every frame would render real-time control unfeasible.
 - **Objective**: Design an architecture that integrates these powerful 3D priors without compromising the real-time performance essential for embodied manipulation. This involves developing mechanisms to efficiently propagate 3D-aware features across frames.

3. **Effectively Model Temporal Context**:
 - **Motivation**: Embodied manipulation tasks are inherently sequential, and interaction trajectories contain rich temporal cues about object motion, contacts, and occlusions. Existing methods often treat temporal context implicitly (e.g., via recurrent modules or memory), which can make it difficult to enforce spatial-temporal consistency or maintain a stable 3D understanding over extended periods.
 - **Objective**: Incorporate explicit temporal modeling to exploit correlations between historical observations and current states, thereby improving the consistency of feature estimation and overall task success rates over long horizons.

4. **Enhance Multi-View Feature Fusion with Camera Geometry**:
 - **Motivation**: In multi-camera setups, different viewpoints provide complementary information. However, many existing policies combine multi-view inputs via simple concatenation, failing to leverage the underlying camera geometry (intrinsics and extrinsics). This implicit fusion can lead to inconsistent 3D representations across views, especially in environments with occlusions or complex layouts.
 - **Objective**: Develop a dedicated module that explicitly aggregates features across multiple views by incorporating ground-truth camera parameters. This aims to form a consistent 3D-aware representation, improving robustness and spatial understanding in complex multi-view scenarios.

In summary, R3DP aims to create a "plug-and-play" solution that enables embodied manipulation policies to combine the strong 3D spatial understanding derived from large foundation models with the agility and responsiveness required for real-time robotic interaction, all while enhancing temporal and multi-view consistency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

R3DP is designed as a plug-and-play 3D-aware perception module that replaces the visual encoders in existing imitation learning frameworks, specifically building upon Diffusion Policy (DP). It integrates 3D priors and temporal context through three core components: the Asynchronous Fast–Slow Collaboration (AFSC) module, the Temporal Feature Prediction Network (TFPNet), and the Multi-View Feature Fuser (MVFF).

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture

R3DP enhances standard 2D image-based imitation policies by focusing on 3D-aware perception, real-time feasibility, explicit temporal context assistance, and geometry-informed multi-view fusion. The system leverages a large 3D vision foundation model (VGGT) as a source of 3D priors.

#### 3.2. Asynchronous Fast–Slow Collaboration (AFSC) Module

The AFSC module addresses the computational bottleneck of large 3D models for real-time control by orchestrating a collaboration between a "slow" and a "fast" system:
- **Slow System (Φ_slow)**: This component uses a large 3D vision foundation model (VGGT) to process multi-view RGB images. It is queried only on sparse "key frames" (every $\tau$ inference steps) to compute high-fidelity Slow 3D-aware Features (S3DF).
- **Fast System (Φ_fast)**: This component is a lightweight Temporal Feature Prediction Network (TFPNet). It propagates the S3DF from key frames to all intermediate frames. TFPNet uses historical context and current observations to predict Real-time 3D-aware Features (R3DF) in real time.
 - Formally, $\Phi_{slow}: I_{i_0} \xrightarrow{\text{VGGT}} F^{S3D}_{i_0}$ and $\Phi_{fast}: (F^{R3D}_{i-1}, I_i) \xrightarrow{\text{TFPNet}} F^{R3D}_i$, where $i_0$ is a key frame and $i \in (i_0, i_0 + \tau)$.
This asynchronous approach allows for efficient injection of 3D priors while meeting real-time latency requirements.

For training, the diffusion policy sampler is modified to expose longer temporal dependencies. Each training tuple contains eight frames uniformly spaced by a stride of eight timesteps, creating an effective temporal window of 64 frames. Within each tuple, VGGT processes the first frame to produce initial S3DF, and TFPNet updates subsequent frames to produce R3DF. During this policy training phase, the vision backbones (VGGT and TFPNet) remain frozen, and only the diffusion head is updated with a unified diffusion loss.

#### 3.3. Temporal Feature Prediction Network (TFPNet)

TFPNet is a lightweight network pre-trained to predict current-frame 3D-aware features from historical features and current RGB images. Its design facilitates real-time online inference:
- **Pre-training Strategy**: TFPNet is trained by distilling supervision from a frozen VGGT model. For each episode, 4 frames ($t_0, t_1, t_2, t_3$) are sampled with random intervals (1-8 frames) to simulate real-world inference delays. All these frames are first processed by VGGT to obtain ground-truth 3D features ($F^{S3D}$).
- **Iterative Prediction**: TFPNet receives the VGGT feature from the initial frame ($t_0$) and iteratively predicts features for subsequent frames. $F^{R3D}_{t_k} = G(F^{S3D}_{t_0}, I_{t_0})$ for $k=0$, and $F^{R3D}_{t_k} = G(F^{R3D}_{t_{k-1}}, I_{t_k})$ for $k \in \{1,2,3\}$.
- **Architecture**: Each view's RGB image is initially encoded by a DINOv2-S backbone to produce 2D features. These features then pass through four Alternating-Attention Transformer blocks for cross-view aggregation. Finally, the aggregated per-view tokens attend to the historical R3DF via cross-attention to incorporate temporal priors.
- **Loss Function**: TFPNet is pre-trained using a cosine similarity loss between the predicted R3DF and the ground-truth VGGT features ($L_{TFP} = \sum_{i=0}^3 (1 - \text{cos}(F^{S3D}_t, F^{R3D}_t))$). This objective encourages accurate feature prediction and leveraging of temporal context.

#### 3.4. Multi-View Feature Fuser (MVFF)

The MVFF module is designed to explicitly integrate camera intrinsic and extrinsic parameters for a consistent 3D-aware representation across multiple camera views:
- **Feature Combination**: While VGGT provides a multi-view representation, it retains view-specific properties. R3DP processes each view's image with a ResNet encoder to obtain 2D features.
- **Cross-Attention Fusion**: Cross-attention is applied to fuse the per-view 2D features ($F^{2D}_t$) with the 3D-aware features ($F^{R3D}_t$) from AFSC, yielding a hybrid feature ($\tilde{F}_t$).
- **Projective Positional Encoding (PRoPE)**: The core of MVFF. This module explicitly incorporates ground-truth camera intrinsics ($K_t$) and extrinsics ($E_t$) into the feature aggregation process. PRoPE, based on relative projective transformations ($\tilde{P}_{i,j}$), guides cross-view fusion, ensuring spatial consistency and enhancing robustness, particularly in complex environments with occlusions.
- The fused feature $F^{fused}_t$ then serves as the final visual embedding for conditioning the diffusion model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of R3DP involved extensive experiments in both simulated and real-world environments, assessing performance across diverse manipulation tasks and visual configurations.

#### 4.1. Simulation Benchmark Results

- **Quantitative Performance**: R3DP demonstrated consistent and substantial performance gains compared to baseline methods on the 10 RoboTwin benchmark tasks:
 - Achieved an average success rate of 69.0%, outperforming single-view Diffusion Policy (DP-single) by 32.9% and multi-view Diffusion Policy (DP-multi) by 51.4%.
 - For tasks requiring fine-grained 3D reasoning (e.g., Block Hammer Beat, Blocks Stack Easy, Block Handover), R3DP achieved success rates of 77%, 69%, and 95% respectively, representing considerable improvements over DP-single (0%, 6%, 1%).
 - The DP-multi baseline exhibited lower success rates than DP-single, suggesting that a naive concatenation of multi-view features is insufficient and may hinder performance without explicit geometric reasoning.
 - R3DP surpassed the depth-aware baseline DP3 by 11.4% and DP3+DA2 (using estimated, noisy depth) by 40.8%, highlighting its robustness and independence from explicit depth sensing.
 - R3DP also outperformed $\pi_0$ (a Vision-Language-Action baseline) with an average success rate of 69.0% vs. 59.9%, suggesting R3DP's advantage in task-specific convergence with limited data.
- **Inference Speed**: R3DP demonstrated efficiency in balancing accuracy and latency:
 - With $\tau=8$, R3DP achieved an observation encoding latency of 40.3ms, representing a 44.8% reduction compared to a naive DP+VGGT integration (73.1ms) and 48.6% faster than DP+VGGT+MVFF (78.3ms).
- **Configurable Efficiency and Robustness**: R3DP proved flexible and robust to changes in the key frame interval $\tau$:
 - Increasing $\tau$ from 4 to 8 reduced encoding latency by 20.2% while maintaining a highly stable success rate (negligible 3.3% fluctuation). This suggests $\tau$ can be tuned for hardware constraints without retraining or significant performance degradation.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

An ablation study systematically validated the contribution of each core component of R3DP:

- **Effectiveness of 3D Foundation Model (DP+VGGT)**: Integrating VGGT features (DP+VGGT) increased the average success rate from 10.8% (DP-multi) to 39.8%, demonstrating that 3D priors significantly enhance spatial reasoning and manipulation precision.
- **Effectiveness of MVFF (DP+VGGT+MVFF)**: Adding the Multi-View Feature Fuser (MVFF) to DP+VGGT further improved the average success rate from 39.8% to 55.3%. This indicates MVFF's effectiveness in leveraging camera intrinsics and extrinsics for geometry-aware cross-view feature alignment, particularly in geometry-sensitive tasks.
- **Effectiveness of AFSC (R3DP)**: Equipping the model with the Asynchronous Fast–Slow Collaboration (AFSC) mechanism (R3DP) further boosted average success from 55.3% to 62.3%. AFSC also substantially reduced observation encoding latency from 78.3ms (DP+VGGT+MVFF) to 50.5ms (R3DP, $\tau=4$), confirming its role in enhancing temporal reasoning while maintaining real-time efficiency.

#### 4.3. Real-world Experiments

R3DP's practical applicability and geometric robustness were evaluated on a bimanual robotic system and a single-arm setup:

- **Performance**: R3DP significantly outperformed DP and DP3, with average success rate margins of 40.9% and 23.3% respectively across tasks like Place Shoe, Place Glass Cup, Pick Peach, and Stack Bowls.
 - For example, in "Place Shoe," R3DP achieved 86.7% success compared to DP's 46.7% and DP3's 50.0%.
 - DP3's performance was noted to degrade due to noisy and incomplete point clouds generated by real-world depth sensors, whereas R3DP's VGGT and MVFF modules provided more stable geometric perception.
- **Inference Efficiency**: On an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, R3DP achieved an observation encoding latency of 62.18 ms, which is comparable to DP3's 57.24 ms while delivering higher success rates. This demonstrated a balance of efficiency and accuracy suitable for real-time high-performance 3D manipulation.
- **Feature Visualization**: Visualizations confirmed that the lightweight TFPNet-predicted features closely resemble those produced by VGGT, validating its ability to capture VGGT-like 3D cues.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

R3DP addresses a significant challenge in embodied manipulation: integrating the rich 3D understanding offered by large-scale 3D vision foundation models with the stringent real-time requirements of robotic control. The proposed framework offers several advancements with potential impact:

- **Enabling Real-time 3D-Aware Control**: By introducing the Asynchronous Fast–Slow Collaboration (AFSC) module and the Temporal Feature Prediction Network (TFPNet), R3DP enables the injection of powerful 3D priors into policies without incurring prohibitive latency. This capability is crucial for dynamic and contact-rich manipulation tasks where immediate reactions are necessary.
- **Enhanced Spatial Perception without Dedicated Depth Sensors**: R3DP demonstrates that robust 3D perception can be achieved directly from RGB inputs, removing the reliance on additional depth sensors that are often prone to noise, cost, or limited coverage in real-world environments (e.g., transparent or reflective surfaces). This simplifies system design and deployment.
- **Improved Manipulation Robustness and Success Rates**: The explicit modeling of temporal context through TFPNet and geometry-informed multi-view fusion via MVFF contributes to more consistent 3D representations and robust policy execution. The significant improvements in success rates across diverse simulated and real-world tasks underscore the framework's effectiveness.
- **Plug-and-Play Integration**: R3DP is presented as a plug-and-play solution, meaning it can be seamlessly integrated into existing 2D image-based imitation learning policy backbones (such as Diffusion Policy). This modularity reduces the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners to leverage advanced 3D perception in their systems.
- **Configurable Efficiency**: The ability to adjust the $\tau$ parameter in AFSC allows for a configurable trade-off between inference speed and success rate, providing flexibility for deployment across various hardware constraints and task demands without requiring retraining.
- **Foundation for Future Work**: This research provides a promising pathway for combining the representational power of large-scale 3D models with the agility of fast control loops. The authors suggest future extensions to more complex scenarios, including contact-rich, long-horizon, and reinforcement-learning-driven manipulation, indicating a potential to generalize this real-time 3D-aware paradigm to broader embodied AI challenges.
