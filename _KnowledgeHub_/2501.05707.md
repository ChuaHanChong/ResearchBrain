# Multiagent Finetuning: Self Improvement with Diverse Reasoning Chains

> [!summary] Summary
> Multiagent Finetuning is a self-improvement recipe that replaces single-model bootstrapping with a society of models spawned from one base LLM. Multiagent debate with majority voting produces the training signal; each copy is then finetuned only on its own debate outputs, some as generation agents and some as critic agents. Specialization keeps reasoning chains diverse, so accuracy keeps rising over five finetuning rounds instead of plateauing.

## Problem

- Self-improvement loops that finetune one model on its own synthetic data plateau after two or three rounds as response diversity collapses into repeated styles.
- Distilling from frontier models caps student quality at the teacher's frontier, incurs heavy inference costs, and is often legally barred for commercial models.
- Bootstrapping methods such as STaR still need ground-truth answers for data selection, yet saturate and overfit to generated responses after a few iterations.

## Method

- ==Multiagent debate== with ==peer-response summarization== builds the finetuning corpus: N agents answer, revise over M rounds, and ==majority voting== supplies pseudo-labels without ground truth.
- ==Generation agents== are each finetuned only on their own first-round responses that match the majority vote, giving per-agent datasets that force specialization and diversification.
- ==Critic agents== are finetuned on full debate trajectories, mixing corrected cases and consistently-correct cases via weight w, then the whole loop iterates L times.

## Results

- With one finetuning iteration and no ground truth, it beats all baselines: GPT-3.5 MATH **60.6%** vs **55.7%** debate and **53.0%** STaR; LLaMA-3 GSM **88.6%** vs **78.4%**.
- Over five iterations Phi-3 MATH rises **58.8%** to **66.0%** and Mistral **22.5%** to **28.2%**, while single-agent finetuning saturates after one round then declines.
- Ablations: dropping critics lowers GPT-3.5 MATH to **50.8%**, dropping summarization to **51.7%**; MATH-finetuned agents transfer zero-shot to GSM at **53.0%** vs **49.7%** debate.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diversity, not label quality, is the binding constraint on iterated self-improvement; measured response diversity tracks accuracy gains across finetuning rounds.
> - Diversity must come from real weight specialization: higher sampling temperature or unique agent ID prompts fail to recover the same gains.
> - Splitting a base model into role-specialized generators and critics buys more than data splitting alone, but multiplies training and inference cost per copy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical challenge in the development of Large Language Models (LLMs): their fundamental limitation by the underlying training data and the diminishing returns observed in successive self-improvement loops. Currently, LLM enhancement strategies broadly fall into three categories:
1. **Human-in-the-loop methods (e.g., RLHF, DPO):** These methods directly incorporate human feedback to align models with desired behaviors and improve responses. While highly effective, they are labor-intensive and costly.
2. **Distillation methods:** Involve using larger, more capable "teacher" models (like GPT-4) to generate data for finetuning smaller "student" models. This transfers knowledge but is limited by the teacher model's capabilities and incurs significant inference costs.
3. **Self-improvement methods:** The most relevant category, where LLMs generate their own synthetic data for subsequent training. Prior works in this domain (e.g., STaR, iterated learning, self-play) have shown promise but often hit a "plateau effect" where performance gains quickly diminish after a few finetuning rounds due to reduced diversity in generated responses, leading to potential model collapse or overfitting.

This research positions itself as a **complementary approach within the self-improvement paradigm**, specifically tackling the "plateauing effect" and the issue of diversity degradation. Instead of finetuning a *single* LLM, the authors propose finetuning a *multiagent society* of language models. This leverages the emerging field of multi-agent interaction systems for LLMs, which has shown success in improving reasoning and factuality through debate, cooperation, or structured thought processes (e.g., Tree-of-Thought, Graph-of-Thought, multi-agent debate).

By combining iterative finetuning with multi-agent interaction, the paper aims to create a more robust and sustained self-improvement loop. The key distinction from prior self-improvement works is the emphasis on **specialization and diversification across multiple models** rather than iteratively improving a single model. This allows for the preservation of diverse reasoning chains, which is posited as crucial for continued autonomous improvement. This work thus represents an evolution in LLM self-improvement, moving from monolithic single-agent loops to a more distributed, specialized, and diverse multi-agent learning system.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations of this research are clearly articulated:

- **Address the Fundamental Limitations of LLMs:** Current LLMs are constrained by their training data. As much of the readily available internet data has already been consumed, new methods are needed to push performance beyond existing datasets.
- **Overcome Diminishing Returns in Self-Improvement:** While autonomous self-improvement through synthetic data generation is a promising direction, existing single-agent methods typically exhibit rapid performance plateaus. The models tend to overfit to their own generated data, leading to a decrease in the diversity of reasoning and a limited capacity for further improvement.
- **Enable Sustained Autonomous Self-Improvement:** The primary goal is to develop a method that allows LLMs to continue improving over many more rounds of finetuning than current techniques, thereby reducing the reliance on costly human annotation or proprietary frontier models.
- **Foster Specialization and Diversification:** The key insight is that by finetuning multiple models within a multi-agent framework, each model can specialize in different aspects of a task or adopt distinct reasoning styles. This specialization across models, in turn, promotes diversification within the collective output, which is hypothesized to mitigate the plateauing issue.
- **Validate Efficacy Across Tasks and Models:** The authors aim to quantitatively demonstrate the effectiveness of their "Multiagent Finetuning" approach across a suite of reasoning tasks (Arithmetic, GSM, MATH) and across various LLM architectures, including open-source models (Phi-3, Mistral, LLaMA-3) and proprietary ones (GPT-3.5).
- **Demonstrate Generalization Capabilities:** A further objective is to show that the finetuned multi-agent system can generalize effectively to novel datasets in a zero-shot manner, indicating a deeper and more robust learning process.

In essence, the motivation is to unlock a new paradigm for LLM growth, moving beyond individual model limitations by harnessing the power of collaborative and specialized multi-agent systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, "Multiagent Finetuning," introduces a novel framework for LLM self-improvement by iteratively training a society of specialized language models. The approach comprises two main components: generating finetuning data via multiagent interaction and then performing specialized finetuning on the individual agents.

#### 3.1. Multiagent Debate as Data Generation

The process begins by leveraging **Multiagent Debate** (as introduced by Du et al., 2023) to generate high-quality synthetic data for finetuning.
- **Initial Responses:** Given an input query (e.g., a math problem), a set of *N* language model agents, all initialized from the same base model, independently generate their initial responses. These agents are initially considered "generation agents."
- **Iterative Debate:** The agents engage in *M* rounds of debate. In each subsequent round, agents receive and summarize the responses from other agents (excluding their own previous response). Based on their prior response and the summarized inputs, each agent then generates a refined response.
- **Majority Voting:** After the final debate round, a majority vote among the agents' final responses determines the "ground truth" for that specific problem. This collective decision-making process is presumed to yield more accurate and robust answers than a single agent. This "ground truth" (ŷ) is crucial for constructing the finetuning datasets.

#### 3.2. Finetuning Generation Models

The first type of agent specialized through finetuning is the **Generation Agent**.
- **Role:** To produce diverse and accurate initial responses to queries.
- **Data Collection:** For each generation agent *A_G_n*, a dedicated dataset *D_G_n* is created. This dataset consists of input-output pairs (x, y_1,n) where *y_1,n* is the *initial* response of agent *n* that matches the final majority-voted result (ŷ).
- **Specialization:** By training each generation agent only on its *own* initially correct responses that align with the collective best answer, the system encourages the preservation of diverse reasoning chains that lead to correct outcomes. This helps prevent all agents from converging to a single, narrow solution path.

#### 3.3. Finetuning Critic Models

The second type of agent is the **Critic Agent**, which plays a crucial role in refinement and correction.
- **Role:** To evaluate and refine the generations of other models, acting as a feedback mechanism.
- **Data Collection:** For each critic agent *A_C_n*, two types of datasets are constructed to enhance its corrective capabilities:
 - **D_C-n (Negative Correction Set):** Contains examples where the agent's *initial* response (y_1,n) was incorrect (did not match ŷ), but its *final* response (y_M,n) after the debate *did* match ŷ. This dataset trains the critic to identify and correct errors, demonstrating how successful self-correction happens.
 - **D_C+n (Positive Reinforcement Set):** Contains examples where both the agent's initial (y_1,n) and final (y_M,n) responses matched ŷ. This reinforces the agent's ability to maintain correct answers throughout the debate.
- **Dataset Combination:** These two datasets are combined using a tunable hyperparameter *w* (D_C_n = w * D_C-n + (1-w) * D_C+n). This allows balancing the emphasis on learning from corrections versus reinforcing consistently correct reasoning.
- **Specialization:** Critic agents are finetuned on these specialized datasets, enabling them to become adept at identifying flaws in reasoning and proposing better solutions, further enhancing the overall system's robustness.

The entire process is designed to be **iterative**.

#### 3.5. Inference

At inference time, the finetuned generation and critic agents collaborate in a multi-agent debate setting. The generation agents produce initial responses, followed by the critic agents refining these responses over subsequent rounds, incorporating summarized inputs from other agents. The final answer is determined by a majority vote from the last round of debate, similar to the data generation phase.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides compelling quantitative and qualitative evidence for the efficacy of Multiagent Finetuning.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance Improvements

- **Outperformance Across Baselines:** The proposed method consistently outperforms a wide range of baselines (Base, Majority, Debate, STaR, Majority FT) across all evaluated reasoning tasks: Arithmetic, Grade School Math (GSM), and MATH. This superiority is observed across diverse base LLMs, including open-source models (Phi-3, Mistral, LLaMA-3) and a proprietary model (GPT-3.5).
 - For instance, on the challenging MATH dataset, Multiagent FT improves GPT-3.5 accuracy from 46.83% (Base) to 60.60% (Ours), Phi-3 from 45.60% to 58.80%, Mistral from 16.60% to 22.50%, and LLaMA-3 from 46.80% to 57.40%.
- **Superiority over STaR:** Notably, Multiagent FT (even with a single iteration and no ground truth access for data selection) surpasses STaR, a prominent self-improvement method that *does* leverage ground truth labels and multiple finetuning iterations. This highlights the efficiency and effectiveness of the multi-agent approach.

#### 4.2. Sustained Gains through Multiple Iterations

- **Overcoming Plateaus:** A critical finding is the ability of Multiagent FT to achieve sustained performance gains over multiple rounds of finetuning, directly addressing the diminishing returns problem. While "Single-agent FT" (finetuning a single model) quickly plateaus and even declines after 1-2 iterations due to potential overfitting and diversity loss, Multiagent FT shows continuous improvement.
 - On the MATH dataset, Phi-3's accuracy increased from 58.8% to 66.0% over five iterations, and Mistral's from 22.5% to 28.2%. This demonstrates the long-term viability of the approach for autonomous self-improvement.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirm the contribution of each component to the overall performance:
- **Summarization:** Removing the summarization step from multiagent debate (direct concatenation of responses) negatively impacts performance, indicating its importance in distilling relevant information.
- **Critic Agents:** Removing critic agents (only finetuning generation agents) also hurts performance, emphasizing the crucial role of the feedback mechanism provided by specialized critic agents in refining outputs.
- **Multiagent Finetuning vs. Single-agent FT:** The comparison clearly shows that multiagent finetuning (specializing multiple agents) is superior to single-agent finetuning, which suffers from "model collapse" and reduced diversity.
- **Multiagent Debate:** The debate procedure itself is vital; "Single-agent FT w/o debate" performs significantly worse, aligning with previous findings that multi-agent interaction at inference time boosts performance.

#### 4.4. Agent Response Diversity Analysis

- **Preservation of Diversity:** Crucially, the analysis confirms that Multiagent Finetuning preserves and even enhances the diversity of agent responses across finetuning iterations, unlike single-agent methods where diversity rapidly decreases.
 - Metrics like Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) of other agents' responses and Embedding Dissimilarity show that multiagent finetuning maintains or increases diversity, while single-agent finetuning causes a significant drop.
 - A positive correlation between increased diversity and improved accuracy is observed, suggesting that fostering diverse reasoning paths is key to sustained performance gains. This is further supported by a mathematical model presented in the appendix.

#### 4.5. Zero-Shot Generalization

- **Transferability of Skills:** The finetuned multi-agent system demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization capabilities. Agents trained on one reasoning dataset (e.g., MATH) can effectively generalize and outperform baselines trained directly on a *different* dataset (e.g., GSM), even without seeing any examples from the target dataset during training. This indicates that the learned reasoning skills are robust and transferable.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant advancement in the field of large language model self-improvement, offering a novel and effective paradigm.

#### 5.1. Key Contributions

- **Multiagent Paradigm for Self-Improvement:** The core contribution is the introduction of a multiagent finetuning framework that leverages multi-agent interaction not just for inference-time collaboration but as a fundamental mechanism for autonomous model improvement.
- **Role Specialization for Enhanced Feedback:** The idea of specializing models into distinct roles (generation agents and critic agents) during finetuning is crucial. This detailed feedback mechanism enhances output quality and fosters a more robust learning environment than single-model loops.
- **Empirical Validation Across Diverse Models and Tasks:** The comprehensive quantitative verification across various open-source (Phi-3, Mistral, LLaMA-3) and proprietary (GPT-3.5) LLMs, and a suite of reasoning tasks (Arithmetic, GSM, MATH, MMLU), attests to the broad applicability and effectiveness of the method.
- **Sustained Performance Gains:** The most impactful finding is the demonstration that multiagent finetuning overcomes the rapid plateauing issue of prior self-improvement methods, enabling continuous and substantial performance gains over many iterations.
- **Preservation of Diverse Reasoning:** The work highlights the critical role of maintaining diversity in reasoning chains for sustained improvement, providing a mechanism (specialized multi-agent finetuning) to achieve this.
- **Zero-Shot Generalization:** The proven ability of finetuned agents to generalize to unseen datasets in a zero-shot manner suggests that the method promotes a deeper understanding of reasoning principles, rather than mere overfitting to training data.

#### 5.2. Overcoming Limitations of Single-Agent Self-Improvement

This work offers a viable solution to the inherent limitations of single-agent self-improvement methods, specifically their tendency to reduce diversity and quickly saturate performance. By introducing a multi-agent society, the paper demonstrates how distributed learning and specialization can mitigate model collapse, leading to more robust and continuously improving LLMs without relying on external human annotation or expensive distillation from frontier models. This makes autonomous self-improvement a more practical reality for scaling LLM capabilities.

#### 5.3. Versatility and Generalization

The method's applicability to both open-source and proprietary models underscores its versatility. Furthermore, its ability to generalize across different types of reasoning tasks and even to unseen datasets suggests that the learned improvements are fundamental and transferable, making the finetuned models more broadly intelligent and adaptable. The cooperative finetuning experiment in the appendix also indicates that the method is agnostic to the exact interaction style, implying broader applicability.

#### 5.4. Limitations and Future Work

The authors acknowledge important limitations, primarily concerning computational cost:
- **High Computational Cost:** Multiagent finetuning is significantly more expensive at both training and inference time compared to single-model approaches. This requires multiple GPU resources (e.g., four H100 or A100 GPUs) and substantial training/inference durations (12-24 hours). This poses a practical barrier to widespread adoption, especially for smaller research groups or individual practitioners.

Potential future work directions highlighted include:
- **Weight Sharing:** Exploring methods to share weights across different instances of models to reduce training and memory overhead.
- **Inference Optimization:** Investigating techniques like distilling the multiagent debate procedure into a single, smaller model or employing quantization during finetuning to improve inference speed and efficiency.
- **Integration with Human Feedback:** Combining multiagent finetuning with human feedback methods like RLHF or DPO could further enhance model alignment and performance, creating a hybrid self-improvement pipeline.

In conclusion, "Multiagent Finetuning" presents a compelling case for moving beyond individual LLM limitations by orchestrating a society of specialized agents. This innovative approach effectively tackles the critical challenge of sustained autonomous self-improvement, opening new avenues for developing more capable, diverse, and generalizable language models.
