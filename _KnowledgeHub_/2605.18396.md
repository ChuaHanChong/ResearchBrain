# NEWTON: Agentic Planning for Physically Grounded Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> NEWTON reframes physics-grounded video generation as agentic tool orchestration: a trainable VLM planner selects among keyframe generation, Python physics computation, and prompt refinement tools, while a frozen verifier scores outputs and drives iterative re-planning. Trained on-policy via Flow-GRPO, it lifts VideoPhy-2 joint accuracy from 21.4% to 29.7% on LTX-Video without touching the generator.

## Problem

- Text prompts are lossy compression of physical dynamics, omitting mass, friction, and velocity parameters that fully determine trajectories, forcing generators to hallucinate.
- Existing physics conditioning approaches (end-to-end retraining, ControlNet-style fixed signals, one-shot test-time search) each satisfy at most one of sufficiency, dynamism, or verifiability, never all three.
- Offline SFT on expert trajectories decouples planners from live system dynamics, preventing recovery from tool failures or adaptation to real verifier feedback, degrading agentic performance.

## Method

- Three-role ==Planner-Executor-Verifier== architecture where a ==VLM== planner reads memory state and prior feedback to select physics-aware tool calls each cycle, iterating up to T times. This is **NEWTON**.
- Physics-aware toolkit of keyframe generation (temporal boundary conditioning), sandboxed Python scientific computation (trajectories, momentum), and natural-language prompt refinement.
- ==Flow-GRPO== on-policy optimization trains only the planner inside the live multi-turn loop using group-normalized advantages and a tiered composite reward with keyframe and computation bonuses.

## Results

- On VideoPhy-2, NEWTON raises joint accuracy from **21.36%** to **29.66%** overall and from **4.44%** to **12.22%** on the HARD subset, a **2.75x** relative gain.
- Cross-benchmark generalization to PhyGenBench without retraining lifts average VLM-judged score from **0.510** to **0.560**, surpassing Wan2.2-TI2V-5B at **0.544**.
- Ablations show Flow-GRPO roughly doubles offline SFT's gain over frozen baseline (HARD-Joint **+3.3** vs **+1.7**), and gains scale with planner size and cycle budget T up to **5**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the generation model from physics reasoning lets a lightweight trainable planner fix specification gaps without any generator retraining or architecture change.
> - On-policy training inside the live multi-turn loop matters more than model scale alone; offline SFT on expert rollouts underperforms because it never observes its own failure modes.
> - Verify-and-correct loops compound: physical consistency benefits keep accruing across iterative re-planning cycles rather than saturating after one shot.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The field of video generation has experienced rapid advancements, with models capable of producing visually compelling and temporally coherent videos from text prompts. However, a persistent challenge in this domain is the systematic failure of these models to adhere to physical commonsense. Generated videos frequently depict phenomena that violate fundamental physical laws, such as objects changing speed without external forces, gravity being ignored, or collisions not conserving momentum.

Prior research efforts to address this issue can be broadly categorized. One line of work attempts to integrate explicit physical simulators as a prior. Methods like PhysMotion, PhysCtrl, and PhysChoreo utilize differentiable simulators or generative physics networks to model specific physical behaviors (e.g., continuum mechanics). While effective for certain scenarios, these approaches often commit to a fixed simulator family and may lack adaptability across diverse scenes. Another approach, NewtonGen, embeds neural Newtonian dynamics but primarily handles single-object continuous motion, struggling with complex interactions like collisions.

A complementary direction involves modifying the video generator itself to internalize physics. Approaches such as VideoREPA, WISA, and ProPhy attempt to distill physical relations or inject hierarchical physical signals into diffusion models, sometimes coupled with reward-based post-training like PhyGDPO. These methods typically operate in a one-shot manner without iterative feedback, and often exhibit a trade-off between physical consistency and semantic adherence.

The authors of NEWTON identify a fundamental limitation in these existing approaches: none jointly satisfy three critical properties for physics conditioning: sufficiency, dynamism, and verifiability.
- **Sufficiency** refers to providing enough physical parameters to fully determine dynamics.
- **Dynamism** necessitates adapting physical specifications to the unique demands of each scene.
- **Verifiability** involves checking whether the generated output conforms to the intended physics and allowing for correction.

Existing end-to-end models implicitly embed physics without guarantees (lacking sufficiency). Conditional generation methods, like those using ControlNet, provide fixed-modality signals that cannot adapt per scene (lacking dynamism). All one-shot methods lack feedback mechanisms (lacking verifiability).

NEWTON positions itself as an agentic system, drawing inspiration from agentic LLM systems that use planners, external tools, and iterative critique for text reasoning and image generation. It extends this paradigm to video generation, specifically targeting the physical commonsense problem. Unlike some agentic systems that rely on frozen LLMs for planning, NEWTON introduces a trainable planner optimized on-policy, coupled with in-loop verification. This approach aims to engineer physical consistency rather than relying on its emergent properties from scaling models or data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that despite significant visual fidelity advancements, current text-to-video generation models systematically violate physical commonsense. The authors diagnose this as a "specification bottleneck." They argue that text prompts, while effective for general scene description, serve as a "lossy compression of the physical world." A simple prompt like "a ball rolls off a table" omits critical physical parameters such as the ball's mass, friction coefficient, table height, and initial velocity. These parameters are essential for accurately determining the ball's trajectory, yet the generator is forced to hallucinate a consistent set of values, leading to physically incoherent dynamics.

From this diagnosis, the authors derive three key properties that any effective physics conditioning must satisfy:
1. **Sufficiency**: The conditioning must provide enough physical information to explicitly determine dynamics, leaving no crucial parameters unspecified.
2. **Dynamism**: The conditioning approach must be adaptable to specific scenarios, as different physical situations require different sets of parameters and physical laws.
3. **Verifiability**: There must be a mechanism to evaluate whether the generated output adheres to the intended physics and to facilitate correction if it does not.

The existing paradigm, where models implicitly learn physics from raw video data, is likened to "rediscovering Newton's laws from unlabeled footage." This approach is deemed data-inefficient and fundamentally limited by the coverage of training data. Human knowledge, codified in structured physical laws, remains largely untapped.

The key objective of NEWTON is to address this specification bottleneck by enriching the generator's conditioning signal with explicit, scene-specific physical knowledge. This involves a shift from solely focusing on rendering quality to prioritizing the precise specification of physical dynamics. The research aims to develop an agentic system that can:
- Reason about what specific physical knowledge is missing or required for a given scene.
- Access and utilize heterogeneous external tools to acquire this knowledge.
- Iteratively correct video generation based on feedback regarding physical plausibility, all without requiring modifications or retraining of the underlying video generator.

By achieving these objectives, NEWTON intends to demonstrate that physical consistency in video generation can be systematically engineered through adaptive planning and tool orchestration, rather than relying on the statistical emergence of physical laws from large datasets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NEWTON (Neural Agentic World-Aware Tool-Orchestrated Navigation) is a trainable agentic system designed to enhance the physical plausibility of videos generated by a frozen text-to-video model. It achieves this by iteratively enriching the generator's conditioning signals with explicit physical knowledge. The system operates through an iterative loop involving three core components: a Planner, an Executor, and a Verifier.

#### 3.1. Three-Role Architecture

- **Planner**: This is the sole trainable component, implemented as a Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically Qwen3.5-9B. At each cycle, the Planner observes the current memory state, which includes the original prompt, prior tool calls, tool outputs, and verifier feedback. Based on this, it generates a structured action, deciding which physics-aware tools to invoke and with what arguments. The Planner's action space is designed to be flexible, allowing for calls to any subset of tools, triggering video generation, or electing to skip a cycle.
- **Executor**: This component is responsible for carrying out the actions specified by the Planner. It dispatches calls to the predefined physics-aware tools and, crucially, to the frozen video generator. When video generation is triggered, the Executor provides the generator with accumulated conditioning signals, which include refined text prompts and generated keyframes. The system is designed to be generator-agnostic, meaning it can interface with various existing video generation models.
- **Verifier**: A multimodal evaluation model (VideoPhy-2-AutoEval) serves as the Verifier. It assesses each generated video on two scalar dimensions: Semantic Adherence (SA) and Physical Commonsense (PC). The scores produced by the Verifier are then appended to the system's memory, thereby closing the feedback loop for subsequent planning cycles.

#### 3.2. Iterative Cycle

The NEWTON system operates over a fixed number of T cycles (set to 5 in experiments). This process is conceptualized as a finite-horizon Markov Decision Process (MDP). In each cycle `t`, the Planner observes the memory `M_t`, selects an action `a_t`, and the Executor executes it, yielding an observation `e_t`. The memory is updated deterministically, incorporating `M_t`, `a_t`, and `e_t`. To maintain a tractable context length, the memory stores scalar verifier scores as a summary rather than the full generated videos. The iterative nature allows early cycles to focus on computation and prompt refinement, while later cycles leverage accumulated knowledge for refined video generation. The video with the highest verifier score across all cycles is designated as the final output.

#### 3.3. Physics-Aware Tools

NEWTON's toolkit comprises three specialized tools designed to address complementary dimensions of the specification bottleneck:
- **Keyframe Generation**: This tool leverages a text-to-image model to generate guiding images at specific temporal positions within a video (e.g., start, middle, end frames). The Planner formulates dedicated prompts for each keyframe, encoding the expected physical state (e.g., "ball at the apex of a parabolic arc"). These keyframes serve as temporal boundary conditions, helping to anchor the generated trajectory to physically consistent states.
- **Python Computation**: This tool provides a sandboxed Python environment for performing scientific computations. This includes calculating projectile trajectories, conservation-of-momentum dynamics, or rotational mechanics. The numerical results from these computations are then stored in memory, informing subsequent keyframe prompts or guiding the specification of physical constraints. This mechanism operationalizes human-engineered physical knowledge.
- **Prompt Refiner**: This tool refines the natural language prompt used for video generation. It augments the original caption with specific physical details, material properties, or scene constraints that were initially absent but are crucial for accurate physical simulation.

#### 3.4. In-the-Flow Optimization (Planner Training)

The Planner is trained using an on-policy approach called Flow-GRPO, which is adapted for multi-turn agents with sparse rewards.
- **Motivation for In-the-Flow**: The authors argue that offline supervised training methods decouple the Planner from the live system dynamics, preventing it from learning from its own errors or adapting to real-time verifier feedback. On-policy training, where the Planner is updated based on actual outcomes within the system's execution flow, is considered more effective.
- **Flow-GRPO**: For each prompt, G parallel rollouts are sampled under the current policy, each executing the full T-cycle trajectory. This ensures the policy experiences the complete planning horizon. A group-normalized advantage `A_i` is calculated for each rollout, comparing its reward `R(τ_i)` to the mean of rewards from all G rollouts. The policy is then updated using a clipped surrogate objective, which includes a KL penalty against a fixed reference policy to maintain stable training.
- **Reward Design**: The composite reward `R(τ)` integrates three components:
 - **Format penalty**: A fixed negative reward is applied for any formatting or length violations in the Planner's output, ensuring basic interface adherence.
 - **Quality reward `R_quality`**: This is a tiered function of the maximum SA and PC scores achieved across all video-producing cycles. It is designed to provide a denser advantage signal by rewarding partial physical correctness, even when joint high scores are not achieved.
 - **Keyframe bonus `R_kf`**: A fixed bonus is awarded if newly generated keyframes are used for conditioning and the resulting video meets a semantic-adherence threshold, encouraging early exploration of keyframe utility.
 - **Computation bonus `R_compute`**: A fixed bonus is provided when a valid physics computation is performed (correct function and parameters) and the overall quality reward is positive, preventing trivial reward hacking.

The Planner is trained on the 3,350-prompt VideoPhy-2 train split for one epoch using specific hyperparameters (G=8 rollouts, T=5 cycles, ϵ=0.2, β=0.01, learning rate 5x10^-7, etc.) on 8 NVIDIA H200 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of NEWTON was conducted across two primary benchmarks and through ablation studies, demonstrating its effectiveness in improving physical plausibility in video generation.

#### 4.1. Main Results on VideoPhy-2

- NEWTON significantly improved physical commonsense (PC) and semantic adherence (SA) simultaneously, which contrasts with other physics-augmented generators that often exhibit a PC-SA trade-off.
- On the full VideoPhy-2 dataset, NEWTON increased Joint accuracy (videos meeting both SA≥4 and PC≥4) from 21.36% (LTX-Video-2B backbone) to 29.66%.
- On the challenging HARD subset, Joint accuracy improved from 4.44% to 12.22%, representing a 2.75x relative gain.
- Qualitative comparisons illustrated NEWTON's ability to generate more physically consistent dynamics, such as progressive filling of a mug with foam, creation of a deepening groove with shavings, and natural peeling of a grapefruit rind, unlike baselines that exhibited inconsistencies or static elements. Similarly, for animation samples, NEWTON accurately depicted bubble inflation and physically plausible ball trajectories.
- A human preference study involving 197 prompts showed that NEWTON was consistently preferred over LTX-Video-2B and Wan2.2 for both physics plausibility and overall video quality.

#### 4.2. Cross-Benchmark Generalization on PhyGenBench

- Without retraining, the planner demonstrated generalization capabilities on the PhyGenBench dataset, which features different physical categories.
- NEWTON raised the average VLM-judged score from 0.510 (LTX-Video-2B) to 0.560, outperforming the strongest open generator, Wan2.2-TI2V-5B (0.544), despite using a 2.5x smaller backbone.
- The gains were concentrated in the Optics (+0.067) and Material (+0.092) categories, with Mechanics remaining largely unchanged.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Planner scale**: Increasing the size of the Qwen3.5 planner from 2B to 9B parameters resulted in monotonic improvements across all metrics, with the 9B planner consistently leading, particularly on the HARD subset Joint accuracy (7.22% → 9.44% → 12.22%). This indicates that larger planner capacity contributes to more effective tool orchestration and reasoning.
- **Number of planning cycles (T)**: Increasing the number of iterative cycles from T=2 to T=5 improved performance, with HARD-Joint accuracy climbing from 4.44% to 12.22%. This validates the utility of the iterative verify-and-correct loop, demonstrating that gains compound with more refinement steps.
- **Training strategy**: The on-policy Flow-GRPO training strategy significantly outperformed alternative approaches. Compared to a FROZEN (prompted only) baseline, Flow-GRPO approximately doubled the gains achieved by offline supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on expert rollouts. For instance, HARD-Joint accuracy improved by +3.3 with Flow-GRPO versus +1.7 with SFT over the frozen baseline. This effect was consistent across cycles, with Flow-GRPO widening its lead in PC and SA scores with each refinement step.
- **Generator backbone**: NEWTON's framework proved adaptable to different underlying video generators. When applied to the stronger Veo-3.1 backbone (on a 100-caption subset), NEWTON further boosted Joint accuracy from 30.74% to 37.41% (+6.7), demonstrating that its benefits are additive rather than substitutive for strong visual generators.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The NEWTON framework introduces a significant contribution to the field of video generation by systematically addressing the challenge of physical commonsense violations. Its core significance lies in identifying and proposing a solution to the "specification bottleneck," where text prompts inherently lack the detailed physical parameters required for accurate dynamic simulations.

The primary impact of this work is a paradigm shift: instead of solely relying on the implicit learning of physics by scaling large generative models, NEWTON demonstrates that physical consistency can be actively "engineered" through agentic planning and external tool orchestration. This approach effectively leverages human-encoded physical knowledge and computational capabilities, which traditional end-to-end models struggle to acquire implicitly from raw video data.

Key impacts include:
- **Improved Physical Consistency**: NEWTON achieves substantial and verifiable improvements in physical commonsense adherence across diverse scenarios, as evidenced by its performance on VideoPhy-2 and PhyGenBench. This directly translates to more realistic and plausible video content, which is critical for applications requiring physical accuracy.
- **Enhanced Semantic Adherence**: Unlike some prior physics-augmented methods that sacrifice semantic adherence for physical accuracy, NEWTON improves both aspects concurrently, indicating a more holistic enhancement of video generation quality.
- **Generator Agnosticism**: The framework's ability to operate with frozen, off-the-shelf video generators (LTX-Video-2B, Veo-3.1) without modification is a notable advantage. This makes NEWTON a versatile and accessible solution that can be integrated with future advances in video generation technology, extending their capabilities without requiring retraining from scratch.
- **Effectiveness of Agentic Planning**: The research validates the effectiveness of agentic systems for complex visual generation tasks, particularly the power of a trainable planner in orchestrating diverse tools and iteratively refining outputs based on objective feedback. The on-policy Flow-GRPO training is shown to be crucial for learning scene-dependent tool-use strategies that generalize.
- **Bridging Human Knowledge and AI Generation**: By incorporating physics-aware tools for scientific computation and prompt refinement, NEWTON effectively bridges the gap between structured human physical knowledge and the generative capabilities of AI models. This approach opens avenues for integrating more explicit domain-specific knowledge into complex AI tasks.

Potential future impacts and directions for this research include:
- **Expansion of Tool Library**: Integrating more sophisticated and specialized physics simulators (e.g., for fluid dynamics, deformable bodies, or articulated-body engines) could further broaden NEWTON's coverage and enhance its physical reasoning capabilities.
- **Richer Verifier Feedback**: Developing verifiers that provide more granular, language-based diagnostic feedback rather than just scalar scores could enable the Planner to engage in more targeted and efficient re-planning, addressing specific physical errors.
- **Applications in Simulation and Content Creation**: The ability to generate physically grounded videos could have significant implications for fields such as scientific simulation, virtual reality, game development, and film production, where realistic physical interactions are paramount.

In conclusion, NEWTON represents a methodological advance by demonstrating that agentic planning, coupled with iterative verification and external tool use, can effectively overcome fundamental limitations in current video generation models concerning physical commonsense. It offers a pathway towards creating more intelligent and physically accurate generative AI systems.
