# Byte Latent Transformer: Patches Scale Better Than Tokens

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces a new byte-level language model architecture that matches tokenization-based performance while improving efficiency and robustness

## Problem

- Traditional language models rely on fixed-vocabulary tokenization which leads to issues with domain sensitivity, noise sensitivity, and multilingual inequity
- Training models directly on bytes has been prohibitively expensive due to longer sequence lengths
- Existing byte-level models struggle to match tokenization-based performance at scale

## Method

- Introduce **Byte Latent Transformer (BLT)** that dynamically groups bytes into ==patches based on entropy==, allocating compute based on input complexity rather than fixed tokens
- Use lightweight encoder/decoder modules around a large transformer to process patches
- Incorporate ==byte n-gram embeddings== and ==cross-attention== to maximize information flow

## Results

- Matches performance of Llama 3 while using up to **50%** fewer inference FLOPs
- Improves robustness to input noise and shows better performance on character-level tasks like orthographic knowledge and phonology compared to token-based models
- Achieves better scaling trends than tokenization-based models by growing both patch and model size

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamic patching allows more efficient compute allocation by using larger patches for predictable sequences, letting the model simultaneously increase patch and model size while maintaining a fixed inference budget
> - Direct byte modeling improves robustness to noise and handling of character-level patterns
> - Patches scale better than tokens at larger model sizes

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Introduction

The vast majority of modern language models rely on a fundamental preprocessing step called tokenization, which converts raw text into discrete tokens before processing. This approach has become so ubiquitous that it's rarely questioned, despite introducing significant limitations. Researchers from Meta AI and collaborating universities have proposed a groundbreaking alternative: the Byte Latent Transformer (BLT), which operates directly on raw byte sequences instead of predefined tokens.

#### 1.2. The Limitations of Traditional Tokenization

Traditional tokenization approaches come with several inherent limitations:

1. **Domain and Modality Sensitivity**: Token vocabularies are typically optimized for specific domains and languages, leading to poor handling of out-of-domain content.

2. **Input Noise Vulnerability**: Small changes to input text can drastically change tokenization patterns, making models sensitive to typos, misspellings, and adversarial attacks.

3. **Limited Orthographic Knowledge**: Token-based models lack character-level understanding, limiting their ability to handle tasks requiring morphological knowledge.

4. **Multilingual Inequality**: Fixed vocabularies often allocate fewer tokens to low-resource languages, creating performance disparities across languages.

5. **Fixed Compute Allocation**: Traditional models allocate the same computational resources to each token, regardless of its complexity or importance.

Previous attempts to build byte-level language models have struggled with computational efficiency due to increased sequence lengths. For example, the English word "tokenization" might be represented as a single token in a traditional model but requires 12 bytes when processed at the byte level.

#### 1.3. Comparison with Other Approaches

Previous approaches to byte-level modeling include:

1. **Character-Level Transformers**: These operate on individual characters but struggle with efficiency at scale due to long sequence lengths.

2. **MegaByte and MegaByte++**: These use mixture-of-experts architectures to reduce computational requirements but still face challenges with scaling.

3. **SpaceByte**: This uses fixed-space patching (dividing text into equal chunks) but lacks the adaptive capabilities of BLT's entropy-based patching.

4. **ByT5 and CANINE**: These approaches use byte or character-level inputs but still rely on some form of fixed tokenization in their processing pipeline.

BLT differentiates itself by solving the compute allocation problem through dynamic patching, allowing it to scale more effectively than previous approaches. The comparison studies showed that BLT outperforms these alternatives, particularly at larger scales relevant to state-of-the-art language models.

The advantages of BLT over these approaches include:
- More efficient resource allocation through entropy-based patching
- Better scaling properties with model size
- No fixed vocabulary or tokenization artifacts
- Improved handling of out-of-distribution inputs

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

BLT represents a significant paradigm shift in language model design by demonstrating that byte-level models can match the performance of traditional token-based models while offering superior inference efficiency and robustness. This paper overview explores how BLT works, its advantages over traditional approaches, and its potential to transform the field of natural language processing.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. BLT Architecture: How It Works

The Byte Latent Transformer consists of three main components:

1. **Local Encoder**: A lightweight transformer-based model that processes input bytes and encodes them into patch representations.

2. **Latent Global Transformer**: The core component that performs autoregressive modeling on the patch representations. This is where most of the computational power is concentrated.

3. **Local Decoder**: Another lightweight transformer that decodes the patch representations back into bytes during generation.

Unlike traditional models that have a fixed vocabulary of tokens, BLT has no fixed vocabulary. Instead, it dynamically segments the byte sequence into variable-sized patches based on the entropy of next-byte prediction. This approach allows the model to allocate more computational resources to complex or uncertain parts of the text, while processing simpler or more predictable segments more efficiently.

The autoregressive nature of BLT means it generates text by predicting one byte (or patch of bytes) at a time, conditioning each prediction on all previously generated bytes. This approach allows for a more nuanced understanding of text at the character level.

#### 3.2. Dynamic Patching: Efficient Compute Allocation

A key innovation in BLT is its dynamic patching mechanism. Unlike fixed-length chunking, BLT segments bytes into patches based on the entropy (uncertainty) of the next-byte prediction from a small byte-level language model.

When the model encounters a highly predictable sequence of bytes (low entropy), it groups them into larger patches. For uncertain or complex sequences (high entropy), it creates smaller patches to allocate more processing power. This approach has several advantages:

1. **Efficiency**: By processing multiple bytes in a single step when appropriate, BLT reduces the number of sequential operations required.

2. **Adaptability**: The model naturally adapts to different languages, domains, and content types without retraining.

3. **Dynamic Resource Allocation**: Computational resources are allocated where they provide the most benefit, rather than uniformly across all input bytes.

The researchers also explored several patching variants, including fixed-space patching (dividing text into equal-sized segments) and entropy-based patching with monotonicity constraints (ensuring patches align with linguistic boundaries).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Performance and Scaling Results

One of the most significant findings of the research is that BLT models exhibit better scaling properties than traditional token-based models when considering fixed inference costs.

The results show that:

1. **Performance Parity**: At scale, BLT achieves comparable or better performance than token-based models like LLaMA 3.

2. **Better Scaling Trends**: As model size increases, BLT improves more rapidly than token-based models, especially at fixed inference costs.

3. **Inference Efficiency**: BLT models can process text with fewer sequential steps, leading to faster inference times.

The researchers conducted a "flop-controlled scaling study" to ensure fair comparisons between different architectures. This involved training models with matched computational budgets and comparing their performance using bits-per-byte (BPB), a tokenizer-independent metric.

Additional experiments explored the impact of model parameters like patch size and width on performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Real-world Applications and Benefits

BLT's ability to process raw bytes directly leads to several practical advantages:

1. **Improved Handling of Noisy Input**: BLT shows enhanced robustness to typos, misspellings, and other text variations, making it more reliable for real-world applications where input quality varies.

2. **Enhanced Character-Level Understanding**: The model exhibits better orthographic knowledge, improving performance on tasks requiring character manipulation or phonological understanding.

3. **Better Code Processing**: By processing bytes directly, BLT can handle programming languages more naturally, without the tokenization artifacts that often plague code generation models.

4. **Multilingual Capabilities**: BLT's byte-level approach provides more equitable treatment of different languages and scripts, potentially closing performance gaps between high and low-resource languages.

The researchers evaluated BLT on various downstream tasks, including classification, reasoning, world knowledge assessments, and code generation. The model showed competitive performance across these tasks, demonstrating its versatility and practical utility.

#### 5.2. Future Implications and Research Directions

The success of BLT opens several promising avenues for future research:

1. **Entropy-based Compute Allocation**: The principle of allocating more compute to uncertain or complex inputs could be extended to other model architectures beyond language models.

2. **Multimodal Applications**: BLT's byte-level approach could potentially handle multiple data types in a unified way, from text to audio to structured data.

3. **Compression Techniques**: The findings could inspire new approaches to data compression, such as improved arithmetic coding methods.

4. **Scaling to Larger Models**: The improved scaling properties suggest BLT could continue to benefit from increasing model size, potentially leading to even more capable systems.

5. **Transfer Learning**: Pre-training on bytes instead of tokens might enable better transfer across domains and modalities, reducing the need for domain-specific pre-training.

The researchers also explored using pre-trained models to kickstart BLT training, showing promising results for hybrid approaches that leverage the strengths of both token-based and byte-based models.

#### 5.3. Conclusion

The Byte Latent Transformer represents a fundamental rethinking of how language models process text. By operating directly on bytes rather than tokens, BLT eliminates many limitations of traditional tokenization while achieving comparable or better performance at scale.

Key advantages of BLT include:
- Elimination of fixed vocabularies and their associated biases
- Dynamic allocation of computational resources based on content complexity
- Improved handling of out-of-vocabulary words and noisy inputs
- Better character-level understanding and multilingual capabilities
- Enhanced scaling properties, particularly for inference efficiency

While tokenization has been a cornerstone of language model design for years, BLT demonstrates that alternative approaches can match or exceed its performance while offering additional benefits. As language models continue to evolve, byte-level processing could become increasingly important, especially for applications requiring robustness, multilingual capabilities, or character-level understanding.

The research from Meta AI and their collaborators not only introduces a powerful new architecture but also challenges fundamental assumptions about how language models should process text. By questioning these assumptions and exploring alternatives, they've opened promising new directions for the field's continued advancement.
