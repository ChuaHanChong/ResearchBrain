# Falcon Perception-HD: High Density Perception via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Falcon Perception-HD post-trains an autoregressive open-vocabulary segmentation model with GRPO, using a Hungarian-matched set reward that penalizes false negatives and positives, sampling only the LM and coordinate heads while keeping size and mask heads frozen. This resolves sequence collapse in dense scenes, eliminates need for NMS/deduplication, and sets new state-of-the-art on PBench and SACO.

## Problem

- Autoregressive perception models are trained via maximum likelihood on a fixed-order reference sequence, misaligned with order-invariant set-matching metrics like precision and recall.
- MLE restricts policies to ground-truth trajectories, leaving models blind to consequences of their own errors, causing duplicated predictions and sequence collapse in dense scenes.
- Prior RL-for-vision works rely on ground-truth-guided greedy matching or auxiliary models like SAM for scoring, and none address extreme-density perception with heterogeneous multi-head action spaces.

## Method

- ==GRPO== with group-relative advantages restricted to sampling only the LM and coordinate heads, exploiting a ==cascade effect== where better center localization improves frozen downstream size and mask heads.
- A ==Hungarian-assignment count reward== that penalizes false negatives at weight one and false positives at weight alpha=0.3, requiring no auxiliary scoring model and no localization-quality term.
- ==Dr. GRPO== length-unbiased loss aggregation plus ==stop-gradient on existence tokens==, and two self-annotation pipelines (human-refined dense pseudo-labels, GPT-5-judged high-variance rollouts) for RL data curation.

## Results

- On PBench, Falcon Perception-HD improves average F1 by **2.6 points** over the SFT baseline, with dense-split gains scaling monotonically from **+1.5** (0-2 objects) to **+21.5** F1 (300+ objects).
- RL removes NMS and coordinate-deduplication dependence: mask redundancy rate drops from **5.7% to 0.7%**, and removing all post-processing costs only **0.4** pmF1 points versus **12.0** points for the SFT baseline.
- On SACO, macro-F1 and pmF1 improve by **1.7 points** and MCC by **2 points** despite training only on positive queries, while extending the recipe to a 70M-parameter Pix2Seq model on VisDrone/COCO reduces the raw-to-NMS gap from **+23.1/+10.9** to **+0.2** points.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Restricting RL exploration to a subset of action heads can still drive holistic quality gains elsewhere via a cascade effect, when architecture conditions downstream heads on earlier predictions.
> - Group-relative advantage RL naturally suppresses duplicate or under-covering rollouts, acting as a learned substitute for hand-tuned heuristics like NMS across different autoregressive architectures.
> - Positive-only RL training can silently erode a model's negative/absence calibration through reward-correlated gradient drift; stop-gradient on unsampled decision tokens preserves it without needing negative examples.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of reinforcement learning (RL) post-training and autoregressive perception models in computer vision. RL post-training has become a standard practice in large language models (LLMs) to align model outputs with desired behaviors and human preferences, addressing the inherent misalignment between maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) training objectives (per-token cross-entropy) and human evaluation metrics. However, its application to computer vision, particularly for dense perception tasks such as detecting, localizing, and segmenting multiple objects, has been less explored.

Autoregressive perception models, exemplified by architectures like Pix2Seq and Falcon Perception (the base model for this work), offer the theoretical capability to generate an unbounded number of instances. However, these models, when trained solely with MLE, often exhibit limitations. These include assigning high likelihoods to degenerate sequences, producing duplicated predictions, generating overlapping masks, and experiencing catastrophic sequence collapse in highly dense visual scenes. This is attributed to the fundamental misalignment between MLE's per-token optimization and perception's order-invariant, set-matching evaluation metrics (e.g., precision, recall, F1-score). Existing solutions often rely on post-hoc heuristics like Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) and coordinate deduplication, which can be fragile, require hyperparameter tuning, and may incorrectly suppress valid detections in crowded scenes.

This work addresses these limitations by investigating the direct application of RL post-training to autoregressive perception models. It aims to bridge the gap between autoregressive generation and spatial evaluation by enabling models to directly optimize set-matching rewards, thereby overcoming the failure modes associated with MLE training in dense perception scenarios. The paper builds upon Falcon Perception, an early-fusion perception model with a "chain-of-perception" architecture, which is suitable for exploring unbounded instance generation and high-resolution mask prediction. The study explores how RL can improve mask quality, eliminate the need for post-processing heuristics, and enhance performance in extreme-density environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether reinforcement learning (RL) post-training can effectively align autoregressive perception models with their evaluation metrics, such as precision and recall, directly addressing the limitations of traditional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) based on maximum likelihood estimation (MLE).

The motivation for this investigation stems from several observations regarding current autoregressive perception models:
- **Misalignment of Training Objectives:** Autoregressive perception models are typically trained using MLE, optimizing a per-token cross-entropy objective. This objective is a proxy and is fundamentally misaligned with set-matching perception metrics (e.g., precision, recall, F1), which are order-invariant. This misalignment often leads to models assigning high likelihoods to sequences that are visually degenerate.
- **Degradation in Dense Scenes:** MLE-trained models frequently suffer from issues like duplicated predictions, overlapping masks, and catastrophic sequence collapse when processing very dense visual scenes containing a large number of objects (e.g., hundreds per image). Their performance degrades sharply or collapses in such regimes.
- **Reliance on Post-Hoc Heuristics:** To mitigate these issues, MLE-trained models often require post-processing heuristics like Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) and coordinate deduplication. These heuristics introduce hyperparameters that require tuning and can incorrectly suppress valid detections, particularly for depth-distributed or heavily overlapping objects.
- **Lack of Direct Metric Optimization:** Unlike RL, MLE training restricts the policy to ground-truth trajectories, preventing the model from exploring its own predictions and learning from the consequences of its errors in terms of the actual evaluation metrics.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of this work include:
- **Designing an RL Framework for Perception:** Developing an RL framework, specifically using GRPO, tailored to perception-specific challenges, including reward design for set-structured outputs and controlling a heterogeneous, multi-head sampling process (language, center, size, mask).
- **Unlocking High-Density Performance:** Enhancing the model's ability to localize visual entities robustly in very dense scenes, a regime where existing systems typically fail.
- **Eliminating Post-Processing Needs:** Reducing or removing the necessity for NMS and coordinate deduplication by enabling the model to structurally internalize spatial suppression and avoid duplicate or overlapping predictions inherently.
- **Improving Referring Expression Segmentation:** Demonstrating performance improvements across various difficulty levels in referring expression segmentation benchmarks.
- **Preserving Existence Knowledge:** Finding a method to maintain the model's ability to determine if an object exists in a scene (as measured by MCC) without requiring training on negative samples in the RL phase.
- **Developing Self-Annotation Pipelines:** Creating hybrid self-annotation pipelines specifically for difficult referring expressions and very dense scenes to generate high-quality training signals for RL.
- **Validating the Cascade Effect:** Investigating whether restricting RL action sampling to discrete coordinate heads can drive holistic improvements in bounding box and mask quality through architectural conditioning.

The Falcon Perception model was selected as the base architecture for this study due to its autoregressive "chain-of-perception" design, which supports unbounded instance generation, and its use of a parallel head for high-resolution masks, which avoids the latency of fully tokenized dense outputs. Its compact parameter count and robust SFT baseline also provide a tractable testbed for RL exploration.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research develops a reinforcement learning framework based on the Falcon Perception model, an autoregressive multimodal model for open-vocabulary segmentation. The methodology focuses on directly aligning the model with perception metrics using RL post-training.

#### 3.1. Base Model and Architecture

The base model, Falcon Perception, employs a structured, multi-head decoding interface. It emits a "chain-of-perception" sequence for each detected instance: `LM tokens -> center (x,y) -> box size (h,w) -> mask`.
- **LM tokens:** Sampled from a standard LM head.
- **Center coordinates (x,y) and bounding box dimensions (h,w):** Generated as categorical samples over discretized spatial bins by two independent, lightweight heads.
- **Segmentation mask:** Generated deterministically by a parallel continuous head, computing the sigmoid of the inner product between dense image features and the emitted token.

#### 3.2. RL Objective and Training

The objective is to maximize the expected reward `E_o~πθ(·|I,q) [r(Set(B̂(o)), Set(B*))]`, where `o` is a sampled rollout, `B̂(o)` is the set of predicted instances, `B*` is the ground-truth set, and `r(·)` is a scalar set-matching reward.
- **Group-Relative Advantages:** For each prompt, 8 rollouts (`G=8`) are sampled. Their rewards are standardized within the group (`Âi = (ri - μG) / (σG + ε)`) to reduce variance without a separate value function.
- **Multi-Head Log-Probability:** The policy gradient `∇θ log πθ(at|st)` decomposes across heads. During RL training, only the LM and coordinate heads are sampled at training temperature. The size head is decoded greedily, and the segmentation head is frozen, leveraging a "cascade effect" where improved discrete spatial predictions enhance deterministic downstream outputs.
- **On-Policy Updates with Engine-Mismatch Importance Sampling:** A detached importance sampling ratio (`w(i)t = sg(πθ(a(i)t | s(i)t) / πθ_rollout(a(i)t | s(i)t))`) is applied. This corrects for small discrepancies between the rollout-time log-probability (from the inference engine) and the training-time recomputation (from the training engine) without contributing additional gradient through the ratio itself, ensuring an unbiased estimator.
- **Length-Unbiased Loss Aggregation (Dr. GRPO):** To prevent bias towards shorter responses, the per-rollout `1/Ti` factor in standard GRPO is replaced with a fixed constant `1/(B · Tmax)`. This ensures each token's gradient contribution is independent of its rollout's length, crucial for dense scenes requiring long sequences.
- **Regularization:** Clip-Cov is employed to preserve exploration and prevent premature distribution sharpening. It zeros the policy-gradient contribution for a small fraction of tokens with the strongest log-probability and advantage correlation, specifically applied per head with separate covariance bounds.

#### 3.3. Reward Function

A simple count reward `r_count = -(FN + α · FP)` (with `α = 0.3`) is used.
- Predictions are paired with ground truth instances via Hungarian assignment on the IoU matrix, accepting pairs with IoU `> τ=0.5`.
- `TP` (True Positives) are accepted pairs, `FP` (False Positives) are unmatched predictions, and `FN` (False Negatives) are unmatched ground-truth instances.
- The asymmetric weighting (`FN` with weight 1, `FP` with weight `α=0.3`) prioritizes recall, as under-prediction is identified as a harder failure mode in dense regimes.

#### 3.4. Stop-Gradient on Existence Tokens

The log-probabilities of existence markers (`<object_found>` or `<no_object_found>`) emitted by the LM head are excluded from the policy gradient using a stop-gradient. This is because RL is only run on positive queries. Without detachment, the model would consistently reinforce `<object_found>`, losing its ability to discriminate scenes where objects are absent (collapsing MCC), despite not seeing negative samples. Detachment preserves the SFT-learned existence boundary.

#### 3.5. Data Annotation Pipeline

Two hybrid self-annotation pipelines were designed to generate high-quality training signals:
- **Dense Scenes:** Falcon Perception generates pseudo-labels on 2.5k curated dense images. These raw outputs, which often include duplicates, fragments, or missed instances, are then refined by human annotators to create high-fidelity ground truth for RL training.
- **Hard Referring Expression Samples:** Falcon Perception is used for inference on 200k image-expression pairs, generating one greedy prediction and 8 sampled rollouts. The pipeline focuses on "high-variance" samples where the greedy prediction fails, but at least one sampled rollout succeeds. GPT-5 acts as a judge to evaluate and select the most plausible candidate rollout as the ground truth. This strategy ensures the training data provides a meaningful learning signal (greedy mode is wrong) within the current policy's support (some rollout is right).

#### 3.6. Training Details

Training was conducted for 600 steps using the Muon optimizer for ≥2D weight tensors and Lion for 1D parameters, with a learning rate of `10^-5` (linear decay to `10^-6`). A global batch size of 64 on 64 A100 GPUs was used, with group packing and FlexAttention for efficient processing of sequences up to 6,144 context tokens and 1,856 new tokens.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the application of RL post-training to autoregressive perception models.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Improvements

Falcon Perception-HD established new performance levels on the PBench benchmark, demonstrating improvements across all difficulty levels (Table 1). It achieved an average F1 increase of 2.6 points. Notably, on Level 4 (difficult relational expressions), performance improved by 2.7 F1 points, surpassing larger generalist Vision-Language Models (VLMs). On the SACO benchmark (Table 3), the model improved by 1.7 points in macro-F1 and pmF1 and by 2% in MCC, closing the gap with some query-based models.

#### 4.2. Unlocking High-Density Perception

One of the most significant effects of RL post-training was the substantial improvement in performance on very dense scenes. Falcon Perception-HD exhibited an 8.2 F1 point increase on the PBench dense split (Table 1), effectively addressing sequence collapse and low recall prevalent in crowded scenes. Analysis revealed that the performance gain monotonically increased with scene density (Table 4), ranging from +1.5 F1 for 0-2 objects to +21.5 F1 for scenes with over 300 objects. This outcome is attributed to the policy gradient's ability to penalize rollouts that under-cover the scene, preventing premature sequence termination.

#### 4.3. Learned NMS: Removal of Inference-Time Deduplication

RL post-training enabled Falcon Perception-HD to internally learn spatial suppression, largely eliminating the need for post-hoc heuristics such as center-deduplication and Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS).
- Removing coordinate deduplication from Falcon Perception (SFT-only baseline) resulted in a 7.5 F1 point drop on the PBench dense split (Table 5). In contrast, Falcon Perception-HD's performance was minimally affected (-0.2 F1), indicating its inherent ability to avoid redundant predictions.
- On the SACO benchmark, removing both NMS and deduplication caused Falcon Perception to drop by 12.0 pmF1 points, with a Mask Redundancy Rate (MRR) of 36.2%. Falcon Perception-HD, however, experienced only a 0.4 pmF1 point drop and maintained a low MRR of 2.1% without any post-processing (Table 6). This suggests that the RL framework directly addresses the generation of duplicate masks and coordinates during training.

#### 4.4. Disentangling RL from Curated Data

An ablation study demonstrated that while Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on the curated dense data improved performance on dense splits, it also exacerbated over-generation and increased reliance on post-processing heuristics. For example, SFT on curated data increased masks per image from 182 to 248 and MRR without deduplication from 35.1% to 46.4% (Table 7). In contrast, models post-trained with RL (whether from the original SFT baseline or the curated-data SFT model) rectified these repetition issues, reduced latency, and improved overall performance, indicating that the RL objective, not just the data, was critical for these gains.

#### 4.5. The Cascade Effect

The research validated the "cascade effect," where sampling and propagating gradients only through the LM and coordinate heads were sufficient to improve overall perception quality. Sampling the size head yielded no additional benefits (Table 2), indicating that enhanced center localization directly improved the hidden state passed to downstream heads, leading to better bounding box and mask predictions. This effect was quantified by observing a 63% reduction in coordinate-head entropy and a 21% reduction in the size-head entropy, even though the size head was frozen and greedily decoded (Table 9). Furthermore, the quality of generated segmentation masks improved, often exceeding the fidelity of the self-annotated ground truth (Figure 2), by filtering out "salt and pepper" noise.

#### 4.6. Preserving Existence Calibration

The application of a stop-gradient mechanism on existence tokens (`<object_found>` / `<no_object_found>`) was critical for preserving the model's ability to discriminate between scenes containing or lacking the queried object. Without this detachment, RL training (conducted only on positive queries) caused MCC (Matthews Correlation Coefficient) to collapse across SACO splits (e.g., from 59.6 to 38.8), as the policy became over-optimistic in predicting object existence. With detachment, MCC recovered and improved beyond the pre-RL baseline (to 62.2 on average), while still showing appropriate increases in pmF1 and macro F1 (Table 10).

#### 4.7. Generalizability and Comparison to DETR-style Models

The efficacy of RL in removing NMS and improving dense perception was also demonstrated on a Pix2Seq-style model trained for closed-vocabulary detection (Table 14). This independent validation replicated the core findings, suggesting the approach generalizes beyond Falcon Perception. The study also highlighted that while DETR-style models solve the repetition problem natively via bipartite matching and do not benefit from RL post-training (Table 17), autoregressive models can dynamically generate an unbounded number of objects, a capability that RL helps to realize robustly, outperforming fixed-query models in extremely dense scenes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Falcon Perception-HD" establishes the significance of reinforcement learning (RL) post-training as a method for addressing fundamental architectural and training limitations in autoregressive perception models.

**Fundamental Paradigm Shift:** The work demonstrates that RL can serve as more than a minor post-processing step; it can fundamentally align autoregressive perception models with order-invariant spatial metrics, rectifying structural flaws inherent in Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) training. This suggests a shift towards RL as a critical component in the training pipeline for vision models, similar to its established role in Large Language Models (LLMs).

**Unlocking Robust High-Density Perception:** Falcon Perception-HD addresses a critical limitation of existing perception systems: their degradation or collapse in very dense scenes (up to 500-600 objects per image). By achieving state-of-the-art performance in these extreme-density regimes, the model enables more robust and scalable real-world applications in areas requiring precise instance counting and localization, such as ecological monitoring, agricultural inventory, and complex scene analysis.

**Enhanced Efficiency and Reliability through Learned Spatial Suppression:** The ability of RL to enable the model to internalize spatial suppression, thereby eliminating the need for fragile post-processing heuristics like NMS and coordinate deduplication, represents a significant impact. This not only streamlines the inference pipeline, potentially accelerating processing, but also removes sources of error where NMS might incorrectly suppress genuine, overlapping instances. The inherent deduplication capability makes the model outputs more reliable and robust to varying scene complexities.

**Improved Generalization and Task Performance:** The improvements observed across various difficulty levels in referring expression segmentation, including complex compositional and relational expressions, indicate a broader enhancement of the model's understanding and localization capabilities. This generalizes the applicability of autoregressive perception models to more nuanced and challenging visual language tasks.

**Methodological Innovations:** The development of perception-specific RL techniques, such as the reward design for set-structured outputs, multi-head sampling control, length-unbiased loss aggregation (Dr. GRPO), and the stop-gradient mechanism for existence tokens, contributes methodological advancements to the field of vision-language RL. The demonstrated "cascade effect," where improvements in discrete spatial predictions propagate to deterministic downstream heads without direct gradients, offers insights into efficient multi-modal model training.

**Scalability and Broader Applicability:** By demonstrating these benefits on Falcon Perception and independently on a Pix2Seq-style model, the research suggests that the RL post-training approach is scalable and applicable to a broader class of autoregressive vision models. This paves the way for building more robust perception systems that do not rely on inference-time heuristics, mirroring the trajectory of LLMs in achieving advanced reasoning capabilities through RL alignment.
