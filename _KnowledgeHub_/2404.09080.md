# Safe Reinforcement Learning on the Constraint Manifold: Theory and Applications

> [!summary] Summary
> A method called ATACOM enables safe online reinforcement learning for robots by projecting control actions onto the tangent space of a constraint manifold, ensuring adherence to safety specifications throughout training. Applied to a KUKA iiwa14 robot for Air Hockey, the framework achieved online policy fine-tuning, boosting the success rate from 12% to 71% with near-zero constraint violations.

## Problem

- Uncontrolled exploration during online reinforcement learning on real robots poses a high risk of catastrophic damage or safety violations.
- Existing Safe Reinforcement Learning (SafeRL) approaches often guarantee safety only for the final learned policy, not during the entire learning process, limiting their utility for real-world online adaptation.
- Current Safe Exploration methods can incur computational overhead from iterative constrained optimization or require task-specific safety mechanisms like backup policies.

## Method

- ATACOM reformulates inequality safety constraints into equality constraints by introducing non-negative slack variables, defining a "Constraint Manifold" in an augmented state space.
- A safe controller is derived that projects arbitrary actions from the Reinforcement Learning agent onto the tangent space of this manifold, ensuring that control inputs keep the system on or rapidly return it to the safe region.
- The framework integrates a contraction term to retract states to the manifold and employs a continuously varying tangent space basis to avoid discontinuities in the action space.

## Results

- ATACOM successfully ensured safety across various simulated environments, with exponential slack dynamics generally leading to better learning performance by less conservatively deforming the action space.
- On a real KUKA iiwa14 robot, ATACOM enabled safe online fine-tuning of an Air Hockey policy, improving the success rate from 12% to 71% with near-zero constraint violations over 3000 episodes.
- The method exhibited superior safety and robustness to disturbances compared to Control Barrier Function (CBF) based Quadratic Programming (QP) approaches in a quadrotor environment, while also being computationally more efficient due to its direct action mapping.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing safety constraints as a manifold in an augmented state space allows for a principled, geometric approach to inherently guarantee safety during online learning.
> - Projecting an RL agent's actions onto the tangent space of this constraint manifold offers a computationally efficient and direct method to ensure safety without requiring iterative optimization or pre-defined backup policies.
> - The theoretical analysis demonstrates that the system achieves convergence to the constraint manifold even under stochastic policies and maintains Input-to-State Stability (ISS) in the presence of disturbances.
