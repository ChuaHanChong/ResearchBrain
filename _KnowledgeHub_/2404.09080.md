# Safe Reinforcement Learning on the Constraint Manifold: Theory and Applications

> [!summary] Summary
> A method called ATACOM enables safe online reinforcement learning for robots by projecting control actions onto the tangent space of a constraint manifold, ensuring adherence to safety specifications throughout training. Applied to a KUKA iiwa14 robot for Air Hockey, the framework achieved online policy fine-tuning, boosting the success rate from 12% to 71% with near-zero constraint violations.

## Problem

- Uncontrolled exploration during online reinforcement learning on real robots poses a high risk of catastrophic damage or safety violations.
- Existing Safe Reinforcement Learning (SafeRL) approaches often guarantee safety only for the final learned policy, not during the entire learning process, limiting their utility for real-world online adaptation.
- Current Safe Exploration methods can incur computational overhead from iterative constrained optimization or require task-specific safety mechanisms like backup policies.

## Method

- ATACOM reformulates inequality safety constraints into equality constraints by introducing non-negative slack variables, defining a "Constraint Manifold" in an augmented state space.
- A safe controller is derived that projects arbitrary actions from the Reinforcement Learning agent onto the tangent space of this manifold, ensuring that control inputs keep the system on or rapidly return it to the safe region.
- The framework integrates a contraction term to retract states to the manifold and employs a continuously varying tangent space basis to avoid discontinuities in the action space.

## Results

- ATACOM successfully ensured safety across various simulated environments, with exponential slack dynamics generally leading to better learning performance by less conservatively deforming the action space.
- On a real KUKA iiwa14 robot, ATACOM enabled safe online fine-tuning of an Air Hockey policy, improving the success rate from 12% to 71% with near-zero constraint violations over 3000 episodes.
- The method exhibited superior safety and robustness to disturbances compared to Control Barrier Function (CBF) based Quadratic Programming (QP) approaches in a quadrotor environment, while also being computationally more efficient due to its direct action mapping.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing safety constraints as a manifold in an augmented state space allows for a principled, geometric approach to inherently guarantee safety during online learning.
> - Projecting an RL agent's actions onto the tangent space of this constraint manifold offers a computationally efficient and direct method to ensure safety without requiring iterative optimization or pre-defined backup policies.
> - The theoretical analysis demonstrates that the system achieves convergence to the constraint manifold even under stochastic policies and maintains Input-to-State Stability (ISS) in the presence of disturbances.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of learning-based techniques, particularly Reinforcement Learning (RL), offers immense promise for enabling robots to solve complex tasks in unstructured, dynamic environments. Classical robotics methods often struggle with real-world complexity, making data-driven approaches attractive. However, the current landscape of robot learning largely relies on extensive training in well-tuned simulators, followed by deployment in the real world without further online adaptation. This "sim-to-real gap" necessitates significant engineering effort, limiting the applicability to complex systems and dynamic scenarios.

The ideal alternative is *online reinforcement learning with real-world interaction data*, which eliminates the sim-to-real gap and allows robots to adapt to changing environments, wear-and-tear, or sensor malfunctions. The primary impediment to online RL on real robots is **safety**. Uncontrolled exploration can lead to catastrophic damage to the robot or its environment, as safety specifications are often complex, non-linear constraints.

This paper positions itself within the field of **Safe Reinforcement Learning (SafeRL)**, specifically focusing on **Safe Exploration**. Traditional SafeRL approaches, often based on Constrained Markov Decision Processes (CMDPs), typically guarantee that the *final learned policy* is safe but do not prevent constraint violations *during the learning process*. This makes them unsuitable for real-world online adaptation. Safe Exploration methods aim to ensure safety throughout the entire learning process, often by integrating prior knowledge (robot dynamics, pre-defined constraints) and employing safety filters (e.g., quadratic programs, backup actions).

The authors note that existing Safe Exploration methods, while effective, often introduce computational overhead (solving constrained optimization problems iteratively) or require task-specific design (backup policies). This work aims to overcome these limitations by introducing a novel approach, ATACOM (Acting on the TAngent Space of the COnstraint Manifold), which constructs a *safe action space* directly, eliminating the need for iterative optimization or backup policies, thus making it compatible with *any* RL algorithm and inherently safe by design. This represents a significant advancement in bridging the gap between theoretical SafeRL and practical, real-world robotic deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations of this research can be summarized as follows:

- **Enable Safe Online Reinforcement Learning on Real Robots:** The primary motivation is to overcome the critical challenge of ensuring safety during the online learning process for robots interacting in the real world. This is crucial for enabling robots to adapt to dynamic environments and bridge the sim-to-real gap without risking damage.
- **Provide a Principled Framework for Complex Safety Constraints:** The authors aim to establish a theoretically sound and practically applicable method for imposing complex, non-linear safety constraints on learning-based robotic systems.
- **Construct an Inherently Safe Action Space:** Unlike methods that correct unsafe actions or rely on backup policies, the goal is to design a mechanism that *guarantees* any sampled action from the learned policy will be safe by construction, without requiring iterative constrained optimization.
- **Extend and Deepen the Analysis of ATACOM:** The paper serves to extend the previously introduced ATACOM method by providing a new formulation, rigorous theoretical analysis (including stability under disturbances and stochastic policies), and practical implementation techniques.
- **Demonstrate Efficacy in High-Dimensional, Real-World Tasks:** A key objective is to validate ATACOM's effectiveness in challenging robotic scenarios, specifically demonstrating safe online fine-tuning in a contact-rich, dynamic task like Robot Air Hockey.

In essence, the authors are motivated to facilitate the deployment of robust and adaptive RL agents in real-world robotics by addressing the fundamental problem of safety during exploration and learning, offering a flexible and computationally efficient solution based on geometric principles.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology, ATACOM (Acting on the TAngent Space of the COnstraint Manifold), is rooted in differential geometry and control theory.

**A. Constraint Manifold and Augmented State Dynamics:**
The approach begins by reformulating inequality safety constraints, $k(s) \leq 0$, into equality constraints by introducing a set of non-negative slack variables, $\mu$. This leads to the definition of a "Constraint Manifold" $M = \{(s, \mu) \in D : c(s, \mu) = k(s) + \mu = 0\}$ in an augmented state space $D = S \times [0, +\infty)^K$. The safety set in the original state space $S$ is then a projection of this manifold.
A dynamic model is introduced for the slack variables, $\dot{\mu}_i = \alpha_i(\mu_i)u_{\mu,i}$, where $\alpha_i$ is a class K function ensuring $\mu_i$ does not reach zero in finite time if initially positive. This augmented state dynamic model, combining the robot's dynamics with the slack variable dynamics, forms the basis for the safe controller.

**B. Safe Controller on the Tangent Space:**
The key insight is that if the augmented state $(s, \mu)$ remains on the constraint manifold $M$, the original robot state $s$ remains within the safe set. To achieve this, the velocity of the augmented state must lie within the tangent space $T_{(s,\mu)}M$. This translates to the condition $\dot{c}(s, \mu) = J_c(s, \mu) \begin{bmatrix} \dot{s} \\ \dot{\mu} \end{bmatrix} = 0$.
The ATACOM controller is then derived as:
$\begin{bmatrix} u_s \\ u_\mu \end{bmatrix} = -J_u^\dagger \psi - \lambda J_u^\dagger c + B_u u$,
where:
- $u_s$ and $u_\mu$ are the control inputs for the robot and slack variables, respectively.
- $\psi$ is the constraint drift term, resulting from the system's inherent dynamics.
- $J_u^\dagger$ is the pseudo-inverse of the combined Jacobian $J_u$ (related to $J_k G$ and $A(\mu)$).
- $-\lambda J_u^\dagger c$ is a **Contraction Term** (with $\lambda > 0$) that retracts the state back to the manifold if it deviates.
- $B_u$ is a basis for the null space (tangent space) of $J_u$.
- $u$ is the arbitrary control input from the RL agent.
This construction ensures that any action $u$ sampled by the RL agent, when transformed by ATACOM, results in a control input that keeps the system on or rapidly returns it to the constraint manifold.

**C. Theoretical Guarantees:**
- **LaSalle's Invariance Principle:** Using a Lyapunov-like function $V(s, \mu) = \frac{1}{2}c^T c$, the authors prove that under certain assumptions (compact state space, continuously differentiable constraint function, full rank of relevant Jacobians, non-empty and non-singular constraint manifold), any trajectory starting within a defined "region of attraction" $\Omega_\eta$ will converge to the constraint manifold $M$ as $t \to \infty$.
- **Stochastic Policies and Switched Systems:** The framework is extended to stochastic policies (common in RL) by treating the system as a switched system. It's shown that the invariance principle still holds, with trajectories attracted to $M$.
- **Input-to-State Stability (ISS) with Disturbances:** For real-world scenarios with model mismatch and external disturbances ($\epsilon$), the system is proven to be Input-to-State Stable (ISS). This means that with bounded disturbances, the system ensures bounded constraint violations, approaching a neighborhood of the constraint manifold.

**D. Practical Implementation Techniques:**
- **Slack Variable Dynamics:** Different forms of $\alpha(\mu)$ (linear vs. exponential) are explored, influencing how the tangent space is deformed. Exponential slack dynamics with higher parameters generally offer better performance but require higher control frequencies due to numerical stability.
- **Continuously Varying Tangent Space Basis:** To avoid discontinuities in the action space, a method based on the Orthogonal Procrustes Problem (Algorithm 3) is employed to construct a smoothly varying tangent basis, addressing a common issue with standard QR/SVD decompositions.
- **Drift Clipping:** The drift compensation term is clipped to be non-negative, meaning the system only compensates for drift that pushes it towards the unsafe region. This prevents over-conservative behavior when the natural dynamics pull the system away from the boundary.

**E. Extensions:**
- **Dynamical Environment with Separable State Space:** For environments where not all states are directly controllable (e.g., moving obstacles), the state is separated into Directly Controllable (DCS) and Directly Uncontrollable (DUS) components. The drift term is augmented to include the influence of DUS motion, allowing the controller to compensate for dynamic environmental elements.
- **Second-Order Dynamics:** For acceleration-controlled systems, simply converting to a first-order system with position-only constraints fails. Instead, a higher-order Control Barrier Function (CBF) like approach is used to redefine the constraint to include velocity information, $k^*(s, \dot{s}) = \zeta(k(s)) + \dot{k}(s) \leq 0$, effectively making it a first-order constraint in terms of system velocities, allowing ATACOM to apply.
- **Equality Constraints:** The method can be extended to handle equality constraints by appropriately augmenting the constraint function $c(s, \mu)$ and its Jacobian $J_u$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides comprehensive experimental validation in both simulated and real-world robotic environments, demonstrating ATACOM's effectiveness across various settings:

- **Impact of Slack Dynamics (2D-StaticEnv):**
 - All tested slack dynamics (linear and exponential) successfully ensured safety with no collisions during training.
 - Exponential slack dynamics with higher $\beta$ parameters generally led to better learning performance (higher discounted return) because they caused less deformation of the action space, allowing the RL agent to explore more effectively without overly conservative behavior.
 - However, stiffer slack dynamics (higher $\beta$) require higher control frequencies to maintain numerical stability due to the increased curvature of the manifold.

- **Velocity Observation in Dynamic Environments (2D-DynamicEnv):**
 - **Exact Velocity:** Provided the best performance and safety, though still showing some collision rates in very challenging scenarios (high obstacle speed, many obstacles) where the robot's control authority was insufficient to avoid impending collisions.
 - **Finite Difference (FD):** Achieved comparable safety and success rates to exact velocity in low to medium-velocity settings and with fewer obstacles, demonstrating its practical utility despite measurement noise.
 - **No Velocity (NONE):** Assuming a static environment at each timestep (i.e., ignoring DUS velocity) led to significantly worse safety performance and success rates, highlighting the importance of accurate DUS velocity estimation for dynamic environments.

- **Safe Control with Disturbances (QuadrotorEnv):**
 - ATACOM demonstrated superior safety (higher "Safe Ratio") and greater robustness to disturbances compared to Control Barrier Function (CBF) based Quadratic Programming (QP) methods (using both non-differentiable OSQP and differentiable CvxpyLayers solvers).
 - ATACOM's direct mapping for control actions made it computationally more efficient than CBF-QP, which relies on iterative optimization. This efficiency, while marginal in total training time, is advantageous for real-time control.
 - The difference was attributed to ATACOM's proactive adjustment of actions as the system approaches the boundary, versus QP-based methods that react *after* a potential violation is detected or imminent.

- **Dynamic Mismatch in Air Hockey Simulation:**
 - The study quantified the impact of dynamic model mismatch (by varying the nominal system's maximum velocity relative to the actual limit).
 - Underestimated nominal dynamics (e.g., 50% of actual limit) resulted in fewer constraint violations but lower performance (puck velocity, success rate).
 - Overestimated nominal dynamics led to higher performance but significantly increased violation rates, as the tracking controller would truncate infeasible commands, causing unsafe behavior.
 - Using an accurate nominal model achieved the best trade-off between performance and safety, confirming the theory.

- **Real Robot Air Hockey (Online Fine-tuning):**
 - ATACOM successfully enabled safe *online fine-tuning* of an RL policy on a real KUKA iiwa14 robot in a high-dimensional, contact-rich Air Hockey task.
 - Initial sim-trained policy suffered a significant performance drop (87% sim success to 12% real-world success) due to the sim-to-real gap.
 - After 3000 episodes (approx. 24 hours) of online fine-tuning with ATACOM, the success rate significantly improved from 12% to 71%, with near-zero constraint violations throughout the training.
 - The fine-tuned RL agent adapted to real-world dynamics, outperforming a planning/optimization-based baseline in adaptability, particularly with uneven puck drift. This demonstrates ATACOM's critical role in enabling real-world adaptive robot learning while maintaining safety.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a highly significant contribution to the field of robot learning and safe AI, with substantial potential impact:

- **Enabling Safe Online Learning for Real Robots:** This is perhaps the most critical impact. By providing a principled and robust method to guarantee safety *during the entire learning process*, ATACOM directly addresses a major bottleneck preventing the widespread adoption of online reinforcement learning on physical robots. This opens doors for robots to continuously learn and adapt in dynamic, unstructured real-world environments without requiring extensive and costly manual supervision or risk mitigation strategies.
- **Principled Integration of Safety and Learning:** The novel formulation of safety constraints as a "Constraint Manifold" and the exploitation of differential geometry (tangent spaces) offer a theoretically elegant and robust framework for handling complex, non-linear safety specifications. This approach is more generalizable than many task-specific safety filters.
- **Computational Efficiency and Compatibility with RL:** ATACOM's ability to construct a safe action space through a direct, explicit mapping (matrix decomposition and multiplication) makes it computationally efficient compared to iterative optimization-based methods like CBF-QP. Crucially, its output can directly feed any arbitrary RL algorithm, making it highly versatile and compatible with existing and future RL advancements. This explicit mapping also facilitates gradient computation for scenarios involving joint learning of constraints and policies.
- **Robustness to Real-World Imperfections:** The theoretical analysis and empirical validation against disturbances and dynamic model mismatches highlight ATACOM's robustness, a vital characteristic for real-world deployment where perfect models are rare. The study on dynamic mismatch provides crucial insights for practitioners in setting up safe learning systems.
- **Advancing Human-Robot Interaction and Complex Manipulation:** As demonstrated in the Air Hockey task and hinted at by prior work mentioned, ATACOM has immediate applications in contact-rich manipulation and human-robot interaction where safety is paramount. Its capability to handle high-dimensional tasks with complex constraints will be invaluable in these areas.

While the current work assumes a time-continuous system (implying high control frequencies) and the existence of feasible actions, it lays a strong foundation for future research. Potential future directions include extending to time-discretized settings, developing methods for automatically designing feasible constraints that account for actuation limits, and integrating long-term safety considerations beyond single-step constraints (e.g., preventing trapping). Overall, ATACOM significantly pushes the boundaries of safe and adaptive robot autonomy, bringing us closer to truly intelligent and safely interacting robotic systems in the real world.
