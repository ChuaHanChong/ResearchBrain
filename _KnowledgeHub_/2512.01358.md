# Modality-Augmented Fine-Tuning of Foundation Robot Policies for Cross-Embodiment Manipulation on GR1 and G1

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Seoul National University developed a framework that enables foundation robot policies to perform cross-embodiment manipulation on GR1 and G1 by leveraging modality-augmented fine-tuning. The approach achieved up to 94% success on the G1 pick-and-place task by integrating contact force and depth information.

## Problem

- Existing foundation robot policies face challenges with cross-embodiment generalization due to data heterogeneity and diverse sensing/action spaces across robots.
- Current VLA models struggle with fine-grained, contact-rich manipulation because public datasets often lack crucial interaction-centric modalities like accurate depth, contact indicators, or force measurements.
- Adapting policies from one robot to another, especially with different morphology and kinematics, remains a significant hurdle for generalist robot controllers.

## Method

- Implemented a ==modality-augmented fine-tuning== strategy on the **GR00T N1.5-3B** foundation model for Fourier GR1 and Unitree G1 robots.
- Created a custom, high-quality multi-modal dataset for the Unitree G1 (==RGB-D==, ==proprioception==, ground-truth ==contact force==) and augmented the public GR1 dataset with synthesized ==depth maps== (via ==ZoeDepth==) and binary ==contact indicators== from simulator collision detection.
- Fine-tuned GR00T by freezing its ==vision tower== and ==VLM backbone==, only updating the ==projector== and embodiment-specific ==state/action encoders==, allowing for efficient adaptation.

## Results

- Zero-shot transfer of GR00T N1.5 to the Unitree G1 failed completely, achieving **0%** success.
- On GR1, adding both depth and contact cues improved success rates from **51%** (RGB only baseline) to **63%** on tasks like "PnP Can to Drawer Close".
- For the G1 "Pick Apple to Bowl" task, fusing contact force signals into the proprioceptive state representation yielded the highest performance at **94%** success, significantly outperforming standard fine-tuning (**48%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modality augmentation, especially with explicit depth and contact information, significantly enhances the robustness and success rates of foundation policies in contact-rich manipulation tasks.
> - Optimal modality design is embodiment-specific; depth augmentation was more impactful for GR1 (simpler gripper), while contact force-aware modalities were crucial for the dexterous G1 hand.
> - High-quality, embodiment-specific multi-modal datasets are essential to bridge the gap between powerful pre-trained foundation models and reliable, real-world robotic deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper is situated within the rapidly evolving landscape of **foundation models for robotics**. Inspired by the success of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) in natural language processing and computer vision, researchers are increasingly developing large-scale, pre-trained **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** for general-purpose robotic control. Prominent examples include Isaac GR00T N1.5, PiZero, RT-2, OpenVLA, and Octo. These models aim to create "generalist controllers" capable of performing diverse manipulation tasks by leveraging massive demonstration datasets and advanced generative techniques like diffusion models for action synthesis.

Despite their impressive capabilities, current foundation robot policies face significant limitations when deployed in complex, real-world manipulation scenarios. The authors highlight several key challenges:
- **Data Heterogeneity and "Data Islands":** Existing robotic datasets are fragmented, collected from diverse embodiments, sensing configurations, and control modes. This heterogeneity prevents VLA models from leveraging internet-scale data as effectively as their language or vision counterparts.
- **Cross-Embodiment Generalization:** Adapting a policy trained on one robot to another with different morphology, kinematics, and action spaces remains a major hurdle. While models like GR00T attempt to address this through embodiment-specific encoders, their generalization is often constrained by the modalities available in the original training data.
- **Limitations in Fine-Grained, Contact-Rich Manipulation:** Many existing datasets, including the public GR1 dataset, lack crucial interaction-centric modalities such as accurate depth information, binary contact indicators, or force measurements. Without these, VLA models struggle to infer contact events, reason about object interaction boundaries, detect stable grasps, or maintain closed-loop stability during tasks requiring precise physical interaction. They must infer such critical information purely from RGB imagery, which is often an ill-posed problem.

This research directly addresses these limitations by proposing a **data-centric and modality-augmented fine-tuning strategy**. It bridges the gap between powerful pre-trained foundation models and the practical demands of reliable, contact-aware manipulation on diverse physical robots. By systematically enriching existing datasets and creating new, high-quality multi-modal data, the authors demonstrate a pathway to enhance the robustness and cross-embodiment transferability of these generalist robot policies. This work contributes to the critical effort of making foundation models truly applicable and robust in complex robotic manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enable a single diffusion-based policy to operate robustly across two distinct robot embodiments (Fourier GR1 and Unitree G1) despite heterogeneous sensing and action modalities**. The authors are motivated by the observation that while foundation robot policies show promise, their practical utility is limited by a lack of crucial sensory information (like depth and contact) in available datasets and the inherent challenges of cross-embodiment generalization.

More specifically, the paper aims to achieve the following:
- **Enhance Foundation Policies for Contact-Rich Tasks:** The authors seek to overcome the limitation of current VLA models struggling with fine-grained manipulation by augmenting datasets with explicit depth and contact information. This is crucial for tasks requiring stable grasps, precise object placement, and reliable interaction-aware control.
- **Improve Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** The research endeavors to develop a method that allows a policy trained on one robot (GR1) to effectively transfer to a morphologically different one (G1). This involves addressing the significant observation-space and action-space mismatches between the two platforms.
- **Develop Modality-Augmentation Techniques:** The paper aims to propose and evaluate specific, lightweight techniques for incorporating additional sensory modalities (depth, binary contact signals, ground-truth force measurements) into the GR00T diffusion policy framework without fundamentally altering its core architecture.
- **Construct High-Quality Multi-Modal Datasets:** A key motivation is the recognition that high-quality, embodiment-specific multi-modal data is essential. For the GR1, this means post-processing existing data, while for the G1, it necessitates building an entirely new dataset that captures critical interaction dynamics.

Ultimately, the motivation is to demonstrate a **unified, data-centric pathway** for extending the capabilities of foundation robot policies, making them more adaptable and robust for real-world deployment across a wider range of robotic platforms and complex manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a **modality-augmented fine-tuning strategy** on the GR00T N1.5-3B foundation model, focusing on two distinct robot embodiments: the Fourier GR1 and the Unitree G1. Their approach is characterized by dataset enrichment and custom dataset creation tailored to the specific sensory and kinematic characteristics of each robot.

**A. G1 Dataset Creation:**
Since no public dataset exists for the Unitree G1, the authors developed a **customized, high-quality multi-modal demonstration dataset** for the "Pick Apple to Bowl" task. This dataset is designed to be rich in physical and perceptual cues for contact-rich manipulation.
- **Trajectory Generation via cuRobo:** GPU-accelerated motion planning using cuRobo generated smooth, collision-free end-effector trajectories. These Cartesian paths were then converted into G1 joint-space commands via an analytical inverse kinematics (IK) solver, ensuring high temporal consistency and physical validity.
- **Proprioception and Joint-Action Recording:** During trajectory execution, the full proprioceptive state (joint positions, velocities, end-effector poses) and the executed joint actions were recorded at each timestep.
- **Ground-Truth Contact Force Measurement:** A crucial aspect of the G1 dataset is the inclusion of accurate contact-force feedback from the dexterous hand's physics engine. Fingertip and palm contact forces were extracted, providing fine-grained interaction cues (grasp stability, slip, contact boundaries).
- **Multi-Modal Observation Structure:** Each demonstration combines RGB-D observations from the onboard camera, full proprioception, cuRobo-generated trajectories, and detailed fingertip/palm contact-force vectors.

**B. Contact Augmentation for GR1 Dataset:**
The public GR1 dataset lacks contact information. To address this, the authors implemented two complementary contact augmentation strategies:
- **Fusing Contact into Proprioceptive State:** A lightweight approach where a binary contact indicator (c_t ∈ {0, 1}), derived from collision detection in the simulator, is concatenated directly to the original proprioceptive state vector. This augmented state (˜s_t = [s_joints_t, c_t]) is then fed through the embodiment-specific state encoder, providing contact-aware embeddings to the GR00T's Diffusion Transformer (DiT).
- **Dedicated Contact Encoder Module:** To treat contact as an independent sensory modality, a separate learnable MLP (ContactEnc) processes the contact signal c_t. The resulting contact embedding (z_contact_t) is then input to the DiT blocks as a distinct token stream, alongside vision, language, and state tokens (Z_t = [z_vision_t, z_text_t, z_state_t, z_contact_t]). This allows the model to learn richer interaction-aware representations by explicitly differentiating contact phases.

**C. Depth Augmentation (RGB-D Fusion) for GR1:**
The GR1 dataset only provides RGB observations. To enable 3D geometric reasoning, metric depth maps were generated for all GR1 demonstrations using ZoeDepth, a state-of-the-art monocular depth estimator.
- **Extending Vision Encoder:** The depth map is concatenated with the RGB channels to form an RGB-D input. GR00T’s Eagle-2 ViT vision encoder's patch embedding layer is expanded from 3 to 4 channels to accommodate the depth information. The depth-channel weights are initialized by averaging the RGB kernel weights to stabilize early training. This early fusion allows the standard ViT encoder to process combined appearance and geometric structure.

**D. Foundation Policy Fine-Tuning:**
The GR00T N1.5-3B base model is fine-tuned using these augmented datasets. Critically, the vision tower and VLM backbone of GR00T are frozen, while only the projector and embodiment-specific state/action encoders are updated. This lightweight fine-tuning strategy aims to adapt the pre-trained foundation model efficiently without retraining its massive core components. Action generation uses GR00T's Diffusion Transformer, which formulates robot control as a conditional denoising process, leveraging transformer-based denoising for scalability and multimodal conditioning. Training employs the AdamW optimizer with a cosine learning rate schedule and bf16 precision.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that modality augmentation consistently improves policy performance across both GR1 and G1 embodiments, with insights into embodiment-specific optimal modality design.

**A. GR1 Experiments: Modality Augmentation Analysis**
Using the publicly available GR1 dataset (with added contact and depth), the authors conducted an ablation study across three manipulation tasks (Pick-and-Place Can to Drawer Close, Potato to Microwave Close, Wine to Cabinet Close).

- **Online Evaluation (Success Rate):**
 - Adding **contact-state cues** provided modest but consistent gains in online success rates.
 - **Depth augmentation (RGB-D fusion)** yielded significant improvements, indicating its importance for visually ambiguous scenarios and 3D geometric reasoning.
 - The **full model, incorporating both depth and contact**, achieved the highest success rate across all tasks, demonstrating complementary benefits. For instance, on the "PnP Can to Drawer Close" task, success improved from a baseline (RGB only) of 51% to 63% with both augmentations.
- **Offline Evaluation (Trajectory MSE):**
 - For the "PnP Can to Drawer Close" task, depth augmentation showed the most substantial improvement in offline action reconstruction quality (lowest MSE), confirming that geometric cues significantly reduce ambiguity in predicting actions.
 - Combining both depth and contact led to the lowest overall error, reinforcing their complementary roles in robust policy learning.

**B. G1 Experiments: Embodiment Transfer with Custom Multi-Modal Data**
The Unitree G1 experiments focused on the "Pick Apple to Bowl" task, using the custom multi-modal dataset with ground-truth contact forces. This allowed for a detailed analysis of embodiment transfer and the impact of contact-rich demonstrations.

- **Zero-Shot GR00T N1.5:** Achieved **0% success**, highlighting the substantial embodiment gap and the failure of direct transfer from GR1 to G1 without specific adaptation.
- **Standard Fine-Tuning:** Fine-tuning with 5,000 embodiment-aligned demonstrations (RGB, proprioception) improved success to **48%**, demonstrating the necessity of platform-specific data.
- **Modality Augmentation with Contact:**
 - Modeling contact force signals as an **auxiliary modality (dedicated contact encoder)** increased success to **74%**. This suggests that contact cues are vital for regulating grasp pressure and mitigating slip.
 - Fusing contact force signals directly into the **proprioceptive state representation** yielded the best performance, reaching an impressive **94% success rate**. This implies that early fusion creates richer latent dynamics and stronger conditioning for the diffusion process.
- **Modality Augmentation with Depth:**
 - Integrating depth features (RGB-D) achieved an **82% success rate**, showing that 3D geometry helps resolve occlusions and stabilize approach trajectories.
- **Cross-Embodiment Insight:** A crucial finding is that the **optimal modality design is embodiment-specific**. For the GR1, depth was the dominant performance factor due to its simpler gripper and reliance on visual cues for interaction. For the G1, with its 7-DoF dexterous hand and explicit force sensing, **contact-force-aware modalities (especially early fusion into proprioception)** provided the most significant gains. Depth alone was insufficient to capture the fine-scale interactions needed for dexterous contact-rich manipulation on the G1.

In summary, the results confirm that tailored modality augmentation, particularly when combined with high-quality, embodiment-specific multi-modal datasets, is critical for successfully adapting foundation robot policies to new platforms and improving performance in contact-rich manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes significant contributions to the field of robotics and embodied AI, offering a practical and effective pathway for advancing the capabilities of foundation robot policies.

**A. Enhanced Robustness and Generalization for Foundation Models:**
The most immediate impact is demonstrating that existing foundation policies, like GR00T, can be significantly enhanced for real-world deployment through **lightweight, data-centric modality augmentation**. By enriching datasets with explicit depth and contact information, the authors show that policies can achieve substantially higher success rates in complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks that often challenge vision-only approaches. This improvement in robustness is critical for deploying generalist robots in unstructured environments.

**B. Practical Pathway for Cross-Embodiment Transfer:**
The work provides a clear methodology for **adapting foundation policies to morphologically distinct robot embodiments**. The zero-shot failure of GR00T on the G1 highlights the severe embodiment gap, underscoring the necessity of the authors' approach. By combining custom multi-modal dataset generation with targeted fine-tuning, the paper shows how to bridge this gap effectively, enabling high success rates on a new platform. This is a crucial step towards truly universal robot policies that can operate across a diverse fleet of robots.

**C. Emphasis on Data Quality and Modality Design:**
A key takeaway is the paramount importance of **high-quality, multi-modal data and careful modality design**. The study highlights that simply using large datasets is insufficient; the *type* of data and *how* it's presented to the model (e.g., fusing contact into state vs. dedicated encoder) significantly impacts performance. Furthermore, the embodiment-specific nature of optimal modality selection (depth for GR1, contact forces for G1) is a profound insight, guiding future dataset collection and policy design efforts. This shifts the focus beyond purely model architecture to a holistic understanding of data, sensing, and robot morphology.

**D. Future Research Directions:**
The paper also outlines several promising avenues for future work, indicating its potential to inspire further research:
- **Real-world Deployment:** Integrating real-world sensory streams (tactile arrays, joint torques, stereo depth) is a natural next step to move beyond simulation and enable robust sim-to-real transfer.
- **Joint Training:** Exploring joint training of vision, language, and diffusion modules, rather than freezing VLM backbones, could lead to stronger multi-modal fusion and broader generalization.
- **Dataset Expansion:** Expanding the G1 dataset to include more task families and diverse environments would facilitate large-scale multi-embodiment benchmarking and further validate the approach.
- **Advanced Transfer Techniques:** Investigating techniques like cross-robot policy distillation or embodiment-conditioned latent representations could further improve transfer between morphologically distinct robots.
- **Contact-Adaptive Denoising Schedules:** A particularly innovative idea is the development of adaptive denoising schedules for diffusion policies, where noise levels and iteration counts are adjusted based on contact state. This could significantly enhance closed-loop stability and robustness during contact-rich interactions.

In conclusion, this research not only addresses critical limitations of current foundation robot policies but also lays down a concrete, data-driven framework for making them more intelligent, adaptable, and robust in real-world, contact-rich manipulation tasks. Its findings have a direct impact on the design of future robotic systems and the development of generalist robot intelligence.
