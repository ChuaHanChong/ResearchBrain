# RGBManip: Monocular Image-based Robotic Manipulation through Active Object Pose Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> RGBManip presents a framework for robust robotic manipulation using only an eye-on-hand monocular RGB camera by leveraging active object pose estimation to infer 6D poses without explicit depth recovery. The system achieves superior manipulation success rates in simulation (e.g., 88.9% for Open Door) compared to RGBD and pure RGB baselines, and demonstrates real-world applicability on a robot arm.

## Problem

- Existing depth cameras are often expensive, prone to noise, and perform poorly on transparent or specular objects.
- Monocular RGB cameras inherently lack direct 3D depth information, making accurate 6D object pose estimation challenging for robotic manipulation.
- A need to balance the accuracy of pose estimation with the efficiency of exploration to minimize overall manipulation time.

## Method

- **RGBManip** is a modular framework integrating a ==Global Scheduling Policy== (RL-driven active viewpoint selection), an ==Active Perception Module== (==6D pose estimation==), and a Manipulation Module (==closed-loop impedance control==).
- The Active Perception Module employs ==kinematics-guided multi-view feature fusion== from an eye-on-hand camera to resolve monocular depth ambiguity without requiring explicit depth reconstruction.
- A Global Scheduling Policy, based on ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)==, dynamically decides whether to explore for more views or initiate manipulation, balancing pose accuracy and movement efficiency.

## Results

- RGBManip significantly outperformed all **six** baselines in simulation across **six** manipulation tasks, achieving an **88.9%** success rate on 'Open Door Test' compared to the best baseline at **74.5%**.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contributions of the global scheduling policy, the kinematics-guided depth-aware pose estimation, and closed-loop impedance control to the overall performance.
- The framework was successfully deployed on a Franka Panda robot arm, demonstrating its real-world applicability and robustness for tasks like opening doors and drawers using only monocular RGB input.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Active perception, guided by an intelligent policy, can effectively compensate for the lack of direct depth information from a monocular RGB camera to enable robust 6D object pose estimation.
> - Leveraging robot kinematics to fuse multi-view features across hypothetical depth planes is a powerful method for inferring 3D object properties from monocular image sequences.
> - A reinforcement learning-based scheduling policy can successfully manage the trade-off between acquiring sufficient visual information for accuracy and minimizing robotic movement for efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation is a highly active and critical field aimed at enabling robots to interact with and alter their physical environments, with immense potential to improve daily life. A fundamental challenge in this domain is accurate perception and understanding of the environment, particularly object identification, localization, and pose estimation.

Traditionally, visual perception in robotic manipulation relies on two primary modalities: RGB cameras and depth cameras (or combinations thereof, RGBD).
- **Depth cameras** provide direct 3D spatial information (point clouds) crucial for manipulation tasks. However, they suffer from inherent limitations: point clouds can be sparse and noisy, especially at greater distances; industrial-grade depth cameras are expensive; and they struggle with transparent and specular objects (e.g., glass, metal) due to optical interference.
- **RGB cameras**, while more affordable and capable of capturing rich texture and color information, fundamentally lack direct 3D depth, leading to spatial ambiguity for 3D pose estimation. Past attempts to use RGB-only inputs for manipulation often demonstrated significant performance drops compared to RGBD methods.
- **RGBD cameras** combine both modalities but inherit the weaknesses of depth sensors when dealing with challenging materials. Recent advancements have explored using multi-view RGB images to *recover depth information*, notably through Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) based approaches (e.g., Dex-NeRF, GraspNeRF).

"RGBManip" carves out a unique niche in this landscape by proposing an **image-only robotic manipulation framework that explicitly avoids depth recovery**. Instead of reconstructing depth, it directly estimates 6D object poses by leveraging an **eye-on-hand monocular camera's active perception** capabilities. The key distinguishing factor is the use of *kinematics-guided multi-view feature fusion* combined with an intelligent reinforcement learning policy for active viewpoint selection. This approach directly addresses the limitations of both RGB and depth modalities by providing high-resolution visual information while inferring 3D pose without the need for a separate depth sensor or explicit depth reconstruction. By demonstrating robust manipulation using only monocular RGB, RGBManip represents a significant step towards more cost-effective, adaptable, and robust robotic systems that can operate effectively in real-world scenarios, including those with transparent or reflective objects that traditionally challenge depth sensors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of RGBManip is to enable robust and dependable robotic manipulation using **solely monocular RGB image inputs** from an eye-on-hand camera. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Visual Modalities:** The authors aim to mitigate the inherent drawbacks of both depth cameras (sparsity, noise, cost, poor performance on transparent/specular objects) and traditional RGB cameras (lack of direct 3D information). By developing an RGB-only approach, they seek to bypass the reliance on depth sensors, which can be expensive and unreliable in complex environments.
2. **Leveraging Active Perception for 3D Information:** Recognizing that static monocular RGB images lack crucial depth, a primary motivation is to leverage the robot's mobility. By installing a camera on the robot's end-effector, the robot can actively move to acquire diverse viewpoints, effectively gathering implicit 3D information over time, which can then be fused to estimate 6D object poses.
3. **Balancing Accuracy and Efficiency:** A critical trade-off exists between acquiring more views (which generally improves pose estimation accuracy) and minimizing manipulation time. The authors are motivated to develop a system that intelligently manages this trade-off, ensuring sufficient accuracy for task success without excessive exploration, thereby achieving manipulation efficiency.
4. **Enhancing Versatility and Adaptability:** The framework is designed to be adaptable to various manipulation tasks (e.g., opening doors, drawers, picking mugs) and object categories, handling intra-class variations. This is crucial for generalizable robotic manipulation in diverse, unstructured environments.
5. **Inspiring Future Research:** The authors explicitly state their belief that their method will "inspire further research on real-world-oriented robotic manipulation," highlighting their motivation to push the boundaries of current capabilities and demonstrate the untapped potential of monocular vision in robotics.

In essence, the paper strives to build a highly practical, robust, and efficient robotic manipulation system that can perceive complex 3D environments and perform tasks using minimal and widely available sensing hardware, specifically a single RGB camera.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RGBManip framework decouples the robotic manipulation process into three coordinated modules: Global Scheduling Policy, Active Perception Module, and Manipulation Module, all working in concert to achieve manipulation through active sensing.

1. **Global Scheduling Policy (S):**
 - **Purpose:** This module acts as the high-level decision-maker, powered by a **reinforcement learning (RL)** algorithm, specifically Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). Its primary role is to decide *whether to explore further* (gather more visual information from new viewpoints) or *to initiate manipulation* based on the accumulated visual information and the current pose estimation quality.
 - **Mechanism:** At each time step, it takes all previous views and the current pose prediction as input. It outputs a waypoint (`p_t`) for the robot to move to if exploration is chosen, or a termination flag (`f_t`) to transfer control to the Manipulation Module.
 - **Accuracy-Efficiency Balance:** A crucial aspect is the explicit incorporation of an "accuracy and efficiency" (A&E) trade-off parameter `α` in its reward function. `α` weighs the reward for pose estimation precision (`r_prec`) against a penalty for moving distance (`r_pen`), allowing the system to be tuned for either higher accuracy (smaller `α`) or greater efficiency (larger `α`).

2. **Active Perception Module (P):**
 - **Purpose:** This module is responsible for estimating the 6D pose (position and orientation) of the target object or its specific parts (e.g., a door handle).
 - **Process:**
 - When the robot moves to a new viewpoint specified by the Global Scheduling Policy, the eye-on-hand camera captures an RGB image (`I_t`).
 - A pre-trained segmentation model (SAM) is used to crop the relevant object region from the image.
 - To handle category-level object pose estimation and intra-class variations, the module first estimates a **normalized coordinate map** from the deep features of the image. This map encodes dense 2D-3D correspondences.
 - **Kinematics-Guided Depth-Aware Module:** This is the core innovation for overcoming monocular depth ambiguity. It leverages the robot's known kinematics. For adjacent RGB images (`I_t`, `I_t+1`), their relative extrinsic parameters (rotation `R_t+1_t`, translation `t_t+1_t`) are derived from the robot's joint encoders. These kinematics are used to fuse multi-view image features.
 - **Multi-Homography Mapping:** The features from `I_t` are warped to `I_t+1` across a set of hypothetical depth planes using corresponding homographies, which are computed using camera intrinsics and relative extrinsics. This process creates a 4D depth-aware feature volume, which is then regularized and used to predict the object's size, rotation (using a continuous 6D representation), and translation.

3. **Manipulation Module (M):**
 - **Purpose:** Once the Active Perception Module provides a sufficiently accurate 6D pose, this module executes the physical manipulation task.
 - **Approach:** It employs a **closed-loop impedance controller**. This type of controller allows the robot's end-effector to move towards its target pose while exhibiting compliant behavior, making it tolerant to minor errors in pose estimation or unexpected environmental interactions.
 - **Mechanism:** It computes joint torques based on the translational and rotational errors from the target pose, incorporating stiffness and damping terms. It also dynamically predicts future points along the manipulation trajectory to ensure smooth and reliable task execution, even for articulated objects like doors or drawers.

**Domain Randomization:** To improve the generalization of the learned policies to real-world scenarios, domain randomization is extensively applied during training. This involves randomizing object textures (transparent, specular, diffuse materials), light source positions and intensities, and the initial poses of objects.

In summary, RGBManip integrates active visual exploration with advanced 6D pose estimation and robust control, all predicated on the premise of using only a monocular RGB camera and the robot's inherent kinematic information.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted comprehensive experiments in both simulated and real-world environments to evaluate RGBManip's effectiveness.

**Task Settings:**
Six challenging manipulation tasks were designed: Open Door, Open Door 45°, Open Drawer, Open Drawer 30cm, Open Pot, and Pick Mug. For each task, object positions and rotations were randomized. Models were trained using 3D models from the PartNetMobility and ShapeNet datasets within the SAPIEN simulator. Objects were split into training and testing sets to evaluate generalization to unseen instances. Performance was measured by average success rate.

**Quantitative Results in Simulator (Table I):**
- **Superior Performance:** RGBManip consistently outperformed all six baselines across nearly all tasks, often by a significant margin. For instance, in "Open Door Test," RGBManip achieved an 88.9% success rate, dramatically higher than the best point-cloud baseline (GAPartNet at 74.5%) and pure RGB baselines (DrQ-v2 at 2.5%, LookCloser at 1.25%).
- **Challenges for Baselines:** Pure RGB baselines (DrQ-v2, LookCloser) showed extremely poor performance, highlighting the difficulty of manipulation without explicit 3D information. Point-cloud baselines, while better, still struggled with specific tasks requiring high precision or complex object interactions (e.g., GAPartNet's low success rates on Pot and Mug tasks). This validates the paper's motivation to address the limitations of existing modalities.
- **Train-Test Generalization:** For tasks like "Open Pot," some methods showed improved performance on the test set, which the authors attribute to the test set containing a higher proportion of simpler instances.

**Ablation Studies (Table II, Fig. 4, Fig. 5):**
The ablation studies clearly demonstrated the critical contribution of each module within the RGBManip framework:
- **`Ours w/o Global Scheduling`:** Removing the RL-driven active viewpoint selection led to performance drops (e.g., 39.1% vs. 52.9% for Open Door 45° Test), underscoring the importance of intelligent exploration for optimal views.
- **`Ours w/o Impedance Control`:** Replacing the closed-loop impedance controller with an open-loop policy resulted in substantial performance degradation, particularly in intricate, long-horizon tasks (e.g., 29.3% vs. 52.9% for Open Door 45° Test; 29.3% vs. 61.9% for Open Drawer 30cm Test). This highlights the indispensability of robust, compliant control.
- **`Ours w/o Domain Randomization`:** Training without domain randomization also led to reduced success rates (e.g., 73.0% vs. 88.9% for Open Door Test), confirming its role in enhancing generalization to diverse environments and unseen object properties.
- **`Ours w/o Pose Estimation Tricks`:** Omitting the kinematics-guided depth-aware fusion module from the Active Perception Module caused a dramatic decline in performance (e.g., 36.0% vs. 88.9% for Open Door Test). This validates the core technical contribution for resolving monocular depth ambiguity.
- **`Ours w/ Different Number of Views` (Fig. 4):** As expected, increasing the number of viewpoints generally improved manipulation success. However, the study showed diminishing returns, with little significant improvement observed beyond 3-4 views. This reinforces the necessity of balancing accuracy with efficiency.
- **`Ours w/ Balancing A&E` (Fig. 5):** The parameter `α` successfully modulated the trade-off. Larger `α` values, prioritizing efficiency, resulted in shorter average moving distances but higher pose estimation errors. Conversely, smaller `α` improved accuracy at the cost of longer distances. The "unintuitive U-shaped error curve" for very small `α` was noted, suggesting potential for further refinement in reward design.

**Real-world Experiment:**
The system was successfully deployed on a Franka Panda robot arm equipped with an RGB-only Realsense camera. The pipeline mirrored the simulator setup, with flexible mask selection (SAM or manual annotation). The qualitative results, demonstrated through videos (link provided), confirmed the method's real-world applicability and robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RGBManip framework presents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for its innovative approach to monocular vision. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Breaking the Reliance on Depth Sensors:** The most profound implication is the demonstration of robust and high-performing manipulation using **only monocular RGB inputs**, completely circumventing the need for expensive and often problematic depth (point-cloud) sensors. This addresses long-standing challenges associated with noisy depth data, interference from light sources, and difficulties with transparent and specular objects.
2. **Novel Active Perception Paradigm:** RGBManip introduces a novel active perception strategy where the robot dynamically explores its environment to gather sufficient visual information from multiple viewpoints. This active, goal-oriented sensing, guided by an RL policy, is crucial for resolving the inherent depth ambiguity of monocular images without relying on explicit depth recovery techniques like NeRF.
3. **Principled Accuracy-Efficiency Trade-off:** The integration of a reinforcement learning policy for global scheduling, which explicitly balances pose estimation accuracy with manipulation efficiency, offers a practical solution to a fundamental problem in robotic systems. This allows for adaptable deployment where either speed or precision can be prioritized based on task requirements.
4. **Robust Control for Real-World Tasks:** The use of a closed-loop impedance controller ensures higher reliability and tolerance to minor perception errors or unexpected contact forces during manipulation, a critical feature for successful deployment in dynamic and uncertain real-world environments.
5. **Cost-Effectiveness and Accessibility:** By reducing the sensor requirements to a single, affordable RGB camera, RGBManip significantly lowers the cost and complexity of setting up robotic manipulation systems. This makes advanced robotic capabilities more accessible for a wider range of academic research, small businesses, and ultimately, everyday applications.
6. **Inspiration for Future Research:** The paper directly calls for inspiring further research in "real-world-oriented robotic manipulation." It opens avenues for exploring more sophisticated active vision policies, integrating multimodal data (even if only RGB, e.g., using tactile sensors), and developing more generalizable pose estimation techniques that are robust to even greater environmental variations. The successful real-world deployment on a standard robot arm, with publicly available videos, further validates its practicality and encourages adoption and extension by the research community.

In conclusion, RGBManip offers a compelling vision for the future of robotic manipulation, demonstrating that sophisticated interaction with the physical world can be achieved with surprisingly minimal and ubiquitous sensory input, paving the way for more adaptable, affordable, and robust robotic systems.
