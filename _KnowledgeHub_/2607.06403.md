# From Foundation to Application: Improving VLA Models in Practice

> [!summary] Summary
> LingBot-VLA 2.0, developed by Robbyant and Ant Digital Technologies, introduces enhancements for Vision-Language-Action models, addressing real-world robotic challenges by improving generalization across embodiments, expanding action spaces for whole-body control, and integrating predictive dynamics. The model, trained on a diverse 60,000-hour dataset, demonstrates improved success rates on bimanual manipulation benchmarks and long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- VLA models exhibit a disparity between laboratory capabilities and real-world deployment, struggling with generalization across heterogeneous robot configurations and diverse data sources.
- Existing VLA systems typically handle limited action spaces (e.g., dual-arm manipulation) and do not fully leverage higher degrees of freedom found in real-world robots (e.g., head, waist, mobile base, dexterous hands).
- Robots often require predictive dynamics to anticipate future scene evolution and action consequences for long-horizon tasks, moving beyond purely reactive control.

## Method

- A revamped data processing pipeline curated 60,000 hours of high-quality robotic and egocentric human video data, covering 20 robot embodiments and diverse manipulation scenarios, to enhance generalization.
- A unified 55-dimensional action representation and a ==Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)== **LingBot-VLA 2.0** model were developed, using an ==auxiliary-loss-free== routing strategy to support expanded whole-body control and diverse robotic platforms.
- A spatiotemporal-aware VLA system, via ==dual-query distillation==, integrated geometric priors from a depth estimation model (**LingBot-Depth**) and causal temporal representations from a robotics-aware video model (**Causal DINO-Video**) to improve temporal reasoning and predictive dynamics.

## Results

- LingBot-VLA 2.0 improved average progress score by **8.0** percentage points and success rate by **4.4** points on the Agilex Cobot Magic platform on the GM-100 bimanual manipulation benchmark compared to LingBot-VLA-1.0.
- The model achieved **77.1%** progress and **60.0%** success on Astribot S1 refrigerator sorting (in-domain), surpassing π0.5 by **11.8** and **13.3** percentage points, respectively, for long-horizon mobile manipulation.
- Ablation studies showed L2 loss outperformed L1 loss (**55.0%** vs. **46.4%** average success rate) for precise regression of small continuous corrections, while EEF and joint actions performed comparably on average but varied by task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Relative joint actions perform significantly better than absolute joint actions (55.0% vs. 33.7% success rate) by converting the prediction target into lower-variance local motion regression.
> - MeanStd normalization is more effective than MinMax or Q01-Q99, preserving a larger effective dynamic range for action targets and better accommodating corrective motions.
> - An auxiliary-loss-free MoE architecture effectively scales model capacity and balances expert load, achieving lower training loss and validation error than dense models with equivalent active parameters.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have positioned them as a paradigm for developing generalist robot policies. This approach leverages the multimodal alignment and semantic representations offered by pretrained vision-language models, which facilitates complex scene understanding and task generalization. Furthermore, scaling the quantity and diversity of robot data has been shown to enhance VLA system capabilities. These developments contribute to VLA models forming a foundation for robot learning.

However, a disparity exists between capabilities demonstrated in laboratory benchmarks and the requirements for real-world deployment. Practical robotic applications often necessitate:
1. **Broader Generalization**: Beyond task transfer, robots must handle heterogeneous configurations and diverse data sources.
2. **Expanded Action Spaces**: Many real-world platforms incorporate more degrees of freedom (DoF), such as head movement, waist control, mobile bases, and dexterous hands, in contrast to standard dual-arm manipulation setups.
3. **Predictive Dynamics**: Real-world execution frequently requires robots to anticipate future scene evolution and action consequences, rather than solely reacting to current observations.

Existing research has begun to address these challenges. Some efforts focus on scaling robot pretraining data to improve cross-task and cross-embodiment robustness. Other approaches integrate embodiment-aware architectural designs to manage heterogeneous robots or augment VLA models with latent action models to enhance decision-making in dynamic environments. Additionally, system-oriented research emphasizes that practical VLA deployment depends on data quality, action coverage, and pretraining objectives aligned with downstream execution. The current work, LingBot-VLA 2.0, aims to integrate these perspectives by simultaneously addressing data, embodiment, and predictive capabilities within a unified framework, moving VLA models from foundational research towards practical applicability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to bridge the gap between VLA foundation models and their practical implementation in real-world robotics. The authors identify specific limitations of current VLA systems that impede their utility in practical scenarios and aim to address these through improvements across three functional domains:

1. **Enhancing Generalization Across Tasks and Embodiments**: Current VLA models, while capable in controlled environments, often struggle with the diversity of robot configurations and operational conditions encountered in real-world deployment. The motivation is to enable robots to operate effectively with varying body types and data sources, extending generalization beyond merely transferring across tasks to include robustness across heterogeneous robot embodiments. This involves providing broader coverage over both embodiment patterns and interaction scenarios during training.

2. **Expanding Action Space to Accommodate Whole-Body Control**: Many real-world robotic platforms possess a greater number of degrees of freedom than typically addressed by standard dual-arm manipulation setups. This includes articulated heads, movable waists, mobile bases, and dexterous hands. The motivation is to empower robots to execute more complex tasks that require coordinated whole-body interaction in practical settings, necessitating a VLA system capable of controlling these expanded action spaces.

3. **Improving Temporal Reasoning Through Predictive Dynamics Modeling**: Real-world robotic tasks often unfold in dynamic environments, requiring robots to anticipate future scene states and the consequences of their actions, rather than only responding to immediate observations. The motivation is to move beyond purely reactive control to enable future-aware decision-making, which is crucial for successful long-horizon tasks and safer operation in unpredictable environments.

The central hypothesis guiding this work is that effective practical VLA systems require not only scaling in model and data size but also a better alignment with the specific demands of real-world robotics: comprehensive embodiment support, richer controllable action spaces, and robust predictive understanding of dynamic scenes. LingBot-VLA 2.0 is presented as a step towards fulfilling these requirements, aiming to transition VLA capabilities from laboratory success to application-oriented usability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LingBot-VLA 2.0 builds upon its predecessor by introducing modifications across three functional domains: data processing, expanded action space, and predictive dynamics modeling.

#### 3.1. Data Processing and Curation

The authors revamped the data processing pipeline to curate a large-scale pretraining corpus of approximately 60,000 hours. This dataset aims to enhance generalization across various tasks and embodiments.

- **Robotic Data**: Approximately 50,000 hours of high-quality robotic trajectories were selected from an initial pool of 90,000 hours. This data spans 20 robot embodiments, including single-arm, dual-arm, and mobile platforms equipped with dexterous hands or grippers. The filtering process involved:
 - **Trajectory Smoothness**: Discarding episodes where the third-order finite difference (jerk) or Z-scores of velocity/acceleration exceeded predefined thresholds.
 - **Activity Detection**: Removing episodes where state and action signals remained static for over 95% of the duration.
 - **Video-State Consistency**: Human annotators identified and removed samples where projected robot kinematics (using URDF) did not align with video observations.
 - **Video Quality**: Human annotators filtered out videos with blur, severe occlusions, dropped frames, or multi-view misalignment.

- **Egocentric Data**: About 10,000 hours of high-quality training data were retained from an initial pool of approximately 20,000 hours of egocentric human videos. The processing steps included:
 - **VLM Pre-filtering**: A unified video-level Vision-Language Model (VLM), Qwen3.6-27B, filtered out videos not depicting egocentric manipulation (e.g., third-person views, scene-walking, absence of hand-object interaction, prominent non-operator hands).
 - **Action/Hand Trajectory Processing**: For data with existing labels, metadata was organized, timestamps aligned, coordinates transformed, and trajectory completeness checked, converting samples to a standardized hand trajectory format.
 - **Action-Free Video Processing**: Egocentric SLAM was used to estimate camera intrinsics and extrinsics, followed by hand pose estimation to recover MANO parameters. Hand motion was then lifted into the world coordinate frame to obtain continuous hand trajectories.
 - **Trajectory-Level Quality Control**: Filtering included discarding videos with insufficient valid hand pose coverage (<20% valid frames), unstable SLAM trajectories, sudden discontinuities or abnormal dynamics in hand trajectories, and violations of human physiological constraints.
 - **Unified Trajectory Representation**: Valid samples were stored as hand trajectories in the world coordinate frame and transformed to the current camera coordinate frame during training, providing a consistent action representation.

- **Data Annotation**: A fully automated pipeline utilizing Qwen3.6-27B was employed to generate language supervision. Each manipulation video was segmented into temporally contiguous subtasks, with corresponding language instructions. Each subtask was assigned an atomic action from an 18-category closed vocabulary (e.g., move, pour, push, pull, rotate) and a primary object of interaction, while a video-level instruction summarized the overall task.

#### 3.2. Unified Action Representation and MoE-based VLA Model

To support diverse robotic platforms and enable more complex tasks, LingBot-VLA 2.0 employs an expanded action space and a specialized model architecture.

- **Unified Action Representation**: A 55-dimensional canonical vector represents both states and actions. This representation accommodates the maximum dimensionality across various robot embodiments, including 14 dimensions for arm joint position, 14 for end-effector pose, 2 for gripper position, 12 for hand joint position, 4 for waist position, 2 for head position, and 3 for mobility signals. For robots with fewer DoF, corresponding dimensions are padded. The study also investigates the use of relative joint actions over absolute joint actions and the impact of different normalization schemes (MinMax, Q01-Q99, MeanStd) and loss functions (L1, L2).

- **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) VLA Model**: Token-level, loss-free MoE layers were incorporated into the action expert, replacing the feed-forward network (FFN) in its transformer blocks. This design includes a lightweight shared expert for universal priors and a set of routed experts for specialized modeling capacity, with K routed experts activated per token. A sigmoid-activated function is used for router logits to avoid strong competition among experts. An auxiliary-loss-free strategy, based on DeepSeek-V3, promotes load balancing by adjusting a routing correction bias without injecting an explicit load-balancing loss into the primary action learning objective. Comparative experiments with dense models demonstrated that the MoE architecture achieved lower training loss and validation error for an identical active parameter count.

#### 3.3. Spatiotemporal-Aware VLA via Dual-Query Distillation

To improve temporal reasoning and geometric awareness, LingBot-VLA 2.0 incorporates a dual-query distillation framework.

- **Dual-Query Design**: Two learnable queries, [Q$_t$, Q$_{t+T}$], are appended to visual and textual tokens. Q$_t$ targets the current observation, while Q$_{t+T}$ targets the future observation at a horizon T (the action chunk size).

- **LingBot-Depth for Geometric Supervision**: LingBot-Depth, a depth estimation model, serves as a teacher, providing explicit geometric priors. The current and future queries are trained to predict depth representations from the corresponding manipulation frames using an L1 loss function (L$_{depth}$). This encourages the VLA model to capture immediate scene geometry and anticipate future geometric configurations.

- **Causal DINO-Video for Temporal Supervision**: DINO-Video, a robotics-aware video representation model, acts as a teacher for temporal dynamics. Built on the DINOv3 image backbone, it is extended with block-wise causal temporal attention and 3D rotary positional embeddings (3D-RoPE) and trained on 5M video clips (Internet, egocentric, robotic). This model produces motion-aware visual representations where features at each time step depend only on current and past observations. The current and future queries are trained to predict the DINO-Video features of the current and future frames, respectively, using an L2 loss function (L$_{video}$). This objective encourages the VLA model to learn current motion-aware representations and their future counterparts, complementing geometric supervision. DINO-Video achieved competitive performance on the LARYBench classification and regression evaluations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of LingBot-VLA 2.0 focused on its generalist capabilities across bimanual manipulation and long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Bimanual Manipulation Experiment Results (GM-100 Benchmark)

LingBot-VLA 2.0 was evaluated on nine tasks from the GM-100 benchmark in a generalist setting, where a single policy was trained on all tasks for each embodiment. Performance was measured by progress score (%) and success rate (%).

- **Overall Performance**: LingBot-VLA 2.0 demonstrated improved overall performance compared to baselines such as GR00T N1.7, $\pi_{0.5}$, and LingBot-VLA-1.0.
 - On **Agilex Cobot Magic**, LingBot-VLA 2.0 achieved an average progress score of 66.2% and a success rate of 34.4%. This represents an improvement of 8.0 percentage points in progress score and 4.4 points in success rate over LingBot-VLA-1.0, and 7.1 and 2.2 points over $\pi_{0.5}$, respectively.
 - On **Galaxea R1 Pro**, LingBot-VLA 2.0 achieved an average progress score of 34.6% and a success rate of 15.6%, surpassing $\pi_{0.5}$ by 7.2 and 6.7 points.

- **Task-Specific Improvements**: The improvements were notable on tasks requiring accurate object grounding and goal-directed action. For example, on "Agilex Retrieve keychain," performance improved from 67.5% progress / 60.0% success (LingBot-VLA-1.0) to 100.0% / 100.0% (LingBot-VLA 2.0). Similar gains were observed on "Agilex Pick out toy bone" and "Galaxea Pick out toy bone."

- **Remaining Challenges**: Disparities between progress and success rates on certain tasks indicate that the model sometimes makes partial progress but struggles with precise final steps (e.g., placement, release). Performance differences between robot platforms also suggest that embodiment-specific factors (kinematics, camera viewpoints) remain areas for further research.

#### 4.2. Long-Horizon Mobile Manipulation Experiment Results

The model's capability for long-horizon mobile manipulation was evaluated on two robotic embodiments: Astribot S1 (object sorting into a refrigerator) and Cobot Magic-ARX X5 (stove cleaning). Evaluation was conducted under both in-domain (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) settings, with 15 trials per task-setting pair.

- **In-Domain (ID) Setting**: LingBot-VLA 2.0 consistently outperformed $\pi_{0.5}$.
 - **Astribot S1 (Refrigerator Sorting)**: LingBot-VLA 2.0 achieved 77.1% progress / 60.0% success, compared to $\pi_{0.5}$'s 65.3% / 46.7%.
 - **Cobot Magic-ARX X5 (Stove Cleaning)**: LingBot-VLA 2.0 achieved 84.3% progress / 66.7% success, compared to $\pi_{0.5}$'s 79.9% / 60.0%.
 These results indicate improved execution of task sequences requiring coordinated base movement, object manipulation, and interaction with articulated objects.

- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Setting**: Both models experienced performance degradation in OOD settings (perturbed initial robot poses and/or unseen objects), but LingBot-VLA 2.0 maintained a lead.
 - **Astribot S1**: LingBot-VLA 2.0 achieved 37.0% progress / 13.3% success (vs. $\pi_{0.5}$'s 30.3% / 6.7%).
 - **Cobot Magic-ARX X5**: LingBot-VLA 2.0 achieved 67.5% progress / 40.0% success (vs. $\pi_{0.5}$'s 62.5% / 33.3%).
 The sustained performance advantage in success rate suggests greater robustness for completing full task trajectories. The larger performance drop for the refrigerator sorting task in OOD conditions is attributed to the simultaneous changes in initial pose and object categories.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies on four GM-100 real-robot tasks investigated the impact of various methodological choices:

- **Action Target**: Relative joint actions significantly outperformed absolute joint actions (55.0% vs. 33.7% average success rate). This is attributed to relative actions converting the prediction target into local, lower-variance motion regression.
- **Action Space**: End-effector (EEF) and joint actions achieved similar average success rates (56.0% vs. 55.0%), but task-specific preferences varied based on distribution alignment and the physical structure of the task. For instance, joint actions performed better on "Barcode Scan" due to closer alignment with the pooled action distribution, while EEF actions were superior for "Squeeze Ketchup," suggesting better representation of contact-rich endpoint motions in Cartesian space.
- **Normalization**: MeanStd normalization achieved the highest average success rate (55.0%). It preserved a larger effective dynamic range for action targets, accommodating corrective motions better than MinMax or Q01-Q99 normalizations, which tended to over-compress the action distribution.
- **Loss Function**: L2 loss achieved better average performance (55.0%) than L1 loss (46.4%). L2 loss was more effective for precise regression of small continuous corrections, while L1 loss showed an advantage for contact-rich or heavy-tailed motions on specific tasks like "Squeeze Ketchup."

#### 4.4. Perceptual Results of Dual-Query Distillation

Visual distillation was shown to enable LingBot-VLA 2.0 to generate visual perceptual outcomes during causal inference. The model's predictions for depth and DINO-Video PCA for both current and future images demonstrated effectiveness in distilling semantic priors and geometric cues from the teacher models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper, LingBot-VLA 2.0, contributes to the field of robotics by advancing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models towards practical real-world deployment. The research systematically addresses critical limitations of current VLA systems, particularly concerning generalization, expanded action spaces, and temporal reasoning.

The primary significance lies in demonstrating a pragmatic approach to bridging the gap between foundational VLA research and its application in diverse, dynamic robotic environments. By revamping the data processing pipeline, curating a large-scale and diverse dataset (60,000 hours across 20 robot configurations and egocentric human videos), and expanding the model's capacity to handle whole-body degrees of freedom, the authors have enhanced the system's ability to generalize. This broader embodiment support is crucial for deploying VLA models on a wider range of existing and future robotic platforms.

The introduction of predictive dynamics modeling through a dual-query distillation framework represents a step towards more intelligent and autonomous robot behavior. By integrating geometric cues from a depth estimation model and causal temporal representations from a robotics-aware video model, LingBot-VLA 2.0 is designed to anticipate future scene evolution and action consequences. This capability is essential for performing long-horizon tasks and operating robustly in dynamic, less structured environments, moving beyond purely reactive control.

The experimental results, particularly the improved performance on the GM-100 benchmark and the strong cross-embodiment mobile manipulation capabilities in both in-domain and out-of-distribution settings, indicate the practical benefits of these modifications. The model's robustness to perturbed initial poses and unseen objects suggests enhanced generalization, which is a key requirement for real-world applicability where conditions are rarely perfectly controlled.

The ablation studies offer insights into the impact of action representation choices, normalization strategies, and loss functions on VLA performance, providing guidance for future model development. The MoE-based architecture, combined with an auxiliary-loss-free load balancing mechanism, demonstrates an effective strategy for scaling model capacity in multi-embodiment VLA pretraining.

In summary, LingBot-VLA 2.0 provides a consolidated approach to developing VLA models that are better aligned with the demands of practical robotics. Its advancements in data utilization, action space coverage, and predictive intelligence have the potential to facilitate the deployment of more versatile and capable generalist robots in various real-world scenarios, thereby influencing the transition of embodied AI from laboratory settings to tangible applications.
