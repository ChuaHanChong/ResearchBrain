# Mitigating Hallucinations in Vision-Language Models through Image-Guided Head Suppression

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from USC and Intel Labs developed SPIN, an image-guided head suppression technique that dynamically deactivates image-inattentive attention heads in Vision-Language Models during inference. This training-free method achieves up to a 2.7x reduction in object hallucinations and 1.8x higher throughput without introducing additional computational overhead or latency.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently generate hallucinations—textual outputs factually inconsistent with visual input—hindering their reliability in critical applications.
- Current hallucination mitigation strategies are either computationally expensive (training-based) or introduce significant inference latency (existing training-free methods), limiting practical deployment.
- A lack of mechanistic understanding exists regarding how specific internal LVLM components, particularly attention heads, contribute to the generation of hallucinations.

## Method

- ==SPIN (SuPpressing image INattentive heads)== introduces an ==attention-guided head suppression mechanism== that dynamically masks specific attention heads during inference.
- It identifies =='image-inattentive' heads==, which allocate low cumulative attention to vision tokens for a given text query, and suppresses their output by a factor 'α'.
- A ==three-stage hyperparameter tuning process== systematically determines the optimal ratio of suppressed heads (r), suppression factor (α), and the specific layers for intervention to maximize hallucination reduction while preserving performance.

## Results

- SPIN achieved up to a **2.7x** reduction in object hallucinations (C_s) and **2.2x** reduction in C_i for LLaVA-1.5 (7B), and up to **3x** lower CHAIR scores compared to existing training-free methods.
- It consistently improved accuracy on POPE benchmarks (up to **3.4%**) and F1 scores (up to **3.7%**), and enhanced scores on MMHal-Bench, demonstrating broad efficacy across hallucination types.
- The method provided the highest throughput among state-of-the-art mitigation techniques, up to **1.8x** higher than alternatives, with no additional computational overhead or increased inference latency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LVLM hallucinations often originate from 'image-inattentive' attention heads, especially in deeper layers, that insufficiently attend to visual input.
> - Dynamically suppressing these visually disconnected attention heads during inference can effectively reduce hallucinations without requiring model retraining or architectural changes.
> - The optimal configuration for head suppression (ratio, factor, and layers) varies across different LVLM architectures, necessitating a structured tuning approach to balance hallucination reduction with overall model performance.
