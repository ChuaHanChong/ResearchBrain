# PretrainZero: Reinforcement Active Pretraining

> [!summary] Summary
> PretrainZero introduces a self-supervised reinforcement active pretraining framework that enables large language models to acquire general reasoning capabilities directly from real-world, noisy data like Wikipedia. This method achieved substantial performance improvements across diverse reasoning benchmarks, including MMLU-Pro and various math tasks, by allowing models to actively learn from self-generated, verifiable challenges.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) in post-training are bottlenecked by costly human annotations or domain-specific verifiers, creating a 'data-wall' for scaling reasoning abilities.
- Existing Reinforcement Learning Pre-Training (RLPT) methods often rely on synthetic, high-quality data or external dependencies like supervised fine-tuning (SFT) cold-starts, and fail when applied to noisy, real-world corpora.
- Passive self-supervised pretraining alone does not effectively foster the explicit and robust Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning abilities desired in advanced LLMs.

## Method

- A ==reinforcement active learning framework== is proposed, where a unified LLM policy performs two coupled tasks: ==mask generation== (proposing challenging word spans to mask) and mask prediction (recovering the masked content with a CoT reasoning process).
- An ==adversarial min-max objective== governs the interaction: the predictor maximizes its accuracy, while the generator minimizes the predictor's accuracy by creating increasingly challenging but verifiable masks.
- Both mask generation and prediction tasks are optimized using the Group Reward-driven Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, training directly on general-domain Wikipedia data without external supervision or SFT cold-starts.

## Results

- PretrainZero significantly outperformed base models and random RLPT baselines across diverse benchmarks; for example, a Qwen3-4B-Base model improved by **8.43** points on MMLU-Pro and **10.60** points on the math average benchmark.
- Models pretrained with PretrainZero exhibited explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, exploring multiple possibilities and verifying steps, which contrasts with the direct answer generation observed in baseline models.
- PretrainZero models served as stronger 'reasoning foundation models,' yielding sustained and significant improvements over base and random RLPT models after subsequent general RLVR post-training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vanilla RLPT methods, including entropy-based masking strategies successful on synthetic data, fail or collapse when applied to noisy, real-world pretraining corpora like Wikipedia due to uninformative or genuinely unlearnable targets.
> - An active learning mechanism that allows the model to dynamically identify and prioritize informative, verifiable, and moderately challenging content is crucial for robust and efficient reinforcement learning from noisy general-domain data.
> - The reinforcement active pretraining process fosters the emergence of explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning patterns in LLMs, demonstrating the development of genuine reasoning capabilities rather than just statistical pattern matching.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language models (LLMs) has witnessed remarkable progress, primarily driven by two foundational paradigms: large-scale self-supervised pretraining and reinforcement learning (RL) in post-training. This paper, "PretrainZero," addresses a critical challenge at the intersection of these two paradigms, aiming to bridge their respective strengths and overcome current limitations.

Initially, LLMs achieved their impressive general capabilities through **self-supervised learning (SSL)** during pretraining, typically involving objectives like next-token prediction or masked language modeling on massive, low-cost text corpora (e.g., GPT-3, BERT, Qwen). This allows models to learn extensive linguistic patterns and contextual dependencies efficiently.

Subsequently, **reinforcement learning (RL)**, particularly Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), became instrumental in "post-training" LLMs. RL fine-tunes these models to align with human preferences, follow instructions, or achieve expert-level performance in specific domains like software engineering, mathematics, or tool use. Examples include AlphaCode (for coding) and DeepSeek-R1 (for reasoning). However, post-training RL faces a severe **"data-wall"**: RLHF demands costly human annotations for reward models, while RLVR requires domain-specific verifiers and environments to provide verifiable ground-truth rewards. This reliance on expensive, domain-specific data significantly bottlenecks the scalability and generalization of RL-driven reasoning capabilities to broader, open-ended domains.

In response to this data-wall, the concept of **Reinforcement Learning Pre-Training (RLPT)** has emerged as a promising direction. The idea is to extend RL directly into the pretraining phase, leveraging abundant, inexpensive general-domain data in a self-supervised manner to foster reasoning abilities without the need for post-training-specific human feedback or verifiers. Early explorations, such as RPT [DDT+25], demonstrated the feasibility of applying RL to next-token prediction objectives for general-purpose reasoning.

However, existing RLPT approaches have faced substantial limitations:
1. **Reliance on Synthetic Data:** Many prior works, including RPT, often relied on synthetic, high-quality datasets (e.g., OmniMath with explicit Chain-of-Thought annotations) that implicitly provide strong reasoning traces, effectively resembling supervised learning rather than true self-supervised RL from raw data.
2. **External Dependencies:** Some approaches required supervised fine-tuning (SFT) cold-starts or external reward models to stabilize training or provide initial learning signals, thus not being truly "stand-alone" or fully self-supervised.
3. **Ineffectiveness on Real-World Noisy Data:** Vanilla RLPT methods, especially those using fixed masking strategies (like random or entropy-based token selection), proved inefficient or prone to collapse when applied to noisy, low-information-density real-world corpora like Wikipedia.

"PretrainZero" directly tackles these critical limitations. It positions itself as the **first stand-alone RLPT method** that successfully operates Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) on real-world, general-domain pretraining corpora (specifically Wikipedia), starting directly from base models without any SFT cold-starts or external reward models. By introducing an "active learning" mechanism inspired by human cognitive processes, PretrainZero makes RL effective and robust even in the presence of noise and low information density. This work represents a significant step towards enabling LLMs to learn complex reasoning abilities in a truly self-supervised, scalable manner during their foundational pretraining, thus breaking the verification data-wall and paving the way for more generally intelligent foundation models. It differentiates itself from contemporaneous works by focusing on a pure, fully self-supervised RLPT setup on noisy, real-world data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of "PretrainZero" is to extend the powerful reasoning capabilities traditionally developed via reinforcement learning in post-training (RLVR/RLHF) into the general pretraining phase of large language models. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Overcoming the "Verification Data-Wall":** The most significant motivation is to circumvent the severe data bottleneck encountered in current RL-based LLM post-training. Both RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) and RLVR (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards) demand enormous amounts of expensive human-annotated data or domain-specific verifiers, which are non-scalable and restrict reasoning improvements to narrow domains. PretrainZero aims to leverage the vast, inexpensive, general-domain pretraining corpora to learn reasoning, thereby breaking this data dependency.

2. **Developing Stand-alone Reinforcement Pre-Training (RLPT):** Previous attempts at RLPT often relied on external aids such as supervised fine-tuning (SFT) cold-starts (initializing RL from an SFT-tuned model) or separate reward models. A core objective of PretrainZero is to demonstrate a truly "stand-alone" RLPT framework that can directly pretrain reasoners from base models using *only* the pretraining data itself, without any additional supervised signals or external verifiers. This simplifies the training pipeline and underscores the self-supervised nature of the approach.

3. **Enhancing General Reasoning Capabilities:** The project seeks to fundamentally improve the general reasoning abilities of LLMs at their foundation. By integrating RL-style learning into pretraining, the goal is for models to develop more robust, explicit, and transferable reasoning patterns (e.g., Chain-of-Thought) that benefit a wide range of tasks, as opposed to merely acquiring statistical patterns.

4. **Mimicking Human Active Learning for Efficiency and Robustness:** Observations from preliminary experiments with vanilla RLPT on real-world data (like Wikipedia) revealed inefficiencies and training instability due to low information density and noise. Humans, however, actively learn by focusing on informative and challenging content. Inspired by this, a key motivation for PretrainZero is to incorporate an "active learning" mechanism where the model itself learns to identify and prioritize informative, verifiable, and not-yet-mastered content within the pretraining corpus. This strategy aims to make learning more efficient and robust on noisy, real-world data distributions.

5. **Scaling Verification in Pretraining:** By actively selecting increasingly challenging masked spans, PretrainZero is motivated to create a self-driven "verification scaling" mechanism during pretraining. This ensures that the model is continually pushed to improve its reasoning capabilities on progressively harder problems derived directly from the data.

In essence, PretrainZero's objectives coalesce around a vision of making LLMs intrinsically more intelligent and robust from the ground up, by enabling them to actively learn "how to think" from general experience without external hand-holding, thereby generalizing RL's power beyond domain-specific fine-tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PretrainZero introduces a novel **reinforcement active learning framework** for pretraining large language models, designed to enable effective learning from general, potentially noisy, pretraining corpora without reliance on external supervision or synthetic data.

#### 3.1. Reinforcement Pre-Training (RLPT) Baselines and Empirical Observations

The authors first establish RLPT baselines on the Wikipedia dataset using a Qwen3-4B-Base model to understand the challenges. They investigate three masking strategies for token prediction, where the model must generate a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and predict the masked token(s):

- **Random Next Token Reasoning:** Randomly truncates a sequence and masks the last token.
- **Random Masked Span Reasoning:** Randomly selects and masks a continuous word span.
- **Entropy-based Next Token Reasoning:** Selects tokens with the top 20% entropy for masking (a strategy previously successful on synthetic datasets like OmniMath).

**Empirical Observations from Baselines:**
- **Finding 1 (Random Masking):** Random Masked Span Reasoning, despite masking more tokens, did not effectively capture richer semantics compared to Random Next Token, suggesting that simply masking more is not enough; the *quality* of the mask selection matters.
- **Finding 2 (Entropy-based Masking Failure):** The Entropy-based Next Token strategy, which performed well on synthetic data, led to training collapse and rapid reward degradation on real-world Wikipedia data. This is attributed to the noise and inconsistencies in raw data, where high-entropy tokens might be genuinely difficult or simply unpredictable noise, leading to unstable learning signals.
These findings underscore the need for a more intelligent, active approach to selecting learning targets from noisy, real-world data.

#### 3.2. Active Pretraining Tasks (PretrainZero Framework)

Inspired by human active learning, PretrainZero proposes a framework where the model *actively* identifies informative content. It leverages a **unified LLM policy** (parameterized by ω) to perform two coupled tasks:

1. **Mask Generation Task (Generator π_ω'):** Given a text sequence `s`, the policy first generates a thinking process and then proposes a word span `m` to mask. This process is guided by a prompt (Figure 5) that instructs the model to generate masks covering one or more verifiable entities, which are continuous, exactly match words in the original passage, and are moderately challenging yet predictable from context. The generator's role is to explore and capture semantic content from the noisy corpus, adapting its masking strategy as its own prediction capabilities evolve.

2. **Mask Prediction Task (Predictor ψ_ω):** Given a sequence `s` with a mask `m` generated by the generator (replacing the span `s[p:q]` with `[mask]`), the policy `ψ_ω` is prompted (Figure 5) to first generate a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning process and then predict the masked content `ˆx`. This task serves as the verifiable reinforcement learning objective.

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Active Learning Objective and Optimization

The core of PretrainZero is a **coupled adversarial min-max objective** that governs the interaction between the mask generator and predictor:

- **Predictor's Objective (Inner Maximization):** The predictor `ψ_ω` aims to maximize its accuracy in recovering the masked span `ˆx`. The reward `r_pred` is a binary exact match (`I[ˆx_i = x_i]`) between the predicted span and the ground truth.
- **Generator's Objective (Outer Minimization):** The generator `π_ω'` aims to minimize the predictor's accuracy on its generated masks. This translates to maximizing a reward `r_gen` defined as `1 - E[R(s, m, x)]` (i.e., 1 minus the average prediction accuracy for that mask). This incentivizes the generator to create masks that are challenging but learnable, pushing the predictor to improve. A crucial detail is that if the mask prediction accuracy is zero (meaning the mask is too noisy or unpredictable), the generator's reward `r_gen` is set to 0 to avoid rewarding unlearnable masks.

This min-max formulation directly implements active learning: the generator actively probes the predictor's weaknesses by creating challenging masks, forcing the predictor to learn and improve its robustness and generalization.

**Reinforcement Optimization (GRPO):**
Both the mask generation and mask prediction tasks are optimized using the **Group Reward-driven Policy Optimization (GRPO)** algorithm. The paper shows that the definition of `r_gen` ensures consistency with the min-max objective, where the generator's advantage function `ˆA_gen` is effectively the negative of the predictor's advantage function `ˆA_pred`. The overall loss (Eq. 11) is a standard GRPO loss applied to a concatenated batch of both generation and prediction samples.

**Implementation Details:**
- **Models:** Directly pretrains base models (Qwen3-4B, 8B, 30B, SmolLM3-3B) without any SFT cold-start.
- **Dataset:** Uses only the general Wikipedia dataset, avoiding specialized QA pairs or synthetic CoT datasets.
- **Training:** Trains for 2000 steps using GRPO, filtering samples with extreme accuracies (0.0 or 1.0) for stability, and using a clip-higher strategy. A learning rate of 5e-7 with a cosine schedule is used. Specific batch sizes are detailed for mask generation (32 paragraphs, 8 rollouts each) and mask prediction (256 masks from generation, 8 rollouts each).
- **Evaluation:** Benchmarks include MMLU-Pro, SuperGPQA, BBEH for general reasoning, and a suite of 6 math benchmarks (Math 500, Olympiad, Minerva, GSM8K, AMC23, AIME24) for mathematical reasoning.

This methodology represents a significant departure from passive self-supervised pretraining and earlier, less robust RLPT attempts, by introducing a dynamic, self-improving active learning loop within the RL pretraining paradigm.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate that PretrainZero significantly advances the state-of-the-art in reinforcement pretraining, especially on real-world, noisy data.

#### 4.1. Superiority in Pretraining Performance

- **Against Supervised Baselines:** Continuous Pre-Training (CPT) and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on Wikipedia actually *degraded* the performance of already optimized base models (e.g., Qwen3-4B dropped by 16.47 and 8.09 points respectively on overall AVG). This highlights that passive learning from low-quality, general data can be detrimental to highly capable models.
- **Against Reinforcement Baselines:** PretrainZero consistently and substantially outperforms Random RLPT across all evaluated general and math reasoning benchmarks and model sizes. For instance, Qwen3-4B-Base with PretrainZero improves by 8.43 on MMLU-Pro, 5.96 on SuperGPQA, and 10.60 on math average benchmarks compared to the base model, and significantly outperforms Random RLPT by 4.20 points on overall average. Similar gains are observed for Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-30B-A3B-MoE, and SmolLM3-3B models (Table 1 & 2). These results confirm that reinforcement learning can effectively extract meaningful supervision from relatively low-quality data when guided by an active learning strategy.

#### 4.2. Emergence of Reasoning Capabilities

- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:** PretrainZero models exhibit a clear emergence of explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning patterns (Figure 8). Unlike Random RLPT, which often provides direct answers, PretrainZero models explore multiple possibilities, analyze and verify them step-by-step, and then summarize to reach a conclusion. This demonstrates that the active learning objective fosters genuine reasoning ability.
- **Stable Training Dynamics:** Figure 6 illustrates that PretrainZero maintains stable training dynamics with consistent improvements in model output entropy, response length (during training rollouts), and overall reward, contrasting with the more volatile behavior of Random RLPT.

#### 4.3. Efficiency and Robustness of Reasoning

- **Reasoning Efficiency:** While the *training* process might involve longer CoT trajectories, the *inference* time for downstream tasks is not negatively impacted. As shown in Figure 7(c), for the same MMLU-Pro questions, the CoT length of PretrainZero models remains similar to or even *improves* efficiency compared to the base model, suggesting the learned CoT is more focused and effective, not just verbose.
- **Generalization to Diverse Benchmarks:** PretrainZero demonstrates consistent improvements across a wide array of benchmarks, spanning diverse general reasoning (MMLU-Pro, SuperGPQA, BBEH) and challenging mathematical reasoning (Math 500, Olympiad, Minerva, GSM8K, AMC, AIME24) tasks.

#### 4.4. Improved Post-Training Performance

- **Foundation for Downstream RLVR:** Models pretrained with PretrainZero serve as stronger "reasoning foundation models" for subsequent RLVR fine-tuning. After 400 steps of general RLVR post-training (on Web-Instruct), PretrainZero models consistently show sustained and significant improvements over both the base model and Random RLPT. For Qwen3-4B, PretrainZero (2000 steps) leads to improvements of 2.35 (MMLU-Pro), 3.04 (SuperGPQA), and 2.81 (math average) points over the base model after RLVR (Table 3 & 4, Figure 9). This indicates that the reasoning abilities acquired during RLPT are transferable and beneficial for further task-specific alignment.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Data Domain:** RLPT performs better on general-domain Wikipedia data than on specialized MathPile (Figure 10a), suggesting that high-quality, broadly available general-domain data is sufficient and cost-effective for this method.
- **Training Robustness:** Various mask regularization strategies (e.g., limiting mask repetition, ensuring complete word spans) all lead to stable training, with PretrainZero's default approach demonstrating superior performance (Figure 10b).

In summary, PretrainZero successfully validates the hypothesis that reinforcement learning can effectively extract and enhance general reasoning capabilities during the pretraining phase, even from noisy, real-world data, by adopting an active learning paradigm.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of PretrainZero marks a significant milestone in the evolution of large language models, presenting several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Breaking the "Verification Data-Wall" in RL:** The most critical impact of PretrainZero is its ability to overcome the severe data bottleneck that has plagued RL-based LLM training. By demonstrating a stand-alone, fully self-supervised reinforcement pretraining method that works on readily available, general-domain data like Wikipedia, PretrainZero eliminates the need for expensive human annotations or domain-specific verifiers during the foundational training phase. This enables scalable acquisition of reasoning capabilities, democratizing access to powerful RL-driven AI development.

2. **Enabling Truly Self-Supervised General Reasoning:** PretrainZero shows that LLMs can learn "how to reason" from raw, noisy text in a truly self-supervised manner, without external reward models or SFT cold-starts. This pushes the boundaries of autonomous learning in AI, suggesting that models can internally generate challenging learning objectives and verify their own progress, leading to more robust and less brittle intelligence.

3. **Foundation for Stronger Reasoning Models:** By fostering the emergence of explicit Chain-of-Thought reasoning during pretraining, PretrainZero creates genuinely stronger "reasoning foundation models." These models are not just good at next-token prediction but also possess a deeper understanding of logical deduction and problem-solving. This enhanced foundational capability makes them more adaptable and efficient for a wide array of downstream tasks and fine-tuning, reducing the resources needed for subsequent alignment.

4. **Novel Active Learning Paradigm for LLMs:** The reinforcement active learning mechanism, inspired by human cognitive processes, represents a powerful new paradigm for LLM training. It allows models to intelligently prioritize informative content and adapt their learning strategy based on their current knowledge and weaknesses. This could revolutionize how models interact with and learn from vast, unstructured datasets, leading to more efficient learning and continuous self-improvement loops in AI.

5. **Re-evaluating the Value of Existing Data:** The finding that PretrainZero can significantly improve models using Wikipedia, a dataset already heavily used in base model pretraining, is striking. It suggests that even extensively processed and seemingly "exhausted" datasets hold untapped potential when approached with novel, active, and reinforcement-based learning strategies. This challenges the notion that performance gains always require new, larger, or cleaner datasets, emphasizing the importance of smarter learning algorithms.

6. **Towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI):** By allowing models to acquire general reasoning abilities in a scalable and self-supervised manner, PretrainZero takes a tangible step towards Artificial General Intelligence. The ability of a model to actively identify and learn from challenging problems within a broad domain, without explicit human guidance, mirrors a fundamental aspect of intelligent behavior and could pave the way for more autonomous and versatile AI systems.

7. **Practical Industrial Applications:** The involvement of Xiaohongshu Inc. highlights the practical relevance of this work. Such advancements in foundational reasoning capabilities could lead to more sophisticated AI functionalities in real-world products, such as intelligent content creation, advanced recommendation engines, nuanced conversational agents, or more robust user-facing AI tools that can understand and respond with greater logical coherence.

In conclusion, PretrainZero not only resolves critical bottlenecks in current LLM training but also introduces a powerful paradigm for self-improving AI. Its significance lies in making reinforcement learning a viable and impactful component of the pretraining process, thereby laying a stronger, more intelligent foundation for future large language models.
