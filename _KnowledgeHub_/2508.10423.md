# MASH: Cooperative-Heterogeneous Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Single Humanoid Robot Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> The MASH framework addresses humanoid locomotion by reframing the control problem for a single robot as a cooperative-heterogeneous multi-agent reinforcement learning task. This approach yields faster training convergence, with bipedal walking policies converging in 1306 iterations compared to 1661 for single-agent methods, and produces policies resulting in smoother actions, improved torso stability, and superior limb coordination on a physical humanoid robot.

## Problem

- Existing deep reinforcement learning approaches for single humanoid robots struggle with effectively coordinating the high-dimensional, coupled movements of the entire body, leading to suboptimal performance.
- Conventional single-agent RL treats the humanoid as a monolithic entity, limiting the explicit modeling and optimization of intricate inter-limb dependencies.
- While Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) is effective for multi-robot cooperation, its potential to enhance coordination *within* a single, high-degree-of-freedom humanoid robot was largely unexplored.

## Method

- **MASH** reformulates single humanoid locomotion as a cooperative-heterogeneous MARL problem, treating each limb (two legs, two arms) as an independent, cooperative agent with distinct action spaces.
- The framework employs the ==Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (MAPPO)== algorithm with a Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) paradigm, utilizing shared actor networks for paired limbs and a single global critic network.
- A comprehensive reward function encourages desired locomotion behaviors, stability, efficiency, and smoothness, while domain randomization facilitates robust sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- MASH achieved significantly faster training convergence (e.g., bipedal walking converged in approximately 1306 iterations for MASH vs. 1661 for single-agent PPO) and consistently attained higher asymptotic rewards.
- The framework demonstrated superior locomotion performance across key metrics, including smoother actions (MASH **0.107** vs PPO **0.547** for bipedal), enhanced torso stability (MASH **8.240** × 10⁻⁴ vs PPO **3.398** × 10⁻³), and better limb coordination (MASH **0.612** vs PPO **0.974**).
- Policies trained using MASH were successfully deployed on a physical BanXing humanoid robot, showcasing robust sim-to-real transfer and achieving coherent, fluid, and dynamically balanced walking gaits in real-world conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Applying cooperative-heterogeneous MARL principles to a single high-degree-of-freedom robot effectively enhances internal body coordination and control efficiency by decomposing the complex task into cooperative sub-tasks.
> - The CTDE paradigm, combined with shared limb-specific actor networks and a global critic, successfully captures inter-limb dependencies and provides stable, efficient learning for complex whole-body movements.
> - Domain randomization is crucial for enabling the learned multi-agent policies to generalize and transfer from simulation to real-world humanoid robots, handling inherent physical discrepancies and disturbances.
