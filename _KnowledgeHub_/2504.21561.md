# Iterative Tool Usage Exploration for Multimodal Agents via Step-wise Preference Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed SPORT, an iterative framework enabling multimodal agents to autonomously enhance tool usage without human-annotated data. This method employs step-wise AI feedback and direct preference optimization, achieving up to 6.41% higher answer accuracy on the GTA benchmark and 3.64% higher answer accuracy on the GAIA benchmark compared to strong baselines.

## Problem

- Developing multimodal agents is hampered by a reliance on expensive and labor-intensive human-annotated task-answer pairs and tool usage trajectories.
- Pre-collected datasets for agent training can introduce biases, leading to poor generalization in dynamic, real-world environments.
- There is a scarcity of readily available datasets with ground truth annotations (answers and trajectories) for complex, domain-specific multimodal tasks.

## Method

- The **SPORT** framework introduces an iterative, closed-loop system for multimodal agents, comprising ==Task Synthesis==, Data Construction (Step Sampling & Step Verification), and Preference Tuning.
- It autonomously generates diverse multimodal tasks, explores various tool usage strategies, and employs an AI verifier to provide step-wise preference feedback on actions.
- Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is then applied to refine the agent's Vision-Language Model (VLM) controller based on this self-generated, fine-grained preference data.

## Results

- On the GTA benchmark, the SPORT Agent improved Answer Accuracy by **6.41%** (to **60.26%**), Tool Accuracy by **7.78%**, and Code Execution by **7.55%** over SFT-tuned baselines.
- For the GAIA benchmark, SPORT achieved **20.61%** Answer Accuracy, an improvement of **3.64%** over the SFT baseline, and more than **11%** over the best open-source model.
- A user study with 20 AI researchers showed an **82%** agreement rate between the AI verifier's preferences and human preferences, validating the reliability and quality of the AI-generated feedback.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Autonomous learning for multimodal agents is achievable without human-annotated data, effectively addressing the data bottleneck in agent development.
> - Step-wise AI-generated feedback proves to be a tractable and effective method for providing fine-grained supervision to agents in complex multimodal tasks, improving data efficiency.
> - The iterative self-exploration framework enables agents to adapt and generalize more effectively by learning directly from their interactions rather than relying on static, pre-collected datasets.
