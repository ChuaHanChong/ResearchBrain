# Iterative Tool Usage Exploration for Multimodal Agents via Step-wise Preference Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed SPORT, an iterative framework enabling multimodal agents to autonomously enhance tool usage without human-annotated data. This method employs step-wise AI feedback and direct preference optimization, achieving up to 6.41% higher answer accuracy on the GTA benchmark and 3.64% higher answer accuracy on the GAIA benchmark compared to strong baselines.

## Problem

- Developing multimodal agents is hampered by a reliance on expensive and labor-intensive human-annotated task-answer pairs and tool usage trajectories.
- Pre-collected datasets for agent training can introduce biases, leading to poor generalization in dynamic, real-world environments.
- There is a scarcity of readily available datasets with ground truth annotations (answers and trajectories) for complex, domain-specific multimodal tasks.

## Method

- The **SPORT** framework introduces an iterative, closed-loop system for multimodal agents, comprising ==Task Synthesis==, Data Construction (Step Sampling & Step Verification), and Preference Tuning.
- It autonomously generates diverse multimodal tasks, explores various tool usage strategies, and employs an AI verifier to provide step-wise preference feedback on actions.
- Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is then applied to refine the agent's Vision-Language Model (VLM) controller based on this self-generated, fine-grained preference data.

## Results

- On the GTA benchmark, the SPORT Agent improved Answer Accuracy by **6.41%** (to **60.26%**), Tool Accuracy by **7.78%**, and Code Execution by **7.55%** over SFT-tuned baselines.
- For the GAIA benchmark, SPORT achieved **20.61%** Answer Accuracy, an improvement of **3.64%** over the SFT baseline, and more than **11%** over the best open-source model.
- A user study with 20 AI researchers showed an **82%** agreement rate between the AI verifier's preferences and human preferences, validating the reliability and quality of the AI-generated feedback.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Autonomous learning for multimodal agents is achievable without human-annotated data, effectively addressing the data bottleneck in agent development.
> - Step-wise AI-generated feedback proves to be a tractable and effective method for providing fine-grained supervision to agents in complex multimodal tasks, improving data efficiency.
> - The iterative self-exploration framework enables agents to adapt and generalize more effectively by learning directly from their interactions rather than relying on static, pre-collected datasets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work is situated within the field of artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on the development of multimodal agents that leverage large language models (LLMs) or vision-language models (VLMs) as controllers to interact with external tools. This approach has demonstrated capability in tackling complex multimodal tasks.

Current methods for training these agents, including supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL), typically depend on extensive human-annotated task-answer pairs and tool trajectories. However, the paper identifies that for complex multimodal tasks, such detailed annotations are prohibitively expensive or impractical to acquire. Furthermore, pre-collected data can introduce bias and limit generalization to new environments or tools.

The paper draws inspiration from advancements in LLMs and VLMs that exhibit self-instruction, self-verification, and self-learning capabilities. Existing agent tuning research is largely categorized into SFT, which faces high costs and generalization issues, and RL. While RL methods aim to learn from environmental feedback, many still rely on reward models or preference data derived from human annotations or complete correct trajectories, which are difficult to obtain for multimodal tool usage tasks.

Within the context of preference tuning, prior work on step-wise preferences (e.g., STEP-DPO, SCDPO) has primarily focused on domains such as code generation and mathematical reasoning, where obtaining correct trajectories to construct preference data is comparatively more straightforward. This paper differentiates itself by addressing the challenge of obtaining such data in complex multimodal scenarios.

The work also relates to learning from AI feedback, which includes using AI feedback for in-context learning, data filtering for SFT, and reinforcement learning. This paper's contribution lies in its design of AI feedback specifically tailored for evaluating tool usage in the complex, multi-step scenarios characteristic of multimodal agents. By focusing on self-exploration and step-wise preference optimization, the paper aims to reduce dependence on human annotation and improve the agent's ability to generalize in diverse multimodal environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable multimodal agents to autonomously discover and refine effective tool usage strategies without relying on pre-collected, human-annotated data. This objective is driven by several motivations and aims to address key challenges inherent in current multimodal agent development:

1. **Eliminate Annotation Bottleneck:** The current dependency on extensive human-annotated task-answer pairs and tool trajectories for training multimodal agents is identified as a significant and often prohibitive bottleneck, especially for complex, multi-step multimodal tasks. The research aims to overcome this by allowing agents to learn through self-exploration.

2. **Improve Generalization:** Existing methods using pre-collected data may lead to biased distributions that are inconsistent with target environments, resulting in inferior generalization. The paper seeks to improve agents' tool usage generalization by enabling them to interact with and learn from real environments autonomously.

3. **Address Lack of Off-the-Shelf Data:** For complex multimodal tasks, there is a lack of readily available datasets with ground truth annotations (answers and trajectories). These tasks are often domain-specific and require long trajectories involving diverse tools, making it challenging to produce correct trajectories or objectively compare their quality. The proposed method aims to circumvent this data scarcity.

4. **Mitigate High Sampling Cost and Low Efficiency:** The exploration process in multimodal environments often involves executing various tools (e.g., large language models, web search, image generation), which can incur substantial monetary and computational costs. The research seeks a more efficient and scalable exploration mechanism.

To address these challenges, the paper proposes an iterative tool usage exploration method that allows agents to:
- Automatically generate diverse and practical multimodal tasks.
- Explore different tool usage strategies to solve these synthetic tasks.
- Evaluate the effectiveness of these explorations using AI feedback.
- Update the agent's controller based on this self-generated feedback, iteratively refining its tool usage capabilities.

Ultimately, the motivation is to build more powerful and adaptive multimodal agents that can learn and improve independently, extending their capabilities to a wider range of complex, real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, named SPORT (Step-wise Preference Optimization to Refine Trajectories), is an iterative tool usage exploration framework designed for multimodal agents. It consists of four main components that operate in an iterative cycle: Task Synthesis, Step Sampling, Step Verification, and Preference Tuning.

1. **Formulation:** The agent operates within the ReAct framework, where a VLM serves as the controller ($\pi_{\theta}$). At each step $i$, given an input $x_i$ (composed of a query $Q$, multimodal files $F$, and the history $h_i$ of previous steps), the controller outputs an action $a_i$. An action consists of a thought $t_i$ and a code snippet $c_i$ for tool calling. The goal of agent tuning is to update the controller's parameters $\theta$ to enhance tool usage capabilities.

2. **Task Synthesis:**
 - This component generates multimodal tasks, each comprising a language query and associated multimodal files. The process prioritizes query generation first, followed by file generation.
 - **Query Generation:** An LLM (Qwen2.5-7B) is prompted with randomly sampled seed queries and example tool usages from the MAT dataset to generate multiple new queries.
 - **Multimodal File Generation:** For each generated query, the LLM is prompted to specify the needed files.
 - If images are required, relevant source images are retrieved from existing datasets based on similarity.
 - For other file types (e.g., DOCX, PPTX, XLSX, PDF), the LLM generates Python code to produce the files.
 - **Task Verification:** A two-step query-file verification process (Revision and Filtering) uses a VLM to ensure the quality of the generated tasks. The VLM revises queries for better alignment with files and then assesses if tasks meet a predefined quality threshold before retention.

3. **Data Construction (Step Sampling and Step Verification):** These two components are executed alternately to build high-quality step-wise preference data, formulated as triplets $(x_i, a^pre_i, a^dis_i)$, where $x_i$ is the input context, $a^pre_i$ is the preferred action, and $a^dis_i$ is a dispreferred action.
 - **Online Search Scheme:** Given a task, the agent expands its search space step-by-step.
 - **Step Sampling:** For the current step, the controller samples $n$ candidate actions (thoughts and codes). These actions are then executed, yielding $n$ corresponding observations.
 - **Step Verification:** A multimodal verifier (an LLM) is employed. It receives the task query, current history, the $n$ candidate actions, and their execution results. The verifier's role is to select the "best" action and its observation from the $n$ candidates. This selection process focuses on desirable observations rather than overall correctness.
 - **Preference Data Collection:** The selected best action becomes the preferred output, and the remaining $n-1$ actions become dispreferred outputs for that step. This process continues for all steps in solving a task, generating $m(n-1)$ preference pairs for a task with $m$ steps. This step-level feedback is designed to be easier for pre-trained LLMs to generate compared to full trajectory-level evaluations.

4. **Preference Tuning:**
 - The collected step-wise preference data $D = \{(x_i, a^pre_i, a^dis_i)\}$ from multiple generated tasks is used to update the agent's controller $\pi_{\theta}$.
 - **DPO Algorithm:** The Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) algorithm is employed, using the loss function: $L(\theta) = -E_{(x_i, a^pre_i, a^dis_i) \sim D} [\log \sigma(\beta \log \frac{\pi_{\theta}(a^pre_i|x_i)}{\pi_{ref}(a^pre_i|x_i)} - \beta \log \frac{\pi_{\theta}(a^dis_i|x_i)}{\pi_{ref}(a^dis_i|x_i)})]$. Here, $\pi_{ref}$ is a reference controller (an SFT-trained model), and $\beta$ is a weighting parameter.
 - **Training Scheme:** The entire SPORT process is initiated after an initial supervised fine-tuning (SFT) stage, where the controller (Qwen2VL-7B) is aligned with desired outputs using 20K trajectories (following MAT). In subsequent iterations, SPORT refines this pre-tuned controller. During inference, sampling and verification are disabled for evaluation.

This iterative framework allows the agent to autonomously generate learning data and refine its tool usage capabilities by interacting with environments, thereby aiming to improve generalization and address the high cost and scarcity of human annotations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The SPORT method was evaluated on two multimodal reasoning benchmarks, GTA and GAIA, and its performance was compared against both open-source and closed-source model-based agents.

**Performance on GTA Benchmark:**
- **Overall Improvement:** The SPORT Agent (using Tuned-Qwen2-VL-7B as the controller) achieved an AnsAcc of 60.26%, ToolAcc of 72.41%, and CodeExec of 91.87%.
- **Against SFT Baselines:** SPORT significantly outperformed an SFT agent (MAT-Qwen2-VL-7B), showing improvements of 6.41% in AnsAcc, 7.78% in ToolAcc, and 7.55% in CodeExec. This indicates that SPORT's online self-exploration and step-wise optimization framework enhances reasoning and planning.
- **Against Closed-Source Models:** SPORT surpassed some agents powered by closed-source models, such as the Lego Agent GPT-4 (46.59% AnsAcc). It also achieved higher ToolAcc (72.41% vs. 63.41%) and comparable CodeExec (91.87% vs. 95.12%) compared to the Huggingface Agent (HF Agent) using GPT-4o, suggesting an improvement in tool planning.

**Performance on GAIA Benchmark:**
- **Open-Source Model Leadership:** SPORT Agent achieved an AnsAcc of 20.61%, marking the best performance among agents utilizing open-source models. It surpassed the best-performing open-source model, Qwen2-VL-7B, by approximately 11% in AnsAcc.
- **Gains over SFT-Tuned Controllers:** Consistent with GTA results, SPORT demonstrated gains over SFT-tuned controllers (MAT-Qwen2-VL-7B) with an improvement of about 4% in AnsAcc.
- **Gap with High-End Closed-Source Models:** A performance gap persisted when compared to agents leveraging larger closed-source models like GPT-4, which the authors attribute to the larger model sizes and more extensive training data of commercial models.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Effectiveness of Iteration Step Size:** An ablation study on the GTA benchmark revealed that an iteration step size ($d$) of 500 (processing a total of 1000 trajectories across 2 iterations) yielded the best overall performance, balancing update frequency and data diversity.
- **Comparison with Static Preference Data:** Applying DPO to a static, pre-collected dataset (MM-Traj, similar to MAT-SFT-DPO) showed modest improvements over vanilla MAT-SFT. However, SPORT (with its online exploration) substantially outperformed MAT-SFT-DPO across all metrics (e.g., +5.13% AnsAcc), demonstrating the effectiveness of generating diverse, multimodal preference data through self-exploration.
- **Training with Different Base Models:** SPORT demonstrated generalizability, improving performance when applied directly to the Qwen2-VL-7B base model (+12.82% AnsAcc) and further enhancing the MAT-tuned Qwen2-VL-7B (+5.13% AnsAcc over MAT alone), confirming its flexibility as an enhancement method.
- **Sensitivity to Task Quality and Diversity:** Reducing the quality and diversity of synthetic tasks in early exploration stages (fewer in-context examples, smaller seed pool) resulted in a moderate performance drop but still significantly outperformed the SFT baseline, indicating robustness.

**Statistical Analysis of Collected Preference Data (16K samples):**
- **Tool Distribution:** A 45.62% difference in tool usage patterns was observed between chosen and rejected steps, with preferred steps showing more frequent use of tools like 'visualizer' and 'objectloc', while rejected steps also highlighted 'ocr' and 'seg' errors.
- **Code Error Rate:** Rejected steps exhibited a substantially higher code execution error rate (81.94%) compared to chosen steps (18.35%), indicating the verifier's ability to identify and prefer robust, executable code. This directly contributed to SPORT Agent's improved CodeExec.
- **Content Difference:** Verifier-selected steps showed lower BLEU scores (e.g., B1 of 0.30 vs. 0.53 for random selection), suggesting that the verifier selects more distinct and higher-quality steps.

**Human Evaluation of Data Quality:**
- A user study with 20 AI researchers evaluated generated tasks and trajectories. Average scores for "Reasonableness," "Naturalness," "Code," "Tool," and "Parameter" were consistently above 8 (out of 10), validating the quality of the preference data.
- A separate study showed an 82% agreement between the verifier's step-wise preferences and human judgments, confirming the verifier's reliability in aligning with human-like preferences.

**Visualizations:** Visual examples illustrated that the SPORT Agent, after step-wise preference tuning, could effectively select correct tools and avoid hallucination, demonstrating an advantage over the SFT Agent.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SPORT framework presents a notable contribution to the development of multimodal agents, particularly in its approach to learning and generalization.

### 6. Significance

- **Reduced Annotation Dependency:** The primary significance lies in providing a method for multimodal agents to learn effective tool usage strategies through self-exploration and optimization, fundamentally reducing the reliance on costly and hard-to-obtain human-annotated task-answer pairs and tool trajectories. This addresses a major bottleneck in scaling multimodal agent capabilities.
- **Enhanced Generalization:** By allowing agents to autonomously generate tasks and explore tool usage trajectories in an iterative manner, SPORT promotes learning that is more robust and adaptable to new environments and tasks, thereby improving generalization beyond the distribution of initial training data.
- **Improved Agent Performance:** The demonstrated improvements on challenging benchmarks like GTA and GAIA, especially against SFT baselines and some closed-source models, indicate that SPORT leads to more capable agents with better reasoning, planning, tool selection accuracy, and code execution reliability.
- **Efficient Data Utilization:** The step-wise preference tuning approach allows for effective learning even from failed or suboptimal trajectories by extracting valuable step-level feedback. This improves data utilization efficiency and enables more stable and scalable self-exploration.
- **Contribution of Data Resource:** The creation of a 16K preference tuning dataset derived from the self-exploration process provides a valuable resource for future research in tool usage and multimodal agents, facilitating further studies in this area.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated AI Development:** By lowering the barrier to creating versatile multimodal agents, SPORT could accelerate research and development in areas requiring complex interactions with diverse data formats and tools. This might enable researchers with limited resources to build domain-adapted systems more easily.
- **Applications in Specialized Domains:** The framework holds potential for applications in specialized domains where human annotation is particularly challenging or expensive, such as scientific data interpretation, complex document analysis, and sophisticated human-computer interaction systems.
- **Foundation for Adaptive Agents:** SPORT's iterative learning mechanism could serve as a foundation for building more adaptive and continuously learning AI agents that can refine their skills and knowledge through ongoing interaction with dynamic environments.

**Limitations and Future Work (as stated by the authors):**
The primary limitation identified is the verifier's heavy reliance on human-designed rules and prompts, which may hinder its generalization to outlier cases. Future work is proposed to explore self-exploration techniques for the verifier itself ("learning to verify") and to develop theoretical guarantees for scaling to open-ended settings.

**Broader Impacts (as stated by the authors):**
- **Positive:** The method can lower the barrier for creating versatile multimodal agents, enabling resource-limited researchers and practitioners to build domain-adapted systems for tasks like document analysis, scientific data interpretation, and accessible educational/healthcare interfaces, fostering inclusive and scalable AI solutions.
- **Negative:** Increased agent autonomy raises risks such as unintended bias, error propagation, and resource inefficiency. The verifier, being heuristic- or model-grounded, could reinforce spurious behaviors or hallucinations, especially in high-stakes fields like legal or medical analysis.
- **Mitigation:** The authors advocate for transparent auditing, human-in-the-loop oversight, and careful management of exploration budgets to ensure responsible deployment of such autonomous agents.
