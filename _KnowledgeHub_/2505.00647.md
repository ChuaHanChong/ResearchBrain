# GeoDEx: A Unified Geometric Framework for Tactile Dexterous and Extrinsic Manipulation under Force Uncertainty

> [!summary] Summary
> Honda Research Institute and Stanford researchers develop a geometric framework called GeoDEx that enables reliable robotic manipulation using noisy tactile sensors, achieving a 14x speedup in force planning compared to traditional optimization methods while successfully performing dexterous grasping and extrinsic manipulation tasks across multiple objects.

## Problem

- Tactile sensors provide less accurate force measurements compared to traditional force/torque sensors, making reliable force-based manipulation challenging
- Direct optimization methods for force planning and estimation are computationally expensive and struggle with noisy tactile data
- Prior work often relies on binary or discrete tactile signals rather than full sensor readings, limiting the fidelity of force-based manipulation strategies

## Method

- Unified geometric framework representing force equilibrium and constraints using primitives like planes, cones, and ellipsoids
- Force estimation pipeline that projects measured forces onto a =="Force-Equilibrium plane"== and uses ==quadratic programming== to handle sensor uncertainty
- ==Admittance controller== for tracking planned trajectories using estimated contact forces

## Results

- **14x** faster force planning and estimation compared to Sequential Optimal Control Programming
- Successful hardware demonstrations of dexterous grasping and manipulation across multiple objects
- Significantly higher grasp success rates using estimated forces versus raw sensor readings, with effective handling of both normal and shear forces despite sensor limitations

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing force constraints geometrically enables more efficient optimization compared to direct methods
> - Projecting forces onto an equilibrium plane helps maintain stability despite noisy measurements
> - Planning grasps within "trusted measurement ellipsoids" improves robustness to sensor errors

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a key challenge in robotics: enabling reliable dexterous manipulation using tactile sensors in the presence of force uncertainty. It sits at the intersection of several active research areas:

- **Tactile Sensing:** The paper acknowledges the rapid development of tactile sensors but highlights the limitations in their accuracy, especially compared to traditional force/torque sensors. This research contributes to the broader effort of bridging the gap between the potential of tactile sensors and their practical usability in force-sensitive tasks.
- **Dexterous Manipulation:** Dexterous manipulation is a long-standing goal in robotics. This work contributes to this field by offering a new approach to handle the inherent uncertainties associated with tactile feedback, allowing robots to grasp and manipulate objects more reliably.
- **Force Estimation and Control:** This research builds upon existing force estimation techniques and introduces a geometric framework for planning and control, specifically tailored for handling noisy tactile sensor data. The focus on force equilibrium and robust planning connects this work to broader research in force control and contact mechanics.
- **Extrinsic Manipulation:** Many prior research focused primarily on simulation. This work extends into hardware experiments on flipping and rotating objects, but previous attempts rely on a more complex setup. This paper enables a dexterous hand with tactile fingertips to interact with different objects, which leads to better extrinsic manipulation possibilities.

The paper explicitly positions itself relative to existing research by:

- Acknowledging the use of binary or discrete tactile information in prior works and aiming to leverage the full tactile sensor readings.
- Building upon existing force estimation methods as a post-processing stage rather than directly working with raw sensor data.
- Extending simulation work into hardware experiments using a dexterous hand equipped with tactile fingertips.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are:

- To develop a unified framework (GeoDEx) for estimation, planning, and control that allows robots to perform dexterous and extrinsic manipulation even when tactile sensors provide inaccurate and noisy force readings.
- To enable robust force control in manipulation tasks by leveraging tactile sensors, overcoming their limitations in accuracy compared to force/torque sensors.
- To achieve successful grasping and extrinsic manipulation of various objects using the proposed framework.
- To improve computational efficiency in force planning and estimation compared to direct optimization methods like SOCP.

The key motivation behind this work stems from the limitations of current tactile sensors. While tactile sensors offer the potential for robots to "feel" and interact with their environment more effectively, their accuracy and reliability are often insufficient for force-sensitive manipulation tasks. Therefore, this research aims to create a method that can exploit the benefits of tactile sensors while mitigating the negative impact of their inherent uncertainties.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology revolves around the following key elements:

- **Geometric Framework:** Representing force equilibrium and constraints using geometric primitives like planes, cones, and ellipsoids. This geometric representation facilitates the formulation of optimization problems that can be solved efficiently.
- **Force Estimation:** Developing a force estimator that leverages tactile sensor readings, robot state, and object pose to estimate contact forces while considering sensor noise and the absence of shearing force readings. This involves projecting measured forces onto a "Force-Equilibrium plane" (FE-plane) and formulating quadratic programming (QP) problems to minimize errors and estimate extrinsic forces.
- **Force Planning:** Planning robust force trajectories that account for potential sensor errors. This involves representing measurement uncertainty using "trusted measurement ellipsoids" and planning grasps that remain feasible within these error bounds.
- **Admittance Control:** Implementing an admittance controller that tracks planned kinematic and force trajectories using the estimated contact forces.
- **Experimental Validation:** Conducting both simulation and hardware experiments to evaluate the performance of the proposed framework in dexterous grasping and extrinsic manipulation tasks.

**Specific Techniques:**

- **FE-Plane:** The concept of the Force Equilibrium plane (FE-plane) is central to the methodology. It provides a subspace within which force equilibrium is guaranteed.
- **M-Cone:** Using measurement cones to represent all possible measurements on all contact points.
- **Projection and Optimization:** The force estimation process relies on projecting measured forces onto the FE-plane and solving optimization problems (QP) to minimize the distance between estimated forces and the measurements.
- **Linear Feasibility Problem:** The safe dexterous grasp depends on solving a linear feasibility problem.
- **QR Decomposition:** The QR Decomposition is used for constructing basis for the measurement sub-space cone.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main findings of the research include:

- The GeoDEx framework enables successful grasping and extrinsic manipulation of different objects, even with inaccurate and noisy tactile sensor readings.
- Directly using inaccurate and noisy force readings from tactile sensors often leads to unstable or failed manipulation, while the proposed method significantly improves performance.
- Force planning and estimation using the GeoDEx framework achieves a 14x speed-up compared to directly running optimization using SOCP.
- Experimental results in both simulation and hardware demonstrate the effectiveness of the framework in various grasping and manipulation scenarios, including grasping a wrench, a cylinder, and turning a cube and a screwdriver.
- Success rates for grasping tasks are significantly higher when using estimated forces compared to raw sensor readings.
