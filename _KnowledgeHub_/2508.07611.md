# End-to-End Humanoid Robot Safe and Comfortable Locomotion Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> An end-to-end framework enables humanoid robots to achieve safe and comfortable locomotion by integrating raw 3D LiDAR perception, Control Barrier Function (CBF)-based safety, and Human-Robot Interaction (HRI)-informed comfort rewards into a P3O reinforcement learning policy. This system demonstrated a 53% reduction in unsafe time and a 30% reduction in uncomfortable time compared to reward-shaping baselines, alongside successful deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) based humanoid locomotion policies are often "blind" or rely on limited 2D exteroceptive sensing, failing to perceive non-ground-level 3D obstacles and posing collision risks.
- Traditional safety methods in RL, like reward shaping, are unreliable and lack strong theoretical guarantees, leading to inconsistent or overly conservative robot behaviors.
- Locomotion policies typically overlook human comfort and social awareness, resulting in robot motions that can be perceived as unpredictable or threatening in shared environments.

## Method

- The framework processes raw 3D LiDAR point clouds directly for robust environmental perception, providing comprehensive 3D awareness for humanoid robots.
- A principled safety mechanism translates ==Control Barrier Function (CBF)== principles into instantaneous costs for a model-free Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP) framework, trained with Penalized Proximal Policy Optimization (P3O).
- A composite reward function incorporates Human-Robot Interaction (HRI)-informed comfort rewards, including proxemic comfort, safe approach dynamics, and tangential avoidance, to encourage socially aware motions.

## Results

- The P3O-CBF policy reduced time spent in unsafe zones by **53%** and uncomfortable zones by **30%** compared to a PPO-RewardShaping baseline in static obstacle course simulations.
- It achieved significantly higher success rates in challenging scenarios, such as narrow passages (**60%** vs **33%** for P3O) and dynamic agent environments (**86%** vs **70%** for P3O).
- Successful sim-to-real transfer was demonstrated on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot, which safely navigated cluttered real-world environments and reacted to dynamic human interaction in real-time.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating raw 3D LiDAR perception directly into an end-to-end RL policy significantly enhances a humanoid robot's ability to navigate complex, cluttered environments beyond the capabilities of 2D vision.
> - Translating model-based safety guarantees from CBFs into cost functions within a model-free CMDP/P3O framework effectively enforces proactive safety constraints during learning, overcoming limitations of reactive reward shaping.
> - Explicitly incorporating HRI-informed comfort-oriented rewards leads to smoother, more predictable robot motions that maintain appropriate social distances, improving human-robot interaction and acceptance.
