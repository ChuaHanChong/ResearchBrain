# The Sim-to-Real Gap of Foundation Model Agents: A Unified MDP Perspective

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Arizona State University formalize the robustness challenges faced by foundation model agents in real-world deployment as a classical "sim-to-real gap," broken down by the four elements of a Markov Decision Process. The work proposes a unified framework for systematic evaluation and mitigation, illustrating how discrepancies in observation, action, transition, and reward lead to performance degradation.

## Problem

- Foundation model (FM) agents, often trained in idealized simulations, experience significant performance degradation when deployed in unpredictable real-world environments.
- The emerging FM community often treats agent robustness issues as novel, overlooking decades of established sim-to-real frameworks and mitigation strategies from classical control and robotics.
- Current evaluation benchmarks for FM agents are often curated and do not accurately reflect their reliability or vulnerabilities in high-stakes production settings.

## Method

- The paper formalizes FM agent robustness challenges as a classical ==sim-to-real gap== problem, dissecting it into discrepancies across the four fundamental elements of a ==Markov Decision Process (MDP)==: Observation, Action, Transition, and Reward.
- It systematically maps traditional sim-to-real gap sources from classical reinforcement learning and robotics to analogous issues encountered by FM agents.
- Specific methodologies are proposed for designing ==stress-test benchmarks== that systematically expose vulnerabilities related to each MDP element, such as injecting noise, distractors, or varying execution dynamics.

## Results

- A unified conceptual framework was established, characterizing FM agent robustness challenges as a classical sim-to-real gap within an MDP perspective, offering a common language for vulnerabilities.
- Analogous sources of the sim-to-real gap were identified across Observation (e.g., multilingual inputs, noisy multi-modal data), Action (e.g., API latency, noisy tool sets), Transition (e.g., unexpected environment responses), and Reward (e.g., misaligned objectives).
- Empirical evidence from existing research on multilingual tool calling demonstrated a significant observation gap, with error rates for models like GPT-5 increasing from **13.5%** for English to **28.5%** for Chinese queries due to parameter value language mismatch, highlighting the practical impact of these discrepancies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robustness issues in foundation model agents are not fundamentally new; they mirror the well-studied "sim-to-real" problem in classical control and robotics.
> - Decomposing the sim-to-real gap into MDP components (Observation, Action, Transition, Reward) offers a unified and structured framework for identifying, analyzing, and addressing vulnerabilities in FM agents.
> - Leveraging established solutions and a common vocabulary from classical sim-to-real research can significantly accelerate progress in developing trustworthy FM agents and prevent redundant research efforts.
