# The Sim-to-Real Gap of Foundation Model Agents: A Unified MDP Perspective

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Arizona State University formalize the robustness challenges faced by foundation model agents in real-world deployment as a classical "sim-to-real gap," broken down by the four elements of a Markov Decision Process. The work proposes a unified framework for systematic evaluation and mitigation, illustrating how discrepancies in observation, action, transition, and reward lead to performance degradation.

## Problem

- Foundation model (FM) agents, often trained in idealized simulations, experience significant performance degradation when deployed in unpredictable real-world environments.
- The emerging FM community often treats agent robustness issues as novel, overlooking decades of established sim-to-real frameworks and mitigation strategies from classical control and robotics.
- Current evaluation benchmarks for FM agents are often curated and do not accurately reflect their reliability or vulnerabilities in high-stakes production settings.

## Method

- The paper formalizes FM agent robustness challenges as a classical ==sim-to-real gap== problem, dissecting it into discrepancies across the four fundamental elements of a ==Markov Decision Process (MDP)==: Observation, Action, Transition, and Reward.
- It systematically maps traditional sim-to-real gap sources from classical reinforcement learning and robotics to analogous issues encountered by FM agents.
- Specific methodologies are proposed for designing ==stress-test benchmarks== that systematically expose vulnerabilities related to each MDP element, such as injecting noise, distractors, or varying execution dynamics.

## Results

- A unified conceptual framework was established, characterizing FM agent robustness challenges as a classical sim-to-real gap within an MDP perspective, offering a common language for vulnerabilities.
- Analogous sources of the sim-to-real gap were identified across Observation (e.g., multilingual inputs, noisy multi-modal data), Action (e.g., API latency, noisy tool sets), Transition (e.g., unexpected environment responses), and Reward (e.g., misaligned objectives).
- Empirical evidence from existing research on multilingual tool calling demonstrated a significant observation gap, with error rates for models like GPT-5 increasing from **13.5%** for English to **28.5%** for Chinese queries due to parameter value language mismatch, highlighting the practical impact of these discrepancies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robustness issues in foundation model agents are not fundamentally new; they mirror the well-studied "sim-to-real" problem in classical control and robotics.
> - Decomposing the sim-to-real gap into MDP components (Observation, Action, Transition, Reward) offers a unified and structured framework for identifying, analyzing, and addressing vulnerabilities in FM agents.
> - Leveraging established solutions and a common vocabulary from classical sim-to-real research can significantly accelerate progress in developing trustworthy FM agents and prevent redundant research efforts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development and deployment of foundation models (FMs) and their integration into agent systems represent a significant area of current artificial intelligence research. These FM agents, increasingly used for real-world decision-making, are being applied in diverse fields such as tool-augmented assistants, robotic control, and autonomous systems. However, the performance of these agents, which are often trained on idealized, curated datasets and in controlled simulated environments, frequently degrades when deployed in unpredictable real-world settings. This discrepancy is commonly referred to as the "sim-to-real gap."

Within the broader research landscape, the sim-to-real gap has been extensively studied in classical control theory and robotics. These fields have developed mature frameworks and mitigation strategies over decades to address the challenges associated with transferring policies from simulation to physical reality, where factors like noisy inputs, distribution shifts, and uncertain execution constraints are prevalent.

The paper argues that despite this established body of knowledge, the emerging foundation model community is largely treating the robustness challenges faced by FM agents as a novel phenomenon. This isolated perspective leads to "reinventing the wheel" rather than leveraging existing frameworks. The authors position their work as a corrective measure, aiming to bridge this conceptual gap by formalizing the robustness issues of FM agents within the established sim-to-real paradigm. This integration is presented as critical due to the rapid transition of FM policies from controlled simulators to production environments, where unaddressed vulnerabilities can lead to significant real-world failures. The paper therefore seeks to unify the discourse on agent robustness under a common framework, drawing parallels between classical sim-to-real problems and the challenges currently observed in foundation model agent deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to formalize the evaluation and training discrepancies observed in foundation model (FM) agents as a classical "sim-to-real gap" problem, structured around the four fundamental elements of a Markov Decision Process (MDP): Observation, Action, Transition, and Reward.

The motivation behind this objective is multi-faceted:

- **Preventing Fragmented Research Efforts:** The authors observe that the foundation model community is currently addressing agent robustness as an entirely new challenge, leading to disparate and potentially redundant research initiatives. By proposing a unified MDP-based framework, the paper aims to provide a common language and conceptual structure, which can help consolidate research efforts and prevent the fragmentation of knowledge and solutions.
- **Leveraging Established Solutions:** Classical control and robotics communities possess mature frameworks and proven techniques for mitigating the sim-to-real gap, such as domain randomization, domain adaptation, and grounded action transformation. A core motivation is to advocate for the adoption of these established solutions within the FM agent domain, thereby accelerating progress and avoiding the need to develop entirely new methodologies for known problems.
- **Accurate Measurement of Deployment Readiness:** Current evaluations of FM agents, often conducted on clean, curated benchmarks, may not accurately reflect their reliability and performance in real-world deployment. By formalizing the sim-to-real gap, the work seeks to enable the design of more rigorous, standardized stress-test benchmarks that can systematically expose vulnerabilities and provide a more realistic assessment of an agent's readiness for production environments. This is crucial for identifying critical weaknesses before deployment.
- **Addressing Critical Vulnerabilities and Preventing Real-World Failures:** FM policies are increasingly being deployed in high-stakes, unpredictable production environments. Ignoring the established sim-to-real frameworks leaves these systems vulnerable to unmeasured risks and can lead to severe real-world failures. The motivation is to proactively address these vulnerabilities by providing a structured methodology for identifying, measuring, and ultimately mitigating the discrepancies between simulated training and real-world operation.
- **Driving a Paradigm Shift Towards Trustworthy Agents:** The ultimate goal is to foster a fundamental paradigm shift within the field. This shift would involve universal adoption of a unified vocabulary for vulnerabilities and standardized stress-test benchmarks. Such a change is expected to yield a new generation of highly trustworthy agents that can maintain performance and robustness even under challenging real-world conditions, including severe multilingual inputs, transition perturbations, and cost-aware reward constraints.
- **Advancing KDD Thematic Topics:** The research agenda is also motivated by its alignment with the thematic topics of the KDD community, specifically establishing a rigorous foundation for Trustworthy and Responsible Data Science and shaping Modern AI for reliable real-world applications. By providing a structured approach to robustness testing and deployment, the work contributes to making AI systems more dependable and accountable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper is conceptual and prescriptive, rather than experimental. It focuses on establishing a theoretical framework and proposing a research agenda. The core approach involves drawing a systematic analogy between the classical sim-to-real problem in reinforcement learning (RL) and robotics, and the emerging robustness challenges faced by foundation model (FM) agents.

The methodology proceeds in several stages:

1. **Problem Formulation through MDP Decomposition:** The paper formally defines the sim-to-real gap for a policy $\pi$ as $G(\pi):= \psi_s(\pi) - \psi_r(\pi)$, where $\psi_s(\cdot)$ and $\psi_r(\cdot)$ are evaluation metrics in simulation and real environments, respectively. It then attributes the sources of this gap to discrepancies in the four core elements of a Markov Decision Process (MDP): Observation ($O$), Action ($A$), Transition ($T$), and Reward ($R$).

2. **Review of Classical Sim-to-Real Gaps and Solutions:** The paper first reviews how these four MDP elements manifest as gaps in traditional RL and robotics. For each element, it describes:
 - **Observation Gap:** Caused by perception completeness issues (e.g., partial vs. ideal signals) and representation mismatch (e.g., sensor modalities). Mitigation techniques include domain randomization, domain adaptation, and sensor fusion.
 - **Action Gap:** Arises from differences in action granularity, execution uncertainty (stochastic actuation), and system delays. Mitigation techniques include action shielding, delay-aware control, and robustification to actuation uncertainty.
 - **Transition Gap:** Occurs when simulated dynamics diverge from real environment dynamics due to inaccurate or incomplete modeling. Mitigation techniques include transition-level domain randomization, grounding methods, and distributionally robust learning.
 - **Reward Gap:** Emerge when simulated rewards do not accurately reflect real-world objectives due to incomplete task modeling or cascading effects from other mismatches. Mitigation techniques include reward shaping and reward augmentation.

3. **Translation to Foundation Model Agents:** The central part of the methodology translates these classical discrepancies into the context of FM-controlled agents. This involves:
 - **Identifying Analogous Sources:** For each MDP element, the paper identifies specific ways the gap manifests in FM agents. For example:
 - **Observation Gap:** Noisy textual inputs (typos, irrelevant context), multilingual misalignment (non-standard languages), and multi-modal noise (imperfect speech transcriptions, noisy OCR).
 - **Action Gap:** Overlapping and noisy functional landscapes in real-world tool sets, contrasting with perfectly distinct tool sets in simulation.
 - **Transition Gap:** Stochastic dynamics with transient timeouts or partial API failures, in contrast to idealized, immediate, and perfect success assumptions in training.
 - **Reward Gap:** Overfitting to binary accuracy metrics and ignoring operational constraints like latency and financial cost, which are not captured in simulated rewards.

4. **Proposing Benchmarking Paradigms:** For each translated gap, the paper proposes concrete methodologies for designing benchmarks that systematically expose these vulnerabilities:
 - **Observation:** Injecting typographical errors, query paraphrasing, multilingual user inputs, or previously unseen tool structures.
 - **Action:** Injecting distractor tools with shared names but incorrect parameters, or redundant similar tools.
 - **Transition:** Modifying execution dynamics via configurable perturbation (e.g., varying timeout rates, unpredictable retry patterns, partial JSON responses).
 - **Reward:** Constructing scenarios with redundant tools requiring evaluation of cost/latency metadata, incorporating misleading tool names, or hidden usage fees.

5. **Canonical Example Illustration:** The paper provides a detailed example of the observation gap, focusing on "parameter value language mismatch" in multilingual tool calling. It cites existing empirical work that demonstrates how FM agents, despite understanding semantic intent, produce operationally invalid tool calls when parameter values are copied from the user's language (e.g., Chinese, Hindi) instead of adhering to the API's fixed conventions (e.g., English). This example serves as practical evidence for the existence and severity of the proposed gaps.

6. **Outlining Research Directions:** Based on the MDP formulation, the paper outlines specific open research directions for mitigating these gaps, organized by the four MDP components (Figure 2). These directions include:
 - **Observation:** Multilingual alignment, multi-modal extension, data augmentation, error decomposition.
 - **Action:** Delay-aware policies, action shielding, distractor benchmarks.
 - **Transition:** Learned dynamics, grounded action transformation (GAT)-style mapping, realism benchmarks.
 - **Reward:** Multi-objective optimization, curriculum randomization, retry-aware training.
 The methodology also addresses cross-cutting challenges such as scaling robustness with model size, developing shared perturbation libraries, and creating joint protocols that reflect real-world correlations among gap types.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper's primary contribution is a conceptual framework and a research agenda, rather than experimental results from novel methods. Consequently, its "findings" are derived from its analysis and synthesis of existing knowledge and observations.

The main findings and results are:

- **Formalization of the Sim-to-Real Gap for FM Agents:** The central finding is that the robustness issues experienced by foundation model agents during real-world deployment can be formally characterized as a classical sim-to-real problem. This gap is effectively decomposed into four components based on the Markov Decision Process (MDP) framework: Observation, Action, Transition, and Reward. This provides a unified conceptual lens through which to analyze and address these challenges.
- **Identification of Analogous Gaps:** The paper successfully maps traditional sim-to-real gap sources from classical reinforcement learning and robotics to their direct analogues in the context of FM agent systems (detailed in Table 1). For instance:
 - **Observation Gap:** Mismatches manifest as perception shifts (e.g., multilingual inputs, noisy multi-modal data) and representation shifts (e.g., typos, paraphrases).
 - **Action Gap:** Differences arise from execution issues (e.g., API latency, granularity) and action space shifts (e.g., vast, noisy tool sets, distractors).
 - **Transition Gap:** Occurs due to unexpected environment responses (e.g., timeouts, partial API failures) and inaccurate dynamics modeling.
 - **Reward Gap:** Caused by misaligned real-world objectives (e.g., cost, latency, user satisfaction) compared to simplistic simulation rewards (e.g., binary accuracy).
- **Empirical Evidence of Observation Gap (Multilingual Tool Calling):** The paper highlights existing research on multilingual tool calling as concrete evidence for the practical manifestation and severity of the observation gap. Specifically, it points to the "parameter value language mismatch," where FM agents correctly interpret user intent and select the appropriate tool but produce parameter values in the user's language (e.g., Chinese, Hindi) that violate the strict, often English-only, execution-level conventions of the API.
 - Quantitative data from demonstrates a significant increase in error rates for several foundation models when instructions are transferred from English to non-English languages. For example, GPT-5's error rate increased from 13.5% for English to 28.5% for Chinese queries, and Qwen3-Next-80B's error rate increased from 5.5% to 46.5% under similar conditions (Table 2). This illustrates that the observed multilingual natural language is drawn from a different distribution than emphasized during training, and the mapping to action parameters fails to satisfy execution constraints.
- **Proposed Benchmarking Paradigms:** For each MDP element, the paper details specific strategies for designing benchmarks to systematically expose these vulnerabilities. These include injecting noise, distractors, varying execution dynamics, and incorporating realistic cost metadata.
- **Structured Research Agenda:** The paper proposes a comprehensive and organized set of research directions (Figure 2) aimed at mitigating these identified gaps. These directions are categorized by the MDP components, suggesting specific techniques and areas of focus for future work, such as multilingual alignment for observation, delay-aware policies for action, learned dynamics for transition, and multi-objective optimization for reward.

In summary, the paper's core "result" is the successful conceptual unification of FM agent robustness challenges under the classical sim-to-real MDP framework, supported by empirical examples from recent literature, and a clear articulation of a path forward for systematic evaluation and mitigation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of this work lies in its potential to establish a foundational framework for understanding and addressing the reliability of foundation model (FM) agents, particularly as they transition to real-world deployment.

- **Unifying Research and Preventing Redundancy:** By formalizing the FM agent robustness problem as a classical sim-to-real gap, the paper aims to unify fragmented research efforts. This can prevent the FM community from "reinventing the wheel" by encouraging the adoption of mature frameworks and established solutions from robotics and classical control. This unification could accelerate progress by providing a common theoretical ground and a shared understanding of vulnerabilities.
- **Enabling Standardized Benchmarking:** The proposed MDP-based framework offers a systematic approach for designing standardized stress-test benchmarks. These benchmarks, which explicitly target observation, action, transition, and reward gaps, can provide more accurate and comprehensive evaluations of FM agent robustness than current idealized benchmarks. This will allow researchers and practitioners to consistently measure and compare deployment readiness, fostering healthy competition and focused development.
- **Developing Trustworthy AI Agents:** A primary impact is the potential to drive the development of a new generation of highly trustworthy FM agents. By systematically identifying and addressing the sources of sim-to-real discrepancies, agents can be trained or adapted to inherently maintain performance and robustness even when encountering noisy inputs, unpredictable execution environments, and complex reward signals in real-world applications. This directly contributes to building more reliable and resilient AI systems.
- **Providing a Structured Methodology for Practitioners:** For practitioners deploying autonomous systems based on FM agents, this work offers a reliable blueprint for understanding potential failure modes and designing safer production environments. It provides a structured methodology for diagnosing vulnerabilities and implementing mitigation strategies, leading to more robust and less error-prone deployments.
- **Advancing Responsible AI and Data Science:** The paper directly contributes to the field of Trustworthy and Responsible Data Science, a key thematic area of the KDD community. By establishing a rigorous foundation for robustness testing and promoting dependable applications of modern AI, it helps ensure that advanced AI technologies are deployed ethically and reliably, with a clear understanding of their limitations and failure modes.
- **Guiding Future Research:** The detailed outline of research directions, organized by the MDP components, serves as a roadmap for future investigations. It highlights specific open challenges such as multilingual alignment, delay-aware policies, learned dynamics, and multi-objective optimization, thereby stimulating targeted research and innovation in critical areas of FM agent development.
- **Mitigating Real-World Failures:** Ultimately, the most significant impact could be the reduction of critical vulnerabilities and the prevention of severe real-world failures associated with the deployment of FM agents in unpredictable environments. By proactively addressing the sim-to-real gap, the work contributes to making FM agents safer, more dependable, and more suitable for high-stakes applications.
