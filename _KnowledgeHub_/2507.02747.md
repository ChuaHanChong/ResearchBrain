# DexVLG: Dexterous Vision-Language-Grasp Model at Scale

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework introduces DexGraspNet 3.0, the largest synthetic dataset for dexterous grasping with 170 million semantically-annotated poses, and DexVLG, a large vision-language-grasp model. The model predicts language-aligned dexterous grasp poses from single-view RGBD input, achieving 80% success and 75% part accuracy in real-world experiments.

## Problem

- Applying large Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to multi-fingered dexterous robot hands is challenging due to the inherent complexity of high degrees of freedom.
- A severe scarcity of large-scale, high-quality, semantically-aware data for dexterous grasping hinders the development of generalizable models.
- Existing dexterous grasp datasets are often "semantic-unaware," preventing robots from performing functional, human-like grasps based on specific object parts or intentions.

## Method

- ==Generated DexGraspNet== 3.0, a synthetic dataset of 170 million dexterous grasp poses on 174,000 objects, featuring part-level semantic annotations and language captions.
- Developed a novel "part-aware energy" framework for grasp optimization, ensuring generated grasps are functionally aligned with specific object parts.
- Trained **DexVLG**, a large Vision-Language-Grasp model, utilizing pre-trained Uni3D and Florence-2 backbones, and a flow-matching denoising module for end-to-end grasp pose prediction.

## Results

- DexVLG achieved an **87.7%** simulation success rate and **62.1%** part grasp accuracy on seen objects, outperforming baseline models by a notable margin.
- The model demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization, achieving **79.1%** simulation success on unseen objects and **76.3%** on objects with different part segmentation methods.
- In real-world experiments using a ShadowHand, DexVLG attained an **80%** success rate and **75%** part accuracy for language-instructed grasps on simple objects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale, semantically-rich synthetic data is essential for achieving robust zero-shot generalization and instruction-aligned dexterous grasping with multi-fingered hands.
> - The capacity of large vision-language models is crucial for effectively integrating language understanding with complex dexterous manipulation without compromising grasp quality.
> - Incorporating color information in point cloud inputs dramatically enhances object and part understanding, leading to a substantial boost in grasp success and part accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is rapidly advancing, with a growing focus on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that enable robots to understand and execute complex tasks described in natural language. Much of this progress, however, has been confined to robots equipped with simple, parallel grippers. Dexterous, multi-fingered hands, which more closely resemble human hands and offer greater manipulation capabilities, have remained largely out of reach for large VLA models. This is primarily due to the inherent complexity of controlling many degrees of freedom and, critically, the severe scarcity of large-scale, high-quality data for dexterous grasping.

This paper directly addresses this critical data bottleneck and the resulting gap in VLA model applicability to dexterous manipulation. Previous attempts to generate dexterous grasping data include:
- **Human motion retargeting or teleoperation:** These methods (e.g.,) are labor-intensive, time-consuming, and difficult to scale, leading to datasets with limited diversity.
- **Semantic-unaware analytic synthesis:** Works like DexGraspNet 1.0/2.0, BoDex, and others can generate large volumes of grasps by optimizing differentiable quality metrics. However, these methods are "semantic-unaware," meaning they don't inherently understand *how* humans would functionally grasp an object (e.g., grasping a hammer by its handle versus its head).
- **Limited-scale functional grasping:** Recent research on functional dexterous grasping (e.g.,) exists but is restricted by small datasets, limiting model capacity and generalizability.

"Dexterous Vision-Language-Grasp Model at Scale" positions itself as a pioneering effort to overcome these limitations. It bridges the gap by creating an unprecedentedly large, semantically-aware dexterous grasping dataset and then leveraging this dataset to train a large vision-language model for language-instructed dexterous grasp prediction. By integrating state-of-the-art part segmentation (SAMesh), advanced LLMs for semantic understanding (GPT-4o), and physics-based grasp synthesis with novel part-aware energies, the authors push the boundaries of functional, language-guided dexterous manipulation. This work is at the forefront of extending the "large model" paradigm, successful in many AI domains, into the complex and data-hungry realm of advanced robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust, language-instructed model capable of predicting dexterous grasp poses for real-world objects based on single-view RGBD input. This model, named DexVLG, aims to bridge the gap between the power of large vision-language models (VLMs) and the intricate control required for human-like dexterous robot hands.

The core motivations driving this work are:
- **Addressing the data scarcity for dexterous hands:** The most significant hurdle for applying large VLA models to dexterous manipulation is the lack of extensive, high-quality datasets. Existing datasets are either too small, too expensive to collect, or lack semantic understanding crucial for functional grasping.
- **Enabling functional, part-aware grasping:** Humans naturally grasp objects based on their intended use or a specific part (e.g., grasping a mug by its handle to drink, or by its body to hand it over). Current large-scale dexterous grasp datasets are "semantic-unaware," limiting robots to non-functional or arbitrary grasps. The goal is to allow robots to perform nuanced, instruction-aligned grasps.
- **Leveraging the power of large foundation models:** The success of large models in vision and language tasks suggests their potential for robotics. The authors are motivated to explore how these models, when properly trained with relevant data, can enhance robotic generalizability and task performance in dexterous manipulation.
- **Facilitating end-to-end learning:** Instead of complex pipelines involving separate perception, planning, and control modules, the objective is to enable an end-to-end learning approach for grasp pose prediction, making the system more unified and potentially more robust.
- **Developing robust generalization capabilities:** The ultimate motivation is to enable robots to perform language-instructed dexterous grasps on novel, unseen objects and parts in real-world environments, requiring strong zero-shot generalization.

To achieve these objectives, the authors focus on two main contributions:
1. **Constructing DexGraspNet 3.0:** A massive, large-scale synthetic dataset of 170 million dexterous grasp poses on 174,000 objects. Crucially, each grasp is mapped to semantic parts and paired with detailed, part-level language captions, validated in a physics simulator.
2. **Developing DexVLG:** A large Vision-Language-Grasp model trained on DexGraspNet 3.0, designed to predict language-aligned dexterous grasp poses from single-view RGBD inputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology is divided into two principal components: the creation of the DexGraspNet 3.0 dataset and the development of the DexVLG model.

#### 3.1. DexGraspNet 3.0 Dataset Generation

The dataset generation pipeline is designed to create a massive, semantically-rich dataset for dexterous grasping.
- **Object Preparation and Part Segmentation:** Objects are sourced from Objaverse (174,000 unique objects), filtered using GPT-4o for quality and relevance, and processed into watertight meshes. SAMesh, a zero-shot geometric part segmentation method, is used to divide objects into functional parts. GPT-4o, with set-of-mark prompting, automatically labels these parts with semantic names. Objects are also normalized to a reasonable size range.
- **Part-aware Dexterous Grasp Generation:** This is a core innovation. Building on previous analytic-based methods (e.g., DexGraspNet, BoDex), the authors introduce "part-aware energies" to guide the optimization.
 - **Part-Aware Hand Pose Initialization:** Initial hand poses, crucial for gradient-based optimization, are aligned with specific object parts using geometric cues from oriented bounding boxes of segmented parts. The paper categorizes parts into "lid-like," "disk-like," "L-shaped," and "shaft-like," each with specific hand alignment strategies, injecting human-inspired priors.
 - **Objectives for Gradient-based Optimization:** The optimization minimizes a comprehensive energy function:
 - **LP-based Differentiable Force Closure (E_FC):** An improved variant from DexGraspNet 2.0 that uses linear programming to adjust contact forces, relaxing the equal-force assumption of standard DFC, leading to more natural grasps.
 - **Part-Contact Energy (E_bar):** A truncated barrier function that strictly penalizes finger contact with object surfaces *outside* the target part, enforcing semantic alignment.
 - **Distance Energy (E_dis):** Minimizes fingertip-object distance to ensure contact and maintains a desired palm-object distance.
 - **Regularization Energies (E_reg):** Prevent hand-object/hand-self collisions, enforce joint limits, and align contact points with the front of fingers.
- **Grasp Validation and Captioning:** After optimization, all 170 million grasp poses are rigorously validated in the Isaac Gym physics simulator against four criteria: low penetration, low self-penetration, ability to counteract gravity (stability), and strict part-alignment. Each valid grasp is then semantically captioned using GPT-4o, incorporating the grasped part, object name, and contacting fingers (e.g., "Grasp the {part} of the {object} object, with contacts on {fingers}").
- **Table-top Scene Generation and Rendering:** Grasps for floating objects are adapted to tabletop scenarios by simulating objects falling onto a table and filtering out table-colliding grasps. Scenes are then rendered from multiple views using a simulated RealSense D415 camera for RGBD input.

#### 3.2. DexVLG Model Architecture and Training

DexVLG is an end-to-end large Vision-Language-Grasp model designed to generate dexterous grasp poses from language instructions and single-view RGBD input.
- **Point Cloud (PC) Encoder:** Uses a pre-trained Uni3D backbone (ViT-based, scaled up to 307M parameters), which aligns 3D features with CLIP features, enabling semantic understanding from raw point clouds. The input point cloud is downsampled.
- **Language Foundation Model:** Adopts the LLM base model and language tokenizer from Florence-2 (up to 771M parameters). PC features are concatenated with language embeddings as input.
- **Flow Matching-based Grasp Generation:** A flow-matching denoising module is used for pose prediction, minimizing a mean square objective to learn the mapping from noisy samples to ground-truth grasp poses. This module shares its transformer architecture with the LLM, conditioned on the LLM's hidden states. An MLP serves as the final pose decoder, outputting wrist pose (translation, rotation) and 22 Shadow Hand joint angles.
- **Training Details:** The entire DexVLG model is fine-tuned end-to-end on the DexGraspNet 3.0 dataset. Only the language tokenizer is frozen; all other modules are updated. The training involves a large-scale setup (64 NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs for 230 epochs), demonstrating the computational resources required for such a large model and dataset.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DexVLG is conducted through extensive experiments in physics-based simulations and real-world scenarios, addressing its zero-shot generalization and instruction alignment capabilities.

#### 4.1. Simulation Benchmarks

Three benchmarks are created to assess performance: LVIS-Seen (40 objects from a seen distribution), Unseen (84 novel objects), and SamPart3D (56 objects with different part segmentation methods). Metrics include:
- **Simulation Success Rate (Suc):** Percentage of successful object lifts.
- **Part Touch Accuracy (PTA):** Percentage of grasps touching the target semantic part.
- **Part Grasp Accuracy (PGA):** Percentage of grasps forming a functional grasp on the target part (at least three fingers in contact).

**Key Findings:**
- **Superior Performance over Baselines:** DexVLG consistently outperforms baselines, including a re-trained DexGraspNet2.0 (DGN2.0*) and its language-augmented variant (DGN2.0*+CLIP).
 - On LVIS-Seen, DexVLG achieves 87.7% Suc, 70.7% PTA, and 62.1% PGA, significantly higher than DGN2.0*+CLIP (67.8% Suc, 55.2% PTA, 40.5% PGA).
 - **Strong Zero-Shot Generalization:** DexVLG demonstrates robust performance on unseen objects (Unseen benchmark: 79.1% Suc, 36.3% PGA) and generalizes well to different part segmentation methods (SamPart3D benchmark: 76.3% Suc, 52.0% PGA). This highlights its ability to interpret instructions for novel objects and parts.
- **Importance of Large Models:** While augmenting DGN2.0* with CLIP features improved language alignment, it sometimes reduced overall grasp quality, suggesting capacity limitations. DexVLG, with its large VLM architecture, successfully integrates language understanding without compromising grasp success, indicating that large model capacity is crucial for complex, instruction-aligned dexterous grasping.
- **Contact Mode-conditioned Grasp Generation:** DexVLG can learn more complex instructions, such as specifying contact fingers. When trained with contact mode labels, DexVLG improves its ability to follow these detailed instructions, achieving higher contact mode accuracy (29.8% vs. 27.0% without contact labels) and better part grasp accuracy (48.1% vs. 33.9%).

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- DexVLG achieves **80% success rate** and **75% part accuracy** on simple objects in real-world experiments using a ShadowHand on a UR10e arm with a RealSense D415 camera. This demonstrates the practical viability of the model.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Training Data Scale:** Performance significantly improves with increased data scale. Moving from 10% to 100% of the dataset leads to substantial gains in success rate and part grasp accuracy, especially for unseen objects and 3D part recognition, underscoring the necessity of large-scale data.
- **Model Size:** Increasing model parameters from 255M to 1B provides marginal improvements in success rate, with the 320M model often performing best for part accuracy. This suggests a balance between model size and the specifics of part-level understanding.
- **Input Information (Color):** Including color in point cloud inputs ("PC w/ color") dramatically boosts both success rate and part grasp accuracy across all benchmarks, particularly on LVIS-Seen (Suc: +27.7, Part G: +23.5) and SamPart3D (Suc: +19.8, Part G: +13.1), emphasizing the critical role of color for object and part understanding.
- **Grasp Style (Wrap vs. Pinch):** Wrap grasps consistently outperform pinch grasps in stability and effectiveness, although pinch grasps offer more flexibility.
- **Denoising Paradigm:** Flow-matching significantly outperforms traditional DDPM and DDIM diffusion models for pose prediction, suggesting it's a more effective paradigm for learning grasp generation in this context.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work represents a substantial leap forward in the field of dexterous robotic manipulation, particularly at the intersection of large models, vision, and language.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
- **Breaking the Data Barrier:** The introduction of DexGraspNet 3.0, the largest-scale synthetic dataset for dexterous grasping (170 million poses on 174,000 objects) with part-level semantic annotations, directly addresses the long-standing data scarcity problem. This dataset is a critical enabler for training large models for dexterous hands.
- **Enabling Functional and Language-Instructed Grasping:** DexVLG demonstrates, for the first time at scale, a model capable of generating dexterous grasp poses that are aligned with natural language instructions and specific object parts. This moves beyond simple force-closure grasps to human-like functional interaction.
- **Leveraging Foundation Models for Robotics:** By effectively integrating pre-trained vision (Uni3D) and language (Florence-2) foundation models and fine-tuning them for dexterous grasping, the authors showcase a powerful paradigm for extending the capabilities of large AI models to complex robotic tasks.
- **Strong Generalization Capabilities:** The demonstrated zero-shot generalization to unseen objects and novel part segmentations in simulation, combined with successful real-world execution, highlights DexVLG's robustness and potential for deployment in diverse, unstructured environments.
- **Advanced Data Generation Techniques:** The "part-aware energy functions" and sophisticated hand pose initialization strategies are significant methodological contributions to the field of synthetic data generation for robotics, ensuring higher quality and semantic relevance in the generated grasps.

**Potential Impact:**
The immediate impact of this work is profound for robotics:
- **More Capable and Intuitive Robots:** Robots equipped with DexVLG could perform more nuanced and human-like manipulation tasks, such as intelligently picking up tools, handling delicate objects, or interacting with a wider variety of everyday items in a functionally appropriate manner.
- **Accelerated Research in Dexterous Manipulation:** DexGraspNet 3.0 provides an invaluable resource for the research community, lowering the barrier for developing and testing new algorithms for dexterous grasping and manipulation.
- **Advancement in General-Purpose Robots:** This work contributes to the grand vision of general-purpose robots that can understand and act upon high-level human commands, moving towards more intelligent and autonomous systems.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors candidly acknowledge limitations:
- **Workspace Constraints:** Training data does not consider hand-arm workspace, leading to potentially unsafe or kinematically impossible poses in the real world. Future work could integrate arm kinematics into the generation or filtering process.
- **Lack of Effective Grasp Ranking:** DexVLG does not support effective ranking of generated grasp poses (e.g., by likelihood scores) due to the computational cost with VLM-based models. Developing efficient ranking methods would improve real-world robustness by allowing the robot to select the most optimal grasp from multiple candidates.

Despite these limitations, "Dexterous Vision-Language-Grasp Model at Scale" represents a monumental step towards unlocking the full potential of dexterous robot hands through the power of large-scale, semantically-rich data and advanced vision-language models.
