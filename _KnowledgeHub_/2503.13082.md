# Free-form language-based robotic reasoning and grasping

> [!summary] Summary
> FreeGrasp is a zero-shot robotic grasping pipeline developed at Fondazione Bruno Kessler that interprets free-form human language to pick specific objects from cluttered bins. The system leverages pre-trained Vision-Language Models for reasoning, achieving robust performance in both synthetic and real-world environments and introducing a new benchmark dataset for evaluating such capabilities.

## Problem

- Robotic grasping systems struggle with cluttered environments, novel objects, and interpreting diverse, ambiguous human instructions in a zero-shot setting.
- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often lack robust spatial reasoning for handling object obstructions and determining optimal action sequences in complex pick-and-place tasks.
- A dedicated dataset was needed to evaluate free-form language-based robotic reasoning and grasping in cluttered scenes with explicit occlusion and ambiguity conditions.

## Method

- ==The FreeGrasp== pipeline integrates Molmo for object localization, GPT-4o for VLM-based reasoning using mark-based visual prompting and structured prompts, LangSAM for segmentation, and GraspNet for grasp estimation.
- A custom dataset, FreeGraspData, was created by extending MetaGraspNetV2 with human-annotated free-form instructions and ground-truth grasp sequences, categorized by difficulty and object ambiguity.
- The system employs a sequential decision-making process where the VLM identifies the next object to grasp (target or obstructor) based on current scene observations and user instructions, aiming to minimize total grasping actions.

## Results

- FreeGrasp outperformed ThinkGrasp in Segmentation Success Rate (SSR) and Reasoning Success Rate (RSR) across most difficulty levels on the FreeGraspData synthetic dataset, particularly in ambiguous scenarios.
- In real-world experiments, FreeGrasp demonstrated superior performance over ThinkGrasp, especially in medium and hard cluttered scenes, though both methods showed reduced success in highly complex 'Hard' scenarios.
- The average execution time per step was **15.39** seconds, with object localization by Molmo (9.12s) and grasp reasoning by GPT-4o (5.46s) being the most time-consuming components.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained VLMs can effectively interpret diverse free-form human language instructions and perform reasoning for object-agnostic grasping in cluttered, zero-shot environments.
> - Mark-based visual prompting, which overlays numerical IDs onto localized objects in the input image, and structured contextualized prompts significantly enhance the VLM's spatial reasoning and decision-making reliability.
> - While powerful for high-level reasoning, current VLMs still face limitations in precise visual-spatial understanding for highly occluded objects in complex real-world grasping tasks, highlighting an area for future VLM development.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly grasping in cluttered environments, has been a longstanding challenge. Traditional robotic grasping methods often rely on explicit geometric models or heuristics, struggling with the complexities of real-world clutter, novel objects, and ambiguous human instructions. The advent of deep learning and, more recently, large Vision-Language Models (VLMs), has revolutionized the capabilities of embodied AI agents, offering new avenues to tackle these challenges.

This paper positions itself at the intersection of **VLM-driven robotics** and **robust object manipulation**. Recent studies have begun to explore using pre-trained large-scale models for robotic control, aiming to leverage their vast semantic knowledge to interpret human commands and reason about complex scenes. Existing works in VLM-driven robotic grasping, such as RoboPoint, SpatialVLA, GLOVER, SpatialCoT, and SpatialPIN, have made strides in predicting spatial affordances, enhancing spatial representations, and improving open-vocabulary reasoning for task-oriented grasping. These systems often integrate coordinate alignment, chain-of-thought reasoning, or 3D priors to boost spatial understanding and task planning.

The current work, FreeGrasp, specifically addresses a critical gap: enabling robots to interpret **free-form language instructions** combined with robust **spatial reasoning** for **bin-picking in cluttered environments** in a **zero-shot setting**. While models like ThinkGrasp also use GPT-4o for grasp reasoning, FreeGrasp differentiates itself by more effectively handling the nuances of free-form language and complex object ambiguities, which are prevalent in human-robot interaction. The emphasis on "zero-shot" learning, meaning no task-specific data training for the core VLM components, aligns with the broader goal of developing generalizable and adaptable robotic systems that can operate in diverse, unseen scenarios without extensive retraining.

The paper also acknowledges the rich history of **learning-based and traditional robotic grasping** methods, including relationship reasoning frameworks like VMRN and HSRN, and large-scale datasets such as REGRAD and MetaGraspNetV2. FreeGrasp builds upon these foundations, particularly by extending MetaGraspNetV2 to create a novel dataset, FreeGraspData, specifically designed to evaluate free-form language-based grasping with explicit occlusion and ambiguity conditions. This contribution is vital, as the lack of task-specific datasets often hinders progress in novel research areas.

In essence, FreeGrasp aims to push the boundaries of VLM capabilities in real-world robotic applications by demonstrating how their reasoning prowess can be effectively harnessed for complex grasping tasks, while simultaneously highlighting and addressing their current limitations in visual-spatial understanding. It contributes to the ongoing shift towards more intelligent, human-friendly, and adaptable robotic assistants capable of understanding and executing high-level commands.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable a robotic arm to perform grasping tasks from a cluttered bin based on **human free-form language instructions**. This overarching goal is motivated by the desire to make human-robot interaction more intuitive and efficient, moving beyond predefined commands to natural language communication.

More specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:

- **Leveraging VLM World Knowledge for Zero-Shot Grasping:** The authors aim to explore whether pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), such as GPT-4o, with their vast world knowledge and strong reasoning capabilities, can be directly utilized (in a zero-shot setting, without task-specific training) to interpret nuanced human instructions and reason about complex object spatial arrangements in a cluttered environment.
- **Addressing Spatial Reasoning and Obstruction Handling:** A significant motivation is to enable the robot to understand spatial relationships between objects, particularly to identify when a target object is obstructed and requires the removal of other objects first. The system should then determine the optimal sequence of actions to efficiently complete the task, minimizing the number of grasping operations.
- **Interpreting Free-Form Language:** Human instructions are inherently varied and ambiguous. The research seeks to develop a method robust enough to interpret diverse phrasing, implicit spatial references, and even potentially ambiguous descriptions to correctly identify the target object.
- **Evaluating VLM Limitations:** Beyond demonstrating capabilities, the paper explicitly aims to investigate the limitations of VLMs in zero-shot settings for this complex task, particularly concerning their robustness to free-form language and their innate visual-spatial reasoning abilities. This diagnostic aspect is crucial for guiding future VLM development.
- **Creating a Specialized Dataset:** Recognizing the absence of a suitable dataset for this specific task setup, a core objective was to create a new benchmark, FreeGraspData, which extends an existing dataset (MetaGraspNetV2) with human-annotated instructions and ground-truth grasping sequences, categorized by difficulty and object ambiguity. This dataset serves to facilitate rigorous evaluation and future research.
- **Minimizing Grasping Actions:** The proposed system is designed to identify and remove only the necessary obstructing objects, thereby aiming for efficient task completion by minimizing the total number of grasping actions.

In summary, the motivation stems from the desire to bridge the gap between human language and robotic action in complex, unstructured environments, pushing the capabilities of current VLMs while also pinpointing areas for improvement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **FreeGrasp**, a novel, modular, and zero-shot pipeline designed to leverage pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for robotic reasoning and grasping based on free-form language instructions. The approach bypasses task-specific model training, relying entirely on the pre-existing knowledge of large models.

The FreeGrasp pipeline operates in distinct, sequential modules:

1. **Vision-Language Grasp Reasoning Module:** This is the core intelligence of FreeGrasp, responsible for interpreting the user's instruction and deciding the next object to grasp.
 - **Object Localization:** Upon receiving an RGB image from an exocentric RGB-D camera, the system first localizes all visible objects within the container. After empirical evaluation, the authors chose **Molmo**, a VLM with visual grounding capabilities, to "point at all objects in the bin" and return their 2D coordinates. This was found to be more effective than a combination of GPT-4o for object names and LangSAM for segmentation, which often produced fragmented or duplicate masks.
 - **Mark-Based Visual Prompting:** To facilitate the VLM's spatial reasoning, a crucial step involves augmenting the input RGB image. Each localized object is assigned a unique numerical ID, and these numbered markers are visually overlaid onto the image at the respective 2D object coordinates. This "mark-based visual prompting" transforms the input into a format more conducive for VLM interpretation.
 - **Grasp Reasoning with GPT-4o:** The augmented image, along with the user's free-form language instruction, is fed into **GPT-4o**. The authors experimented with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning but found it unreliable for occlusion reasoning due to frequent hallucination. Instead, they devised a **structured, contextualized prompt** that explicitly defines the task (bin picking with a parallel gripper), the objective (grasp target without obstruction), the reasoning logic (determine actions based on obstructions), and the possible actions (return target ID or identify top obstructor ID). This structured prompt guides GPT-4o to reliably output the ID and class name of the *next object to grasp*, indicating whether it's the final target or an obstructor.
2. **Object Segmentation:** Once GPT-4o identifies the object's class name and ID, the system needs to segment that specific object instance. This is achieved using **LangSAM** (Language Segment-Anything) for semantic segmentation with the provided class name. If multiple instances of the same class exist, the object ID (from Molmo's localization and GPT-4o's reasoning) is used to filter and identify the precise instance mask.
3. **Grasp Estimation:** With the segmented object instance and depth information from the RGB-D camera, the system uses **GraspNet** to regress the most suitable grasp pose. GraspNet takes a cropped point cloud of the object of interest and predicts grasp poses with associated confidence scores.
4. **Robotic Execution:** Finally, a UR5e robotic arm equipped with an OnRobot RG2 parallel gripper plans and executes its trajectory to grasp the identified object and place it into a predefined empty bin. The pipeline then repeats, acquiring new RGB-D observations, until the user-specified target object is successfully grasped.

**Dataset Creation (FreeGraspData):** To evaluate this novel setup, the authors created **FreeGraspData**, a synthetic dataset built upon MetaGraspNetV2. This involved:
- Deriving **ground-truth grasp sequences** from MetaGraspNetV2's occlusion graphs by pruning less significant occlusion edges.
- Categorizing **task difficulty** into "Easy," "Medium," and "Hard" based on the number of obstructing objects (hop distance in the pruned occlusion graph) and "with/without ambiguity" based on the presence of multiple instances of the target object class.
- Collecting **free-form language instructions** from ten human annotators for 300 selected scenarios (50 per difficulty category), resulting in 900 diverse instructions.

This comprehensive methodology allows FreeGrasp to tackle the complexities of free-form language, spatial reasoning, and real-world clutter in a zero-shot manner.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on both the newly introduced FreeGraspData synthetic dataset and in real-world robotic scenarios, comparing FreeGrasp against ThinkGrasp, another VLM-based reasoning approach.

**A. Experiments on FreeGraspData (Synthetic Data):**
- **Superior Performance:** FreeGrasp significantly outperformed ThinkGrasp in **Segmentation Success Rate (SSR)** and **Reasoning Success Rate (RSR)** across nearly all difficulty levels. FreeGrasp effectively handled object ambiguity due to its robust localization and mark-based visual prompting.
- **Localization Impact:** An ablation study showed that Molmo-based object localization achieved slightly higher RSR than using ground-truth (GT) localizations. This counter-intuitive result was attributed to Molmo tending to miss highly occluded objects, which in turn led the reasoning module to focus on more surface-level, graspable objects.
- **VLM Robustness to Language:** Analysis of the human-annotated instructions in FreeGraspData revealed significant variations in sentence structure (low Rouge-L scores) but moderate semantic similarity (medium SBERT embedding scores). Crucially, GPT-4o demonstrated consistently high **GPT scores** (interpretability), indicating its robustness in identifying the correct target object despite diverse phrasing.
- **ThinkGrasp's Behavior:** ThinkGrasp's performance showed an unusual pattern where it sometimes performed better in ambiguous cluttered cases than non-ambiguous ones. Qualitative analysis suggested this was due to its tendency to segment *all instances* of a specified object, increasing the chance that one of them (or an obstructor of the same class) would align with ground truth in ambiguous scenarios.

**B. Experiments in the Real World:**
- **Outperformance in Clutter:** FreeGrasp generally outperformed ThinkGrasp in real-world settings, particularly as scene clutter and object ambiguity increased. ThinkGrasp struggled considerably in Medium and Hard scenarios, failing all episodes under the most stringent failure criteria.
- **Difficulty of Hard Scenarios:** Both methods showed a significant drop in performance for "Hard" scenarios, highlighting the inherent difficulty of complex obstruction reasoning and manipulation in real-world clutter. The task remains far from solved.
- **Impact of Failure Criteria:** The authors devised three operational settings (S, P, M for Segmentation, Pose, Motion failures) to analyze localized failures:
 - **(S, P, M):** Most stringent, FreeGrasp succeeded in up to 80% of easy ambiguous cases, but only 10-20% in medium scenarios, and 0-10% in hard ones. ThinkGrasp largely failed in medium and hard.
 - **(P, M):** Relaxing segmentation failures sometimes led to slightly better performance, as incorrect but broadly inclusive segmentation masks could still lead to correct grasp poses.
 - **(P):** Ignoring motion failures (focusing only on pose) naturally yielded the best performance, emphasizing that robotic execution remains a significant challenge.
- **Qualitative Observations:** The real-world results illustrated that FreeGrasp's vision-language grasp reasoning module was effective in identifying the correct object to pick, even when segmentation or pose estimation had minor inaccuracies.

**C. Computational Analysis:**
- FreeGrasp demonstrated an average total execution time of 15.39 seconds per step on a workstation with an NVIDIA RTX 4500 GPU.
- The most time-consuming components were **object localization with Molmo (9.12s)** and **grasp reasoning with GPT-4o (5.46s)**. Object segmentation (LangSAM, 0.71s) and grasp estimation (GraspNet, 0.10s) were relatively faster.
- The modular nature allows for parallelism, with VLM reasoning and pose estimation potentially running concurrently with robotic manipulation after the initial step.

Overall, FreeGrasp demonstrated strong capabilities in interpreting free-form language and performing reasoning for cluttered bin-picking, significantly advancing the state-of-the-art in VLM-driven robotic grasping, despite highlighting the remaining challenges in highly complex real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The FreeGrasp research makes several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact on the fields of robotics, artificial intelligence, and human-robot interaction:

1. **Advancing VLM-driven Robotic Manipulation:** FreeGrasp demonstrates a highly effective method for leveraging the world knowledge of pre-trained VLMs (like GPT-4o) in a **zero-shot setting** for complex robotic grasping tasks. This is crucial for developing generalizable robotic systems that can adapt to novel objects and scenarios without extensive, costly, and time-consuming task-specific data collection and training. It paves the way for more versatile and rapidly deployable robots in logistics, manufacturing, and service industries.

2. **Robust Free-Form Language Interpretation:** The ability to command robots using natural, free-form language is a cornerstone of intuitive human-robot interaction. FreeGrasp's success in interpreting diverse, ambiguous, and context-dependent human instructions marks a significant step towards more natural and accessible robot programming, reducing the need for specialized coding or constrained vocabulary.

3. **Enhanced Spatial and Obstruction Reasoning:** The method's focus on actively reasoning about spatial relationships and object obstructions for task planning is a critical advancement. By identifying and strategically removing occluding objects, FreeGrasp enables robots to tackle previously intractable grasping problems in highly cluttered environments, which are ubiquitous in real-world applications (e.g., warehouses, domestic settings).

4. **Novel Benchmark Dataset (FreeGraspData):** The introduction of FreeGraspData, a synthetic dataset with human-annotated instructions, ground-truth grasp sequences, and difficulty categorizations, is a valuable resource for the research community. It provides a standardized and challenging benchmark for evaluating future advancements in free-form language-based robotic reasoning and grasping, accelerating research in this specific domain.

5. **Identification of VLM Limitations and Future Research Directions:** Beyond showcasing capabilities, the paper provides critical insights into the limitations of current state-of-the-art VLMs in visual-spatial reasoning and understanding object occlusion. This diagnosis is highly significant, as it informs developers and researchers about crucial areas for improving next-generation VLMs. The proposed future work on memory mechanisms to track scene changes and adaptive instruction updates based on VLM feedback presents clear, actionable pathways for further research.

6. **Modular and Extensible Pipeline:** The modular design of FreeGrasp, separating object localization, VLM reasoning, segmentation, and grasp estimation, makes the system flexible and adaptable. Individual components can be upgraded or replaced as new models emerge, allowing for continuous improvement and integration of specialized modules.

The potential impact spans various applications where robots need to interact with objects in unstructured settings and understand human commands, from automated fulfillment centers to assistive robotics in homes and hospitals. By making robots more intelligent, adaptable, and easier to instruct, FreeGrasp contributes to the broader vision of creating truly useful and collaborative robotic partners.
